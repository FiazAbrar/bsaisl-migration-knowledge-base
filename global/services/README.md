## 1 In one minute

Most of the state's help for migrants comes from two bodies funded by migrants themselves. The Wage Earners' Welfare Board runs on a fund formed from migrants' own money, and it provided 95% of the capital of Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrants <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. PKB lends up to Tk 3 lakh to go abroad, at 9% simple interest for up to 3 years, without collateral but with two guarantors, and the borrower pays fees of 1% plus VAT and Tk 900 per lakh for a risk cover that writes off the rest of the loan if the borrower dies <sup>[1](#o1), [N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Since 3 December 2025 a worker can apply for it online on MyGov, the government's service portal; more than 10,000 had applied by November 2026, and approval was taking 3 to 4 days, according to a press release from the government's a2i programme <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Under a policy of November 2025 PKB also lends Tk 1 lakh to Tk 10 lakh at 11% for a short language or skills course abroad, and by October 2026 Bangladesh Bank had launched a Tk 1,000 crore scheme through PKB that lends workers up to Tk 10 lakh, without collateral and at no more than 9%, for language or skills training in the destination country <sup>[2](#o2), [N4](#n4)</sup>. BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) runs online evening courses in Japanese, German, Korean, English and Arabic for Tk 1,000 to Tk 3,000 at its Foreign Language and Skills Centre, and hires trainers for languages from Japanese to Portuguese at its TTCs (technical training centres), though a Cornell University language-centre coordinator estimated that only about 2,500 of some 13 lakh workers who went abroad in a year had learned Japanese, Korean, English or Chinese at a government centre <sup>[3](#o3), [N5](#n5), [N6](#n6), [N7](#n7)</sup>. The Welfare Board's desks at the three international airports check clearances on the way out, send the sick to hospital and receive the dead on the way back, and hand the family a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial <sup>[4](#o4), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. At home, the Board pays scholarships to migrants' children and a monthly allowance for a disabled child, for workers who left with BMET clearance or joined the Board, and for workers who died and whose family got its grant <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8), [N9 (2024)](#n9)</sup>. On one day in April 2025 the Board handed 103 people Tk 1 crore 93 lakh in grants, scholarships, insurance and other help, and said it had given Tk 273 crore 81 lakh to 12,158 people during the interim government's term <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. Abroad, a worker in some Gulf countries, South Korea or Italy can finish SSC (Secondary School Certificate), HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) or a degree through Bangladesh Open University via the embassy <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11), [N12 (2024)](#n12), [N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. In March 2024 the state minister promised welfare cells to take services to the grassroots, and an NGO project, Helvetas's SIMS, worked in 23 upazilas of three districts <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. The 2026-27 budget announced a Probashi card to make government services and welfare benefits easier to get, and "Bangladesh Support Centres" at embassies to rescue workers in jail or cheated and give them legal help; on 12 November 2026 the ministry said the card had not been launched and no applications were being taken, and warned that brokers were taking money in its name <sup>[N15](#n15), [N16](#n16), [5](#o5)</sup>. In the 2026 Middle East war the ministry ran a round-the-clock hotline for migrant workers, and the Welfare Board paid to bring 186 people home from Iran <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18)</sup>. For returnees, the Welfare Board's World Bank-funded RAISE project, due to end in June 2026, registered returnees at district welfare centres and paid cash incentives that averaged Tk 13,500, a sum the state minister called far too little; BRAC's Prottasha project also works on rehabilitation <sup>[6](#o6), [N19](#n19), [N20](#n20), [N21](#n21)</sup>. The Centre for Women and Children Studies (CWCS), a women's advocacy group, said in May 2024 that some returnee women are not taken back by their husbands or families, and asked for guarantor-free loans for returnee women and free legal help for those who suffered sexual exploitation, abuse, forced labour or trafficking <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>.

## 2 Before you go

<a id="advice-near-home-before-you-decide"></a>

### 2.1 Advice near home before you decide

Helvetas Swiss Intercooperation's SIMS project ran in 23 upazilas (sub-districts) of Cumilla, Narsingdi and Chattogram; by Helvetas's count it made about 10 lakh people aware of safe migration and trained about one lakh potential migrants in how to make a migration decision <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. It also trained about 3,500 families in managing money and linked them to markets to start small businesses <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. Its first phase closed in March 2024 <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. The state minister for expatriates' welfare, Shafiqur Rahman Chowdhury, said in March 2024 that Probashi Kallyan (migrant welfare) cells would be started to take services to the grassroots and that PKB's branches would be expanded <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

At a roundtable on 5 May 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare, Nurul Haque, spoke of plans for a one-stop service at district level for women who want to go abroad, bringing the district manpower office, PKB and the welfare centre into one building, along with a "Probashi Card" for migrants, a standard migration cost and a grading system to identify good recruiting agencies <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>. At the same roundtable Farzana Shahnaz, a national programme officer working on reintegration and labour mobility, said entrepreneurs at Union Digital Centres (UDCs, the government's union-level service centres) should be trained to bring correct information and counselling to the grassroots <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Did the Probashi Kallyan cells promised in March 2024 open, and where does someone go to find one <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>?
> - Has any district opened the one-stop service described in May 2026, and which <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>?
> - Is the SIMS project running a second phase in 2026, and in which upazilas <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>?

How far the state's own offices reach: Mohiuddin Ahmed, a Canada-based analyst, wrote in July 2026 that a World Bank assessment found BMET's databases are not linked to each other and that the DEMO (district employment and manpower) offices cannot reach the large population in remote areas <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. He cited a 2024 CPD (Centre for Policy Dialogue) study that of more than 11 lakh workers who went abroad in 2023, only 15,294 (1.3%) went through BOESL, the government's recruiting agency <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. The government said in its 2026-27 budget that the migration process had already been made fully digital through OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system for registration and clearance) <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. What BOESL offers and how to apply: see "Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

**Migrants' records.** TechGlobal Institute (TGI), a technology research institute, claims in a report covering January 2023 to May 2026 that in one incident the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's database put migrants' passports, NIDs (national identity cards), e-TINs (tax numbers) and financial and banking documents at risk of exposure together, and that there were three attempts to break into the migrant workers' database, which holds the data of more than 10 lakh workers, within about six weeks in April and May 2026 <sup>[N25](#n25)</sup>.

<a id="the-probashi-card"></a>

### 2.2 The Probashi card

The Probashi card, its announced benefits and the July 2026 pilot plan (Janata Bank first, then Probashi Kallyan Bank) are in "The Probashi Card" in the Sending money home doc <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>. In short:
- **Announced:** in the 2026-27 budget of 11 June 2026, for people going abroad and migrants already working abroad, to keep workers' information and make government services and welfare benefits easier to get <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
- **13 October 2026:** the state minister, Md. Nurul Haque, said it was being piloted in four to five countries, including Jordan, South Korea and several Middle Eastern countries, and that card holders were planned to get a 3% incentive on money sent home through legal channels, against the 2.5% paid now <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.
- **12 November 2026:** the ministry said the card was at the final stage of preparation, had not been formally launched, and no application process had started <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.

**The card scam.** The ministry warned the same day that dishonest people and brokers at home and abroad were taking money from migrants in the card's name, promising a pension or a fast card <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>:
- Pay no one for the card; the ministry asks people not to <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.
- How to apply, who qualifies and the benefits will be announced only through official media, the ministry's website (probashi.gov.bd) and its official social media <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.
- If cheated, tell the nearest police station or the government office concerned; the ministry's contacts for the card are Md. Jasim Uddin, deputy secretary, 01712-870968, dsservice@probashi.gov.bd, and Md. Mahbubul Islam, assistant private secretary to the minister, 01331-116600, aps2minister@probashi.gov.bd <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.

The airport benefits promised to card holders are under At the airport (see [The migrant lounge and helpers at Dhaka airport](#the-migrant-lounge-and-helpers-at-dhaka-airport)) <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>.

### 2.3 Ami Probashi: CV builder and online doctors

Ami Probashi, a private digital platform for would-be migrants, said in September 2024 that about 60 lakh of them had used its services and more than 2,200 licensed recruiting agencies were linked to it <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>. What happened to its role in BMET registration and clearance, and the government's own platform, OEP, that replaced it: see "How do I register on OEP?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc. Two services it announced:
- **CV builder (May 2025):** free on the Ami Probashi website and app; tell its chatbot, in Bangla, your skills, experience and education, and it makes a CV in 5 to 7 minutes that you download as a PDF, by the company's account <sup>[N29](#n29)</sup>.
- **Doctors online (September 2024):** under an agreement with Grameen Digital Healthcare Solutions, a telemedicine service of Grameen Telecom Trust, Ami Probashi users abroad can consult specialist doctors online; that service's Sukhi app books a specialist and connects by video call <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>.

### 2.4 Training centres

Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina said in May 2024 that her government had built 112 technical training centres (TTCs) across the country to train a skilled workforce <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>. In March 2024 the state minister said the TTCs would be overhauled and new training equipment bought, and an MP, Tanvir Shakil Joy, said many problems remain with pre-departure training <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. In July 2024 South Korea's development agency KOICA offered USD 1 crore (Tk 117 crore) to help produce skilled migrant workers, through modernising the Bangladesh-Korea Technical Training Centre in Chattogram <sup>[N31 (2024)](#n31)</sup>. How to join a TTC course and what the pre-departure orientation covers: see "How do I join a TTC course?" and "What is the pre-departure orientation (PDO)?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

> **Need to know**
> - Was KOICA's USD 1 crore for the Chattogram Bangladesh-Korea TTC signed, and what courses does the centre now run <sup>[N31 (2024)](#n31)</sup>?

At the 5 May 2026 roundtable Nurul Haque said the government had noticed 23 ministries giving similar training, wasting public money without results <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>. Answering an MP in parliament, as reported on 3 September 2026, he said the TTCs lack skilled staff to run them and that new staff cannot be recruited because of bureaucratic complications <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>.

Md. Mamun Hasan, an instructor at Satkhira Government Polytechnic Institute, wrote in October 2026 that by 2025 figures about 43% of Bangladeshis going abroad were low-skilled and 34% semi-skilled, and that 621 of the 3,440 vocational institutions approved by the Technical Education Board had not admitted a single student in five years <sup>[N33](#n33)</sup>.

Training in elderly caregiving is also given under the National Skills Development Authority (NSDA), alongside BMET's TTCs, nursing education and private training institutes, though Mohammad Jalal Uddin Sikder, a researcher at North South University, wrote in September 2026 that these efforts are scattered <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>.

<a id="language-courses-at-the-ttcs"></a>

#### 2.4.1 Language courses at the TTCs

BMET hires guest trainers to run language courses at the government TTCs, and its 2026 adverts show which languages they teach <sup>[N5](#n5), [N6](#n6)</sup>:
- **March 2026, 99 trainers:** Japanese 50, English 20, Arabic 8, Italian 8, German 7, Korean 6 <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **April 2026, 70 trainers:** Japanese 40, and 6 each for Portuguese, Spanish, Russian, French and Italian <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.

A trainer for Japanese, Korean, German or Italian needs at least HSC and a set language level such as JLPT N3 (Japanese-Language Proficiency Test) or TOPIK (Test of Proficiency in Korean) level 3, and is paid Tk 800 an hour for at most 4 hours a day <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. On 5 March 2026 Prime Minister Tarique Rahman directed that language training centres be opened, with Bangladeshi and foreign teachers, so that unemployed young people can learn other countries' languages and get jobs abroad, his additional press secretary said <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>. How to apply for a TTC course on OEP: see "How do I join a TTC course?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

How far this reaches: Bdjobs' chief executive, Fahim Masroor, said in December 2025 that 12 lakh people go abroad every year and 90 to 95% of them have only secondary education <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>. Ahmed Shameem, a coordinator at Cornell University's Language Resource Center, wrote in April 2026 that of about 13 lakh workers who went abroad in a recent year an estimated 2,500 learned Japanese, Korean, English or Chinese at government centres, that organised Arabic courses are lacking although the Middle East is the largest destination, and, citing Dhaka Tribune and The Business Standard, that Arabic-speaking workers can demand 20 to 30% higher wages in the Gulf <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.

#### 2.4.2 Online courses at BMET's Foreign Language and Skills Centre

BMET's Foreign Language and Skills Centre (FLSC), on the DTTTI (Dhaka Technical Teachers' Training Institute) campus, Technical More, Mirpur Road, Darus-Salam, Dhaka, runs language courses online in the evening, five days a week, Sunday to Thursday <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. Its August 2026 call <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>:
- **Japanese:** level A1-A2, age 18 to 35, Tk 3,000, 6 months.
- **German:** level A1-A2, age 18 to 35, Tk 3,000, 6 months.
- **English:** level A1-B1, age 18 to 40, Tk 1,000, 3 months.
- **Arabic:** beginner, age 18 to 40, Tk 1,000, 3 months.
- **Korean:** TOPIK-2 (level 3), age 18 to 35, Tk 1,000, 4 months.

Each course has 40 seats and needs at least an SSC pass; for Korean, the applicant must already have passed TOPIK-1 (level 2) or finished a 4-month TOPIK-1 course at a TTC <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. To apply <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>:
1. Register with BMET by paying Tk 200 at https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp.
2. From the profile that gives you, choose Training portal, then Training, Trade/Skill, Division, District, FLSC, select the course, Apply and Confirm.
3. Sit the viva on Zoom; the schedule comes by SMS or email.
4. If selected (by SMS), enrol online within the dates given, or the place goes to the waiting list.

In 2026 applications ran from 16 to 30 August and classes began on 14 September <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. Contacts: 01883-208284 (English), 01864-595182 (Korean), 01968-613447 (Arabic), 01815-472675 (German), 01727-498659 (Japanese); dirflsc.bmet@gmail.com <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

#### 2.4.3 Heavy-vehicle driver training

<a id="the-pkb-migration-loan"></a>

### 2.5 The PKB migration loan

PKB was opened on 20 April 2011; helping jobless people go abroad and helping returnees find work are two of its four aims <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. In July 2024 it had 122 branches, 65 at district level and 57 at upazila level, plus an arrivals booth and a departures booth at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport in Dhaka <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The basic terms, eligibility and the helpline are in "Can I get a government loan to go abroad?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="what-you-get"></a>

#### 2.5.1 What you get

- **Who can borrow:** a Bangladeshi citizen aged 18 to 60 who is not in default on any bank, financial institution or NGO loan; the borrower and both guarantors must be permanent residents of the branch's area <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Amount and term:** up to Tk 3 lakh, for at most 3 years including the grace period, repaid in equal monthly instalments after it <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Interest:** 9% simple a year, with penal interest under Bangladesh Bank rules on late instalments <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. PKB's managing director, Mujibur Rahman, said in July 2024 the bank had kept the rate at 9% even as bank interest rates rose <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Security:** no collateral; two guarantors instead <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Decision:** within 7 working days of an application with the right papers <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.

<a id="what-it-costs"></a>

#### 2.5.2 What it costs

The application form is free, but the borrower pays <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>:
- a processing fee of 0.50% of the loan plus VAT;
- a documentation fee of 0.50% plus VAT;
- Tk 900 per lakh for the loan risk cover (see [The loan risk cover](#the-loan-risk-cover)), so Tk 2,700 on a Tk 3 lakh loan;
- 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10, and three Tk 100 adhesive stamps for each guarantor.

<a id="what-to-bring"></a>

#### 2.5.3 What to bring

Open a savings account at the PKB branch with at least Tk 500 before taking the loan <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. Bring <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>:
- **Your own papers:** three recent passport-size photos; a photocopy of your NID (national identity card); your passport and visa copy; a photocopy of the BMET manpower card (the clearance smart card), not needed for a re-entry visa; the labour contract if you have it.
- **Your own cheques:** three signed MICR cheque leaves from an active bank account in your name, with its statement.
- **Guarantor 1:** a person able to repay, who gives three signed MICR cheque leaves from an active account in his or her own name or sole business, the account statement and proof of income or occupation.
- **Guarantor 2:** a member of your family or a close relative.
- **For each guarantor:** a passport-size photo, an NID photocopy and proof of permanent address (a utility bill or similar).

**Going back on a re-entry visa?** Also bring proof that you sent money home through legal channels, proof of your job abroad (contract, appointment letter, work permit, residence permit or pay slip) and an account of what going back costs (money receipt, contract, air ticket receipt or your own statement of costs) <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.

Because the visa and smart card are needed, you can apply only near the end of the process (see "When can I apply for the PKB loan?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc) <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.

<a id="applying-online-on-mygov"></a>

#### 2.5.4 Applying online on MyGov

Since 3 December 2025 the migration loan can be applied for online through MyGov, the government's digital service platform; after a pilot, the service runs at 122 of PKB's branches, and PKB asks borrowers to apply this way <sup>[N3](#n3), [8](#o8)</sup>. The amount is set after PKB checks what the worker's migration actually costs, up to the Tk 3 lakh limit, at 9% interest <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. How it works on MyGov <sup>[9](#o9)</sup>:
1. On mygov.bd, open "অভিবাসন ঋণ (প্রবাসী কল্যাণ ব্যাংক)" (migration loan, Probashi Kallyan Bank) and fill in the form.
2. Upload your passport-size photo, passport copy, visa copy, BMET card and NID copy, and each guarantor's NID copy and photo, as JPEG, JPG or PNG files of at most 1 MB each.
3. Click the button to submit the application to the office; a saved form that is not submitted does not reach the bank.
4. Pay nothing yet: the fees (see [What it costs](#what-it-costs)) are paid after the bank decides, and the decision comes to your MyGov dashboard or by SMS.
5. Follow progress with the application and tracking numbers MyGov sends by SMS.

You can have only one application open until it is approved or rejected <sup>[9](#o9)</sup>. As reported in November 2026 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>:
- **Uptake:** more than 10,000 workers had applied online and PKB had approved more than Tk 200 crore in about a year.
- **Speed:** approval was taking 3 to 4 days from application, according to a press release from the government's a2i programme.
- **Cost to the applicant:** a survey PKB published found that applicants' time, travel and application costs each fell by 50%.

<a id="how-the-loan-is-decided-and-repaid"></a>

#### 2.5.5 How the loan is decided and repaid

1. If every paper is in order, the bank gives its decision within 7 working days of the application; for online applications on MyGov, a2i said in November 2026 that approval takes 3 to 4 days <sup>[1](#o1), [N3](#n3)</sup>.
2. Once the application is processed, PKB tells you by SMS that the loan is approved <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
3. Repay the instalments at any PKB branch or at any Sonali Bank branch, under an agreement between the two banks <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
4. While abroad, tell the bank if your address, mobile number or email changes <sup>[8](#o8)</sup>.

For problems getting the loan, call PKB on 16238 from Bangladesh or +880 9677-787878 from abroad <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. If the branch does not resolve a complaint, write to PKB's General Manager (Administration and Accounts), +88-02-48321195, gm2@pkb.gov.bd, Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden Road, Dhaka-1000, who has 30 working days; then the Deputy Managing Director, dmd@pkb.gov.bd (20 working days); then the Managing Director, md@pkb.gov.bd (60 working days) <sup>[8](#o8)</sup>.

<a id="the-loan-risk-cover"></a>

#### 2.5.6 The loan risk cover

Every migration-loan borrower must pay Tk 900 per lakh of the loan to join PKB's loan risk cover scheme <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2), [1](#o1)</sup>. It pays out in two cases:
- **The borrower dies during the loan term:** 100% of what is still owed is written off <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. By July 2024 the bank had written off Tk 2 crore 74 lakh owed by 168 borrowers who died at home or abroad, according to PKB sources <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **The borrower comes back within six months of taking the loan:** half (50%) of what is still owed is written off <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="who-uses-it-and-how-it-is-going"></a>

#### 2.5.7 Who uses it, and how it is going

From 2011 to the 2023-24 financial year PKB lent Tk 3,311 crore 86 lakh to 1,70,194 migrants, by its own count <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Most applicants are going to Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Oman, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain, and applications for Italy, Romania, South Korea and Singapore were rising in 2024, according to PKB sources <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The managing director said in July 2024 that the share of loans in default, 12% two years earlier, had fallen to 7.4% after the bank set up 15 regional offices <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. PKB also runs savings schemes for migrants and their families, but few people use them because the interest is low <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

**One case: a loan after a viral video.** Sarowar Hossain (27), from a poor family in Hossainpur, Kishoreganj, had his visa but could not raise the money; he could not get a bank loan despite many attempts, and asked NGOs and others for help without success <sup>[N42](#n42)</sup>. On 16 October 2025 he hired a microphone for Tk 500, abused the people of his area for not standing by him, and posted the video on Facebook, where it spread <sup>[N42](#n42)</sup>. After media reports, PKB's Kishoreganj branch phoned him, checked his papers and lent him Tk 2 lakh, with repayment in instalments from two months later; he said he had a ticket to fly to Saudi Arabia on 30 October <sup>[N42](#n42)</sup>.

On 5 March 2026 the Prime Minister told the ministries concerned to arrange low-interest bank loans and make loans easier to get, so that no one has to sell their property to go abroad for work, his additional press secretary said <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>. The 2026-27 budget, presented on 11 June 2026 by the finance minister, Amir Khasru Mahmud Chowdhury, said PKB loans of up to Tk 10 lakh on easy terms for migrant workers would continue and the programme would be widened <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. In parliament in June 2026 an MP, Kamrul Hasan, asked whether the government would arrange interest-free bank loans for people going abroad; the state minister, Nurul Haque, described the government's plans, which the report does not detail <sup>[N43](#n43)</sup>.

In November 2026 the migration loan was still up to Tk 3 lakh <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>; the two loans of up to Tk 10 lakh in this doc are for language or skills training (see [The PKB loan for language study and skills abroad](#the-pkb-loan-for-language-study-and-skills-abroad); see [The skills and language loan funded by Bangladesh Bank](#the-skills-and-language-loan-funded-by-bangladesh-bank)) <sup>[N36](#n36), [N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="the-pkb-loan-for-language-study-and-skills-abroad"></a>

### 2.6 The PKB loan for language study and skills abroad

PKB lends Tk 1 lakh to Tk 10 lakh to students and trainees who want to take a language or skills course abroad as a route to work there, especially in Japan, Germany, South Korea, Italy and Russia; it can be taken at any PKB branch <sup>[2](#o2), [N36](#n36)</sup>. The terms below are those of PKB's loan policy, circular 22/2025 of 16 November 2025 <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>.

**Who can borrow:**
- a Bangladeshi citizen, permanent resident of the branch's area, under 40, not in default on any loan <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>;
- with an offer letter from the institution in the destination country <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>;
- with at least an intermediate-level language certificate accepted there, for example N4 for Japan or TOPIK level 3 for South Korea <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>;
- for a short course of at most two years <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>.

**Bring** <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>:
- three recent passport-size photos, an NID photocopy and one proof of present address;
- the offer letter, and copies of your passport and latest academic certificate;
- the language certificate;
- one guarantor able to repay, who can be your father, mother, brother, sister, spouse or a close relative: a photo, NID photocopy and proof of address, three signed MICR cheque leaves and a bank statement or account-opening certificate.

**Security, by amount** <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>:
- **Tk 1 to 3 lakh:** no collateral; the guarantor's personal guarantee and three cheque leaves from the guarantor's active bank account.
- **Above Tk 3 to 5 lakh:** the same, plus the original deed of immovable property worth at least twice the loan, deposited with the bank.
- **Above Tk 5 to 10 lakh:** a registered mortgage and an irrevocable power of attorney in PKB's favour on property worth at least twice the loan.

**Interest, fees and term:** 11% simple a year, which may change, plus 1.5% on any overdue amount; a 0.50% processing fee and a 0.50% documentation fee; at least 2 and at most 5 years including a 6-month grace period, repaid in equal monthly instalments; you must open a savings account <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>.

**How it is paid and repaid:** the bank decides within 7 working days for Tk 1 to 3 lakh, 10 for up to Tk 5 lakh and 15 for up to Tk 10 lakh; it checks the visa and language certificate, then pays the whole loan by an account-payee cheque in your name or into your PKB account; while you are abroad, your closest relative or the guarantor pays the instalments <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>.

**Apply** online or in person at the nearest branch only; PKB has warned of fraud in loan processing and says it has no agents <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>. Helpline 16238 <sup>[1](#o1), [N44](#n44)</sup>. The student loan of up to Tk 10 lakh as proof of solvency for higher study is in "Can I get a government loan to go abroad?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

<a id="the-skills-and-language-loan-funded-by-bangladesh-bank"></a>

### 2.7 The skills and language loan funded by Bangladesh Bank

Bangladesh Bank, the central bank, has launched a Tk 1,000 crore pre-finance scheme, lent through PKB, to pay for skills and language training for Bangladeshis going abroad to work, as reported in October 2026 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. Bangladesh Bank's additional director Mohammad Salahuddin Tafadar said the money goes to PKB in five instalments of Tk 200 crore, each released after the previous one is checked to have been properly used <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. By PKB's figures, Tk 13.03 crore had been lent to 481 people by 4 October 2026 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

**What it pays for:** tuition fees for language or job-skills training in the destination country after the worker arrives, and in certain cases living costs; not the air ticket <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

**Who can borrow:** anyone going abroad legally to work; students going abroad to study cannot <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. PKB's managing director, Wahida Begum, said people going through any legal channel, government or private, can borrow under the bank's migration loan policy <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

**Terms** <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>:
- **Amount:** up to Tk 10 lakh, with no collateral.
- **Guarantors:** two, one a closest family member and the other an established person in society able to repay the loan.
- **Interest:** at most 9%.
- **Term:** three years, including six months' grace before repayment, extendable to five years if needed.

**Bring:** a valid passport and visa, the "certificate of eligibility" required, the certificate of qualification for the language or skills training, and an offer letter from the training or educational institution stating the tuition fee, how many months the training lasts and who pays living costs <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

To apply <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>:
1. Apply on PKB's website; there is no need to go to a branch to apply, and the application's progress can be followed online.
2. The branch checks the information and papers and sends the application to PKB's head office.
3. If head office approves, it sends the approval letter to the branch.
4. Go to the branch in person to give consent to take the loan.

## 3 At the airport

### 3.1 The Probashi Kallyan desk

The Welfare Board runs a Probashi Kallyan desk at every international airport (Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet), under section 8(5) of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board Act 2018 <sup>[4](#o4), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. What it does:
- **On the way out:** checks each new worker's BMET clearance, so that no worker becomes a victim of human trafficking; helps workers get and fill in the boarding card; checks the re-entry visas of workers going back to Saudi Arabia <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [4](#o4)</sup>.
- **For workers on leave from the Middle East:** a worker going back must carry the leave permission, which is printed at the desk, and the desk's staff check and put the worker's papers in order, as officials described it in July 2024 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **On the way back:** sends a worker who arrives sick, or falls sick at the airport, to the nearest government hospital in the Board's own ambulance; gives food and drink to workers back from jail or on an outpass (a one-way travel permit); helps with customs and reports lost luggage to the airport's Lost and Found; takes complaints from workers who were abused or cheated abroad; can arrange a stay at the Wage Earners' Centre <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.
- **After a war, disaster or lay-off:** may give returning workers financial help <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.
- **Bodies:** receives the bodies of migrants who die abroad, hands them to the family and gives a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.

BMET lists the desks and their heads, all assistant directors <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>:
- **Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, Dhaka:** mobile 01711228121, welfaredesk@gmail.com.
- **Shah Amanat International Airport, Chattogram:** phone 02333300957, mobile 01913631797, pdkctg@gmail.com.
- **Osmani International Airport, Sylhet:** mobile 01677555393, pkdsylhet@gmail.com.

The desk's hours and other phone numbers are under "At the airport: the welfare desk" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.

**The third terminal.** At a meeting on 10 June 2024, the Civil Aviation Authority's chairman said that in Dhaka airport's new third terminal BMET clearance would not be checked as before but online, in an automated system, with BMET's database merged with the immigration department's, and the airport's executive director said all welfare work for migrants there would run through the terminal's information centre <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. On 24 June 2024 the desk wrote to BMET's director general asking for urgent action to secure it a place in the terminal <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. A Civil Aviation Authority member said the desk's services were planned within the terminal's own operations, which a Japanese company would run, and that space would have to be asked of that company <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The Welfare Board's director, Shoaib Ahmad Khan, said three to four thousand new workers leave every day and that he hoped the desk would get space; Tasneem Siddiqui, founding chair of the migration research group RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), called leaving the desk out a wrong decision <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

<a id="in-a-war-or-disaster"></a>

### 4.1 In a war or disaster

When war or disaster has hit a host country, help has come through the embassies and the ministry's hotline, and the 2026 evacuation from Iran was paid for by the Welfare Board <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18)</sup>.

**What the embassies told workers to do.** On 28 February 2026, as the Middle East war began, the Bangladesh embassies in Lebanon, Qatar, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait and Iraq told Bangladeshis to stay away from military sites, stay in a safe place, not spread rumours and keep papers and essentials at hand <sup>[N58](#n58)</sup>. They advised keeping these with you at all times <sup>[N58](#n58)</sup>:
- cash, passport and local ID card;
- health card or health insurance, and needed medicines;
- mobile charger and power bank;
- dry food and water.

Some embassies warned that posting photos or videos of the situation on social media, or spreading baseless news, breaks those countries' laws <sup>[N58](#n58)</sup>.

**The ministry's hotline and control room.** Any migrant worker could call the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's hotline, +8809610102030 (the Probash-Bondhu number from abroad), at any time about the war, and from 10 to 16 March 2026 a control room ran from 8 am to 8 pm daily at the Probashi call centre, Lift 7, Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, Eskaton, Dhaka <sup>[N17](#n17), [N59 (2024)](#n59)</sup>. The minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, said on 7 March 2026 that the ministry would help migrants whose visas were running out because flights were cancelled, as far as the government could, if they contacted it; on 8 March he said anyone injured would be treated and that bringing workers home depended on how the war went <sup>[N60](#n60), [N61](#n61)</sup>. He said on 3 April 2026 that six Bangladeshis had died in the war so far (what one dead worker's family got: see [If a worker dies abroad](#if-a-worker-dies-abroad)) <sup>[N62](#n62)</sup>.

**How evacuations from Iran worked.** Two evacuations, as reported (why many Bangladeshis in Iran have no papers: see "Waiting in Iran" in the Illegal routes doc):
- **June 2025, the Israel-Iran war:** the Foreign Ministry opened emergency hotlines, with WhatsApp, at the embassy in Tehran and at the ministry in Dhaka, and asked people to rely on them rather than unverified sources <sup>[N63](#n63)</sup>. The government decided to move the Tehran mission's own staff to safety because it was within a kilometre of sites under attack <sup>[N64](#n64)</sup>. About 100 of some 400 Bangladeshis in Tehran contacted the embassy to return <sup>[N65](#n65)</sup>. The Foreign Ministry asked everyone wishing to return to register with the embassy in Tehran with their name, address and other details <sup>[N66](#n66)</sup>. By 24 June, 250 had registered; women, children and people in Iran for medical treatment were to go first, and 90 were to cross by land from Mirjaveh in Iran to Taftan in Pakistan, then fly from Karachi through Dubai to Dhaka <sup>[N66](#n66)</sup>.
- **March 2026, the war of the United States and Israel with Iran:** the government prepared to bring about 300 people home in two phases, by road to Azerbaijan and then on a Biman charter flight from Baku <sup>[N67](#n67)</sup>. Azerbaijan gave on-arrival visas to 274, and the embassy in Tehran gave travel passes to those without a passport <sup>[N67](#n67)</sup>. The ministry told parliament's standing committee on 8 October 2026 that 186 people, including 12 women and 8 children, had been brought back from Iran with Welfare Board money <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

**Lebanon, October 2024.** On 21 October 2024 the first 54 Bangladeshis, 7 of them children, came home from war-hit Lebanon on a Saudi Airlines flight, with 65 more due two days later <sup>[N68 (2024)](#n68)</sup>. The expatriates' welfare adviser, Asif Nazrul, said about one lakh Bangladeshis were in Lebanon and 1,800 had asked to return, that the government would bear the cost and bring home everyone who wanted to come, and that IOM would bring 200 at its own cost <sup>[N68 (2024)](#n68)</sup>. IOM's acting head in Bangladesh, Fathima Nusrath Ghazzali, said IOM helps the government bring Bangladeshis home from many countries, that each of the 54 got food and Tk 5,000 from IOM along with mental-health support, and that IOM was also working to bring home Bangladeshis without legal papers <sup>[N68 (2024)](#n68)</sup>.

**An industrial disaster: Russia.** After an explosion at a gas complex in Amur, Russia, in August 2026, the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow said it would oversee the treatment of seven injured Bangladeshi workers being moved to a Moscow hospital, and opened a 24-hour hotline run by its head of chancery and its labour wing's first secretary <sup>[N69](#n69)</sup>.

### 4.2 Passports and NIDs from abroad

Bangladesh missions abroad issue e-passports: the embassy in France became the 48th mission to do so on 24 November 2024, and its ambassador said it would take few applications at first and more as staff grew, and that applicants should take care over the required papers <sup>[N70 (2024)](#n70)</sup>. The Election Commission has begun voter registration and NID for migrants: Bangladeshis in Ottawa could apply online or offline, as reported in September 2025, and the Election Commissioner, A K M Humayun Kabir, said a digital registration centre would be set up at Bangladesh's missions <sup>[N72](#n72)</sup>. ### 4.3 Sending parcels by post

The Department of Posts sends parcels of up to 20 kg abroad from district and upazila post offices, by international air parcel to 183 countries or by EMS (Express Mail Service) to 43, its officials said in December 2025; migrants abroad also use it to send goods to relatives at home, because it costs up to several times less than many foreign couriers <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>. Prices and times, as reported in December 2025:
- **Air parcel:** usually 15 to 20 days; no separate duty or tax <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
- **EMS:** 7 to 8 days at a little more; a fixed charge for the first 100 grams and for each further 100 grams, plus 15% duty and tax <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
- **A 1 kg parcel, Dhaka to Montreal:** Tk 2,400 by EMS plus Tk 180 for packing and data entry, Tk 2,580 in all, against Tk 7,630 at a well-known international courier, which took 3 to 4 working days <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
- **Air parcel, first 500 grams (2022 rates):** Canada Tk 2,050, United States Tk 1,850, Britain Tk 1,670, Italy Tk 1,610, Saudi Arabia Tk 1,250, Japan Tk 1,220, Qatar Tk 1,050, the UAE Tk 1,030 <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
- **Each further 500 grams by air parcel:** for example the UAE Tk 170, Italy Tk 270, the United States Tk 380 <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.

To send from the Dhaka GPO (General Post Office), as Prothom Alo found it in December 2025:
1. Enter by the GPO's back gate and walk left to the shed where parcels are taken in <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
2. Letter writers, registered with the Department of Posts, check and pack the goods and enter the sender's and recipient's details online, for a set fee <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
3. Pay at the designated office to book the parcel, and take the tracking number <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.
4. Follow the parcel on your phone with the tracking number; if it does not arrive on time, an inquiry can find out why <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.

Every outgoing parcel is scanned and opened if anything looks suspicious, and every incoming parcel is opened and checked, under CCTV, the Deputy Postmaster General, Shahnaz Siddiqui, said <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>.

### 4.4 Finishing school or a degree from abroad

Bangladesh Open University (BOU) runs overseas programmes (called NISH-2) that Bangladeshi migrants join through the Bangladesh embassy in their country <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11), [N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. Its International Academic Program Wing, set up in 2019, began them in South Korea and now offers them in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, the UAE and Italy <sup>[15](#o15)</sup>. As of the 2024 batch:

<a id="ssc"></a>

#### 4.4.1 SSC

- **Who:** Bangladeshi migrants or people of Bangladeshi origin in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Italy who have passed JSC, JDC, class 8 or an equivalent <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- **Streams:** humanities and business studies <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 140 for the first year and USD 150 for the second, each paid at that year's admission; registration lasts at least two and at most five years <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- **Papers:** one photo; copies of the class 8 (or equivalent) certificate and mark sheet, the passport, and the NID if you have one <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- **How:** apply online and pay the fee, then complete admission at the embassy; in 2024 the deadline was 20 February <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.

<a id="hsc"></a>

#### 4.4.2 HSC

- **Who:** Bangladeshi migrants or people of Bangladeshi origin in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE, South Korea and Italy who have passed SSC, Dakhil, O-level or an equivalent <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 180 for the first year and USD 165 for the second <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

Contacts for both: the embassy, the Dean of BOU's Open School (dean.os@bou.ac.bd) and BOU's International Academic Program Wing (iapw@bou.ac.bd) <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.

<a id="ba-and-bss"></a>

#### 4.4.3 BA and BSS

- **Who:** Bangladeshis in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Italy, South Korea and the UAE who have passed HSC, Alim, A-level or an equivalent <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
- **Length:** three years in six semesters of six months; registration stays valid for up to five academic years <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 250 for the first year, USD 190 for the second and USD 220 for the third, each paid at that year's admission <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
- **Classes:** eight online tutorials per course and exams on the host country's weekly day off, so you need a device with internet; mainly e-books, with printed books free on request from the International Academic Program Wing <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.

To apply <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>:
1. Scan a recent photo (300x300 pixels), your signature (300x100 pixels), SSC and HSC certificates and mark sheets, NID, smart card or birth certificate, passport and residence permit card.
2. Pay the USD 250 first-year fee as the embassy's website or Facebook page instructs, and scan the proof.
3. Fill in the online form.
4. Print the form and hand it in at the embassy with the papers and proof of payment.

In 2024 admission closed on 30 March and classes began in April <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. Contacts: the embassy, the Dean of BOU's SSHL school (bousshl2019@bou.ac.bd) and iapw@bou.ac.bd; website iapw.bou.ac.bd <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.

<a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad"></a>

## 5 If a worker dies abroad

What the family gets, and how the body comes home, are also in "If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?" and "How is the body brought home?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc. The Welfare Board's steps:
1. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad-item-1"></a>**To bring the body home:** a family member applies to the Welfare Board (form sample on www.wewb.gov.bd, or call 16135); the Board sends the family's wishes to the embassy and follows up until the body arrives; the service is free <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>. For a worker who was irregular, undocumented, destitute or helpless, the ministry pays under its 2022 policy on the cost of transporting such workers' bodies: a close relative applies to the ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) with what happened, the cause of death, proof that the dead person was a migrant worker (passport, work permit, smart card or foreign ID) and the death certificate; the ministry answers within 7 working days <sup>[17](#o17)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad-item-2"></a>**At the airport:** the Probashi Kallyan desk hands the body to the family with a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, for every dead worker <sup>[18](#o18), [4](#o4)</sup>. Bring an heirship (warish) certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor, and the nominated family member's photo and NID or passport photocopy; anyone else collecting the cheque brings their own photo and NID or passport copy <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>. If the family does not collect the cheque within three months of the body's arrival, it is not paid <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad-item-3"></a>**The Tk 3 lakh grant:** paid if the worker went abroad with BMET emigration clearance or Board membership and died in the country the clearance was for (any Schengen country, for a worker cleared for one; Saudi Arabia, for one who died on Hajj or Umrah) <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. A family that took the airport cheque need not apply: the Board writes asking for the papers <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. If the worker was buried abroad, apply within one year of the death to the Board's director general or through the district Probashi Kallyan centre <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>.
4. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad-item-4"></a>**Papers for the grant:** a family-member certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or councillor, countersigned by the UNO (upazila nirbahi officer); a "release, undertaking and authorisation" on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper, countersigned the same way (samples on www.wewb.gov.bd); the embassy's NOC or the death certificate; the receiving bank's certificate or original statement with routing number (a minor child's own account, where there is one); each family member's colour photo, attested by the chairman and by the district Probashi Kallyan centre; and the dead worker's passport photocopy <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. The money goes by electronic transfer to the bank account, with an SMS <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>.
5. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad-item-5"></a>**Compensation and dues from the employer:** where the embassy reports that compensation can be claimed, the Board asks the family for a power of attorney and papers and sends them to the embassy, which runs the case abroad; the Board's legal cell helps prepare the power of attorney <sup>[20](#o20)</sup>.

Speakers at a May 2025 dialogue said handing bodies over at Dhaka airport had been made easier and faster than before <sup>[N74](#n74)</sup>. If the worker had a PKB migration loan, the loan risk cover writes off everything still owed (see [The loan risk cover](#the-loan-risk-cover)) <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

**What one family got.** The family of S M Tarek, killed in Bahrain in the Middle East war, was given Tk 35,000 for burial and Tk 50,000 at once by the Welfare Board, and was to get Tk 3 lakh in financial help and Tk 10 lakh of insurance, the minister said on 3 April 2026 <sup>[N62](#n62)</sup>.

A Bangladeshi migrant in Doha wrote in June 2024 that sending a body home costs a large sum many families can't pay, that in many Middle Eastern countries other migrants have to collect money to send a body home, and that a family left with the dead worker's migration debt gets no help from the state with it <sup>[N75 (2024)](#n75)</sup>. He wrote that at the embassy too, without going through a broker, a one-day task can take three months <sup>[N75 (2024)](#n75)</sup>. In November 2024 Boishommobirodhi Probashi Andolon asked that the bodies of poor migrants be brought home free at the government's initiative <sup>[N55 (2024)](#n55)</sup>. The 2026-27 budget of 11 June 2026 announced a move to make it easier to bring home the bodies of workers who die abroad <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, promised on 30 August 2026 that when a Bangladeshi dies abroad the body would be brought home and the family given financial help and help with the children's education <sup>[N76](#n76)</sup>.

**One woman worker's body.** Dipali Begum, from Charbhadrasan, Faridpur, in Lebanon since 2011, died after an Israeli air strike in Beirut on 8 April 2026; her body came home about a month later, after the embassy had obtained a certified police report and the permit to send the body <sup>[N77](#n77)</sup>. The Faridpur Probashi Kallyan Centre's assistant director, Md. Ashik Siddiqui, said the body would go to her village by free government ambulance, and the family would get Tk 35,000 for burial at the airport and later Tk 3 lakh in financial help <sup>[N77](#n77)</sup>.

**When the family doubts the cause of death.** Another migrant in Doha, Minhaj bin Mahbub, wrote in September 2025 that many families of migrants who took their own lives abroad believe their relative was murdered, pointing to wounds on the body, that most such bodies are of women domestic workers, and that the body should be examined again by post-mortem when it reaches Bangladesh <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>. A paper presented at a dialogue held by RMMRU and the National Human Rights Commission in May 2025 said the government does no post-mortem to check the death certificates sent with bodies, though many bodies sent home as natural deaths had injury marks, and that 48% of dead workers' families do not believe the cause on the certificate; it recommended cold storage at the airport to keep bodies for post-mortem <sup>[N74](#n74)</sup>. The ministry's senior secretary, Neyamat Ullah Bhuiyan, said there that a post-mortem after the body arrives is possible, but that the reaction of the country the body came from must be considered <sup>[N74](#n74)</sup>. A former National Human Rights Commission member, Selim Reza, said there are allegations in Middle Eastern countries of kidneys being taken from dying patients and kept to sell, and that embassies must make sure no worker's organs are sold <sup>[N74](#n74)</sup>.

### 6.1 Scholarships for migrants' children

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to high-achieving children of migrants; the amounts are in "Is there help for my children?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. The 2026 round, announced in the Board's notice of 8 October 2026, takes online applications from 25 October to 24 November 2026 <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. Its rules:

Earlier rounds also named children of a worker who died and whose family got or will get the Board's grant <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.
- **Which students:** those who passed SSC in 2026 and are in class 11 or the first semester or year of a Diploma in Engineering; those who passed HSC in 2025 and are in the first semester or year at a university or medical college; and those who passed the Diploma in Engineering in 2025 and are in the first semester or year of a BSc in Engineering <sup>[N79](#n79)</sup>.
- **Results needed, child of a worker abroad:** SSC GPA 5.00 in science or 4.75 in humanities and business studies; HSC GPA 4.80 in science or 4.50 in humanities and business studies; Diploma in Engineering GPA 3.25, down from 3.50 in the 2025 round <sup>[21](#o21), [N80](#n80)</sup>.
- **Results needed, child of a worker who died abroad:** GPA 4.00 in any group at SSC or HSC; GPA 3.00 for the diploma <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>.
- **How long it is paid:** from SSC, two years for classes 11 and 12 or four years for a diploma; from HSC, three years for a pass degree, four for honours and five for MBBS, BDS, DVM, B.Arch and similar courses, whatever the session delays; from the Diploma in Engineering, four years <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8), [N79](#n79), [N80](#n80)</sup>.

Papers to scan, as listed for the 2026 round <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>:
- a copy of the migrant worker's passport, and the BMET emigration clearance or proof of Welfare Board membership;
- a certificate from the student's institution, carrying the institution head's mobile number;
- for a child of a worker who died abroad, an NOC (no-objection certificate) from the Bangladesh mission abroad;
- a statement of the student's own account at a scheduled bank, with the account and routing numbers; an agent-banking account is not accepted;
- the student's photo and signature;
- a copy of the student's birth registration or NID.

To apply:
1. Go to https://portal.wewb.gov.bd; applications are online only, not in person or by post <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>.
2. Give the student's mobile number and a parent's or guardian's mobile number <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.
3. Upload the scanned papers <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>.
4. Check the parents' names on the student's certificate match the name in the migrant's passport: a mismatch, or an incomplete or wrong application, is rejected <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>.

Past rounds: the 2025 round closed on 25 November 2025, and its final lists of winners were published on the Welfare Board's website on 16 March 2026; the 2024 round ran from 25 November to 24 December 2024; the round before closed on 31 January 2024, and its winners' lists were published in April 2024 <sup>[N80](#n80), [22](#o22), [N8 (2024)](#n8), [N81 (2024)](#n81), [N59 (2024)](#n59)</sup>. ### 6.3 If a husband is violent at home

A Supreme Court lawyer, Barrister Miti Sanjana, answering the child of a returnee who beats the mother, wrote in Prothom Alo in April 2024 that under the Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2010 the woman can seek protection <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>:
1. Apply to the officer in charge of any police station, to a women affairs officer, or directly to the court; an enforcement officer, a service provider or anyone else can also apply on her behalf <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.
2. The court sets a hearing date within seven days of receiving the application <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.

What the court can order, as she listed it:
- **Interim protection order:** made without hearing the husband first, if the court is satisfied violence has happened or may happen (section 13) <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.
- **Residence order:** bars the husband from living in or visiting the place where she lives, with safe shelter arranged for her and the children if the home is unsafe (section 15) <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.
- **Compensation and maintenance:** money for her and the children's upkeep, as a lump sum or monthly (section 16) <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.
- **Free legal help:** advice and help at no cost under the Legal Aid Act 2000, where it applies <sup>[N82 (2024)](#n82)</sup>.

## 7 Coming home

<a id="pkb-loans-to-restart"></a>

### 7.1 PKB loans to restart

In July 2024 PKB's six loans were the migration loan, the rehabilitation loan, the special rehabilitation loan, the Bangabandhu migrant large-family loan, the self-employment loan, and the women's migration and rehabilitation loan <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. What returnees say about the help that reaches them: see "What help is there when I come back?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc and "Help after you get home" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.

**The rehabilitation loan**, for a returnee starting any lawful business <sup>[25](#o25)</sup>:
- **Amount and security:** up to Tk 50 lakh; no collateral up to Tk 3 lakh, collateral above Tk 3 to 5 lakh, and a registered mortgage on immovable property above Tk 5 lakh.
- **Terms:** 9% simple interest, up to 10 years; fees of 0.50% for processing and 0.50% for documentation, each at most Tk 15,000, and no service charge.
- **Where and how fast:** apply at the PKB branch in the area of the business, on the bank's free form; the bank decides within 15 working days of a complete application.

Bring three recent photos, the passport, an NID photocopy and a municipality or union parishad certificate of present and permanent address; an up-to-date trade licence (compulsory); details of the business with two years' income and expenditure; the lease or ownership papers of the business premises; a declaration of your own investment and of any other loans; copies of all papers on your return from abroad; and three signed MICR cheques from the business's account <sup>[25](#o25)</sup>. The guarantor can be anyone able to repay, such as a parent, sibling, spouse, close relative or a solvent person, gives two photos, an NID or passport copy, an address certificate and three cheques, and must have a clean CIB (credit bureau) report <sup>[25](#o25)</sup>.

**For women returnees.** PKB's women's rehabilitation loan, for a woman who came home after harassment abroad and starts a business, carries 7% simple interest and no service charge, up to Tk 50 lakh, collateral-free up to Tk 3 lakh; it still needs a guarantor (a parent, spouse, sibling, close relative or a solvent, respected person) <sup>[26 (2024)](#o26)</sup>. PKB's managing director said in July 2024 that it lends to returnee women for self-employment at 4 to 7% interest <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

**Treatment for a returned sick or injured worker.** The Welfare Board gives up to Tk 2 lakh to treat a worker who came home injured, sick, disabled or dying, if he or she left with BMET clearance or Board membership <sup>[27](#o27)</sup>. Apply to the Board's director general within one year of return, with the medical reports and prescriptions, a bank statement, a copy of the passport's arrival stamp and the passport copy; a committee sees the worker on a WhatsApp video call before the director general approves <sup>[27](#o27)</sup>.

**Legal help at home.** A migrant or the family who needs legal help in Bangladesh applies to the Probashi Kallyan branch of the deputy commissioner's office, to the DEMO (district employment and manpower office) or directly to the Welfare Board; the Board writes to the local administration or the embassy to act and tells the applicant <sup>[28](#o28)</sup>. In November 2024 Boishommobirodhi Probashi Andolon asked for jobs for returnees, enough interest-free PKB loans for those who want to start a business, compensation for workers cheated by brokers, and a pension for Middle East workers who retire and come home <sup>[N55 (2024)](#n55)</sup>.

<a id="government-and-brac-reintegration-projects"></a>

### 7.2 Government and BRAC reintegration projects

RAISE (Recovery and Advancement of Informal Sector Employment), the government's returnee project, and BRAC's Prottasha project both work on rehabilitating returned workers <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. What returnees say about the help: see "Help after you get home" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.

**RAISE.** The Welfare Board lists it as its project for July 2021 to June 2026, costing Tk 427.30 crore, of which Tk 425 crore is a World Bank loan; the project director is Dr A.T.M. Mahbub-ul Karim, 02-55138261, pdraise6841@gmail.com <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>. By the World Bank's April 2025 count, over 1,40,000 returnees had registered at 31 new district welfare centres and received counselling, cash transfers and referrals to job training <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. RMMRU, which ran four of the welfare centres covering eight districts, reported in January 2026 that Tk 39 crore 84 lakh 93 thousand had been paid to 29,518 returnees as cash incentives, Tk 13,500 each on average, and that many returnees were left on the waiting list because of the project's limited scope and time <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. On 14 May 2026 the state minister, Nurul Haque, called the government's help to migrants perfunctory and said the Tk 13,500 given for rehabilitation is far too little and must be raised <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. On 13 October 2026 he said help, training and rehabilitation for workers who come home harmed within six months of going abroad is given through a project run with World Bank and donor support, which the report calls "RACE" <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has RAISE been extended or followed by a new project after June 2026, and where does a returnee register now <sup>[6](#o6), [N20](#n20)</sup>?

**Prottasha.** Prottasha II is a four-year project funded by the European Union and co-financed by BRAC; IOM worked with BRAC in its first phase and gives policy support in the second <sup>[N83](#n83), [N21](#n21)</sup>. BRAC describes help at the airport for vulnerable returnees, psychosocial counselling, skills training, material help to start a livelihood and referrals; by 30 September 2025 it had given airport help to 4,242 returnees, counselling to 1,159, skills training to 3,707 and material help to 3,091 <sup>[N83](#n83), [N84](#n84)</sup>. A Bangladeshi returned from the United Kingdom under the UK Home Office's returns programme gets help through BRAC if he or she contacts BRAC within 3 months of return: BRAC Centre, 75 Mohakhali, Dhaka-1212, Sunday to Thursday 9 am to 5 pm, +8801964964292 or +8801329742981; the help lasts up to 12 months <sup>[29](#o29)</sup>. Two returnees told a Prottasha event in May 2026 what it gave them:
- **Selina Begum, Tangail, back empty-handed from Cyprus:** a scooter bought with BRAC's help, which she uses to supply tea across her thana <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>.
- **Sohel Rana, Tangail, back from Italy unable even to walk:** a grocery shop set up with Prottasha's help <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>.

A concept paper presented in August 2026 by BRAC's associate director (migration), Shariful Islam Hasan, put returnees' main problems as having no savings, no chance to use skills gained abroad and being unable to start a new livelihood at home <sup>[N85](#n85)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a returnee join Prottasha: in which districts does it work, where does he or she register, who qualifies, and what papers are needed <sup>[N83](#n83)</sup>?

<a id="farming-after-return-two-cases"></a>

### 7.3 Farming after return: two cases

Shah Alam (45), of Niamatpur village in Sadar upazila, Sirajganj, came home after about 12 years in Saudi Arabia and in 2022 began farming chui jhal, a spice vine, with the help of NDP (National Development Programme), a local NGO running a high-value spice demonstration for PKSF (Palli Karma-Sahayak Foundation) <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>. He leased 33 decimals next to his house for three years for Tk 60,000 on 30 August 2022 <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>. By his own account, he had spent Tk 1 lakh 17 thousand since starting, and in 2025 sold Tk 7 lakh 37 thousand of chui jhal in two batches <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>.

Lokman Hossain, of Charwadel village in Bauphal upazila, Patuakhali, went abroad in 2018 after graduating, came home in early 2022 and began farming with his father <sup>[N87](#n87)</sup>. In the 2025 monsoon he grew watermelon out of season for the third time, on 5 decimals beside his house, and he says that the time before he sold about Tk 1 lakh of watermelon after spending Tk 20,000 <sup>[N87](#n87)</sup>. Bauphal's upazila agriculture officer, Md. Milon, said off-season watermelon is very profitable and that the agriculture department would give Lokman every kind of help <sup>[N87](#n87)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Do PKSF's partner NGOs give returnees priority for farm demonstrations or loans, and how does a returnee find the partner NGO in his upazila <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>?
> - What help (seed, training, advice) does an upazila agriculture office give a returnee starting a farm, and does it cost anything <sup>[N87](#n87)</sup>?

### 7.6 Bank loans to start a business

A deputy managing director of Dhaka Bank PLC wrote in August 2026 of these refinance schemes and lenders for small businesses <sup>[N88](#n88)</sup>:
- **Bangladesh Bank's New Entrepreneurs Refinance Scheme:** collateral-free working-capital and term loans of up to Tk 30 lakh for cottage, micro and small enterprises, at no more than 7% interest.
- **Women Entrepreneur Refinance Fund:** for women who cannot give collateral, up to Tk 25 lakh on a personal guarantee, and up to Tk 1 crore for growing small entrepreneurs, at 5% interest.
- **Karmasangsthan Bank (the state employment bank):** small loans of up to Tk 75 lakh.

He wrote that many would-be entrepreneurs miss these schemes for lack of information, complex applications and poor coordination <sup>[N88](#n88)</sup>.

### 7.7 Investing at home: BIDA's desk for non-resident Bangladeshis

The Bangladesh Investment Development Authority (BIDA) set up a separate desk for non-resident Bangladeshi (NRB) investors after it was restructured, with a designated officer giving them end-to-end support, its executive chairman, Chowdhury Ashik Mahmud Bin Harun, said in April 2026 <sup>[N89](#n89)</sup>. An investor can submit a proposal directly to BIDA or through a Bangladesh consulate <sup>[N89](#n89)</sup>.

<a id="how-the-services-are-paid-for"></a>

## 8 How the services are paid for

The Welfare Board's help is not government money but migrants' own: the fees workers pay before going abroad go into the Board's fund, as was said at a Board event on 16 April 2025 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. At that event, by the Board's data, 103 people got Tk 1 crore 93 lakh 13 thousand <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>:
- **Death grants:** Tk 36 lakh to 12 families <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Scholarships:** 65 students <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Insurance claims:** Tk 1 crore 4 lakh to 11 people <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Compensation, arrears and service benefits from abroad:** Tk 27 lakh 67 thousand to two families <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Treatment help:** Tk 3 lakh 30 thousand to three people <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Disability allowance:** Tk 1 lakh 20 thousand to 10 children <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

It was also said there that during the interim government's term the Board had given Tk 273 crore 81 lakh in various help to 12,158 people <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

The Board's own accounts for 2023-24, the latest annual report, show where the money comes from and goes <sup>[30 (2024)](#o30)</sup>:
- **In:** Tk 444 crore in welfare fees paid by departing workers, Tk 121 crore for workers' insurance, Tk 129 crore from 42 missions abroad and Tk 90 crore in interest on fixed deposits.
- **Out, to migrants and families:** Tk 128 crore in grants to families of workers who died, Tk 121 crore on departing workers' insurance, Tk 18 crore on body transport and burial, Tk 13.6 crore on scholarships, Tk 5.7 crore on treatment for sick and injured workers and Tk 1 crore for disabled children. In June 2025 Nahid Islam, convener of the National Citizen Party (NCP), condemned the halving of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's budget in the budget the interim government announced <sup>[N90](#n90)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Welfare Board published an annual report or accounts for 2024-25 or 2025-26, and what did it collect in welfare fees and spend on migrants and families <sup>[30 (2024)](#o30)</sup>?

<a id="women-workers"></a>

## 9 Women workers

<a id="returnee-women"></a>

### 9.1 Returnee women

The Centre for Women and Children Studies (CWCS), a Dhaka research and advocacy group, said in May 2024 that returnee women face many risks, that most people look down on them, and that in some cases husbands or families refuse to take them back <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>. CWCS ran a project on empowering returnee women in Keraniganj and Nawabganj, two upazilas of Dhaka district, from 2020 to 2023, arranging work for them through training <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>. In 2022 it formed Nari Obhibashi Kontho (Women Migrants' Voice), an alliance of organisations working with women migrants, with 25 members by May 2024 <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>.

In May 2024 CWCS asked the government for services that did not then exist <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>:
- loans of Tk 1 to 3 lakh to returnee women on migration papers alone, without a guarantor;
- free legal help, through the government's legal aid service, for women who suffered sexual exploitation, abuse, forced labour or trafficking;
- migrant service centres at people's doorsteps, and a separate database of returnee women.

PKB's loans for returnee women, which need a guarantor: see PKB loans to restart <sup>[26 (2024)](#o26)</sup>. The airport payment to women back after abuse and PKB's eased conditions for returnee women: see "What help is there when I come back?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

Selim Reza, coordinator of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies, wrote in August 2026 that returnee women's skills are not assessed, their chances of starting a business are limited, rehabilitation is weak and mental health care is almost absent <sup>[N91](#n91)</sup>. Fewer women are going: 1,05,466 in 2022, 76,108 in 2023, 61,158 in 2024 and 61,977 in 2025, about 41% below 2022, by his count <sup>[N91](#n91)</sup>.

At a roundtable on 5 May 2026, the ILO's senior national programme manager, Rahnuma Salam Khan, spoke of a plan to introduce a special cash scheme for returnee women with ILO support, and a UNDP (United Nations Development Programme) Bangladesh gender expert, Sharmin Islam, called on the government to ensure psycho-social counselling for returnee women workers <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has the ILO-backed cash scheme for returnee women started, and how does a woman apply <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>?
