## 1 In one minute

Most of the state's help for migrants comes from two bodies funded by migrants themselves. The Wage Earners' Welfare Board runs on a fund formed from migrants' own money, and it provided 95% of the capital of Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrants <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. PKB lends up to Tk 3 lakh to go abroad at 9% simple interest, with no property mortgaged, and if the borrower dies the rest of the loan is written off <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Since late 2025 it also lends up to Tk 10 lakh at 11% for a language or skills course abroad <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The TTCs (technical training centres) of BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) teach languages from Japanese to Arabic and Portuguese, though a Cornell University language-centre coordinator estimated that only about 2,500 of some 13 lakh workers who went abroad in a year had learned a language at a government centre <sup>[N4](#n4), [N5](#n5), [N6](#n6)</sup>. The Welfare Board's desk at the airport checks clearances on the way out, receives the sick and the dead on the way back, and pays the burial grant <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. At home, the Board pays scholarships to migrants' children and a monthly allowance for a disabled child, for workers who left with BMET clearance or joined the Board, and for workers who died and whose family got its grant <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. On one day in April 2025 the Board handed 103 people Tk 1 crore 93 lakh in grants, scholarships, insurance and other help, and said it had given Tk 273 crore 81 lakh to 12,158 people during the interim government's term <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. Abroad, a worker in some Gulf countries, South Korea or Italy can finish SSC, HSC or a degree through Bangladesh Open University via the embassy <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10), [N11 (2024)](#n11), [N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. In March 2024 the state minister promised welfare cells to take services to the grassroots, and an NGO project, Helvetas's SIMS, worked in 23 upazilas of three districts <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. A women's advocacy group, CWCS, says some returnee women are not taken back by their husbands or families, and in 2024 it asked for guarantor-free loans and free legal help for them <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

## 2 Before you go

<a id="advice-near-home-before-you-decide"></a>

### 2.1 Advice near home before you decide

Helvetas Swiss Intercooperation's SIMS project ran in 23 upazilas (sub-districts) of Cumilla, Narsingdi and Chattogram; by Helvetas's count it made about 10 lakh people aware of safe migration and trained about one lakh potential migrants in how to make a migration decision <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. It also trained about 3,500 families in managing money and linked them to markets to start small businesses <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. Its first phase closed in March 2024 <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. The state minister for expatriates' welfare, Shafiqur Rahman Chowdhury, said in March 2024 that Probashi Kallyan (migrant welfare) cells would be started to take services to the grassroots and that PKB's branches would be expanded <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.

At a roundtable on 5 May 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare, Nurul Haque, spoke of plans for a one-stop service at district level, bringing the district manpower office, PKB and the welfare centre into one building, along with a "Probashi Card" for migrants, a standard migration cost and a grading system to identify good recruiting agencies <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>. At the same roundtable Farzana Shahnaz, a national programme officer working on reintegration and labour mobility, said entrepreneurs at Union Digital Centres (UDCs, the government's union-level service centres) should be trained to bring correct information and counselling to the grassroots <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

<a id="language-courses-at-the-ttcs"></a>

#### 2.2.1 Language courses at the TTCs

BMET hires guest trainers to run language courses at the government TTCs, and its 2026 adverts show which languages they teach <sup>[N4](#n4), [N5](#n5)</sup>:
- **March 2026, 99 trainers:** Japanese 50, English 20, Arabic 8, Italian 8, German 7, Korean 6 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **April 2026, 70 trainers:** Japanese 40, and 6 each for Portuguese, Spanish, Russian, French and Italian <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

A trainer for Japanese, Korean, German or Italian needs at least HSC and a set language level such as JLPT N3 or TOPIK level 3, and is paid Tk 800 an hour for at most 4 hours a day <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. On 5 March 2026 Prime Minister Tarique Rahman directed that language training centres be opened, with Bangladeshi and foreign teachers, so that unemployed young people can learn other countries' languages and get jobs abroad, his additional press secretary said <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. How to apply for a TTC course on OEP: see "How do I join a TTC course?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

How far this reaches: Bdjobs' chief executive, Fahim Masroor, said in December 2025 that 12 lakh people go abroad every year and 90 to 95% of them have only secondary education <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Ahmed Shameem, a coordinator at Cornell University's Language Resource Center, wrote in April 2026 that of about 13 lakh workers who went abroad in a recent year an estimated 2,500 learned Japanese, Korean, English or Chinese at government centres, that organised Arabic courses are lacking although the Middle East is the largest destination, and, citing Dhaka Tribune and The Business Standard, that Arabic-speaking workers can demand 20 to 30% higher wages in the Gulf <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.

#### 2.2.2 Heavy-vehicle driver training

On 13 January 2026 the government purchase committee approved Tk 231 crore to train 60,000 drivers through the state Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation (BRTC), with BMET assisting, to create jobs and increase remittance <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. The plan, as reported <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>:
- **New drivers:** 40,000, trained for 80 days over 4 months.
- **Current drivers:** 20,000, given 5 days of skills training.
- **Eyes:** 40,000 get an eye test and 20,000 are given glasses.
- **Papers:** training, certificate and licence given under set rules and standards.

The finance adviser, Salehuddin Ahmed, said the drivers would be trained to drive heavy machinery such as cranes and excavators for jobs abroad, and that current drivers have no demand abroad <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>.

<a id="the-pkb-migration-loan"></a>

### 2.3 The PKB migration loan

PKB was opened on 20 April 2011; helping jobless people go abroad and helping returnees find work are two of its four aims <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. In July 2024 it had 122 branches, 65 at district level and 57 at upazila level, plus an arrivals booth and a departures booth at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport in Dhaka <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The basic terms, eligibility and the helpline are in "Can I get a government loan to go abroad?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="what-you-get"></a>

#### 2.3.1 What you get

- **Amount and term:** up to Tk 3 lakh, set by the visa's duration; three years for a new visa, two years for a re-entry visa <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Interest:** 9% simple, with a two-month grace period before repayments <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. PKB's managing director, Mujibur Rahman, said in July 2024 the bank had kept the rate at 9% even as bank interest rates rose <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Security:** no land or other property is mortgaged <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="what-to-bring"></a>

#### 2.3.2 What to bring

Applying costs nothing; you fill in the bank's own form, and you must first open an account at PKB <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Bring:
- **Your job papers:** a valid visa and the appointment letter from the company that will employ you <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Your own papers:** four attested passport-size photos; a certificate of present and permanent address from the municipality or union parishad; copies of the passport and visa; a photocopy of the manpower smart card (the BMET clearance card); a photocopy of your NID (national identity card) <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Two guarantors' papers:** for each, a passport-size photo, NID and a municipality or union parishad address certificate; and three bank cheque leaves signed by one of them <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

Because the visa and smart card are needed, you can apply only near the end of the process (see "When can I apply for the PKB loan?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc) <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="how-the-loan-is-decided-and-repaid"></a>

#### 2.3.3 How the loan is decided and repaid

1. If every paper is in order, the bank gives its decision within seven days of the application <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
2. Once the application is processed, PKB tells you by SMS that the loan is approved <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
3. Repay monthly at any PKB branch or at any Sonali Bank branch, under an agreement between the two banks <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="the-loan-risk-cover"></a>

#### 2.3.4 The loan risk cover

Every migration-loan borrower must pay a premium to join PKB's loan risk cover scheme <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. It pays out in two cases:
- **The borrower dies during the loan term:** 100% of what is still owed is written off <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. By July 2024 the bank had written off Tk 2 crore 74 lakh owed by 168 borrowers who died at home or abroad, according to PKB sources <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **The borrower comes back within six months of taking the loan:** half (50%) of what is still owed is written off <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="who-uses-it-and-how-it-is-going"></a>

#### 2.3.5 Who uses it, and how it is going

From 2011 to the 2023-24 financial year PKB lent Tk 3,311 crore 86 lakh to 1,70,194 migrants, by its own count <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Most applicants are going to Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Oman, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain, and applications for Italy, Romania, South Korea and Singapore were rising in 2024, according to PKB sources <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The managing director said in July 2024 that the share of loans in default, 12% two years earlier, had fallen to 7.4% after the bank set up 15 regional offices <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. PKB also runs savings schemes for migrants and their families, but few people use them because the interest is low <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

**One case: a loan after a viral video.** Sarowar Hossain (27), from a poor family in Hossainpur, Kishoreganj, had his visa but could not raise the money; he could not get a bank loan despite many attempts, and asked NGOs and others for help without success <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>. On 16 October 2025 he hired a microphone for Tk 500, abused the people of his area for not standing by him, and posted the video on Facebook, where it spread <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>. After media reports, PKB's Kishoreganj branch phoned him, checked his papers and lent him Tk 2 lakh, with repayment in instalments from two months later; he said he had a ticket to fly to Saudi Arabia on 30 October <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>.

On 5 March 2026 the Prime Minister told the ministries concerned to arrange low-interest bank loans and make loans easier to get, so that no one has to sell their property to go abroad for work, his additional press secretary said <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

<a id="the-pkb-loan-for-language-study-and-skills-abroad"></a>

### 2.4 The PKB loan for language study and skills abroad

PKB lends Tk 1 lakh to Tk 10 lakh to students and trainees who want to take a language or skills course abroad as a route to work there, especially in Japan, Germany, South Korea, Italy and Russia; it can be taken at any PKB branch <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The terms below are those PKB announced, as reported in December 2025 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

**Who can borrow:**
- a local resident of the branch's area, under 40 <sup>[N3](#n3), [N23](#n23)</sup>;
- with an offer letter (admission confirmation) from the institution in the destination country <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>;
- with at least an intermediate-level language certificate accepted there, such as TOPIK level 3 for South Korea; the December 2025 report gives N-4 for Japan, the April 2026 report N5 <sup>[N3](#n3), [N23](#n23)</sup>;
- for a short course of at most two years <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

**Bring:** three recent passport-size photos, a photocopy of your NID, the offer letter, photocopies of your passport and latest academic certificate, and the language certificate; plus the name, address and papers of one guarantor able to repay the loan, who can be a family member or relative <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

**Security, by amount** <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>:
- **Tk 1 to 3 lakh:** no collateral; the guarantor's personal guarantee and three cheque leaves from the guarantor's active bank account.
- **Tk 3 to 5 lakh:** the same, plus the original deed of land or other immovable property worth at least twice the loan, deposited at the branch.
- **Tk 5 to 10 lakh:** a registered mortgage and an irrevocable power of attorney in PKB's favour on property worth at least twice the loan.

**Interest and term:** 11% a year, which may change; the December 2025 report gives a term of two to five years, the April 2026 report up to three years <sup>[N3](#n3), [N23](#n23)</sup>. The April 2026 report says the money is paid as tuition fees sent directly to the institution and as money sent for living costs abroad <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>.

**Apply** online or in person at the nearest branch only; PKB has warned of fraud in loan processing and says it has no agents <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Helpline 16238 <sup>[N3](#n3), [N23](#n23)</sup>. The student loan of up to Tk 10 lakh as proof of solvency for higher study is in "Can I get a government loan to go abroad?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>.

## 3 At the airport

<a id="the-probashi-kallyan-desk"></a>

### 3.1 The Probashi Kallyan desk

The Welfare Board's Probashi Kallyan desk works at all three international airports (Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. What it does, as officials described it in July 2024:
- **On the way out:** checks each new worker's BMET clearance, so that no worker becomes a victim of human trafficking <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **For workers on leave from the Middle East:** a worker going back must carry the leave permission, which is printed at the desk, and the desk's staff check and put the worker's papers in order <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **On the way back:** receives workers who come back physically or mentally ill, and those who come back on an outpass (a one-way travel permit) after arrest <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Bodies:** receives the bodies of migrants who die abroad, hands them to the family and pays the grant for burial <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

The desk's other services, its hours and phone numbers are under "At the airport: the welfare desk" in the Departure bottlenecks doc <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

**The third terminal.** At a meeting on 10 June 2024, the Civil Aviation Authority's chairman said that in Dhaka airport's new third terminal BMET clearance would not be checked as before but online, in an automated system, with BMET's database merged with the immigration department's, and the airport's executive director said all welfare work for migrants there would run through the terminal's information centre <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. On 24 June 2024 the desk wrote to BMET's director general asking for urgent action to secure it a place in the terminal <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. A Civil Aviation Authority member said the desk's services were planned within the terminal's own operations, which a Japanese company would run, and that space would have to be asked of that company <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The Welfare Board's director, Shoaib Ahmad Khan, said three to four thousand new workers leave every day and that he hoped the desk would get space; Tasneem Siddiqui, founding chair of the migration research group RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), called leaving the desk out a wrong decision <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

**Sylhet.** The minister for expatriates' welfare and for labour, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, said on his first visit to Sylhet as minister, on 19 February 2026, that the service centres he had seen at Dhaka airport do not exist at Sylhet airport <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.

<a id="the-migrant-lounge-and-helpers-at-dhaka-airport"></a>

### 3.2 The migrant lounge and helpers at Dhaka airport

On 11 November 2024 the Chief Adviser, Muhammad Yunus, opened the first lounge for migrant workers (the Probashi lounge) at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, with space to rest and food at low prices subsidised by the government <sup>[N27 (2024)](#n27)</sup>. The UN sponsored the lounge, according to the IOM (International Organization for Migration) deputy chief of mission in Bangladesh <sup>[N27 (2024)](#n27)</sup>. The expatriates' welfare adviser, Asif Nazrul, said 100 staff sponsored by IOM had been appointed to look after and help migrants at the airport; IOM described them as about 100 volunteers <sup>[N27 (2024)](#n27), [N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>.

The lounge was in use in March 2025: when Prothom Alo visited, 15 to 20 travellers were there, some talking to relatives, some eating food from the shop beside it and some charging their phones <sup>[N29](#n29)</sup>. The shop's worker said it was set up so migrants can buy food at low cost; Alif Hossain of Bancharampur, Brahmanbaria, in Kuwait since 1996, said he heard of the lounge after clearing immigration, and that it was much better than other places, with cheaper food <sup>[N29](#n29)</sup>. On 16 April 2025 Asif Nazrul said the lounge offers quality food up to 30% cheaper, free Wi-Fi, a free telephone and mobile charging <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

> **Need to know**

> - How does a migrant recognise and reach one of the IOM-sponsored helpers at the airport <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>?

<a id="finishing-school-or-a-degree-from-abroad"></a>

### 4.1 Finishing school or a degree from abroad

Bangladesh Open University (BOU) runs overseas programmes (called NISH-2) that Bangladeshi migrants join through the Bangladesh embassy in their country <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10), [N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. As of the 2024 batch:

<a id="ssc"></a>

#### 4.1.1 SSC

- **Who:** Bangladeshi migrants or people of Bangladeshi origin in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Italy who have passed JSC, JDC, class 8 or an equivalent <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.
- **Streams:** humanities and business studies <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 140 for the first year and USD 150 for the second, each paid at that year's admission; registration lasts at least two and at most five years <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.
- **Papers:** one photo; copies of the class 8 (or equivalent) certificate and mark sheet, the passport, and the NID if you have one <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.
- **How:** apply online and pay the fee, then complete admission at the embassy; in 2024 the deadline was 20 February <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.

<a id="hsc"></a>

#### 4.1.2 HSC

- **Who:** Bangladeshi migrants or people of Bangladeshi origin in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE, South Korea and Italy who have passed SSC, Dakhil, O-level or an equivalent <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 180 for the first year and USD 165 for the second <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.

Contacts for both: the embassy, the Dean of BOU's Open School (dean.os@bou.ac.bd) and BOU's International Academic Program Wing (iapw@bou.ac.bd) <sup>[N10 (2024)](#n10)</sup>.

<a id="ba-and-bss"></a>

#### 4.1.3 BA and BSS

- **Who:** Bangladeshis in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Italy, South Korea and the UAE who have passed HSC, Alim, A-level or an equivalent <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Length:** three years in six semesters of six months; registration stays valid for up to five academic years <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Fee:** USD 250 for the first year, USD 190 for the second and USD 220 for the third, each paid at that year's admission <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Classes:** eight online tutorials per course and exams on the host country's weekly day off, so you need a device with internet; mainly e-books, with printed books free on request from the International Academic Program Wing <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

To apply <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>:
1. Scan a recent photo (300x300 pixels), your signature (300x100 pixels), SSC and HSC certificates and mark sheets, NID, smart card or birth certificate, passport and residence permit card.
2. Pay the USD 250 first-year fee as the embassy's website or Facebook page instructs, and scan the proof.
3. Fill in the online form.
4. Print the form and hand it in at the embassy with the papers and proof of payment.

In 2024 admission closed on 30 March and classes began in April <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. Contacts: the embassy, the Dean of BOU's SSHL school (bousshl2019@bou.ac.bd) and iapw@bou.ac.bd; website iapw.bou.ac.bd <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

<a id="if-a-worker-dies-abroad"></a>

## 5 If a worker dies abroad

What the family gets, and how the body comes home, are in "If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?" and "How is the body brought home?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. At the airport, the Probashi Kallyan desk receives the body, hands it to the family and pays the burial grant <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. If the worker had a PKB migration loan, the loan risk cover writes off everything still owed (see [The loan risk cover](#the-loan-risk-cover)) <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

A Bangladeshi migrant in Doha wrote in June 2024 that sending a body home costs a large sum many families can't pay, that in many Middle Eastern countries other migrants have to collect money to send a body home, and that a family left with the dead worker's migration debt gets no help from the state with it <sup>[N32 (2024)](#n32)</sup>. He wrote that at the embassy too, without going through a broker, a one-day task can take three months <sup>[N32 (2024)](#n32)</sup>. In November 2024 Boishommobirodhi Probashi Andolon asked that the bodies of poor migrants be brought home free at the government's initiative <sup>[N31 (2024)](#n31)</sup>.

**When the family doubts the cause of death.** Another migrant in Doha, Minhaj bin Mahbub, wrote in September 2025 that many families of migrants who took their own lives abroad believe their relative was murdered, pointing to wounds on the body, that most such bodies are of women domestic workers, and that the body should be examined again by post-mortem when it reaches Bangladesh <sup>[N33](#n33)</sup>.

> **Need to know**

> **Need to know**
> - When a body is sent home through the embassy, who pays the cost abroad (the employer, the Welfare Board or the family), and is a collection among workers still needed in 2026 <sup>[N32 (2024)](#n32)</sup>?

## 6 Help for the family at home

<a id="scholarships-for-migrants-children"></a>

### 6.1 Scholarships for migrants' children

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to high-achieving children of migrants; the amounts are in "Is there help for my children?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. The rules, from the latest round reported, which closed on 25 November 2025 <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>:
- **Whose children:** of a worker who left with BMET emigration clearance or holds Welfare Board membership, or of a worker who died and whose family got or will get the Board's grant <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
- **Which students:** in the 2024 round, those who passed SSC in 2024 and were in class 11 or a diploma course, or passed HSC in 2023 and were at a university or medical college; the 2025 round took SSC 2025, HSC 2024 and Diploma in Engineering 2024 results <sup>[N35 (2024)](#n35), [N34](#n34)</sup>.
- **Results needed, child of a worker abroad:** SSC GPA 5.00 in science or 4.75 in humanities and business studies; HSC GPA 4.80 in science or 4.50 in humanities and business studies; Diploma in Engineering GPA 3.50 <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>.
- **Results needed, child of a worker who died abroad:** GPA 4.00 in any group at SSC or HSC; GPA 3.00 for the diploma <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>.
- **How long it is paid:** from SSC, two years for classes 11 and 12 or four years for a diploma; from HSC, three years for a pass degree, four for honours and five for medical, whatever the session delays; from the Diploma in Engineering, four years <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N34](#n34)</sup>.

Papers to scan, as listed for the 2025 round <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>:
- proof that the mother or father is a migrant worker;
- a certificate from the head of the student's institution, with the head's mobile number;
- for a child of a worker who died abroad, a certificate from the Bangladesh embassy;
- a statement of the student's own bank account, with the account and routing numbers;
- one photo and the student's signature;
- a copy of the student's NID card (the 2024 round accepted a birth registration copy instead) <sup>[N35 (2024)](#n35)</sup>.

To apply:
1. Go to www.wewb.gov.bd or http://stipen.wewb.gov.bd/stipend; applications are online only, not in person or by post <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
2. Give the student's mobile number and a parent's or guardian's mobile number <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
3. Upload the scanned papers <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N34](#n34)</sup>.
4. Check the parents' names on the student's certificate match the name in the migrant's passport: a mismatch, or an incomplete or wrong application, is rejected <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.

Past rounds: the 2025 round closed on 25 November 2025; the 2024 round ran from 25 November to 24 December 2024; the round before closed on 31 January 2024, and its winners' lists were published in April 2024 <sup>[N34](#n34), [N7 (2024)](#n7), [N36 (2024)](#n36), [N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>. At one Welfare Board event on 16 April 2025, 65 students were given scholarships <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. For details the Board asks people to call Probash-Bondhu: 16135 toll-free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.

<a id="allowance-for-a-migrants-disabled-child"></a>

### 6.2 Allowance for a migrant's disabled child

The Welfare Board pays Tk 1,000 a month (Tk 12,000 a year) for five years to a selected disabled child of a worker who took BMET emigration clearance or Board membership, or of a worker who died and whose family got the Board's grant; the 2024 round paid from 1 January 2024 <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. A parent or guardian applies, separately for each child <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. Bring <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>:
- photocopies of the parents' passports, including the page with the BMET emigration clearance;
- if the worker died abroad, an NOC (no-objection certificate) from the Bangladesh embassy;
- the child's photos: two attested passport-size photos on paper, one for an online application;
- an attested photocopy of the child's Subarna Nagorik card (the disability identity card from the upazila social services office);
- a bank statement, with routing number, of the parent or guardian who will receive the cheque.

To apply, either <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>:
1. apply online; or
2. collect the form free from the Board's head office, its website, a Welfare Centre or the district employment and manpower office (DEMO), and send it with the papers to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, 9th floor (despatch section), Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka.

In 2024 applications ran from 16 May to 27 June, and those who had already applied did not need to again <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.

> **Need to know**

## 7 Coming home

<a id="pkb-loans-to-restart"></a>

### 7.1 PKB loans to restart

PKB lends to returnees for self-employment without any mortgage of property, deciding within seven days if the papers are in order <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. In July 2024 its six loans were the migration loan, the rehabilitation loan, the special rehabilitation loan, the Bangabandhu migrant large-family loan, the self-employment loan, and the women's migration and rehabilitation loan <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Its managing director said in July 2024 that it lends to returnee women for self-employment at 4 to 7% interest <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The rehabilitation loan's limits and what returnees say about the help that reaches them: see "What help is there when I come back?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc and "Help after you get home" in the Departure bottlenecks doc <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. In November 2024 Boishommobirodhi Probashi Andolon asked for jobs for returnees, enough interest-free PKB loans for those who want to start a business, compensation for workers cheated by brokers, and a pension for Middle East workers who retire and come home <sup>[N31 (2024)](#n31)</sup>.

<a id="help-from-ngo-farm-projects-one-case"></a>

### 7.2 Help from NGO farm projects: one case

Shah Alam (45), of Niamatpur village in Sadar upazila, Sirajganj, came home after about 12 years in Saudi Arabia and in 2022 began farming chui jhal, a spice vine, with the help of NDP (National Development Programme), a local NGO running a high-value spice demonstration for PKSF (Palli Karma-Sahayak Foundation) <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. He leased 33 decimals next to his house for three years for Tk 60,000 on 30 August 2022 <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. By his own account, he had spent Tk 1 lakh 17 thousand since starting, and in 2025 sold Tk 7 lakh 37 thousand of chui jhal in two batches <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>.

### 7.5 Investing at home: BIDA's desk for non-resident Bangladeshis

## 8 How the services are paid for

The Welfare Board's help is not government money but migrants' own: the fees workers pay before going abroad go into the Board's fund, as was said at a Board event on 16 April 2025 <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. At that event, by the Board's data, 103 people got Tk 1 crore 93 lakh 13 thousand <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>:
- **Death grants:** Tk 36 lakh to 12 families <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Scholarships:** 65 students <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Insurance claims:** Tk 1 crore 4 lakh to 11 people <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Compensation, arrears and service benefits from abroad:** Tk 27 lakh 67 thousand to two families <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Treatment help:** Tk 3 lakh 30 thousand to three people <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Disability allowance:** Tk 1 lakh 20 thousand to 10 children <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

It was also said there that during the interim government's term the Board had given Tk 273 crore 81 lakh in various help to 12,158 people <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In June 2025 Nahid Islam, convener of the National Citizen Party (NCP), condemned the halving of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's budget in the budget the interim government announced <sup>[N39](#n39)</sup>.

<a id="women-workers"></a>

## 9 Women workers

<a id="returnee-women"></a>

### 9.1 Returnee women

The Centre for Women and Children Studies (CWCS), a Dhaka research and advocacy group, said in May 2024 that returnee women face many risks, that most people look down on them, and that in some cases husbands or families refuse to take them back <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. CWCS ran a project on empowering returnee women in Keraniganj and Nawabganj, two upazilas of Dhaka district, from 2020 to 2023, arranging work for them through training <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. In 2022 it formed Nari Obhibashi Kontho (Women Migrants' Voice), an alliance of organisations working with women migrants, with 25 members by May 2024 <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

In May 2024 CWCS asked the government for services that did not then exist <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>:
- loans of Tk 1 to 3 lakh to returnee women on migration papers alone, without a guarantor;
- free legal help, through the government's legal aid service, for women who suffered sexual exploitation, abuse, forced labour or trafficking;
- migrant service centres at people's doorsteps, and a separate database of returnee women.

PKB's loan for returnee women: see PKB loans to restart <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The airport payment to women back after abuse and PKB's eased conditions for returnee women: see "What help is there when I come back?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

At a roundtable on 5 May 2026, the ILO's senior national programme manager, Rahnuma Salam Khan, spoke of a plan to introduce a special cash scheme for returnee women with ILO support, and a UNDP Bangladesh gender expert, Sharmin Islam, called on the government to ensure psycho-social counselling for returnee women workers <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does PKB lend to a returnee woman without a guarantor in 2026, and up to what amount <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>?
> - Has the ILO-backed cash scheme for returnee women started, and how does a woman apply <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>?
