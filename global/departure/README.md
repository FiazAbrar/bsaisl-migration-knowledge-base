## 1 In one minute

Before a worker boards, five offices each hold a stamp he cannot travel without: the passport office, the police (for the police clearance), an approved medical centre (for the Gulf, one approved by the Gulf Health Council), the destination's embassy, and BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which issues the emigration clearance or "smart card") <sup>[1](#o1), [2](#o2), [N1](#n1), [N2 (2019)](#n2), [3](#o3)</sup>. At each one, surveys, news reports and an anti-corruption case show people paying on top of the official fee: TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) found three in four households that used the passport office in November 2024 to October 2025 paid a bribe or unauthorised money, medical centres have been reported to threaten fit workers with an "unfit" result and to sell guaranteed "fit" reports, and in July 2025 the Anti-Corruption Commission filed a case against BMET officials it accuses of clearing women on fake passports <sup>[N3](#n3), [N4 (2017)](#n4), [N1](#n1), [N5](#n5)</sup>. The last gate is immigration at the airport, run by the police's Special Branch (SB), which turned back 81,500 travellers from 2021 to June 2026, often over mismatched visas, clearance discrepancies, forged papers or answers that did not add up <sup>[4](#o4), [N6](#n6)</sup>. Some were stopped with valid papers, most visibly three adult women flying to Nepal on 8 August 2026 who, their lawyer says, were asked why they were unmarried; on 29 September 2026 the High Court asked the government to explain why stopping them should not be declared illegal <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. A Home Ministry official has admitted that some complaints of people paying police to get through, or being unjustly stopped, are true <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. Even past Dhaka, visitors can be refused on arrival, as hundreds of Bangladeshis were at Kuala Lumpur in 2025 <sup>[N8](#n8), [N9](#n9)</sup>. Workers who come back deported or on an outpass are met by the government's airport welfare desk, though in 2020 some deportees were jailed on arrival, and destinations such as Kuwait and Malaysia bar deportees from returning for years or for life <sup>[5](#o5), [N10 (2020)](#n10), [N11](#n11), [N12 (2017)](#n12)</sup>.

## 2 Passport and police clearance

<a id="nid-and-birth-registration"></a>

### 2.1 NID and birth registration

The passport application must match the NID (national identity card) or the online birth registration (see [The passport office](#the-passport-office)), and the offices behind both papers are bottlenecks of their own.

**Two birth registrations.** The Uttara passport office cancelled an e-passport application in 2024 because the applicant had two online birth registrations <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. The fix is to apply at the Registrar General's office (birth and death registration) in Dhaka to cancel one; the office investigates and sends its report to the ward office, which takes about a month, and a fresh registration is then applied for <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. The Registrar General said in June 2025 that one registration is cancelled for anyone with two who applies <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. A father back from Saudi Arabia was asked Tk 10,000 by a dalal (broker) before the passport office sent him to the Registrar General's office <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

**NID bribes.** On 10 September 2026 Rasel Molla, a migrant in Bahrain, went to the Goalanda upazila election office for a new NID; the upazila election officer, Khabirul Alam, demanded Tk 5,000 and took Tk 2,000 after he pleaded, which Molla secretly filmed and posted on Facebook <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. The officer was transferred to Jhenaidah on 16 September pending an inquiry <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. It is alleged that local computer shops there are part of a dalal ring, and that new NIDs and corrections of names and parents' names are done for bribes of Tk 5,000 to over Tk 50,000 a job; one man said he paid Tk 50,000 for a voter ID card to go abroad urgently <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.

<a id="the-passport-office"></a>

### 2.2 The passport office

**The rule.** An e-passport (48 pages, 5 years) costs Tk 4,025 regular, Tk 6,325 express or Tk 8,625 super express, VAT included <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. Regular delivery is within 15 working days of biometric enrolment, express within 7 and super express within 2 <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. The application must match the NID (national identity card) or online birth certificate exactly, and anyone over 20 must use the NID <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>. The deputy director of the Sylhet divisional passport and visa office said in April 2026 that the regular fee is Tk 4,025 for five years and Tk 5,750 for ten years, that super express costs Tk 10,350, and that with super express someone who gives biometrics in the morning can get the passport in Dhaka by the afternoon <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>. He said anyone 18 or over applies with the NID and anyone younger with a birth registration, pays the fee at a bank and gives fingerprints <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>. The Department of Immigration and Passports' additional director general said in August 2026 that super express passports are being delivered within 24 hours or two days <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The 10-year passport's express fee is Tk 8,050 <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The home affairs adviser said on 20 October 2025 that a decision had been taken to cut passport fees for migrants to a reasonable level <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has the passport fee cut for migrants announced in October 2025 taken effect, and at what fee?

**One person, one passport.** Since e-passports began in 2020, no passport is issued without online verification of the NID and birth registration data, and the gaps that once let some people hold more than one passport are now easily detected, the Cox's Bazar regional passport office said in June 2026 <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. Because an e-passport takes all 10 fingerprints, the same person can no longer get a passport under a different identity, and there is no scope to issue one without valid information and papers, even to someone who went abroad by an illegal route, the department's additional director general said in August 2026 <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. He said the department has cases of people who took passports by repeatedly changing their name, date of birth and other details, which is reducing foreign embassies' and immigration authorities' acceptance of Bangladeshi passports <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

**What happens.** In TIB's National Household Survey 2025 (November 2024 to October 2025), passport services were the most corrupt sector: 84.4% of households using them met corruption and 76.6% paid bribes or unauthorised money <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The average bribe was Tk 4,532, and 39.7% reported harassment by brokers ("dalals") <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Most irregularities happened while preparing and submitting the application (76.8%), and the biometric stage carried the highest average bribe, Tk 6,680 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. People reissuing an old MRP (machine-readable passport) paid the most, Tk 12,444 on average <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Barishal (93.3%) and Sylhet (91.7%) divisions were the worst <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. TIB says that despite online services many people still depend on brokers, which keeps bribery alive <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. The state minister for expatriates' welfare said in October 2026 that migrants complain of dalals and extra cost when getting a passport <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>, and the Department of Immigration and Passports' additional director general said in August 2026 that there are complaints of harassment and extra cost in renewing one <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>.

**How the broker trade works.** At the Mymensingh regional passport office, a file sent through a dalal is called a "channel file" (চ্যানেল ফাইল); three dalals told Prothom Alo in 2024 that every shop outside the office does them for Tk 2,500 above the government fee, of which Tk 1,200 goes to the passport office and the rest to the dalals <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. By their account, a fake "nationality and character certificate" in the name of Mymensingh City Corporation is attached to the file with a code on it; the clerk taking applications knows from the code whose dalal sent it and accepts it without scrutiny, the office keeps a daily count of coded forms, and an office employee collects the money by that count every Wednesday <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. The dalals sit in at least 30 computer, photocopy and online-application shops in front of the Mymensingh office in Maskanda, having moved off the street because of occasional raids, and by their account they take at least Tk 5 lakh a day <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. In Gazipur in 2025, 25 to 30 shops on both sides of the road in front of the office fill in forms and deposit the bank fee themselves <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. Applicants there said applying alone meets harassment on various pretexts while through a dalal everything is done at once for Tk 1,000 to 1,500; a reporter saw people wait about an hour for document checks and two to two and a half hours for fingerprints and photo, while those who paid the dalal went straight to the fingerprint and photo room <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. In Madaripur in 2025, 17 computer and stationery shops with no signboards, on both sides of the office, fill in applications online and take the fee, and everyone working in them is known as a local dalal <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.

**Cases:**
- **24 April to 16 May 2024, Mymensingh.** Azharul Islam of Tarakanda waited an hour and a half in line and was refused over an error in his papers, failed on two days with new errors found each time, and refused to use a dalal; on 28 April he asked the office's deputy assistant director why his application was not taken when all papers were in order, the officer accepted it quickly, and he got his passport on 16 May <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>.
- **4 May 2024, Mymensingh.** A reporter saw Hashem Ali of Phulpur's application accepted without scrutiny, though his nationality and character certificate had blank ward and neighbourhood fields and an unidentified signature, and carried a handwritten code of three capital letters and the number 53 beside the serial number, a code the report says the dalal gives <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. The same day Sabbir Ahmed of Churkhai, told his papers had an "error" and to come back the next day, went with two friends to a shop outside to find a dalal <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>.
- **June 2024, Mymensingh.** The secretary of SUJAN (Citizens for Good Governance) in Mymensingh said nothing gets done at the office without a dalal, and that his nephew failed for a month to submit an application and got a passport only through one <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. The office's deputy director said applicants are told to beware of dalals outside, that he did not know of codes on fake City Corporation certificates or of staff taking money, and that he would look into it, as did the deputy assistant director <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>.
- **20 November 2024, Rajshahi.** People under the banner "Victims: the ordinary people of Rajshahi" formed a human chain in front of the divisional passport and visa office in Shalbagan against dalal files being processed first, and gave the deputy director a month to end it <sup>[N25 (2024)](#n25)</sup>. They said dalal files go straight upstairs to deputy director Rozy Khandaker's room for her signature and are entered on the same floor, then come quickly down for the photograph, while people without a dalal stand for hours in the ground-floor line <sup>[N25 (2024)](#n25)</sup>. They said officers look only for mistakes in files without a dalal and check nothing in dalal files, and that the ground-floor clerk is often away from his desk <sup>[N25 (2024)](#n25)</sup>. She denied all the allegations, asked them for proof, and told journalists she could not speak without the Director General's permission <sup>[N25 (2024)](#n25)</sup>. An intelligence report cited by Prothom Alo describes a "G-25" syndicate of 24 passport officers said to collect at least Tk 25 lakh in bribes a day; the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) questioned its members in 2021 and filed cases against some, and the Rajshahi protesters said she is known as a member <sup>[N25 (2024)](#n25)</sup>.
- **17 February 2025, Rajshahi.** A Department of Immigration and Passports inquiry committee heard complaints about the office at the Rajshahi circuit house <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. A woman said her form was returned over an error until she paid Tk 2,000 through an intermediary, after which she was sent straight to the photo room and the same form went through <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. A man said the deputy director held his uncle's file, which included a court's permission because of a pending case, for three months until Tk 20,000 was paid through an intermediary, and the passport then came within 15 days <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. A social worker said correct papers are shown as wrong and applicants sent round until they take a dalal, that Ansar guards there solve every problem, that a bribe paid through a dalal puts the payer's work ahead of the line, and claimed 98% of people at the office pay <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.
- **May 2025, Gazipur.** Ariful Islam of Kapasia joined the fingerprint and photo line at 9 am and finished at 1:30 pm, while those who paid a dalal finished within 10 to 15 minutes of arriving <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. Riad Mia of Tongi waited until 1:30 pm on a Wednesday without submitting his wife's file, came back on Thursday and finished after more than two hours, having refused a man who asked Tk 1,000 to get it done quickly <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. The office's deputy director said pressure of applicants had risen so it could take time, though not two to three hours, and that she has not let dalals inside since she arrived <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>.
- **May 2025, Madaripur.** Bayezid Munshi, 18, paid a dalal Tk 9,500 on contract for a 10-year passport whose government fee is Tk 5,750 <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. Rubel Howlader of Dasar was asked Tk 10,000 at one of the shops outside and paid Tk 8,000 <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. Applications without a dalal were returned when the applicant lacked the original NID or an attested printout of the online NID: Akter Hossain was turned away for each in turn and accepted only on his third trip, while others around him submitted without these demands <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The deputy director, Rotika Sarkar, said the office had noticed dalals in the shops and told the deputy commissioner, and claimed the office was 100% free of corruption <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.
- **13 July 2025, Faridpur.** Two local dalals, Siam Ahmed and Rashed Khan, took Tk 30,000 from a Rohingya couple on a promise of passports, did no passport work, and beat the couple's son-in-law when he protested, according to local sources; he asked the office's Ansar guards for help, and police detained the couple, the son-in-law and the two dalals <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>. The Kotwali police station's officer in charge said legal action was being taken against the dalals, and the three Rohingya would be sent to a camp if they lived in one <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>.

**When an office closes.** The Narayanganj regional passport office was set on fire on 18 July 2024, during the student-led movement, burning more than 8,000 ready passports and police verification papers; for nine months its applicants had to go to the Keraniganj, Narsingdi or Munshiganj offices <sup>[N28](#n28)</sup>. It reopened on 4 May 2025, taking applications and biometrics, to a large crowd on the first day, and the deputy commissioner, Mohammad Zahidul Islam Mia, said mobile courts would run regular drives to keep it free of dalals <sup>[N28](#n28)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Were the more than 8,000 passports burnt in Narayanganj reissued free, and did their owners have to apply and give biometrics again <sup>[N28](#n28)</sup>?

**Police verification.** TIB counts the abolition of police verification for passports among recent reforms <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. In a 2017 study, TIB had found that three in four new-passport applicants paid bribes or irregular money during police verification, and its executive director said there is "a trap of harassment" at every step from application to delivery <sup>[N29](#n29)</sup>. In the 2025 survey (November 2024 to October 2025), passport verification was the police service with the most corruption (80.7%) and bribery (77.5%) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The DIG (Deputy Inspector General) for operations of APBn (the Airport Armed Police Battalion) said in August 2026 that the risk of irregularities rose after police verification for passports was removed <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is police verification still done for any new passport in 2026, and if so, for whom?
> - Which office takes a complaint about a passport-office broker or bribe: the Department of Immigration and Passports, its GRS (grievance redress) officer, or only the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission)?

<a id="police-clearance"></a>

### 2.3 Police clearance

**The rule.** The police clearance certificate (PCC) is applied for online at pcc.police.gov.bd and verified by police at your present address <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>. The fee was raised from Tk 500 to Tk 1,500 by a Security Services Division circular of 4 December 2024 <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>. You track it by texting "PCC S \<reference number\>" to 26969; the PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825, help.pcc@police.gov.bd, Sunday to Thursday 9 am to 5 pm <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>.

**What happens.** TIB's 2017 study found police took Tk 500 to 1,000 per visa for a clearance <sup>[N30 (2017)](#n30)</sup>. In TIB's 2025 survey, 66.1% of households dealing with the Special Branch paid unauthorised money, though police clearance had the lowest average bribe of any police service, Tk 1,279 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Cumilla's additional superintendent of police said in April 2026 that despite some complaints, people with no case against them get clearance within the set time, and that a delay is resolved quickly by contacting the office concerned <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>. The home minister, Salahuddin Ahmed, said at Police Week on 11 May 2026 that work was under way to deliver clearances faster, and directed that past complaints of harassment over police clearance must not recur <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. Brokers also sell forged certificates: police said a ring, whose arrested members were mainly brokers, took Tk 1,23,000 from a man in Banshkhali, Chattogram, in 2026 on a promise of a quick clearance and gave him a forged one; his brother said the man had a criminal case and so had not applied to the police directly, and that the family paid Tk 80,000 in advance and the rest on delivery (see "Forged documents and impersonation" in the Illegal routes doc) <sup>[N33](#n33)</sup>. Chattogram's Superintendent of Police asked people not to get a clearance through any broker or middleman but to follow the government process, and called forging officers' signatures and using a fake website and QR code a very serious crime <sup>[N33](#n33)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How long does a PCC take in 2026, and what is the usual delay at the address check?
> - What is the street price for a "fast" PCC through a broker in 2026?

<a id="driving-licence-for-driver-jobs"></a>

### 2.4 Driving licence for driver jobs

A worker going abroad on a driving licence cannot go without the smart card from BRTA (the Bangladesh Road Transport Authority) <sup>[N34 (2024)](#n34)</sup>. In October 2024 BRTA had failed to print cards for more than 6.25 lakh people who had paid and passed the test, some waiting three years, and was handing out paper licences instead <sup>[N34 (2024)](#n34)</sup>. Sources said the few cards printed went to influential officials and to people going abroad, who had to show their visa at the BRTA chairman's office to be listed, and that those who knew this were alleged to pay large sums <sup>[N34 (2024)](#n34)</sup>. Abdul Matin of Cumilla, holding a Saudi visa, paid a dalal Tk 13,000 for a licence correction and had waited two months for the card before he could submit his passport to the embassy; the owner of a visa-processing firm in Paltan, Dhaka, said some of his clients paid dalals Tk 50,000 for a licence <sup>[N34 (2024)](#n34)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has BRTA cleared the smart-card backlog, and how long does a driver's card take in 2026?

<a id="the-gulf-medical-wafid-formerly-gamca"></a>

## 3 The Gulf medical (Wafid, formerly GAMCA)

<a id="the-rule-and-the-fee"></a>

### 3.1 The rule and the fee

Workers going to the Gulf states must pass a medical at a centre approved by the Gulf Health Council (GHC), booked through its Wafid system; the approved centres were previously known as GAMCA (GCC-approved medical centres, the GCC being the Gulf Cooperation Council) <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. The agency buys a USD 10 referral token from Wafid, and the whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. Wafid says the result appears within 26 days, and a certificate is valid two months for issuing the visa <sup>[8](#o8), [9 (2021)](#o9)</sup>. Centres are licensed first by Bangladesh's health ministry, then approved by the GHC <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>. Qatar uses its own centres: only two are approved, in Dhaka and Sylhet, and the fee is at least USD 137 <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>.

<a id="the-unfit-then-pay-scheme-and-the-slip-racket"></a>

### 3.2 The "unfit, then pay" scheme and the slip racket

- **Unfit, then pay.** TIB reported in 2017 that some centres threaten fit workers with an "unfit" result to charge more, or make them repeat tests <sup>[N4 (2017)](#n4)</sup>. A worker told TBS (The Business Standard) in 2025 that, found unfit in Chattogram for Tk 8,500, he was declared fit at a Panthapath centre in Dhaka for Tk 50,000, then re-tested in Kuwait for 5 dirhams <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>.
- **"Fit" packages.** A Dhaka agency offered one worker a Tk 10,000 "standard" medical that could end "unfit", or a Tk 20,000 package that guaranteed "fit" <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. Syndicate centres reportedly charge Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed "fit" <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. A former BAIRA (recruiting agencies' association) leader said only 2-5% of workers pay the set fee, and the rest pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>.
- **Why people pay.** An "unfit" result leaves the passport unusable for a Gulf medical until it expires, and workers fear a lifetime blacklist, so many fix a "contract" with a broker in advance <sup>[N1](#n1), [N35](#n35)</sup>.
- **Who controls the slips.** Industry insiders say about 20 of more than 200 approved centres get nearly 90% of referrals because syndicate-linked centres pre-book slots <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. Approved centres grew from 26 in 2017 to 260, against 167 in India <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>.
- **Reports without tests.** Some centres issue certificates without testing, or upload reports from a passport photocopy, and some tests done in Dhaka are filed under district centres' names <sup>[N4 (2017)](#n4), [N35](#n35), [N1](#n1)</sup>.
- **What it costs later.** A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for a "fit" certificate failed the re-test in Saudi Arabia and was deported <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>.

In July 2025 the ministry's monitoring wing said it had heard of extra charges but received no formal complaints, and had contacted the Gulf Cooperation Council; by September 2025 the ministry had written to the GHC asking it to act against irregular centres <sup>[N1](#n1), [N35](#n35)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has the GHC suspended or delisted any Bangladeshi medical centre since 2024, and where is the list published?
> - How does a worker appeal an "unfit" result: a re-test at another centre, or an appeal to the GHC through Wafid?
> - How long does an "unfit" record block a passport in Wafid?

<a id="malaysias-medical"></a>

### 3.3 Malaysia's medical

The ministry fixed the Malaysia-bound medical at Tk 8,000 on 31 October 2026, and no centre may take more <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. No fee is due for the MiGRAMS slip (the booking from Malaysia's recruitment system), no centre may work under a particular agency, and a worker may test at any approved centre <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. If a worker changes agency after the slip, the first agency must send the medical report on without delay <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. BAIRA said that over the previous three years a syndicate of about 100 agencies had charged Tk 11,000 against a Tk 6,500 fee, taking Tk 355 crore extra <sup>[N35](#n35)</sup>.

**Complain:** email the ministry at secretary@probashi.gov.bd if a centre asks more than Tk 8,000 or harasses you <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.

<a id="attestation-and-visa-stamping"></a>

## 4 Attestation and visa stamping

Every document going abroad needs attestation by Bangladesh's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA), after the issuing body and, for Arabic translations, a notary, the Islamic Foundation and the Law Ministry <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. MOFA also runs online apostille (attestation of public documents and certificates) on mygov.bd, the MyGov portal of the a2i (Aspire to Innovate) programme, so documents can be attested from home; a2i said on 13 December 2025 that about 17 lakh applications had been settled in the previous 11 months <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>.

**The apostille.** The online system under the 1961 Apostille Convention was launched on 29 January 2025 <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. A document with an apostille no longer needs attestation at a foreign embassy, where embassies in Dhaka charge Tk 3,000 to 20,000 a page <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. MoFA's directory lists 114 of the convention's 127 member countries as accepting Bangladesh's apostille <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. Before it, an education certificate went to the Ministry of Education, then MoFA, then the destination's embassy, and for a country with no embassy in Bangladesh people had to go to Delhi; attesting paper copies at government offices meant long queues and brokers <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38), [N37](#n37)</sup>. Public examination certificates have been attested online since 1 October 2024, so the originals no longer go to the Ministry of Education <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38)</sup>.

**Getting a document attested or apostilled on mygov.bd** (steps 1 to 6 from a December 2024 guide to online certificate attestation, steps 7 and 8 from the January 2025 apostille launch):
1. Register at www.mygov.bd with your name and mobile number or email address, and set a password <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39)</sup>.
2. Verify the profile at once with your NID, or your birth registration number and year of birth; an NID or a birth registration listed online is the only ID needed, and one NID can verify only one account <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39)</sup>.
3. Scan each document as a JPEG, JPG or PNG file under 1 MB <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39)</sup>.
4. On the home page, choose certificate attestation for people going abroad, then the application for your document type <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39)</sup>.
5. Pay the fee, where there is one, by online banking, card or mobile payment; students of UGC (University Grants Commission) universities can pay through ekPay, the national payment gateway <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39), [N40](#n40)</sup>.
6. Track the application with its tracking number under "Sheba Babosthapona" (service management) <sup>[N39 (2024)](#n39)</sup>.
7. The issuing authority verifies and attests the document and sends it to its controlling ministry, which countersigns it and sends it online to MoFA for the e-apostille <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>.
8. When MoFA finishes, a notification comes to your registered mobile; download the e-apostille with the "Download" button on your MyGov dashboard <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>.

For Saudi Arabia, the employer sends visa numbers online, the agency submits the passports, and since 31 October 2022 passports, stamping and fingerprints go through Tasheer, a visa service centre at Jamuna Future Park, not the embassy <sup>[N2 (2019)](#n2), [N41 (2022)](#n41)</sup>. Stamping can take one week to two months after fingerprints in some cases <sup>[N42](#n42)</sup>. In 2022 an agency could submit at most 30 passports a week, with no cap for women workers <sup>[N43 (2022)](#n43)</sup>.

**What happens.**
- In 2017 TIB reported delays, harassment and illegal charges by some host-country embassies at stamping, and that a file with any error is returned <sup>[N4 (2017)](#n4)</sup>.
- In 2022 agencies said they paid USD 220-250 per Saudi visa application through middlemen, which raised workers' costs by at least Tk 20,000; the embassy said only a small fee for Enjaz (the online Saudi visa application) applies and brokers may misuse its name <sup>[N43 (2022)](#n43)</sup>.
- TIB has reported that some officials at Bangladeshi missions abroad attest demand letters without proper checks or for bribes <sup>[N44](#n44)</sup>.
- Under a Bangladesh Bank circular of June 2026, a local visa-processing agent may send visa fees collected in taka to the embassy's account abroad through a bank only if it collected them at the embassy's set rate, against an invoice from the embassy or its authorised agency with a statement for each applicant <sup>[N45](#n45)</sup>.

<a id="bmet-clearance-and-the-smart-card"></a>

## 5 BMET clearance and the smart card

<a id="the-rule-and-the-fee-1"></a>

### 5.1 The rule and the fee

The emigration clearance, printed as a smart card, is BMET's permission to leave for work <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. If an agency recruited you, it applies; if you found the visa yourself, you apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office (DEMO) <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. Either way it takes 3 working days <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>. Fees (the welfare, insurance and smart card fees by bank pay order, the tax by treasury challan) <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>:
- welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>;
- insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>;
- clearance (smart card) fee: Tk 250 <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>;
- source income tax: Tk 400 for an unattested single visa, Tk 250 attested, Tk 500 for a Saudi visa; Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

For women going as domestic workers, BMET needs proof of age 25 to 45 and a guardian's no-objection letter <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

<a id="bribes-brokers-and-fake-clearances"></a>

### 5.2 Bribes, brokers and fake clearances

- TIB's 2017 study found Tk 100-200 taken unofficially per clearance at BMET, and Tk 5,000-15,000 for low- and semi-skilled Malaysia-bound workers <sup>[N30 (2017)](#n30), [N4 (2017)](#n4)</sup>.
- TIB reported in 2017 that some workers buy the pre-departure training certificate through brokers without attending <sup>[N30 (2017)](#n30)</sup>.
- In 2016 only 301 workers used BMET's one-stop service to get their own clearance <sup>[N4 (2017)](#n4)</sup>.
- According to sources cited by Prothom Alo, 20 smart card applications rejected in the Ami Probashi app (the government's migrant-worker app) between 27 December 2023 and 4 January 2024 were later given clearance; separately, on 31 December 2023 the agency Alif Overseas (RL-847) took out smart cards for 24 Saudi-bound workers for which BMET officials later found no matching data on the server (see "Fake BMET cards and fake clearance" in the Dalal scams doc) <sup>[N46 (2024)](#n46)</sup>.
- On 3 July 2025 the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) filed a case against four BMET officials and the owners or officials of five agencies; it says its 29 May 2025 raid on BMET found clearances for Gulf domestic work approved on another woman's passport number without a check against BMET's database, and issued to four women under 25 <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The accused are BMET deputy director (emigration) Md Sazzad Hossain Sarkar, assistant director (emigration) Md Hossain Ullah Akanda, manpower survey officer Md Nizamuddin Patwari and office assistant Md Azad Hossain, with Md Anwar Hossain of H A International, Md Salauddin of K H Overseas, Md Jamal Hossain of Makkah Overseas, Md Anwar Hossain Bhuiyan of Tasnim Overseas and Ekramul Haque of S M Manpower <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The ACC alleges the agencies colluded to use fake passports for women sent to the Middle East as domestic workers, that clearing four women under 25 broke a ministry circular, and that the officials gained financially; it said it was investigating further and would take legal action when the investigation ends <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- In 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare said some BMET officials clear fake documents, that BMET can check visas for only four or five countries, and that a fake-visa clearance case for Serbia was under inquiry <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. BMET's charter lists online visa checks for Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and the UAE <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.
- A columnist wrote in November 2024 that BMET's smart card, issued since 2010 for Tk 250, was temporarily suspended in February 2024 because of repeated forgery by manpower brokers <sup>[N47 (2024)](#n47)</sup>.
- The expatriates' welfare minister said on 18 August 2026 that the whole process of sending workers abroad is to be put online, with more transparency on agencies' legitimacy and on foreign demand for workers <sup>[N48](#n48)</sup>.
- In August 2026 a Civil Aviation Authority deputy director said passengers often arrive at the airport with fake BMET cards, fake visas or other fake papers, and that 24 agencies working at the airport, including immigration and the airlines, check these together <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

**Check it is real** at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check <sup>[15](#o15)</sup>. The welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards before departure <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

<a id="departure-day-at-the-airport"></a>

## 6 Departure day at the airport

<a id="buying-the-ticket"></a>

### 6.1 Buying the ticket

Migrants buy 60% of the tickets on Bangladesh's international flights, and research by the ticketing company Go Zayan found that 32% of migrant workers had been cheated at least once buying one and that they often pay 20% above the market price, its founder said in 2025 <sup>[N49](#n49)</sup>. A Ministry of Civil Aviation and Tourism circular of 11 February 2025, published as a notification on 18 February, set these rules <sup>[N50](#n50), [N51](#n51)</sup>:
- every booking, group bookings included, carries the passenger's name, passport number and a passport photocopy, and is cancelled automatically after 72 hours if it is not issued as a ticket in that name within three days <sup>[N50](#n50)</sup>;
- no airline or travel agency may sell above the fare approved by CAAB (the Civil Aviation Authority of Bangladesh), and an agency must give the passenger the ticket at the airline's set price with a sale receipt <sup>[N50](#n50)</sup>;
- an agency whose hoarding of surplus tickets for resale through other agents pushes prices up has its registration suspended or cancelled <sup>[N50](#n50)</sup>;
- airlines and agencies are to arrange special fares for workers going abroad <sup>[N50](#n50)</sup>.

A Prothom Alo editorial of April 2025 said the circular broke the ticket syndicate, but that tickets from Bangladesh still cost more than from other countries because of NBR (National Board of Revenue) taxes and other charges <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>. Its figures <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>:
- **Tax on the route, by airlines' account:** Tk 9,890 a passenger collected by Bangladesh on flights from Dhaka to Singapore, Dubai, Oman, Sharjah or Kuala Lumpur, against Tk 2,568 taken by Malaysia, Tk 5,878 by Singapore, Tk 3,879 by Oman and Tk 4,332 by Dubai and Sharjah on the same routes.
- **Travel tax on top:** Tk 2,000 to a SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation) country, Tk 4,000 to any other.
- **Who pays it:** 80% of air passengers in Bangladesh are migrant workers.

Two workers' return tickets, as the editorial reported them:
- **Oman:** a single ticket home, bought four months earlier, cost Tk 20,000; the single ticket back, bought in Bangladesh, cost Tk 32,000 <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>.
- **Malaysia:** a single ticket home, bought three months earlier, cost RM 1,000 (Tk 25,000); in Bangladesh he was asked Tk 40,000 to 45,000 for the return, and he flew back on AirAsia on a cheaper ticket bought through a friend in Malaysia <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>.

The president of ATAB (the Association of Travel Agents of Bangladesh), Abdus Salam Aref, said the government needs to monitor airlines' ticket sales and enforce the law against irregularities <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>.

Since 19 November 2025 a ticket of any price can be bought from home on any card enabled for international use, within the card's foreign-currency limit; the USD 300 limit on each foreign-currency transaction no longer applies to air tickets <sup>[N53](#n53)</sup>.

<a id="who-checks-what"></a>

### 6.2 Who checks what

1. <a id="who-checks-what-item-1"></a>**Papers to carry.** BMET's checklist: valid passport, work visa, medical certificate, demand letter, the contract attested by the destination's embassy, the agency's name and phone, the BMET smart card and the air ticket <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>. Keep one set of copies at home and three with you <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>.
2. <a id="who-checks-what-item-2"></a>**Airline check-in.** Air Arabia requires passengers from Bangladesh holding a copy of a UAE visa or e-visa to have an "OK to Board" message on the booking, done at least 24 hours before departure for AED 15 to 20 <sup>[17](#o17)</sup>.
3. <a id="who-checks-what-item-3"></a>**The welfare desk.** The Probashi Kallyan (expatriate welfare) desk sits directly opposite immigration at Dhaka; a worker with a BMET card who gets its seal clears immigration faster, according to the airport <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>. The desks check smart cards and help fill boarding cards <sup>[3](#o3), [5](#o5)</sup>.
4. <a id="who-checks-what-item-4"></a>**SB screening.** In 2021, before the immigration counter, SB officers at a separate desk held the passports of first-time Middle East travellers and interviewed them <sup>[N54 (2021)](#n54)</sup>.
5. <a id="who-checks-what-item-5"></a>**Immigration counter.** Counters are open 24 hours; the officer stamps the passport and may ask about your travel plans <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. In March 2025 Dhaka had 42 departure and 48 arrival immigration counters, staffed by more than 500 police in four shifts <sup>[N55](#n55)</sup>. Its 26 e-gates, meant to clear an e-passport holder in 18 seconds, were mostly out of order, and passengers who used one still had to go through the normal counter <sup>[N55](#n55)</sup>.

TIB found in 2017 that some workers are handed their job contract only on reaching the airport <sup>[N4 (2017)](#n4)</sup>. A Canada-based Bangladeshi wrote in October 2024 that some airport officials fill in low-skilled workers' checking forms for money <sup>[N56 (2024)](#n56)</sup>. A UNDP Bangladesh gender expert said in May 2026 that women workers are often seen standing confused at the airport with no idea what to do, and that the same goes for men <sup>[N57](#n57)</sup>.

Besides SB immigration, the air force, the police, the Airport Armed Police Battalion (APBn, at the airport since 2013) and an anti-human-trafficking investigation team work at the airport, APBn's DIG (Deputy Inspector General) for operations said in August 2026 <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. A Home Ministry joint secretary said at a July 2026 roundtable that the Passenger Name Record and Advance Passenger Information systems, which give the authorities airline passenger data, are being expanded at airports to detect fake travel documents <sup>[N58](#n58)</sup>. From September 2024 to 8 March 2025 immigration police's Document Analysis Centre at Dhaka airport detected 46 fake visas, 5 residency cards, 4 fake or tampered passports and 30 impostors travelling on another person's passport details with their own photo <sup>[N55](#n55)</sup>. At Sylhet's Osmani airport, APBn said in April 2026 that an active group of traffickers and smugglers works the airport area and tries to mislead passengers with inducements, and that rings use first-time travellers as easy targets to carry drugs or smuggled goods (see "What should I carry?" in the FAQ doc) <sup>[N59](#n59), [N15](#n15)</sup>. Its unit there should have more than 300 members but has about 100, with intelligence and plain-clothes members deployed to stop anyone luring passengers <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

A separate migration counter for migrant workers, required by the 2016 expatriates' welfare and overseas employment policy, was set up by the interim government in 2024, a columnist wrote that November <sup>[N47 (2024)](#n47)</sup>. A Probashi (migrant) lounge was opened in the presence of the Chief Adviser <sup>[N60](#n60)</sup>; the expatriates' welfare adviser said in April 2025 that it offers food at up to 30% lower prices, free Wi-Fi, free phone calls and mobile charging, and that harassment of migrants at the airport had stopped <sup>[N61](#n61)</sup>. A reader's column in January 2025 called the lounge not enough <sup>[N60](#n60)</sup>. On 8 October 2026 the parliamentary standing committee on the expatriates' welfare ministry recommended keeping watch so that migrant workers suffer no neglect or harassment at the airport <sup>[N62](#n62)</sup>.

### 6.3 When the flight is disrupted

**War in the Middle East, 2026.** After the US and Israel attacked Iran on 28 February 2026, 54 Dhaka-Middle East flights were cancelled by noon on 1 March and about 102 in three days, leaving several thousand Bangladeshis stranded at Dhaka and Middle East airports <sup>[N63](#n63), [N64](#n64), [N65](#n65)</sup>. Many stranded passengers said they got no food or lodging; the expatriates' welfare minister said no such complaint had reached him, that food had been given and lodging would be, and that a 24-hour hotline was open <sup>[N63](#n63)</sup>. The ministry's senior secretary said in March 2026 that people whose flights were cancelled were being registered through hotline 16135 and would be contacted when flights resumed, and that the government would ask for visa extensions for those whose visas had expired <sup>[N66](#n66)</sup>. The government had asked the countries to let intending and stranded workers enter once things improved, some gave positive assurances, and it promised to arrange travel quickly once flights resumed <sup>[N67](#n67), [N68](#n68)</sup>. Many whose visas were running out faced uncertainty over the jobs they were going to <sup>[N64](#n64)</sup>.

**Weather on the islands.** Cyclone Remal stopped Sandwip-Chattogram sea crossings in late May 2024, stranding people whose visas or tickets were about to expire <sup>[N69 (2024)](#n69)</sup>. On 28 May a speedboat ran, at the request of the UNO (upazila nirbahi officer, the sub-district's chief administrator), only for patients and people going abroad; the UNO said boats must stop while weather signal 3 is up <sup>[N69 (2024)](#n69)</sup>. On 16 June 2025, with crossings banned, a speedboat left Guptachhara ghat for Kumira under pressure from passengers, including some who would otherwise miss their flights; its owner said it sailed because they insisted <sup>[N70](#n70)</sup>. Passengers said it ran out of fuel and took in water through a cracked hull, and the owner denied that it ran out of fuel <sup>[N70](#n70)</sup>. It was stranded in the channel, and its passengers came ashore at Akilpur, Sitakunda, around 2 pm <sup>[N70](#n70)</sup>.

<a id="offloading-who-decides-and-on-what-law"></a>

### 6.4 Offloading: who decides and on what law

Immigration at Bangladesh's airports is run by the Special Branch of Bangladesh Police under the Ministry of Home Affairs, headed by an Additional Inspector General of Police (Additional IGP) <sup>[4](#o4), [20](#o20)</sup>. "Offloading" is immigration's refusal to let a checked-in passenger leave; in cases reported in 2021 it was marked with an "offload" stamp or note in the passport <sup>[N54 (2021)](#n54)</sup>. SB lists the laws it applies, including the Passport Order 1973 and the Emigration Ordinance 1982 and Rules 2002 <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. The Passport Order bars leaving without a valid passport or travel document, and lets a passport be impounded if a court has barred its holder from leaving <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. The Ordinance, as SB quotes it, makes emigration for work lawful for a citizen with a work permit, employment visa or appointment letter, or selected through a recognised agent, and punishes departing otherwise with up to one year's simple imprisonment or a Tk 5,000 fine <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. In the three women's case below, their lawyers argued that an adult woman's marital status, or travelling without a parent or guardian, cannot by itself be a lawful ground to stop her <sup>[N71](#n71)</sup>.

<a id="who-gets-stopped"></a>

### 6.5 Who gets stopped

Reported reasons include mismatched visas, clearance discrepancies, forged documents and conflicting answers at the interview <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. Cases show who else gets stopped:
- **Women travelling alone.** Women report officers asking whether their husband knows, and phoning husbands or parents to check <sup>[N72](#n72), [N73](#n73)</sup>. A rights activist says officers are most suspicious of women going to Thailand or Nepal <sup>[N72](#n72)</sup>. Sakia Haque, co-founder of the women's travel group Bhromonkonna-Travelettes of Bangladesh, wrote in September 2026 that women have for years been asked whether they are married, whether the husband let them go alone, and where they get so much money <sup>[N74](#n74)</sup>.
- **Nepal.** Police officers told Prothom Alo that Nepal has been flagged as a trafficking route and, because it gives visas on arrival, Nepal travellers' papers get special scrutiny <sup>[N72](#n72)</sup>. Salma Ali, chairperson of ATSEC South Asia and adviser to the Bangladesh National Women Lawyers' Association, said in July 2026 that a suspicious passenger can be spotted in minutes, and that a young woman bound for Nepal had recently been suspected and was, in her words, rescued and brought back <sup>[N58](#n58)</sup>.
- **Tourist-visa travellers going for work.** APBn's DIG (Operations) said in August 2026 that officers can often tell when someone on a tourist visa is really going for work, and then verify and, if needed, report or try to stop the person; passengers are also checked against a database of cases <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.
- **Spouse visas to closed markets.** A woman with BMET clearance going to her "husband" in Oman, where worker and housemaid visas are shut, had no registered nikahnama; the same spouse-visa approval had been used for five women <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.
- **Students and first-time visitors.** A student with a valid Thai visa was told his trip would be cancelled until he named his university <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. A first-time Malaysia traveller with a valid visa and BMET clearance was stopped and a GD (police general diary) started <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.
- **Risky destinations.** BMET was told to check visas strictly for Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. Immigration police link visa fraud by Bangladeshis to at least 23 countries, including Thailand, India, Malaysia and Saudi Arabia <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.

<a id="your-rights"></a>

### 6.9 Your rights

- The Constitution guarantees equality, non-discrimination by sex, protection of law, personal liberty and freedom of movement (Articles 27, 28, 31, 32 and 36); the three women's writ rests on these <sup>[N71](#n71)</sup>.
- On 29 September 2026 the High Court issued a rule asking why stopping them should not be declared unlawful, ordered the Additional IGP (immigration) to investigate and resolve their complaint within seven days and report to the court, and told immigration not to obstruct their future travel, subject to law <sup>[N7](#n7), [N71](#n71)</sup>.
- The court also asked why the government should not issue guidelines so adult women are not stopped for being unmarried or travelling without guardians <sup>[N71](#n71)</sup>.
- After the order, police formed a three-member committee to investigate the women's complaint and questioned them at the relevant police office in Rajarbagh, Dhaka, on 8 October 2026 for about six hours, their lawyer said <sup>[N75](#n75)</sup>.
- Withholding a would-be emigrant's travel documents for money is an offence under the Emigration Ordinance <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>.
- A Supreme Court advocate wrote in Prothom Alo in October 2026 that the Appellate Division has held that no citizen's lawful foreign travel can be stopped without a court order barring travel, and that a criminal case alone is not enough <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.
- By the same account, an officer may ask the purpose of the trip, the destination, where and with whom you will stay, whether you have a hotel booking, your plan, whether you have a return ticket, and to see enough foreign currency, cash, an international card or a bank solvency certificate or statement; whether you are married, why you travel alone, where your husband is, or whether a guardian knows are not relevant <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.

- A registered nikahnama for a spouse visa <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.
- By the advocate's account: a passport valid at least six months; for an employee, an office ID card and an NOC; for a businessperson, a trade licence; for a student, a student ID or a leave letter or permission from the college or university <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.
- For workers, the BMET card; the airport says getting the welfare desk's seal speeds immigration <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>.

<a id="if-you-are-offloaded"></a>

### 6.11 If you are offloaded

The three women were given no written document when they were sent out <sup>[N73](#n73)</sup>. The routes they used, SB's own and those a Supreme Court advocate lists are:

1. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-1"></a>**At the counter,** note the officer's name, rank or badge number, the time and the counter number, and tell the airport's complaint-settlement officer, the shift in-charge, the duty officer or the immigration OC, or call the airport immigration 24-hour hotline 01320007588; a complaint can also go to the executive magistrate's office at the airport, a Supreme Court advocate wrote in October 2026 <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-2"></a>**Complain to SB immigration** through the online form at immi.specialbranch.gov.bd/Your_Complain; for emergencies, SS Immigration +88-01713373071, or the OC (officer in charge) of Immigration at Dhaka airport +880-2-8914226, 01320005902 <sup>[22](#o22), [23](#o23)</sup>. SB also runs an Offload Management System (OFMS) online "for settling offloads" <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-3"></a>**Write to the Additional IGP (immigration)** asking for an investigation, preservation of CCTV footage, action and compensation, as the three women did in late August 2026 <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
4. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-4"></a>**If there is no reply,** a lawyer's legal notice, then a writ petition to the High Court <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. The advocate wrote that a complaint to the proper authority must come before a writ <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.
5. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-5"></a>**For discrimination or abuse,** complain free to the National Human Rights Commission (see [Where to complain](#where-to-complain)) <sup>[25 (2018)](#o25)</sup>.
6. <a id="if-you-are-offloaded-item-6"></a>**For a bribe demand,** the ACC (see [Where to complain](#where-to-complain)) <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>.

<a id="refused-entry-at-the-destination"></a>

## 7 Refused entry at the destination

Destinations also turn Bangladeshis back on arrival. Over 3,500 were refused entry and sent home in January to April 2025 <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. Border officers flag visitors with little money, unclear plans or conflicting papers <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.

- **Malaysia (KLIA, Kuala Lumpur International Airport).** Immigration refused 80 Bangladeshis with valid tourist visas in July 2025 for lacking "satisfactory justification", put 123 on the "Not to Land" list the same month, and refused 204 on 13 August and 98 on 15 August 2025 <sup>[N79](#n79), [N80](#n80), [N8](#n8), [N81](#n81)</sup>. Reasons given were fake hotel bookings, no return ticket, too little money and no clear purpose <sup>[N81](#n81), [N82](#n82)</sup>. Travellers are told to carry RM500 to 1,000, and officers may look at photos on phones <sup>[N83](#n83)</sup>. More than 200 refused at KLIA were deported in 2025 <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In 2024 a "counter setting" syndicate was reported to sell entry through bribed officers at RM200 to 2,500 a head; Malaysia's anti-corruption agency arrested 60 people, including 49 immigration officers <sup>[N84 (2024)](#n84)</sup>.
- **UAE.** The UAE tightened visitor and employment visas for Bangladeshis from July 2024 <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis did Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Oman refuse on arrival in 2025-26?
> - Who pays the return ticket of a worker refused on arrival: the airline, the agency or the worker?

<a id="coming-back"></a>

## 8 Coming back

<a id="who-comes-back-and-why"></a>

### 8.1 Who comes back and why

The BBS (Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics) Socio-Economic and Demographic Survey 2023, published in June 2024, counted migrants who had come home for good in the previous five years <sup>[N85 (2024)](#n85)</sup>:
- **Why they came back:** by choice 17.28%, visa expired 12.80%, sent back by the authorities 11.67%, health 9.24%, Covid 9.21%, to live with family 7.96%, lost the job 7.18% <sup>[N85 (2024)](#n85)</sup>.
- **Where from:** Saudi Arabia 38.37%, the UAE 14%, Malaysia 13.15%, Oman 9.20%, Qatar 5.67%, Kuwait 4.37%, Singapore 3.12% <sup>[N85 (2024)](#n85)</sup>.
- **Home division:** Chattogram 36.36%, Dhaka 35.25% <sup>[N85 (2024)](#n85)</sup>.
- **Schooling:** 42.28% had studied to class five to eight, and 25.38% had never been to school <sup>[N85 (2024)](#n85)</sup>.

A Wage Earners' Welfare Board joint director, speaking in December 2025 about RAISE (Recovery and Advancement of Informal Sector Employment, the returnee project run by the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and the Wage Earners' Welfare Board), said more than 4 lakh workers came back in 2020 <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>. By the 2025 count of the Migration Programme of BRAC (a Bangladeshi development organisation) about 5 lakh workers came back unexpectedly in six years, and a Bangladesh Open University researcher wrote in September 2026 that the rate in 2026 is much higher and that the Iran-United States war has pushed the number up <sup>[N87](#n87)</sup>. BRAC said in May 2025 that more than 67,000 women had been forced home by crises in the previous six years, many of them after abuse <sup>[N88](#n88)</sup>.

<a id="at-the-airport-the-welfare-desk"></a>

### 8.2 At the airport: the welfare desk

The Probashi Kallyan desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet, run by the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, meet returning workers free <sup>[5](#o5), [3](#o3)</sup>. They:
- give food and water to workers back from jail or on an outpass <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>;
- help with customs and report lost luggage to Lost and Found <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>;
- take complaints from workers abused or cheated abroad <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>;
- give Tk 5,000 to women back within a year after abuse or fraud <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>;
- may give money to workers back because of war, disaster or lay-off <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>;
- send sick returnees to hospital by ambulance and arrange a stay at the Wage Earners' Centre <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.

A deputy director of the Civil Aviation Authority of Bangladesh said in August 2026 that the airport's Probashi Kallyan desk is open 24 hours, and that departing passengers are offered luggage wrapping at low cost <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

Desks: Dhaka +88 02 8901040, +880 1971 430203, welfaredesk@gmail.com; Chattogram +880 2333300957, +880 1912542185; Sylhet 01321158330, 01973-898375 <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which desk numbers answer in 2026, given that BMET's charter gives Chattogram 01913631797 and Sylhet 01558277904 <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>?

<a id="deportees-and-outpass-holders"></a>

### 8.3 Deportees and outpass holders

Bangladesh missions issue outpasses (Travel Permits) to send home people without a usable passport; the Abu Dhabi embassy's labour wing, for example, visits jails and the deportation centre to find Bangladeshis and issues them <sup>[26](#o26)</sup>. In Malaysia it costs RM38 from 16 January 2026 <sup>[27](#o27)</sup>. Government agencies count only returnees who come on an outpass, recorded by the Welfare Board's airport desk; according to sources cited by Prothom Alo, 86,621 workers came back empty-handed in 2023, 2,902 of them women <sup>[N89 (2024)](#n89)</sup>; about 89,000 came back this way in 2024 <sup>[N90](#n90)</sup>. Airport sources counted 15,245 travel-pass returns in January to March 2026 and 8,215 in April alone <sup>[N91](#n91)</sup>.

- In 2020 at least 255 workers deported from Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain were jailed at Kashimpur after quarantine under section 54 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (arrest on suspicion), though most had been deported for overstaying or petty offences and pardoned <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10)</sup>.
- Nearly 70,000 Bangladeshi workers were deported from Saudi Arabia in 2022, mostly for lacking a valid iqama (residence permit) <sup>[N92 (2024)](#n92)</sup>.
- Hasan Gazi of Shyamnagar, Satkhira, went to Kuwait through India in 2018 on Tk 5.5 lakh borrowed from relatives, did various jobs for four years, was detained by police and sent home, and in 2025 was driving an auto-rickshaw in Keraniganj and living in the garage <sup>[N90](#n90)</sup>.
- More than 250 were deported from the US from early 2024 to December 2025; one said he was shackled for 75 hours before landing in Dhaka <sup>[N93](#n93)</sup>. BRAC says at least seven of one flight's 31 had gone to Brazil with BMET clearance and then entered the US irregularly via Mexico, and that such journeys cost Tk 30 to 35 lakh <sup>[N93](#n93)</sup>.
- About 5,000 have been returned from Europe under a 2017 agreement, most from Cyprus, Italy, Romania and France, though BRAC says it has received the most in recent years from Italy, Greece and Germany <sup>[N94](#n94), [N95](#n95)</sup>. On 23 October 2026 a chartered flight (PVG 9445) landed at Dhaka at 8:15 am with 35: 19 from Italy, 10 from Greece and 6 from Cyprus <sup>[N95](#n95)</sup>. BRAC's Migration Programme met them with emergency help, counselling and money to get home, working with Civil Aviation Security and the Probashi Kallyan desk <sup>[N94](#n94), [N95](#n95)</sup>. Later economic help follows counselling and training under the EU-BRAC Prottasha-2 project <sup>[N94](#n94)</sup>. The EU delegation's programme manager said in August 2026 that EU-funded reintegration work began in 2017 and that the projects run through BRAC and IOM are essentially a pilot <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.
- Bangladeshi workers found HIV-positive in Middle Eastern countries are sent home in haste without any treatment, a practice of many years, a Prothom Alo editorial said in February 2026; WHO (the World Health Organization) and the ILO (International Labour Organization) have said expelling someone only for HIV is discriminatory <sup>[N96](#n96)</sup>. The editorial said that at home medicine is free but counselling, mental-health support and livelihood help are very limited, that many are afraid to tell even their families, and that although an HIV-negative certificate is mandatory before going abroad, workers get no practical training on risk or sexual health <sup>[N96](#n96)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Are deportees questioned or held by police at Dhaka airport in 2026, and on what legal basis?
> - Does SB keep a record of deportation that affects a later passport or emigration clearance?

<a id="customs-and-luggage"></a>

### 8.4 Customs and luggage

Every arriving passenger fills in the baggage declaration (Schedule 1) or submits it online; with nothing dutiable you use the green channel, where up to 5% of bags are scanned at random <sup>[28](#o28)</sup>. If by mistake or another unavoidable reason you could not declare, you can declare within 30 days of arrival on the Schedule 1 form to a customs officer not below Assistant Commissioner, under rule 8(3) of the Baggage Rules 2025 <sup>[28](#o28)</sup>. A passenger aged 12 or over may bring 65 kg duty-free plus 35 kg of clothes and personal items <sup>[28](#o28)</sup>. The welfare desk helps with customs and lost luggage <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>. A Civil Aviation Authority deputy director said in August 2026 that its investigations found that in 95% of cases of the cut or damaged luggage seen in social-media videos, the damage happened at the airport where the journey began, not in Bangladesh or in transit <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. A Canada-based Bangladeshi wrote in October 2024 that returning workers' bags are cut open and goods stolen <sup>[N56 (2024)](#n56)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Where does a returnee complain about a customs officer demanding money: NBR (the National Board of Revenue), the customs house, or the welfare desk?

<a id="on-the-road-from-the-airport"></a>

### 8.5 On the road from the airport

On 19 May 2024 at about 3 pm, Abdul Khalek, back from Saudi Arabia, was being driven from Shah Amanat International Airport in Chattogram when Aminul Islam, a sub-inspector of Khulshi police station who was not on duty, stopped his car at Tiger Pass and with two associates took 16 bhori of gold, police said <sup>[N97 (2024)](#n97)</sup>. By the police account they drove him around the city and tried to put him out on the Akhtaruzzaman Chowdhury flyover; he held on to the officer's clothes, a crowd gathered, and Panchlaish police detained the sub-inspector and one associate while the other fled <sup>[N97 (2024)](#n97)</sup>. The city police said the sub-inspector was suspended and a case was being filed against him <sup>[N97 (2024)](#n97)</sup>.

Dubai returnee Jamal Hossain said he and his friend Sohel landed at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, Dhaka, at about 1:30 am on 4 November 2026 and set off for Kanchan in the private car of Shahin, a driver they already knew <sup>[N98](#n98)</sup>. At about 3 am, in the Shamu Market area of the Purbachal 300-feet road, Shahin helped another car block theirs; armed men took the two hostage and looted 18 bhori of gold jewellery brought from Dubai, five phones, a laptop and a tab, worth about Tk 46 lakh by their account, then tied their hands and feet, taped their mouths and left them in the trade fair area of the Dhaka Bypass road <sup>[N98](#n98)</sup>. Shahin then called the national emergency number 999 to cover himself; police rescued the two, caught Shahin, his brother Sohel and Ayub of Charpara, Rupganj, as they tried to flee with the goods, and recovered 8.5 bhori of the gold <sup>[N98](#n98)</sup>. A case was filed at Rupganj police station, the Purbachal police camp's inspector said <sup>[N98](#n98)</sup>.

<a id="after-deportation-bans-on-going-back"></a>

### 8.6 After deportation: bans on going back

- **Kuwait:** deportees are fingerprinted at the airport and banned for life <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.
- **Malaysia:** deported undocumented migrants are blacklisted for 1 to 5 years, and serious cases for life <sup>[N12 (2017)](#n12)</sup>; all 140 deported from KLIA in September 2026 were blacklisted <sup>[N99](#n99)</sup>.
- **UAE:** a deportee may return only with permission of the ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security) chairman <sup>[29 (2021)](#o29)</sup>.
- **Qatar:** someone deported by court judgment may return only by the Minister's decision <sup>[30 (2015)](#o30)</sup>.
- **Italy:** a re-entry ban of 3 to 5 years, longer in some cases, entered in the Schengen Information System, so it also bars entry to EU and other Schengen states <sup>[31](#o31)</sup>.

<a id="help-after-you-get-home"></a>

### 8.7 Help after you get home

**Where to apply.** The Minister said in August 2026 that a returnee who is jobless can apply to the Ministry or the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, and that the government plans to give financial and logistic support suited to the person's occupation and to monitor it <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The government's RAISE project says it has brought 2,53,000 returnees under its reintegration programme and gives referral, training and counselling through 35 district offices, working with seven NGOs including BRAC and IOM (the International Organization for Migration) <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. A Welfare Board official said in December 2025 that the project registers, counsels and gives incentives to its returnees, about 7% of whom are women, and that it could not rehabilitate everyone who came back <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>. In May 2025 its project director said the government had identified and served about 31,000 returnee women, and a ministry additional secretary said the government was working to improve services for returnee women from the airport onward <sup>[N88](#n88)</sup>. The government's Reintegration Policy for Returnee Migrant Workers 2024 includes gender, according to RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a research group) <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>. The chairperson of the SME (small and medium enterprise) Foundation said in May 2025 that most returnees have no skills and that the foundation would work on entrepreneurship, training and finance for returnee women; BRAC said its Network for Bangladeshi Women Migrants would bring organisations together to serve them <sup>[N88](#n88)</sup>. The SME Foundation trains returnees in business planning, financial literacy, trade licences, TIN (tax identification number), BSTI (Bangladesh Standards and Testing Institution) certificates and markets, work its managing director called still at a very early stage <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

**Loans.** PKB's returnee loans are the rehabilitation, women's rehabilitation, special rehabilitation and self-employment loans; it also lends where a migrant's family runs a business at home, and has lent Tk 702 crore to 24,971 people under them, its deputy managing director said in August 2026 <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. PKB's acting regional head in Sylhet said in April 2026 that the bank lends to returnees who suffered losses at 8% interest, but that in many cases this is not succeeding <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>. Under an agreement between the NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) and PKB, returnees with a skills certificate can get a collateral-free loan of up to Tk 5 lakh, and RPL (recognition of prior learning, certifying skills learnt on the job) is being extended to workers in Saudi Arabia and Singapore, an NSDA director said in August 2026 <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. An ILO (International Labour Organization) officer said in August 2026 that 94% of returnees want to start a business but find loans hard to get because of paperwork, and that Karmasangsthan Bank now lends to them across the country at 7% interest on just three documents <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. PKB's own rehabilitation-loan page (September 2026) gives 9% simple interest, and up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does PKB lend a returnee with an NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) skills certificate up to Tk 5 lakh without collateral, as an NSDA director said in August 2026, beyond the Tk 3 lakh on PKB's loan page <sup>[N16](#n16), [32](#o32)</sup>?
> - Which three documents does Karmasangsthan Bank ask returnees for, and up to what amount <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>?

**What reaches people.** The ILO officer said the government recently drew up a reintegration policy for returnees, that many people don't know PKB lends, and that support is usually project-based and stops when the project ends <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. BRAC's Migration Programme said many people return empty-handed after finding no work, and many have no savings even after 20 to 25 years abroad <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. RAISE's project director said many women returnees miss out on support because their families sent them abroad but suspect and undervalue them on return, and that the project is considering a union-level officer to record which woman has returned to which house and when <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The general secretary of the Ovibashi Nari Sramik Federation said in May 2026 that women who come home after harassment and abuse get hardly any rehabilitation help and struggle to fit back into family and society, and that those returning at 45 to 50 are in a pitiable state; another speaker, Sheikh Romana, said many women return after abuse with a child, and questioned how many returnees really get the help that exists <sup>[N57](#n57)</sup>. Three women back from Jordan and Saudi Arabia told a December 2025 roundtable that all had paid a dalal to go abroad and had heard much negative talk in society since returning; one, Asma Begum, runs a tea stall in Dhaka <sup>[N86](#n86)</sup>.

## 9 Where to complain

- **Offloading by immigration:** SB's online complaint form at immi.specialbranch.gov.bd/Your_Complain; SS Immigration +88-01713373071 <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>. At the airport: OC Immigration, Dhaka, +880-2-8914226, 01320005902; SSP (Senior Superintendent of Police) Chattogram airport 01320005108; SSP Sylhet airport 01320005107; Additional DIG (Deputy Inspector General) Immigration 01320005035 <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>. Airport immigration 24-hour hotline, as given by a Supreme Court advocate in October 2026: 01320007588 <sup>[N78](#n78)</sup>.
- **Bribes, anywhere in government:** ACC hotline 106, toll-free; in writing to the Chairman, Anti-Corruption Commission, 1 Segunbagicha, Dhaka, or a divisional director <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>.
- **Rights violations by any state body:** the National Human Rights Commission, online at complaint.nhrc.org.bd, or on its form or plain paper by post, fax, email or in person, with any documents, photos or video; free; it replies within seven days if the case is outside its remit, otherwise it investigates and mediates <sup>[25 (2018)](#o25)</sup>.
- **BMET and agencies:** see BMET clearance and the smart card <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.
- **Medical centres (Malaysia):** secretary@probashi.gov.bd <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.
- **Government offices generally:** the Cabinet Division's GRS at grs.gov.bd <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

In TIB's 2025 survey, 61.3% of households that met corruption did not complain, about half of all households did not know where or how to complain, and only 29.5% had heard of the ACC and 1.4% of the GRS <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
