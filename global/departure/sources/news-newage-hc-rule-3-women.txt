                  HC issues rule over blocking 3 women from travelling to Nepal                

Staff Correspondent

                      29 September, 2026, 16:10                    

                        Print

The High Court on Tuesday issued a rule asking the government authorities to explain why the legality of alleged obstruction of three adult women from travelling to Nepal recently despite having valid passports, visas or entry permits and air tickets.

The court also directed the additional inspector general of police concerned to dispose of the women’s complaints within seven days and submit a compliance report. The three women sought legal action against the immigration officers responsible for their obstruction on travelling Nepal.

It further directed the immigration authorities not to obstruct the petitioners from travelling abroad in the future, subject to law.

The bench of Justice Kamrul Kader and Justice Fatema Anwar passed the order after hearing a writ petition filed by the three women.

In the rule, the court wanted to know why the actions preventing the petitioners from travelling abroad should not be declared to have been taken without lawful authority.

The court also asked why the authorities should not be directed to formulate and issue appropriate guidelines to ensure that adult women are not prevented from making lawful foreign trips merely because they are unmarried, travelling without parents or guardians, or on any similar gender-based consideration.

The petitioners named the Ministry of Home Affairs, the inspector general of police, an additional inspector general of police, the Police Headquarters and concerned immigration officials at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport as respondents.

According to the writ petition, there was no court order or other legal restriction preventing the petitioners from leaving the country.

They alleged that they were stopped at the airport despite having all valid travel documents.

The petitioners argued that an adult woman’s marital status or whether she is travelling with her parents or guardian cannot, by itself, constitute a lawful ground to restrict her right to travel abroad.

The writ petition said such actions raise questions of compliance with Articles 27 and 28 of the Constitution, which guarantee equality before law and protection against discrimination on grounds including sex.

It also raised issues under Articles 31 and 32 concerning protection of law and personal liberty, and Article 36 concerning freedom of movement.

Supreme Court lawyer Ishrat Hasan appeared for the petitioners, assisted by lawyer Tanzila Rahman Jui.
