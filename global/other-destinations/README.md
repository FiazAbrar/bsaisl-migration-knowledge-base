## 1 In one minute

For work, the reporting shows Bangladeshis going to Iraq through relatives already there, some working irregularly, with deaths from fire, missiles, poisoning and murder over unpaid wages <sup>[N1](#n1), [N2](#n2), [N3](#n3), [N4](#n4)</sup>; Bangladesh and Iraq signed an MoU (memorandum of understanding) in October 2025 for recruitment on embassy-attested demand letters <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. In November 2025 Bangladesh asked Bahrain to reopen visas for Bangladeshis, and Bahrain said it was reopening them step by step, but in July 2026 new labour visas were still frozen, as they had been since 2018 <sup>[N6](#n6), [N7](#n7)</sup>. Fiji takes small numbers through BOESL, the government recruiting company <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>, and Mauritius agreed in principle in June 2026 to reopen its closed market <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In one South Africa case, brokers took an 18-year-old overland through Ethiopia and Zimbabwe and news of his death came the night he arrived, in February 2026 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. In Spain, Portugal and France there are settled communities; Spain's 2026 regularisation could cover about 20,000 Bangladeshis who arrived before 1 January 2026 <sup>[N11](#n11), [N12](#n12)</sup>, while Portugal has made residence renewals harder <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

For study, Australia, Ireland, Germany, Austria, Taiwan, Denmark, New Zealand, Cyprus, Egypt and China all take Bangladeshi students, several of them through embassies in New Delhi <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14), [N15 (2024)](#n15), [N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>. Australia and Denmark tightened student rules in 2025-26; both limited family members, Australia also targeted course-hopping, and Denmark named Bangladesh and Nepal outright, and in January 2026 Australia put Bangladesh in its highest-risk student visa category <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18), [N19](#n19)</sup>. What goes wrong most in this reporting: a family pays a dalal for "Australia" and the young man is flown to Nepal or Kazakhstan, held and beaten for more money, and returned with the family in debt <sup>[N20](#n20), [N21](#n21)</sup>; and workers die abroad, after which the body takes weeks or months to come home <sup>[N2](#n2), [N3](#n3)</sup>.

## 2 Australia

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go"></a>

### 2.1 Is it open, and how people go

Australia granted student visas to more than 166,000 of 179,000 applicants (all nationalities) in 2022-23 <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>. In May 2024 the two foreign ministers discussed skilled workers from Bangladesh and seasonal work for Bangladeshis, and Bangladesh asked for help under TAFE (Australia's Technical and Further Education system) to train people at its TTCs (Technical Training Centres) <sup>[N23 (2024)](#n23)</sup>. For what the real work visas need and how Australia work-visa frauds operate, see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc.

**What the student visa (subclass 500) needs, from the Department of Home Affairs:**

- **Where to apply.** From outside Australia, unless you already hold an eligible visa and meet an exemption that lets you apply inside <sup>[1](#o1), [2](#o2)</sup>.
- **Enrolment and insurance.** A valid Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from the institution, and Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) unless exempt <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. The visa lasts up to 6 years, in line with the course <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Family.** You may not bring a partner or children unless exempt <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Money to show:** travel AUD 2,000 <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>; 12 months of course fees <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>; 12 months' living costs of AUD 29,710 for you, AUD 10,394 for a partner and AUD 4,449 for a child <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. Instead of savings you can show that your parents or partner earned at least AUD 87,856 in the last 12 months (AUD 102,500 if family come with you), proved with tax assessments under 12 months old; bank statements or employer letters are not accepted for this <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. If the document checklist asks for financial evidence and you don't attach it, the visa can be refused without a further request <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **English.** IELTS 6.0, or 5.5 with at least 10 weeks of ELICOS (English Language Intensive Courses for Overseas Students) packaged with the course, or 5.0 with at least 20 weeks <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>. Tests taken wholly online or at home are not accepted <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Genuine student answers.** The online form asks questions on the genuine student criteria, each answered in English in up to 150 words <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **Fee.** AUD 2,500 from 1 July 2026, up from AUD 2,000 <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

**Student rules as reported:**

- **Genuine student test.** Australia brought in a "genuine student" requirement in March 2024, aimed at separating real study from using the student visa mainly to work or stay, a Bangladeshi academic at Charles Darwin University wrote in October 2026 <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. By August 2026 the government had also raised the minimum English score and the funds to be shown in the bank; its bill to cap student numbers was blocked in the upper house <sup>[N25](#n25)</sup>.
- **Highest-risk listing.** On 8 January 2026 Australia moved Bangladesh, with India, Nepal and Bhutan, into Evidence Level 3, the highest-risk category of its Simplified Student Visa Framework (SSVF), citing "emerging integrity issues", meaning fraud and irregularities <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. A country's level is set from its visa refusal rate for fraud or other reasons, cancellation rate, the share of student visa holders who become unlawful non-citizens, and the share who later claim refugee status <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. At Level 3 applicants and institutions must submit detailed papers on finances and academic history, and applicants must show funds for their own and their family's travel, 12 months' living costs, tuition and dependants' schooling <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. Abul Rizvi, a former deputy secretary of Australia's immigration department, said visa officers then check papers directly instead of relying on the institution, may phone the institution to verify transcripts and the bank to verify statements, and that processing will take much longer and refusals will rise <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. About 8,000 Bangladeshi students applied in 2025, against an estimated 2,000 to 2,500 a year before <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>.
- **What the papers must show since the listing,** as Kawsar Khan, an immigration lawyer and Prothom Alo's Sydney correspondent, described it in February 2026:
  - money in the bank for less than six months needs its specific source shown, where three months' record used to be enough <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>;
  - all family income must come through the bank, with at least six months' statements; cash income is not accepted <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>;
  - money from selling land, investments or leases needs a bank cheque or transfer as proof; an affidavit or declaration is not enough <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>;
  - postgraduate applicants must submit salary statements or slips for their work experience, and for a cash salary the employer's genuineness and tax payments are checked <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>;
  - interviews may be long, with detailed questions on the course, housing plans, the chosen city, career goals and family ties <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.
  He wrote that each paper may be checked separately, including with the bank, so visas may take much longer, and that families who deal in cash for land or business may be unable to apply for lack of bank proof <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.
- **Work while studying.** Up to 48 hours a fortnight while the course is in session; master's by research and doctoral students and their families have no limit <sup>[1](#o1)</sup>.
- **After graduating.** The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) gives two years after a bachelor's or coursework master's and three after a research master's or PhD, with no job guarantee <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>. The applicant must be 35 or under (with exceptions), have held a student visa in the last 6 months, have finished a degree at an approved Australian institution, meet health and character rules, hold health insurance and owe no debt to the Australian government <sup>[2](#o2), [N27](#n27)</sup>. From 13 November 2026 new rules limit which family members can be included <sup>[2](#o2)</sup>. Holders may work in any job, whatever they studied <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. The fee is AUD 5,750 from 1 July 2026, plus the medical, police certificate and biometrics <sup>[3](#o3), [N27](#n27)</sup>.
- **Australia Awards.** A scholarship from Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade for master's study in fields including health, development, environment, trade, public policy, economics, governance, infrastructure, science and engineering <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>. It pays full tuition, books and study costs, a return economy fare, living costs and health insurance, and fieldwork costs on some courses <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>. Bangladeshi citizens over 18 may apply, except those with Australian citizenship, those engaged or married to an Australian citizen and those linked to military service; IELTS (the International English Language Testing System) must be at least 6.5 with no band below 6 <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>. In 2024 applications ran from 1 February to 30 April at australiaawardsbangladesh.org/opportunities <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>.
- **Scholarships named in 2025.** The Melbourne international undergraduate scholarship (up to 10,000 dollars off first-year fees) and the UTS Vice-Chancellor's international scholarship (up to 50% of costs) <sup>[N29](#n29)</sup>.

**The September 2026 tightening.** Minister for home affairs and immigration Tony Burke announced <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>:

- net migration cut to 245,000 in 2026-27 and 225,000 a year after that <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- an end to "visa hopping", meaning moving to a lower-level course after finishing study or switching visas again and again <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- no expansion of students bringing family, with exemptions for those already studying in Australia, applicants from Pacific and Southeast Asian countries, and PhD students, who can bring family <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- a "no further stay" condition on all tourist visas, which in effect ends going as a tourist and applying for another visa inside Australia <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- faster removal of people whose visas have expired <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- a lottery for second- and third-year Working Holiday visas in farming and services, and priority for skilled migration in construction, health care and farming <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>;
- powers to cancel visas quickly and deport migrants involved in violence against children, domestic violence or hate crimes <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.

**From 2 October 2026,** people in Australia on certain temporary visas can no longer apply for a student visa from inside the country; they must apply from outside it <sup>[2](#o2), [N30](#n30)</sup>. They may still apply inside Australia if they need up to 12 more months to finish their current main course at the same institution, or if they move up to a higher degree (bachelor's to master's, a PhD), study at school level or hold a government scholarship <sup>[N30](#n30)</sup>. No family member can be added to a student visa after it is granted <sup>[N30](#n30)</sup>. From 5 October 2026 no new international students may enrol in the Graduate Diploma of Management; the government said some institutions tried to bring forward the start dates of about 1,500 students on hearing of the ban, and Julian Hill, the assistant minister for international education, citizenship and migration, said an institution that tried to evade the rules would lose its registration <sup>[N30](#n30)</sup>. He said migration professionals who knowingly lodge applications with no real or legal basis face heavy penalties, including loss of their registration, and that studying two full-time courses at once could put a student's current and future visas at risk <sup>[N30](#n30)</sup>. A Bangladeshi student in Australia said going home to apply again takes time and puts heavy financial pressure on the family <sup>[N30](#n30)</sup>.

The academic quoted above wrote that bridging visas, new applications and reviews of visa decisions all have legal conditions, and the belief that using them lets a person stay indefinitely is wrong <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. He gave as an example a student who gets a visa on admission to a well-known university, then moves on arrival to a cheaper institution with no intention of studying at the first <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. In August 2026 Pauline Hanson, leader of the far-right One Nation party, called for stopping students' spouses and families from coming at all <sup>[N25](#n25)</sup>. For how these changes fit the wider pattern, see "Student visas used as work routes" in the Illegal routes doc.

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays"></a>

### 2.2 What it costs and pays

A Bangladeshi doctoral researcher at Curtin University wrote in August 2026 that in almost every Australian city a large share of Bangladeshi students do hard work alongside study, in restaurants, cleaning companies, ride-sharing or delivery, to meet tuition and living costs <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>. He described a Sri Lankan student in Perth who broke his hand at work and was dismissed after missing a few days <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>.

<a id="what-goes-wrong"></a>

### 2.3 What goes wrong

The commonest case in this reporting is a family paying a dalal for Australia and the young man being flown somewhere else and held for ransom <sup>[N20](#n20), [N21](#n21)</sup>. Both cases below are from Bagherpara, Jashore, and both families filed cases at Jashore's Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Prevention and Suppression Tribunal-1 <sup>[N20](#n20), [N21](#n21)</sup>. For the pattern, see "A flight to a different country first" in the Dalal scams doc.

- **Sakib Hossain, Nepal, September 2025 to May 2026.** His father, a van driver, paid a broker Tk 15 lakh to send Sakib, 21, to Australia; on 14 September 2025 he was taken to Kathmandu instead <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Sakib said a broker's man held the group's passports at Dhaka airport, and at Kathmandu took his passport and about Tk 80,000 in dollars <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. He said he was locked in a hotel room, beaten daily for money and made to repeat scripted words on calls home with a knife beside him; the captors took Tk 1.5 lakh more and kept beating him <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. After four months he was moved to a basement, where his stomach was slashed with a knife <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. He was finally taken by bus to Siliguri and Jalpaiguri in India and pushed into Bangladesh through a culvert under the fence at the Nilphamari border <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. His father had sold all land but the homestead and his van and borrowed Tk 12.5 lakh from five NGOs and savings groups; creditors come daily <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. The case filed on 16 February 2026 names two men from the village, who were absconding in May <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- **Sohel Rana, Kazakhstan, January to April 2026.** Sohel, who had run two restaurants in Malaysia, came home in November 2024 on a broker's promise of Europe; the broker, Nazma Akter alias Nurjahan, then agreed to send him to Australia for a job at 1,000 dollars a month <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. Under a Tk 16 lakh deal he paid Tk 12 lakh, from savings, money borrowed against paddy, an NGO loan and other debts <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. He was flown on 15 January 2026 to Dubai on a one-month business visa and the next day to Kazakhstan <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. The broker's representative took his and two other Bangladeshis' passports, drove them about 900 km to Shymkent, locked them in a metal-smelting factory, demanded Tk 30 lakh and threatened to kill them, giving one piece of bread a day <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. He escaped over the wall with one companion, texted a Bangladesh immigration officer on 14 April, paid a South African lawyer Tk 1.2 lakh plus Tk 1 lakh for the fare, and came home on a travel permit on 20 April <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. The case, recorded on 6 April, names Md Kaiyum, 48, and Nazma Akter, 45, of Keraniganj, Dhaka; police said Kaiyum was abroad and Nazma was being sought, and her phone was off <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. Sohel owes about Tk 15 lakh <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>.

## 3 Ireland

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-1"></a>

### 3.1 Is it open, and how people go

Bangladeshis go to Ireland as students and, rarely, as doctors and nurses <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14), [N32](#n32)</sup>.

**The student visa.** Ireland has no visa office in Bangladesh; the Embassy of Ireland in New Delhi handles applications from people living in Bangladesh, at Thapar House, 124 Janpath Road, Connaught Place, New Delhi 110001, phone +91 11 4940 3200 <sup>[5](#o5), [6](#o6)</sup>.

1. Get an offer letter from an Irish institution, then fill in the long-stay Type D visa form (for stays over 90 days) on AVATS Online, Ireland's visa website; print and sign it <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14), [5](#o5)</sup>. Pay the college by bank or approved money transfer: Irish colleges may not take fees in cash, and must publish the names of the recruitment agents they use <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>.
2. Gather the papers, as an August 2024 guide listed them: proof of tuition fee payment (at least 6,000 euros, or the full fee at some institutions), a personal bank statement of at least 10,000 euros or a sponsor's signed letter and national ID copy, the financial summary form, private medical insurance of at least 25,000 euros, and a police clearance certificate <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. Private medical insurance is compulsory for every non-EEA (European Economic Area) student <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>. All papers must be originals, and anything in Bangla needs a full English translation <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
3. Buy an Indian-rupee demand draft made out to the Embassy of Ireland, from Sonali Bank, BRAC Bank or Pubali Bank: 5,300 rupees for a single-entry visa or 9,000 for multiple entry <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>. The fee is not refunded if the visa is refused <sup>[8](#o8)</sup>.
4. Post the form, papers and draft to the embassy in New Delhi, or to a third-party agent in Kolkata who lodges them at VFS Kolkata <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.
5. Wait 30 working days to two calendar months <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. The embassy keeps the passport until the decision; you then arrange to collect it from VFS in Delhi or Kolkata <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>. If the visa is refused with a right of appeal, the refusal letter explains how to appeal <sup>[5](#o5)</sup>.

**Work.** On a Stamp 2 student permission you may work up to 20 hours a week in term and up to 40 hours a week in June, July, August and September and from 15 December to 15 January <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>. Registering the permission in Ireland costs 300 euros <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>. Working needs a PPS (Personal Public Service) number, obtained on being hired <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. After graduating, the Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) lets you stay to look for graduate work and work 40 hours a week without an employment permit: 12 months after a Level 8 award (honours bachelor's), and 12 months renewable for 12 more after a Level 9 award (master's) or above if you show you have been looking for graduate work <sup>[7](#o7), [9](#o9)</sup>. Apply within six months of being told you passed, while still on Stamp 2 <sup>[9](#o9)</sup>. Graduates then move mainly to a General Employment Permit or a Critical Skills Employment Permit: each first allows up to two years at 40 hours a week, takes 28 days to process, and can be applied for up to 12 weeks before the first day of work; the General permit can be renewed once for three years, the Critical Skills one every two years <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

**Doctors and nurses.** About 40% of doctors in Ireland in 2023 were foreign and about 51.8% of nursing staff were trained abroad, according to a February 2025 article by a Bangladeshi in Ireland <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. The same article says the Irish Medical Council (IMC) readily recognises degrees from India, Pakistan, the Philippines and Egypt but recognition is hard for Bangladesh, so Bangladeshi doctors face extra exams <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. A doctor whose degree is not recognised takes the PRES (Pre-Registration Examination System: a written exam, a clinical skills exam and a supervised internship); one who has passed PLAB or MRCP (the UK's licensing and membership exams) may register without PRES; IELTS of at least 7.0 or OET (Occupational English Test) grade B is compulsory <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. Ireland's nursing board, NMBI, recognises nurses from the Philippines, India and Nigeria under formal agreements and has none with Bangladesh <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. The article says Bangladeshi doctors in Ireland are few, mostly arriving from Middle Eastern countries, nurses almost none, and that hospital jobs need sponsorship with no recruitment agency linking Bangladeshis to Irish hospitals <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. Demand is highest for specialists in anaesthesia, cardiology, neurology and surgery <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Irish Medical Council now list any Bangladeshi medical degree as recognised?

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays-1"></a>

### 3.2 What it costs and pays

- **Tuition:** 9,850 to 25,500 euros a year for undergraduate study and 9,500 to 34,500 for postgraduate and doctoral study <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
- **Living:** 550 to 1,000 euros a month in most cities, 1,500 to 1,800 in Dublin and other big cities <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
- **Part-time pay:** at least 733 euros a month (about Tk 93,190) <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
- **Health jobs:** entry-level doctors about 50,000 to 60,000 euros a year and specialists 120,000 to 200,000; nurses start at about 30,000 and can pass 55,000 when senior <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>.

## 4 Iraq

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-2"></a>

### 4.1 Is it open, and how people go

At a joint committee in Dhaka on 13-14 October 2025, Bangladesh and Iraq signed an MoU and discussed sending skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled workers and regularising irregular Bangladeshis in Iraq, according to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The agreed route: Iraq gives work visas, workers are recruited on demand letters from Iraqi employers attested by the Bangladesh embassy, and each worker signs an employment contract before leaving <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The embassy is to find out which sectors need workers <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. Before the MoU, Iraq's Cabinet had banned the entry of Bangladeshi workers; in March 2024 Iraq's labour minister said tens of thousands had entered anyway, mostly undocumented, that few had used the ministry's 45-day regularisation window, and that foreign workers should come only as skilled workers, as carers for people with special needs, or matched one-to-one with an Iraqi worker on a project <sup>[N33 (2024)](#n33)</sup>. The Iraqi side was positive about regularisation, and Bangladesh was to ask through the foreign ministry for an amnesty; the committee is to meet at least once a year <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. Whether BMET now clears workers for Iraq: see "Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

In the cases reported, people went through relatives already in Iraq: a cousin in Azad Ali Khan's case and a father in Mehedi Hasan's <sup>[N1](#n1), [N4](#n4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has any recruitment under the October 2025 MoU taken place, through which agencies, and what does a worker pay?
> - Did Iraq announce the amnesty for irregular Bangladeshis, and how does a worker apply?

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays-2"></a>

### 4.2 What it costs and pays

## 6 Spain

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-3"></a>

### 6.1 Is it open, and how people go

Spain's government announced in 2026 a plan to regularise 500,000 irregular migrants by 31 December 2026 <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. About 20,000 Bangladeshis could benefit <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's government approved the programme on 14 April 2026 <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Applications ran online from 16 April and in person from 20 April, both until 30 June 2026 <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. Spain declared amnesties in 2001 and 2005, but none on such easy terms <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.

**Who qualified,** from the government's official questions and answers:

- people who applied for international protection (asylum) in Spain before 1 January 2026, and people in irregular status who arrived before that date <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>;
- in Spain before 1 January 2026, with at least five months' uninterrupted stay on the day of applying <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>;
- no criminal record and no threat to public order, security or health <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>;
- those who had never applied for international protection also had to show one of: work in Spain under a contract or self-employment, a family unit with minor or disabled children or parents, or vulnerability certified by a registered organisation or social services <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.

**Papers:** a full copy of the passport; proof of being in Spain before 1 January 2026 and through the last five months; and a criminal record certificate from Bangladesh, or from wherever you lived for the 5 years before Spain <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. Proof of stay can be a passport entry stamp, air tickets in your name, receipts of bank transactions at branches in Spain, official training certificates, rental contracts in your name or transport tickets inside Spain, and every paper must carry your name and a date <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. If the criminal record certificate did not come within a month, you could submit proof you had asked for it, a sworn declaration and an authorisation for Spain to get it through diplomatic channels <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. Organisations and unions on Spain's register of immigration collaborators gave free advice and could apply for you <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.

**What it gives.** The official notice that your case has started (sent by the processing unit; the application receipt is not this notice) lets you work anywhere in Spain in any sector <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. The permit lasts one year; after that you must move into one of the ordinary permits of Spain's Immigration Regulation <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. It is for Spain only, not other EU countries <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. After approval, apply for the foreigner's identity card (TIE) at the National Police within one month <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.

**Result.** The window closed with 1,174,978 applications, 609,737 of them already processed by 2 July 2026; 8.3% of applicants were from Asia <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. Incomplete applications could be corrected in person at selected post offices until 30 September 2026 <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.

**The bottleneck is Bangladeshi paperwork.** Bangladeshis in Spain feared slow passport renewal and police clearance from Bangladesh's offices could keep about 10,000 of them out <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. In May 2026 a home ministry team led by deputy secretary Kazi Arifur Rahman gave police clearance certificates in Spain directly, the first time in any European country: about 600 people in Barcelona on 9-11 May <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. It worked 10 am to 5 pm in a hall in the Josep Tarradellas area; new online applicants got the certificate on the spot after checks, old certificates were corrected, and stuck offline applications were redone online <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>. Sessions followed at the embassy hall in Madrid on 14, 17, 18 and 19 May <sup>[N37](#n37)</sup>.

## 7 Germany

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-4"></a>

### 7.1 Is it open, and how people go

**The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte),** as an October 2024 guide described it, is a residence permit of up to one year for skilled people from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland to come and look for a job <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>. It goes to people with a qualification fully recognised by Germany, or to those with a degree or at least two years' vocational training recognised in the country where it was obtained <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40), [12](#o12)</sup>. The second group needs a confirmation from the ZAB (Germany's Central Office for Foreign Education), a Goethe Institute certificate of at least A1 German or at least B2 English from an examiner certified by ALTE (the Association of Language Testers in Europe) or TOEFL, and at least 6 points <sup>[12](#o12), [N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>:

- partial recognition of the degree or training: 4 <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>;
- experience: 2 points for two years in the last five, 3 for five in the last seven <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>;
- German: 1 for A2, 3 for B2 or above; 1 more for English at C1 or native <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>;
- age: 2 under 35, 1 for 35 to 40 <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>.

The card allows work of up to 20 hours a week on average and one trial of up to two weeks with an employer; other work needs the visa changed to a work residence permit first <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. It can be extended up to two years from inside Germany <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>. A spouse and children can apply for family reunion visas but must wait their turn on the embassy's family reunion waiting list <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>.

**How to apply at the German embassy in Dhaka:**

1. Get the qualification recognised: vocational qualifications through the ZAB for 150 euros; university degrees must appear in the anabin database with the exact same university and degree as on the diploma, or have a ZAB statement of comparability <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40), [13](#o13)</sup>. The embassy does not accept WES (World Education Services) evaluations <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>.
2. Open a blocked account (Sperrkonto) in Germany with at least 1,091 euros for each month of the card, plus money for the return flight, or get a formal declaration of commitment from someone who undertakes to pay your costs <sup>[12](#o12), [13](#o13)</sup>. Take Schengen-wide health insurance with at least 30,000 euros cover for the whole card period <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. Both can be handed in later, but before the decision <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>.
3. Apply on the Consular Services Portal, the only way the embassy takes Opportunity Card applications <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>. Have English or German translations of every Bangla document <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. Flight and hotel bookings are not needed <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>.
4. Pay the 75-euro fee in taka at the embassy, 11 Madani Avenue, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Dhaka 1212; it is not refunded if the card is refused <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40), [12](#o12)</sup>.
5. Wait at least about 6 weeks <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>; the embassy emails or phones when the card is ready <sup>[N40 (2024)](#n40)</sup>.

**Students.** The German embassy in Dhaka now takes student visa applications through the Consular Services Portal: register, answer the questionnaire and upload every document, then, after a preliminary review, book an appointment with VFS to hand in the documents and biometrics <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>. The old appointment waiting list is closed; anyone on it without an appointment must register on the portal and upload their documents there <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>. Funds are shown with a blocked account of at least 11,904 euros (992 euros a month) or a sponsor's formal declaration of commitment <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>.

## 8 Austria

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-5"></a>

### 8.1 Is it open, and how people go

On 29 April 2024 Austria's foreign minister proposed taking skilled workers from Bangladesh and agreed to sign an MoU on migration and mobility <sup>[N41 (2024)](#n41)</sup>.

**Students.** Austria has no embassy in Bangladesh; the Austrian Embassy in New Delhi covers it, at EP-13, Chandragupta Marg, Chanakyapuri, New Delhi 110021, phone +91 11 2419 2700 <sup>[15](#o15)</sup>. A first residence permit must be applied for at the embassy responsible for where you live <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>. As an August 2024 guide described the steps:

1. Book an appointment through the embassy's online appointment system as early as allowed (at the earliest 6 months before travel), since waits can be long; at the appointment give fingerprints and a photo <sup>[15](#o15), [N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>. The file goes to Austria for decision, so apply at least three months before entry <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.
2. Bring proof of at least 6,630 euros for a year's living costs and health insurance (about 69.13 euros a month); documents in Bangla need a certified German translation <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>. You need not show a lease, but rent counts against the money you must show <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>.
3. Pay 120 euros; processing takes about 15 working days, or 30 to 60 if more papers are needed <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.
4. Once told the permit is approved, apply within three months for entry and get a Type D visa, then reach Austria and collect the permit within three more months <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.

The permit is usually issued for 12 months, and to extend it you must show you made successful progress in your studies the year before <sup>[17](#o17)</sup>. Public universities charge foreign students about 1,452 euros a year on average; living costs are 900 to 1,300 euros a month in Vienna or Salzburg <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>. To work, even in a small job, a student needs an employment permit, which the employer applies for at the AMS (Public Employment Service), for up to 20 hours a week without a labour market test <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>. After graduating you can renew the permit once for 12 months to look for work or start a business, or switch to a Red-White-Red Card, the work permit, after a job offer (decision in 15 days, up to 40 hours a week) <sup>[16](#o16), [N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.

## 10 Taiwan

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-7"></a>

### 10.1 Is it open, and how people go

Taiwan has no mission in Bangladesh; its Bureau of Consular Affairs sends people in Bangladesh to the Taipei Economic and Cultural Center (TECC) in India or the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Thailand <sup>[18 (2022)](#o18)</sup>. TECC India is at No. 34, Paschimi Marg, Vasant Vihar, New Delhi 110057, phone (91-11) 4607-7777, email ind@mofa.gov.tw <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. It takes applications only in person at its counter, Monday to Friday 9:00 to 11:00, with collection 15:30 to 16:30; nothing by post, courier, fax or email <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. The steps, from TECC and a September 2024 guide:

1. Fill in the resident visa form (for stays over 180 days, valid one year) at visawebapp.boca.gov.tw, upload the papers, print it with its barcode and sign <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>.
2. Get a visa guarantee letter from the person inviting you in Taiwan, which every Bangladeshi passport holder needs: download the form from TECC's website and send it to the inviter, who fills it in, scans it to you and keeps the original <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>. TECC gives you a visa number, which you pass to the inviter, who then submits the original letter to Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs; checking takes at least seven working days <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>.
3. Show funds with the original and a copy of your own or a parent's income tax return and the latest 3 months' bank statement <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>; the guide says they should cover at least six months' living costs <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>.
4. Bring the admission letter and the original degree certificate and transcript; a student with only a provisional certificate gets a 60-day visitor visa instead of a resident visa <sup>[19](#o19)</sup>.
5. Pay for document authentication (400 NTD, New Taiwan dollars, per original and 200 per copy) and the visa: 2,200 NTD single entry or 4,400 multiple <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>. Processing takes eight working days <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>.

Students may work up to 20 hours a week in term, with an ARC (Alien Resident Certificate) and a work permit from the Workforce Development Agency renewed every semester or six months; part-time jobs pay 120 to 400 NTD an hour <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>. Graduates may stay six months to find work, extendable by six more, and may apply for permanent residence after five continuous years with no criminal record <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>.

## 12 Denmark

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-9"></a>

### 12.1 Is it open, and how people go

In a notice of 18 September 2025 its Ministry of Immigration and Integration said study would become much harder for students without the required qualifications, and the government said the measures target especially students from Bangladesh and Nepal entering the labour market on student visas <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. The former minister Kaare Dybvad Bek said some people had used the student visa as a back door into the labour market and that student and family numbers from both countries had risen sharply <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. The measures:

- entrance exams or set language tests for students from outside the EU <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>;
- strict checks of certificates with the National ID Centre, and new rules against admission through agents and fake papers <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>;
- a review for fraud of residence permits already given to Bangladeshi and Nepali students <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>;
- higher tuition fees and closer watch on agents selling Denmark as an easy route to residence <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

**The rules in force.** For students from outside the EU who apply on or after 1 October 2026 for a state-approved bachelor's or master's programme, the job-seeking permit after graduating is cut from up to 3 years to up to 1 year, and spouses, partners and children can no longer get residence permits to accompany them; PhD students are not affected <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. The date the application is submitted decides which rules apply <sup>[21](#o21)</sup>. How to apply, from SIRI (the Danish Agency for International Recruitment and Integration):

1. Get admission; the Danish institution fills in its part of the ST1 application form and forwards it to you <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>.
2. Pay SIRI's DKK 3,060 processing fee and keep the receipt <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>.
3. Show money of DKK 7,426 a month of study (2026 level), at most DKK 89,112 for a year; a paid first-semester tuition fee counts as enough <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>.
4. If you apply online, have your fingerprints and photo taken at a Danish mission or application centre within 14 days, or the application can be rejected <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>.
5. Wait about 2 months, up to 3 if SIRI asks for more <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>.

A student may work 90 hours a month from September to May and full time in June, July and August; working more can bring a warning, a fine or loss of the permit <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>. The Danish Embassy in Dhaka does not endorse or work with agents <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>.

Between 2022 and 2024, 58% of Bangladeshi students brought family, against 1% of Chinese and 2% of US students <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. An Aarhus University survey found a 13% first-year dropout rate among Bangladeshi students against 4% for other international students, and pass rates of 55-65% against 90% <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

**Residence.** Migrants could not renew residence cards for a long time; when AIMA (Portugal's immigration agency) reopened renewals in June 2025 it asked for a declaration from the property's actual owner as proof of address and detailed proof of income scaled to family size <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. A Bangladeshi there six years said he had always used a municipality address certificate, and his landlord would not give the declaration <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. Parliament passed a stricter immigration law on 30 September 2025, including on family reunification, pending the president's approval <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

In October 2025 a Chega party billboard in Montijo with its leader André Ventura read "This is not Bangladesh", part of its anti-immigration campaign <sup>[N51](#n51)</sup>. The Casa do Bangladesh organisation said the matter had come to the attention of the Bangladesh embassy and government <sup>[N51](#n51)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the new immigration law in force, and what does it now require for family reunification?

<a id="south-africa"></a>

## 17 South Africa

<a id="what-goes-wrong-6"></a>

### 17.1 What goes wrong

Md Fahad, 18, of Chandanaish, Chattogram, whose two uncles run businesses in South Africa after going the same way, was sent by his father on Tk 9 lakh borrowed from various people <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. The father paid the broker ring Tk 2 lakh first and Tk 7 lakh into a bank account when the journey began, with Tk 1.5 lakh more due on arrival <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. Fahad flew from Dhaka on 30 January 2026, meant to fly on via Ethiopia and Zimbabwe; instead brokers took him from Ethiopia through forest to Zimbabwe and by road to South Africa, walking long stretches over about two weeks <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. He reached Musina on 15 February, and news of his death came by phone that night <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. The family believe hunger, illness and lack of treatment killed him, and say everything he carried was robbed in the forest; the brokers promised to return the money <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

Six Bangladeshis died in a fire at a supershop in Queenstown, Eastern Cape, on the night of 27 July 2026; one, Md Rajikul Islam, 53, was from Mirkadim, Munshiganj, and the other five from Narayanganj <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>. Relatives said Rajikul worked at the shop and slept there at night, and the men inside could not get out <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>. He had gone on 13 May 2026, leaving a wife four months pregnant and three daughters, on about Tk 10 lakh borrowed from relatives <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>. The local MP said steps were being taken with the local administration to give the family whatever government help was possible <sup>[N52](#n52)</sup>.

The Bangladesh High Commission in South Africa is at 410 Farenden Street, Sunnyside, Pretoria 0002, phone 012 343 2105 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is there any legal work visa route from Bangladesh to South Africa, and does BMET clear workers for it?

## 19 Thailand

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-14"></a>

### 19.1 Is it open, and how people go

From 2 January 2025 Bangladeshis apply for Thai visas online at thaievisa.go.th: open an account, fill in the form, upload documents and pay; the e-visa comes by email within 10 working days and the printout is shown at immigration <sup>[N54 (2024)](#n54)</sup>.
