## 1 In one minute

For work, the reporting shows Bangladeshis going to Iraq through relatives already there, some working irregularly, with deaths from fire, missiles, poisoning and murder over unpaid wages <sup>[N1](#n1), [N2](#n2), [N3](#n3), [N4](#n4)</sup>; Bangladesh and Iraq signed an MoU (memorandum of understanding) in October 2025 for recruitment on embassy-attested demand letters <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. In November 2025 Bangladesh asked Bahrain to reopen visas for Bangladeshis, and Bahrain said it was reopening them step by step <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. Fiji takes small numbers through BOESL, the government recruiting company <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>, and Mauritius agreed in principle in June 2026 to reopen its closed market <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. In the one South Africa case reported, brokers took an 18-year-old overland through Ethiopia and Zimbabwe and he died on arrival in February 2026 <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In Spain, Portugal and France there are settled communities; Spain's 2026 regularisation could cover about 20,000 Bangladeshis who arrived before 31 December 2025 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>, while Portugal has made residence renewals harder <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.

For study, Australia, Ireland, Germany, Austria, Taiwan, Denmark, New Zealand, Cyprus, Egypt and China all take Bangladeshi students, several of them through embassies in New Delhi <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12), [N13 (2024)](#n13), [N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. Australia and Denmark tightened student rules in 2025-26 and named family members and course-hopping as targets, Denmark naming Bangladesh and Nepal outright <sup>[N15](#n15), [N16](#n16)</sup>. What goes wrong most in this reporting: a family pays a dalal for "Australia" and the young man is flown to Nepal or Kazakhstan, held and beaten for more money, and returned with the family in debt <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18)</sup>; and workers die abroad, after which the body takes weeks or months to come home <sup>[N2](#n2), [N3](#n3)</sup>.

## 2 Australia

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go"></a>

### 2.1 Is it open, and how people go

Bangladeshis go to Australia mainly as students: Australia granted student visas to more than 166,000 of 179,000 applicants (all nationalities) in 2022-23 <sup>[N19 (2024)](#n19)</sup>. In May 2024 the two foreign ministers discussed skilled workers from Bangladesh and seasonal work for Bangladeshis, and Bangladesh asked for help under TAFE (Australia's Technical and Further Education system) to train people at its TTCs (Technical Training Centres) <sup>[N20 (2024)](#n20)</sup>. For what the real work visas need and how Australia work-visa frauds operate, see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc.

**Student rules as reported:**

- **Genuine student test.** Australia brought in a "genuine student" requirement in March 2024, aimed at separating real study from using the student visa mainly to work or stay, a Bangladeshi academic at Charles Darwin University wrote in October 2026 <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. By August 2026 the government had also raised the minimum English score and the funds to be shown in the bank; its bill to cap student numbers was blocked in the upper house <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>.
- **Work while studying.** Students may work 24 hours a week, raised from 20, according to a July 2024 report <sup>[N19 (2024)](#n19)</sup>.
- **After graduating.** The Temporary Graduate visa gives two years after a bachelor's or coursework master's and three after a research master's or PhD, with no job guarantee <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>. The applicant must hold a valid student visa, have finished a degree at an approved Australian institution, meet health and character rules, hold health insurance and owe no debt to the Australian government; family can be included <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>. The fee is AUD 2,300 plus the medical, police certificate and biometrics <sup>[N23](#n23)</sup>.
- **Scholarships named in 2025.** The Melbourne international undergraduate scholarship (up to 10,000 dollars off first-year fees) and the UTS Vice-Chancellor's international scholarship (up to 50% of costs) <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>.

**The September 2026 tightening.** Minister for home affairs and immigration Tony Burke announced <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>:

- net migration cut to 245,000 in 2026-27 and 225,000 a year after that <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- an end to "visa hopping", meaning moving to a lower-level course after finishing study or switching visas again and again <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- no expansion of students bringing family, with exemptions for those already studying in Australia, applicants from Pacific and Southeast Asian countries, and PhD students, who can bring family <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- a "no further stay" condition on all tourist visas, which in effect ends going as a tourist and applying for another visa inside Australia <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- faster removal of people whose visas have expired <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- a lottery for second- and third-year Working Holiday visas in farming and services, and priority for skilled migration in construction, health care and farming <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>;
- powers to cancel visas quickly and deport migrants involved in violence against children, domestic violence or hate crimes <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

The academic quoted above wrote that bridging visas, new applications and reviews of visa decisions all have legal conditions, and the belief that using them lets a person stay indefinitely is wrong <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. He gave as an example a student who gets a visa on admission to a well-known university, then moves on arrival to a cheaper institution with no intention of studying at the first <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>. In August 2026 Pauline Hanson, leader of the far-right One Nation party, called for stopping students' spouses and families from coming at all <sup>[N22](#n22)</sup>. For how these changes fit the wider pattern, see "Student visas used as work routes" in the Illegal routes doc.

> - What bank balance and IELTS score does a Bangladeshi student visa applicant need in 2026?

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays"></a>

### 2.2 What it costs and pays

A Bangladeshi doctoral researcher at Curtin University wrote in August 2026 that in almost every Australian city a large share of Bangladeshi students do hard work alongside study, in restaurants, cleaning companies, ride-sharing or delivery, to meet tuition and living costs <sup>[N25](#n25)</sup>. He described a Sri Lankan student in Perth who broke his hand at work and was dismissed after missing a few days <sup>[N25](#n25)</sup>.

<a id="what-goes-wrong"></a>

### 2.3 What goes wrong

The commonest case in this reporting is a family paying a dalal for Australia and the young man being flown somewhere else and held for ransom <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18)</sup>. Both cases below are from Bagherpara, Jashore, and both families filed cases at Jashore's Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Prevention and Suppression Tribunal-1 <sup>[N17](#n17), [N18](#n18)</sup>. For the pattern, see "A flight to a different country first" in the Dalal scams doc.

- **Sakib Hossain, Nepal, September 2025 to May 2026.** His father, a van driver, paid a broker Tk 15 lakh to send Sakib, 21, to Australia; on 14 September 2025 he was taken to Kathmandu instead <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. Sakib said a broker's man held the group's passports at Dhaka airport, and at Kathmandu took his passport and about Tk 80,000 in dollars <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. He said he was locked in a hotel room, beaten daily for money and made to repeat scripted words on calls home with a knife beside him; the captors took Tk 1.5 lakh more and kept beating him <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. After four months he was moved to a basement, where his stomach was slashed with a knife <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. He was finally taken by bus to Siliguri and Jalpaiguri in India and pushed into Bangladesh through a culvert under the fence at the Nilphamari border <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. His father had sold all land but the homestead and his van and borrowed Tk 12.5 lakh from five NGOs and savings groups; creditors come daily <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The case filed on 16 February 2026 names two men from the village, who were absconding in May <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.
- **Sohel Rana, Kazakhstan, January to April 2026.** Sohel, who had run two restaurants in Malaysia, came home in November 2024 on a broker's promise of Europe; the broker, Nazma Akter alias Nurjahan, then agreed to send him to Australia for a job at 1,000 dollars a month <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. Under a Tk 16 lakh deal he paid Tk 12 lakh, from savings, money borrowed against paddy, an NGO loan and other debts <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. He was flown on 15 January 2026 to Dubai on a one-month business visa and the next day to Kazakhstan <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. The broker's representative took his and two other Bangladeshis' passports, drove them about 900 km to Shymkent, locked them in a metal-smelting factory, demanded Tk 30 lakh and threatened to kill them, giving one piece of bread a day <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. He escaped over the wall with one companion, texted a Bangladesh immigration officer on 14 April, paid a South African lawyer Tk 1.2 lakh plus Tk 1 lakh for the fare, and came home on a travel permit on 20 April <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. The case, recorded on 6 April, names Md Kaiyum, 48, and Nazma Akter, 45, of Keraniganj, Dhaka; police said Kaiyum was abroad and Nazma was being sought, and her phone was off <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. Sohel owes about Tk 15 lakh <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

## 3 Ireland

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-1"></a>

### 3.1 Is it open, and how people go

Bangladeshis go to Ireland as students and, rarely, as doctors and nurses <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12), [N26](#n26)</sup>.

**The student visa, as an August 2024 guide described it:**

1. Get an offer letter from an Irish institution, then apply online for a long-stay Type D visa (for stays over 90 days); print and sign the form <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
2. Gather the papers: proof of tuition fee payment (at least 6,000 euros, or the full fee at some institutions), a personal bank statement of at least 10,000 euros or a sponsor's signed letter and national ID copy, the financial summary form, private medical insurance of at least 25,000 euros, and a police clearance certificate <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. All papers must be originals, and anything in Bangla needs a full English translation <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
3. Book a visa application centre appointment and give fingerprints and a photo on the day <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. The embassy address the guide gives is C-17 Malcha Marg, Chanakyapuri, New Delhi 110021, India <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
4. Pay the visa facilitation service charge of 3,518 rupees and the visa fee of 5,300 rupees (single entry) or 9,000 rupees (multiple entry) <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
5. Wait 30 working days to two calendar months; the visa and original papers come back by courier <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

**Work.** Students may work up to 20 hours a week in term and 40 in the summer and winter holidays, on a valid Stamp 2 immigration permission <sup>[N19 (2024)](#n19)</sup>. Working needs a PPS (Personal Public Service) number, obtained on being hired <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. After graduating, students may work 12 to 24 months <sup>[N19 (2024)](#n19)</sup>. Graduates then move mainly to a General Employment Permit or a Critical Skills Employment Permit: each first allows up to two years at 40 hours a week, takes 28 days to process, and can be applied for up to 12 weeks before the first day of work; the General permit can be renewed once for three years, the Critical Skills one every two years <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

**Doctors and nurses.** About 40% of doctors in Ireland in 2023 were foreign and about 51.8% of nursing staff were trained abroad, according to a February 2025 article by a Bangladeshi in Ireland <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. The same article says the Irish Medical Council (IMC) readily recognises degrees from India, Pakistan, the Philippines and Egypt but recognition is hard for Bangladesh, so Bangladeshi doctors face extra exams <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. A doctor whose degree is not recognised takes the PRES (Pre-Registration Examination System: a written exam, a clinical skills exam and a supervised internship); one who has passed PLAB or MRCP (the UK's licensing and membership exams) may register without PRES; IELTS of at least 7.0 or OET grade B is compulsory <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. Ireland's nursing board, NMBI, recognises nurses from the Philippines, India and Nigeria under formal agreements and has none with Bangladesh <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. The article says Bangladeshi doctors in Ireland are few, mostly arriving from Middle Eastern countries, nurses almost none, and that hospital jobs need sponsorship with no recruitment agency linking Bangladeshis to Irish hospitals <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>. Demand is highest for specialists in anaesthesia, cardiology, neurology and surgery <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What are the 2026 Irish study visa fees in taka or rupees, and does a Bangladeshi still apply through New Delhi or now through a centre in Dhaka?
> - Does the Irish Medical Council now list any Bangladeshi medical degree as recognised?

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays-1"></a>

### 3.2 What it costs and pays

- **Tuition:** 9,850 to 25,500 euros a year for undergraduate study and 9,500 to 34,500 for postgraduate and doctoral study <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Living:** 550 to 1,000 euros a month in most cities, 1,500 to 1,800 in Dublin and other big cities <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Part-time pay:** at least 733 euros a month (about Tk 93,190) <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **Health jobs:** entry-level doctors about 50,000 to 60,000 euros a year and specialists 120,000 to 200,000; nurses start at about 30,000 and can pass 55,000 when senior <sup>[N26](#n26)</sup>.

## 4 Iraq

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-2"></a>

### 4.1 Is it open, and how people go

At a joint committee in Dhaka on 13-14 October 2025, Bangladesh and Iraq signed an MoU and discussed sending skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled workers and regularising irregular Bangladeshis in Iraq, according to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The agreed route: Iraq gives work visas, workers are recruited on demand letters from Iraqi employers attested by the Bangladesh embassy, and each worker signs an employment contract before leaving <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The embassy is to find out which sectors need workers <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The Iraqi side was positive about regularisation, and Bangladesh was to ask through the foreign ministry for an amnesty; the committee is to meet at least once a year <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. Since 4 November 2026 BMET clears no one for Iraq without the Ministry's recruitment permission (see "Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc).

In the cases reported, people went through relatives already in Iraq: a cousin in Azad Ali Khan's case and a father in Mehedi Hasan's <sup>[N1](#n1), [N4](#n4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has any recruitment under the October 2025 MoU taken place, through which agencies, and what does a worker pay?
> - Did Iraq announce the amnesty for irregular Bangladeshis, and how does a worker apply?

<a id="what-it-costs-and-pays-2"></a>

### 4.2 What it costs and pays

- Azad Ali Khan paid Tk 7.25 lakh through his cousin in 2025 for a promised well-paid job, and in four months could send home only Tk 60,000 <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>.
- Mehedi Hasan's family borrowed about Tk 10 lakh to send him through his father in October 2025 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="what-goes-wrong-1"></a>

### 4.3 What goes wrong

- **Unpaid, then killed, Baghdad, September 2025.** Azad, 47, a tailor from Goalanda, Rajbari, went on 24 June 2025; relatives said his cousin left him at a hotel in Kazimiya, Baghdad, and he took work in a house <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>. He was not paid for more than three months, and on 12 September the house owner killed him in an argument over it; the body was found in a drain six days later and the owner surrendered and confessed <sup>[N1](#n1)</sup>.
- **Fire, Baghdad, June 2025.** Mohammad Ali, 25, of Bhola, in Iraq since 2020, was burned when his workplace generator room caught fire on 15 June 2025 and died in hospital <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. His employer did not object to sending the body home, but flights from Iraq were suspended because of the Iran-Israel conflict <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. He had borrowed from his company's owner to build a house at home <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Bhola's deputy commissioner said no letter had come from the ministry <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
- **Poisoning, Kurdistan, March 2026.** Mehedi Hasan, 21, of Feni, and Mohammad Tarek, 45, and his son Md Sabbir, 22, of Brahmanbaria, fell ill after iftar, got first aid and went home, and were found dead in their room the next afternoon; the family and the Bangladesh embassy in Baghdad said the cause was food poisoning <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. The Feni Sadar upazila executive officer said his office had not been told <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **Missile, Baghdad, March 2026.** Md Srabon, in Iraq since 2016 and working irregularly in Baghdad, was killed in a missile attack on 14 March 2026 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. His body came home on a Turkish Airlines flight under government arrangement, with help from Munshiganj local administration and the Bangladesh embassy, about two and a half months later, on 29 May <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. His widow asked the government for help to build a house on his village land <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="help-and-contacts"></a>

### 4.4 Help and contacts

In the death cases above, the Bangladesh embassy in Baghdad and the district administration were the offices involved <sup>[N4](#n4), [N2](#n2)</sup>; for what the family can claim, see "If a worker dies abroad" in the Services for migrants and their families doc.

> - Does the Wage Earners' Welfare Board pay death compensation for a worker who went to Iraq without BMET clearance?

## 5 Spain

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-3"></a>

### 5.1 Is it open, and how people go

Spain's government announced in 2026 a plan to regularise 500,000 irregular migrants by 31 December 2026 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. About 20,000 Bangladeshis could benefit, but only those who entered Spain before 31 December 2025 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. Applications were to run from April to 30 June 2026, with no work contract needed, only a passport and proof of police clearance <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. Spain declared amnesties in 2001 and 2005, but none on such easy terms <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

**The bottleneck is Bangladeshi paperwork.** Bangladeshis in Spain feared slow passport renewal and police clearance from Bangladesh's offices could keep about 10,000 of them out <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. In May 2026 a home ministry team led by deputy secretary Kazi Arifur Rahman gave police clearance certificates in Spain directly, the first time in any European country: about 600 people in Barcelona on 9-11 May <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>. It worked 10 am to 5 pm in a hall in the Josep Tarradellas area; new online applicants got the certificate on the spot after checks, old certificates were corrected, and stuck offline applications were redone online <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>. Sessions followed at the embassy hall in Madrid on 14, 17, 18 and 19 May <sup>[N27](#n27)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Did Spain's regularisation open as planned, and how many Bangladeshis applied and were approved?
> - What did the final bulletin require beyond passport and police clearance (proof of arrival date, address registration)?

<a id="what-goes-wrong-2"></a>

### 5.2 What goes wrong

- **Paying for "help".** The president of the Bangladesh Association in Spain told people not to pay anyone promising help with the regularisation before the final bulletin came out <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Seafarers held, 2020-2024.** Five Bangladeshi crew of the ship MV Karar were detained by Spain's coast guard with 19 other foreigners on suspicion of smuggling drugs from Colombia, held in custody from 20 March 2020, and bailed on 24 April 2024 on condition they attend hearings and do not leave Spain <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>. The embassy in Madrid said it pressed Spanish ministries for a quick hearing <sup>[N28 (2024)](#n28)</sup>.

<a id="help-and-contacts-1"></a>

### 5.3 Help and contacts

Bangladesh's ambassador to Spain, Masudur Rahman, said in 2026 he had raised the paperwork delays with all ministries concerned <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. In 2024 the embassy held a meeting in Alicante where it gave limited consular services and took Wage Earners' Welfare Board membership applications <sup>[N29 (2024)](#n29)</sup>. Bangladeshis near Elche run a farm whose vegetables are exported to several EU countries <sup>[N29 (2024)](#n29)</sup>.

## 6 Germany

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-4"></a>

### 6.1 Is it open, and how people go

**The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte),** as an October 2024 guide described it, is a residence permit of up to one year for skilled people from outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland to come and look for a job <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>. It goes to people with a qualification fully recognised by Germany, or to those with a German or German-recognised degree or at least two years' vocational training, plus at least A1 German or B2 English (IELTS 5.5 to 6.5) <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>. The second group needs at least 6 points <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>:

- partial recognition of the degree or training: 4 <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>;
- experience: 2 points for two years in the last five, 3 for five in the last seven <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>;
- German: 1 for A2, 3 for B2 or above; 1 more for English at C1 or native <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>;
- age: 2 under 35, 1 for 35 to 40 <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.

The card allows part-time work of up to 20 hours a week and job trials of up to two weeks per company, and can be extended up to two years from inside Germany <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.

**How to apply:**

1. Get the qualification recognised: vocational qualifications through the ZAB (Germany's Central Office for Foreign Education) for 150 euros; university degrees must appear in the anabin database or have a ZAB statement <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.
2. Open a blocked account in Germany with at least 1,027 euros a month, and hold enough money for a return flight <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.
3. Book an appointment online and fill in the application on the VIDEX portal; print and sign it <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.
4. Take the papers to the German embassy, 11 Madani Avenue, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Dhaka 1212, and pay the 75-euro fee <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.
5. Wait from a few weeks to six months; the embassy emails or phones when the card is ready <sup>[N30 (2024)](#n30)</sup>.

**Students.** From 1 January 2027 the German embassy in Dhaka takes bachelor's and master's student visa applications through the online Consular Services Portal (Auslandsportal), where students fill in the form, upload papers and track the application <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>. Documents can first be checked through VFS, and biometrics, fee and checks are done on the appointment day <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>. ## 7 Austria

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-5"></a>

### 7.1 Is it open, and how people go

On 29 April 2024 Austria's foreign minister proposed taking skilled workers from Bangladesh and agreed to sign an MoU on migration and mobility <sup>[N32 (2024)](#n32)</sup>.

**Students,** according to an August 2024 guide:

1. Apply first for a residence permit at the Austrian embassy in New Delhi (EP-13 Chandragupta Marg, Chanakyapuri, New Delhi 110021), booking the appointment by email and giving fingerprints and a photo <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. The file goes to Austria for decision, so apply at least three months before entry <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
2. Bring proof of at least 6,630 euros for a year's living costs, health insurance (about 69.13 euros a month) and proof of accommodation; documents in Bangla need a certified German translation <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
3. Pay 120 euros; processing takes about 15 working days, or 30 to 60 if more papers are needed <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
4. Once told the permit is approved, apply within three months for entry and get a Type D visa, then reach Austria and collect the permit within three more months <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.

Public universities charge foreign students about 1,452 euros a year on average; living costs are 900 to 1,300 euros a month in Vienna or Salzburg <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. A student needs a job offer and the residence permit to get a work permit, valid a year and renewable, for up to 20 hours a week <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. Graduates can stay up to a year on a job-seeker permit (applied for before the student permit expires, up to 20 hours a week) or on a Red-White-Red Card after a job offer (decision in 15 days, up to 40 hours a week) <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.

<a id="taiwan"></a>

## 8 Taiwan

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-6"></a>

### 8.1 Is it open, and how people go

Taiwan has no mission in Bangladesh; students apply to its office in India, 34 Paschimi Marg, Vasant Vihar, New Delhi 110057 <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. As a September 2024 guide described it:

1. Fill in the resident visa form (for stays over 180 days, valid one year) at visawebapp.boca.gov.tw, upload the papers, print it with its barcode and sign <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
2. Get a letter of guarantee from a person living in Taiwan who invites you; the guide calls it the most important part for Bangladeshi passport holders <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. The TECC (Taipei Economic and Cultural Center) gives you a visa number, the guarantor submits the original letter to Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs, and checking takes at least seven working days <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
3. Show funds through the sponsor's income tax return and three months' bank statements covering at least six months' living costs <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.
4. Pay for document authentication (400 NTD, New Taiwan dollars, per original and 200 per copy) and the visa: 2,200 NTD single entry or 4,400 multiple <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. Processing takes eight working days <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

Students may work up to 20 hours a week in term, with an ARC (Alien Resident Certificate) and a work permit from the Workforce Development Agency renewed every semester or six months; part-time jobs pay 120 to 400 NTD an hour <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. Graduates may stay six months to find work, extendable by six more, and may apply for permanent residence after five continuous years with no criminal record <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>.

<a id="denmark"></a>

## 10 Denmark

<a id="is-it-open-and-how-people-go-8"></a>

### 10.1 Is it open, and how people go

In a notice of 18 September 2025 its Ministry of Immigration and Integration said study would become much harder for students without the required qualifications, and the government said the measures target especially students from Bangladesh and Nepal entering the labour market on student visas <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The former minister Kaare Dybvad Bek said some people had used the student visa as a back door into the labour market and that student and family numbers from both countries had risen sharply <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. The measures:

- entrance exams or set language tests for students from outside the EU <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>;
- strict checks of certificates with the National ID Centre, and new rules against admission through agents and fake papers <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>;
- a review for fraud of residence permits already given to Bangladeshi and Nepali students <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>;
- fewer spouses and dependants, and post-study work cut from three years to one <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>;
- higher tuition fees and closer watch on agents selling Denmark as an easy route to residence <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

Between 2022 and 2024, 58% of Bangladeshi students brought family, against 1% of Chinese and 2% of US students <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. An Aarhus University survey found a 13% first-year dropout rate among Bangladeshi students against 4% for other international students, and pass rates of 55-65% against 90% <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.
