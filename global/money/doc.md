# Sending money home

How people working abroad send money to Bangladesh, what it costs, the official incentive, the hundi trade and its risks, and what customs allows on the way home, as of November 2026. Each country's own doc gives what is specific to sending money from that country. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## In one minute

A new worker usually needs six months to a year abroad before sending money home, experts told Prothom Alo in 2024 [GM0005]. Money then comes one of two ways. Through a bank, an exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, it earns the family the government's 2.5% cash incentive; in early 2024 banks added another 2.5% of their own, until they stopped in May 2024, and in September 2024 the government's 2.5% was the only incentive paid [GM0001, GM0039, GM0059, GM0122]. Through hundi, the informal and illegal money-transfer trade, an agent abroad takes the worker's riyals or dirhams and a partner in Bangladesh pays the family in taka, often at a better rate, through networks that reach remote villages, and sometimes on credit [GM0007, GM0006, GM0010, GM0011]. Workers who borrowed heavily to go are pulled to whichever channel pays more, because they want to clear the debt fast [GM0009]. The foreign currency hundi collects is used abroad to move money out of Bangladesh [GM0050]. In 2024 a former finance minister put the legal share at only 51% of the money migrants send [GM0045]. The gap between the bank rate and the hundi rate decides much of this, and it moves: in December 2023 a migrant got Tk 109.50 a dollar through a bank, while hundi paid more; in May 2024, after Bangladesh Bank changed how the rate is set, a remittance dollar fetched about Tk 120 with the government incentive, and from late August 2024 banks agreed to pay exchange houses no more than Tk 120 a dollar [GM0001, GM0007, GM0059, GM0112]. When the official rate was allowed to rise in 2024, legal remittances grew and the gap with hundi narrowed a lot, economic analyst Mamun Rashid wrote [GM0108]. In September 2024 Bangladesh Bank's governor said that, for the first time, a remittance dollar fetched more at a bank than on the open market; by December 2024 banks were paying up to Tk 127.50, and that month migrants sent USD 2.639 billion, the most ever [GM0150, GM0164, GM0169]. Since 1 December 2024 a migrant can put any amount into the Wage Earners' Development Bond, a government savings bond paying 9 to 12% free of income tax (see Wage Earners' Development Bond) [GM0130, GM0142, GM0137].

## Ways to send money and what each costs

### Banks and exchange houses

Legal remittances arrive through banks in Bangladesh; in 1 to 23 February 2024 private banks brought USD 1.404 billion of USD 1.646 billion, state-owned banks USD 175.5 million, one specialised bank USD 63 million and foreign banks USD 3.5 million [GM0018]. Through a bank or licensed exchange house the family gets the government incentive (see The government cash incentive) [GM0001]. In the first week of June 2024 Islami Bank brought in the most among private banks (USD 159.1 million), then BRAC Bank (USD 52.9 million) and Bank Asia (USD 33 million); among state-owned banks Janata Bank brought USD 60 million and Agrani Bank USD 57.5 million [GM0071, GM0127]. Of about USD 730 million that week, private banks carried USD 540 million, state-owned banks USD 170 million, specialised banks USD 10 million and foreign banks USD 2 million [GM0072].

How long it takes: Bangladesh Bank officials said in July 2024 that a bank must pay out a remittance within 24 hours of receiving it; if the exchange house abroad holds the money, it can take longer, usually no more than two or three days [GM0096]. Exchange houses have held money back on purpose: bank treasury heads said in August 2024 that exchange houses were holding migrants' remittances in hope of a better price from banks in Bangladesh [GM0113].

### Foreign-currency accounts in Bangladeshi banks

Since the end of 2023 a Bangladeshi living abroad can hold a foreign-currency account in a bank in Bangladesh, called an offshore or international banking account [GM0088, GM0087]. As reported in July 2024 [GM0087, GM0089, GM0090, GM0091, GM0092, GM0093, GM0094, GM0095, GM0128]:
- **Who opens it:** the migrant, from abroad, on the bank's website; or a close relative in Bangladesh on his behalf.
- **What to submit:** proof of Bangladeshi citizenship, proof of living abroad, a work ID card and a photo; if someone in Bangladesh operates the account, that person's details too; when a relative opens it, both give ID, a photo and other documents. Each bank's website explains its account under "offshore banking account" or "international banking account".
- **What it pays:** up to 8.75% a year, a limit set by Bangladesh Bank.
- **Tax:** profit is not taxed, while profit on a taka deposit is taxed at 15% without a TIN (tax identification number) or 10% with one.
- **Taking it out:** under the Offshore Banking Act 2024 no agency asks the source of the deposit, and the whole amount with profit can be taken out at any time.
- **Banks offering it:** The City Bank, BRAC Bank, Dutch-Bangla Bank, Pubali Bank, Eastern Bank, Islami Bank and several others; more than USD 50 million had been deposited, USD 24 million of it at City Bank.

### Mobile wallets: bKash

Money sent into a bKash account earns the family the government incentive of Tk 25 per Tk 1,000, bKash officials said in April 2024 [GM0039]. According to bKash, about 1 million migrants sent a little over Tk 965 crore through it from 12 March to 10 April 2024, and Tk 6,252 crore came through bKash in 2023 [GM0038]. In July 2024 the money-transfer company TapTap Send let migrants in 15 countries, among them the US, UK, Italy, France, Canada, the UAE, Portugal, Germany, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Ireland and Austria, send any amount to a bKash account with no charge; the money arrived at once, with the government incentive [GM0082, GM0083].

Taking the money out, as of April 2024 [GM0034, GM0035, GM0036, GM0037]:
- **At a bank ATM:** Tk 7 per Tk 1,000 for remittance money, at about 2,700 ATMs of 19 banks, started from the bKash app or by dialling *247#. The banks: The City Bank, BRAC Bank, Jamuna Bank, Trust Bank, First Security Bank, Social Islami Bank, Mercantile Bank, Community Bank, NCC Bank, Shahjalal Islami Bank, NRB Commercial Bank, Union Bank, Midland Bank, Rupali Bank, BASIC Bank, Shimanto Bank, Bangladesh Krishi Bank, ICB Islamic Bank and Citizens Bank.
- **At a bKash agent:** normally Tk 18.60 per Tk 1,000.
- **At one of two "priyo" (favourite) agents:** Tk 14.90 per Tk 1,000, for up to Tk 50,000 a month at that rate.

### What keeps people off the legal channels

A parliamentary standing committee on the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment listed 10 obstacles to sending money legally, as reported in April 2024 [GM0046, GM0047, GM0048]:
- the gap between the legal and the illegal exchange rate;
- workers without valid papers;
- no Bangladeshi bank branches abroad, or too few;
- no Bangladeshi money exchange firms or exchange points, or too few;
- high fees or service charges, and fixed sending limits;
- the power of hundi traders;
- migrants or their close relatives at home often have no bank account;
- money that does not match the sender's income is obstructed;
- income from unauthorised business or work has no legal channel;
- too little awareness and encouragement to send legally.

The committee on the finance ministry advised in April 2024 that migrants be given national ID cards and birth registration certificates so they can send money without obstacles, and that a smart app be used [GM0044].

## The government incentive

### The government cash incentive

The government pays a 2.5% cash incentive on money sent through legal channels, credited to the family with the remittance; bKash described it in April 2024 as Tk 25 per Tk 1,000 [GM0001, GM0039]. It began at 2% on 1 July 2019 and rose to 2.5% from 1 January 2022 [GM0123, GM0124]. As of September 2024 it was 2.5%, and banks paid nothing on top [GM0122]. When banks stopped their own incentive in May 2024, Bangladesh Bank's spokesperson said the government incentive would remain and that the government would decide on it [GM0063]. Former Bangladesh Bank governor Atiur Rahman said in May 2024 that cash support on remittances should continue for several more years and even be increased [GM0064]. For whether it is 2.5% or 2% in 2026, see "How do I send money home safely?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

### Compensation money

Since 12 September 2024, compensation paid by an employer or insurance company for a migrant who died or was physically harmed while working abroad earns the incentive at the normal rate when it comes through legal channels; Bangladesh Bank made the change on the instruction of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare [GM0118, GM0121]. Two conditions: the source of the money must be confirmed, and it must be converted to taka as soon as it arrives [GM0119]. Compensation received before that date does not get the incentive [GM0120].

### Tax on money sent to other relatives

The 2024-25 budget decided to tax remittances sent to anyone other than a spouse, children or parents, banker Faruque Mainuddin wrote in June 2024; he said a significant share of remittances comes through other relatives, so the tax risks cutting legal remittances [GM0075, GM0076].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the tax on remittances sent to relatives other than a spouse, children or parents in force in 2026, at what rate, and who deducts it?

### The banks' own incentive, October 2023 to May 2024

In October 2023 BAFEDA (the Bangladesh Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association) and ABB (the Association of Bankers, Bangladesh), both bodies of commercial banks, decided that banks would pay their own 2.5% on top of the government's [GM0061, GM0023]. In May 2024, when Bangladesh Bank introduced a new way of setting the dollar rate (see How the rate is set), banks stopped it: a remittance dollar then fetched Tk 120, and would have fetched Tk 123 with it [GM0059]. BAFEDA's chairman, Sonali Bank's managing director Afzal Karim, said they stopped because the government incentive alone takes the dollar close to Tk 120, and other banks would decide for themselves; Bangladesh Bank's spokesperson Mezbaul Haque said banks had been advised to stop [GM0062, GM0063].

### Who gets the incentive

Former Bangladesh Bank governor Mohammed Farashuddin said in March 2024 that a large part of the incentive was going to big players and middlemen, who pass part of it to the people sending money, keep the rest, and bring the money in through illicit routes [GM0024].

### Proposals not known to be in force

- **A card for remittance senders:** in April 2024 the parliamentary standing committee on the finance ministry recommended a special smart card for remittance senders and their families, giving migrants' parents, spouses and children priority at government hospitals, government offices and with law enforcement, and smooth immigration and customs clearance at airports [GM0042, GM0043].
- **VIP treatment:** the chief adviser of the interim government said in 2024 that remittance senders should be treated as VIPs, Unilever Consumer Care chairman Masud Khan wrote in September 2024 [GM0126].
- **A tax on all remittances:** in March 2024 the executive director of PRI (the Policy Research Institute, a private research body), Ahsan H Mansur, said the IMF (International Monetary Fund) was talking of taxing remittances; he said he supported it but that it is a political question with different effects on society [GM0027]. He became Bangladesh Bank's governor on 14 August 2024 [GM0111].

> **Need to know**
> - Was the special smart card for remittance senders, or any VIP service for them, ever introduced, and if so, how does a family get it?

## Exchange rates and timing

### How the rate is set

BAFEDA and ABB have set the dollar rate from time to time by supply and demand, on Bangladesh Bank's advice [GM0023]. On 8 May 2024 Bangladesh Bank introduced the "crawling peg", a managed rate, with a mid-rate of Tk 117 a dollar against the earlier official Tk 110; exchange houses abroad then set the remittance dollar at Tk 117 to Tk 117.50 [GM0060]. Bank sources said in mid-2024 that Bangladesh Bank had also eased its oversight of the dollar price and relaxed the rules on bringing money in legally [GM0080]. Its monetary policy of 18 July 2024 kept the dollar at Tk 117 and signalled a market-based rate later [GM0086]. In August 2024 the band for interbank dealing was widened from 1% to 2.5%, letting banks raise the mid-rate from Tk 117 to as much as Tk 120 [GM0110]. On 29 August 2024 the treasury heads of the banks agreed that none would pay more than Tk 120 a dollar for remittances bought from exchange houses abroad, to end the exchange houses' practice of holding money back for the highest bidder [GM0112, GM0113]. Bank treasury officials said in September 2024 that the race to outbid each other had eased [GM0117].

### What a remittance dollar fetched

- **Late December 2023:** Tk 109.50 through legal channels, down from Tk 110.50 six weeks earlier, plus the government's 2.5% and the banks' 2.5%; some banks were paying Tk 123, and hundi paid more than the banks [GM0001, GM0002, GM0007].
- **February 2024:** official rate Tk 110, but banks paid more than Tk 120 because of the dollar shortage [GM0021].
- **Late March 2024:** banks' rate down to Tk 115-116 [GM0028].
- **End of April 2024:** Bangladesh Bank let banks pay up to Tk 117; with the 5% in incentives that came to about Tk 123; some troubled banks paid more [GM0053, GM0054, GM0052].
- **May 2024:** about Tk 120 with the government incentive, after the banks dropped theirs [GM0059].
- **June 2024:** Tk 117 on paper, but banks paid exchange houses up to Tk 118.30 early in the month and Tk 118-119 later; the bank paying most got the most dollars [GM0070, GM0073].
- **July 2024:** Tk 117-118, until Bangladesh Bank told the top dozen banks to pay more if needed to keep remittances out of hundi [GM0098].
- **End of July to end of August 2024:** Tk 120, then agreed by the banks as the most they would pay [GM0106, GM0112].
- **October 2024:** Tk 122 including the incentive, and some banks paid more [GM0154].
- **December 2024:** Tk 121-122 before the month, then up to Tk 127.50, after Bangladesh Bank told banks to pay off old import bills within December [GM0164, GM0160]. Exchange firms abroad bought at Tk 124 (Sonali Exchange Company), Tk 124.18 (Ria Money Exchange), Tk 124.70 (TapTap Send) and Tk 125.60 (Western Union), so banks at home had to pay more [GM0159]. Mutual Trust Bank's managing director said some firms were buying dollars from smaller ones and selling them on to banks at a higher price [GM0165].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a remittance dollar fetch at a bank and from a hundi agent in November 2026?

### When money moves

- **The first money home:** usually six months to a year after a new worker goes abroad, experts told Prothom Alo in January 2024 [GM0005].
- **Before Eid:** every year, towards the end of Ramadan before Eid-ul-Fitr, migrants send more than usual; in April 2024 the usual Eid rise did not show [GM0032, GM0033].
- **Before Eid-ul-Azha:** in June 2024, the month of Eid-ul-Azha (17 June), USD 2.542 billion came, the most in any month for three years [GM0081, GM0077, GM0078].
- **Month-end:** a bank managing director said money that arrived earlier is often counted only when accounts are updated at month-end [GM0056].

### When the system shuts down

During the quota-reform protests of July 2024, a curfew was imposed from the night of 19 July; banks were closed from 19 to 23 July, broadband internet was off for 5 days and mobile internet for 10, and no remittance could arrive through banks in those days [GM0084, GM0105]. Mutual Trust Bank's managing director Syed Mahbubur Rahman said money sent in that time should arrive once banks reopened [GM0085]. On 24 July, the day banks reopened, the SWIFT system (the network banks use to move money between countries) was cut off and many banks got no remittances; they were back to normal the next day [GM0097]. Some migrants campaigned to send money outside banks, a bank managing director said; in early August many announced they would send nothing, then, after the government fell on 5 August, campaigned to send more [GM0099, GM0115]. Remittances fell to about USD 1.91 billion in July and rose to USD 2.22 billion in August [GM0104, GM0114].

## Hundi: how it works and the risks

### How it works

Hundi rings abroad buy dollars from migrants and pay the family in taka through partners in Bangladesh, people in the sector told Prothom Alo in January 2024; Bangladeshi-owned money exchange and remittance companies are also alleged to be involved in laundering [GM0006]. Banker Mamun Rashid wrote in April 2024 that the family gets the same amount in taka, or even 5 to 10% more, and relatives are even given loans in advance [GM0049]. The Saudi riyals, UAE dirhams and other currency stay abroad, where they are used to under-invoice imports, evade tax or move assets out of Bangladesh [GM0050]. PRI's Ahsan H Mansur said in January 2024 that money launderers were buying migrants' dollars abroad [GM0004]. Recruiting-agency owners told Prothom Alo that agency owners also use hundi for visa trading, because there is no legal way to send money abroad [GM0008]. Licensed firms take part too: economic analyst Mamun Rashid wrote in August 2024 that many exchange houses under common ownership had worked with hundi, and, with banks among their owners, gave migrants advance loans and delivered more taka to families door to door [GM0107]. A Prothom Alo editorial of October 2024 said rings in several countries lure migrants with a better price, take their earnings and pay the families through relatives and business partners in Bangladesh [GM0153]. Mohammad Jalal Uddin Sikder of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies wrote in December 2024 that USD 10-12 billion leaves Bangladesh every year through hundi and funds drugs, human trafficking, gold smuggling and false information in India and Myanmar [GM0166].

### Gold bars instead of remittance

In July 2024 many migrants in the Middle East were reported to be buying gold bars instead of sending money, because a bar brought home fetches a little more taka and can cover the return air ticket and even the fare from the airport to the village [GM0109, GM0116]. For how much gold may be brought in, see "How much gold and cash can I bring?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

### Why people use it

- **Rate:** the gap between the formal and informal dollar rates pushes many migrants to hundi, said Zahid Hussain, former lead economist at the World Bank's Dhaka office, in January 2024 [GM0012]; if hundi pays more, money comes that way, said Mustafa K Mujeri, executive director of InM (the Institute for Inclusive Finance and Development) and former chief economist of Bangladesh Bank [GM0030].
- **Debt:** a study by North South University's Centre for Migration Studies found the pull of hundi is tied to migration costs: workers who borrowed to go want to repay fast and use the channel that pays most [GM0009]. Mujeri said most recent migrants went on loans and are sending money, but not through legal channels [GM0031].
- **Reach and credit:** the same study found hundi networks reach remote areas and promise faster, cheaper delivery, and if a worker cannot send one month, the hundi trader sends the money on credit, to be repaid the next month; banks and exchange houses have not reached migrants' doorsteps [GM0010, GM0011].
- **Incentive alone:** a Prothom Alo editorial of November 2024 said the incentive announced when the dollar crisis grew did little, because nothing effective was done to stop hundi [GM0157].
- **Ease:** an unskilled worker in Dubai, rather than go through an exchange house's complicated process, makes one phone call to a hundi operator, agrees a rate and the family is paid, Unilever Consumer Care chairman Masud Khan wrote in September 2024 [GM0125].

### After the change of government

The chief adviser of the interim government, Muhammad Yunus, called on migrants in his first address to the nation to send more through legal channels [GM0167]. Bankers said in October 2024 that the campaign of July-August not to send money legally had turned into a campaign to send it legally, and many were using banks [GM0152]. In January 2025 Bangladesh Bank's spokesperson Husne Ara Shikha said hundi had come somewhat under control because money laundering had fallen, and that banks were working harder to collect remittances to settle overdue foreign debts; Mutual Trust Bank's managing director Syed Mahbubur Rahman said less careless bank lending had cut laundering and so brought hundi under control [GM0171, GM0172].

For the law on hundi, see "Is hundi illegal?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc; for its use in scams, see "Hundi and money mules" in the Dalal scams doc.

> **Need to know**
> - When a hundi payment to the family goes missing, what can the worker or the family do, and has anyone recovered money?

## Bringing cash home, and saving it

### Cash at the airport

Bangladesh Bank's governor Abdur Rouf Talukder said in March 2024 that anyone arriving from abroad who declares the dollars they carry will not be questioned, and the dollars can be deposited in an RFCD (Resident Foreign Currency Deposit) account at a bank; he said banks were taking about USD 500,000 in cash deposits a day without asking questions [GM0025, GM0026]. For the amount you may carry without declaring, see "How much gold and cash can I bring?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

### Changing money

In Dhaka's open market in mid-December 2024 a dollar was bought at Tk 126.70 and sold at Tk 127.50, up from Tk 122.50 and Tk 123 ten days earlier, mainly because people travelling abroad in December wanted dollars [GM0161, GM0163]; in early November 2024 banks sold cash dollars at Tk 120 [GM0156]. Bangladesh has about 250 authorised money changers but more than 700 firms doing the business, former ICAB (Institute of Chartered Accountants of Bangladesh) president Jamaluddin Ahmed said in January 2024 [GM0065].

> **Need to know**
> - How can a returnee check that a money changer is one of those authorised by Bangladesh Bank?

### Wage Earners' Development Bond

The Wage Earners' Development Bond is a five-year government savings bond for Bangladeshis earning abroad, introduced in 1981, with profit paid at simple interest [GM0134]. From 3 December 2020 it and the two US dollar bonds together were limited to foreign currency worth Tk 1 crore, and because the money could not be reinvested automatically, migrants were forced to withdraw it [GM0135, GM0136]. A notification of the IRD (Internal Resources Division, the government division that sets these rules) removed the limit from 1 December 2024 [GM0130, GM0144]. As reported in November 2024:
- **Who can buy:** the worker himself, or in the name of a person he nominates; government employees at Bangladesh embassies abroad; and, again from 1 December 2024, non-resident Bangladeshi mariners, pilots and cabin crew working in the offices abroad of foreign-owned shipping and airline companies [GM0138, GM0133].
- **Where:** Bangladesh Bank, exchange houses, exchange companies, and the foreign and AD (authorised dealer) branches of scheduled banks; no foreign-currency account is needed [GM0137].
- **What it pays:** 12% on foreign currency worth up to Tk 15 lakh, 11% from Tk 15 lakh to Tk 30 lakh, 10% from Tk 30 lakh to Tk 50 lakh and 9% from Tk 50 lakh to Tk 1 crore; the profit is free of income tax [GM0142, GM0137].
- **How long:** five years, reinvested for two more terms if wanted, 15 years in all [GM0134, GM0132].
- **Loans:** can be taken against the bond [GM0137].
- **If the holder dies:** a death-risk benefit of 40 to 50% is paid to the nominee if the holder dies before the bond matures, up to Tk 20 lakh, only if the holder was under 55, and the nominee must apply within three months of the death; the heirs get the principal and profit at maturity [GM0139, GM0140, GM0141].

The US Dollar Premium Bond and the US Dollar Investment Bond already had no limit; under the November 2024 rules they allow one term of investment and four of reinvestment [GM0131, GM0143].

Seafarers, pilots and cabin crew had been barred from the bond by IRD and Bangladesh Bank notifications of August and September 2020 [GM0145]. They send about USD 600 million a year through foreign banks and money exchanges; the Bangladesh Merchant Marine Officers' Association (BMMOA) says more than 12,000 seafarers have no pension or provident fund and work on 4- to 9-month contracts [GM0146, GM0147]. BMMOA's vice-president feared the word "non-resident" in the 2024 notification would cause trouble for seafarers; the IRD secretary said the professions are named and IRD would clarify if needed [GM0148, GM0149].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the bond pay on amounts above Tk 1 crore now the limit is gone, and are the 12% to 9% rates still the rates in 2026?
> - Which papers does an exchange house abroad ask for to sell a worker the bond, and can a worker whose permit has expired buy one?

### Pension

In January 2024 Bangladesh's ambassador in Geneva, Md Sufiur Rahman, spoke of including expatriates in Bangladesh's universal pension scheme with special priority, and Bangladeshis in Switzerland asked the government to act on the obstacles to sending money into the pension scheme [GM0067, GM0068].

## Remittance numbers, kept short

### By year

- **Calendar years:** about USD 27 billion in 2024, 23% more than 2023; USD 21.90 billion in 2023, against USD 21.30 billion in 2022 [GM0170, GM0003]. The World Bank and KNOMAD had forecast USD 23 billion for 2023 [GM0013].
- **Fiscal years:** USD 24.78 billion in 2020-21, the highest so far as of April 2024; USD 21.03 billion in 2021-22; USD 21.61 billion in 2022-23; USD 23.91 billion in 2023-24 [GM0014, GM0015, GM0079].
- **Workers against money:** in 2023 more than 1.3 million workers went abroad, the most in any year and 13% more than in 2022, while remittances rose 2.88%, RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) said [GM0016]. Experts linked the gap to hundi and money laundering [GM0006, GM0012].
- **The hidden part:** former finance minister AHM Mustafa Kamal said only 51% of the money migrants send comes through legal channels, putting the total at USD 40-42 billion [GM0045]. Bank officials believed some money laundered out of Bangladesh was coming back disguised as remittance [GM0022]; in June 2024 a bank managing director said many were bringing laundered money back, encouraged by the budget's offer to whiten black money and by offshore banking [GM0074].

### Months in 2024

USD 2.101 billion in January, USD 2.166 billion in February (38.77% more than February 2023), USD 1.997 billion in March, USD 2.043 billion in April (21% more than April 2023), USD 2.25 billion in May (the most in 46 months), USD 2.542 billion in June, about USD 1.91 billion in July (the lowest in 10 months), USD 2.22 billion in August, USD 2.40 billion in September (80% more than September 2023), USD 2.39 billion in October, USD 2.20 billion in November and USD 2.639 billion in December, the most in any month so far [GM0017, GM0019, GM0020, GM0029, GM0055, GM0069, GM0077, GM0104, GM0114, GM0151, GM0155, GM0158, GM0169]. Why June and July moved as they did is under When money moves and When the system shuts down.

### Where it goes

- **Districts receiving it, July 2023 to February 2024:** Dhaka USD 5.23 billion, Chattogram USD 1.42 billion, Sylhet USD 870 million, Cumilla USD 810 million, Noakhali USD 460 million [GM0040]. Bank officials said districts with many migrants receive less than expected because many migrants have settled abroad and are selling assets at home to take the money out [GM0041].
- **Districts sending workers, fiscal year 2023-24:** Cumilla 80,572, Brahmanbaria 49,391, Chattogram 43,008, Tangail 38,712, Chandpur 34,956, Kishoreganj 32,907 [GM0051].
- **Rural effect:** former state minister for planning Shamsul Alam said in January 2024 that remittances through formal and informal channels were softening inflation in rural areas [GM0066].

### Worldwide

IOM (the International Organization for Migration) put global remittances at USD 128 billion in 2000 and USD 831 billion in 2022, of which USD 647 billion went to low- and middle-income countries [GM0057, GM0058]. Global remittances reached USD 857.3 billion in 2023; India received the most (USD 115 billion), then Mexico, China and the Philippines, and Bangladesh ranked seventh [GM0102, GM0103, GM0101]. Under the UN Sustainable Development Goals, sending USD 200 should cost no more than 3%; receiving money in India cost 5.01% on average in January-March 2024 [GM0100, GM0129].
