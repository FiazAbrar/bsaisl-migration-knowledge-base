# Sending money home

How people working abroad send money to Bangladesh, what it costs, the official incentive, the hundi trade and its risks, and what customs allows on the way home, as of November 2026. Each country's own doc gives what is specific to sending money from that country. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## In one minute

A new worker usually needs six months to a year abroad before sending money home, experts told Prothom Alo in 2024 [GM0005]. Money then comes one of two ways. Through a bank, an exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, it earns the family the government's 2.5% cash incentive; in early 2024 banks added another 2.5% of their own, until they stopped in May 2024 [GM0001, GM0039, GM0059]. Through hundi, the informal and illegal money-transfer trade, an agent abroad takes the worker's riyals or dirhams and a partner in Bangladesh pays the family in taka, often at a better rate, through networks that reach remote villages, and sometimes on credit [GM0007, GM0006, GM0010, GM0011]. Workers who borrowed heavily to go are pulled to whichever channel pays more, because they want to clear the debt fast [GM0009]. The foreign currency hundi collects is used abroad to move money out of Bangladesh [GM0050]. In 2024 a former finance minister put the legal share at only 51% of the money migrants send [GM0045]. The gap between the bank rate and the hundi rate decides much of this, and it moves: in December 2023 a migrant got Tk 109.50 a dollar through a bank, while hundi paid more; in May 2024, after Bangladesh Bank changed how the rate is set, a remittance dollar fetched about Tk 120 with the government incentive [GM0001, GM0007, GM0059].

## Ways to send money and what each costs

### Banks and exchange houses

Legal remittances arrive through banks in Bangladesh; in 1 to 23 February 2024 private banks brought USD 1.404 billion of USD 1.646 billion, state-owned banks USD 175.5 million, one specialised bank USD 63 million and foreign banks USD 3.5 million [GM0018]. Through a bank or licensed exchange house the family gets the government incentive (see The government cash incentive) [GM0001].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a bank or exchange house charge a worker in the Gulf or Malaysia to send USD 200 home in 2026, and how long does it take to reach the family?

### Mobile wallets: bKash

Money sent into a bKash account earns the family the government incentive of Tk 25 per Tk 1,000, bKash officials said in April 2024 [GM0039]. According to bKash, about 1 million migrants sent a little over Tk 965 crore through it from 12 March to 10 April 2024, and Tk 6,252 crore came through bKash in 2023 [GM0038].

Taking the money out, as of April 2024 [GM0034, GM0035, GM0036, GM0037]:
- **At a bank ATM:** Tk 7 per Tk 1,000 for remittance money, at about 2,700 ATMs of 19 banks, started from the bKash app or by dialling *247#. The banks: The City Bank, BRAC Bank, Jamuna Bank, Trust Bank, First Security Bank, Social Islami Bank, Mercantile Bank, Community Bank, NCC Bank, Shahjalal Islami Bank, NRB Commercial Bank, Union Bank, Midland Bank, Rupali Bank, BASIC Bank, Shimanto Bank, Bangladesh Krishi Bank, ICB Islamic Bank and Citizens Bank.
- **At a bKash agent:** normally Tk 18.60 per Tk 1,000.
- **At one of two "priyo" (favourite) agents:** Tk 14.90 per Tk 1,000, for up to Tk 50,000 a month at that rate.

> **Need to know**

### What keeps people off the legal channels

A parliamentary standing committee on the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment listed 10 obstacles to sending money legally, as reported in April 2024 [GM0046, GM0047, GM0048]:
- the gap between the legal and the illegal exchange rate;
- workers without valid papers;
- no Bangladeshi bank branches abroad, or too few;
- no Bangladeshi money exchange firms or exchange points, or too few;
- high fees or service charges, and fixed sending limits;
- the power of hundi traders;
- migrants or their close relatives at home often have no bank account;
- money that does not match the sender's income is obstructed;
- income from unauthorised business or work has no legal channel;
- too little awareness and encouragement to send legally.

The committee on the finance ministry advised in April 2024 that migrants be given national ID cards and birth registration certificates so they can send money without obstacles, and that a smart app be used [GM0044].

> **Need to know**
> - Which papers does a bank or exchange house abroad ask for before sending money, and can a worker whose permit has expired still send through them?

## The government incentive

### The government cash incentive

The government pays a 2.5% cash incentive on money sent through legal channels, credited to the family with the remittance; bKash described it in April 2024 as Tk 25 per Tk 1,000 [GM0001, GM0039]. When banks stopped their own incentive in May 2024, Bangladesh Bank's spokesperson said the government incentive would remain and that the government would decide on it [GM0063]. Former Bangladesh Bank governor Atiur Rahman said in May 2024 that cash support on remittances should continue for several more years and even be increased [GM0064]. For whether it is 2.5% or 2% in 2026, see "How do I send money home safely?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

### The banks' own incentive, October 2023 to May 2024

In October 2023 BAFEDA (the Bangladesh Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association) and ABB (the Association of Bankers, Bangladesh), both bodies of commercial banks, decided that banks would pay their own 2.5% on top of the government's [GM0061, GM0023]. In May 2024, when Bangladesh Bank introduced a new way of setting the dollar rate (see How the rate is set), banks stopped it: a remittance dollar then fetched Tk 120, and would have fetched Tk 123 with it [GM0059]. BAFEDA's chairman, Sonali Bank's managing director Afzal Karim, said they stopped because the government incentive alone takes the dollar close to Tk 120, and other banks would decide for themselves; Bangladesh Bank's spokesperson Mezbaul Haque said banks had been advised to stop [GM0062, GM0063].

### Who gets the incentive

Former Bangladesh Bank governor Mohammed Farashuddin said in March 2024 that a large part of the incentive was going to big players and middlemen, who pass part of it to the people sending money, keep the rest, and bring the money in through illicit routes [GM0024].

### Proposals not known to be in force

- **A card for remittance senders:** in April 2024 the parliamentary standing committee on the finance ministry recommended a special smart card for remittance senders and their families, giving migrants' parents, spouses and children priority at government hospitals, government offices and with law enforcement, and smooth immigration and customs clearance at airports [GM0042, GM0043].
- **A tax on remittances:** in March 2024 the executive director of PRI (the Policy Research Institute, a private research body), Ahsan H Mansur, said the IMF (International Monetary Fund) was talking of taxing remittances; he said he supported it but that it is a political question with different effects on society [GM0027].

> **Need to know**
> - Was the special smart card for remittance senders ever introduced, and if so, how does a family get one?
> - Is any tax charged on remittances received in Bangladesh in 2026?

## Exchange rates and timing

### How the rate is set

BAFEDA and ABB have set the dollar rate from time to time by supply and demand, on Bangladesh Bank's advice [GM0023]. On 8 May 2024 Bangladesh Bank introduced the "crawling peg", a managed rate, with a mid-rate of Tk 117 a dollar against the earlier official Tk 110; exchange houses abroad then set the remittance dollar at Tk 117 to Tk 117.50 [GM0060].

### What a remittance dollar fetched, 2023 to 2024

- **Late December 2023:** Tk 109.50 through legal channels, down from Tk 110.50 six weeks earlier, plus the government's 2.5% and the banks' 2.5%; some banks were paying Tk 123, and hundi paid more than the banks [GM0001, GM0002, GM0007].
- **February 2024:** official rate Tk 110, but banks paid more than Tk 120 because of the dollar shortage [GM0021].
- **Late March 2024:** banks' rate down to Tk 115-116 [GM0028].
- **End of April 2024:** Bangladesh Bank let banks pay up to Tk 117; with the 5% in incentives that came to about Tk 123; some troubled banks paid more [GM0053, GM0054, GM0052].
- **May 2024:** about Tk 120 with the government incentive, after the banks dropped theirs [GM0059].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a remittance dollar fetch at a bank and from a hundi agent in November 2026?

### When money moves

- **The first money home:** usually six months to a year after a new worker goes abroad, experts told Prothom Alo in January 2024 [GM0005].
- **Before Eid:** every year, towards the end of Ramadan before Eid-ul-Fitr, migrants send more than usual; in April 2024 the usual Eid rise did not show [GM0032, GM0033].
- **Month-end:** a bank managing director said money that arrived earlier is often counted only when accounts are updated at month-end [GM0056].

## Hundi: how it works and the risks

### How it works

Hundi rings abroad buy dollars from migrants and pay the family in taka through partners in Bangladesh, people in the sector told Prothom Alo in January 2024; Bangladeshi-owned money exchange and remittance companies are also alleged to be involved in laundering [GM0006]. Banker Mamun Rashid wrote in April 2024 that the family gets the same amount in taka, or even 5 to 10% more, and relatives are even given loans in advance [GM0049]. The Saudi riyals, UAE dirhams and other currency stay abroad, where they are used to under-invoice imports, evade tax or move assets out of Bangladesh [GM0050]. PRI's Ahsan H Mansur said in January 2024 that money launderers were buying migrants' dollars abroad [GM0004]. Recruiting-agency owners told Prothom Alo that agency owners also use hundi for visa trading, because there is no legal way to send money abroad [GM0008].

### Why people use it

- **Rate:** the gap between the formal and informal dollar rates pushes many migrants to hundi, said Zahid Hussain, former lead economist at the World Bank's Dhaka office, in January 2024 [GM0012]; if hundi pays more, money comes that way, said Mustafa K Mujeri, executive director of InM (the Institute for Inclusive Finance and Development) and former chief economist of Bangladesh Bank [GM0030].
- **Debt:** a study by North South University's Centre for Migration Studies found the pull of hundi is tied to migration costs: workers who borrowed to go want to repay fast and use the channel that pays most [GM0009]. Mujeri said most recent migrants went on loans and are sending money, but not through legal channels [GM0031].
- **Reach and credit:** the same study found hundi networks reach remote areas and promise faster, cheaper delivery, and if a worker cannot send one month, the hundi trader sends the money on credit, to be repaid the next month; banks and exchange houses have not reached migrants' doorsteps [GM0010, GM0011].

For the law on hundi, see "Is hundi illegal?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc; for its use in scams, see "Hundi and money mules" in the Dalal scams doc.

> **Need to know**
> - When a hundi payment to the family goes missing, what can the worker or the family do, and has anyone recovered money?

## Bringing cash home, and saving it

### Cash at the airport

Bangladesh Bank's governor Abdur Rouf Talukder said in March 2024 that anyone arriving from abroad who declares the dollars they carry will not be questioned, and the dollars can be deposited in an RFCD (Resident Foreign Currency Deposit) account at a bank; he said banks were taking about USD 500,000 in cash deposits a day without asking questions [GM0025, GM0026]. For the amount you may carry without declaring, see "How much gold and cash can I bring?" in the Frequently asked questions about going abroad doc.

### Changing money

Bangladesh has about 250 authorised money changers but more than 700 firms doing the business, former ICAB (Institute of Chartered Accountants of Bangladesh) president Jamaluddin Ahmed said in January 2024 [GM0065].

> **Need to know**
> - How can a returnee check that a money changer is one of those authorised by Bangladesh Bank?

### Pension

In January 2024 Bangladesh's ambassador in Geneva, Md Sufiur Rahman, spoke of including expatriates in Bangladesh's universal pension scheme with special priority, and Bangladeshis in Switzerland asked the government to act on the obstacles to sending money into the pension scheme [GM0067, GM0068].

> **Need to know**
> - Which universal pension scheme can a worker abroad join, and how does he pay into it from abroad?

## Remittance numbers, kept short

### By year

- **Calendar years:** USD 21.90 billion in 2023, against USD 21.30 billion in 2022 [GM0003]. The World Bank and KNOMAD had forecast USD 23 billion for 2023 [GM0013].
- **Fiscal years:** USD 24.78 billion in 2020-21, the highest so far as of April 2024; USD 21.03 billion in 2021-22; USD 21.61 billion in 2022-23 [GM0014, GM0015].
- **Workers against money:** in 2023 more than 1.3 million workers went abroad, the most in any year and 13% more than in 2022, while remittances rose 2.88%, RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) said [GM0016]. Experts linked the gap to hundi and money laundering [GM0006, GM0012].
- **The hidden part:** former finance minister AHM Mustafa Kamal said only 51% of the money migrants send comes through legal channels, putting the total at USD 40-42 billion [GM0045]. Bank officials believed some money laundered out of Bangladesh was coming back disguised as remittance [GM0022].

### Months in 2024

USD 2.101 billion in January, USD 2.166 billion in February (38.77% more than February 2023), USD 1.997 billion in March and USD 2.043 billion in April (21% more than April 2023) [GM0017, GM0019, GM0020, GM0029, GM0055].

### Where it goes

- **Districts receiving it, July 2023 to February 2024:** Dhaka USD 5.23 billion, Chattogram USD 1.42 billion, Sylhet USD 870 million, Cumilla USD 810 million, Noakhali USD 460 million [GM0040]. Bank officials said districts with many migrants receive less than expected because many migrants have settled abroad and are selling assets at home to take the money out [GM0041].
- **Districts sending workers, fiscal year 2023-24:** Cumilla 80,572, Brahmanbaria 49,391, Chattogram 43,008, Tangail 38,712, Chandpur 34,956, Kishoreganj 32,907 [GM0051].
- **Rural effect:** former state minister for planning Shamsul Alam said in January 2024 that remittances through formal and informal channels were softening inflation in rural areas [GM0066].

### Worldwide

IOM (the International Organization for Migration) put global remittances at USD 128 billion in 2000 and USD 831 billion in 2022, of which USD 647 billion went to low- and middle-income countries [GM0057, GM0058].
