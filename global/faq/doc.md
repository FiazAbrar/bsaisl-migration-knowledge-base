# Frequently asked questions about going abroad

The questions people in Bangladesh ask most before, during and after working abroad, answered for every country, as of November 2026. Each country's own doc, in the `countries` folder, answers them for that country. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## Deciding

### Which countries can I go to for work right now?

As of November 2026:
- **Saudi Arabia:** open, and the largest destination, though departures from January to 17 October 2026 were 45% below the same period of 2025 [F0016, F0015].
- **Singapore:** open; BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared 66,657 workers for it from January to 7 November 2026, the second destination [F0016].
- **Qatar:** open, the third destination, with 53,611 cleared from January to 6 November 2026 [F0017].
- **The Maldives:** issuing new work visas; 32,408 cleared from January to 7 November 2026 [F0018, F0019].
- **Kuwait:** open for company jobs; closed to new Bangladeshi domestic workers, since Kuwait's June 2026 list of approved countries leaves out Bangladesh and the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0020, F0021, F0022, F0197].
- **Jordan:** open for garment factories and for women in domestic work, closed to new recruits in most other jobs since 1 June 2026 [F0023, F0175].
- **Italy:** only through an employer in Italy who names you under the yearly quota; about 10,000 Bangladeshis got work visas in 2025 [F0176, F0177].
- **The UAE:** work and visit visas for Bangladeshis have been restricted since July 2024, with no published ban [F0178, F0179].
- **Malaysia:** a restart was announced on 15 October 2026, but the medical tests it needs had not been approved by 4 November 2026 [F0180, F0181].
- **Oman:** new visas for Bangladeshis suspended since 31 October 2023, apart from a few categories [F0182, F0183, F0184].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** BMET cleared only 11 workers from January to 9 November 2026 [F0185].
- **Cambodia:** BMET cleared none in October 2026, and the Ministry told BMET not to clear workers for 12 countries, Cambodia among them, without its own approval [F0186, F0187].

Prime Minister Tarique Rahman told parliament on 15 April 2026 that steps had been taken to sign bilateral agreements to expand labour markets in Serbia, Greece, North Macedonia, Romania, Portugal, Brazil and Russia, and a recruitment agreement to open Thailand's labour market [CQ0152, CQ0153].

**How many go, and where.** Departures fell in 2024, rose in 2025 and fell again in 2026, and most go to a handful of countries, above all Saudi Arabia:
- **2022 to 2024:** by BMET data reported on 18 December 2024, more than 11 lakh workers went abroad in 2022, 13 lakh in 2023 and 9 lakh in the first 11 months of 2024; overseas employment fell further after Malaysia closed in June 2024 and rose again on Saudi Arabia, which took 60% of the year's workers and more than 80% in October and November [CQ0055, CQ0056, CQ0057]. RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a migration research group) counted 9,06,355 departures in January to November 2024, against 13,05,453 in the whole of 2023 [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. The Ami Probashi 2024 annual report gives 10,09,146 workers in 2024, 27% fewer than the 13,90,811 of 2023, with 62% going to Saudi Arabia [CQ0070, CQ0071, CQ0072]. RMMRU's December 2024 report said no labour migration took place in 2024 to several countries including Oman, Bahrain, the UAE and the Maldives, that Italy was stalled by forged documents, and that Serbia was stalled because the application server was out of order [MA0078].
- **Saudi Arabia's share:** about 6,25,000 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work in 2024, over 7,55,000 in 2025 and about 2,15,000 in the first half of 2026, and recruiting agencies say the rise may not continue [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083].
- **2025:** the expatriates' welfare minister told parliament on 5 April 2026 that 11,32,519 workers went abroad for jobs in 2025, 62,352 of them women, and that more than 1.5 crore (15 million) Bangladeshi workers were then working in 176 countries [CQ0150, CQ0151]. BMET data cited in a Prothom Alo column of 3 April 2026 give 11,16,399 for 2025, about 82.4% of them (9,19,981) to the Middle East or GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) countries, and say about 46% (USD 15.07 billion) of the USD 32.8 billion sent home in 2025 came from GCC countries [CQ0148, CQ0149]. According to BMET, workers went to 141 of the 168 countries on its list in 2025, 90% of them to just five countries, and 13 countries received one worker each [CQ0210]; a Prothom Alo editorial of 28 March 2026 put Saudi Arabia's share of 2025 departures at 67% [CQ0142, CQ0143, CQ0144]. RMMRU said in March 2026 that 4,663 Bangladeshis went to Russia in 2025 against 993 the year before, and that a ring had begun recruiting migrants with promises of good pay and Russian citizenship, part of which, with Russian rings, was sending Bangladeshi workers to the Russia-Ukraine war (see "Russia: recruited into the war" in the Illegal routes doc) [CQ0211, CQ0212].
- **The last fiscal year:** the expatriates' welfare minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, told parliament in a written answer on 3 September 2026 that 9,85,120 Bangladeshis went abroad for work in the last fiscal year, including 6,46,062 to Saudi Arabia, 89,023 to Qatar, 75,041 to Singapore, 42,130 to the Maldives and 39,875 to Kuwait [CQ0176].
- **2026:** BMET says about 5 lakh workers took clearance in January to August 2026, against about 7,40,000 in the same months of 2025 [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083]. BMET data cited in a Prothom Alo editorial of 28 March 2026 show that clearances in the first 10 days of March 2026 fell by about 50% from the same period a year earlier, after the Middle East war closed airspace and several hundred flights from Dhaka's Shahjalal airport were cancelled [CQ0140, CQ0141].
- **Markets that closed or shrank:** Malaysia closed in June 2024 over irregularities, Oman has been effectively closed since 2024, and workers going to the UAE fell from about 1 lakh in 2023 to 47,000 in 2024 and 13,750 in 2025; analysts told Prothom Alo that the closure or shrinking of these markets is a major reason workers have become interested in going to risky countries [RU0079, RU0080].

### Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?

On 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET not to issue clearances for 12 countries without the Ministry's recruitment permission: Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [F0187]. The State Minister has named Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya as high-risk countries where visas must be strictly checked [F0012]. In Cambodia, a study by BRAC (the Bangladeshi development organisation) found 98% of returnees got no job and 90% were forced into online scams [F0189].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the list's Congo actually Kosovo, and is Cambodia on it or Croatia, as other reports of the same 4 November 2026 order give [F0188, F0187]?

### Which countries can a woman go to?

- **Saudi Arabia:** as a domestic worker, aged 25 to 45, and she should pay nothing (see Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?) [F0190, F0191].
- **Jordan:** garment factories, through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government's recruiting agency); 12,784 women went from January to 8 November 2026, the second destination for women after Saudi Arabia [F0192, F0193].
- **Qatar:** domestic work, with the same 25 to 45 age rule for BMET clearance; in August 2025 BMET let one agency send 100 women housemaids to a Qatar employer [F0195, F0233].
- **Kuwait:** company jobs only (230 women from January to 8 November 2026); the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0196, F0197].
- **The Maldives:** women can go, but few do (148 in 2026 to 7 November) [F0198].
- **Singapore:** almost none: 126 women of 70,832 workers cleared in 2025 [F0199].
- **Oman:** 3 women cleared in 2026 to 8 November, after 6,636 in 2023 [F0200, F0201].

Fewer women go than a few years ago. BMET data show the most in one year, 1,21,925, went in 2017, and more than 1 lakh a year went until 2019; in 2023, 77,263 of more than 13 lakh workers were women [AA0028, AA0029, AA0030]. In January to November 2024, 54,696 women went, 6% of all workers and the lowest share in ten years apart from the pandemic, by RMMRU's count [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. Bangladesh began sending women workers in 1991; Saudi Arabia signed its agreement with Bangladesh in 2015, after Indonesia and the Philippines stopped sending domestic workers over allegations of abuse, and most Bangladeshi women migrants go there as domestic workers [AA0031, AA0032]. A joint secretary general of BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) said in January 2024 that Saudi employers want workers quickly once they place a demand, that delays stop the women being sent, and that employers therefore take workers from other countries [AA0045, AA0046].

> **Need to know**
> - Are UAE recruitment offices getting domestic-worker entry permits for Bangladeshi women in November 2026, or are they refused at the visa stage?

### Is there a job for someone with no skills?

Saudi Arabia's skill certificate is not required of domestic workers, though women going to the Middle East for domestic work must take the housekeeping course [F0204, F0094]. Saudi Arabia's skill test, the SVP (Skills Verification Program, run by Takamol), is compulsory for listed trades, and the list includes street, hospital and office cleaners, loading and unloading workers and construction workers [F0202, F0203]. Qatar requires a training and test certificate only for professions its labour ministry lists [F0205]. A Singapore construction job needs a skills test at one of five centres in Bangladesh approved by Singapore's Building and Construction Authority, until the test moves to Singapore in January 2028 [F0024, F0025]. BMET's TTCs (Technical Training Centres) teach 55 trades, and project-funded courses are free and pay a monthly stipend [F0026]. Most who go are not counted as skilled: by BMET data reported in December 2025, about 26% of the 11,98,900 workers who went abroad in FY2023-24 were skilled, against 18% of the 10,15,312 in FY2024-25, when 59% went as low-skilled and only 3% as professionals [CQ0124, CQ0125].

### How much does it really cost to go?

People pay several times the legal maximum (see What is the most an agency may charge me?): workers who went to five Gulf countries on a "free visa" (a visa bought from a sponsor with no real job behind it) spent Tk 4,99,802 on average, against a government-set average of Tk 1,16,224 [F0356]. The ILO (International Labour Organization) and IOM (International Organization for Migration) heads in Bangladesh wrote in December 2025 that, according to the Labour Force Survey 2024, the average cost of migration is over USD 3,500 (Tk 4.62 lakh), about 10 months' earnings, and that Bangladeshi workers pay two to three times what Indian or Nepali workers pay for the same jobs in the GCC countries and Malaysia [CQ0128, CQ0129]. Asif Nazrul, then the expatriates' welfare adviser, said in August 2025 that people spend Tk 8 to 10 lakh to go abroad and many get no work when they arrive [CQ0122]. By country:
- **Saudi Arabia:** about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [F0027].
- **Qatar:** Tk 4 to 7 lakh for a "free visa" [F0028].
- **Malaysia:** Tk 5.44 lakh on average in 2022-24 [F0029].
- **Singapore:** first-time workers are asked for S$9,000 to 13,000, about Tk 8.7 to 12.6 lakh, and some pay up to Tk 16 lakh [F0030, F0357, F0031].
- **Kuwait:** a visa is sold through brokers for KD 2,000 to 2,200, about Tk 8 to 9 lakh [F0032].
- **The UAE:** at least Tk 3.5 lakh for driving jobs, the State Minister said in May 2026 [F0033].
- **The Maldives:** about Tk 3 to 4 lakh in 2024 [F0034].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person, by the CID's (Criminal Investigation Department's) 2024 estimate [F0035].
- **Italy:** one worker paid an intermediary EUR 20,000 [F0036].
- **Jordan garment jobs through BOESL:** the factory pays; the worker pays Tk 200 for fingerprints and Tk 200 for the orientation, and the company repays these before departure [F0206].

On 24 March 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare said the government was working, as part of its 180-day action plan, to fix migration costs, and that once final this would stop extra money being taken (see What is the most an agency may charge me?) [CQ0139].

### What is the most an agency may charge me?

The government fixes the maximum by order [F0207]. If BMET has issued a permission letter for your job, the most is the amount written on it (see What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?) [F0208]. Otherwise the caps reported for each country, with the year each was set or reported, are:
- Saudi Arabia: Tk 1,65,000, set in 2016 [F0209].
- Singapore: Tk 2,62,270, as reported by Bangladesh Bank in 2019 [F0210].
- The UAE: Tk 1,07,780, set in 2017 [F0211].
- Kuwait: Tk 1,06,780, as stated by the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait in August 2025 [F0212].
- Malaysia: Tk 78,990, the cap for the 2022-24 recruitment [F0029].
- Italy: none; the 2017 caps don't cover it [F0213].

A woman going as a domestic worker may be charged nothing [F0191].

### Which fees do I pay to the government?

The main Bangladesh-side fees (the full list for Saudi Arabia is in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Who pays what):
- OEP (Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's website) registration: Tk 200 [F0215].
- A 48-page, 5-year e-passport: Tk 4,025 (regular) [F0217].
- Police clearance: Tk 1,500 on the portal [F0218].
- Welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [F0219].
- Migrant insurance: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card (clearance) fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax at clearance: Tk 250 to 1,200, depending on the visa [F0222].

### How much will I earn?

- **Saudi Arabia:** cleaners and general workers earn SR 800 to 1,000 a month; there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0223, F0224].
- **Qatar:** the legal minimum is QR 1,000 a month, plus QR 500 for housing and QR 300 for food if the employer doesn't provide them [F0225].
- **Malaysia:** the minimum wage is RM1,700 a month [F0226].
- **Singapore:** construction basic pay averages S$820 a month for Bangladeshis [F0227].
- **Kuwait:** the Bangladesh Embassy attests a government-contract cleaner's or labourer's visa only if it pays at least KD 90 a month [F0358, F0228].
- **Jordan garment factories:** JD 230 a month, of which at least JD 130 is cash and JD 100 is paid in kind as housing and food [F0229].
- **The Maldives:** migrant workers surveyed in 2024 averaged MVR 6,760 a month, and there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0230, F0231].
- **Oman:** a minimum of RO 90 a month was agreed between the two governments but is not rigorously enforced [F0232].

### How long will it take to pay back what I spend?

In 2020 men took 19 months or more on average to recover what they spent [F0359]. A Saudi worker on SR 800 with free housing who buys his own food (SR 500 or more a month) and phone (SR 50 or more) keeps at most SR 250, about Tk 8,250 at Tk 33 a riyal [F0223, F0037]; at that rate a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes about 55 months to repay before interest, against a usual two-year contract [F0038]. Many low-skilled Gulf workers struggle to send even Tk 15,000 to 20,000 a month; at that rate the same debt takes 23 to 30 months [F0039]. Malaysia workers who paid high fees stayed in debt for two to three years (2024) [F0040]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column worked through a Saudi pest-control job at the set wage of SR 700 a month, which it gives as Tk 22,400: two years under the contract earn Tk 5,37,600, and after about Tk 2 lakh in living costs Tk 3,37,600 is left to send home [AA0201, AA0202]. It puts the cost of that job at about Tk 5,70,000, including about Tk 4,60,000 to the recruiting agency and about Tk 50,000 to sub-agents [AA0203]; by its arithmetic the worker is still about Tk 2,32,000 short of that cost after two years, and in effect has to work four years for almost no gain [AA0204].

### Who pays for the air ticket?

It depends on the job. On Saudi jobs with a BMET permission letter, the letter can put the airfare on the employer, as Bonanza Overseas' September 2025 letter does [F0360]. On BOESL's Jordan garment jobs the factory bears all the costs [F0206]. On a visa a relative sends from Qatar, the worker usually buys the ticket through the Dhaka broker [F0041]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column says Saudi law puts the worker's visa and recruitment costs on the employer but in practice this is often not followed, and that under Malaysia's G2G (government-to-government) recruitment many workers went for just Tk 35,000 in airfare, a system it says ended because recruiting agencies opposed it [AA0205, AA0206, AA0207].

**What the ticket costs.** BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) members said in a memorandum on 5 February 2025 that every year a syndicate hoards air tickets and sells each at double the price or more, ruining poor outgoing workers, and asked for action against it and for a labour fair for migrant workers [CQ0073, CQ0074, CQ0113]. Ticket agencies said in April 2025 that under the previous government a ring formed around ticket sales in which Biman (Biman Bangladesh Airlines, the state carrier) held back cheap tickets and released dear ones and the ring resold cheap tickets at high prices, and the aviation expert Kazi Wahidul Alam said a Tk 1 lakh ticket abroad carries Tk 40,000 in government taxes [CQ0091, CQ0092, CQ0093]. In February 2025 the civil aviation and tourism ministry issued 10 directives against abnormal ticket prices, requiring tickets to be sold at the Civil Aviation Authority's approved price list and discounted tickets to be given to migrant workers, and Biman's managing director, Md Safiqur Rahman, said in April 2025 that Biman was giving workers discounted tickets [CQ0088, CQ0089, CQ0090]. Biman's T-class fares announced in parliament on 18 June 2026 cut Dhaka-Doha from Tk 53,000 to Tk 46,000, Dhaka-Muscat from Tk 39,000 to Tk 30,000 and Dhaka-Kuala Lumpur from Tk 28,000 to Tk 21,000 [CQ0218].

> **Need to know**

### Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?

BOESL has sent 1.43 lakh workers in 14 years, 1.06 lakh of them to Jordan, where it sends women to garment factories at no cost [F0042, F0193]. It sent 16,066 workers to various countries in FY2024-25, 4% more than the year before, Prothom Alo reported in August 2025 [CQ0123]. In November 2026 it advertised 112 zero-cost jobs for skilled men at DHL in Malaysia, with applications on OEP [F0043]. Recruitment under the October 2025 Saudi agreement goes through private agencies, not BOESL [F0044], and BOESL lists no Qatar recruitment [F0045]. An additional superintendent of police in Cumilla said at an April 2026 roundtable that people should be encouraged to go through government channels such as BOESL, and that those who do are comparatively better off [AA0222, AA0223].

## Agencies and dalals

### How do I check whether an agency is real?

1. Ask for the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and find it on OEP's agency list at www.oep.gov.bd/agencies, which shows its name, address, office phone, licence status and validity date [F0046].
2. Read the status, not the date: a licence can show "Cancelled" while its validity date is years ahead [F0047]. Recruiting after cancellation is a crime carrying 1 to 2 years' prison [F0048]. Status can change back and forth in court: the withdrawal of 49 agencies' licences on 19 July 2026 was suspended by a High Court order on 15 September 2026, and restored when the Appellate Division's chamber court stayed that order on 30 September, according to the Attorney General [MB0096, MB0097].
3. Check the job too, because licensed agencies also cheat: workers paid agencies Tk 3 to 4.2 lakh for Qatar jobs and three of them got fake contract letters [F0049, F0050, F0051].

The number of licensed agencies has grown: RMMRU said in January 2026 that Bangladesh's licensed recruiting agencies, once limited to 850-900, were raised to 2,300 by the previous government, and that 252 new licences were approved in 2025 with 300 more in process, against 192 agencies in India, 464 in Pakistan, 416 in Nepal and 248 in Sri Lanka [CQ0130, CQ0131, CQ0132].

The most active agencies, with their cited red flags, are in the knowledge base's Recruiting agencies doc; the steps for one country are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1.

### Is it legal to go through a dalal?

A dalal (দালাল, broker) is legal only if a licensed agency has registered and appointed him as its sub-agent [F0052, F0053]. Acting as a sub-agent without registration carries 1 year's prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh, and the agency that uses one risks 1 year or Tk 1 to 5 lakh [F0054, F0055]. Most people still go through one: 56% of 1,084 Gulf migrants in a 2025 OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) survey relied on informal or illegal agents [F0056, F0057], and in the Maldives 59% went through a sub-agent or dalal [F0058]. The broker is often a friend or relative [F0059]. Dr Selim Reza wrote in September 2026 that more than 2,000 agencies have been added to RAIMS (the Recruitment Agents Information System), that a 2024 amendment brought sub-agents and intermediaries under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act, and that a 2025 code of conduct provides for holding sub-agents to account [MB0087, MB0088, MB0089]. The Ami Probashi phone app, which works with BMET, has a free chat-based CV builder in Bangla and English, and Prothom Alo wrote in July 2025 that a proper CV reduces dependence on dalals [OA0115, OA0116].

### What is a demand letter?

A demand letter is the employer's written request for workers; for Qatar, the company or its agent files it at the Bangladesh Embassy with the government's visa approval, and the Embassy attests it [F0060]. The Bangladesh mission checks the employer and attests the job demand and visa before the agency selects workers [F0061]. Migration experts told Prothom Alo in February 2024 that migrants already abroad collect job demand from their employers and send it to acquaintances and relatives at home, and that about 90% of demand letters reach Bangladesh this way [CQ0016]. Ask the agency to show you the attested copy (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4) [F0060].

### What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?

It is BMET's approval for an agency to send a set number of workers to a named employer; it shows the job, the salary, the contract terms, who pays the migration cost, and the most the agency may take [F0233, F0234, F0208]. It also makes the agency promise, on stamp paper, to pay the worker's food, lodging, return and compensation if the job is not as promised [F0235]. BMET posts these letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, but the newest one there is dated 10 December 2025 [F0236]. A single visa (up to eight workers for one employer on one demand letter) needs no Ministry recruitment permission, so it has no such letter [F0237].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET still issue permission letters after 10 December 2025, and can a worker get a copy from BMET [F0236]?

### A relative abroad is sending me a visa. Is that safe?

Many are bought "free visas", sold on by a sponsor who will not employ the worker (see "A free visa lets me work for anyone") [F0361]. From Cumilla and other districts about 80% go on visas from relatives and friends [F0238]. Relatives working abroad often get the visa by paying the employer or others and pass it on to people at home [F0239]. A bought visa also shows as genuine on Qatar's visa checker, so check that the company on the visa is the one you will work for and that the job matches [F0240]. Buying or selling a visa inside Bangladesh carries 2 to 7 years' prison [F0241]. What a relative may be sending is explained in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 5.

### How should I pay the agency, and when?

Pay through the bank into the agency's own account and keep every receipt [F0242]. BMET asks people not to go to middlemen and to pay only through the proper channel against a receipt [F0243]. BMET's September 2025 permission letter for Saudi kitchen workers, for example, let the agency take its fee only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and only after the visa, clearance and flight were confirmed [F0208]. In practice most workers pay sub-agents in instalments before the visa is confirmed and get the contract only at the airport [F0244].

### Is there a list of good agencies?

One agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment: Bonanza Overseas (RL-0579) was the Bangladeshi agency in a fair-recruitment pilot that the ILO (International Labour Organization) began in 2018 with QDVC, a construction company in Qatar [F0245]. The Recruiting agencies doc lists the most active agencies with their red flags. The State Minister for expatriates' welfare said on 30 July 2026 that most of the roughly 3,000 licence holders are involved in irregularities, that no more than 400 or 500 licences would survive if the rules were followed, and that many agencies renew their licences without qualifying [MB0061, MB0062]. In May 2026 he said the government will introduce a grading system to identify good agencies, fix a standard migration cost, introduce a "Probashi card", bring the district manpower office, PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) and the welfare centre into one building for a one-stop service, appoint law firms to protect women workers abroad and create a fund to rehabilitate returnees [OA0193, OA0194]. The Prime Minister's spokesman had said on 18 April 2026 that work had begun on a "Probashi card" for migrants, giving no details [CQ0217]. The Prime Minister's deputy press secretary, Shahadat Swadhin, wrote in Prothom Alo on 19 August 2026 that the card was to start on a pilot basis from August 2026, under the Prime Minister's direct planning, to improve migrants' social standing, legal protection and living standards [CQ0174, CQ0175].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the agency grading system announced in May 2026 been introduced, and where are the grades published [OA0193]?
> - Has the Probashi card pilot started, who can get the card, where, and what does it give the holder [CQ0174]?

### The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?

1. Write down who took the money, from whom, when and how much; a Ministry joint secretary said in 2025 that action follows when these are given [F0246].
2. Apply to BMET's Director General with your proof at the workers' complaint cell; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [F0247].
3. Or send an application with proof, your address and the agency's RL number to the Ministry's enforcement branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd; action starts within 3 to 5 working days [F0062, F0063].
4. Mobile courts give immediate remedies to people about to go and returned migrants, free, within 7 working days [F0064].
5. If nothing happens, go up BMET's grievance ladder to the Ministry's Secretary and then the Cabinet Division's system at www.grs.gov.bd [F0065].

The full route is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Scams, If the agency cheats you.

### Is cheating a migrant a crime?

Yes. Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, keeping a migrant's passport or papers, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay carries up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [F0066, F0067]. Publishing an overseas job advert without government or BMET approval carries up to 1 year [F0068]. Migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [F0069]; OKUP gives free legal advice and help filing cases on 01842773300 [F0070].

## Papers

### How do I get a passport, and what does it cost?

Apply for an e-passport exactly as your NID (national ID card) reads; anyone over 20 must use the NID [F0071]. A 48-page, 5-year e-passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express) [F0217]. Pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay, or at a bank [F0072], and bring the original NID to the passport office [F0073]. Delivery after biometrics is within 15 working days (regular), 7 (express) or 2 (super express) [F0074]. At the Gazipur regional passport office in May 2025, people who paid a dalal Tk 1,000 to 1,500 more went straight to the fingerprint and photo room while others waited hours (see "The passport office" in the Departure bottlenecks doc) [OA0089, OA0090]. A mismatch with the NID traps workers already abroad: the opposition leader, Shafiqur Rahman, told parliament on 10 June 2026 that many expatriates could not get e-passports because their MRP (machine-readable passport) and NID data did not match, through small spelling differences in vowel signs or names or a mismatch in age, and that many abroad were becoming or risked becoming undocumented as a result [CQ0173].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker abroad whose MRP and NID differ get the NID corrected or the e-passport issued: at the mission, or only in Bangladesh, and with which papers [CQ0173]?

### How much validity must my passport have?

It depends on the step: the police clearance needs at least 3 months [F0218], and the Qatar Visa Center (QVC, where Qatar-bound workers sign the contract, give fingerprints and take the medical) books only passports with more than 195 days left [F0367, F0075]. Immigration's hotline gets calls from people asking whether four months is enough to travel [F0002]. Since 19 August 2026 the Special Branch runs a 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport, 01320007588, for questions on passport validity and travel bans [F0001].

### How do I get a police clearance certificate?

Apply at pcc.police.gov.bd: register, fill in your details and addresses, upload the scans and submit [F0218, F0076]. Pay Tk 1,500 by treasury challan at Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank, or online by card [F0218]. The police verify you at your present address; track it by texting "PCC S <reference number>" to 26969, and collect it with a copy of your passport [F0077]. The PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825 [F0077]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2. Brokers sell forged certificates: police said a ring, whose arrested members were mainly brokers, took Tk 1,23,000 from a man in Banshkhali, Chattogram, in 2026 on a promise of a quick police clearance and gave him a forged one; his brother said he had a criminal case and so had not applied to the police directly (see "Forged documents and impersonation" in the Illegal routes doc) [OB0012, OB0016, OB0025, OB0026].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the fee Tk 1,500 (the portal) or Tk 500 (Bangladesh Police's guidance) [F0218, F0078]?

### What is attestation, what is an apostille, and which do I need?

An apostille is a single certificate from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) that member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention accept; it is applied for on mygov.bd at Tk 200 a page and takes 5 working days [F0079, F0080]. MoFA lists 114 countries that accept it, including Oman, Saudi Arabia and Italy, but not Qatar [F0081, F0082]. For a country that doesn't accept it, each document is first verified by its issuing body and then attested by MoFA: SSC and HSC (Secondary School Certificate and Higher Secondary Certificate) certificates by the Education Board, then the Ministry of Education, then MoFA [F0083, F0084, F0085]. The Qatar steps, including legalisation by the Qatari mission, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2, item 3; the apostille steps in the Italy doc, Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 4.

### How do I register on OEP?

Registration on OEP is the first step: agencies must pick workers from BMET's database, and BMET clears no one outside it [F0248, F0249]. Open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp in Google Chrome, type your details exactly as on the NID, enter the code sent to your mobile, fill in the form, and pay by bKash, Nagad or another mobile wallet [F0250, F0251]. The fee is Tk 200 [F0215]. Download your Registration Card at the end [F0250]. If it fails, call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [F0252]. The DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office) also does registration and fingerprinting, and gives a three-day pre-decision training on legal migration, avoiding brokers and safety; Sylhet's DEMO said in April 2026 it had registered and fingerprinted 35,290 people in 2025 and 6,290 up to March 2026, and that many people fall in with brokers before the training [LB0232, LB0233].

**What OEP is.** OEP, built under the Ministry with the ILO (International Labour Organization) and the Swiss government, has run since 19 April 2025, after the interim government scrapped the "Ami Probashi" app, through which workers had paid the private operating company a fee on top of BMET's; it was formally launched on 19 November 2025, and its data stay with BMET [CQ0224, CQ0225]. On it a worker registers with a mobile number, fills in a profile, pays the Tk 200 BMET registration fee online, applies for BMET clearance and downloads the clearance smart card, can contact recruiting agencies directly about jobs and can enrol at a TTC; foreign employers can register and, once the Bangladesh mission attests them, post demand letters that go to agencies [CQ0226, CQ0227, CQ0228]. By 19 November 2025, 2,647 recruiting agencies had joined OEP, 10,91,161 workers had applied to BMET for clearance through it and 10,24,468 had got it, and 8,37,042 had registered for TTC training, by the figures shown on the OEP site [CQ0229, CQ0230].

### What is the medical, and how do I book it?

For the Gulf countries it is the Wafid check, booked online and done at a medical centre assigned by your city; people still call it "GAMCA", after the system Wafid replaced [F0253, F0254]. Book the slip yourself at wafid.com: USD 10 for a centre assigned to you, or USD 25 to choose the centre and date [F0255]. The whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 [F0256], yet most workers pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 [F0257]. Check the result on wafid.com's status search; it can take up to 26 days [F0258, F0259]. Qatar-bound workers take their medical at the QVC instead (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4); the Wafid steps are in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

### Can I pay to be passed "fit" in the medical?

The Daily Star reported in July 2025 that agencies offer "fit" packages, Tk 20,000 against a Tk 10,000 standard one [F0260], and that a syndicate reportedly charges Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed fit certificate [F0261]. A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [F0262]. The same report said the approved centres are allegedly controlled by a few owners who issue fake certificates [F0263].

### What if I'm declared unfit?

You can't retake the Wafid test for three months [F0086]. If you think the result is wrong or the centre demanded extra money, complain in writing to the Gulf Health Council within two weeks of the test; anonymous complaints are refused [F0087, F0088]. Overcharging can also be reported to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's enforcement branch with your receipts and the centre's name and address (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4) [F0089].

### Do I need a skill test?

- **Saudi Arabia:** the SVP is compulsory for listed trades; it costs USD 85 per attempt, runs at 27 TTCs and BRTC Gazipur (the Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation's training institute), and a score of 50% passes [F0090, F0091, F0092, F0093]. A test centre had opened in Cox's Bazar by June 2026, and the principal of the Ramu TTC said passing makes a Saudi job easier to get, with a chance of higher pay; his centre runs pre-departure training and three-month technical courses [MB0040, MB0041]. Steps: the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 3.
- **Singapore construction:** a skills test at a Bangladesh test centre until January 2028, when workers will be tested in Singapore after arrival [F0025].
- **Qatar:** only for professions on the labour ministry's list [F0205].
- **Domestic workers:** no Saudi skill test, but women must take the housekeeping course (see What is the housekeeping course?) [F0204, F0094].

There is no way to pass the Saudi test by paying extra, and a worker caught paying has the account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [F0095, F0096].

### How do I join a TTC course?

Log in on OEP, open Training portal, then Training, then Trade/Skills, choose your division, district and centre, pick the trade and press Apply [F0097]. Pay the BMET fee for short courses against a money receipt; project-run courses are free and pay a stipend, and courses last from 3 days to 4 years [F0026]. If anyone offers to get you into or through a TTC for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0098]. Courses vary by centre: the Sylhet TTC said in April 2026 it runs regular Japanese, Italian, Korean and Arabic language courses, and, with ILO (International Labour Organization) support, level-three courses in electrical installation and maintenance and in food preparation that combine language with the trade, with a possibility of work in Italy afterwards [LB0236, LB0237]. A Sylhet TTC senior instructor said many who most need training don't come, college and university students with no immediate need to go abroad come instead, and in the centre's experience about 99% of people go abroad without acquiring a skill [LB0238, LB0239]. The Sandwip TTC trained 750 people in 2025, and BMET data show 2,080 people from Sandwip went abroad that year [FV0109, FV0110, FV0111]. Nationally, according to Ami Probashi's 2024 annual report, enrolment in TTC courses fell to 1,12,166 in 2024 from 2,36,270 in 2023, while BMET registrations rose to 7,98,276 from 6,60,088, with women's share of registrations rising from 2.78% to 4.79% [CQ0110, CQ0111, CQ0112]. RMMRU's acting executive director, Tasneem Siddiqui, said in December 2025 that the skills of TTC-trained workers are not internationally recognised [CQ0126, CQ0127].

### What is the pre-departure orientation (PDO)?

It is a compulsory 3-day course at one of 70 training institutes, booked on OEP; the institute sets the fee and gives a money receipt [F0094, F0099]. Booked through the Ami Probashi phone app, the enrolment fee is Tk 100 plus a Tk 100 platform charge [F0216]. The general secretary of the Ovibashi Nari Sramik Federation (a federation of women migrant workers) said in May 2026 that the three-day training at the district employment office before departure mainly covers first notions of the language, culture and rules, with no in-depth training on the reality of the work, its risks or the long term; a UNDP gender expert said language, culture and practical work skills can't be learnt in three days [OA0218, OA0219]. Expatriates' Welfare Ministry officials said in October 2026 that BMET's pre-departure training is only a three-day workshop in which nothing is taught well, and that workers need training on the destination's type of work, conditions, working hours and staying healthy [CQ0241, CQ0242]. An HIV-negative certificate is compulsory before going abroad, but pre-departure briefings say very little about HIV, safe behaviour or the right to health care abroad, Prothom Alo reported in February 2026 [CQ0206, CQ0207].

> **Need to know**
> - Can the PDO still be booked through the Ami Probashi app, which the interim government is reported to have scrapped in April 2025, or only on OEP [F0216, CQ0224]?

### How do I check my visa is real?

Take copies of the visa and the passport's visa page to BMET in Dhaka or your district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [F0362]; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [F0101]. BMET checks Saudi, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and UAE work visas online, and has a visa-check app on Google Play [F0101]. The State Minister said in 2026 that BMET can verify visas online for only four or five countries [F0011]. Each country doc gives its own online check (for example, see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7).

## BMET clearance and the smart card

### What is the smart card, and do I need it?

The smart card is BMET's emigration clearance, and welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check it before departure [F0102]. It also makes you a member of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB, the Ministry's welfare body for migrants), whose members get help when sick, easier loans from Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), scholarships for their children and Tk 3 lakh for the heirs if they die [F0103, F0355]. It gives you the migrant insurance too [F0220]. Families of workers without clearance or membership don't get the Welfare Board's death grant [F0104].

### How do I get BMET clearance?

- **Through an agency:** the agency applies after the visa, with the visa copy, the attested contract, your registration card and the fees; BMET clears within 3 working days [F0105, F0194].
- **On a visa you got yourself:** apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office with the visa copy, the contract if any, your registration card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and the fees; 3 working days [F0106].

Since July 2023 the application can also be made online through the Ami Probashi phone app, and the card downloaded, without going to BMET [F0365]. Prothom Alo reported in August 2025, and the expatriates' welfare adviser said on 20 August 2025, that the interim government had shut "Ami Probashi", the private app the previous government had allowed for worker registration, and that BMET now issues emigration clearance 100% online on the government's own integrated platform for overseas employment (see How do I register on OEP?) [CQ0219, CQ0220, CQ0222, CQ0223]. The full list of papers is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 6, item 3.

At a press conference of the expatriates' welfare adviser on 24 September 2024, the Ministry stated that sending a worker abroad needed approvals from three bodies and that its own recruitment permission would no longer be required, that brokers would be registered, and that recruiting agencies would be given category certificates based on experience [CQ0025, CQ0026, CQ0027].

> **Need to know**
> - Which three bodies must approve before a worker can be sent, and is the Ministry's recruitment permission, dropped in September 2024, now needed again only for the 12 countries in the 4 November 2026 order (see Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?) [CQ0025, F0187]?
> - Does the Ami Probashi app still give clearance in 2026, or is OEP the only route [F0365, CQ0222]?

### How much does clearance cost?

- Welfare fee: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026, up from Tk 3,500 [F0219].
- Insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax: Tk 400 on a single visa not attested by the embassy, Tk 250 if attested, and Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [F0222]; Tk 500 on a Saudi single visa [F0107].

### How long does clearance take?

BMET's charter promises clearance within 3 working days of a complete application, whether the agency applies or you do [F0105, F0106].

### How do I check that my smart card is real?

Check it at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [F0108]. Airport welfare desks checked 1,391,988 cards in 2023-24 and stopped 1,098 travellers with no or a fake clearance [F0109]. If something is wrong, contact BMET's Director (Emigration), 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [F0110].

### Do I have to pay a bribe to get clearance?

BMET's charter lists only the fees above, and BMET asks anyone offered clearance for money to tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0222, F0098]. The State Minister said in 2026 that some dishonest BMET officials give clearance on fake papers [F0013]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of passengers paying police at the airport to get through are true [F0010].

### I went abroad without clearance. Can I still join the Welfare Board?

Yes. Apply online at portal.wewb.gov.bd; the application is processed through the Bangladesh embassy or high commission [F0111]. Members get a card with a QR code and the Board's benefits [F0112]. The steps for Qatar, including the fee, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7, Welfare Board sign-up if you left without BMET clearance.

## Loans

### Can I get a government loan to go abroad?

PKB, the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days of complete papers [F0113]. The same loan is offered for a re-entry visa over 2 years, with instalments starting after a two-month grace period [OA0110]. Women migrants have a separate loan on the same terms [F0114]. You must be 18 to 60, not in default, and live permanently in the branch's area with your guarantors [F0115]. You need two guarantors, one financially solvent and one family member [F0116]. A Prothom Alo guide of July 2025 lists the papers as your photo, voter ID, address, certificates, copies of the passport, visa and smart card, one guarantor's papers and three bank cheques, with no collateral, and says you must first open an account at the bank [OA0108, OA0109]. A senior PKB officer in Cumilla said in April 2026 that an applicant needs a BMET card, a valid visa, a passport, an NID from the branch's area and a capable guarantor who will repay if needed [AA0237]. Helpline: 16238 [F0117].

> **Need to know**
> - Does PKB ask for two guarantors or one [F0116, AA0237]?

Many borrowers have to take Tk 3 to 4 lakh, with a monthly instalment of about Tk 14,000, and many take four or five months or longer to find work abroad, while the loan weighs on them, PKB's acting regional head in Sylhet said in April 2026 [LB0242]. PKB gave migration loans to 2,63,689 people from 2011-12 to June 2025, Tk 384 crore in all, its deputy managing director said in August 2026 [AB0131, AB0132]. The expatriates' welfare adviser, Asif Nazrul, announced on 24 September 2024 that migrants could now repay their PKB loan instalments by sending remittance directly to the bank, which had not been possible before, and that 12 commercial banks had agreed to lend to migrants once Bangladesh Bank gave a credit guarantee [CQ0023, CQ0024].

**For students.** On 24 March 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare, Md Nurul Haque Nur, said the Prime Minister had directed that students going abroad for higher study (to South Korea, Japan, China, Germany and other countries) get loans of up to Tk 10 lakh through PKB as proof of bank solvency, paid not in cash but against tuition fees or other required payments [CQ0136, CQ0137, CQ0138]. On 18 April 2026 the Prime Minister's adviser Mahdi Amin said a collateral-free bank guarantee of up to Tk 10 lakh had been arranged for higher study abroad, and that a "Probashi Card" for expatriates was being introduced (see Is there a list of good agencies?) [CQ0160, CQ0161].

> **Need to know**
> - Did the 12 commercial banks start lending to migrants under a Bangladesh Bank credit guarantee, and which banks [CQ0023]?
> - How does a student apply for PKB's Tk 10 lakh solvency loan or guarantee, at which branch and with which papers [CQ0136, CQ0160]?

### When can I apply for the PKB loan?

Only once you hold the visa and the BMET smart card, near the end of the process [F0118]. The bank doesn't arrange visas, and finding guarantors stops many people from borrowing [F0119, F0120]. Branches are few: Cumilla's 17 upazilas (sub-districts) have 4 PKB branches, and the Tomson Bridge branch serves 9 upazilas with three officers, a senior PKB officer said in April 2026; she said staff shortages make giving and recovering loans hard, people often complain that the bank won't lend or asks for extra papers, and some borrowers' late repayment makes new lending harder [AA0238, AA0239, AA0240, OA0183, OA0184]. You can apply at the branch or online on mygov.bd, paying nothing when you submit; processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each come after the decision [F0121, F0122].

### Should I borrow from a moneylender?

In a 2020 government survey, 81% of men had borrowed to go abroad [F0123]. In a 2020 IOM (International Organization for Migration) survey, most family loans were interest-free, NGO and bank loans carried 10-15%, and 62% of moneylender debt carried 50-150% interest [F0124]. A microcredit NGO may charge at most 24% a year [F0125]. A quarter of borrowers risk losing the collateral they pledged if they can't repay [F0264], and families mortgage land and sell cattle: a Natore farmer mortgaged his land and sold four cows, then could not fly [F0265]. A former expatriates' welfare secretary said what workers send home often doesn't even cover the interest [F0014].

### Can I borrow abroad?

In Singapore, a worker found borrowing from an unlicensed moneylender has his Work Permit revoked and is sent home [F0266]. Many workers in Malaysia borrow at 10% a month to renew their permits, an economist of the CPD (Centre for Policy Dialogue, a Dhaka think tank) said in October 2025 [F0267].

## At the airport

### What should I carry?

Before you go, check you have [F0268]:
- a valid passport;
- the work visa;
- the medical certificate;
- the demand letter;
- the contract attested by the destination country's embassy;
- the agency's name, address and phone;
- the BMET smart card;
- the air ticket.

Keep one set of copies at home and at least three with you [F0269]. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know: drug offences abroad can carry the death penalty [F0270]. Airport police at Sylhet said in April 2026 that rings target first-time travellers to carry drugs or smuggled goods, and many don't realise they are joining a crime ring [LB0226]. In two cases Amnesty International documented in Saudi Arabia, the accused said they had been forced to carry the drugs, but the court neither examined nor accepted that [OB0058].

### What does immigration ask?

Immigration police ask about your purpose and family, and they compare your visa, BMET clearance, papers and answers [F0271, F0004]. A woman with BMET clearance who said she was joining her husband in Oman was questioned closely, because Oman's general worker visas and women's domestic-worker recruitment are closed, and was stopped when the same spouse-visa approval turned out to have sent five women to Oman [F0008]. The Special Branch's 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport is 01320007588 [F0001].

### Why do people get offloaded?

Immigration police can stop a traveller they doubt from flying and stamp "offload" in the passport [F0271]. The usual reasons are a visa that doesn't match the BMET clearance, fake papers, a breach of visa conditions, or answers that don't match the papers [F0004]. The Special Branch turned back 81,500 passengers from 2021 to June 2026, 7,819 of them in the first half of 2026 [F0005, F0006]. People with valid papers have been stopped too: a man with a valid Malaysia visa and BMET clearance was turned back and then let through after a senior officer intervened [F0009]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of people stopped unfairly are true [F0010].

### Who helps me at the airport?

WEWB's welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards, help returnees with customs and lost luggage, send the sick to hospital by ambulance and take complaints from abused or cheated returnees [F0102, F0272, F0273]. The Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport gives departing and returning workers a short stay; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd [F0274]. A deputy director of the Civil Aviation Authority of Bangladesh said in August 2026 that the Probashi Kallyan desk at the airport is open 24 hours (see "At the airport: the welfare desk" in the Departure bottlenecks doc) [AB0125].

**Lounge and promised services.** The expatriates' welfare adviser said on 24 September 2024 that VIP service for migrants at the airport would start within a month, first for those arriving from the Middle East, with every VIP facility except the lounge and a special immigration desk [CQ0028]. On 5 October 2024 he said an assistant would accompany outgoing workers at every stage at the airport and that a special lounge for migrants was being set up, with the space chosen, and might open within about a month [CQ0029, CQ0030]. The chief adviser of the interim government opened a lounge for migrants at the airport on 11 November 2024 [CQ0109]. The Ministry's August 2025 report said a "Probashi Lounge and Waiting Lounge" had been set up at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, with healthy food at a 30% discount [CQ0221].

**Women coming home.** BRAC's Shariful Hasan said in December 2025 that there is no clear gender-sensitive SOP (standard operating procedure) at the airport for a dignified return, and that immigration or police often do not treat women returning with abuse and trauma humanely [CQ0135].

> **Need to know**
> - Where in Shahjalal airport is the Probashi Lounge, who may use it, and do departing workers get an assistant or a special immigration desk in 2026 [CQ0221, CQ0029, CQ0028]?

## In the destination

### My salary isn't paid. What do I do?

The routes:
- **Qatar:** complain to the labour ministry, which must first try to settle the dispute; a Qatari official advised complaining within a month, because 3 to 4 months' unpaid wages become hard to recover [F0275, F0276]. An unpaid worker may ask to change employer or end the contract, with the journey home at the employer's cost [F0277].
- **Saudi Arabia:** file a friendly-settlement claim with the labour ministry within 12 months; unsettled claims go to the labour court [F0363, F0364]. After 3 months unpaid you may move to another employer without consent [F0279].
- **Every country:** your family can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-1 branch in Dhaka with proof of your status, the employer's name and address and the amounts owed; it is free and the Ministry writes to the labour wing within 7 working days [F0280].

The steps for each country are under "If you aren't paid" in its doc (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, If something goes wrong, If you aren't paid, are abused or your passport is taken).

### My employer has taken my passport. Is that allowed?

No, though it is common:
- **Qatar:** the employer must return it once the residence permit is done, unless you ask in writing for the employer to keep it, and hand it over whenever you ask; keeping it is punishable by a fine of up to QR 25,000 [F0281, F0282].
- **Saudi Arabia:** your passport and iqama (residence card) may never be withheld [F0283].
- **The UAE:** passport confiscation is banned, and you need no employer permission to leave the country [F0284].
- **Kuwait:** keeping it is illegal but widespread; complain to the PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) office where your residence was stamped [F0285, F0286].
- **Singapore:** employers should not keep workers' passports, yet over 31% of Work Permit holders didn't hold their own in 2024 [F0287, F0288].

In Bangladesh, withholding a migrant's passport without lawful reason carries up to 5 years' prison [F0066, F0067].

### Can I change my employer?

- **Qatar:** since 2020 the law lets you move without the employer's consent, under the labour ministry's rules [F0289].
- **Saudi Arabia:** after 12 months, with 90 days' notice, if your contract is on Qiwa (the labour ministry's platform); sooner if you go 3 months unpaid or your work permit or visa expires [F0290, F0279]. One worker wrote in 2025 that he found out how for himself, moved to another company and had worked there about four years on good pay [AA0166].
- **The UAE:** transfers between companies have been restricted for Bangladeshis since July 2024 [F0291].
- **Kuwait:** normally after one year and with the employer's consent [F0292].
- **Malaysia:** the PLKS (Temporary Employment Visit Pass, the work pass) doesn't let you change employer or sector [F0293].
- **Singapore:** you may work only for the employer and in the job on your Work Permit [F0294].
- **Jordan garment factories:** only to another garment factory [F0295].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** the permit can't be moved to another employer [F0296].
- **Italy:** losing or leaving the job doesn't cancel your permit [F0297].

In Saudi Arabia you may not be employed in a profession other than the one on your work permit [F0298].

### I arrived and there is no job, or the visa was fake. What now?

This is the most common failure of a bought visa: in OKUP's 2025 survey, 21% of free-visa workers paid about Tk 1.5 lakh more after arriving to get a work permit, and 14% never got one and stayed on illegally [F0299, F0300]. BMET says 90% of migrants' complaints in 2025 concerned Saudi Arabia, mostly no iqama, no work or unpaid wages [F0301]. The embassy can advise and help in a labour dispute (in Qatar, for example) [F0302], and your family can complain about the agency in Bangladesh (see The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?) [F0247]. If you can't get home, a relative can ask the Ministry for help to bring you back [F0126].

### What happens if I overstay?

- **Saudi Arabia:** without a valid iqama you are undocumented, and police detain and deport undocumented workers who go out [F0127, F0366]; overstaying a visit or Umrah visa brings a 3 to 5 year ban [F0128].
- **The UAE:** AED 50 a day after the grace period that follows the end of a residence permit [F0129].
- **Qatar:** overstaying a visit visa costs QR 200 a day, up to QR 12,000 [F0130].
- **Cambodia:** USD 10 a day, and deportation can take more than six months [F0131].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** a fine, and an expulsion brings a 2-year entry ban [F0132, F0133].

Countries sometimes open grace periods: Qatar gave three months from 9 February 2025 for overstayers to leave [F0134]. How to get home is under "Going home without valid papers" in each country doc.

### How do I send money home safely?

Use a bank, a licensed exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, not hundi (unlicensed money agents) [F0135]. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with another 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps it [F0136, F0137]. In a rejoinder of 24 June 2025 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare said the government was continuing a 2% cash incentive for money sent through banking channels and had stepped up surveillance of hundi [CQ0203]. Sending more at once costs less: in April to June 2026 the average cost was 2.94% for up to USD 200 and 0.50% above USD 500 [F0138]. For bKash, give the partner branch the receiver's bKash number and full registered name [F0139]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [F0140]. The channels for each country are in its doc, under Sending money home.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the government's remittance cash incentive 2.5% or 2% in 2026 [F0136, CQ0203]?

### Is hundi illegal?

Yes, in Bangladesh sending money outside banking channels is a punishable offence [F0141]. Abroad it can cost you the job: in Singapore a worker involved in hundi has his permit revoked [F0142].

### Who can I call for help while abroad?

- Probash-Bondhu, WEWB's 24-hour call centre: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [F0143].
- The Ministry's Complaint Resolution Cell: minister.expat@gmail.com [F0144].
- The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in your country (for example, in Saudi Arabia, 8001000125 free) [F0145].

The labour wing's numbers are under Contacts in each country doc. On legal aid through the missions: 395 migrant workers, 11 of them women, received legal aid through Bangladesh missions abroad in the 2024-25 financial year, according to Wage Earners' Welfare Board data cited in a Prothom Alo column of 17 October 2026, and in September 2026 parliament was told legal-aid agreements had been made with 22 law firms in 13 countries [CQ0199, CQ0200].

> **Need to know**

## Women

### How many women work abroad?

More than 10 lakh Bangladeshi women were working abroad in May 2026, by BMET data, and more than 10 lakh 95 thousand went abroad between 2001 and 2022, though the number of women migrating has fallen in the last few years; the programme director of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) puts women at about 13.5% of all migration [OA0211, OA0212, OA0213]. By BMET data, 1,05,466 Bangladeshi women went abroad for work in 2022 and 62,317 in 2025, a fall of about 41%, while in the first eight months of 2026 more than 42,000 women went to 48 countries, about 14% (5,214) more than a year before, over 90% of them to Saudi Arabia and Jordan [CQ0231, CQ0232, CQ0233]. The expatriates' welfare minister told parliament on 5 April 2026 that 62,352 of the 11,32,519 workers who went abroad in 2025 were women [CQ0215, CQ0216]. About 799 to 800 women migrants have come home dead in the past eight years, by Wage Earners' Welfare Board data cited in May 2026 by Jasiya Khatun, director of WARBE Development Foundation (a migrant workers' rights organisation); she said the cause is mostly given as heart attack or illness, sometimes suicide, and the real cause is not known [OA0210]. A 2023 study by RMMRU found that 31% of migrant women workers' deaths abroad were unnatural, from accidents and suicide [CQ0104].

> **Need to know**
> - How many women went abroad for work in 2025: 62,317 (BMET data reported in September 2026) or 62,352 (the minister in parliament) [CQ0231, CQ0215]?

### Can a woman go abroad for work without her guardian's permission?

For domestic work, no: BMET clears a woman domestic worker only with proof that she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0190]. A woman going on a single visa also needs her guardian's no-objection letter on stamp paper [F0146]. The housekeeping course asks for the same letter [F0303].

### Who can go as a domestic worker?

A woman aged 25 to 45 when she enrols in the housekeeping course (returnees up to 50), who has passed class 5 (which can be relaxed) and is fit [F0304, F0305]. She can't enrol if she is pregnant, if her youngest child is under 6, or if she has a contagious or incurable disease [F0306].

### What is the housekeeping course?

It is compulsory for women going to the Middle East for domestic work: one month (24 working days), residential, at one of 41 TTCs [F0094, F0307, F0308]. Apply on OEP's training portal and pay the set fee [F0309]. You must attend 90% of sessions and score 70% in the final test, and the course teaches how to contact the police and the Bangladesh embassy abroad [F0308, F0310]. The certificate is valid two years for a first departure [F0311]. Brokers have sold course certificates for money [F0312].

**In practice:** people in the migration sector said in January 2024 that the course is skipped in some cases before women are sent to Saudi Arabia, and its quality is questioned [AA0041]. Many women back from Saudi Arabia say the month is not enough; some cannot read or write, many can only give a thumbprint instead of a signature, and they do not know the language of the country they go to, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of January 2024 [AA0047, AA0048]. In April 2026 the principal of the Cumilla TTC said attendance at women workers' training is now monitored online, and that the rule that agencies take at least 10% trained workers must be enforced [AA0247, AA0248]. The project director of RAISE, a government returnee project, said in August 2026 that domestic work abroad means working in 50-degree heat, cooking local food and using modern appliances, which need training [AB0113].

### Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?

No. An agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from her [F0191], and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker [F0313]. The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi worker [F0314]. Yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [F0315].

### Which agencies may send women domestic workers?

Only agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved, each with a Tk 15 lakh security deposit and a woman on the staff; the September 2025 order approved 21 [F0316]. Ask for the approval letter and check the agency's name and RL number are in it (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Step 2) [F0316].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Ministry publish one current list of all agencies approved to send women domestic workers?

### The agent wants to change my age on the passport. Is that a problem?

Yes: agents have raised girls' ages with a second birth certificate, in one case giving a girl under 13 a passport showing 27 [F0317, F0318]. Under a February 2025 circular the national ID data is the basis of the passport, and the person whose passport it is bears the responsibility for any mismatch, the assistant director of the Cox's Bazar regional passport office said in June 2026 [MB0038, MB0039]. Check the birth record your passport will use at everify.bdris.gov.bd with the 17-digit registration number and your real date of birth [F0319]. The Probashi call centre, 16135 (free from Bangladesh), gives workers and families advice and help [F0320].

### What are a domestic worker's rights?

Saudi Arabia's rules are an example:
- at most 10 hours' work a day, with at least 8 hours' continuous rest [F0321];
- one paid day off a week [F0147];
- the employer may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [F0148];
- the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy [F0149].

The rules for other countries are in each country doc's domestic-worker section (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Your rights at work). In practice most women workers work 12 to 14 hours instead of 8, according to the director of BNSK (Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra, a women workers' organisation) in May 2026 [OA0217].

### What if I'm abused? Where can I go?

In Saudi Arabia, the embassy labour wing's free line is 8001000125, and the labour wings run safe homes for women in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [F0145, F0150]. The labour ministry's abuse line, 1919, takes reports round the clock in confidence [F0151]. A relative in Bangladesh can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) for help or to bring you home; it is free and the Ministry acts within 7 working days [F0152, F0153]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash at the airport welfare desk [F0102]. A study by OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) found that 94% of women workers forced to return had suffered regular physical and mental abuse abroad and 47% sexual harassment, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of 19 October 2026 [CQ0201].

**How the way home works in Saudi Arabia:** officials said in January 2024 that most women who leave a household before the two-year contract ends run away to the embassy's safe home, are moved from there to the Saudi government's safe home, and are flown home at the Saudi government's cost; a woman who wants to go back within three months of arriving is sent home through the Saudi company that recruited her [AA0035, AA0036].

**A case:** Lutfa Begum of Brahmanbaria went to Saudi Arabia as a domestic worker in March 2020 and worked 1 month 5 days, in two neighbouring houses where she said the men behaved badly; the person who sent her did not find her other work, and when she asked to go home she was sent to a shelter, where she stayed about three months [AA0037, AA0038, AA0039]. She said she was paid nothing, and after coming home received Tk 30,000 in cash help in two instalments [AA0040].

**Counselling:** the Cumilla manpower office's assistant director said in April 2026 that there is almost no psychological counselling for migrant workers, especially women [AA0252].

### Can women get garment jobs in Jordan?

Yes, through BOESL: its November 2026 notice took women aged 20 to 35 who can run plain and overlock sewing machines, at no cost to the worker [F0322, F0206]. In June 2026 the CID arrested three people who used a Jordan garment company's name and seal to take Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person without a licence [F0323]. Steps: see the Jordan doc, Work visa: garment and factory worker.

## Problems and coming home

### What does the migrant insurance cover?

Workers who left with BMET clearance from 1 January 2023 are insured for Tk 10 lakh by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC), for workers aged 18 to 55, for a one-time Tk 1,000 [F0324]. The cover lasts 5 years from the smart card's date [F0220]. It pays the full Tk 10 lakh on death or total permanent disability, 10% to 90% for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going [F0325]. Job loss is not covered if you went entirely at the employer's cost [F0326]. WEWB helps the worker or family claim [F0327].

**Death after the cover ends, and how the money is paid.** Under the insurance contract of 12 March 2023, a worker who dies naturally or in an accident during the cover period, or within 90 days after it ends, is paid the full Tk 10 lakh, and when a worker dies abroad the money goes to the worker's bank account after the body comes home, without any application, according to the Welfare Board and JBC in January 2024 [CQ0097, CQ0098].

**What it does not pay for.** Under the same contract, as Prothom Alo reported in January 2024, there is no insurance benefit for [CQ0094, CQ0095, CQ0096]:
- suicide or self-harm within six months of cover starting;
- death or illness from HIV/AIDS or a similar disease;
- death in dangerous sports or adventure activities;
- death from alcohol or drug addiction;
- death in war, a riot or an attack by criminals;
- execution under a court sentence.

**In practice.** By JBC's count, 32,24,343 workers went abroad from the start of the compulsory migrant insurance in late 2019 to the end of 2023, and only 1,397 workers or families claimed on it; by the Welfare Board's 2022-23 annual report, Tk 31 crore 72 lakh 10 thousand in death insurance was paid in FY2022-23 to the families of 860 workers [CQ0099, CQ0100]. Disability and early-return benefits must be claimed, the government agencies hold no information on workers who come back disabled or within six months, and people involved said there is no special effort to tell workers about the insurance before they leave, Prothom Alo reported in January 2024 [CQ0101, CQ0102, CQ0103].

> **Need to know**
> - Where and with which papers does a worker who came home disabled or within six months claim the insurance, and how long does JBC take to pay [CQ0101]?

### If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?

1. **At the airport:** a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial for every body that arrives, free, collected within 3 months of the body's arrival [F0328].
2. **From the Welfare Board:** a Tk 3 lakh grant, if the worker had BMET clearance or membership and died in the country the clearance was for [F0104, F0329]. If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the body was buried abroad, apply within 1 year [F0330].
3. **From the insurance:** Tk 10 lakh [F0324].
4. **From the employer:** labour wings recover death compensation, wages and benefits, and the Board pays the heirs; 1,852 cases brought in Tk 127.6 crore in 2023-24 [F0331].

The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents [F0332]. The Cumilla Probashi Kallyan Centre's assistant director said in April 2026 that financial help for bringing a body home is given for BMET card holders, that a free ambulance takes the body from the airport, and that Tk 25,000 is paid after the body arrives and up to Tk 3 lakh in eligible cases [AA0233]. The president of a Cumilla advocacy forum called it a major humanitarian problem that the family is not told when someone dies or goes missing abroad [AA0253]. By Welfare Board data, in the first six months of 2026 it paid Tk 9.45 crore for burial, Tk 61.56 crore in grants and more than Tk 44 crore in insurance, and recovered about Tk 75 crore in compensation, unpaid wages and insurance from abroad; people in the sector said families must visit the Board day after day after applying [CQ0238, CQ0239]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the airport payment for transport and burial Tk 35,000 or Tk 25,000 in 2026 [F0328, AA0233]?

### How is the body brought home?

The family applies to the Welfare Board, which takes their wish (body home or burial abroad) to the embassy and follows the case, free [F0333, F0334]. The Ministry also brings home bodies of irregular, undocumented and destitute workers: the next of kin applies with the cause of death, proof of status and the death certificate; free, 7 working days [F0335]. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports is available on request [F0336]. The Minister said in August 2026 that the government brings home the body of any Bangladeshi who dies abroad, whether the person went legally or not, and gives the family financial help and help with the children's education [AB0102]. Bodies can take months: eight Bangladeshis who died on a boat off Tunisia in February 2024 reached Dhaka on 2 May 2024, and went to the Dhaka Medical College morgue for post-mortem before being handed to their families [LB0025, LB0026]. Welfare Board officials said in October 2026 that bodies from Middle Eastern countries need approval from several offices before they can be sent, which takes some time though less than before, and that outside pandemic years under 1% of migrants who die are buried in the destination country [CQ0236, CQ0237].

**The numbers.** From January to 30 September 2026, 4,060 bodies of migrant workers reached Bangladesh, more than 14 a day, Prothom Alo reported, while Welfare Board data show 3,651 bodies came home in 2019 and 3,140 in 2020 [CQ0234, CQ0235]. In a Welfare Board analysis of 2018 to 2022, 18,166 bodies came home, of which 3,698 died in road accidents, workplace accidents, fires, falls from buildings or electric shock, 2,649 of them in road accidents [CQ0240].

### Why do so many families get no compensation from the employer?

Many deaths are recorded as "natural causes": 71% of deaths of Bangladeshis in Qatar from November 2016 to October 2020, by Amnesty International's reading of Bangladeshi government records [F0337]. Amnesty found in 2021 that without investigation or post-mortem, this rules out compensation from employers [F0338]. The Welfare Board's grant is paid anyway [F0104].

### What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?

The Welfare Board asks the embassy to bring a sick or injured worker home on the worker's or family's application [F0339]. Back home, workers with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment, applying within 1 year of return [F0340, F0341]. The insurance pays for permanent disability (see What does the migrant insurance cover?) [F0342].

### What happens if I'm arrested abroad?

The cases below are from Saudi Arabia, where Bangladeshis are the largest group of foreign workers, at least 25 lakh [OB0064, OB0065, OB0066].

- **Executions:** according to ESOHR (the European Saudi Organisation for Human Rights, based in Berlin), 59 of the 108 people Saudi Arabia executed from January to June 2026 were foreigners, including 49 of the 71 executed for drug offences, and Bangladeshis were among the 108, though how many is not known [OB0059, OB0060, OB0061]. Two Bangladeshis convicted of an "immoral act" in a mosque in Mecca were executed shortly before October 2026 [OB0063]. According to Amnesty International, 597 people were executed in Saudi Arabia for drug offences alone from January 2014 to June 2025, about 75% of them foreigners, in a country whose population of about 3 crore 53 lakh is about 44% foreign [OB0062].
- **Your rights on paper:** Saudi Arabia's 2013 Law of Criminal Procedure gives an accused person the right to legal help during both investigation and trial, and bans torture [OB0067, OB0068, OB0069].
- **In practice:** ESOHR documented that death-row prisoners in Khamis Mushait, Najran and Tabuk prisons had no professional interpreter or legal help, did not understand the proceedings, and often did not know when their sentence was upheld or their plea for pardon rejected [OB0067, OB0068, OB0069]. Amnesty, documenting 25 foreigners convicted of drug offences (from Egypt, Ethiopia, Jordan, Pakistan and Somalia), found many went through the system alone, without understanding or receiving the court papers or charges, without consular help, and at risk of confessions taken without an adequate interpreter or lawyer [OB0070, OB0071]. It found that low education, poor language skills and lack of money harm these migrants twice: brokers cheat or exploit them from the start of going to Saudi Arabia, and if they face legal trouble they cannot hire a good lawyer or defend themselves properly, and in most cases have less access to consular help [OB0077, OB0078, OB0079]. In December 2024 the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination found racial and ethnic discrimination in Saudi Arabia's criminal justice system, with migrant and domestic workers, including women, subjected to arbitrary detention, torture and ill-treatment, and called on Saudi Arabia to impose a moratorium on the death penalty [OB0082, OB0083].
- **What the family is told:** Amnesty says Saudi law has no clear rule on warning the prisoner or family before an execution; families said the prisoner may at most phone 24 hours before, some prisoners were told by prison officers the day before "your turn has come", and many families learned of an execution from other prisoners' calls or from the state news agency [OB0072, OB0073, OB0074].
- **The body:** Saudi law does not guarantee the body is returned after an execution; in all three of Amnesty's 25 cases executed between January and June 2025, Saudi Arabia neither returned the body nor told the family where it was buried [OB0075, OB0076].

ESOHR could learn or record nothing before the execution about 91 of the 108 people executed in early 2026, so there is no answer on how many Bangladeshis are held, on what charges, or whether they had lawyers, interpreters or consular help [OB0064, OB0065, OB0066].

> **Need to know**
> - When a Bangladeshi is arrested in Saudi Arabia, does the embassy's labour wing learn of it, and does it arrange a lawyer or interpreter? Whom should the family contact first?
> - How many Bangladeshis are on death row in Saudi Arabia, and for which charges?

### What help is there when I come back?

Airport welfare desks give food and water to returnees from jail, help with customs and lost luggage, and take complaints [F0272, F0273]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [F0102]. PKB's rehabilitation loan lends returnees up to Tk 50 lakh for a project at 9% simple interest, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral [F0343]. It needs a trade licence and a guarantor, and in 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees got PKB's special reintegration loan [F0344, F0345]. PKB's managing director, Md. Mujibur Rahman, said in March 2024 that loan conditions for returnee women had been eased: a woman whose husband or family won't take her back can borrow without a fixed address, her loan carries no service charge, and loans up to Tk 1 lakh need no equity, though few women apply [CQ0105, CQ0106]. Prothom Alo reported in March 2025 that few workers benefit from the projects begun for returnees after the pandemic [AA0142]. Hasan Imam of the Center for Development Communication said in December 2025 that the government's reintegration policy, begun in 2022, received final approval in 2025, with six objectives: employment and financial self-reliance, entrepreneurship, social reintegration and health care, psychosocial support, support for exploited and abused women, and sustainability [CQ0133, CQ0134]. Other lenders, the RAISE reintegration project and what returnee leaders say: see Help after you get home [AB0110, AB0136, OA0221].

**Returnees with HIV.** According to the national AIDS/STD control programme of the Directorate General of Health Services, 1,891 people were found infected with HIV in Bangladesh from December 2024 to November 2025, 12% of them people who had come back from abroad [CQ0208, CQ0209]. The government supplies antiretroviral (ARV) drugs free, but counselling, mental health support and livelihood rehabilitation for returnees are very limited, Prothom Alo reported in February 2026; Mir of the youth organisation Brave Dimension Global said one deported worker got counselling not from any government agency but from a private organisation [CQ0204, CQ0205].

### What can I bring home without paying duty?

Under the Baggage Rules 2025 a returning worker aged 12 or over may bring up to 65 kg free, plus 35 kg more of clothes and personal items [F0346, F0347]. One of each of a list of goods is duty-free, including a laptop, an oven, a rice cooker, a fan and a TV up to 29 inches [F0348]. You may bring 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one, the new one once a year [F0349]. Bigger items such as a fridge or air conditioner pay a fixed duty [F0350]. A Bangladeshi passport holder may bring no alcohol [F0351]. The full list is in the Qatar doc, Coming home, What you can bring home.

### How much gold and cash can I bring?

Up to 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery in all, declared, once a year, with no more than 12 pieces of one type, is duty-free [F0352]. One gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) is allowed once a year if declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; anything more, or a hidden bar, is confiscated [F0353, F0354]. Up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency needs no declaration [F0154].

### Is there help for my children?

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC level) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [F0156].

## Myths

### "A free visa lets me work for anyone."

Brokers sell it as "go and work wherever you like," for Tk 5 to 7 lakh [F0157]. In fact a Saudi employer may not employ a worker in a profession other than the one on the work permit [F0298], and buying and working on a free visa is illegal and punishable in Qatar [F0158]. The company named can cancel the visa at any time, making the worker illegal [F0159]. Of 10 men one Cumilla broker sent to Saudi Arabia, three got a proper job with a contract (2024) [F0160].

### "A visit visa can be changed to a work visa there."

- **Saudi Arabia:** a visit visa can't be converted to an iqama, and offers to convert one for a fee are fraud [F0161].
- **Qatar:** you must leave and re-enter on a work visa [F0162].
- **Oman:** conversion inside the country stopped for all nationalities on 31 October 2023 [F0182].
- **The UAE:** a visit visa gives no right to work, and working on one brings fines [F0163].

Working on a tourist or Umrah visa in Saudi Arabia brings a ban of 3 to 10 years [F0128].

### "The agency guarantees the job."

The Bangladesh High Commission in the Maldives warned in October 2026 against paying anyone who promises a guaranteed job or a quick work permit [F0164]. A Bangladeshi construction worker in the UAE told a 2019 study that the contract signed in Bangladesh differs from the one the company keeps [F0165]. In Cambodia, 98% of returnees BRAC studied found no job [F0189].

### "Pay a little extra and the medical will be fit."

A bought "fit" result can still get you deported: a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and sent home [F0262].

### "Pay extra and you'll pass the skill test."

There is no way to pass Saudi Arabia's SVP for extra money; a worker caught paying has the account locked [F0095, F0096].

### "Saudi Arabia abolished the kafala."

The kafala (sponsorship system) still stands: the October 2025 story began in an article by a UK think tank and cited no Saudi source [F0166].

### "The iqama is the worker's cost."

The employer pays the iqama, the work permit and their renewals [F0167]. A Bangladesh embassy official confirmed the employer should pay, though employers push it onto workers [F0168].

### "If I have a job, my visa is safe."

In the UAE an employer can cancel a visa online without the worker knowing [F0169]. The UAE began cancelling many Bangladeshis' visas in July 2026, and on 13 October 2026 the Law Minister said 5,000 had been cancelled [F0170, F0171].

### "Malaysia has opened again."

In early September 2026 the Ministry warned that the process of sending workers to Malaysia was not final, that it would be announced by notice, and that no one should pay anyone before then [MB0076, MB0077]. Recruitment was announced on 15 October 2026, but on 4 November 2026 the minister said the medical tests it needs had not yet been approved [F0180, F0181]. BOESL ran its own call for 112 DHL jobs (see Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?) [F0043].

### "Oman is opening soon."

In August 2026 the Bangladesh Embassy in Muscat said Oman had made no formal announcement on recruiting Bangladeshis, and in September 2026 the State Minister said visas remain restricted [F0172, F0184].

### "Kyrgyzstan is a door to Europe."

A man who paid a broker Tk 4.8 lakh on a promise of Italy was taken to Bishkek and spent 44 days in a Central Asian prison [F0173, F0174].
