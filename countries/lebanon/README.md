# Lebanon: migration guide

Sources checked up to 23 Nov 2026.

What happens to Bangladeshis who go to Lebanon for work, for Bangladesh passport holders, as of November 2026. This first version is built only from Prothom Alo reporting (2024 to May 2026) and still needs official sources: it says little yet about how a Lebanon job is found, what it costs or what it pays. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh). IOM is the International Organization for Migration, the UN migration agency, which helped bring Bangladeshis home from Lebanon in 2024 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2 (2024)](#n2), [N3 (2024)](#n3)</sup>. "Kafil" is the word the Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut used for the employer of Dipali Begum, a Bangladeshi woman worker in Beirut (see Cases) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

## 1 Background

<a id="in-one-minute"></a>

### 1.1 In one minute

Somewhere between 70,000 and 150,000 Bangladeshis live in Lebanon, by the government's and a research group's estimates, and by the research group's count more than half are women; the reporting shows one woman who worked there as a domestic worker, and another who lived and moved with her employer's family (see [The size of the community](#the-size-of-the-community)) <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5), [N4](#n4), [N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>. Lebanon has twice been a war zone for them since 2024 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N4](#n4)</sup>. In the Israeli strikes and ground attack of autumn 2024, about 1,800 Bangladeshis registered to come home, and only 167 of them had valid papers; the government and IOM flew 1,246 home free of charge by January 2025 (see [Leaving during a war: the 2024 evacuation](#leaving-during-a-war-the-2024-evacuation)) <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2), [N5 (2024)](#n5), [N7 (2024)](#n7), [N8](#n8)</sup>. Many did not want to come home despite the danger, the Foreign Adviser said, because they had spent a lot of money to go and did not know what they would do at home <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. In the 2026 war, a woman worker was killed with her employer and members of his family in Beirut in April and two men in south Lebanon in May; her body took a month to come home (see [If a worker dies](#if-a-worker-dies)) <sup>[N4](#n4), [N9](#n9), [N10](#n10)</sup>.

<a id="is-it-open-now-and-who-can-go"></a>

### 1.2 Is it open now, and who can go?

<a id="status"></a>

#### 1.2.1 Status

In April 2026 Israel was still attacking Lebanon, and Bangladeshi workers there were living in fear; the Middle East war began on 28 February 2026, and the attacks on Lebanon did not stop with the US-Iran ceasefire of 8 April 2026 <sup>[N4](#n4), [N10](#n10)</sup>. On 11 May 2026 an Israeli drone strike killed two Bangladeshi workers in south Lebanon <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In the earlier war, Israel began air strikes on Lebanon on 17 September 2024 and a ground attack across its southern border on 30 September 2024 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which clears Bangladeshis going abroad for work) clear workers for Lebanon in 2026, and how many did it clear in 2024, 2025 and 2026, men and women separately?
> - Has the Bangladesh government restricted sending new workers, especially women domestic workers, to Lebanon since the 2024 or 2026 wars?
> - Which Lebanese work visa or permit does a Bangladeshi domestic worker or company worker get, and who applies for it?

<a id="the-size-of-the-community"></a>

#### 1.2.2 The size of the community

- **Government estimates, October 2024:** 70,000 to 100,000 Bangladeshis in Lebanon (the Foreign Adviser and the Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut); about 100,000 (the Expatriates' Welfare Adviser) <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5), [N11 (2024)](#n11), [N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- **RMMRU, April 2026:** about 150,000 Bangladeshi workers, more than half of them women. RMMRU is the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a Dhaka migration research group <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="who-works-there-and-where"></a>

#### 1.2.3 Who works there and where

The reporting shows a woman who worked as a domestic worker, and a woman worker who lived and moved with her employer's family <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6), [N4](#n4)</sup>. It shows men working in Beirut (one was killed in the Hazmieh area on his way to work) and living in the south, in Nabatieh, one of them as a labourer in a fruit orchard <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13), [N9](#n9), [N14](#n14)</sup>. One man lived there with his wife, and their son was born there (see [Family in Lebanon](#family-in-lebanon)) <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

<a id="how-a-job-reaches-you-and-who-takes-a-cut"></a>

### 1.3 How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

In 2026 a Satkhira man went to Lebanon through a neighbour, his family said <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. The Foreign Adviser said in October 2024 that many did not want to come home from the war because they had spent a lot of money to go <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a Lebanon domestic-worker job reach a woman in Bangladesh: through licensed agencies with BMET permission letters, through relatives already in Lebanon, or through brokers?
> - Do men go to Lebanon on visas sold with no real job behind them, and who sells them in Lebanon?

<a id="what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep"></a>

### 1.4 What you pay, and what you earn and keep

In 2026 a Satkhira man who went to work in a fruit orchard spent about Tk 12 lakh, most of it borrowed, his family said: his father sold a cow, took a loan from an NGO and borrowed from relatives <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. A man who returned in October 2024 after 10 years in Lebanon said he came back empty-handed <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What does a woman pay to go to Lebanon as a domestic worker in 2026, and what does a man pay?
> - What is the monthly wage of a Bangladeshi domestic worker in Lebanon, and is it paid in US dollars or Lebanese pounds?

<a id="what-ties-you-to-the-employer"></a>

### 1.5 What ties you to the employer

In 2026 one woman worker moved with her employer's (kafil's) family from the Tyre area to Beirut to escape the war, where she, her employer and members of his family were killed in an air strike on his house <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. About 90% of those who registered to leave in 2024 had become irregular, the Foreign Adviser said, and to leave they needed an exit pass that carries a fine (see [Without valid papers](#without-valid-papers)) <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Can a Bangladeshi worker in Lebanon change employer or leave the country without the kafil's consent?
> - Does the kafil hold workers' passports, and what happens to a worker who leaves the employer's house?

<a id="what-goes-wrong-most-and-what-people-wrongly-believe"></a>

### 1.6 What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

<a id="war"></a>

#### 1.6.1 War

People who moved from south to north Lebanon in 2024 to escape the bombing were attacked there too, and were living in inhuman conditions by the sea, returnees told the Expatriates' Welfare Adviser <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>. A returnee who lived 15 days by the sea with his wife and baby said no place in Lebanon was safe <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>. At least one Bangladeshi was killed in the 2024 war and three in the 2026 war by May 2026 (see [Cases](#cases)) <sup>[N8](#n8), [N4](#n4), [N9](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="losing-papers"></a>

#### 1.6.2 Losing papers

Of the 1,800 who registered to leave in October 2024, no one except 167 had valid papers, the Foreign Adviser said <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="work-visa-domestic-worker"></a>

## 2 Work visa: domestic worker

More than half of the Bangladeshi workers in Lebanon are women, by RMMRU's count, and the reporting shows one woman who worked there as a domestic worker (see [Who works there and where](#who-works-there-and-where)) <sup>[N4](#n4), [N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What are the steps from Bangladesh: who gets the Lebanese visa, which medical test, which BMET clearance, and is there a training course at a Technical Training Centre for Lebanon?
> - What are the minimum age, the contract length and the rules on rest days, the passport and phone for a domestic worker in Lebanon?
> - Where does a domestic worker complain if she is not paid or is mistreated, and what has worked in practice?

<a id="without-valid-papers"></a>

## 3 Without valid papers

About 90% of the Bangladeshis who registered to leave Lebanon in October 2024 had become irregular, the Foreign Adviser said; to leave, they needed an exit pass, which carries a fine, and he said the government was arranging to have the fine waived <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. IOM said it was also working to bring home Bangladeshis without valid papers <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. How deportees and people on an outpass are received at Dhaka airport is covered elsewhere (see "Deportees and outpass holders" in the Departure bottlenecks doc).

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker become irregular in Lebanon: an expired residence permit, leaving the employer, or the employer not renewing?
> - How large is the exit-pass fine, who issues the pass, and was the fine waiver of 2024 actually granted?

<a id="family-in-lebanon"></a>

## 4 Family in Lebanon

A man from Narsingdi who went to Lebanon seven years before October 2024 took his wife there three years before; their son was born there and was 11 months old when the family came home <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>. A domestic worker from Rupganj who came home with her five-year-old son had five more family members still in Lebanon <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which Lebanese visa lets a Bangladeshi worker bring a spouse, and can a child born in Lebanon get a Bangladesh passport from the embassy in Beirut?

<a id="leaving-during-a-war-the-2024-evacuation"></a>

## 5 Leaving during a war: the 2024 evacuation

When Israel's 2024 attacks began, the Bangladesh government said it would bring home everyone who wanted to return and bear the cost, and it paid the repatriation costs, with IOM's help <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12), [N7 (2024)](#n7), [N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. For those who chose to stay, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs said steps were being taken for their safety <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. These are the steps returnees went through.

<a id="step-1-register-with-the-embassy-or-iom"></a>

### 5.1 Step 1: Register with the embassy or IOM

1. On 9 October 2024 the Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut asked Bangladeshis who wanted to come home to register their names with it <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
2. Some registered with IOM instead: of 82 who came home on 21 November 2024, 76 had registered with the embassy and came at government expense, and 6 had registered with IOM and came on IOM's funding <sup>[N3 (2024)](#n3)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How did people register with the embassy in 2024: by phone, by a form, or in person, and what did they need to give?

<a id="step-2-the-exit-pass-if-your-papers-have-expired"></a>

### 5.2 Step 2: The exit pass, if your papers have expired

1. An irregular worker needed an exit pass to leave, and the pass carries a fine; the government said it was arranging for the fine to be waived <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="step-3-the-flight"></a>

### 5.3 Step 3: The flight

1. In early October 2024 Beirut's airport was not safe for flights, the Foreign Adviser said, and the government had advised looking for alternative places outside the war zone from which to evacuate Bangladeshis <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
2. No more than about 50 seats a day were available from Beirut, the Foreign Adviser said, so the government considered taking people by sea to Mersin in Turkey and flying them on <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.
3. Groups flew from Rafic Hariri International Airport in Beirut to Dhaka through Jeddah, or through Dubai; later groups came on Emirates, including a flight the government chartered <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2), [N11 (2024)](#n11), [N3 (2024)](#n3), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.

<a id="step-4-at-dhaka-airport"></a>

### 5.4 Step 4: At Dhaka airport

1. Officials from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and IOM met the returnees at the airport <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
2. IOM gave each returnee Tk 5,000, food and first aid, and the first group also psychological support <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N8](#n8), [N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

<a id="how-many-came-home"></a>

### 5.5 How many came home

- 54 on 21 October 2024, the first group, including 7 children <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.
- 119 by 23 October 2024, after a second group of 65 <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- 268 by 31 October 2024, in six groups <sup>[N11 (2024)](#n11)</sup>.
- 697 by 21 November 2024 <sup>[N3 (2024)](#n3)</sup>.
- 963 by 5 December 2024 <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
- 1,246 on 19 flights by 17 January 2025 <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.

By 21 October 2024 the government had spent Tk 17.55 crore on support, Tk 55 lakh of it on food aid, the Expatriates' Welfare Adviser said <sup>[N12 (2024)](#n12)</sup>.

<a id="health-care"></a>

## 6 Health care

On 23 June 2024 the Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut ran a free health camp at the Al Classico stadium in Beirut, where more than 300 Bangladeshis got free care and medicine; the medical team of the Bangladesh Navy ship BNS Sangram, serving in the UN peacekeeping mission in Lebanon, gave the advice and medicine <sup>[N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does a domestic worker in Lebanon have health insurance through her employer, and who pays a hospital bill?

<a id="sending-money-home"></a>

## 7 Sending money home

The Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut gave awards for the first time to the workers who sent the most money home through legal channels in 2023: nine workers, two of them women <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>. One woman worker sent money home through a shop: her family learned of her death from the shop's owner, who phoned them <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. Informal transfers carry their own risks (see "Hundi: how it works and the risks" in the Sending money home doc).

> **Need to know**
> - Which banks and exchange houses in Lebanon send money to Bangladesh legally, and what do they charge?

<a id="if-a-worker-dies"></a>

## 8 If a worker dies

<a id="bringing-the-body-home"></a>

### 8.1 Bringing the body home

In both wars, flights limited when a body could come home. In November 2024 a killed man's wife in Lebanon was told that his body could not be sent to Bangladesh because there were no flights during the war, diplomatic sources said <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>. In April 2026 the State Minister for Foreign Affairs said flights were not good enough to bring a killed woman's body home at once <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the embassy would bring her body home when the situation allowed <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. Her body came home a month after her death; the steps in her case were:

1. The body was kept at the hospital morgue in Beirut, and the embassy contacted the hospital <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
2. Her sister said officials told the family to come to Faridpur, their home district, and apply; she said she would apply to the Foreign Minister and to the Faridpur Probashi Kallyan Centre, the government's migrant welfare office in the district <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.
3. The Faridpur centre started work and contacted Lebanon through the embassy <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>.
4. The embassy got a certified police report and a permit to send the body, and completed the other paperwork <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
5. The body flew from Beirut on Emirates through Dubai to Dhaka <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
6. At Dhaka airport on 7 May 2026 the State Minister for Foreign Affairs handed the body to the family, who took it to the village overnight in a government-arranged ambulance <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>.

The Faridpur centre's assistant director said the war and disrupted flights caused the delay <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. The State Minister said that in wartime a body comes home only through talks with various countries and when flights are running, often with Saudi Arabia's help, and that following each government's rules takes time <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Who pays to fly a body home from Lebanon, and what does the employer, the embassy or the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the Bangladesh government board that runs welfare services for migrant workers and their families) each cover?

<a id="what-the-family-gets"></a>

### 8.2 What the family gets

In the April 2026 case, Faridpur officials said the government would take the body home by free ambulance, give the family Tk 35,000 for burial at the airport and later Tk 3 lakh <sup>[N17](#n17), [N10](#n10)</sup>. When the body arrived in May, the Tk 35,000 was paid at the airport, with the Tk 3 lakh to follow under government rules <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. It could not be said whether any help would come from the Lebanese government, since she died in war, the district employment and manpower office said <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The upazila nirbahi officer (UNO, the head of the sub-district administration) gave the family Tk 10,000 and a dry-food package from the Ministry of Relief <sup>[N17](#n17)</sup>. The general rules for all countries are covered elsewhere (see "If a worker dies abroad" in the Services for migrants and their families doc).

<a id="cases"></a>

### 8.3 Cases

- **2 November 2024:** Mohammad Nizam, 31, of Kasba, Brahmanbaria, killed by an air strike at a coffee shop in Beirut's Hazmieh area on his way to work <sup>[N13 (2024)](#n13)</sup>.
- **8 April 2026:** Dipali Begum, 34, of Charbhadrasan, Faridpur, a woman worker, killed with her employer and his family, seven people in all, when an Israeli air strike brought down the employer's house in Beirut's Hamra area; she died in Rafic Hariri Hospital <sup>[N4](#n4), [N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **11 May 2026:** two men from Satkhira district, Shafikul Islam, 45, of Satkhira Sadar, a fruit-orchard labourer who had arrived two and a half months earlier, and Nahidul Islam, 40, of Ashashuni, the neighbour through whom he went, killed by an Israeli drone strike on a residence in the Zebdine area of Nabatieh, south Lebanon; their bodies were at Nabih Berri Hospital in Nabatieh; Shafikul's wife, left with two children, asked how she would repay the money he borrowed to go <sup>[N9](#n9), [N14](#n14)</sup>.

<a id="coming-home"></a>

## 9 Coming home

Returnees of 2024 asked the government for help to restart: one, back with his wife and baby, asked to be rehabilitated; another, back empty-handed after 10 years, asked for work at home or to be sent to another country <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>. What is offered to returnees is covered elsewhere (see "Coming home" in the Services for migrants and their families doc).

## 10 Contacts

<a id="bangladesh-embassy-in-beirut"></a>

### 10.1 Bangladesh Embassy in Beirut

- **Who:** in May 2026 the embassy was headed by Mohammad Anwar Hossain, First Secretary (Labour) <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. In early 2024 he was First Secretary (Labour) and Md. Baki Billah was Counsellor (Political) and head of chancery <sup>[N16 (2024)](#n16)</sup>.
- **What it has done:** registered people to fly home in 2024; handled the papers to send a body home in 2026; held a special service week for consular and welfare services from 22 to 31 December 2023, with a public hearing, and a free health camp in June 2024 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N10](#n10), [N16 (2024)](#n16), [N15 (2024)](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What are the embassy's address, phone, WhatsApp and emergency numbers, and its hours for workers?
