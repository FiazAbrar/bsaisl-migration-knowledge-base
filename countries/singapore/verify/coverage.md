# Singapore doc: coverage audit

**1. The migrant's questions.** Every question is answered.
- How long can I stay: up to 2 years a permit, renewable, work until 64, no cap on total years since July 2025 (Step 1 and Your Work Permit); visitors under How long you can stay.
- Earnings: by sector and by years of experience, with what the IPA says (What the job pays).
- Rights at work: full section on pay, deductions, hours, leave, medical care, housing, lorries, heat and passport.
- Jail: overstaying, side work, drugs, vapes, alcohol, littering, riots and strikes, hundi, moneylenders, money mules, SIM cards, bribery, marriage and pregnancy, bans.
- Bank account and SIM: the POSB Payroll Account, freezing on exit, free SIM at Onboard, SIM registration limits.
- Pension: none in Singapore (no CPF for Work Permit holders); Probash pension, WEDB and NRCTA on the Bangladesh side.
- Family: Work Permit holders can't bring dependants; Family passes and Having a child cover everyone else.
- What I can bring home: the 2025 baggage rules, cash limits on both sides.

**2. The original aim.** Each visa has its types, requirements, where to apply, cost, processing time, appeal, scams done to the migrant and tried by the migrant, and whether the holder can work. Bringing a spouse and having a child are covered (Family passes; Having a child), and so are changing visa type, asylum (written to discourage) and corridor information (BMET numbers and rank, comparisons with Indian and Myanmar workers, the 2020-21 closures, the 2028 test move, NTS list growth).
- Comparing Bangladesh with other origin countries by size isn't possible: MOM doesn't publish by nationality, and the doc already says so.

**3. The insider test.** All present: syndicates (13 agencies in 2022, 10 in 2024, "six companies" in 2026, "OTC-centred syndicate"), Weltec and Oasis, kickback convictions (Lian Cheng, Weishen), the 2026 unpaid-wage firms, phantom-worker companies, real costs (Tk 8 to 16 lakh, S$9,000 to 13,000), training-centre package scams, visa trading, Seatrium, the 2015 and 2026 radicalisation cases, COVID dormitories, the 2021 entry ban, lorry deaths, repatriation agents, the Mustafa Centre and Little India. Two gaps:
- The "six companies" behind the State Minister's monopoly claim are unnamed, and whether the promised summons happened is unknown. No fact names them.
- The doc doesn't say what happens to a worker kept in Singapore to help an investigation (a likely outcome after reporting a kickback or an employer under investigation).

**Uncited facts.** 159 facts are uncited. Almost all were rightly left out:
- Duplicates of cited facts: B0257 to B0260, R0092, R0201 to R0205, R0215, R0219 to R0222, R0228, R0274, C0039, C0144, B0204, B0205, B0209, B0210, B0212, E0023, E0114, P0317, P0337 and others.
- 2018 rules restated by newer cited facts: R0189, R0192 to R0195, C0102, C0105.
- Statistics with no bearing on a decision: C0012, C0015, C0021, C0030, C0033, C0062, C0069, C0125, C0128, C0189, B0074, D0219, D0239, D0243, D0253.
- Employer-side detail: D0050, D0051, D0072, D0079, D0148, D0180, R0055.
- Stale High Commission IPA-attestation details: P0111 to P0113, B0185, B0186, D0195, D0197, D0202. IPA attestation ended in April 2025 (B0287).
- B0173 (Board membership through missions) is already covered by B0040.
- P0315 (14 days to issue after transfer) belongs to the existing 7-or-14-days Need to know, which already cites the same page's rule.
- C0131 and B0280 (remittance from Singapore of US$550 million in July to November 2025, US$475 million in FY2024-25) don't change a migrant's decision. They do make the minister's US$1.49 billion figure, printed in the doc, look doubtful, and the fact-checkers may want to look at it.
- Only E0287 to E0290 (Special Pass holders and the Temporary Job Scheme during investigations) fill a gap: fix 1.

### FAIL Work while held for an investigation

MOM refers eligible Special Pass holders who need help finding a job to employment agencies, and helps those who can't pay for their upkeep [E0290, E0289].
