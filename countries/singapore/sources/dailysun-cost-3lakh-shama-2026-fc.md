# Govt targets cutting migration cost to Tk3 lakh: Shama

**BSS, Dhaka**

Published: 10 Aug 2026, 10:49 PM

State Minister for Foreign Affairs Shama Obaed Islam on Monday said the government is working to reduce overseas migration costs to a maximum of Tk3 lakh to protect aspiring migrant workers from excessive recruitment fees.

“The initiative, taken after the government assumed office, aims to curb current migration expenses,” a foreign ministry statement quoted her as saying at a migration-related event.

The junior minister said in many cases currently the migration costs ranged between Tk1 million and Tk1.4 million “due to syndicates and unscrupulous intermediaries”.

The Bonik Barta newspaper and Singapore’s Thakral Group jointly staged the event titled “Bangladesh Remittance Awards 2026” recognising expatriate Bangladeshis for their contribution through remittances.

She said around 150,000 Bangladeshis are currently employed in various sectors in Singapore alone.

Shama said Bangladesh received about US$45 billion in the 2024-25 fiscal year, of which US$475.03 million came from Singapore.

The state minister said a dedicated “Migration Wing” was established off late at the foreign ministry for the first time to provide services to migrant workers and ensure quick resolution of their problems.

The wing operates through a joint working group with the Expatriates’ Welfare and Overseas Employment Ministry.

She said Bangladesh and Singapore are expanding cooperation in manpower and employment, with ongoing discussions on skills development, re-employment, inter-company transfers and fair working conditions.

Shama urged expatriates to send remittances through legal banking channels instead of hundi, pay respect to host countries' laws and work culture, and use social media responsibly.

She thanked Singaporean officials at the country’s foreign and manpower ministries for their support in assisting Bangladeshi workers affected by unpaid wages and job losses.

Fifteen outstanding Bangladeshi expatriate “remittance warriors” in Singapore were honoured at the event, which acknowledged four organisations, two Commercially Important Persons CIPs and three leading exchange houses operating under Bangladesh-based banks in Singapore.
