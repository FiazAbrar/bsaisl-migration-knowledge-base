                                         Highlights

Migrant workers' satisfaction with both their working and living conditions continued to improve.
In 2024, 95.3% of workers were satisfied with working and living in Singapore, an improvement
from 2018 (86.3%) and it is the highest level recorded since the survey began in 2011.1 96.7%
of workers in 2024 indicated they would continue working in Singapore or return in the future,
an increase from 91.9% in 2018. This reflects continued confidence in Singapore as a
preferred destination for work.

92.3% of migrant workers would recommend Singapore as a work destination, up from 84.0%
in 2018. The top reasons cited in 2024 were a safe working environment (84.4%), high salary
(71.2%), and adequate worker protection (65.2%). 97.0% of workers agreed that the Ministry
of Manpower (MOM) has been effective in protecting them and 97.0% also agreed that their
working conditions were safe, up from around 87% in 2018 for both indicators.

92.0% of workers received their salaries digitally. In addition, 90.1% of workers reported
receiving their In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter before commencing work, and 91.5% received
salaries consistent with their IPA. In terms of daily living, a majority of workers (88.5%) reported
that their current food arrangements ­ typically either cooking their own meals or buying food
from stalls ­ matched their preferences, suggesting employers' responsiveness to worker
preferences.

Migrant workers have high trust in MOM as well as their employer. 99.5% of workers said they
would approach their supervisors or employers in the event of a work injury. 89.3% of workers
found it easy to approach MOM on employment-related matters. Additionally, employers
(60.3%) and MOM (45.0%) are workers' top preferred channels for receiving information about
employment rights.

From the employers' perspective2, sentiments toward the migrant workforce are generally
positive. 70.8% of employers expressed satisfaction with the quality of their workers, and 75.2%
of employers renewed the work permits of more than 80% of their workers on average. This
points to a stable employment relationship. Employers are also proactive in retaining workers
as 88.8% of employers would offer benefits to encourage workers to stay and 80.6% provided
additional training to upskill their workers. These demonstrate commitment to maintaining a
skilled migrant workforce.

However, access to skilled labour remained a concern. While nearly half (47.5%) of employers
felt they had good access to migrant workers overall, only 27.3% said it was easy to find
migrant workers with the relevant skills. These findings suggest there is room for improvement
to enable employers to hire higher-skilled workers, particularly in more specialised roles.

1 Introduction

1.1 This report analyses the experience of migrant workers in Singapore, and employers'
           perspectives on hiring and managing their migrant workforce3. It is based on surveys
           conducted by the Manpower Research and Statistics Department,4 covering:

           a. Migrant workers' overall satisfaction with working in Singapore; pre-employment
                  and in-employment experience; preferred channels of assistance and
                  information; and perceptions of the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) and their
                  workplaces.

           b. Employers' overall satisfaction with migrant workers; experience with the
                  recruitment of migrant workers; and perceptions on access to and skills of
                  migrant workers.

1.2 To ensure that the survey results can be generalised to the migrant worker population
           and the employers that hire them, the survey samples were selected randomly from
           sampling frames designed to be representative of the population profiles of migrant
           workers (including sectors in which the workers were employed and the nationalities
           of the workers) and employers of migrant workers in Singapore (including sectors
           which they were based in and the number of migrant workers they employ).

3 For this report, "migrant workers" refer to non-domestic Work Permit (WP) Holders and "employers" refer to the
firms that employ them.
4 Where comparable data have been collected from past surveys on migrant workers conducted in 2011, 2014,
and/or 2018, comparisons with previous survey findings are included in this report. For the survey on employers,
this is the first run of the study.

                   SURVEY COVERAGE & METHODOLOGY

Introduction
The Migrant Worker Experience and Employer Survey 2024 was conducted by the Manpower
Research and Statistics Department of the Ministry of Manpower under the Statistics Act 1973
(2020 Revised Edition). The survey was conducted in 2024 to both migrant workers and
employers of migrant workers.
Objective
The survey was conducted to collect comprehensive information on the experience of migrant
workers in Singapore, and employers' perspectives on hiring and managing their migrant
workforce.
Coverage
The survey on migrant workers covered a total of 3,020 migrant workers who hold non-
domestic work permits, of whom 2,825 or 89.1% responded to the survey.
The survey on employers covered a total of 2,580 firms employing migrant workers, of which
2,212 firms or 85.7% responded to the survey.

Methodology
The survey on migrant workers was a self-administered survey in English or the native
language of the respondent. In cases where the respondent requested for assistance to
understand the question, the interviewers explained the questions to the respondent.
For the survey on employers, respondents submitted their returns online, by post or by email,
with clarifications made either over the phone or via email.
