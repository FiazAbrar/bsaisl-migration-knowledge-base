# Despite Labor Shortages, Kyrgyzstan Continues to Round-Up Undocumented Migrants

- August 13, 2024
- Posted by ![Stephen M. Bland](https://timesca.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Stephen-M-Bland-150x150.jpg) [Stephen M. Bland](https://timesca.com/our-people/stephen-m-bland/)

Bishkek Unrest May 17th; image: TCA, Aleksandr Potolitsyn

Kyrgyzstan is grappling with a severe labor shortage, yet the government continues to crack down on undocumented migrants. [Police raids](https://akipress.com/news:790244:Police_raids_reveal_28_foreign_nationals_working_illegally_in_Bishkek/) in Bishkek over the past few days have resulted in the detention of 28 Bangladeshi nationals, who face administrative penalties and were fined for working without valid documentation. This crackdown, however, comes despite an urgent need for laborers, the head of the construction agency in the Kyrgyz Republic stating that the construction industry alone [urgently needs](https://akipress.com/news:790037:Construction_sector_of_Kyrgyzstan_needs_at_least_10,000_workers_-_head_of_construction_agency/) at least 10,000 workers.

The tension between labor shortages and the crackdown on migrant workers has been an ongoing theme of President Japarov’s tenure. Since March, Kyrgyz authorities have intensified efforts to find and [deport illegal migrant laborers](https://rus.azattyk.org/a/32952505.html), with some 1,500 Pakistanis and 1,000 Bangladeshis apprehended. This campaign reflects the [nationalist policies](https://timesca.com/signs-of-racism-in-central-asia/) promoted by Japarov and his longtime ally Tashiyev, emphasizing Kyrgyz traditions and customs, which have gained them significant popularity. The support of young Kyrgyz men, who are a crucial pillar for Japarov and Tashiyev, is evident in their backing of measures to curb undocumented migration.

The situation escalated on May 17-18, when [unrest](https://timesca.com/mass-brawl-between-citizens-of-pakistan-and-bangladesh-reported-in-bishkek/) broke out in Bishkek, leading to the [hospitalization](https://timesca.com/an-uneasy-calm-after-unrest-in-bishkek/) of 41 people. The unrest underscored splits within the government, with Deputy Cabinet Chairman Edil Baisalov labeling the attackers as “hooligans,” while President Japarov defended the actions of “patriotic youth” who demand strict measures against illegal migrants.

The violence targeted foreign students and workers, exacerbating tensions and prompting diplomatic concerns, as seen in the [meeting](https://timesca.com/foreign-ministries-of-kyrgyzstan-and-pakistan-discuss-safety-amid-more-raids-on-foreign-citizens/) between Kyrgyz Deputy Foreign Minister Almaz Imangaziev and a Pakistani delegation discussing the safety of foreign nationals.

Despite assurances from the Kyrgyz government that such incidents would be prevented in the future, the crackdown on migrant workers continues, highlighting the complex interplay between labor needs and nationalist policies. President Japarov has vowed [swift action](https://timesca.com/kyrgyzstans-president-warns-of-swift-crackdown-if-unrest-flares-again/) against any threats to state integrity, signaling a tough stance on both undocumented migration and internal dissent. This ongoing tension reveals the challenges Kyrgyzstan faces in balancing economic demands with political and social stability.
