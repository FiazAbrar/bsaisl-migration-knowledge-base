# N findings: corridor, chain and money (Kyrgyzstan)

Facts: `countries/kyrgyzstan/work/N/facts.csv` (N0001-N0176). Sources: `work/N/sources.csv` (42 registered + OEP pulls). Taka conversions of USD/som to be added by the lead (G's rate).

## a. Draft doc text

### In one minute

(Draft for the lead; rewrite last.)

**Kyrgyzstan is effectively closed to new Bangladeshi workers: BMET cleared 11 people for it in the whole of 2026, against 12,747 in 2024 and 6,652 in 2025, and no Kyrgyz decision announcing a stop has been published** [N0005, N0003, N0004]. Kyrgyzstan raised its foreign-worker quota to 100,000 for 2026, but permits for Bangladeshis fell while those for Chinese and Pakistani workers rose [N0051, N0038, N0035, N0036, N0037]. A job starts as a Kyrgyz employer's quota, mostly in garment workshops and construction, turned into an invitation and a work visa through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' online portal [N0045, N0057, N0055, N0099]. Between that employer and the village stand Kyrgyz recruiting firms, their Bangladeshi partners and Bangladeshis already in Bishkek, and most of the trouble comes from them: people paid Tk 3 to 7 lakh, or USD 5,000 for a visa, and many found no job, no visa or pay far below the promise [N0069, N0076, N0124, N0118, N0101, N0070]. The worst cases ended in locked rooms, unpaid months, jail and, in September 2025, a special flight home for 180 people [N0071, N0108, N0103, N0106]. Kyrgyzstan is also sold as a road to Europe; the people who bought it ended in jail or stranded [N0105, N0140].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

**Closed in practice. BMET cleared 11 workers for Kyrgyzstan from 1 January to 9 November 2026, and none in October 2026** [N0005, N0025]. Clearances stopped abruptly in August 2025: 1,410 in July, 57 in August, 1 in September and none in October [N0021, N0022, N0023, N0024]. No Kyrgyz or Bangladeshi order closing the corridor has been published: Kyrgyzstan is not on the Ministry's October 2026 list of 12 countries needing prior approval for clearance [N0157]. Since July 2025 Bangladeshi students say Kyrgyz visa applications are not being approved, with no reason given and no advance notice [N0093, N0167, N0094].

> **Need to know**
> - Has Kyrgyzstan stopped issuing work visas or invitations to Bangladeshis since July-August 2025, and by which order or instruction?
> - Who received the 3,872 Kyrgyz work permits issued to Bangladeshis in 2026 (to 1 October) when BMET cleared only 11: renewals of workers already there, people regularised in the 2025 campaign, or people who left Bangladesh without BMET clearance [N0035, N0046]?

#### Status

- **April 2024:** Kyrgyzstan suspended quotas for workers from Bangladesh, Pakistan and India after a rise in arrivals, to resume only after those present illegally were found, deported or registered [N0064, N0065].
- **December 2024:** the deputy labour minister said quotas were being issued again little by little [N0083].
- **August 2025:** the Cabinet raised the 2025 quota from 25,000 to 42,000; BMET clearances for Kyrgyzstan stopped the same month [N0042, N0022].
- **January 2026:** the garment association Legprom asked parliament to extend the work visas of Bangladeshis employed through companies, warning of disruption to production if they were not extended [N0089].
- **30 April 2026:** a Cabinet order raised the 2026 quota from 52,000 to 100,000; the countries named as asking most for quotas are China, India, Turkey and Pakistan, not Bangladesh [N0050, N0051, N0052].
- **14 October 2026:** Bangladesh's Foreign Secretary, Asad Alam Siam, discussed jobs for Bangladeshi workers with Kyrgyzstan's First Deputy Minister of Labour, Social Security and Migration, Arlen Aliyasov, in Bishkek; a memorandum of understanding (MoU) was discussed, not signed [N0153, N0154].
- **29 October 2026:** the Labour Ministry said permits for Bangladeshis rose sharply in 2025 and fell in 2026 [N0038].

#### The channels

- **A Bangladeshi recruiting agency with BMET clearance:** the route of the 2023-2025 workers, almost all men, mostly in garment workshops (Work visa: company worker, Step 1) [N0002, N0003, N0004, N0027, N0030]. Shut in practice since August 2025 (see Status) [N0022, N0005].
- **A Kyrgyz recruiting firm and its partner in Bangladesh:** Bishkek firms sign agreements with garment and construction employers and bring workers mainly from Bangladesh through a partner there who finds the people [N0069]. Kyrgyz law caps such a firm's fee at half of one month's wage [N0145].
- **A Bangladeshi already in Kyrgyzstan:** workers there offer to invite friends and relatives, and a Bishkek recruiter says most deceived Bangladeshis were lured by their own compatriots (see The middlemen in Bishkek) [N0069, N0076].
- **No legal route:** a tourist or student visa used to work (the Ministry of Foreign Affairs says no work visa at all has been issued for taxi or delivery work, so every foreigner doing it works illegally), and Kyrgyzstan as a road to Europe (see What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe) [N0163, N0061, N0105].

#### Who qualifies

The workers are men: BMET cleared 39 women in 2024, 56 in 2025 and none in 2026 [N0007, N0008, N0009]. Garment shops keep the workers who can sew and let go of those who cannot, leaving them without a roof or money [N0078].

> **Need to know**
> - Does a Kyrgyz garment employer test sewing skill before inviting a worker (video test, trade test at a BMET centre), and is there an age limit?

#### Size

- **BMET clearances:** 0 in 2022, 1,713 in 2023 (17th destination), 12,747 in 2024 (8th), 6,652 in 2025 (10th), 11 in 2026 to 9 November [N0001, N0002, N0010, N0003, N0011, N0004, N0012, N0005].
- **Peaks:** 920 in November 2023; 5,236 in April 2024, the highest month, then 3,358 in May, 1,547 in June and 232 in July; 1,499 in May 2025 and 1,410 in July 2025 [N0013, N0016, N0017, N0018, N0019, N0020, N0021].
- **Jobs on the clearances:** in 2024, 9,384 were cleared simply as "Worker", 1,213 as garments workers and 892 as sewing line checkers ("Swing Line Checker"); in 2025, 2,356 as "Worker", 874 as sewing equipment operators, 649 as casual workers, 646 as garments workers and 555 as general labour [N0026, N0027, N0028, N0029, N0030, N0031, N0032, N0033].
- **Kyrgyz work permits held by Bangladeshis:** 2,758 in 2024, 11,677 in 2025 and 3,872 in 2026 to 1 October [N0035].
- **Against other nationalities:** in December 2025, of 47,686 foreign workers with permits, 40% were Chinese, 24% Bangladeshi, 17% Pakistani, 5% Indian and 4% Uzbek [N0043, N0044]. By 1 October 2026 Chinese citizens held 37,133 permits, Pakistanis 11,404, Indians 2,652 and Uzbeks 2,182 [N0036, N0037, N0039]. In the first half of 2026 Kyrgyzstan issued over 60,000 work visas, about 60% to Chinese citizens [N0161, N0059].

> **Need to know**
> - In 2024 BMET cleared 12,747 workers for Kyrgyzstan, but Kyrgyzstan issued Bangladeshis only 2,758 work permits that year [N0003, N0035]: what happened to the rest (no permit, permit refused, moved on)?
> - How many Nepalis hold Kyrgyz work permits?

#### What closed it before

- **September 2021:** Kyrgyzstan blocked tourist visas for citizens of Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal and India, whom a tour operator said were passing through on their way to work visas in the UAE or Saudi Arabia; the Ministry of Foreign Affairs suspended self-applied tourist visas and moved to invitations by a host organisation responsible for the visitor [N0150, N0151, N0170, N0152].
- **April 2024:** quotas for Bangladesh, Pakistan and India were suspended (see Status) [N0064]. By 1 April 2024, 12,000 quotas had been issued for Bangladeshis but only 3,000 used, and the deputy labour minister said Bangladeshis broke the law most, so quotas for Bangladesh and Pakistan were stopped; about 4,000 issued quotas were cancelled [N0081, N0082, N0067]. A crackdown followed: the national security chief, Kamchybek Tashiev, said 1,000 Bangladeshis and 1,500 Pakistanis had been deported and officers were catching 20 to 50 irregular migrants a day, and mobs attacked South Asian students in Bishkek on 17-18 May 2024 [N0085, N0087, N0086]. BMET clearances fell from 5,236 in April to 232 in July 2024 [N0016, N0019].
- **August 2025 to now:** clearances stopped (see Status) [N0022, N0005].

Why 2025 rose: quotas were issued again from December 2024, garment owners and MPs pressed to hire Bangladeshis, and the quota was raised to 42,000 [N0083, N0084, N0089, N0042].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A job starts as a Kyrgyz employer's quota and invitation, issued through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' online portal, and reaches the village through a Bishkek recruiting firm, its partner in Bangladesh, a Bangladeshi agency or a compatriot already in Kyrgyzstan [N0055, N0069, N0128, N0076]. The choke point is the invitation and visa: Kyrgyzstan's State Committee for National Security (GKNB) tells foreigners to use only the Ministry's portal for permits, and the people who sell visas and "legalisation" outside it are where workers lose money [N0099, N0097, N0101].

#### The quota

Kyrgyzstan's quota for foreign workers was just over 16,000 in 2022-2023, 25,000 in 2024, 42,000 and then 52,000, and 100,000 in 2026; 120,000 has been set for 2027 [N0040, N0042, N0051, N0053]. Since 1 January 2025 quotas and work permits are handled by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, not the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration, with all documents filed through a single-window online portal (how: Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [N0054, N0055]. After a quota is issued, the invitation, visa, medical, permit and fee must all be done within 90 days or the quota is cancelled [N0068, N0176].

#### The employer

The jobs are mostly in garment workshops and construction [N0045, N0057]. Garment owners and their association, Legprom, have lobbied to hire Bangladeshis, and Legprom says those employed through companies are registered and pay tax [N0084, N0089, N0090]. Legprom's director says some employers break their contracts by not providing housing [N0091]. A deputy labour minister said in April 2026 that foreigners will work even for low wages [N0058].

#### The middlemen in Bishkek

Kyrgyz recruiting firms bring workers through partners in Bangladesh [N0069]. One recruiter, Nurislam Kambarov, said in 2024 that 70-80% of such intermediaries take large sums, then arrange no visa or job, or a job at lower pay than promised, and leave people on the street [N0070, N0174]. He described houses where dozens of Bangladeshis waited 5-6 months with no visa or work, 80 of them at Orozbekova 158 in Bishkek, many with their passports taken [N0071, N0072, N0073]. In his account most were brought by fellow Bangladeshis, working with a few Kyrgyz partners, and one such organiser had been arrested [N0076, N0077]. The 2022 forced-labour case in a Bishkek sewing shop involved a broker, Syed Mahamosul Hasan, who denied the workers' account [N0173, N0130].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a list of Kyrgyz private employment agencies licensed to recruit foreign workers, and where can a worker check a firm's licence? (The US State Department says the government publishes one [N0147].)

#### The visa trade in Dhaka

A Kyrgyz work visa sold for USD 5,000 through intermediaries in July 2025, with no job behind it [N0101]. In 2024 an unlicensed Naya Paltan office, Jannat Trade International, handed out e-visa copies, "manpower" papers and tickets for Tk 5 lakh; at the airport the visa was not on record and the ticket was fake [N0112, N0113, N0116, N0117]. In 2025 Facebook pages, WhatsApp groups and YouTube channels advertised Central Asian jobs using real employers' names without their knowledge [N0110, N0111]. A Bishkek agency head said in 2024 he opened visas for about 250 Bangladeshis in four months but brought only 50, because leaving Bangladesh with the required training and papers is hard [N0139].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Kyrgyz invitation or work visa sell for in Dhaka in 2026, and who sells it?

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

People have paid Tk 3 to 7 lakh, or USD 5,000 for the visa alone, for jobs advertised at Tk 55,000 to 65,000 a month; many were paid far less or nothing [N0124, N0118, N0101, N0122, N0112, N0123].

#### The cap

Kyrgyz law lets a recruiting agency charge a one-time fee of at most half the worker's monthly wage [N0145].

> **Need to know**
> - Has BMET set a maximum migration cost for Kyrgyzstan, and what do the Kyrgyzstan permission letters (if any) allow an agency to take? (For B.)

#### What people really pay

- 2021-22: Tk 3.10 lakh to an agency [N0124].
- 2024: Tk 5 lakh to an unlicensed office; the CID (Criminal Investigation Department) said it took Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person [N0113, N0118].
- July 2025: USD 5,000 for a work visa bought from intermediaries [N0101].
- "Europe through Kyrgyzstan" packages: Tk 6 lakh (2023) and Tk 4.8 lakh (2025) [N0140, N0105].

#### How they borrow

Families sell land, a rickshaw, cattle or the house and borrow from relatives or a bank [N0124, N0115, N0102].

> **Need to know**
> - How is a Kyrgyzstan deal paid in stages (with the passport, on the visa, before the flight), and with what receipts?

#### What the job pays

Offers in Bangladesh have promised Tk 55,000 to 65,000 a month with food and housing [N0122, N0172, N0112]. In Kyrgyzstan, messenger groups advertise jobs at USD 500-600 a month [N0092]. What workers reported getting:

- a garment shop where Bangladeshis worked 11 hours a day, six days a week, plus two paid extra hours, with housing from the employer and food at their own cost; one said he was satisfied after earning USD 150 in Dhaka [N0159, N0160, N0137, N0138, N0136];
- a worker who stayed with a 2022 employer said he earned Tk 40,000-50,000 a month [N0131];
- workers flown to Osh whose promised USD 500 was cut to USD 300 [N0075];
- a 2022 contract for USD 600 paid as 15,000 som [N0133].

From 1 January 2026 individual entrepreneurs, like companies before them, must pay foreign workers' wages by bank transfer, with cash allowed only in exceptional cases [N0048, N0049]. Minimum wage, hours and overtime: see Your rights at work.

#### What goes unpaid

- Months with no pay: seven months for one 2025 returnee, four for another [N0108, N0109].
- The broker's fee taken from pay: in 2022 an employer took Tk 7,000 of a Tk 10,000 first wage, saying he had paid the broker [N0129].
- Pay a fraction of the promise: Tk 10,000 for 17-18-hour days against a promised Tk 55,000 [N0122, N0123].

How to claim: see If you aren't paid.

### What ties you to the employer

The work visa and permit name one employer: changing employer needs a reissued work visa and work permit (Changing employer or leaving) [N0148]. A worker who walks out is reported by the employer and becomes illegal [N0092]. Employers and brokers have kept workers' passports, in one 2022 case "for registration", and a factory owner said workers could leave only if they paid back what he had paid for them [N0132, N0073, N0127]. What an expired permit or overstay does to you: see What can get you jailed or deported.

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The commonest outcomes reported are no job or no visa on arrival, pay far below the promise, passports taken, and then months undocumented ending in jail or deportation [N0070, N0071, N0075, N0073, N0107, N0108]. In 2025 Kyrgyzstan identified 27 male forced-labour victims, all from Pakistan and Bangladesh, and did not report referring victims to protection services [N0143, N0149]. On 10 September 2025, 180 Bangladeshis lured with garment and construction jobs came home on a special flight; BRAC said many had been unpaid, jobless, undocumented or tortured, and some families paid ransom [N0103, N0106, N0104].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Kyrgyzstan is a door to Europe."** A 21-year-old from Shariatpur was taken via Dubai on a promise of Italy, paid Tk 4.8 lakh and spent 44 days in jail in Kyrgyzstan [N0105]. A Fenchuganj man paid Tk 6 lakh for Poland; the broker, Ruhel Ahmed, left for Dubai promising a Ukrainian visa and disappeared, and a Pakistani broker held his passport (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [N0140, N0141, N0142].
- **"A valid visa, permit and BMET card mean a real job."** In 2022 workers holding all three said their agency had sold them to a garment factory owner for USD 8,300 [N0126, N0125].
- **"The quota is 100,000, so it's easy to go."** The quota rose, but Bangladeshi permits fell in 2026 and BMET cleared 11 people (see Status) [N0051, N0038, N0005].
- **"Videos of happy workers getting visas prove the agency is real."** Jannat Trade filmed visa handovers for YouTube; it had no licence and its visas and tickets were fake [N0114, N0117, N0116].
- **"You can earn more in taxi or delivery."** No work visa is issued for those jobs; everyone doing them is illegal, and the Ministry says irregular workers register with a firm and then move to taxis [N0163, N0061, N0062].
- **"An agent in Bishkek can legalise you."** In 2025 the GKNB arrested a Bangladeshi who took USD 66,500 to "legalise" migrants and forged their permits; over 120 expired Bangladeshi passports were found [N0097, N0098].

How irregular stays end: see Irregular routes and undocumented workers.

## Work visa: company worker

### Scams

#### Done to workers

- **Visas sold with no job:** a work visa bought for USD 5,000 in July 2025 led to no work and no fare home [N0101, N0162]. Check the visa yourself before paying (Work visa: company worker, Step 5) [N0099].
- **Fake agencies with e-visa copies:** Jannat Trade International (Naya Paltan, 2024) took Tk 5 lakh, gave e-visa copies and tickets, and the visa was not on record at Dhaka airport [N0112, N0113, N0116]. After a victim filed a human trafficking case at Paltan police station on 23 April 2024, the CID's anti-trafficking team arrested the owner, Ibrahim Mollik Nahid, and two associates [N0119, N0164, N0120].
- **Social media job ads:** Facebook, WhatsApp and YouTube ads naming real Kyrgyz employers who knew nothing of them [N0110, N0111].
- **Sold to an employer:** workers with BMET cards said they were sold to a factory for USD 8,300, and the broker's fee was taken from their pay [N0125, N0129].
- **Passports held and work changed:** a 2022 group sent to sew was made to work on a building site and tend livestock, with passports held [N0165, N0132].
- **"Legalisation" and fake permits:** a Bangladeshi in Bishkek took USD 66,500 for forged permits (2025); the GKNB says to use only the Ministry of Foreign Affairs portal [N0097, N0099].
- **Europe via Kyrgyzstan:** see What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe [N0105, N0140].

Complain in Bangladesh as under Work visa: company worker, Step 1 (complaints to BMET), or to police under the human trafficking law, as the Jannat Trade victim did [N0119].

#### Tried by workers

Forged permits bought through a "legalisation" agent are what the GKNB prosecuted in 2025 (penalties: see What can get you jailed or deported) [N0097]. Working in taxi or delivery on a firm's papers is illegal [N0061, N0062].

Already in `agencies/agencies.csv`.
- **Jannat Trade International (Naya Paltan):** unlicensed; owner arrested by CID, 2024 [N0117, N0120].
