### For the Background (lead or N to place under "What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe")

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

Brokers sell Kyrgyzstan as a step to Europe: in 2024 Bangladesh police's CID (Criminal Investigation Department) said it had become a popular trafficking route because it is seen as close to Europe, with people sent through Dubai on visit visas and then on to Kyrgyzstan [U0034, U0035]. In 2025 a 21-year-old from Shariatpur paid a broker Tk 4.8 lakh for Italy, was flown Dubai-Bishkek, and spent 44 days in prison instead [U0011, U0012]. In 2023 a Sylhet broker took Tk 6 lakh for Poland, left his client in Kyrgyzstan saying he would fetch a Ukraine visa from Dubai, and disappeared; the man's family borrowed for his ticket home [U0037, U0039]. The same pitch was being made in 2004, when 32 Bangladeshis were arrested in Bishkek waiting for forged papers to go on through Kazakhstan and Russia to Europe [U0059, U0060]. Bangladeshis who crossed from Kyrgyzstan into Kazakhstan away from checkpoints in June 2024 were caught at a checkpoint in Kostanay region and got six months' prison and a five-year expulsion [U0055, U0056, U0057]. Inside Kyrgyzstan, undocumented workers are fined and can be expelled (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [U0043, U0090].

## Irregular routes and undocumented workers

Many Bangladeshis without papers in Kyrgyzstan came in legally on a visa and lost their status there: the visa ran out, no work permit came, or the job did not exist [U0040, U0041, U0045, U0009]. In May 2025 the GKNB (State Committee for National Security, Kyrgyzstan's security service, which leads the raids) estimated about 5,000 undocumented migrants in the country [U0072]. A foreigner without valid papers is fined and can be expelled, and expulsion goes ahead even if the fine is unpaid [U0043, U0090].

### How people lose their papers

- **A tourist visa sold as a work visa.** In 2024 Jannat Trade International, which held only a tours and travel licence, sent people on 59-day tourist visas with promises of garment, chocolate and food-packaging jobs; none got work [U0031, U0032, U0151, U0033]. Working on a tourist visa breaks the purpose of entry, a fine of 10,000 som, and in May 2025 each of 46 tourist-visa holders found on a Bishkek building site was fined 10,000 som [U0091, U0061, U0062].
- **A visa that ends on arrival.** A Lalmonirhat man who reached Bishkek on 3 June 2024 for a welding job found his visa expired the next day; he worked seven months unpaid and was jailed for three months [U0009, U0010].
- **"Your work visa comes later."** Many South Asians were brought on ordinary visas with promises of work visas that never came; they move from workshop to workshop without wages, and many have had their passports taken [U0045, U0046].
- **The sponsor does not renew.** The GKNB chairman said in 2025 that employers and universities often fail to extend visas, leaving foreigners with expired documents [U0074].
- **Brought legally, then sold.** In 2021-2022, men sent as garment workers with visas, work permits and BMET smart cards by M/S Makkah Madina Trade Int (RL-810) and M/S Al-Jabeen Establishment Ltd (RL-884) said the agency sold them to a factory owner for USD 8,300; they were promised Tk 55,000 a month for seven to eight hours' work a day and made to work 17-18 hours for Tk 10,000 [U0018, U0021, U0019, U0017, U0016]. The owner said they could leave only if they paid him back [U0020].
- **Papers to "legalise" you that are forged.** In May 2025 the GKNB arrested a Bangladeshi who took USD 66,500 from fellow Bangladeshis for legal-stay papers and gave them forgeries; more than 120 passports with expired documents were seized [U0063, U0064]. In March 2026 it broke up a Bishkek scheme selling form 086 medical certificates without any examination, used to extend the stay of mainly Pakistanis and Bangladeshis [U0066, U0067]. The GKNB tells foreigners to apply for permits only through the MFA's official e-Visa portal [U0065].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a broker in Bishkek charge a Bangladeshi to "fix" an expired visa or get a work permit after arrival in 2026, and who are the usual fixers (Bangladeshi, Kyrgyz, Pakistani)?
> - Do agencies still send workers on 30- or 59-day tourist or business e-visas with a promise to "convert" them to work visas in Bishkek?

### Onward routes: Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia

- **Into Kyrgyzstan from Uzbekistan.** In June 2024 border guards in Jalal-Abad caught 11 Bangladeshis who had gone to Uzbekistan for work on 8 June, found none and tried to cross into Kyrgyzstan away from official checkpoints; the Kyrgyz organiser was arrested with them [U0051, U0052]. A Bishkek facilitator said in 2024 that stranded Bangladeshis had to go to Uzbekistan and come back to get new visas [U0047].
- **Out to Kazakhstan.** In November 2023 Kazakh prosecutors arrested three organisers who had smuggled 20 South Asians from Kyrgyzstan around the border checkpoints [U0058]. Bangladeshis who crossed without a visa in June 2024 were caught at a Kostanay region checkpoint and sentenced to six months in prison and expulsion from Kazakhstan for five years [U0055, U0056, U0057].
- **Russia and its war.** In October 2026 Bangladesh's Special Response Battalion said 30 Bangladeshis who paid about Tk 10 lakh each to Jabal-e-Noor, RS International and TS Group for construction jobs in Russia had their passports and phones taken on arrival and were handed to the army; 20 are confirmed dead [U0115, U0118, U0116]. The network included a Kyrgyz woman named Julia [U0117].
- **Belarus.** Kyrgyzstan ratified a readmission agreement with Belarus in March 2026, covering the return of people who break entry or stay rules [U0113].

> **Need to know**
> - What do brokers charge in 2026 for Bishkek-Almaty, Bishkek-Russia or Bishkek-Europe "packages", and which crossing points (Korday, Chaldybar, Ak-Zhol) do smugglers use?
> - Are Bangladeshis being recruited in Bishkek for Russian army contracts, and how is Julia's network connected to Kyrgyzstan?
> - Does the Kyrgyzstan-Belarus readmission agreement cover third-country nationals such as Bangladeshis?

### Raids

Raids under Operation "Nelegal" ("Illegal") are run by the GKNB with the Interior Ministry (MVD), several times a year [U0069, U0078, U0080, U0081]. They target building sites and sewing workshops [U0077].
- **2024:** the GKNB found over 130 Pakistanis and Bangladeshis in Bishkek lodgings in April, 64 with expired visas and no registration [U0040]. In May 46 Bangladeshis were detained for illegal work in Bazar-Korgon, and in August 28 were detained and fined in Bishkek [U0053, U0048]. The GKNB chairman, Kamchybek Tashiev, said 1,000 Bangladeshis and 1,500 Pakistanis had been deported [U0044].
- **2025:** Decree No. 164 of 22 May 2025 gave foreigners until 10 June 2025 to legalise or leave without fines; Operation Nelegal-2025 started on 11 June [U0152, U0068, U0069].
- **2026:** raids on 1-10 June produced 922 reports against foreigners; 16 Pakistanis and 4 Bangladeshis were expelled after June raids in Issyk-Kul region; on 1-10 October the GKNB and MVD wrote 1,487 reports with fines of 13,407,000 som [U0078, U0076, U0081, U0082].

**What happens when you are caught:**
1. **You are taken to a filtration centre** and handed to the MVD's Service for Combating Extremism and Illegal Migration [U0070].
2. **You get a report (protocol) and a fine** under the Code of Offences, by length of the violation [U0043, U0083]:
   - up to 1 month: 5,500 som [U0084]
   - 1 to 3 months: 10,000 som [U0085]
   - 3 to 6 months: 15,000 som [U0086]
   - 6 to 12 months: 17,500 som [U0087]
   - over 1 year: 20,000 som [U0088]
3. **A court decides on deportation** [U0054]. Failing to fix a violation within 30 days of an order leads to expulsion, and expulsion is carried out even if the fine is unpaid [U0089, U0090].
4. **Jail is possible.** Returnees in 2025 described three months in jail, more than three months after surrendering to the police, and 44 days in prison, with detainees made to stand in strong sun [U0010, U0013, U0012, U0014].
5. **An entry ban can follow** (see Going home without valid papers, Coming back to Kyrgyzstan) [U0079].

> **Need to know**
> - Where is the filtration centre in Bishkek, how long are people held, and can they phone the Embassy or family from there?
> - Who pays the deportation ticket: the worker, the employer or inviting party, or the Kyrgyz state?

### If an employer holds you or you are trafficked

In June 2024 a sewing-shop owner near Bishkek and a district police officer took workers' passports, withheld wages and threatened them with prison for illegal stay, and both were arrested [U0049, U0050]. Kyrgyzstan gives foreign trafficking victims the same benefits as citizens, but no legal alternative to removal, and there are no shelters for men [U0120, U0121, U0122]. The US rates Kyrgyzstan Tier 2 Watch List [U0119]. In the 2021-2022 case, seven of the workers got home with help from the NGOs Rights Jashore and RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), and one filed a case in a Kishoreganj court against those who sent him [U0022, U0023].

> **Need to know**
> - Which Bishkek NGO or hotline takes trafficking reports from foreign men, and does the Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent send staff to Bishkek for such cases?
> - What are the current phone numbers of Rights Jashore and RMMRU's case desk for families of workers held in Kyrgyzstan?

### Stranded workers: what has happened

- **September 2025: 180 sent home.** The Kyrgyz government flew home 180 Bangladeshis who had become irregular; they reached Dhaka on a special flight from Bishkek on 10 September 2025, with the Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent handling the case [U0001, U0003, U0004, U0002]. They had been lured by promises of garment and construction jobs; BRAC said many got no job or pay, became undocumented, and some families paid ransom [U0005, U0006, U0007]. A Kushtia welder injured at work got no help from his employer, went unpaid for four months and surrendered to the police [U0015, U0013].
- **2024: stuck on tourist visas.** The six people Jannat Trade International sent on tourist visas were left without work in Kyrgyzstan [U0032, U0033]; CID said the syndicate takes no responsibility once people arrive [U0036].
- **2021-2022: held in a garment factory.** Seven of the workers sent through M/S Makkah Madina Trade Int and M/S Al-Jabeen Establishment Ltd came home; they said the broker took Tk 3.25 lakh per visa, and the broker said he took no more than Tk 2.80 lakh [U0022, U0021, U0024, U0025]. The employer deducted Tk 7,000 of a first month's Tk 10,000, saying he had paid the broker [U0026].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis are undocumented in Kyrgyzstan in November 2026, and how many are in detention?
> - Did any of the 180 returnees of September 2025 get wages or compensation, or a case filed against the agencies or brokers who sent them?

## Going home without valid papers

On 10 November 2026 no fine-free exit window is open; the last ones were Decree No. 164 (until 10 June 2025) and Decree No. 233 (1-31 July 2026) [U0152, U0068, U0093, U0092]. Outside a window, a foreigner who overstayed applies for an exit visa (выездная виза) on the MFA's e-Visa portal, evisa.e-gov.kg, and pays the fines [U0107, U0103]. The MFA's consular director put the total at about 120,000-130,000 som: almost 100,000 som for the exit visa plus the MVD fine [U0103]. The alternative is arrest and expulsion (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers, Raids) [U0090].

### Step 1: Get a passport or Travel Permit

1. **If your passport is valid and in your hands,** go to Step 2 [U0107].
2. **If it is expired, lost or held by the employer,** ask the Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, which covers Kyrgyzstan, for a Travel Permit (see Contacts) [U0002].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Embassy in Tashkent issue Travel Permits to Bangladeshis in Kyrgyzstan without the person travelling to Tashkent, what does it cost, and how long does it take?
> - When an employer holds the passport, will the MVD or the Embassy get it back?

### Step 2: Get a certificate of no criminal record

1. **Get the certificate of no criminal record** (справка об отсутствии судимости); the exit visa application needs it [U0101].
2. **Go early.** On 2 July 2026 foreigners queued outside the Main Directorate of Internal Affairs (GUVD) in Bishkek from the morning and none had been issued a certificate by midday [U0102].

> **Need to know**
> - Which office issues the certificate outside Bishkek, what does it cost, and how many days does it take?

### Step 3: Report to the MVD and pay the fine

1. **Go to the MVD office where you are staying.** In the July 2026 window this office gave the decision letter needed for the exit visa [U0098, U0094].
2. **Pay the fine** for the length of your overstay (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers, Raids, item 2) [U0084, U0088]. In a window the fine is waived, except for people with criminal cases, court judgments, unpaid fines or tax debts [U0093, U0096].

> **Need to know**
> - Outside a window, does an overstayer still need an MVD letter before applying for the exit visa, and is the fine paid at a bank or at the MVD?

### Step 4: Apply for the exit visa on evisa.e-gov.kg

1. **Apply yourself.** The MFA says to apply without third parties or intermediaries [U0097].
2. **Apply in advance.** Overstayers can apply ahead through the portal [U0107]. Getting the exit visa at Manas airport before the flight is only for citizens of 67 listed states, not Bangladesh, and costs three times the fee [U0108, U0109].
3. **If stuck,** message the MFA Consular Department on WhatsApp +996 999 312 002, or call the MVD on 0312 266170 or 0312 266165 [U0099, U0100].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the exit visa fee for a Bangladeshi in 2026, how many days does the portal take, and which documents does the form ask for?

### Step 5: Fly home

1. **Buy your ticket.** One returnee flew Manas (Bishkek) to Sharjah on Air Arabia, then to Dhaka; his family borrowed for the ticket [U0038, U0039].
2. **At Dhaka airport,** BRAC's Migration Programme gave the September 2025 returnees emergency money and help (see Coming home) [U0008].

### Coming back to Kyrgyzstan

The GKNB warns that expulsion is followed by a ban on entry [U0079]. In 2022 the Deputy Interior Minister said a ban for administrative violations lasts two years, for non-grave crimes five years, and for especially grave crimes up to life [U0112]. From September 2026 the automated system EISUM counts each foreigner's days of stay, and overstayers are refused re-entry under the visa-free regime but may apply for a residence permit [U0110, U0111].

> **Need to know**
> - How long is the entry ban in 2026 for a Bangladeshi who leaves on an exit visa after paying fines, compared with one expelled by a court?
> - Can a Bangladeshi check online whether he is banned from Kyrgyzstan before buying a new visa?

## Asylum

Asylum is protection for people who cannot return home; the claim must explain why [U0147]. Kyrgyzstan is a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention and has its own refugee law [U0123]. As of 30 June 2025 there were 2,186 refugees and asylum seekers in the country, including 524 Afghans, and the state had recognised 168 refugees [U0146, U0139, U0141]. In September 2026 the Ministry of Labour proposed refusing to register repeat claims with no new facts, saying they let people with no other legal grounds stay longer; about 160 were filed from 2022 to mid-2026 [U0143, U0145, U0144].

### How it works

1. **Claim inside Kyrgyzstan or at an official border crossing** [U0125]. Someone who entered irregularly is not punished for that if he applies without delay [U0130].
2. **Write the application in Kyrgyz or Russian,** with your details and your reasons for not being able to go home; missing documents do not stop you applying [U0126, U0147, U0149]. The NGO Legal Clinic Adilet helps free with writing and translation [U0127].
3. **Submit it to the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration,** whose Department for work with kairylmans (ethnic Kyrgyz returnees) and refugees decides claims: 215 Tynystanov Street, Bishkek, tel. +996 312 62-13-93 [U0124, U0128, U0129].
4. **Register.** You get a certificate of registration of your application, valid 3 months and renewed every 3 months until the final decision, and you must register your address at the local public service centre (TsON) within 5 working days [U0131, U0132, U0133].
5. **Interviews.** You are interviewed once or more with a free interpreter, and the Ministry decides [U0148]. The procedure is free [U0136].
6. **If refused,** you have 3 months to appeal to a district court, then the appeal court and the Supreme Court; if the last appeal fails and you have no other status, you may have to leave [U0134, U0135].

**If recognised:** a refugee may work without a permit, must re-register every year, and can apply for citizenship by a simplified procedure after 3 years [U0137, U0150, U0138].

> - Is an asylum seeker protected from expulsion during an Operation Nelegal raid if he shows the registration certificate?

**What happens in practice:**
- The undocumented population is mainly people who came legally and lost status, not border-crossers: tourist visas sold as work visas, visas expiring on arrival, "work visa later" promises, employers not renewing [U0040-U0046, U0009, U0074].
- Fixers sell fake legalisation: a Bangladeshi forger (USD 66,500, Feb 2025) and a fake medical certificate (form 086) ring (March 2026) [U0063, U0066].
- Enforcement has hardened every year since 2024: Tashiev's 1,000 Bangladeshi deportations (2024), Decree 164 window then Nelegal-2025, Law No. 80 fines (April 2025), Nelegal-2026 (June, August, October raids), Decree 233 exit window (July 2026), Cabinet resolution 602 and EISUM (September 2026). Uptake of the July 2026 window was low: about 500 exit visas by 13 July against 5,000 expected [U0104].
- The 180 returnees of September 2025 are the biggest single stranded-worker event; the Kyrgyz government organised the flight. I found no IOM-assisted return of Bangladeshis from Kyrgyzstan.
- "Door to Europe": every case found ends in prison, deportation or a self-paid ticket home; none ends in Europe. Kyrgyz press reports (2023-2024) of South Asian smuggling to Kazakhstan concern mostly Pakistanis, Nepalis and Sri Lankans; Bangladeshis appear in the June 2024 Kazakhstan case.
