# Kyrgyzstan: migration guide

How to go to Kyrgyzstan on a Bangladeshi passport, visa by visa, step by step. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO, research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh); KGS is the Kyrgyz som, converted throughout at KGS 1 = about Tk 1.42, from the National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic's rate of USD 1 = KGS 87.45 and Bangladesh Bank's rate of USD 1 = Tk 124.42 on 10 November 2026; US dollars are converted at that Tk 124.42 [G0001, G0002, G0003]. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is the Bangladesh government agency that registers jobseekers on its OEP (Overseas Employment Platform) website and gives each worker emigration clearance before departure; the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment tells workers never to go without that clearance [B0003, B0011, B0006]. Bangladeshis need a visa to enter Kyrgyzstan [W0028, V0155]. To work there, a foreigner needs the Unified Permit (Единое разрешение, also called the Single Permit), an electronic document with a QR code that combines the work visa, the employer's permit to hire foreign labour and the work permit; it replaced the separate work permit and work visa on 1 January 2025 [W0027, W0052]. The first Unified Permit lasts up to 60 days, and the stay is then extended with a resident card (резидент-карта), a plastic bank debit card with a QR code that lets a foreigner live and work for up to a year [V0048, V0161, W0079]. The employer applies for the Unified Permit online on the e-Visa portal (evisa.e-gov.kg), the same site that issues electronic visas, and the employer or the worker applies for the resident card [W0091, B0151, V0093, V0062]. The MFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) issues visas [V0012]. Since presidential decree No. 337 took effect on 29 September 2026, the Unified Permit, the resident card, residence permits and the registration of foreigners belong to the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration (Минсоцтруда), through its new Department for Migration Affairs; before that the MFA issued the Unified Permit and the resident card [K0013, K0014, K0015, K0017, K0019, K0020, K0022, W0135]. The same Ministry decides asylum claims, and its labour inspection service is being given migration control over foreign workers [U0124, K0023].

## Background

### In one minute

Kyrgyzstan took Bangladeshi workers in numbers only from 2023 to 2025: BMET cleared 1,713 in 2023, 12,747 in 2024 and 6,652 in 2025, almost all men, with garment work the commonest named job, then clearances fell to 57 in August 2025 and to 11 in all of 2026 to 9 November [B0074, B0076, B0078, B0075, B0077, B0079, B0103, B0104, B0107, B0109, B0111, B0091, B0080]. Kyrgyzstan still issued 3,872 work permits to Bangladeshis in 2026 to 1 October, far more than BMET cleared [N0035, B0080]. The job is a named place on a Kyrgyz employer's Unified Permit application inside a yearly quota that rose to 100,000 in 2026; the worker can't apply himself, so the place reaches the village through Bishkek recruiting firms and their partners in Bangladesh, Bangladeshi agencies and compatriots already in Kyrgyzstan [W0049, W0053, N0051, N0069, N0128, N0076]. People have paid Tk 3 to 7 lakh, or USD 5,000 (about Tk 6.2 lakh) for a visa alone, while Kyrgyz law caps a recruiting agency's fee at half of one month's wage [N0124, N0118, N0101, N0145, G0002]. Many found no job or no work permit on arrival, pay far below the promise, or their passport taken [N0070, N0071, N0073, U0045]. The permit names one employer, can't be moved to another and lapses when the job ends, leaving 30 days to leave; after that a worker is fined and can be expelled and banned from returning [L0108, L0107, L0109, L0074, L0080, L0094]. Raids by the security services write hundreds of reports against foreigners at a time, and on 10 September 2025 the Kyrgyz government flew 180 stranded Bangladeshis home [U0077, U0081, U0001, U0004]. Brokers also sell Kyrgyzstan as a door to Europe; one man who bought that spent 44 days in a Central Asian prison and another came home on a ticket his family borrowed for, and Bangladeshis who crossed from Kyrgyzstan into Kazakhstan without a visa got six months in prison there [U0011, U0012, N0140, U0039, U0055, U0057].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Closed in practice for workers going through BMET, as of 10 November 2026: BMET cleared 11 workers for Kyrgyzstan from 1 January to 9 November 2026, and none in October [B0080, B0100]. Kyrgyzstan still issued Bangladeshis 3,872 work permits in 2026 to 1 October, but its Labour Ministry says their permits fell in 2026 while those of Chinese and Pakistani workers rose [N0035, N0038, N0036, N0037]. Bangladesh and Kyrgyzstan have no labour agreement; their governments only explored one in October 2026 [B0139].

#### Status

- **1 January 2025:** the Unified Permit replaced the separate work permit and work visa, and quota and permit work moved from the Ministry of Labour to the MFA, with every document filed through the e-Visa portal [W0052, N0054, N0055].
- **May and July 2025:** BMET cleared 1,499 and 1,410 workers, the year's busiest months [B0089, B0090].
- **August 2025:** the Cabinet of Ministers raised the 2025 quota from 25,000 to 42,000 (order No. 710 of 18 August 2025); the same month BMET clearances fell to 57, then to 1 in September and none in October [N0042, W0014, B0091, B0092, B0093].
- **Since July 2025:** over 150 Bangladeshi medical students have been stuck in Bangladesh; they say Kyrgyzstan stopped issuing them visas without notice, and the portal shows the student category open but does not approve Bangladeshi applications, with no reason given (see Student visa) [N0093, N0094, N0168].
- **January 2026:** Legprom, the Kyrgyz garment industry association, asked parliament to extend the work visas of Bangladeshis employed through companies [N0089].
- **30 April 2026:** a Cabinet order raised the 2026 quota from 52,000 to 100,000; the countries named as asking most for quotas were China, India, Turkey and Pakistan, not Bangladesh [N0050, W0010, N0052].
- **29 September 2026:** decree No. 337 moved the Unified Permit and the resident card from the MFA to the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration, which is to outsource the service; the Cabinet had two months to bring its rules into line [K0013, K0014, K0015, K0016, K0021, K0022].
- **1 October 2026:** Bangladeshis had received 3,872 Kyrgyz work permits in 2026, against 11,677 in 2025, while Chinese citizens had received 37,133 and Pakistanis 11,404 [N0035, N0036, N0037].
- **14 October 2026:** in Bishkek, Bangladesh's Foreign Secretary, Asad Alam Siam, and Kyrgyzstan's First Deputy Minister of Labour, Social Security and Migration, Arlen Aliyasov, discussed jobs for Bangladeshi workers and explored signing an MoU (memorandum of understanding); none was signed [N0153, B0139]. The Kyrgyz side named construction, textiles, pharmaceuticals, digital services, agriculture and teaching as sectors that want more foreign workers [B0140].
- **4 November 2026:** Kyrgyzstan was not among the 12 countries for which the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET to give no clearance without its approval [B0114, N0157].

#### The channels

- **A Bangladeshi recruiting agency, with BMET clearance:** the route of the 2023-2025 workers, almost all men, with garment work the commonest named job; the Kyrgyz employer files the Unified Permit and the worker gets BMET clearance (see Work visa: company worker) [B0075, B0077, B0079, B0103, B0107, W0049, B0011]. Shut in practice since August 2025 (see Status) [B0091, B0080].
- **A Kyrgyz recruiting firm and its partner in Bangladesh:** a Bishkek recruiter, Nurislam Kambarov, said in 2024 that he brings workers mainly from Bangladesh through a partner there who finds them [N0069].
- **A Bangladeshi already in Kyrgyzstan:** workers there offer to invite friends, and the same recruiter says most deceived Bangladeshis were lured by compatriots (see The middlemen in Bishkek) [N0069, N0076].
- **Not a work route:**
  - a tourist or business visa: a business visa gives no right to work, and staying for a purpose other than the one declared on entry is fined (see Visit visa) [V0016, L0079];
  - a student visa: Kyrgyzstan's security chief says foreigners come as tourists and students and in fact work illegally (see Student visa) [U0073];
  - taxi and delivery work: no work visa has been issued for it, so every foreigner doing it works illegally [N0061, N0163];
  - Kyrgyzstan as a road to Europe (see What people believe, and the facts) [U0034].
- **Bangladeshis living in the Gulf:** holders of a valid resident card of the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Brunei, Oman or Bahrain can get a tourist visa of up to 1 month the simplified way, without an invitation; it is still a tourist visa (see Visit visa) [V0002, V0023].

#### Who qualifies

Anyone a Kyrgyz employer names in its Unified Permit application; any company or individual in Kyrgyzstan may bring a foreign worker [W0049, W0100]. Almost all the workers are men: BMET cleared 55 women in 2023, 39 in 2024, 56 in 2025 and none in 2026 [B0075, B0077, B0079, B0081]. The commonest named jobs are garment work: garments workers, sewing equipment operators and sewing line checkers [B0103, B0104, B0107, B0109, B0111]. A Bishkek sewing-shop owner says the shops keep the workers who can sew and leave the rest without shelter or money [N0078].

#### Size

- **Workers cleared by BMET:** none in 2022, 1,713 in 2023 (17th destination), 12,747 in 2024 (8th), 6,652 in 2025 (10th) and 11 from 1 January to 9 November 2026 [B0073, B0074, N0010, B0076, N0011, B0078, N0012, B0080].
- **Month by month:** 920 in November 2023; none in February and March 2024, then 5,236 in April (the highest month), 3,358 in May, 1,547 in June, 232 in July and 172 in September; 1,499 in May 2025 and 1,410 in July, then 57 in August and 1 in September; 13 in November 2025, 1 in December, and 5 each in January and February 2026 [B0082, B0083, B0084, B0085, B0086, B0087, N0019, B0088, B0089, B0090, B0091, B0092, B0094, B0095, B0096, B0097].
- **Job titles on OEP:** in 2024, 9,384 of 12,747 were recorded only as "Worker", with 1,213 garments workers, 892 "Swing Line Checker" (sewing line checker) and 182 construction workers; in 2025, 2,356 "Worker", 874 sewing equipment operators, 649 "A Casual Worker", 646 garments workers, 555 general labour and 522 sewing line checkers; in 2026, 7 general labour, 3 casual workers and 1 worker [B0102, B0103, B0104, B0105, B0106, B0107, B0108, B0109, B0110, B0111, B0112].
- **Kyrgyz work permits for Bangladeshis:** 2,758 in 2024, 11,677 in 2025 and 3,872 in 2026 to 1 October [N0035]. By 30 April 2025 Bangladeshis held 2,573 of 12,232 first (60-day) permits, second only to Chinese citizens (2,844) [W0096, W0097]. In April 2024, 47% of applications for 16,814 quota places came from Bangladesh [N0066].
- **Against other nationalities:** of 47,686 foreign workers with permits on 18 December 2025, 40% were Chinese, 24% Bangladeshi, 17% Pakistani, 5% Indian and 4% Uzbek [N0043, N0044]. In 2026 to 1 October, Chinese citizens received 37,133 permits and Pakistanis 11,404, up from 2,146 in 2024; Indians held 2,652 and Uzbeks 2,182 [N0036, N0037, N0039]. Kyrgyzstan issued over 60,000 work visas in the first half of 2026, about 60% of them to Chinese citizens [N0161, N0059].
- **Without papers:** the GKNB (State Committee for National Security, Kyrgyzstan's security service) estimated about 5,000 undocumented migrants in May 2025, and the MFA gave the same figure in September 2026 [U0072, N0063].

#### What closed it before

- **September 2021:** Kyrgyzstan blocked tourist visas for citizens of Bangladesh, Pakistan, India and Nepal, and the MFA replaced self-application with an invitation from a host organisation, which is made responsible for the visitor; a Bishkek tour operator said such "tourists" were passing through to work visas in the UAE or Saudi Arabia [N0150, N0170, N0152, N0151].
- **April 2024:** the Ministry of Labour suspended quotas because of the rise in migrants, especially from Bangladesh, Pakistan and India, until everyone illegally present was found, deported or registered [N0064, N0065]. By 1 April 2024, 12,000 quotas had been issued for Bangladeshis but only 3,000 used, and a deputy labour minister said Bangladeshis broke the law most, so quotas for Bangladesh and Pakistan were stopped; about 4,000 issued quotas were cancelled [N0081, N0082, N0067]. The security chief, Kamchybek Tashiev, said 1,000 Bangladeshis and 1,500 Pakistanis had been deported and officers were catching 20 to 50 irregular migrants a day, and mobs attacked South Asian students in Bishkek on 17-18 May 2024 [N0085, N0087, N0086]. BMET clearances fell from 5,236 in April to 232 in July and 172 in September [B0085, N0019, B0088].
- **December 2024:** the Ministry said it was issuing quotas again little by little, and an MP, Chingiz Aidarbekov, asked the authorities to help garment shops hire Bangladeshis and Pakistanis [N0083, N0084].
- **August 2025:** BMET clearances stopped (see Status) [B0091, B0080].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A legal job starts as a Kyrgyz employer's application for a Unified Permit naming one worker, inside the yearly quota, and reaches the village through a Bishkek recruiting firm and its partner in Bangladesh, a Bangladeshi agency or a compatriot already in Kyrgyzstan [W0053, W0095, N0069, N0128, N0076]. The choke point is the permit: only the employer can file it, on the e-Visa portal, and the GKNB tells foreigners to apply only through that portal; the people who sell visas and "legalisation" outside it are where workers lose money [W0091, N0099, N0101, N0097].

#### The quota

The Cabinet of Ministers sets the number of foreign workers each year, and Unified Permits are issued only within it [W0015, W0095]. It was just over 16,000 in 2022-2023 and 25,000 in 2024, rose to 42,000 in 2025, was set at 52,000 and then 100,000 for 2026, and is 120,000 for 2027 [N0040, N0042, W0011, W0010, N0053]. Since 2025 the quota no longer lists types of work, and the commission that shared out places is gone: employers apply directly for a named worker [W0012, W0053]. How the employer applies and pays: Work visa: company worker, Step 3 [W0091].

#### The employer

The jobs are mostly in garment workshops and construction, the sectors that want foreign workers most, and the Labour Ministry says Pakistani, Indian and Bangladeshi citizens usually work in garments [W0013, N0045, N0057]. 46% of all foreign workers are in Jalal-Abad region in the south, not in the capital, Bishkek [W0104]. Garment owners and Legprom have lobbied to hire Bangladeshis, and Legprom says those employed through companies are registered and pay tax [N0084, N0089, N0090]. Legprom's director, Azamat Kerimbek uulu, says some employers break their contracts by not providing housing [N0091]. A deputy labour minister, Mirlan Baigonchokov, said in April 2026 that foreigners will work even for low wages [N0058].

#### The middlemen in Bishkek

Nurislam Kambarov said in 2024 that 70-80% of the intermediaries who bring foreigners take large sums, then arrange no visa or job, or a job at lower pay than promised [N0070]. He described dozens of Bangladeshis living in houses for 5-6 months with no visa or work, 80 of them at Orozbekova 158 in Bishkek, many with their passports taken [N0071, N0072, N0073]. In his account they were brought mostly by fellow Bangladeshis, whom he named as Haris (arrested), Sharif, Asat and Ali, working with a few Kyrgyz partners [N0076, N0077]. Bangladeshis flown to Osh, Kyrgyzstan's second city, were given no visas, locked in, and had their promised USD 500 (about Tk 62,200) a month cut to USD 300 (about Tk 37,300) [N0075, G0002]. Kyrgyzstan ratified ILO Convention 181 on private employment agencies in October 2025 and keeps a public database of licensed agencies, but in 2025 it did not report holding any recruiter liable for fraudulent recruitment [N0047, N0147, N0146]. In May 2025 the GKNB arrested a Bangladeshi who had taken USD 66,500 (about Tk 82.7 lakh) to "legalise" migrants and forged their permits; over 120 expired Bangladeshi passports were found [N0097, N0098, G0002]. In March 2026 it broke up a Bishkek scheme selling form 086 medical certificates without any examination, used to extend the stay of mainly Pakistanis and Bangladeshis [U0066, U0067].

> **Need to know**
> - Where is Kyrgyzstan's database of licensed private employment agencies, and can a worker check a Bishkek firm's licence there by name?

#### The visa trade in Dhaka

- **A visa with no job:** in July 2025 a Bangladeshi bought a Kyrgyz work visa from intermediaries for USD 5,000 (about Tk 6.2 lakh) and found no work [N0101, G0002].
- **A fake agency:** in 2024 Jannat Trade International, an office in Naya Paltan, Dhaka, with only a tours and travel licence, advertised car-wash jobs at Tk 60,000-65,000 a month on YouTube, filmed people receiving "visas", and took Tk 5 lakh for a visa, papers and a ticket; the CID (Criminal Investigation Department of the Bangladesh police) arrested its owner, Ibrahim Mollik Nahid, and two associates (see Done to workers) [N0112, B0143, N0114, N0113, N0120, N0166].
- **Social media:** in 2025 Facebook pages, WhatsApp groups and YouTube channels advertised Central Asian jobs using real employers' names without their knowledge, and those who went found no jobs, contracts or sponsors [N0110, N0111].
- **Licensed agencies:** 2022 documents show that Makkah Madina Trade Int (RL 810) and Al-Jabeen Establishment Ltd (RL 884) sent as garment workers the men who said their agency sold them to a factory owner for USD 8,300 (about Tk 10.3 lakh) [N0128, N0125, G0002]. Al-Jabeen's managing director, Mohammed Hanif, said the returnees could not do the work, and the broker the workers named, Syed Mahamosul Hasan, denied their claims [N0130, N0173].
- **Visas that are never used:** a Bishkek agency head said in 2024 that he opened visas for about 250 Bangladeshis in four months but brought only 50, because leaving Bangladesh with the required training and papers is hard [N0139].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The legal costs are small for the worker: the employer pays the Unified Permit fees, and the worker pays BMET's fees [W0091, W0054, W0055, B0019, B0010, B0153]. People have paid Tk 3 to 7 lakh, or USD 5,000 (about Tk 6.2 lakh) for a visa alone, for jobs advertised at Tk 55,000 to 65,000 a month that often paid far less [N0124, N0118, N0101, G0002, N0122, N0112, N0123].

#### The cap

- **Recruiting agency in Kyrgyzstan:** a one-time fee of at most half of the worker's monthly wage [N0145].
- **Unified Permit:** under the 2025 rules (Resolution No. 367), 30,000 som plus USD 300 per worker (about Tk 80,000), paid by the employer in two stages [W0062, W0063, W0091, W0054, G0002, G0003]. Garment companies in the Chamber of Commerce and Industry paid the same amounts as a special rate (USD 75 plus 20,000 som and a 5,000 som deposit for the permit, USD 225 plus 5,000 som for the resident card) until that rate lapsed on 1 November 2026 (see Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [K0001, K0002, K0003, K0004, K0006, K0007, K0008]. Whoever invites a foreigner must arrange his stay documents [W0018].
- **BMET's fees:** a Tk 5,500 welfare fee, Tk 1,000 insurance and a Tk 250 clearance fee (Work visa: company worker, Step 6) [B0019, B0010, B0153].

#### What people really pay

- **Tk 2.8 to 3.25 lakh (2021-22):** workers said the broker took Tk 3.25 lakh per visa, the broker said he took no more than Tk 2.80 lakh, and one worker paid his agency Tk 3.10 lakh [U0024, U0025, N0124].
- **Tk 5 lakh (2024):** Jannat Trade's price for a visa, "manpower" papers and a ticket; the CID said it took Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person [N0113, N0118].
- **USD 5,000 (about Tk 6.2 lakh, 2025):** a work visa bought from intermediaries, with no job behind it [N0101, G0002].
- **Tk 4.8 lakh (2025) and Tk 6 lakh (2023):** "Europe through Kyrgyzstan" deals for Italy and Poland (see Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends) [U0011, N0140].

#### How it is paid

BMET tells workers to pay an agency only into its bank account and to keep every receipt (Work visa: company worker, Step 1) [B0007]. Part can be taken later from the wage: in 2022 an employer kept Tk 7,000 of a worker's first Tk 10,000, saying he had paid the broker to bring them [N0129, U0026]. Workers who said their agency had sold them to a factory owner were told they could leave only if they paid him back [N0125, N0127].

> **Need to know**

#### How they borrow

Families sell land, a rickshaw, cattle or the house, and borrow from relatives or a bank [N0124, N0115, N0102]. Some have borrowed again for the ticket home [U0039]. PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank, the government bank for migrants) lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest (Work visa: company worker, Step 1) [B0059].

> - What monthly rate does a village moneylender charge on a Kyrgyzstan loan in 2026?

#### What the job pays

Offers in Bangladesh have promised Tk 55,000 to 65,000 a month with food and housing [N0122, N0112]. Inside Kyrgyzstan, messenger groups advertise jobs at USD 500-600 a month (about Tk 62,200 to 74,700) [N0092, G0002]. For comparison, a 2024 job order for garment workers in Bishkek approved by Pakistan's government paid 35,000 to 37,000 som (about Tk 49,800 to 52,600) a month, with free housing, food, medical care and transport but no free air ticket [W0143, G0003]. What Bangladeshi workers reported getting:

- **A garment shop in Bishkek, 2024:** 11 hours a day, six days a week, plus two paid extra hours; housing from the employer, food at the workers' own cost; one worker who had earned USD 150 (about Tk 18,700) in Dhaka said he was satisfied [N0159, N0137, N0138, N0136, G0002].
- **A worker who stayed with a 2022 employer:** Tk 40,000 to 50,000 a month [N0131].
- **A 2022 sewing-shop contract:** USD 600 (about Tk 74,700) promised, 15,000 som (about Tk 21,300) paid [N0133, G0002, G0003].

Kyrgyzstan's legal minimum wage is 3,280 som (about Tk 4,670) a month in 2026 and 3,608 som (about Tk 5,130) in 2027, and minimum pay for foreign workers is set by region and sector [K0051, K0052, G0003, W0090]. The average wage was 46,300 som (about Tk 65,900) across Kyrgyzstan in the first quarter of 2026 and about 60,000 som (about Tk 85,400) in Bishkek [G0051, G0003]. Wages must be paid to a bank card, and for a worker that card is the resident card [L0129, L0185, W0075]. Income tax and Social Fund (state social insurance) contributions come out of the pay (see Tax and deductions from your pay) [G0040, G0041].

A worker who pays his own housing and food spends far more than the government's subsistence minimum for Bishkek, which was 9,569.55 som (about Tk 13,600) per person a month in early 2026 [G0046, G0003]:

- **A room or a shared place:** 8,000 to 20,000 som a month (about Tk 11,400 to 28,500) in June 2026 listings [G0047, G0003].
- **Food, cooked at home:** about 10,000 to 13,000 som a month (about Tk 14,200 to 18,500) [G0048, G0003].
- **Bus:** 17 som a ride by card, 20 som in cash; a monthly transport card costs 1,020 som (about Tk 1,450) [G0049, G0003].
- **In all, living as cheaply as possible:** about 27,000 to 42,000 som a month (about Tk 38,400 to 59,800) [G0050, G0003].

#### What goes unpaid

- **Months with no pay:** a welder whose visa expired the day after he arrived worked seven months unpaid, then served three months in jail; another man went four months unpaid and surrendered to the police [N0107, N0108, N0109].
- **The broker's fee taken from pay:** see How it is paid [N0129].
- **A fraction of the promise:** workers promised Tk 55,000 a month for 7-8 hours a day were made to work 17-18 hours for Tk 10,000 [N0122, N0123].
- **Piece rates instead of a wage:** garment workers told they would earn Tk 52,000 a month were paid by the number of pieces sewn [L0187, L0181].

How to claim: see If you aren't paid.

#### Money sent home

Bangladesh Bank publishes no separate figure for remittances from Kyrgyzstan; it counts them under "Others" [G0020]. bKash's list of countries with partners that pay into a bKash wallet does not include Kyrgyzstan [G0013]. Western Union, MoneyGram and Ria are on the National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic's register of licensed money-transfer systems, and hundi (money passed outside the banks through private agents) is illegal in Bangladesh (see Sending money home) [G0005, G0006, G0007, G0022].

#### How long the debt takes

A Tk 5 lakh deal equals 10 to 12.5 months of the Tk 40,000 to 50,000 a month that one worker who stayed reported earning [N0113, N0131].

> - How many months does a garment worker in Bishkek who paid Tk 4 to 5 lakh take to clear the debt in 2026, after food and rent?

### What ties you to the employer

Fully: the Unified Permit names one employer, position and period, can't be passed to another employer, and lapses when the job ends [W0141, L0108, L0107].

- **To enter:** only the employer can apply, by a statement letter naming you, your passport number, the purpose and the period (Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [W0049, W0091].
- **To renew:** the employer or you apply for the resident card before the 60-day permit ends, and the employer that applied renews it; each card lasts up to a year (see Your residence permit) [V0048, W0004, V0062, W0079]. If the employer pays no tax for 3 months, the permit is cancelled, and the resident card too if tax and social contributions go unpaid for 3 months [L0120, L0121].
- **To quit:** give one month's written notice; leaving a fixed-term contract early without a valid reason can cost up to one month's average pay, and the permit lapses with the job [L0013, L0016, L0107].
- **To change employer:** a new employer must get you a new work visa and permit (see Changing employer) [L0108, N0148]. If you leave without notice and the employer tells the police, no other employer may hire you, nor may the same one again [L0114, L0115]. Legprom says workers drawn by USD 500-600 job ads leave at night, are reported by their employers and become illegal [N0092].
- **To leave:** you have 30 days after the job ends; the employer must pay for your departure and see that you leave, or go on the Register of Bad-Faith Employers (see Leaving at the end of the job) [L0109, L0111, W0005, L0113].
- **Which law covers you:** the Labour Code covers foreigners employed by organisations in Kyrgyzstan (see Your rights at work); the Law On External Migration and the Temporary Regulation cover permits and stay [L0001, W0016, L0106]. The tax patent, which lets a foreigner work for private individuals, applies only during a visa-free stay, which Bangladeshis do not have [W0133, W0028].
- **Your passport:** employers and brokers have kept workers' passports, in one 2022 sewing shop "for registration" [N0132, N0073].
- **If the permit expires:** you are fined from 5,500 som (about Tk 7,800) for up to a month to 20,000 som (about Tk 28,500) for over a year, a court orders expulsion if you don't put it right within 30 days of a written order, and expulsion brings a 2-year entry ban (see What can get you jailed or deported) [L0074, L0078, L0090, G0003, L0080, L0081, L0094].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The commonest outcomes reported are no job or no permit on arrival, pay far below the promise and passports taken, then months without papers ending in jail or deportation [N0070, N0071, N0073, N0075, U0045, N0108]. All 27 male forced-labour victims Kyrgyzstan identified in 2025 were from Pakistan and Bangladesh, and the government did not report referring any victim to protection services [N0143, N0149]. On 10 September 2025, 180 Bangladeshis lured with garment and construction jobs came home on a special flight from Bishkek; BRAC said many had been unpaid, jobless, undocumented or tortured, and some families paid ransom [N0103, N0106, N0104].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Kyrgyzstan is a door to Europe."** In 2024 the CID called Kyrgyzstan a popular trafficking route because it is seen as close to Europe [U0034]. A 21-year-old from Shariatpur paid Tk 4.8 lakh on a promise of Italy, was flown via Dubai to Bishkek in May 2025, and spent 44 days in prison instead [U0011, U0012]. A Fenchuganj man paid a broker, Ruhel Ahmed, Tk 6 lakh for Poland; the broker left for Dubai promising a Ukrainian visa and disappeared, a Pakistani broker held his passport, and his family borrowed for his ticket home [N0140, N0141, N0142, U0039]. Bangladeshis who walked on into Kazakhstan in 2024 got six months in prison and a five-year ban, and the 180 flown home on 10 September 2025 had been left jobless and undocumented [U0055, U0057, U0005].
- **"A tourist visa now, the work permit after you arrive."** A business visa gives no right to work, and a valid visa or registration alone doesn't let anyone hire you (see Work visa: company worker, Step 2) [V0016, W0142]. Six people Jannat Trade sent on 59-day tourist visas got no jobs, and in May 2025 each of 46 tourist-visa holders found on a Bishkek building site was fined 10,000 som (about Tk 14,200) [U0032, U0033, U0061, U0062, G0003].
- **"A valid visa, permit and BMET card mean a real job."** In 2022 workers holding all three said their agency had sold them to a garment factory owner for USD 8,300 (about Tk 10.3 lakh) [N0126, N0125, G0002].
- **"The quota is 100,000, so it's easy to go."** The quota rose, but permits for Bangladeshis fell in 2026 and BMET cleared 11 people (see Status) [N0051, N0038, B0080].
- **"Videos of people getting visas prove the agency is real."** Jannat Trade filmed visa handovers for YouTube; it had no recruiting licence, and at Dhaka airport its visa was not on record and its ticket was fake [N0114, B0143, N0116].
- **"You can earn more in taxi or delivery."** No work visa is issued for those jobs, so everyone doing them works illegally; the MFA says irregular workers register with a firm and then move to taxis [N0163, N0061, N0062].
- **"An agent in Bishkek can legalise you."** The GKNB arrested a Bangladeshi who took USD 66,500 to "legalise" migrants and gave them forged permits, and it tells foreigners to apply for permits only on the official e-Visa portal (see The middlemen in Bishkek) [N0097, N0099].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **To Europe via Dubai and Bishkek: it ends in a Central Asian prison or a borrowed ticket home.** The CID says the syndicates send people to Dubai on visit visas and then on to Kyrgyzstan [U0035]. A Shariatpur man promised Italy in 2025 spent 44 days in prison, and a Fenchuganj man promised Poland in 2023 flew home from Manas airport, Bishkek, on Air Arabia through Sharjah, on a ticket his family borrowed to pay for [U0011, U0012, N0140, U0038, U0039]. The pitch is old: in 2004, 32 Bangladeshis were arrested in Bishkek and Chui region waiting for forged papers to travel on through Kazakhstan and Russia to Europe [U0059].
- **Across the border to Kazakhstan: it ends in a Kazakh prison.** Bangladeshis who crossed from Kyrgyzstan away from the checkpoints in June 2024 were caught in Kostanay region, on the Russian border, and each got six months in prison and expulsion with a five-year ban [U0055, U0056, U0057]. Kyrgyz law punishes illegal border crossing with up to 2 years in prison [L0135].
- **In from Uzbekistan: it ends at the Kyrgyz border.** In June 2024 border guards in Jalal-Abad caught 11 Bangladeshis who had found no work in Uzbekistan and tried to cross away from the checkpoints, with the Kyrgyz citizen who organised it [U0051, U0052].
- **Staying on after the visa or permit ends: it ends in a raid, a fine and expulsion.** The GKNB and the MVD (Ministry of Internal Affairs) raid building sites and sewing workshops in Operation "Nelegal" ("Illegal"); on 1-10 October 2026 they wrote 1,487 reports and fines of 13,407,000 som [U0069, U0077, U0081, U0082]. Those caught are taken to a filtration centre and handed to the MVD's service against illegal migration, and expulsion goes ahead even if the fine is unpaid (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [U0070, L0084]. The Kyrgyz government flew 180 such Bangladeshis home on 10 September 2025 (see Going home without valid papers) [U0001, U0004].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Visa-free entry:** Bangladesh is not on Kyrgyzstan's visa-free list; it is on the list of countries whose citizens get short-term visas in a simplified way [V0155, W0028].
- **A tourist visa on your own application from Bangladesh:** the simplified tourist visa is only for Bangladeshis holding a valid resident card of the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Brunei, Oman or Bahrain, and in September 2021 the MFA suspended self-applied tourist visas for Bangladeshis and others and required an invitation from a host organisation instead (see Visit visa) [V0002, V0094, V0095].
- **7-day visa-free stay with a long-term US, UK or Schengen visa:** Chinese and Indian citizens only [V0005].
- **Investment visa:** needs a contribution of at least 10 million som (about Tk 1.42 crore) to the Kyrgyz economy [V0040, G0003].
- **Sapar visa (type J):** issued only on a reasoned request from a Kyrgyz state or local government body [V0039].
- **Digital nomad visa (type DN):** only for people in software and information technology, on the request of the digitalisation authority [V0041].
- **Meken visa (type M):** only for ethnic Kyrgyz and people born in Kyrgyzstan or the Kyrgyz SSR [V0168].
- **Religious visa (type R):** only for missionaries with a permit from the state religious affairs body [V0042].
- **Driver's visa (type T):** only for drivers of international freight trucks [V0043].
- **Diplomatic (D) and official (O) visas:** only for diplomatic and service passports [V0044].
- **Working on a tourist or business visa:** a business visa gives no right to work, and staying for a purpose other than the one declared on entry is fined 100 calculation units (10,000 som) (see What can get you jailed or deported) [V0016, L0079, U0091].

## Work visa: company worker

A Kyrgyz employer applies online for a Unified Permit (Единое разрешение, Yedinoye razresheniye), one electronic document that combines the work visa, the permit to employ foreign labour and the work permit; you enter on it for up to 60 days, and the employer then applies for a resident card (резидент-карта), a plastic card that lets you stay for up to a year and carries your work and pay details [W0027, V0048, V0062, W0081, W0079]. These rules sit in a Temporary Regulation approved by Cabinet Resolution No. 312 and valid only until 31 December 2026, and presidential decree No. 337 of 28 September 2026 moved the Unified Permit and resident card from the MFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) to a new Department for Migration Affairs in the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration, which is to hand the service to an outside contractor [W0001, W0003, L0106, K0013, K0014, K0015, K0020, K0016]. The Cabinet was given two months to bring its rules into line [K0021]. BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) cleared 11 workers for Kyrgyzstan from 1 January to 9 November 2026 (see Status) [B0080].

> **Need to know**
> - Since October 2026, does the employer still apply for a Unified Permit on the e-Visa portal (evisa.e-gov.kg), or through a new Ministry of Labour portal or contractor, and who is the contractor?
> - What replaces the Temporary Regulation after 31 December 2026?

### Step 1: Find a real job

A company worker's Unified Permit is applied for by the employer, for a worker it names [W0049, W0053]. Bangladesh and Kyrgyzstan have no labour agreement: on 14 October 2026 their officials only explored signing an MoU (memorandum of understanding) [B0139]. How names reach employers, and who sells them, is in Background (see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut).

1. **Register on the OEP (Overseas Employment Platform),** BMET's website for job-seekers [B0003]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare warns never to go without BMET clearance (Step 6, item 2) [B0006].

   You'll need [B0002, B0003]:

   - your NID (national ID card);
   - a mobile number;
   - Tk 200 for the government fee.

   1. Open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) and type your name in English, your mobile and NID numbers, and your date of birth, division and district exactly as on the NID; type the captcha, accept the terms and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [B0002].
   2. Finish the form and pay the Tk 200 fee [B0003].
   3. Take 2 printed copies of the registration slip to your DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office) to give your fingerprints; the DEMO list is on BMET's website [B0003, B0004].

   **If it fails:** call the Probashi call centre, 16135 (free), or +8809610102030 from abroad [B0023].

2. **Know which jobs exist.** The Kyrgyz Ministry of Labour names construction and light industry as the sectors most in need of foreign workers, and says Bangladeshis usually work in garments [W0013, N0057]. Beyond the plain "Worker" title, the jobs BMET cleared for Kyrgyzstan were mostly garment jobs: garments worker, sewing equipment operator and swing line checker (sewing line checker) [B0102, B0103, B0104, B0107, B0109, B0111]. Garment shops keep the workers who can sew and leave the rest without shelter or money, a Bishkek shop owner said in 2024 [N0078]. Nearly half of all foreign workers (46%) are in Jalal-Abad region, not in Bishkek [W0104]. BMET's TTCs (Technical Training Centres) are listed on its website [B0005].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Do Kyrgyz garment employers test sewing skill before hiring Bangladeshis, and where (in Dhaka, by video, or on arrival)?
   > - Has BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government recruiting agency) ever posted a Kyrgyzstan circular?

3. **Through a Bangladeshi recruiting agency: check the licence and the approval.** You'll need the agency's name and RL number (the recruiting licence number every licensed agency has) [B0154].

   1. On [oep.gov.bd/agencies](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies), find the RL number and check the status says Active, not Expired or Cancelled, and that the validity date has not passed [B0154, B0155].
   2. Go to the address on that list, not a middleman's office [B0154].
   3. Ask whether the job order has BMET's permission or the Ministry's approval. Industry insiders said in November 2026 that demand letters for up to seven workers had needed no Ministry approval, and that this was used to send workers on fake job offers [B0117].

   A licence and a BMET smart card are no guarantee: in 2022, garment workers sent with valid visas, work permits and BMET smart cards by Makkah Madina Trade International (RL 810) and Al-Jabeen Establishment Ltd (RL 884) said they had been sold to a garment factory owner in Kyrgyzstan for USD 8,300 (about Tk 10.3 lakh) [N0126, N0128, N0125, G0002]. Al-Jabeen's managing director said the returnees could not do the work [N0130].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which licensed agencies sent workers to Kyrgyzstan in 2024-2025, and for which Kyrgyz employers?
   > - Does BMET issue permission letters for Kyrgyzstan job orders, and where are they published? None appear on BMET's list of posted letters.

4. **If the job comes through a Kyrgyz recruiting firm or a Bangladeshi already in Kyrgyzstan,** know the rules they work under. Bishkek recruiting firms bring workers mainly from Bangladesh through a partner there who finds the people, and Bangladeshis already working in Kyrgyzstan offer to invite friends and relatives [N0069]. Kyrgyz law lets a private employment agency charge a worker a one-time fee of at most half of one month's wage, and the government keeps a public database of licensed agencies [N0145, N0147]. A Bishkek recruiter said in 2024 that 70-80% of intermediaries take large sums and then arrange no visa or job, or a job at lower pay than promised, and that most deceived Bangladeshis were brought by fellow Bangladeshis [N0070, N0076].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Where is the Kyrgyz database of licensed private employment agencies, and can a worker in Bangladesh search it by firm name?

5. **Never go on a tourist or business visa on a broker's promise of a job.** The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare warns against it [B0006]. A business visa (type B) gives no right to work or earn income in Kyrgyzstan, and a valid visa or registration alone does not let anyone hire you [V0016, W0142]. Working on one is fined (see Tried by workers) [L0079].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Can a Bangladeshi already in Kyrgyzstan on a tourist or business visa get a Unified Permit without leaving, and do agents sell this "conversion"?

6. **Have the papers checked before you pay.** BMET checks job visas free within 3 working days at its DEMOs or at BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka; its online check covers only Saudi, Qatari, Bahraini, Singapore and UAE visas, not Kyrgyz ones [B0008, B0013, B0052]. Check the Unified Permit yourself as in Step 5 [W0043].

7. **Know what you should pay.** Pay any Bangladeshi agency only into its own bank account, and keep every receipt [B0007]. Under Kyrgyz law whoever invites you must arrange your stay documents, and the employer files and pays the permit fees (Step 3, item 2) [W0018, W0064, W0055]. The official costs on your side, each in the step that owns it:

   - OEP registration: Tk 200 (Step 1, item 1) [B0003];
   - passport: Tk 4,025 to Tk 10,350 (Step 4, item 1) [B0024];

   What people really pay, and to whom, is in Background (see "What you pay, and what you earn and keep").

   > **Need to know**

   > - Who pays the flight to Bishkek in practice: the employer or the worker?

8. **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years, and decides within 7 working days [B0038, B0059].

   You'll need [B0056, B0057, B0058, B0040]:

   - to be 18 to 60, with you and your guarantors living in the branch's area;

   - the free bank form, 3 photos and an NID copy;
   - copies of your passport, visa and BMET card, and the contract if you have one;

   Women can take PKB's women's migration loan with one guarantor [B0060]. Apply at a branch or on mygov.bd, which sends application and tracking numbers by SMS; helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0061, B0042].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does PKB lend against a Kyrgyz Unified Permit, which has no visa sticker?

9. **If an agency or broker takes your money,** complain in writing to BMET's Director General with your evidence (receipts, passport copy, messages); it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0018]. Hand it in at BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000 [B0052]. Then, in this order:

   1. BMET's Additional Director General (Employment): 02-48321949, 01720105492, adgemp@bmet.gov.bd [B0014].
   2. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's grievance officer: 02-41030224, 01712992086, adsadmin@probashi.gov.bd (30 working days) [B0055].

   4. For an unlicensed office, the police: Jannat Trade International had no legal papers to send workers abroad, a victim filed a case under the human trafficking law at Paltan police station, and the CID (Criminal Investigation Department) arrested the owner (see Done to workers) [N0117, N0119, B0156].

### Step 2: The job offer and contract

1. **Get the employer's details in writing before you pay anyone.** The employer's application is a statement letter to the Director of the MFA's Consular Department naming you, your passport number, the purpose and the period, in which the head of the company undertakes to keep your stay legal [W0049, W0050]. The permit ties you to that employer, the type of work, the position and the period, and it cannot be passed to another employer [W0141, L0108]. Ask whoever brings the job for:

   - the employer's full legal name and its address in Kyrgyzstan [W0049];
   - the job title and the town or region of the workplace [W0141, W0090];
   - the monthly wage in som (KGS, the Kyrgyz currency), since minimum pay for foreign workers is set by region and sector [W0090];
   - a copy of the statement letter [W0049].

   **For comparison:** a 2024 job order approved by Pakistan's emigration bureau for garment workers in Bishkek paid 35,000 to 37,000 som a month (about Tk 49,700 to Tk 52,500), with free housing, food, medical care and transport, but no free air ticket [W0143, G0003].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What minimum monthly wage must a Unified Permit application show for garment or construction work in 2026, and where is it published?
   > - Do Kyrgyz employers give Bangladeshi workers a copy of the statement letter before departure?

2. **Get the employment contract before the permit is filed, and read it.** The employer uploads the employment contract with its application, so the contract exists before the permit is issued [W0092]. Documents on the portal must be in Kyrgyz or Russian or translated into one of them [W0035]. Get the contract from the agency at least 2 days before you go and check the job, employer, workplace, length, pay, leave and airfare against what you were promised [B0062]. Check in particular:

   - **Housing, food and medical care:** the inviting side pays for your keep and medical care unless you agree otherwise in writing [W0019]. The garment association Legprom's director said in January 2026 that some employers break their contracts by not providing housing [N0091].
   - **How you are paid:** the employer must pay wages to a bank card, not in cash (Step 7, item 4) [W0068, L0129].
   - **Your copy:** the contract is made in two copies, and one is yours [L0006].

   What a contract must contain under the Labour Code is in Your rights at work [L0001].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What wage and conditions do BMET permission letters for Kyrgyzstan state, and do workers get the same contract in Bishkek?
   > - Is the contract signed in Dhaka, or only on arrival in Kyrgyzstan, and do workers get a Bangla or English copy?

3. **Give the employer exactly what is in your passport.** The passport must be valid at least 6 months beyond the permit and have at least 2 free pages, and at the border you must show the same passport the visa was issued on [W0036, W0037, V0030]. Get the passport first (Step 4, item 1) [W0036].

**Scams:** an offer of "a visa now, the work permit after you arrive" leaves you on a visa that does not allow work (Step 1, item 5); many South Asians brought on such promises never got the work visa and moved from workshop to workshop without wages [V0016, U0045, U0046].

### Step 3: The employer gets your work permit

You do nothing at this step except send documents; the employer files everything on the e-Visa portal, evisa.e-gov.kg, and pays [W0064, W0066, W0054, W0055]. There is no longer a commission that shares out quota places: since 1 January 2025 the employer applies directly for a named worker [W0052, W0053]. The MFA's Consular Department issued the permit under Resolution No. 312 until decree No. 337 of 28 September 2026 moved this to the Ministry of Labour [W0001, W0135, K0013, K0014].

**The quota.** Each year the Cabinet of Ministers sets the maximum number of foreign workers, and Unified Permits are issued only within it [W0015, W0095]. The 2026 limit was set at 52,000 and raised to 100,000 by an order of 30 April 2026 [W0011, W0010]. Since 2025 the quota no longer lists types of work [W0012]. When Kyrgyzstan stopped quotas for Bangladeshis before is in Background (see What closed it before).

1. **The employer applies on the e-Visa portal.** In its personal account (Личный кабинет, Lichnyy kabinet) on [evisa.e-gov.kg](https://www.evisa.e-gov.kg/) the employer [W0091, W0064, W0065, W0066]:

   1. creates the company account;
   2. fills in the online quota form;
   3. fills in the form for you by name and uploads scans of the documents.

   The documents include the employer's statement letter, copies of documents, the employment contract, medical certificates and any licence the work needs [W0092]. Your part is a scan of your passport (Step 2, item 3) and any certificate the employer asks for, apostilled and translated (Step 4, item 3) [W0036, V0029].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What exact documents does the portal ask of a Bangladeshi worker for a Unified Permit in 2026 (police clearance, education certificate, medical certificate)?

2. **The employer pays.** The fee is paid when the application is filed and covers the work visa and the work permit; the employer also pays a non-refundable guarantee deposit [W0054, W0055]. The guarantee fee, 50 RP (calculation units) per worker, pays for the departure or expulsion of a worker who has no money of his own [L0127, L0128]. Under the 2025 rules (Resolution No. 367) the costs per worker were [W0057, W0058, W0059, W0060, W0061, W0062, G0002, G0003]:

   - permit to employ foreign labour: 20,000 som (about Tk 28,400);
   - guarantee deposit: 5,000 som (about Tk 7,100);
   - electronic work permit: 5,000 som (about Tk 7,100);
   - Unified Permit for entry and the first 60 days: USD 75 (about Tk 9,300);
   - extension to 360 days: USD 225 (about Tk 28,000);
   - in total, 30,000 som plus USD 300 (about Tk 80,000).

   They are paid in two stages: first for two months, then for 12 months [W0063]. Resolution No. 312 replaced these rules on 30 April 2026 [W0137]. Resolution No. 506 of 23 July 2026 gave garment companies a special rate until 1 November 2026, on a letter from the Chamber of Commerce and Industry confirming membership [K0001, K0008]: USD 75 for the Unified Permit, paid in som at the National Bank rate, plus 20,000 som state duty and a 5,000 som guarantee deposit; and USD 225 for the resident card plus 5,000 som for the electronic work permit [K0002, K0003, K0004, K0006, K0007]. These are the same amounts as the 2025 rates, and the special rate lapsed on 1 November 2026 [W0057, W0058, W0060, W0061, K0001]. Fees paid on the portal are not refunded if the application is refused [W0038].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What are the standard Unified Permit and resident card fees under Resolution No. 312 (Annex 5) since 1 November 2026, and was the garment rate extended?
   > - How much of the permit cost do agencies or brokers charge Bangladeshi workers, and under what name?

3. **The permit is decided.** Under the 2025 rules a decision took 7 to 10 working days, and the 2026 garment rate set up to 7 working days [W0087, K0002]. Since 2026 the permit can be issued urgently for an extra fee [W0007]. If the portal returns the application for corrections, the 5-working-day review starts again once they are made [W0042]. The approved permit, with its QR code, appears in the employer's account [W0067].

   **If it is refused:** the reasons are not explained, and the refusal can be challenged in court [L0122]. A permit is annulled for false information, a crime, a dangerous disease, a security threat or terrorist links [W0093].

**What you get back:** a copy of the Unified Permit with its QR code, and the 8-character reference number of the application (Step 5) [W0067, W0044].

### Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates

1. **Get an e-passport** before the employer files, so the permit carries your exact details (Step 2, item 3) [W0049]. Over age 20 you need your NID, and you fill in the form exactly as on it [B0027].

   1. Fill in the form at [epassport.gov.bd](https://www.epassport.gov.bd/) and choose the passport office for your present address [B0068, B0069].
   2. Pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay, or at any bank through A-Challan [B0026]. A 48-page passport costs Tk 4,025, Tk 6,325 or Tk 8,625 for 5 years and Tk 5,750, Tk 8,050 or Tk 10,350 for 10 years (regular, express, super express) [B0024].
   3. Go to the office with your original NID and any technical certificate [B0070]. With an online-verified NID there is no police verification, and delivery is within 15, 7 or 2 working days of enrolment [B0029, B0025].

   **If it is lost or stolen:** file a GD (general diary, a police report) at the nearest police station quickly, then re-apply with a copy of the old passport and the GD [B0028]. Passport helpline: 16445 (09666716445 from abroad), 24 hours [B0030]. For a passport lost or expiring in Kyrgyzstan, see The Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent [B0127].

2. **Get a police clearance if the employer or agency asks for one.**

   You'll need [B0031, B0032]:

   - a passport valid at least 3 months;
   - a present address that is one of the addresses in your passport;
   - Tk 1,500 by treasury challan, code 1-7301-0001-2681, at any Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank branch, or online by card with a service charge.

   1. Register at [pcc.police.gov.bd](https://pcc.police.gov.bd/), log in and click "Apply" [B0065].
   2. Fill in your personal details and your present and permanent addresses; the police verify you at the present address [B0066].
   3. Upload the scanned documents [B0067].
   4. Track it by texting "PCC S <reference>" to 26969, and collect it with your passport or application copy [B0033, B0071].

   **If it fails:** helpdesk Sunday to Thursday, 9 am to 5 pm, 01320001824, 01320001825, help.pcc@police.gov.bd [B0034].

   > **Need to know**

### Step 7: After arrival

The first Unified Permit lasts up to 60 days; get registered at your address as soon as you arrive, and before the permit ends you or the employer that invited you applies for a resident card to extend your stay [W0004, K0029, V0048, V0062].

1. **Get registered at your address at once.** Bangladesh is not on the list of 92 countries whose citizens get a registration-free period, and you must register within 5 working days of entry [K0029, W0034]. Whoever invites you must arrange your registration, and an employer that gives you company housing must register you [W0018, K0036]. You need not go to the office yourself unless the system has no record of your border crossing; registration is made on the passport you entered with [K0037, K0032]. The rules are in Cabinet Resolution No. 600 of 4 September 2026, and decree No. 337 moved registration from Kyzmat, the state service agency, to the Ministry of Labour [K0026, K0019].

   You'll need [K0038, K0039]:

   - the host's petition (ходатайство, khodataystvo) naming the actual address where you live;
   - your valid passport with its visa;
   - the Unified Permit or resident card;
   - the host's Kyrgyz passport or permanent residence permit;
   - if the host does not own the flat or hostel, the owner's notarised consent;
   - a receipt for the state duty and registration fee.

   1. The host applies through the ESUVM (Unified External Migration Accounting System) at any office of the population registration service, on the Online Registration of Foreign Citizens website, or in its mobile app [K0030]. Kyzmat ran Service Centres for Foreign Citizens in Bishkek at 121 Jumabek Street (Wedding Palace building) and in Osh at 211 Kurmanjan Datka Street, Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00 [W0131, W0132].
   2. The host pays the state duty and fee within 3 working days of getting the payment code, or the application is deleted; a fee is not refunded if registration is refused [K0035, K0046].
   3. An office registers you in 1 working day; an online application takes 3 working days, or 1 if urgent [K0031, K0049]. You get a registration slip, on paper or electronic, with a QR code that shows the registration [K0050].

   Registration lasts as long as your Unified Permit or resident card [K0041]. At most one foreigner per 12 square metres of living space can be registered at a house or flat, close relatives excepted [K0034]. Workers who move from site to site or work in the mountains can be registered at the company's legal address [K0042]. After a change of address, passport, purpose or term of stay, re-register within 10 working days; if you are out of Kyrgyzstan for 30 days or more, the registration ends and must be done again [K0043, K0044].

2. **Get the medical papers for the resident card.** A resident card, and a long-term special work visa (type SW1 or SW2), needs a certificate of no HIV antibodies from the Republican Centre for control of blood-borne hepatitis and HIV, a medical certificate on form 086/U (форма 086/У) and a compulsory medical insurance policy [K0009, K0010, K0011, V0053, V0054]. The QR code on the card shows how long you may stay; check its end date [W0078]. Renewing it, and what happens if it is refused, are in Your residence permit [W0079].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

- **The visa with no job.** A Bangladeshi who came in July 2025 on a work visa bought from intermediaries for USD 5,000 (about Tk 6.2 lakh) found no work and had no money for a ticket home [N0101, N0165, G0002]. In 2025, Facebook pages, WhatsApp groups and YouTube channels drew recruits with Central Asian jobs, naming employers who did not know their names were used; arrivals found no jobs, contracts or sponsors [N0110, N0111]. Check the permit yourself (Step 5) [W0043].
- **The unlicensed office with tourist visas.** In 2024 Jannat Trade International, an office in Naya Paltan, Dhaka, with only a tours and travel licence, advertised car-wash jobs in Kyrgyzstan on YouTube at Tk 60,000-65,000 a month and took Tk 5 lakh for a visa, ticket and BMET card [N0112, B0143, B0152]. It filmed people receiving visas at its office for its YouTube channel [N0114]. At Dhaka airport the papers proved fake, and the six people it did send went on 59-day tourist visas and got no jobs [B0142, B0144, U0033]. The CID's counter-trafficking unit arrested the owner, Ibrahim Mollik Nahid, and two associates [N0120, B0156].
- **The visa that ends on arrival.** A Lalmonirhat welder who reached Bishkek on 3 June 2024 found his visa expired the next day; he worked seven months unpaid and served three months in jail [U0009, U0010].
- **Sold to an employer.** In 2022 a worker with a valid visa, work permit and BMET card said the recruiting agency had sold his group to a garment factory owner, who would let them leave only if they paid him back (Step 1, item 3) [N0125, N0127]. One was paid Tk 10,000 in his first month, of which the employer kept Tk 7,000, saying he had paid the broker [N0129].
- **A different job, and the passport taken.** A 2022 group sent to sew was made to work on a building site and tend livestock, and the shop owner took their passports "for registration" and kept them [N0164, N0132]. In June 2024 a sewing-shop owner near Bishkek and a district police officer took workers' passports, withheld wages and threatened them with prison for illegal stay; both were detained [U0049, U0050].
- **"Legalisation" in Bishkek.** In 2025 the GKNB arrested a Bangladeshi who took USD 66,500 (about Tk 82.7 lakh) from fellow Bangladeshis for papers to stay legally and gave them forged permits; more than 120 passports with expired documents were seized [U0063, U0064, G0002].
- **"Europe through Kyrgyzstan."** See What people believe, and the facts [N0105, N0140].

Complain in Bangladesh as in Step 1, item 9 [B0018].

#### Tried by workers

- **Working on a tourist or business visa.** Staying for a purpose other than the one declared on entry is fined 100 RP (10,000 som, about Tk 14,200); in May 2025, 46 foreigners found working on tourist visas on a Bishkek building site were each fined 10,000 som [L0079, U0091, U0061, G0003, R0001].
- **Forged or bought papers.** Using a document you know is forged is punished with a fine of 500 to 1,000 RP or 2 to 5 years in prison; RP is the unit fines are set in, and a 100 RP fine is 10,000 som [L0136, L0073, L0079, U0091]. False information in an application gets a visa refused and a Unified Permit annulled [L0098, W0093].

- **Crossing into Kazakhstan without papers.** Illegal border crossing is punished with 1 to 3 years' corrective labour, a fine of 500 to 1,000 RP or up to 2 years in prison (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [L0135].

### Your rights at work

The Labour Code of the Kyrgyz Republic covers foreigners working for organisations in Kyrgyzstan, unless a law or treaty says otherwise [L0001]. Forced labour, making someone work under threat of violence, is banned [L0002]. For any broken right, use the routes under If you aren't paid [L0062].

#### Contract

- **Your copy.** The contract is made in two copies signed by both sides, and one is yours [L0006]. The employer may not make you do work that isn't in it [L0009].
- **What it must say.** The workplace and the pay terms: the wage rate or salary, extra payments and bonuses [L0004, L0003]. A term changes only if both sides agree, in writing [L0005].
- **Length.** A fixed-term contract lasts at most 5 years; if it ends and you keep working with neither side ending it, it becomes open-ended [L0007, L0008].
- **Probation.** Only if the contract says so, and at most 3 months [L0010, L0011].
- **Papers about your job.** Ask in writing: within 5 working days the employer must give you certified copies of documents about your job, even after you have left [L0012].

> **Need to know**
> - In what language are Bangladeshi workers' contracts in Kyrgyzstan written, and do they get a Bangla or English copy?

#### Pay and deductions

**Pay**

- At least the legal minimum wage [L0042]: 3,280 som (KGS, the Kyrgyz currency; about Tk 4,700) a month in 2026, the pay for unskilled work over a full month of normal hours [K0051, K0053, G0003]. The budget law sets 3,608 som (about Tk 5,100) for 2027 [K0052, G0003]; in October 2026 the government proposed raising it to 9,800 som (about Tk 13,900), which parliament has to pass [L0163, G0003]. Kyrgyzstan also sets minimum pay for foreign workers by region and sector [W0090].
- In money, in som: pay in IOUs, receipts or food or goods coupons is banned [L0043, L0044].
- At least once a month, by bank transfer, not cash; this applies to companies and to individual entrepreneurs who employ foreigners, and the wage goes onto your bank card [L0046, L0185, L0045, L0129].
- With each payment, a written statement of every part of your wage and the size and reason of every deduction (a payslip) [L0053].
- If wages, holiday pay or final pay are late, the employer must add 0.25% of the unpaid sum for every calendar day of delay [L0048].

**Deductions**

Only in the cases the Labour Code or another law allows, and all together at most 20% of each payment (50% where a law allows) [L0051, L0052]. In 2022 one Bangladeshi got Tk 10,000 for his first month, of which the employer kept Tk 7,000, saying he had paid the broker to bring the workers [L0179]. Garment workers told by the broker they would earn Tk 52,000 a month were paid by the number of pieces sewn [L0187, L0181].

> **Need to know**

#### Tax and deductions from your pay

Income tax is 10% [G0040]. When a company or an individual entrepreneur employs a foreigner, the employer, as tax agent, must take the income tax out of your pay and pay it, with every other compulsory payment, to the State Tax Service [G0042]. Contributions to the Social Fund (the state social insurance fund) are also charged on foreign employees' pay, at main rates of 27.25% or 12.25%, of which 10% comes out of your pay and the rest is paid by the employer [G0041]. For garment workers, the State Tax Service said in March 2026 that it had cut the compulsory payments per worker (income tax and social insurance together) from 5,500 to 9,500 som (about Tk 7,800 to 13,500) to a single 2,500 som (about Tk 3,560) [G0044, G0003].

Unpaid tax can cost you the permit: the Unified Permit (Единое разрешение, the electronic document that is both work visa and work permit) can be refused or cancelled when no tax has been paid for 3 months or more, and the resident card (резидент-карта, the card that replaces it after the first 60 days) when tax and social contributions go unpaid for 3 months or more [L0120, W0004, L0121]. Check your payslip shows both (Pay and deductions) [L0053].

#### Hours, rest and leave

- **Hours.** At most 40 a week; 36 for heavy, harmful or dangerous work [L0024, L0025]. A daily break of 30 minutes to 1 hour for rest and meals, unpaid [L0031].
- **Overtime.** Only with your written consent, and at most 4 hours over any 2 days in a row [L0026, L0027]. It pays at least 1.5 times for the first 2 hours and double after that; night hours pay at least 1.5 times [L0029, L0030]. The employer must record your overtime and tell you the hours if you ask [L0028].
- **Rest days.** 2 a week on a 5-day week, 1 on a 6-day week; Sunday is the usual rest day [L0032, L0033]. Work on a rest day or a non-working holiday pays at least double, or, if you choose, single pay plus another day off [L0034, L0035]. Eid al-Fitr (Orozo Ait) and Eid al-Adha (Kurman Ait) are non-working holidays [L0036].
- **Annual leave.** 28 calendar days, paid; in the first year you can take it after 11 months' work [L0037, L0038]. Unused days are paid when you leave, once you have worked at least a month [L0039, L0040].
- **Sick leave.** The employer pays a sickness benefit [L0054]. You can't be dismissed while on sick leave or holiday unless the business closes [L0022].
- **Maternity leave.** 70 days before the birth and 56 after (70 after a complicated birth or twins) [L0041].

#### Probash pension (Bangladesh)

#### Renewing

1. **The employer applies, online, before the current document runs out.** Renewal is filed by the inviting party that applied before, and you need not appear in person [V0062, W0077]. Under the 2025 rules (Resolution No. 367) it had to be filed at least 10 working days before expiry [W0086, W0057]. Arranging your stay documents is the employer's duty (Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [W0018].

For a new passport, the Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent charges workers USD 33 (about Tk 4,100) for a 48-page, 5-year e-passport and USD 55 (about Tk 6,800) for 10 years, and has taken applications from Bangladeshis living in Kyrgyzstan (see The Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent) [B0130, B0131, B0127, G0002].

4. **Check the new card:** scan its QR code and read the end date [W0078].

#### If you lose your job

**Your permit ends with the job.** The Unified Permit lapses when the employer closes or the contract ends or is ended early, whatever time is left on it, and the permit is cancelled if you stop working [L0107, L0119]. The employer must tell the MFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) within 3 calendar days so the permit is cancelled; presidential decree No. 337 of 28 September 2026 moves the issuing of permits and resident cards from the MFA to the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration [L0112, K0013, K0014, K0015]. You then have 30 days to leave on your own; after that the permit is cancelled automatically [L0109, L0110]. Staying on makes you an overstayer (What can get you jailed or deported) [L0074].

**If you are dismissed:**

- Dismissal grounds include absence of over 3 hours in a row in a working day without good reason, and forged documents or knowingly false information given when you were hired [L0018, L0019].
- You can't be dismissed while on sick leave or holiday, unless the business closes [L0022].
- Challenge a dismissal within 2 months of the dismissal order (If you aren't paid) [L0066].

**If the employer closes or cuts staff:** it must warn you in writing, against your signature, at least 1 month before; during that month you get one paid day a week to look for work; on dismissal you get at least two months' average pay [L0020, L0021, L0023].

- **Citizenship:** marrying a Kyrgyz citizen doesn't change your citizenship [V0152]. A recognised refugee can apply by a simplified procedure after 3 years (Asylum) [U0138].

### What can get you jailed or deported

#### No valid papers, or overstay

**The fine.** Being in Kyrgyzstan without a valid visa, registration, residence permit or work permit, with expired ones, or staying after your legal stay ends [L0074, L0090, G0003]:

- up to 1 month: 55 RP, 5,500 som (about Tk 7,800) [L0074, L0090, R0001];
- 1 to 3 months: 100 RP, 10,000 som (about Tk 14,200) [L0075, L0090, R0001];
- 3 to 6 months: 150 RP, 15,000 som (about Tk 21,300) [L0076, L0090, R0001];
- 6 months to a year: 175 RP, 17,500 som (about Tk 24,900) [L0077, L0090, R0001];
- over a year: 200 RP, 20,000 som (about Tk 28,500) [L0078, L0090, R0001].

Staying for a purpose other than the one you declared on entry, such as working on a tourist visa, is fined 100 RP (10,000 som, about Tk 14,200) [L0079, U0091, U0061, G0003]. Working without the required permit makes you liable to expulsion [L0091, W0016, R0001].

**The 30 days.** With the fine comes a written order to put things right; if you don't within 30 days, a court orders expulsion [L0080, L0092].

**Leaving after an overstay.** You need an exit visa (type L), valid up to 10 days, and your name goes into a database of violators [L0123, L0124]. It costs [L0102, L0103, L0104, L0105, G0003, G0002]:

- overstay up to 3 months: 17,500 som or USD 200 (about Tk 24,900) [L0102, G0003, G0002];
- 3 months to a year: 96,250 som or USD 1,100 (about Tk 1.37 lakh) [L0103, G0003, G0002];
- 1 to 2 years: 192,500 som or USD 2,200 (about Tk 2.74 lakh) [L0104, G0003, G0002];
- each further year: USD 1,100 (about Tk 1.37 lakh) more [L0105, G0002].

It is free when a court has ordered expulsion [L0125]. Someone too ill to leave alone gets a free emergency exit visa (type E); with no passport, see Going home without valid papers [L0126, W0032].

**In practice:** a Bangladeshi welder whose visa expired the day after he reached Bishkek worked seven months unpaid, was caught and served three months in jail [L0189, L0174]. How people fall out of status, and the raids: Irregular routes and undocumented workers [U0045].

#### Expulsion and entry bans

- A court orders expulsion; you are handed to Bangladesh's authorities or leave on your own under control [L0081, L0083].
- If you don't leave by the court's deadline, you are held in a special centre for up to 30 days until you are removed [L0082].
- Unpaid fines don't stop it [L0084]. You pay its cost; if you can't, the employer or host who invited you pays, and only exceptionally the state [L0096]. Employers pay a guarantee fee of 50 RP (5,000 som, about Tk 7,100) per worker that funds this when the worker has no money [L0127, L0128, L0073, L0090, G0003, R0001].
- Expulsion for an offence brings a 2-year entry ban; after a prison sentence it is 5 years for less serious crimes, 10 for serious ones and life for especially serious ones [L0093, L0094, L0095]. No visa is issued until the ban ends [L0097].

In detention, the Ombudsman's (Akyikatchy's) free hotline, 115, is meant for people in closed institutions facing torture, violence or cruel treatment [L0184, L0139]. A returnee held over three months said detainees were made to stand for hours in the sun [L0178, L0175].

#### False papers

Using a document you know is forged: a fine of 500 to 1,000 RP (about Tk 71,100 to 1.42 lakh) or 2 to 5 years in prison [L0136, L0073, L0090, G0003]. False information in a visa, registration or work permit application gets the visa refused, and forged documents given at hiring are grounds for dismissal [L0098, L0019, R0001].

#### Crossing the border illegally

Crossing the state border illegally, for example into Kazakhstan without papers, is punished by 1 to 3 years' corrective labour, a fine of 500 to 1,000 RP (about Tk 71,100 to 1.42 lakh) or up to 2 years in prison (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [L0135, U0055, L0073, L0090, G0003, R0001].

#### Drugs

- Making, buying, keeping or carrying drugs to sell, or selling them: 5 to 8 years in prison, with property confiscated [L0132].
- Especially large amounts, or sale to a child: 12 to 15 years [L0133].
- Keeping or carrying a large amount without intent to sell: a fine of 1,000 to 2,000 RP (about Tk 1.42 to 2.85 lakh) or up to 5 years [L0134, L0073, L0090, G0003, R0001].

### If you aren't paid

**Deadlines:** complain within 3 months of learning of the breach (2 months from a dismissal order); unpaid wages can be claimed for 3 years; the same limits apply to complaints to the prosecutor and the labour inspection [L0066, L0067, L0068].

**Keep evidence, even without a contract:** WhatsApp or Telegram messages, photos of documents, payment records and colleagues' statements [L0169]. Ask the employer in writing for certified copies of your job papers (Contract) [L0012]. An employer that puts people to work without a written contract is fined 75 RP (about Tk 10,700) as a person or 230 RP (about Tk 32,700) as a company [L0088, L0073, L0090, G0003]. Late pay earns you 0.25% a day (Pay and deductions) [L0048, R0001].

**Act in this order:**

1. **Complain to the labour inspection,** the Service for Control and Supervision of Labour Legislation (Служба по контролю и надзору за соблюдением трудового законодательства) under the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration [K0023].
   1. Write a complaint to its local office where the employer is, or hand it in at a TsON (ЦОН, public service centre) in the regions [L0166, L0167].
   2. State the breach. Sample wording: "Я, [ФИО], паспорт [номер], работаю в [работодатель, адрес] [должность] с [дата]. Не выплачена зарплата за [месяцы]. Прошу провести проверку." ("I, [name], passport [number], have worked at [employer, address] as [job] since [date]. My wages for [months] have not been paid. I ask for an inspection.") [L0170]
   3. You may complain anonymously, but the check may then go less deep [L0168].

   What you get back: an order to the employer to put the breach right [L0171]. In the first half of 2026 inspectors recovered 4.4 million som (about Tk 62.6 lakh) of unpaid wages for all workers [L0164, G0003]. Presidential decree No. 362 of 26 October 2026 orders the Cabinet to give the same Service migration-control powers over foreign workers, within three months [K0023, K0024, K0025].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### Changing employer

**You can't move your permit.** The Unified Permit can't be passed to another employer, and you can't be transferred [L0108]. A new employer would have to apply for a new permit in your name (Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [W0053]. A resident card can list up to three organisations you work for at the same time [L0130].

**Resigning.** Give one month's written notice [L0013]. If you leave a fixed-term contract early without a valid reason, the employer may claim the compensation set in the contract, up to one month's average pay [L0016]. You can also end it by agreement: the other side must answer a written proposal within 3 working days [L0017]. If the employer has broken the law or the contract, and the labour inspection, a trade union or a court confirms it, the employer must release you on the date you ask [L0014, L0015].

**Leaving without notice.** If you walk out and the employer reports you to the police, no other employer may hire you, nor may the same one again [L0114, L0115]. Your permit then ends with the job (If you lose your job) [L0107].

> **Need to know**

#### Going home on leave

Annual leave is 28 paid days (Hours, rest and leave) [L0037]. If you are out of Kyrgyzstan for 30 days or more, your address registration ends automatically and must be done again when you return [K0044, W0126].

> **Need to know**

#### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Collect what you are owed by your last working day:** all wages, and pay for unused leave once you have worked a month [L0047, L0039, L0040]. Ask in writing for certified copies of your job papers before you go (Contract) [L0012].
2. **The ticket.** The employer must pay for your departure; one that doesn't goes on the Register of Bad-Faith Employers (If you aren't paid) [L0111, L0113].
3. **Leave within 30 days** of the job ending, on your Unified Permit (If you lose your job) [L0109, L0110].
4. **Overstayed?** You need the paid exit visa (What can get you jailed or deported) [L0123, L0102].
5. **Too ill to travel alone?** Ask for the free emergency exit visa (type E) [L0126, W0032].
6. **Cash.** What you may take out of Kyrgyzstan and bring into Bangladesh: Coming home [G0029].

### Bringing your family

Your spouse, children under 18 and dependent parents can come on a family visa (type FF) of up to 90 days, based on your Unified Permit or resident card, and then get a resident card [W0033, V0066, V0067, V0060]. They may not work or run a business [V0068]. The papers and steps are in Family [V0069].

### Staff travelling with a Gulf employer

A Bangladeshi with a valid resident card of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman or Bahrain who works as staff of a citizen of one of those countries can get an accompanying visa (type A) to travel with that employer to Kyrgyzstan, for up to 90 days with no extension [V0037]. ### The patent: not open to Bangladeshis

The State Tax Service's patent, a fixed monthly tax of 800 som (KGS, the Kyrgyz currency; about Tk 1,140) that serves as a work permit, lets a foreigner work only for private individuals, and only during a visa-free stay [W0134, W0133, G0003]. The Tax Service describes it as for migrant workers from neighbouring countries hired by Kyrgyz citizens for construction, crop farming, livestock or personal services [G0043]. Bangladeshis have no visa-free stay, so they can't use it [W0028, V0155].

## Student visa

A foreign student enters on a study visa (type S), valid up to 90 days, and then gets a resident card (резидент-карта, the plastic card that lets a foreigner stay up to a year), renewed through the university [V0049, V0058, V0057, V0062, V0161]. In 2022, 28,239 foreigners made up 77% of students at Kyrgyzstan's 22 medical universities [V0123]. In May 2024 at least 800 Bangladeshi medical students lived in Bishkek [V0126].

Bangladeshis have had trouble getting this visa. Over 150 Bangladeshi medical students have been stuck in Bangladesh since July 2025, saying the Kyrgyz government stopped issuing them visas without notice; in April 2026 the e-visa portal showed the student category as open, but Bangladeshi applications were not approved, and no reason was given [N0093, N0094, N0168].

### Step 2: Plan for BMDC registration before you go

To practise in Bangladesh, a doctor with a foreign degree must pass the Registration Qualifying Examination (RQE) of the BMDC (Bangladesh Medical and Dental Council), open only to graduates whose degree is considered equivalent to a Bangladeshi MBBS or BDS [V0137, V0138]. ## Visit visa

Kyrgyzstan has two short visit visas: the tourist visa (type TS, up to 30 days) and the business visa (type B, up to 90 days), which also covers medical treatment [V0013, V0015, V0018]. A business visa gives no right to work or earn money, people on tourist visas have no right to work, and a tourist visa can't be changed to another visa inside Kyrgyzstan (see Changing visa type) [V0016, U0061, V0045]. Every Bangladeshi needs a visa [V0155, W0028]. Visas are issued by the MFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) in Bishkek, Kyrgyz embassies and consulates, and online on the e-Visa portal, evisa.e-gov.kg [V0012, V0093]. Bangladesh's Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare warns people not to go to work on a tourist or visit visa on a broker's promise [B0006].

4. **If you mean to look for work, a visit visa won't do.** Work needs a Unified Permit (Единое разрешение, the electronic permit that combines the work visa and work permit), which the employer applies for on the e-Visa portal (see Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [V0048, W0027, W0064, W0066].

### Step 2: Apply on the e-visa portal

You'll need:
- a passport valid at least 6 months after the visa ends, with at least 2 free pages [V0083];
- a colour photo on a white background (35 x 45 mm) [V0034];
- scans of your documents, in Kyrgyz or Russian or translated into one of them [V0093, V0089];
- for the simplified route, your Gulf or Brunei resident card [V0002];
- the inviting company's, clinic's or tour company's letter, if your route needs one [V0014, V0017, V0018];
- a debit or credit card; it need not be in your name [V0093, V0081].

1. **Check the rule for Bangladesh:** on evisa.e-gov.kg, the "Do I need visa?" tool shows, for your passport country, whether a visa is needed and how to get it [V0090].
2. **Fill in the form in English** [V0080]. Each traveller, children and infants included, needs a separate e-visa [V0092].
3. **Open the verification email within 72 hours,** or it expires [V0088].
4. **Pay by card** [V0093, V0081]. The fees:
   - single-entry visa of up to 1 month: 3,589 som or USD 50 (about Tk 5,100 to 6,200) [V0156, G0003, G0002];
   - multiple-entry visa of up to 1 month: 4,315 som or USD 60 (about Tk 6,100 to 7,500) [V0157, G0003, G0002];

5. **Wait:** an e-visa takes up to 5 working days, or up to 2 at the urgent rate [V0019]. The application also goes to the national security body for up to 4 working days (1 at the urgent rate) [V0021].
6. **Print the e-visa** when it arrives by email [V0031]. Don't buy your air ticket before it is approved [V0085].

To apply at a Kyrgyz embassy instead (the ambassador in New Delhi is also accredited to Bangladesh; see Contacts in Bangladesh), bring the visa form, a 35 x 45 mm colour photo, proof of payment and the documents for your visa type; the embassy may also ask for a guarantee letter from your host to pay all costs of your stay and departure [V0034, V0033, B0138].

### Step 3: At the border and after arrival

1. **State your true purpose to the border officer:** it is recorded in the migration database [V0009].
2. **Carry enough money for the stay and the trip home:** the visa's length can be cut if you lack it [V0032].
3. **Get registered at your address within 5 working days of arrival** (see Work visa: company worker, Step 7) [V0084].
4. **Leave before the visa ends,** or extend it (Step 2, item 4) [V0158]. An overstayer leaves on an exit visa, valid up to 10 days (see What can get you jailed or deported) [V0056].

### Can you work?

No. A business visa gives no right to work, run a business or earn income [V0016]. Staying for a purpose other than the one declared on entry, such as working on a visit visa, is fined 100 calculation units (расчетный показатель, RP, the unit Kyrgyz fines are set in), 10,000 som (about Tk 14,200), and working without a permit makes you liable to expulsion [L0079, U0091, G0003, L0091]. In May 2025, 46 foreigners who had come on tourist visas were found working on a Bishkek building site; each was fined 10,000 som, and a criminal case was opened against the organisers [U0061, R0001].

2. **Gather the papers:**

   - your application [V0069];

3. **Apply on evisa.e-gov.kg** as in Visit visa, Step 2; each family member, children included, needs a separate visa [V0093, V0092].

## Changing visa type

- **Tourist visa to anything else:** Not allowed inside Kyrgyzstan; a tourist, transit or religious visa can't be switched [V0045].
- **Other visa, or a visa-free stay, to a visa matching your real purpose:** Allowed inside Kyrgyzstan [V0045, V0006]. For a long-term visa, the inviting party that got your current visa files the request at least 5 working days before it ends [V0046, V0047].
- **Any visa to work:** Only through an employer: it gets a Unified Permit for up to 60 days, then a resident card (see Work visa: company worker, Step 3) [V0048]. A valid visa or registration alone does not let anyone hire you [W0142].

- **Resident card to temporary residence permit (вид на жительство):** Possible after more than six months with a legal income, including work under a contract with a work permit (see Your residence permit) [W0020].
- **Study visa to resident card:** The usual path for students (Student visa, Step 4) [V0058, V0059].
- **Family visa to work:** The family visa gives no right to work [V0068].

## Having a child in Kyrgyzstan

A child born in Kyrgyzstan to two Bangladeshi parents is not Kyrgyz: Kyrgyz citizenship by birth there goes to children of a Kyrgyz parent, of stateless parents, or of ethnic Kyrgyz parents [V0151, V0149, V0150]. The child is Bangladeshi by descent if the father or mother is Bangladeshi at the birth [V0153]. For a working mother's maternity leave, see Your rights at work [L0041].

## Asylum

Asylum is protection for someone who cannot return to their own country, and the claim must explain why [U0147]. Kyrgyzstan has signed the 1951 Refugee Convention and has its own refugee law, run by the Ministry of Labour, Social Security and Migration (Минсоцтруда, Kyrgyzstan's labour and migration ministry) [U0123, U0124]. On 30 June 2025 there were 2,186 refugees and asylum seekers in the country, 524 of them Afghans, and the state had recognised 168 refugees in all [U0146, U0139, U0141]. If the last appeal fails and you have no other legal status, you may have to leave [U0135]. In September 2026 the Ministry proposed refusing to register repeat claims that bring no new facts, saying they let people with no other legal grounds stay longer; about 160 were filed from 2022 to mid-2026 [U0143, U0145, U0144].

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi applied for refugee status in Kyrgyzstan since 2023, and with what result?

### How to apply

1. **Claim inside Kyrgyzstan or at an official border crossing** (the airport or a land border) [U0125]. Someone who entered irregularly is not punished for that if he applies without delay [U0130].
2. **Write the application in Kyrgyz or Russian,** with your personal details and the reasons you cannot return to Bangladesh; missing documents do not stop you applying, and it should be registered as soon as possible [U0126, U0147, U0149]. The NGO Legal Clinic Adilet helps free with writing and translation [U0127].
3. **Hand it to the Ministry's Department for work with kairylmans (ethnic Kyrgyz returnees) and refugees,** which decides claims: 215 Tynystanov Street, Bishkek, +996 312 62-13-93 [U0124, U0128, U0129].
4. **Register.** You get a certificate of registration of your application, valid 3 months and renewed every 3 months until the final decision [U0131, U0132]. Within 5 working days, register your address at the local TsON (public service centre) [U0133].
5. **Go to the interviews.** You are interviewed once or more with a free interpreter, then the Ministry decides [U0148]. The whole procedure is free [U0136].

> **Need to know**
> - May an asylum seeker with a registration certificate work legally while the claim is pending?
> - Does the registration certificate protect an asylum seeker from expulsion if he is caught in a police raid?

### If it is refused

You have 3 months to appeal to a district court, then to the appeal court and the Supreme Court [U0134]. If the last appeal fails and you have no other status, you may have to leave (see Going home without valid papers) [U0135].

### If you are recognised

A refugee may work without a permit, must re-register with the Ministry every year, and can apply for Kyrgyz citizenship by a simplified procedure after 3 years [U0137, U0150, U0138].

## Irregular routes and undocumented workers

Many Bangladeshis without papers in Kyrgyzstan came in legally on a visa and lost their status there: the visa ran out, the promised work visa never came, or there was no job [U0040, U0041, U0045, U0009, U0006]. In May 2025 the GKNB (State Committee for National Security, Kyrgyzstan's security service, which runs the raids with the police) estimated about 5,000 undocumented migrants in the country [U0072, U0081]. A foreigner without valid papers is fined and can be expelled, and expulsion goes ahead even if the fine is unpaid (see What can get you jailed or deported) [L0074, L0092, L0091, L0084].

### How people lose their papers

- **A tourist visa sold as a work visa.** In 2024 Bangladesh police's CID (Criminal Investigation Department) said Jannat Trade International, which held only a tours and travel licence, sent six people on 59-day tourist visas with promises of garment, chocolate and food-packaging jobs; none got work [U0031, U0032, U0151, U0033]. Working on a tourist visa breaks the purpose of entry: in May 2025 each of 46 tourist-visa holders found on a Bishkek building site was fined 10,000 som (Kyrgyz som, KGS; about Tk 14,200) [L0079, U0061, U0062, G0003].
- **A visa that ends on arrival.** A Lalmonirhat man who reached Bishkek on 3 June 2024 for a welding job found his visa expired the next day; he worked seven months unpaid and was jailed for three months [U0009, U0010].
- **"Your work visa comes later."** Many South Asians were brought on ordinary visas with promises of work visas that never came; they move from workshop to workshop without wages, and many have had their passports taken [U0045, U0046]. Bangladeshis flown to Osh, in the south, were given no visas, locked in and had their promised pay cut from USD 500 to USD 300 a month [N0075].
- **The sponsor does not renew.** The GKNB chairman said in 2025 that employers and universities often fail to extend visas, leaving foreigners with expired documents [U0074].
- **Brought legally, then sold.** In 2022 men sent as garment workers with visas, work permits and BMET smart cards (the emigration clearance card of BMET, the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) by M/S Makkah Madina Trade Int (RL-810, its recruiting licence number) and M/S Al-Jabeen Establishment Ltd (RL-884) said the agency had sold them to a factory owner for USD 8,300 (about Tk 10.3 lakh) [U0018, U0021, U0019, G0002]. They were promised Tk 55,000 a month for seven to eight hours' work a day and made to work 17 to 18 hours for Tk 10,000; the owner said they could leave only if they paid him back [U0016, U0020].
- **Moving to taxi or delivery work.** No work visa has been issued for taxi or delivery work; the MFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) says irregular workers usually register with a firm and then move to taxis for higher pay [N0163, N0062].

### Onward routes: Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia

- **Towards Europe.** In 2024 CID said Kyrgyzstan had become a popular trafficking route because it is seen as close to Europe, with people sent through Dubai on visit visas and on to Kyrgyzstan [U0034, U0035]. In 2025 a 21-year-old from Shariatpur paid a broker Tk 4.8 lakh for Italy, was flown Dubai to Bishkek, and spent 44 days in prison instead [U0011, U0012]. In 2023 a Sylhet broker took Tk 6 lakh for Poland, left his client in Kyrgyzstan saying he would fetch a Ukraine visa from Dubai, and disappeared; the man's family borrowed for his ticket home [U0037, U0039].
- **In from Uzbekistan.** In June 2024 border guards in Jalal-Abad caught 11 Bangladeshis who had gone to Uzbekistan for work on 8 June, found none, and tried to cross into Kyrgyzstan away from the official crossings; the Kyrgyz organiser was arrested with them [U0051, U0052]. A Bishkek facilitator said in 2024 that stranded Bangladeshis had to go to Uzbekistan and come back to get new visas [U0047].
- **Out to Kazakhstan.** In November 2023 Kazakh prosecutors arrested three organisers who had smuggled 20 South Asians from Kyrgyzstan around the border checkpoints [U0058]. Bangladeshis who crossed from Kyrgyzstan without a visa in June 2024 were caught at a checkpoint in Kostanay region, on the Russian border, and each got six months in prison and expulsion from Kazakhstan with a five-year ban [U0055, U0056, U0057]. Crossing the Kyrgyz border illegally is itself a crime (see What can get you jailed or deported) [L0135].
- **Russia and its war.** In October 2026 Bangladesh's Special Response Battalion said 30 Bangladeshis who paid about Tk 10 lakh each to Jabal-e-Noor, RS International and TS Group for construction jobs in Russia had their passports and phones taken on arrival and were handed to the army; 20 are provisionally confirmed dead [U0115, U0118, U0116]. The network included a Kyrgyz woman named Julia [U0117].

### Raids

Raids under Operation Nelegal ("Illegal") are run by the GKNB with the MVD (Ministry of Internal Affairs, the police) several times a year, on building sites and sewing workshops [U0069, U0077, U0080, U0081].

- **2024:** in April the GKNB found over 130 Pakistanis and Bangladeshis in Bishkek lodgings, 64 with expired visas and no registration [U0040]. In May 46 Bangladeshis were detained for illegal work in Bazar-Korgon, Jalal-Abad region, and in August 28 were detained and fined in Bishkek [U0053, U0048]. The GKNB chairman, Kamchybek Tashiev, said 1,000 Bangladeshis and 1,500 Pakistanis had been deported, with officers catching 20 to 50 irregular migrants a day [U0044, N0087].
- **2025:** Decree No. 164 of 22 May 2025 gave foreigners until 10 June 2025 to legalise their stay or leave without fines; in that campaign about 9,600 foreigners legalised their stay without leaving and over 1,700 left [U0152, U0068, N0046]. Operation Nelegal-2025 began on 11 June [U0069].
- **2026:** raids on 1-10 June produced 922 reports against foreigners, and 4 Bangladeshis and 16 Pakistanis were expelled after June raids in Issyk-Kul region [U0078, U0076]. On 1-10 October the GKNB and MVD wrote 1,487 reports with fines totalling 13,407,000 som [U0081, U0082].

#### What happens when you are caught

1. **You are taken to a filtration centre** and handed to the MVD's Service for Combating Extremism and Illegal Migration [U0070].
2. **You get a report (protocol) and a fine** by the length of your overstay, from 5,500 som (about Tk 7,800) to 20,000 som (about Tk 28,500), with a written order to fix the violation (amounts under What can get you jailed or deported) [L0074, L0078, L0090, L0080, G0003].
3. **A court orders expulsion** if the violation isn't fixed within 30 days, and working without a permit makes you liable to expulsion [L0092, L0081, L0091]. Unpaid fines do not stop it [L0084].
4. **Jail is possible.** Returnees in 2025 described three months in jail, more than three months after surrendering to the police, and 44 days in prison, with detainees made to stand in strong sun [U0010, U0013, U0012, U0014].
5. **An entry ban follows expulsion** (see Bans after you leave) [L0093].

## Sending money home

**Send through a bank or a money-transfer company on the register of the NBKR (National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic, the central bank), never hundi** [G0004, G0022]. Hundi (sending money through unlicensed private agents outside the banking system) is illegal in Bangladesh, and hundi brokers settle their deals through bKash and Nagad, Bangladesh's mobile-money wallets [G0022, G0023]. By June 2026 Bangladesh's Financial Intelligence Unit (BFIU) had frozen or suspended about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling, and in August 2026 it decided to freeze about 14,000 more [G0024]. Kyrgyzstan prosecutes informal channels too: in May 2025 the GKNB (State Committee for National Security, Kyrgyzstan's security service) detained two men who had moved more than USD 20 million (about Tk 249 crore) through Bishkek exchange points outside official records and kept some clients' money, including USD 2 million (about Tk 24.9 crore) handed over by one man [G0025, G0002]. Money sent legally earns the family Bangladesh's 2.5% cash incentive, kept in the FY2026-27 budget, and in some cases a further 2.5% from the bank [G0021, G0017]. Bangladesh Bank publishes no separate figure for money sent from Kyrgyzstan; it counts it under "Others" [G0020].

### Your salary and a bank account

Companies, and since 1 January 2026 individual entrepreneurs, must pay a foreign worker's wages by bank transfer to a card; cash is allowed only in exceptional cases [L0185, W0105, L0129]. The resident card (the plastic permit that follows the first Unified Permit) works as a bank card, a special bank account is opened for it, and your wages are paid onto it (Work visa: company worker, Step 7: After arrival) [W0081, V0048, W0075, W0083]. A bank can also open a current account of your own for a foreigner who doesn't live permanently in Kyrgyzstan (a "non-resident"); Bakai Bank, for example, does [G0045].

You'll need [G0045]:
- your passport and a copy;
- where the bank asks for it, the document that gives you the right to stay in Kyrgyzstan (the Unified Permit or resident card) and a copy.

1. **Fill in the bank's papers:** a client form, an application, a signature card and the account agreement [G0045].
2. **Show the originals** of the documents you copied, for comparison [G0045].

### How to send

**The channels:**
- **Transfer systems licensed in Kyrgyzstan.** The NBKR's register lists the operators allowed to send money; Western Union (registered 2018), MoneyGram (2014) and Ria Money Transfer (2017) are on it, among 20 international systems in July 2026, with Zolotaya Korona, Unistream and Hello [G0004, G0005, G0006, G0007, G0008].
- **Banks.** Optima Bank, for example, sends money without a bank account through Western Union, Zolotaya Korona, MoneyGram, Astrasend and Ria, and to bank accounts abroad through SWIFT (the international bank-transfer network), at all its branches [G0009]. Western Union has agents in Bishkek, Osh and Jalal-Abad [G0012].
- **Bank apps.** MBANK's app sends money to 160 countries, to phone numbers, mobile wallets and bank accounts; the fee depends on the bank's partner [G0018].
- **Straight to bKash: no Kyrgyz partner.** bKash's list of countries with partners that pay into a bKash wallet does not include Kyrgyzstan (checked 10 November 2026) [G0013]. Profee, a transfer app, advertises card payments from Kyrgyzstan to bKash, Nagad and Rocket wallets, but it is not among the 20 international operators on the NBKR register [G0019, G0004, G0008].

You'll need:
- the receiver's surname, first name and patronymic, and his bank account number, or if he has no account, his identity document details [G0010];
- for a bKash wallet, if your channel pays into bKash, the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered when the account was opened [G0015].

1. **Check the company is licensed.** On [nbkr.kg](https://www.nbkr.kg/index1.jsp?item=2665&lang=RUS), open "Реестр международных и локальных систем денежных переводов" (register of international and local money transfer systems) and look under "Операторы международных систем денежных переводов" (operators of international money transfer systems) [G0004].
2. **Change som into dollars only at a bank or licensed exchange bureau.** In February 2026 the NBKR fined 14 people in Bishkek 17,500 som (about Tk 24,900) each for changing cash currency without a licence [G0026, G0003].
3. **Send.**
   1. At a bank branch, write the receiver's exact details on the bank's application form, hand over the money and pay the commission at the bank's tariff [G0010, G0011].
   2. In the MBANK app, tap Payments, then Transfers, then "В другую страну" (to another country), then choose the transfer type and payment service [G0018].
4. **The family collects.** A bKash wallet takes up to Tk 2.5 lakh (about USD 2,000) per transfer, any number of times, and cashes out at Tk 7 per thousand at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs; the 2.5% government incentive is added [G0016, G0017, G0002].

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

In Kyrgyzstan the employer is liable for work injuries, and a claim for them goes straight to court [L0056, L0070]. In Bangladesh the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the Welfare Board, the government board for migrants' welfare) pays grants to injured workers and to dead workers' families, and runs the Probashi call centre, 16135 [L0140, L0149, B0023]. Its grants go only to workers who left with BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) clearance or are Board members, and the Tk 10 lakh migrant-worker insurance only to those who left with BMET clearance [L0140, L0150, B0010]. Bangladesh has no embassy in Kyrgyzstan; the Embassy in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, covers it (see The Bangladesh Embassy in Tashkent) [B0120].

### If you are injured or fall ill

1. **Get treated.** The employer is responsible for work injuries, including on transport it provides to and from work [L0056]. Unless agreed otherwise in writing, the side that invited you bears your upkeep and medical care [L0099]. Sick pay is under Your rights at work [L0054].
2. **Ask the employer for a work-accident report.** If it refuses, or you disagree with what it says, take it to court [L0071].
3. **Claim compensation in court.** It covers lost earnings, extra costs, a one-off sum set by the Cabinet of Ministers for disability, and moral damage, and can't be refused entirely even if you were careless [L0057, L0059, L0058, L0070]. A Kushtia welder injured at work, without a contract, got no help from his employer, went four months unpaid, surrendered to the police and spent over three months in jail [U0015, L0177, L0175].
4. **Too ill to travel alone?** A free emergency exit visa (type E) is issued on a medical opinion and a petition from the health authority [L0126, W0032].
5. **Going home.** The Welfare Board's airport desks take sick returnees to hospital, with a free ambulance from Dhaka or Chattogram airport [B0016, B0047].
6. **Claim the treatment grant in Bangladesh.** The Welfare Board pays up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment to workers who come home disabled or critically ill, if they had BMET clearance or Board membership [L0149, L0150].

   You'll need [L0151]:
   - medical reports and prescriptions;
   - a bank statement;
   - a copy of the arrival stamp in your passport, and a passport copy.

   Apply within 1 year of coming home; a committee sees you on a WhatsApp video call [L0151, L0152].

### If you lose the job

1. **Your permit ends with the job.** The Unified Permit (Единое разрешение, the combined work visa and work permit) lapses when the job ends, early or on time, or the employer closes, and can't be moved to another employer; you have 30 days to leave on it, then it is cancelled automatically (see Your residence permit, If you lose your job) [L0107, L0108, L0109, L0110].

4. **If you were dismissed unfairly or not paid,** bring a dismissal dispute within 2 months of the dismissal order, and any other claim, such as unpaid pay, within 3 months of learning of it (see If you aren't paid) [L0066].
5. **Staying on without a permit** brings a fine and can lead to expulsion (see What can get you jailed or deported) [L0074, L0091, U0079].
