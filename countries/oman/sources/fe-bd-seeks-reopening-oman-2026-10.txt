BD seeks reopening  of Oman labour mkt

   TALHA BIN HABIB   |  

       October 27, 2026 00:00:00 

The government has made a move to persuade the Sultanate of Oman into reopening its market for Bangladeshi workers, considering the huge demand for this segment of manpower in the Gulf country. 
"Our efforts are underway so that the Sultanate of Oman reopens its market for Bangladeshi workers," Managing Director of Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited (BOESL) Mohammad Rashedul Haque has told the FE. 
The two countries started discussing the matter following the Bangladesh visit of Al Sayyid Mohammed Salim Ali Al Said, a member of the Royal Family of Oman, last month. 
During the visit, Said held a meeting with State Minister for Foreign Affairs Shama Obaed Islam and expressed interest in recruiting 10,000 workers from Bangladesh - 5,000 farmers and an equal number of fishermen. 
The two countries signed an agreement on hiring Bangladeshi manpower, but they have to complete some formalities before hiring can be initiated. 
"The Bangladesh government has initiated the move to reopen the manpower market in Oman for Bangladeshi workers following interest expressed by the Oman government," a high official of BOESL told the FE, preferring anonymity. 
He said a six-member delegation of the labour ministry of Oman recently visited Bangladesh and held a discussion with high officials of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment in Dhaka.
During the meeting, the delegation expressed high interest in hiring manpower from Bangladesh, he said. 
He also said BOESL had started preparing detailed proposals for this. 
The proposals would be sent to the government of Oman through proper channels, he said. 
"One of the members of the top brass of the Oman government will make the final decision on reopening its labour market for Bangladeshi workers," he added. 
Despite bilateral discussions between the two countries, Omani visas for Bangladeshi nationals remain restricted due to various issues originating during the term of the previous government.
Sources at the foreign affairs ministry told the FE the government was pursuing diplomatic initiatives with Muscat to restore Bangladeshi workers' regular access to the Omani labour market.
"We are making efforts through diplomatic initiatives to ensure that Bangladeshi workers can resume their migration to Oman on a regular basis," said an official at the Middle East desk of the ministry. 
He said the Bangladesh government was prioritising the Omani authorities as Dhaka sought to send more skilled workers capable of contributing to different sectors of the Gulf country's economy. 
Oman is a middle-to-high-income market, traditionally driven by petroleum and natural gas while aggressively diversifying under the Oman Vision 2040.
The gross domestic product (GDP) of Oman during 2025-26 was estimated at $105-109.6 billion. 
The real GDP Growth of Oman at constant prices in 2024 was recorded at OMR 37.7 billion, with non-oil sectors growing by 3.7 per cent and a 2.6 per cent growth forecast. 
Petroleum (oil and gas) accounts for roughly 50 per cent of the GDP, 64 per cent of export revenue, and 45-75 per cent of government income. 
Agriculture and fisheries comprise 1.7-2.8 per cent of the GDP, with a focus on date palm cultivation, fruits, vegetables, livestock, and commercial fishing supported by the government.
