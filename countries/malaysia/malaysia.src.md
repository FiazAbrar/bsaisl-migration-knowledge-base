# Malaysia: migration guide

How to go to Malaysia from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step, for Bangladesh passport holders. Researched 6 Nov 2026. Citations like \[3\] are official sources; \[N3\] are news or other third-party sources, used only where an official search failed, and listed under "Needs an official source". A year after a citation, like \[N3 (2022)\], means the source is older than 2025. In the reference list, a source dated before 6 Nov 2025 or with no date is marked "possibly outdated". "Need to know" marks what we haven't found in an official source yet; anything reported only unofficially is labelled "unofficial, not verified".

In this doc, Tk means the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000) and RM the Malaysian ringgit. BMET is the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the Bangladesh government agency that gives workers emigration clearance [B0026]. IMI (Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia, JIM) is the Immigration Department of Malaysia, which runs the eVISA (electronic visa) system, applied for online on its MyVISA website, malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, and approves work visas [V0001, V0005, Z0032, W0045]. Bangladeshis need a visa to enter Malaysia for any purpose, even a short visit [V0001, V0002].

## Work visa: company worker (PLKS)

For factory, construction, plantation, farm and service jobs. The pass is the PLKS (Pas Lawatan Kerja Sementara, Temporary Employment Visit Pass), Malaysia's work pass for foreign workers: it is valid for 12 months at a time and renewable [W0048, L0044]. The sectors open to foreign workers are manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services, and Bangladesh is allowed in all of them except where intake is frozen [W0041, W0042]. Workers must be 18 to 45 [W0073, L0064]. The employer pays the levy (a charge the Malaysian government takes from the employer for each foreign worker, set by sector), the visa, the pass and a deposit, and, under the memorandum of understanding (MoU) between the two countries, airfare and all costs on the Malaysian side [W0054, M0226, W0027]. BMET's 2021 order dates that MoU 19 Dec 2021, while the Ministry's 2026 releases date it 18 Dec 2022 and say it is in force until 31 Dec 2026 [W0027, Z0152, Z0156]; the permission letters of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (Bangladesh's ministry for migrant workers) also require the employer to pay airfare both ways [W0036].

**Is recruitment open now?** Malaysia's foreign-worker system, FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralized Management System, through which employers apply online for workers' visas [W0045]; it was developed by the company Bestinet [Z0001]), lists 338 Bangladeshi recruiting agencies: 25 principal agencies, each with 10 associates, and BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd) with 62 associates [W0008]. BOESL describes itself as Bangladesh's only state-owned recruiting organisation [Z0091]. On 5 Nov 2026 BOESL published a zero-cost Malaysia circular, for 112 jobs [W0001]. On 15 Oct 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare announced that recruitment to Malaysia had started [Z0151]. Malaysia agreed to list 338 Bangladeshi agencies: BOESL, the only government recruiting agency, 25 private processing and service agencies, and 312 recruiting agencies [Z0144]; Malaysia's Cabinet allowed 25 approved recruiting agencies each for Bangladesh and Nepal and 10 each for India, Pakistan and Myanmar [Z0155]. At Bangladesh's request Malaysia also agreed in principle to take 10,000 workers completely free of cost [Z0146]: the Ministry's 28 Aug 2026 release says these workers go free of all migration costs, including airfare, with the first batch going through BOESL on special chartered flights [Z0154]. Before that, recruitment was mostly closed: by early 2025 only plantation jobs were open, with an entry deadline of 31 Jan 2025 [W0024]; in Sep 2025 BOESL warned that the regular market had not opened and that only 7,873 workers left behind on 31 May 2024 could go, through BOESL, in construction or tourism [W0020]; sending of those workers began on 25 Nov 2025 [W0018]. By early Oct 2026 employers had reportedly started issuing demand letters (an employer's letter asking a Bangladeshi agency for a set number of workers) and the Bangladesh High Commission (Bangladesh's embassy) in Kuala Lumpur had started attesting them (unofficial, not verified) [A0035]. The Ministry says a worker must not be made to bear any extra financial burden for the job [Z0150], and that until it issues instructions people should make no payment, do no medical test and hand over no passport [Z0158]. **Need to know:** the maximum a private agency may take from a worker outside the 10,000 zero-cost places. News reports say that in Apr 2026 the two governments agreed that employers would bear the full cost of recruitment, at zero cost to workers, with fewer intermediaries and a Malaysian AI-based recruitment system, and that in Jun 2026 they agreed no syndicate would be involved (unofficial, not verified) [A0097, A0104, A0022, A0105].

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP).** BMET's 2021 order says no worker can go to Malaysia without registering in BMET's database [W0027], which is done on OEP [B0139]; BOESL's circular also takes applications on OEP [W0001]. Open [employee.oep.gov.bd](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) in Chrome, enter your mobile number and the code sent to it, fill in the form exactly as on your NID (national ID card), pay by mobile banking (bKash, Nagad or another mobile money service), and download your registration card [B0139]. OEP hotline: 09614445271 [B0144]. In June 2022 BMET charged a non-refundable Tk 200 for registering in its database for Malaysia [W0040]. **Need to know:** the current OEP registration fee.
2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET's Technical Training Centres (TTCs) run 55 trades; enrol on OEP under Training, then Trade/Skills: choose your division, district and centre, press Search, then Apply, Process to Enroll and Confirm; "My Course" shows your applications [B0140, B0037]. Short-course fees are set by BMET and paid against a money receipt; project-funded courses are free and pay a stipend [B0037]. BMET tells Malaysia-bound workers to pass the skill test of CIDB (Malaysia's Construction Industry Development Board), held at Khulna TTC [B0001]. CIDB's own skills training is open to foreign construction workers aged 18 to 55, and CLAB (Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad) handles applications for foreign construction workers [D0040, D0042]. **Need to know:** whether the CIDB test is still required before leaving Bangladesh, for which jobs, and its fee.
3. **Apply for government (BOESL) jobs at zero cost.** BOESL posted its 5 Nov 2026 Malaysia circular as a job notice on [boesl.gov.bd](https://www.boesl.gov.bd/), titled "সরকারিভাবে শূন্য অভিবাসন ব্যয়ে মালয়েশিয়ায় দক্ষ কর্মী নিয়োগ বিজ্ঞপ্তি" (government zero-cost recruitment of skilled workers to Malaysia) [Z0003, W0001]. For this one, 112 operations assistants and technical workers for DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia) at RM1,700 a month basic pay, you apply online on OEP at [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) from 10:00 on 6 Nov to 16:00 on 8 Nov 2026, for one post only [W0001, Z0034]. BOESL's own portal has been closed since 11 Oct 2026 [W0007]. BOESL is state-owned, according to its own notice [Z0091]. A 2024 news report calls its zero-cost Malaysia recruitment the government-to-government (G2G) arrangement between the two countries and says it sent 1,381 workers that way from Oct 2022 to May 2024 (unofficial, not verified) [Z0084, A0059]. Check the circular's rules. In this one: men only [W0001]; operations assistants need at least SSC (Secondary School Certificate, the secondary school exam) or equivalent, English, basic computer skills including MS Excel, numeracy and 1 to 2 years' experience in the work, and technical workers at least HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate, the higher secondary exam) or equivalent, MS Excel and spoken and written English [Z0033]; age 22 to 35, a passport valid at least 18 to 24 months, a 3-year renewable contract, 8 hours a day and 6 days a week, and housing, basic medical care and transport from the employer [W0003]. A software lottery picks 300 applicants, whose list BOESL posts on its website; the employer's representative then tests them and picks 112 [W0004]. Bring to the interview 4 colour passport photos with a white background, colour copies of all your passports, your original education and experience certificates, your NID and a CV showing computer skills, numeracy and any driving licence or overseas or warehouse experience [W0006]. You pay nothing at any stage, so pay no one [W0005]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents and never takes cash [W0068]. BOESL's office is at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban (5th floor), 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka [W0006]; phone 02-58311838, 02-48319125, 02-48317515 or 01765411653 [W0068].
4. **Or go through a private agency.** FWCMS lists the Bangladeshi agencies registered with it: open [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/#recruitment-agencies), choose Recruitment Agencies, then Bangladesh [W0008]. These are the agencies Malaysia approved for the reopened market [Z0144]. Under the Ministry's 15 Oct 2026 process: (1) a listed recruiting agency finds the employer and collects the job order or demand itself [Z0145]; (2) it selects its own workers under Bangladesh's laws [Z0147]; (3) BOESL and the 25 processing agencies handle the Malaysian paperwork, such as the calling visa and e-visa [Z0148]; and (4) the recruiting agency gets your BMET clearance under its own licence, and BOESL or a processing agency can give it a no-objection certificate (NOC) for that clearance [Z0149]. [OEP's agency list](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies) gives each agency's RL (recruiting licence) number, address, office phone, licence status (Active, Expired, Cancelled or Suspended) and validity date [X0001, X0002, Z0035].
5. **Find the permission letter for your job.** The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare or BMET issues permission letters for Malaysia job orders, and BMET posts them on its site; each names the agency and its RL number, the Malaysian employer, the jobs, the salary, the age range, hours, food, housing, airfare, contract length and the most the agency may take from each worker [W0026, W0028, W0029, W0033, W0038]. Some letters cite each employer's Malaysian approval (for example KSM/FWCMSBW490000267/QA03/1; KSM is Malaysia's Ministry of Human Resources and KDN its Ministry of Home Affairs [W0044, W0043]), while the Sep 2025 letter cites the Bangladesh High Commission's attestation letter instead [W0037, W0038]. Open [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos), type "Malaysia" (or মালয়েশিয়া) into the search box; the entries are titled with the letter's DN number and the agency's RL number, for example "DN25-225 RL1274", so find the one with your agency's RL number [W0026]. On 6 Nov 2026 the newest Malaysia permission found by an English search was dated 27 Feb 2025, and a Bangla search also showed an approval dated 21 Sep 2025 [W0026]. **Need to know:** whether every Malaysia job order's letter is posted. The letters seen from Oct 2023 to Feb 2025 also have an English "Recruitment Permission Letter" page with a permission ID such as MY-RL1274-2025-0000883 and a "block visa number", with the number of visas and their issue date: in some letters this is a Malaysian approval reference (KSM/FWCMSBW490000267/QA03/1 or KDN/16031/PHAWP2146128), in others a reference beginning BHC-LW (BHC-LW-36) [Z0004, Z0005, W0032, W0033]. The Sep 2025 letter has no English page [W0038]. Compare the letter with what the agency tells you: the employer's name, the salary (RM1,500 in the letters from Oct 2023 to Feb 2025, RM1,700 in the Sep 2025 letter and in BOESL's Nov 2026 circular) and the maximum fee [W0037, W0033, W0029, W0038, W0001]. A one-line BMET notice of 27 Sep 2023 asks agencies to send demand letters to its IT branch for recruitment permission for any country, including Malaysia, "ব্যতীত" (except, or without) the Malaysian KSM, KDN or FWCMS number, and says that otherwise BMET can't give permission [W0039]; the notice doesn't explain this further. Among the letters seen, some cite a Malaysian KSM or KDN reference, others a reference beginning BHC-LW, and the Sep 2025 letter the Bangladesh High Commission's attestation letter [Z0005, W0032, W0033, W0038]. **Need to know:** which Malaysian or High Commission reference BMET requires on a demand letter now.
6. **Check the employer exists.** Search its name or registration number on [SSM e-Info](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/), the Companies Commission of Malaysia's search; a company profile costs RM15.40 [W0067].

**Before paying:**

- **Know what you should pay.** Under the 2021 agreement, the Ministry's office order of 5 Jul 2022 lists what a Malaysia-bound worker pays inside Bangladesh (passport, medical check, registration, welfare fee, insurance, smart card, pre-departure training, clothing, the agency's service charge and other costs) and what the Malaysian employer pays, including the security deposit, the pass, insurance and the air tickets [R0008, R0009]. The smart card is BMET's card for a worker going abroad legally for a job [Z0057], and its fee is paid at BMET clearance [B0030]. The order caps what the worker pays inside Bangladesh at Tk 78,990 [R0008], and the Bangladesh High Commission's labour FAQ (undated, written before 31 Jan 2025) gives the same government-set amount [W0024].
- **Pay no more than the permission letter allows, and only after the visa and flight are confirmed.** In letters from 2023 to 2025 the maximum was Tk 78,990, Tk 78,740, Tk 72,040, Tk 41,240, Tk 38,710 or nothing, payable only by cheque, bank draft or pay order [W0037, W0033, W0035, W0028, W0034]. In 2022-24 workers reported paying about Tk 4.5 to 5.44 lakh, partly because agencies paid about Tk 1.07 lakh per worker to syndicate leaders among the 100 agencies Malaysia had selected then and Tk 1.6 to 2 lakh to middlemen for demand letters, and in a 2024 survey 96% had borrowed to pay (unofficial, not verified) [W0070, W0072, A0001, A0002, A0064]. Workers have also been pressed to sign declarations that they paid only the official fee (unofficial, not verified) [A0061]. **Need to know:** the official maximum for private-agency jobs since the 2026 reopening. In Aug-Sep 2026 members of BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies, the agency owners' association) alleged that a new syndicate would charge RM5,000 to RM5,500 per worker and that each visa had to be bought for RM8,000 to RM9,000 (unofficial, not verified) [A0026, A0011].
- **Pay into the agency's bank account and keep the receipts** [B0003].
- **Get the contract and a Bangla translation before you go.** The agency must sign a contract with each worker before BMET clearance and give each worker a Bangla translation attested by BMET [W0030].
- **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid in monthly instalments over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days [B0017]. You must be 18 to 60, have no loan in default, and you and your guarantors must live permanently in the branch's area [B0013]. Bring the free application form, 3 photos, copies of your NID, passport, visa and BMET smart card, the contract if you have one, 3 signed cheque leaves from your own bank account with its statement, and open a PKB savings account with at least Tk 500 [B0014]. Bring 2 guarantors: one well-off person with 3 signed cheque leaves, a bank statement and proof of income, and one family member; each brings a photo, NID and proof of address [B0016]. Fees are 0.5% for processing and 0.5% for documentation, plus VAT, and Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover; you also need 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10 and 3 adhesive stamps of Tk 100 for each guarantor [B0018]. You can also apply online on [mygov.bd](https://www.mygov.bd/) [B0019]. Helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018].
- **If an agency takes your money and doesn't send you, or sends you to a different job,** write a self-explanatory application to the Director General of BMET with your proof; BMET's complaint cell sets up an inquiry and a hearing; it's free and meant to take 30 working days [B0034, B0035]. If BMET doesn't resolve it, write to BMET's Director (Employment) at diremi@bmet.gov.bd (3 months), then to the Secretary of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare at secretary@probashi.gov.bd (1 month), then file with the Cabinet Division's Grievance Redress System (GRS) at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/) (3 months) [B0041].
- **Or complain straight to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare:** send an application with your proof, your full address and the agency's name and RL number to its enforcement branch (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255). It's free; action starts within 3 to 5 working days, both sides are heard, and the complaint is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].
- **What happens to complaints in practice:** victims report that BMET takes 4 months to a year, against the official 30 working days [A0073, B0034]; a BMET official said workers who can show all their documents get more compensation [A0077]; in 2023 BMET resolved about a third of complaints (865 of 2,380), awarding Tk 4.87 crore in all, and in Aug 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 (unofficial, not verified) [A0074, A0076].
- **Need to know:** which agencies place Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia in the promised job at a fair cost. The ILO (International Labour Organization) cites only a Qatar fair-recruitment pilot for Bangladeshi workers, none for Malaysia (unofficial, not verified) [A0092].

### Step 2: Medical check in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical)

Every worker must pass a medical check at a centre in Bangladesh accredited by FWCMS before the employer can apply for the visa [W0009]. FWCMS lists the accredited centres at [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/) (51 in Bangladesh); they are registered with local authorities and audited by Bestinet [W0009, W0010]. If you pass, the employer applies for the visa, and the health report is available online in the Immigration system [W0058]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has set the medical test fee for Malaysia-bound workers at **Tk 8,000** per person; no centre may charge more, and no fee is charged for the MiGRAMS slip [Z0164, Z0168]. Medical tests started on 31 Oct 2026 at approved centres; a test done before then isn't accepted for clearance [Z0159]. No centre works for a particular agency: you may test at any approved centre, and if you change agency, the first agency must send your report on without delay [Z0165, Z0166]. Check the approved centres on the Ministry's website; a centre that breaks the rules is removed from the list [Z0160]. Brokers and dishonest agencies have tried to take passports and money promising unauthorised tests: don't hand over your passport or money unless you have confirmed the approval [Z0161]. Report a centre that charges more than Tk 8,000 or harasses you, or any unauthorised test, to secretary@probashi.gov.bd with the date, place, names and evidence [Z0167, Z0163]. On 1 Oct 2026 a mobile court (the on-the-spot court the Ministry runs under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 [B0047]) reportedly sealed Human Diagnostic and Medical Center in Paltan, Dhaka, for testing Malaysia-bound workers without authorisation (unofficial, not verified) [D0017]; a centre with the same name, at 102/1/A New Paltan, is on FWCMS's accredited list [D0019]. Until further notice, agencies applying for BMET clearance must give the approved centre the relevant papers and the VDR visa papers for the test [Z0162]; this differs from FWCMS's order, where the medical comes before the employer applies for the visa [W0009]. **Need to know:** the Ministry's current list of approved centres, what the MiGRAMS slip is (2023 news described MiGRAMS as Bestinet's screening portal; unofficial, not verified) [D0024], and how a worker sees their own result.

### Step 3: The employer gets your visa approval (VDR)

1. **The employer applies; you wait in Bangladesh.** After getting quota approval from the One Stop Centre (an IMI page places it under the Home Ministry, KDN, another under the Human Resources Ministry, KSM; news reports say it moved to the Human Resources Ministry on 30 Jun 2026, unofficial, not verified [R0001]) and paying the levy, the employer applies online through FWCMS for a VDR (Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference) [W0043, W0044, W0045]; FWCMS calls this the Calling Visa [Z0059]. The application includes the Home Ministry approval letter, the levy receipt, a security deposit or guarantee and your passport copy and photo [W0055]. The visa fee for a Bangladeshi worker is RM20 [W0051]. IMI's foreign-worker page gives a security bond of RM500 for a Bangladeshi worker, while IMI's general security-bond table and its 2022 personal-bond table put Bangladesh at RM750 [Z0087, Z0088, Z0169]; either way the employer pays [W0054]. **Need to know:** which bond rate applies to PLKS workers. IMI's fee table for PLKS renewals lists a levy of RM1,850 per worker in manufacturing, construction and services and RM640 in plantation and agriculture in Peninsular Malaysia (lower in Sabah and Sarawak), plus RM60 for the pass and RM125 processing [M0226, M0227]; the employer pays [W0054]. An IMI briefing of Apr 2026, hosted by an industry association, gives RM750 for plantation and agriculture (unofficial, not verified) [Z0006]; IMI's own table is the official rule [M0227]. **Need to know:** whether the levy is charged once a year.
2. **Track the approval yourself.** Ask the agency for the employer's company registration number and the application number, then open IMI's [MyIMMs eServices](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main?pra) and choose "Status Permohonan Pembantu Rumah Asing / Pekerja Asing" (application status for foreign maids or workers) [W0065]. BARU means being processed, LULUS approved, BAYAR paid, CETAK the visa sticker printed, TOLAK rejected, BATAL cancelled and TANGGUH deferred [W0065]. IMI's client charter approves an eVDR (the online VDR) for foreign workers in Peninsular Malaysia in 5 working days and a PLKS renewal in 3 working days; in Sabah a new VDR takes 14 working days and in Sarawak 30, with renewals in 7 working days in both; all for complete, normal applications [C0045, C0039, Z0036, Z0037, Z0038]. **Need to know:** whether and how an employer can appeal a refused VDR or PLKS.
3. **The entry visa is issued in Dhaka.** Once IMI approves, the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka issues the VDR [W0064]. Applications go through the High Commission's authorised visa application agencies, not to the High Commission directly [W0061, W0062]. For a Bangladesh passport the High Commission gives the visa fee as Tk 600 [W0063] and IMI's fee table as RM20 [V0017]; the agencies' own charges are at their discretion [Z0007]. **Need to know:** whether the RM105 processing charge on eVISA applications [V0016] also applies to worker visas. **Need to know:** the names and addresses of the authorised agencies in Dhaka and how long they take; the High Commission's agencies page lists only Tourism Malaysia's office [V0049].

### Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates

- **Passport:** apply for an e-passport at the passport office for your present address; over the age of 20 you need your NID [D0055, D0054]. A 48-page passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular, about 15 working days), Tk 6,325 (express, 7 working days) or Tk 8,625 (super express, 2 working days) for 5 years, or Tk 5,750, Tk 8,050 or Tk 10,350 for 10 years [D0049, D0050]. If it is lost or stolen, file a GD (general diary) at the nearest police station and re-apply with a copy of the old passport and the GD [D0057]. BOESL's DHL circular asked for a passport valid at least 18 to 24 months [W0003].
- **Need to know:** whether Malaysia requires a police clearance certificate for PLKS workers; it isn't in IMI's document list for the visa [W0055]. If the agency asks for one, apply yourself at [pcc.police.gov.bd](https://pcc.police.gov.bd/) with a passport valid at least 3 months; the present address you give must be one in your passport [B0105]. Upload a scan of your passport data page attested by a first-class gazetted officer, and your NID or birth certificate if the passport has no address [B0106]. The fee is Tk 1,500 on the portal (treasury challan or card), while Bangladesh Police's own guidance says Tk 500 [B0105, B0109]. Track it by SMS: type PCC S and your reference number and send it to 26969; the police verify you at your present address [B0107]. If you apply from abroad, someone else can collect it with an attested authorisation letter giving their name and NID number [B0108]. **Need to know:** how long it takes.
- **Certificates:** Bangladesh has been a party to the Apostille Convention (the Hague treaty under which one certificate, an apostille, authenticates a public document for use in the other member countries) since 30 Mar 2025; Malaysia is not a party [B0110]. **Need to know:** how Malaysia wants Bangladeshi certificates attested for work passes.

### Step 5: Check your visa and pass are real

- **The agency:** look it up on FWCMS's list and check its licence status on OEP (step 1) [W0008, X0001].
- **The job:** BMET's permission letter for your job, on [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) (step 1) [W0026].
- **The visa approval:** the IMI status check (step 3) [W0065].
- **At BMET:** BMET checks job visas and demand letters free within 3 working days; bring the visa and a copy of the passport page with the visa stamp [B0032, B0033]. Its online visa check covers only Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and the UAE, not Malaysia [B0032]. You can also take them to your district employment and manpower office [B0005].
- **After arrival:** IMI's [ePass check](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid?semakan) shows your name, nationality, pass type and expiry when you enter the ePass number (the number of your electronic pass record, for example EP141011C23105313148), passport number and nationality; "Pas Tidak Sah" means the pass is not valid [W0066]. The page says it is meant for authorities and agencies [W0066]. **Need to know:** where a worker finds the ePass number.

### Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight

1. **Attend the compulsory 3-day pre-departure orientation** at one of BMET's 70 training institutes; enrol on OEP [B0036, B0012].
2. **BMET clearance:** the agency applies after the visa; BMET's emigration branch checks the papers and gives clearance within 3 working days [B0026]. For Malaysia the agency must also submit the demand letter, power of attorney and skill certificates, and choose workers only from BMET's database [W0030]. If you got your visa yourself, apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district office with an application, the visa copy, the contract if any, an attested copy of your OEP registration card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and a pay order for the welfare fee and insurance [B0029]. Fees: a fee of Tk 5,500 for the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the Bangladesh government's welfare board for migrant workers) from 1 Oct 2026 (the Ministry's circular raised it from Tk 3,500, which BMET's charter still shows), a one-time insurance premium of Tk 1,000, a clearance or smart-card fee of Tk 250 (news reported in Dec 2024 that the smart-card fee was abolished, but BMET's Sep 2026 charter still lists it; unofficial, not verified) [D0059], and source income tax of Tk 250 to Tk 400 for single visas or Tk 800 to Tk 1,200 for group visas [B0043, B0028, B0030, B0008]. Pay only through the proper channel and against a receipt [B0042].
3. **At the airport,** the expatriate welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet check your BMET smart card [B0038]; in 2023-24 they stopped 1,098 people with no or fake clearance [B0128].
4. **The employer pays your ticket both ways** [W0036, L0141]. An agency representative must travel with each flight or meet workers at the Malaysian airport [W0031].
5. **Before you fly,** leave one set of copies of all your papers at home and carry 3 sets; open a bank account in your name and one in a family member's [B0004]. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].

### Step 7: After arrival

1. **Register the MDAC before you fly.** All foreign nationals must register the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days before arriving (see Tourist visa, Step 4, for how) [V0058]; permanent residents and long-term pass holders are exempt [V0060].
2. **Wait for your employer at the airport.** You may enter only through official entry points with your VDR and visa, and the employer must collect you within 6 hours [W0046, L0041], then take you to the hostel [L0034]. IMI's English page gives both 6 hours and, for a later phase, 24 hours [M0225, M0224]. The employer pays your transport from the airport to the workplace [M0147].
3. **FOMEMA medical within 30 days.** FOMEMA (FOMEMA Sdn. Bhd.) is the company that appoints the panel clinics for foreign workers' medical checks in Malaysia [Z0008]; the employer has 30 days to take you to a FOMEMA-registered clinic [L0042]. The PLKS is issued only if you're certified fit; if not, you're sent home with a Check-Out Memo (Memo Periksa Keluar, the employer's application for a foreign worker to leave for home) [L0043, W0047, L0071]. The Ministry of Human Resources says the employer pays for it [M0144]. FOMEMA itself urges employers to register workers for the examination within 72 hours of arrival [Z0128]; an IMI briefing hosted by an industry association says within 3 days (unofficial, not verified) [W0069]; the 30 days on IMI's own page is the official deadline [L0042, M0144]. The fee is RM207 for men and RM217 for women, plus 8% SST (sales and service tax); the women's fee is higher because it includes a pregnancy test [Z0125, Z0126]. A payment is valid 30 days; if the examination isn't done in time, the employer must register and pay again [Z0127]. The examination includes a mental health assessment, a doctor's check (blood pressure, height and weight, vision), a chest X-ray, urine tests including a drug test for opiates, methamphetamine and cannabis, and blood tests including infectious disease screening [Z0135]. Results usually come within 7 working days [Z0129]. If you are found unfit, your employer or agency can appeal within 2 weeks of the result on portal.fomema.my, and the case is closed if the extra test results aren't sent within 4 weeks [Z0131, Z0132]; confirmed tuberculosis, malaria, pregnancy, syphilis, HIV and hepatitis B can't be appealed [Z0133, Z0134]. **Need to know:** how a worker sees their own result; 2025 news reports say neither workers nor employers see the report (unofficial, not verified) [D0028].
4. **PLKS sticker and i-Kad.** Every PLKS holder gets an i-Kad, a foreign-worker identity card, free and without applying: under IMI's Feb 2026 poster the employer collects it from the Immigration office 7 days after IMI's email [L0126, L0127, W0053]; IMI's undated English Foreign Worker page instead says an authorised vendor sends it to the employer [Z0092]. IMI's Malay page says the card is grey for all sectors, while its English page says the colour depends on the sector [Z0093, Z0092]. Carry it at all times and show it when asked [L0128].
5. **Get your passport back.** Under the 2021 contract the employer may not keep your passport; you hand it over only for the medical, the PLKS, the i-Kad and renewal, and it must be returned [L0149, L0151]. IMI tells foreigners to carry their passport at all times [L0085]. The Ministry of Human Resources says employers must not keep a foreign worker's passport [M0143]. Under the Passports Act, holding someone else's passport without lawful authority is an offence for anyone, with a fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [K0045, K0046]. The US government's 2026 trafficking report says employers keeping passports is still widespread (unofficial, not verified) [F0084].
6. **Wages into your own bank account.** Wages must be paid monthly, by the 7th of the next month, into a bank account in your name [L0131, L0132]; Malaysian law requires wages to be paid into a bank account unless the worker asks in writing for cash and the Labour Director General approves [L0104, L0105]. The High Commission expects the employer to open the account [L0034]. At Maybank, for example, a foreigner opens a savings account at a branch with a passport, may be asked for the work permit, visa and an employer's letter, and needs at least RM250; the account can't be opened online in its app, because that needs a MyKad, the Malaysian identity card [F0011, F0012, F0014, F0015]. In practice some employers deduct the levy from workers' wages, though the employer must pay it (unofficial, not verified) [F0082, W0054].
7. **SIM card.** Every prepaid SIM must be registered in person at a mobile operator's service centre or authorised dealer, with your original passport and your work-permit document; copies aren't accepted and nobody can register for you [F0001, F0002, F0003, F0004, F0013]. You can have at most 2 prepaid SIMs with each operator, and you hear within 12 hours that the SIM is active [F0005, F0007]. Don't give your documents or codes to anyone offering to "help"; each operator offers a free check of all numbers registered in your name [F0009, F0010]. **Need to know:** whether the "work permit document" means the i-Kad.
8. **Social security and insurance.** Your employer must register you with PERKESO (SOCSO, Malaysia's social security organisation) for two compulsory schemes: the Employment Injury Scheme, for accidents, work diseases and death at work, and the Invalidity Scheme, which covers critical illness or death in Malaysia before age 60 from any cause, 24 hours a day [M0166, M0167, M0168]. The employer pays 1.25% of your wages for the first and 0.5% for the second, and you pay 0.5% [M0170, M0171]. You need a valid passport and work pass, and the employer registers you online; failing to register or pay is an offence for the employer [M0173, M0174]. Each registered worker gets a 12-digit Foreign Workers Social Security number [M0184]. SOCSO's customer line is 1-300-22-8000 [M0179]. Renewal of the PLKS also needs SPIKPA (Skim Perlindungan Insurans Kesihatan Pekerja Asing, the foreign workers' health insurance protection scheme), except in plantation [M0220, L0046]. The Ministry proposed in Feb 2025 that foreign workers and employers each pay 2% into the EPF (Employees Provident Fund, the retirement fund), with no start date given [M0163, M0164]. **Need to know:** what SPIKPA covers, and whether EPF contributions for foreign workers have started.
9. **If you came without BMET clearance,** join the Wage Earners' Welfare Board from Malaysia to get its insurance and help: apply online at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/); the High Commission's labour wing processes it, and the fee is the equivalent of Tk 6,500 in ringgit, with life insurance [B0057]. Check whether you already count as covered at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [B0058].

### Your rights at work

The Employment Act 1955, as amended in 2022, is the main labour law in Peninsular Malaysia [L0107]. The 2021 contract between Bangladeshi workers and Malaysian employers is in Malay, Bangla and English, and the English text prevails [L0153].

- **Hours:** at most 8 hours a day and 45 a week, with a break of at least 30 minutes after 5 hours, and never more than 12 hours a day except in emergencies [M0003, M0004, L0092]. Overtime only if you agree [L0134]; it is paid at least 1.5 times your hourly rate, which for a monthly-paid worker is the monthly wage divided by 26 and then by your normal daily hours [M0001, M0007].
- **Rest days and public holidays:** work beyond normal hours on a rest day is paid at least 2 times the hourly rate [M0013]. You get 11 paid public holidays a year [M0014]; if you work on one, you get 2 days' wages on top of the holiday pay, and overtime that day is paid at least 3 times the hourly rate [M0019, M0020].
- **Rest and leave:** one rest day a week [L0135]. Annual leave is 8 days a year for under 2 years' service, 12 days for 2 to 5 years and 16 days after 5 years [L0109]. Sick leave is 14 days a year under 2 years' service, 18 days for 2 to 5 years and 22 days after 5 years under the Employment Act when no hospital stay is needed (a government MyGOV page says 16 for 2 to 5 years), or up to 60 days a year in all when a doctor certifies that hospitalisation is needed; sick leave needs a doctor's certificate and must be reported to the employer within 48 hours [M0023, L0110, M0025]. Untaken annual leave is paid when the contract ends, unless you are dismissed for misconduct [M0022]. Women get 98 days' maternity leave and can't be dismissed while pregnant except for misconduct, wilful breach of contract or closure [L0112, L0094]; married men get 7 days' paternity leave after 12 months with the employer [L0095].
- **Pay:** wages are due within 7 days after the end of each wage period, and overtime and holiday pay by the end of the next one [M0029, M0030]. The basic wage must meet the Malaysian minimum wage and be the same as local workers get for the same job [L0130, M0153, M0152]. Under the Minimum Wages Order 2024 (P.U.(A) 376), the minimum wage is **RM1,700 a month**, or RM8.72 an hour; by the day it is RM65.38 for a 6-day week, RM78.46 for 5 days and RM98.08 for 4 days [Z0137, Z0136]. It has applied since 1 Feb 2025 to employers with five or more workers and since 1 Aug 2025 to all employers [Z0138, Z0139]. A worker paid only by piece rate, task, trip or commission must still get at least RM1,700 a month [Z0140]. The order doesn't cover domestic workers [Z0141]. In its Budget 2027 speech of 9 Oct 2026 the government said it had agreed to raise the minimum wage from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, with firms selling less than RM50 million a year exempted [R0002]. **Need to know:** when the RM2,000 rate becomes law; a company-services blog says it binds only once a new order is published in the Federal Gazette, and that Budget 2027 also proposed RM2,500 a month for semi-skilled workers and graduates (unofficial, not verified) [Z0142, Z0143]. The employer may deduct only what the Employment Act allows; deductions for housing rent, food or payments to others need your written request and the Labour Department's approval, and all deductions together may not exceed 50% of the month's wages [M0044, M0047, M0049, M0050, L0137]. In Sarawak no deduction may be made for recruitment or repatriation costs, and foreign workers must get the same rate as local workers [L0170, L0169].
- **Housing:** the employer gives housing with basic amenities, as Act 446 (the workers' housing law) requires [L0138, M0146]. The housing must have a Certificate for Accommodation from the Labour Department, men and women must be housed separately, and a person in charge must take a worker who is unwell to a clinic or hospital [M0193, M0201, M0203]. Rent may be deducted from wages only within limits set by regulations [M0197, M0198]. If your job ends with less than 4 weeks' notice, you may stay up to 4 weeks [M0205]. The Labour Department can inspect housing at any time without notice [M0210, M0212]. **Need to know:** the detailed standards (space, beds, toilets) and the maximum rent, which are set in regulations not yet found.
- **Forced labour is a crime:** an employer who threatens, deceives or forces you to work and stops you leaving the workplace can be fined up to RM100,000 or jailed up to 2 years [L0100, L0101].
- **Notice:** the 2021 contract says either side gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages [L0144]; where a contract says nothing, the Act requires 4, 6 or 8 weeks depending on length of service [M0052]. **Need to know:** the termination and lay-off benefit rates, which are set in regulations [M0056].
- **Pension:** PERKESO's Invalidity Scheme, which covers foreign workers for critical illness or death in Malaysia before age 60, pays an invalidity pension or grant and a survivors' pension (see Step 7 and If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job) [M0168, M0172]. Compulsory EPF (retirement fund) contributions for foreign workers were proposed in Feb 2025, with no start date given [M0163, M0164]. **Need to know:** whether a PLKS worker can get any retirement pension, in Malaysia or through a Bangladesh government scheme, and how.

### How long you can stay

- **12 months at a time.** The employer renews the PLKS from 3 months before it expires and pays any penalty if it is late; a renewal filed after expiry is treated as an overstay [L0044, L0139, L0045]. Only the employer can renew [L0079]; IMI's 2021 FAQ says Bangladeshi workers' renewals are done only at Immigration in Putrajaya [L0174]. Your passport must be valid for over 12 months [L0047]. IMI's pages ask for a FOMEMA medical for the 2nd and 3rd year renewals only [L0049, M0221], while FOMEMA's own page (Aug 2026) says that since December 2023 the examination is mandatory every year [Z0130]. **Need to know:** which rule IMI applies now.
- **In total:** up to 10 years (3 years for workers registered under the 6P programme, one of Malaysia's programmes for legalising undocumented foreign workers [Z0009]), according to IMI's foreign worker page and the Ministry of Human Resources [L0050, M0229, M0157, R0007]. IMI's PLKS page (also undated) says no more than 5 years [L0066].
- **No family, no marriage:** PLKS holders can't bring family to live in Malaysia and may not marry a Malaysian or another foreigner while on the PLKS [L0053, L0056, E0138, E0139].
- **Need to know:** an official statement on whether a PLKS worker can ever get permanent residence.

### What can get you jailed

- **Being without a valid pass:** illegal entry or staying without valid papers carries a fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, and up to 6 strokes of the cane; it can instead be settled with a compound (an out-of-court fine) of up to RM3,000 [L0083, L0084]. Overstaying is punished by a fine of not less than RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, according to the Immigration Act's 2006 text and IMI's English enforcement page, or it may be settled with a compound [K0015, R0004]; IMI's Malay page says a fine of up to RM10,000 and a compound of up to RM3,000 [L0086]. Once you break any condition of your pass, the pass stops being valid [K0025]; PLKS holders may not change employer or sector [L0055].
- **After arrest:** someone convicted of unlawful entry or of being a prohibited immigrant can be removed from Malaysia by order of Immigration's Director General [K0017], and anyone ordered to be removed can be held in custody for as long as it takes to arrange the removal [K0020]. People ordered deported are held in Immigration's 13 depots (11 in Peninsular Malaysia, 2 in Sarawak) [L0181]. Family who want to visit a detainee need a letter of permission from an Immigration officer first: for the Klang Valley (the Kuala Lumpur area) from the Depot and Deportation Management Division at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya, elsewhere from the state Immigration Enforcement Division [L0179, L0180]. There is no appeal against removal if your pass has expired or been cancelled [K0019].
- **False papers:** a forged visa or pass means at least RM30,000, 5 to 10 years' jail and up to 6 strokes [L0088]; a false passport, RM15,000 to RM100,000, 2 to 10 years and up to 6 strokes [L0089].
- **Drugs:** Malaysia's civil aviation authority says drug smuggling is punished by death [L0155]. The Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act 2023 (Act 846) amends section 39B of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952: in subsection (2) it lowers the minimum whipping from 15 to 12 strokes, and it deletes subsections (2A) and (2B) [K0041]. **Need to know:** the current text of section 39B(2), which we have not found. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].
- **Banned goods:** obscene pictures or films on any media, daggers and flick knives, and cloth printed with verses of the Quran may not be brought in; declare anything dutiable and expect your bags to be opened [L0158, L0159, L0160, L0156].

### If you aren't paid

1. **Complain on the Working for Workers (WFW) app**, the Ministry of Human Resources' complaint channel for all workers, local or foreign. It takes complaints about unpaid wages, unfair conditions and any breach of the Employment Act, at any time and without going to an office; it keeps your identity confidential and lets you follow the complaint [L0163, L0164, L0165, L0166]. Download it from Google Play or the Apple App Store [L0167].
2. **Contact the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing** in Kuala Lumpur: email counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, ss.lab.kl@gmail.com or fsl.bhc.kl@gmail.com, or call +601 2290 3252 [L0021, B0117]. The High Commission's officer page lists +603 2604 0934 as the Minister (Labour)'s phone [B0117], while its labour FAQ gives the same number as a fax [Z0014]. In a 2023 case the mission took workers' complaints to the Human Resources Ministry, which summoned the employer; the High Commission reportedly says more than 330 of about 450 complaints since recruitment restarted in 2022 were resolved, through mediation between employers and workers and with the help of Malaysian authorities, and that it recovered RM11.42 million in compensation and allowances for workplace accidents, disability and death on 2,857 claims in 2025 (unofficial, not verified) [L0193, F0022, F0023, F0025].
3. **For compensation after an injury,** file a case at the labour office with your payslips, contract and attendance record, and give the High Commission a copy [L0037].
4. **You can also apply to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare:** send its Welfare-1 branch (saswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030251) an application with what happened, proof of status (passport, work permit, smart card, foreign ID number), the employer's name and address and what you are owed; within 7 working days of receiving it, the ministry writes to the labour wing or the Wage Earners' Welfare Board asking them to act, and tells you by letter. It is free [B0048].
5. **File a claim at the Labour Court** (the Labour Department's tribunal for wage claims): it is free [M0232, M0093]. Go in person to the nearest Labour Office and give a sworn statement to an officer [M0112, M0087]. Bring your passport, the name and address of the workplace, your payslips if any, your appointment letter or contract and any termination letter [M0113]. The officer sets a mention date, summons the employer and tells you when the hearing is; bring your witnesses [M0114, M0089]. If the employer doesn't come, the claim can be decided without it [M0091]. Workers with the same claim against one employer can file together [M0092]. A dismissal for misconduct must be challenged within 60 days [M0083]. The order carries interest of up to 8% a year if unpaid, and if the employer doesn't obey, the Labour Department can enforce it through the courts [M0085, M0096, M0116]. Either side can appeal to the High Court within 14 days [M0115]. The employer may not stop you from going to the Labour Court [M0099]. Free advice: 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my [M0118]. **Need to know:** how long a case takes. Workers report employers' agents warning them not to complain (unofficial, not verified) [L0192].

### If you want to change employer

PLKS holders may not change employer or sector on their own [L0055, W0052], and the 2021 contract bars changing jobs [L0146]. A change is allowed only if the employer is declared bankrupt, has closed, has suffered a disaster and can't keep its workers, has been restructured or renamed, or has died [M0123, M0124]. The new employer must be in the same sector and allowed to hire foreign workers [M0126]. It isn't open to workers in their 10th year or later, to workers from 6P, the Labour Recalibration programme (RTK) or any other legalisation programme for undocumented workers, or to domestic helpers [M0127, Z0009], and your PLKS must have at least 3 months left [M0130]. The employers apply, not you: both register on the Labour Department's ePPAx system (eppax.gov.my, its online system for employers' applications to hire foreign workers), the Labour Department investigates and gives a support letter, and the old employer then submits the form, which is free, with the checklist documents to the Ministry of Human Resources' foreign workers division (BPPA) in Putrajaya [M0121, Z0010, M0128, M0132, M0134, M0142]. **Need to know:** how long it takes. In May 2025 the Home Minister was reported as saying employers may now apply to move workers across sectors (unofficial, not verified) [H0031, F0077]. This conflicts with IMI's rule that a PLKS worker may not change sector, which is the official rule [L0055].

### If your employer reports you absent (lari)

Leaving the workplace without telling the employer, with the intention of running away, or not coming back on the agreed date after a trip home, counts as running away (lari) [L0057, L0058]. The employer reports it to Immigration with a police report, the worker is blacklisted and the employer loses its deposit [L0059, L0060, L0061]; it must also tell the Labour Director General within 14 days [L0099, M0066]. **Need to know:** how a worker checks whether they have been reported, and how to challenge a false report.

### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **The employer applies for your Check-Out Memo** with the form, your original passport and a ticket home; you leave on the date and through the exit point on the memo [L0071, L0072, L0073]. Until it does, the employer stays responsible for you [L0074]. **Need to know:** whether employers must be present at the exit point to register the worker's departure; in May 2025 the Home Minister was reported as saying they must (unofficial, not verified) [H0029].
2. **The employer pays your ticket home** at the end of the contract, if it ends the contract early (except for your own breach), if you leave because it broke the contract, and after serious work injury or illness [L0141, L0142, M0149]. If you resign or are dismissed for misconduct, you pay [L0143, M0150].
3. **Collect everything owed:** if you are sent home early, the employer must pay all outstanding wages [L0152].
4. **To leave Malaysia** you need a passport valid at least 6 months and a valid pass [L0186].

### Going home on leave

Not coming back on the agreed date counts as running away [L0058, M0214]. **Need to know:** the PLKS re-entry rules. News reports say that from 8 Aug 2025 Bangladeshi workers get a multiple-entry visa when their PLKS is renewed, without a separate application (unofficial, not verified) [H0027, H0028].

### Bringing your family

PLKS workers can't bring family to live in Malaysia [L0053, E0138]. See Family passes for the routes open to professionals, and Having a child in Malaysia for registering a birth [S0114]. A pregnant worker can't be dismissed except for misconduct, wilful breach of contract or closure (see Your rights at work) [L0094]. FOMEMA's examination of women workers includes a pregnancy test, and a confirmed pregnancy is one of the results that can't be appealed [Z0126, Z0134]; a worker found unfit is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [L0043]. **Need to know:** what happens to a worker who becomes pregnant after the PLKS has been issued.

### Scams

Done to workers:

- **Paying before the government opens recruitment.** BMET warned in 2021 not to pay anyone before recruitment is announced [W0027], and in 2025 BOESL warned that people claiming the market had opened might be collecting money [W0020].
- **False approvals.** A Ministry warning notice (undated on its face) said that, despite social-media news that Malaysia had approved 25 Bangladeshi agencies, Malaysia had not informed the High Commission or the Ministry of any approval, and asked people not to be misled [W0021].
- **Fraud in BOESL's name.** BOESL has no agents and takes no money before final selection, and then only by bank pay order [W0068].
- **Fake work passes in Malaysia.** In Sep 2026 Malaysia's Home Minister reportedly said 27 Bangladeshis were arrested in Kuala Lumpur raids: 11 of them are accused of running a ring that sold foreign workers fake e-PLKS copies for RM150 and fake police reports for RM70, and the other 16 were held for immigration offences (unofficial, not verified) [D0045]. The e-PLKS is the electronic PLKS, which employers have applied for through FWCMS since 1 Feb 2025 [Z0002]. Check your pass yourself (step 5).
- **No job on arrival.** Workers have arrived to find no job and no work permit and have not got their passports back (unofficial, not verified) [L0190]. The High Commission makes Malaysian employers sign a commitment that workers will not sit idle [W0059].

Tried by applicants, and punishable: forged visas or passes and false passports (see What can get you jailed) [L0088, L0089]. Bangladeshi workers who had failed the FOMEMA medical have reportedly bought forged Bangladeshi passports to sit it again; in Feb 2026 Immigration reportedly broke the ring, seized 22 forged passports and detained a Bangladeshi suspect who held a student pass (unofficial, not verified) [D0035, N0035]. **Need to know:** any official Malaysian or Bangladeshi warning about fake ages or documents for Malaysia.

## Work visa: domestic worker

Not open to Bangladeshis. IMI's domestic helper page lists the approved source countries as Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Laos and Nepal [L0194]; its PLKS page lists the same without Nepal [L0076]. Bangladesh is on neither list [L0076, L0194].

## Student visa (Student Pass)

A Student Pass lets you stay in Malaysia to study full time at an approved institution [S0023, S0042]. Applications go through EMGS (Education Malaysia Global Services, a company under the Ministry of Higher Education), for both public universities and private institutions [Z0013, S0002, S0060].

### Step 1: Check the institution and the course

1. **The institution must be approved.** You need an offer from a public university, or from a private higher education institution approved by the Ministry of Higher Education (KPT) whose programme is accredited by MQA (the Malaysian Qualifications Agency) and which has the Home Ministry's permission to take international students; language and training centres need their own ministry approval and the Home Ministry's permission [S0001]. **Need to know:** an official public list of private institutions allowed to take international students, and of those whose permission was withdrawn.
2. **Check the course is accredited** on the [Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR)](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr/): type the course name in "Name of Qualification", choose the level and press Search. Only accredited programmes appear, for the campus named; "no record" can mean the course has only provisional accreditation, was never accredited or failed [S0084, S0085, S0086, S0087].
3. **Check you qualify:** 12 years of schooling and a recognised exam, and an English test certificate: MUET (Malaysian University English Test) or an international English test (IELTS, TOEFL iBT or TOEFL Essentials, PTE Academic, Cambridge English, Linguaskill, OET or ELS CIEP); without one the pass won't cover the whole course [S0043, S0050].

### Step 2: Apply from Bangladesh

1. **Get and accept an offer letter** from the institution [S0024, S0047].
2. **Apply online from outside Malaysia,** yourself on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/) or the EMGS HUB app, or through the institution [S0002, S0024]. Upload a 35x45 mm photo with a white background, your passport's data, visa and observation pages (valid at least 18 months), the offer letter, the Health Declaration Form, your certificates and transcripts and your English certificate [S0045, S0046, S0047, S0048, S0050].
3. **Pay.** EMGS's fee calculator gives RM1,728 for a new application, RM159 for the eVAL (electronic Visa Approval Letter, Immigration's approval to enter as a student), RM340 for the medical (RM350 in Sarawak; the calculator also lists an RM250 rate and public-university clinics), RM60 for the pass and RM50 for the i-Kad [S0060, S0061, S0062, S0063, S0027]; since 1 Mar 2024 an 8% service tax is added to the processing fees [Z0094]. Public-university students may instead do the medical at their university [Z0095]. Health insurance bought through EMGS costs RM498 to RM644 depending on the plan, or you can use a policy arranged through your institution; the price varies with the length of the pass [S0064]. IMI's fee table gives RM20 for a Bangladeshi's single- or multiple-entry visa [V0017, S0065]. Lodge a personal bond of RM750 (the Bangladesh rate) with the institution; you get it back when you finish or withdraw without breaking immigration rules [S0059, S0049]. First-year processing and eVAL fees aren't refunded once invoiced [S0077, S0078].
4. **If EMGS returns your application asking for more documents,** send them within 14 working days of the date it was returned [S0020]. **Need to know:** what happens if they arrive late. EMGS checks your academic papers, then IMI's Student Pass unit checks your passport and security and issues the eVAL [S0003]. EMGS's service charter is 14 working days for a complete application [S0026].

### Step 3: eVAL and visa

The eVAL is valid 6 months, and you may not enter Malaysia before it is issued [S0027, S0028]. Bangladeshis then apply for a Single Entry Visa on [eVISA](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/), which can't be done from inside Malaysia [S0004, S0029, S0053, V0007].

### Step 4: How to check your application is real

On [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/), open Student Visa, then Track My Application, enter your passport number and nationality, tick the terms box and press Track My Application [S0080]. IMI's own status page sends Student Pass checks to EMGS [S0081]. **Need to know:** how to check that an eVAL document itself is genuine.

### Step 5: Arrival and the pass

1. **Before you fly,** register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) online within 3 days before arriving, as all foreign nationals must (see Tourist visa, Step 4) [V0058]; permanent residents and long-term pass holders are exempt [V0060]. **At the airport** use EMGS's 24-hour International Student Arrival Centre (ISAC) counter: at Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) Terminal 1 on Level 3 of the arrival hall before immigration, and at Terminal 2 on Level 3A after the Skybridge [S0082, S0083].
2. **Within 7 working days do the medical** at an EMGS panel clinic; if you fail, you can appeal at the same clinic within 3 days [S0055, S0056, S0030].
3. **The institution gets your Student Pass sticker** with your passport, its letter, EMGS's support letter, your stamped personal bond and 12 months' Malaysian insurance [S0017].

### Keeping the pass

The pass usually lasts up to a year at a time; renew it before it expires, ideally 3 months early [S0032, S0033]. Renewal needs a CGPA (cumulative grade point average) of at least 2.0 and 80% attendance, or else an explanation letter from the institution [S0051]. Breaking any condition can get the pass cancelled [S0016]. IMI's client charter gives 5 working days for its Student Pass step [C0047]. **Need to know:** whether a refused Student Pass application can be appealed. To change course, your institution may submit a Variation application once, within 18 months after your first semester, if you have finished at least one semester and study at bachelor's level or above; otherwise the institution shortens your pass with a Check-Out Memo and you must leave Malaysia before applying again [S0034, S0035, S0036].

### Can students work?

Only students at universities and private higher education institutions, not language or training centres [S0012]. Only during semester breaks or holidays of more than 7 days, at most 20 hours a week, in restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels or on campus, never as a cashier, at a front desk or as a recruiter of students [S0013, S0014, S0039, S0040]. A 2025 news guide told Bangladeshi students they could also work on weekends, which conflicts with the official rule above (unofficial, not verified) [N0053, S0039]. You must first get Immigration's approval: go in person with a representative of your institution, with the job offer, the institution's letter giving the break dates, your results and attendance, your passport and pass, and the employer's Labour Department approval [S0041, S0015].

### Family

IMI allows dependants (spouse, children under 18, disabled children of any age, parents and parents-in-law) only for master's and PhD students [S0008, S0009, S0010]; EMGS also allows bachelor's students from a list of 17 countries, which does not include Bangladesh [S0038]. Either way a Bangladeshi bachelor's student can't bring family, and dependants may not work [S0008, S0038, S0011]. A master's or PhD student's dependants apply through EMGS's online STARS system [S0021]; EMGS charges RM250 per dependant (RM140 to renew), and each dependant buys their own insurance [S0067]. IMI's Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP) page also lists a category for close family of a foreign student [C0019]; **Need to know:** whether it is open to the family of a bachelor's student. For a child born in Malaysia, see Having a child in Malaysia [S0114].

### School pupils

IMI also gives Student Passes to foreign children at pre-school and school [C0024]. The child needs a place at a government or government-aided school under the Ministry of Education (KPM), or at an international, expatriate, private or religious school, or a Chinese independent secondary school, registered with the Ministry's Private Education Division; an international school also needs the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission to take foreign pupils [Z0110, Z0111]. The school's appointed representative, a parent or a legal guardian applies, for a new pass or a renewal, at IMI's Student Pass Unit at Immigration headquarters, the state Visa, Pass and Permit Division or a branch Immigration office offering Student Passes, according to the school's address [Z0112, Z0113]. Bring Forms IMM.14 and IMM.38 [Z0114], for a new pass a copy of every page of the child's passport, including the cover, or for a renewal a copy of the bio-data page and the current pass, the passport valid at least 18 months [Z0115, Z0124], a passport photo (3.5 x 5.0 cm, white background) [Z0116], health insurance covering at least 12 months [Z0118], a stamped personal bond for a new application [Z0117], and, for a government or government-aided school, the approval letter of the state or district education office [Z0119]; bring all the originals for checking [Z0120]. The child's parents, legal guardian or adoptive parents, and siblings under 7, may be considered for a Long-Term Social Visit Pass as the pupil's companions, but may not work or run a business [Z0121, Z0122, Z0123]. IMI's fees are RM60 for the Student Pass and RM90 for a companion's Long-Term Social Visit Pass, plus the visa fee for the nationality, and IMI's client charter gives 5 working days for a Student Pass [S0007, C0047]. PLKS workers can't bring family to live in Malaysia [E0138]. **Need to know:** the full checklist for each type of school (the IMI page lists more items for international and private schools), and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Scams

A student visa is not a work visa (see Can students work?) [S0013]. Tried by applicants, and punishable: getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement, or using someone else's pass, is an offence with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030], and breaking any condition can get the Student Pass cancelled [S0016]. Agents have reportedly charged Bangladeshi students Tk 6 to 8 lakh and told them a student visa lets them work freely (unofficial, not verified) [S0134, S0135]. **Need to know:** any official warning from KPT, EMGS, IMI or the Bangladesh High Commission about fake colleges or student-visa agents. A 2026 news report says KPT has shut down colleges and tightened scrutiny of Bangladeshi applications after a surge in fake-university enrolments, and in 2017 Bangladeshi students reported paying agents over RM20,000 for admission to colleges with no real classes (unofficial, not verified) [S0137, S0140, S0138].

## Tourist visa (social visit)

Bangladeshis must get an eVISA to visit Malaysia for tourism, family visits or short business trips, and may stay up to 30 days [V0001, V0003]. From 1 Sep 2026 ordinary tourists get a single-entry visa, according to the MyVISA FAQ [V0009]; MyVISA's terms and conditions still say a social-visit multiple-entry visa is open to all nationalities except Israel and North Korea [Z0016], while IMI's undated Types of Visas page offers a social-visit multiple-entry visa only to citizens of India and China [Z0068]. A Sep 2026 news report says Bangladeshi tourists will generally get single-entry visas instead of one-year multiple-entry visas (unofficial, not verified) [V0108]. **Need to know:** whether a Bangladeshi tourist can still get a multiple-entry visa.

### Step 1: Prepare

1. **Passport:** valid more than 6 months from your travel date, with at least 3 blank pages [V0026, V0032].
2. **Documents, as files:** a studio photo (plain white background, 35 x 50 mm, taken in the last 6 months; no selfies or scans) [V0033, V0034]; your passport's data page and the page with your previous passport number; a return ticket; proof of paid accommodation; and proof of money [V0026]. MyVISA (malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, IMI's eVISA website) asks for bank statements for the last 3 months; the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka asks for 6 months' statements with a solvency letter [V0005, V0027, V0028]. If you'll stay with friends or relatives, upload a cover letter to the immigration officer giving your host's name, Malaysian ID card number, phone number and address [V0029]. Merge several documents into one file for each upload field [V0031].

### Step 2: Apply on MyVISA

1. **Use only [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/)**; applications through third parties or agents can't be verified, and you can't apply from inside Malaysia [V0005, V0006, V0007]. The portal is sometimes down for maintenance: a Malaysian mission announced a pause and the restoration of service in July 2026 [N0005, N0006]. On 10 Feb 2026 the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka announced that the MyVISA website would be unavailable "until further notice" for maintenance (unofficial, not verified) [N0001]. **Need to know:** when the service came back.
2. **Open an account:** Individual, then Sign Up; fill in the form and the CAPTCHA; a temporary password arrives by email; change it when you first log in [V0021]. Log in with your email, a 6-digit code and your password [V0022]. Apply yourself; one account can hold 5 applications at a time, for at most 5 people [V0023]. **Need to know:** whether a spouse or child travelling with you is applied for from your account or must apply separately.
3. **Submit on a weekday between 9 am and 5 pm Bangladesh time:** the portal accepts applications from Bangladesh only then [V0025]. Write your name exactly as in your passport [V0035]. Apply at least 14 days ahead; the High Commission in Dhaka says 30 days [V0013, V0014]. Unpaid drafts expire after 7 working days [V0024].
4. **Pay online:** the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, a RM105 processing charge and a 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) charge; nothing is refunded, even if you're refused [V0016, V0017, V0019].
5. **Watch the status.** If asked for more documents or an interview, respond within 5 days or the application is rejected automatically [V0040, V0041]. A decision takes 2 to 7 working days [V0012].
6. **Download and print the eVISA** from your account (Actions, then Download eVISA) [V0037]. The single-entry visa for a visit is valid for one journey within 6 months of issue; 3 months applies only to foreign workers, cruise and wedding visas [R0005, V0038]. The High Commission in Dhaka still says 3 months [V0039], and so does IMI's own undated "Types of Visas" page [C0002]; the MyVISA terms are the only one of the three that is dated (last updated 6 Oct 2026) [R0005]. If your travel date changes within the 6 months, you don't need to apply again [R0006].
7. **If refused,** no reasons need be given and no appeal is published; you can apply again and pay again [V0042, V0043, V0019].

### Step 3: Check your eVISA is real

Enter your passport number and the visa sticker number at [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa) [V0067]. **Need to know:** what a genuine eVISA looks like (for example, whether it has a QR code).

### Step 4: Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)

Every foreign visitor must register the MDAC within 3 days before arriving, at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main) [V0058, V0059, V0060]. It asks for your name, passport number, date and place of birth, nationality, sex, passport expiry, email, mobile number, arrival and departure dates, flight number, mode of travel and last port of embarkation [V0061]. Check it with your passport number, nationality and PIN under "Check Registration" [V0062]. IMI warns that malaysiacard.com is a fake site, which charged USD 29.99 [V0065]. **Need to know:** whether the MDAC is free; a 2026 news report says it is, and that sponsored search ads lead to fake MDAC sites charging about RM145 (unofficial, not verified) [N0057].

### Step 5: At the border

The immigration officer checks your passport, your eVISA, your MDAC, a confirmed return ticket and proof of paid accommodation [V0050, V0059]. A visa doesn't guarantee entry: you can be refused if you can't explain your plans, and you must prove you are not a "prohibited immigrant", which includes anyone who can't show means of support [V0051, V0052, V0053, V0054]. Autogates aren't open to Bangladeshis, according to an undated IMI infographic that lists 10 eligible countries [V0057]. In 2025 Malaysia's border agency turned back Bangladeshi visitors for fake hotel bookings, no return tickets and too little money (unofficial, not verified) [V0109, V0110]. Refused travellers are reportedly sent back within 24 hours, and in 2023 the Home Minister said the airline must fly them back (unofficial, not verified) [N0010, N0015]. Under the Immigration Act, the right to appeal to the Minister against being refused entry as a prohibited immigrant is given to a person holding a Permit [V0055, K0011]. **Need to know:** whether a visitor refused entry has any appeal, any official minimum amount of money, and what a "Not to Land" order (the order under which a refused traveller is sent back by the airline [N0015]) means for future visits.

### Scams

The Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur has reportedly warned that there is no agreement for recruiting Bangladeshi women, that fraudsters bring women on tourist visas, and that a ring was collecting passports and cash for jobs in Sabah (unofficial, not verified) [N0028, N0029]. A tourist visa never allows work (see Don't work) [V0046]. Tried by applicants, and punishable: getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030].

### Don't work

A visit pass doesn't allow work [V0046, V0104]. A pass stops being valid once its conditions are broken [V0100], and overstaying brings fines and possible bans on entering again [V0047].

## Family visit sponsored by a Malaysian citizen (VDR)

A Malaysian citizen can sponsor a relative's visit through a VDR (visa with reference): the sponsor applies while you are still in Bangladesh, and after IMI's headquarters approves, a Malaysian mission abroad issues the visa [C0008, V0090]. VDR is open to every nationality that needs a visa, including Bangladesh [V0089].

1. **The sponsor applies in person** (the sponsor must attend) with Form Imm.12 (IMI's application form, linked for download on its VDR page) in 2 copies with your photo attached [Z0058], a copy of their identity card and a copy of every printed page of your passport [Z0025]; a personal bond stamped RM10 at the tax office (LHDN) and the sponsor's sworn declaration [Z0026]; proof of the sponsor's income, such as the last 3 months' payslips showing RM2,000 or more, or a business licence and 3 months' bank statements, with the employer's confirmation letter, a cover letter and the marriage certificate where relevant [Z0027, V0090]; and documents proving how you are related [Z0028]. **Need to know:** which Immigration office takes a social VDR application.
2. **Processing:** IMI's VDR page says 7 working days for a complete application, while its client charter says 5 working days [V0090, C0044].
3. **Then apply for the eVISA** in MyVISA's visa-with-reference category with the approval [C0006, Z0096]. IMI's visa fee for a Bangladeshi is RM20 [V0017]. **Need to know:** whether the RM105 eVISA processing charge [V0016] and a security bond (IMI's rate for Bangladeshis is RM750 [V0112]) apply to a social VDR, whether a refusal can be appealed, and whether Bangladesh is on MyVISA's list of countries exempted from this eVISA (our saved copy of that list could not be confirmed as the page's own text).
4. **During the visit:** a social visit pass doesn't allow any work [V0104]. Getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030]. **Need to know:** any official warning about scams in family-visit sponsorship.

## Business visit

A social visit pass covers meetings, conferences, business talks, factory inspections, audits, signing agreements, investment surveys and seminars [V0068]. Apply on MyVISA as for a tourist, with your company's letter or an invitation from a Malaysian government body or government-linked company, and a return ticket [V0070]. From 1 Sep 2026 multiple-entry visas are limited to eligible nationalities (31 in the MyVISA FAQ and announcement, 30 in IMI's notice) for Fly & Cruise, business, medical and wedding-tourism trips [C0007, V0009, V0011, V0010]. None of these notices names the eligible nationalities; the same FAQ separately lists 31 nationalities, Bangladesh among them, for which an eVISA is compulsory, without saying whether this is the multiple-entry list [C0007, V0001, V0010]. **Need to know:** whether Bangladesh is one of the eligible nationalities. For those eligible, a business multiple-entry visa is valid 6 months with up to 30 days per entry [V0071]; older official pages still describe 3 to 12 months [V0072, V0114]. The costs are the eVISA's: RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, RM105 processing and a card or e-wallet charge [V0017, V0016]; a decision takes 2 to 7 working days, and a refusal comes without reasons and with no published appeal [V0012, V0042]. Fly & Cruise visas need flight and cruise bookings and the sailing schedule, and Wedding Tourism visas a return ticket, proof of journey or itinerary; both are valid 3 months [Z0030, Z0031, V0071]. A social visit pass doesn't allow professional work; that needs a work pass such as the Professional Visit Pass [V0094, V0091]. Getting a pass by a false statement is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030]. A spouse who comes along needs a visa of their own, as every Bangladeshi does [V0001]; one MyVISA account can hold applications for up to 5 people [V0023] (see Tourist visa, Step 2). **Need to know:** any official warning about fake business invitations or business-visa agents.

## Medical visit

1. **Get an appointment letter** from a hospital that is a member of MHTC (Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council); the hospital gives an MHTC support letter [V0073, V0076].
2. **Apply for the medical eVISA on MyVISA at least 14 working days before the appointment,** with your passport, the letters, a return ticket, accommodation and a photo; MHTC says it takes 3 to 5 working days [V0073, V0076]. The High Commission in Dhaka says Bangladeshis apply through MHTC's authorised partners [V0075].
3. **Pay** the eVISA charges: the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, RM105 processing and a 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) charge, none of it refundable [V0016, V0017, V0019]. If refused, no reasons need be given and no appeal is published [V0042].
4. **Up to 2 companions** may come: a spouse, parent, child, grandparent, sibling, nurse or domestic helper [V0074]. MHTC's optional Meet and Greet service, which your hospital requests at least 3 working days ahead, costs RM189 per companion [V0077]. At entry show your passport, eVISA, the hospital appointment letter and your companions' details [V0074].
5. **No work:** a visa holder may not take unauthorised employment in Malaysia [V0046].
6. **Scams:** getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement, or using someone else's pass, is an offence with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030]. **Need to know:** any official warning about medical-visa scams, and MHTC's current authorised partners in Bangladesh; in 2022 MHTC named GD Assist Limited [V0078], and a 2026 news report names Irving Aviation Limited (unofficial, not verified) [N0042].
7. **Longer treatment:** for treatment at a government or specialist hospital, IMI gives a Long-Term Social Visit Pass with a single companion (see Family passes, Other relatives) [C0018].

## Professional Visit Pass

The Professional Visit Pass (PVP) is for a professional sent by an overseas company to work on a project or train with a Malaysian company for up to 12 months; it isn't for general workers or operators [V0091, V0093]. Only a registered Malaysian sponsor (a company, organisation or institution) can apply, mainly through its account with ESD (the Expatriate Services Division of IMI) or through another approving agency's system; you stay in Bangladesh until it is approved [V0092, V0094]. As for an Employment Pass, the company first needs the Labour Department's approval to employ a foreigner (under section 60K of the Employment Act) and an acknowledgement that it advertised the job on MYFutureJobs, PERKESO's job portal [E0050]; for a training programme, the organiser applies for the pass before you travel [V0097]. The fees are RM1,200 (plus tax) and RM90 for each quarter [V0095]. The security bond for a Bangladeshi is RM750, and for a Professional Visit Pass it goes with a bank guarantee addressed to the Director General of Immigration [V0112, Z0017]. After approval you get a VDR and enter Malaysia [V0096]. You can work only for the named company, and family can't come on a dependant pass [V0091]. IMI's bond table has a rate for Bangladeshis [V0112]; **Need to know:** whether any nationality rule applies to Bangladeshis. IMI's client charter gives, for complete, normal applications, 5 working days for a fast-track PVP-Expert (Pas Lawatan Ikhtisas-Pakar, for technical experts), 5 for foreign artistes and 25 for missionaries [C0048, C0023, C0047]; in Sarawak it gives 30 working days for a manual PVP application [Z0079]. **Need to know:** how long a standard PVP takes through ESD. If refused, only the company can appeal, within 14 days in ESD Online; the appeal is decided within 30 working days [E0063]. False information in an application can lead to rejection, cancellation of the approval, withdrawal of the pass, immigration action and blacklisting [E0070]. **Need to know:** any official warning about a "PVP" sold to general workers as a work visa. IMI also handles PVPs outside ESD for jockeys, volunteers and students on exchange, mobility or industrial-training programmes (at most 6 months for students); foreign artistes and film crews apply through PUSPAL, the Communications Ministry's centre for foreign filming and performances [C0021, C0022].

## Transit

If you stay inside the airport and continue on the same flight, you need no transit visa [V0080]. The Malaysian High Commission in New Delhi tells Indian travellers that no visa is needed in transit if all flights are on a single booking (PNR) and you stay in the transit hall, but that with separate bookings you need a visa if you must go through customs and immigration to check in or collect luggage, change from KLIA to KLIA2 (Kuala Lumpur International Airport 2) [Z0060], or leave the airport; changing terminals without passing immigration is fine [V0081]. **Need to know:** whether the same applies to Bangladeshis. For a short layover without an overnight stay, the MyVISA FAQ says to give the airport as your address and upload a cover letter explaining the layover [V0079]. Malaysia's 120-hour transit without visa, which was for citizens of Indian subcontinent countries, is suspended [V0082]. IMI's fee for a Bangladeshi's transit visa is RM20 [V0017], and an eVISA decision takes 2 to 7 working days [V0012]; a refusal comes without reasons and with no published appeal [V0042].

**Ship crew:** a seaman signing on to a ship at a Malaysian port needs a passport valid at least 6 months from entry, a Seaman Book, a Letter of Guarantee issued by IMI at the port, and, since Bangladeshis need a visa, a transit visa [C0032, C0033, V0002].

## Employment Pass

For expatriates hired by a Malaysian company at a basic salary of at least RM5,000 a month who meet ESD's qualification rule (see Step 1) [E0011, E0022]. It is valid for one employer, and only the employer can apply, never the worker [E0002, E0006]. Employment Passes in some sectors and regions stay with other approving authorities: manufacturing (the Malaysian Investment Development Authority), companies with MSC Malaysia status and ICT (information and communications technology) (the Multimedia Development Corporation; **Need to know:** what MSC Malaysia status is, which ESD's page doesn't explain), banking, finance and insurance (Bank Negara Malaysia, the central bank), the capital market (the Securities Commission), government contract posts (the Public Services Department), and jobs in the regions of the East Coast Economic Region Development Council (ECERDC) and the Iskandar Regional Development Authority (IRDA), whose region is Iskandar Malaysia [E0007, C0013, Z0063, Z0090]. ESD's visa-charge table for these passes lists a rate for Bangladesh [E0045]; **Need to know:** whether any nationality restriction applies to Bangladeshis.

### Step 1: Check you qualify

From 1 June 2026, counting basic salary only: Category I is RM20,000 a month or more and Category II RM10,000 to RM19,999, each for up to 10 years; Category III is RM5,000 to RM9,999 (RM7,000 to RM9,999 in manufacturing) for up to 5 years with the same employer [E0008, E0009, E0010, E0011, E0018]. You need a degree and 3 years' relevant experience, a diploma and 5 years, or a technical certificate and 7 years [E0022].

### Step 2: The employer applies

1. **The employer registers with ESD** and must get the Labour Department's approval to employ a foreigner (under section 60K of the Employment Act) and an acknowledgement that it advertised the job on MYFutureJobs (the job portal of PERKESO, Malaysia's social security organisation), both on Xpats Gateway (the online platform between employers and government agencies for Employment Pass and Professional Visit Pass applications) [E0016, E0050, E0051, Z0012, Z0011]. ESD's Aug 2026 salary booklet gives 10 working days for this step, while the Labour Department's 2023 FAQ for ePPAx (its online prior-approval system) says it decides within 14 working days [Z0086, Z0085]. **Need to know:** which time applies now. Hiring without that approval carries a fine of up to RM100,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [E0052].
2. **Send the employer** a colour scan of every page of your passport (valid more than 12 months), a photo, attested certificates and the signed contract [E0025].
3. **The employer applies on ESD Online and pays RM2,160;** the decision takes 2 to 5 working days [E0040, E0016, E0054]. If refused, only the employer can appeal, within 14 days [E0063]; if it doesn't appeal, it can request a refund of 75% of the processing fee within 14 days of the rejection [E0048, Z0039].
4. **You enter on a VDR** that the employer applies for with the Employment Pass, then have the pass endorsed at the satellite centre at KLIA Terminal 1 or 2, without an appointment [E0058, E0059]. The employer pays RM200 a year, RM125 processing and RM20 a year for the visa [E0042, E0045]. Before you fly, register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) within 3 days before arriving, as all foreign nationals must; long-term pass holders are exempt [V0058, V0060].

### Step 3: Check it is real

The pass (ePASS) carries a QR code that authorities and the company can scan; banks and embassies can check it at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan) [E0060]. **Need to know:** any official warning about Employment Pass scams aimed at Bangladeshis.

Tried by applicants, and punishable: getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement, or using someone else's pass, is an offence with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030].

## Family passes

### Family of an Employment Pass holder

From 1 June 2026 a spouse and children under 18 get a Dependant Pass; parents, parents-in-law, children aged 18 to 25 and a common-law spouse get a Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP) [E0014]; ESD's older Employment Pass page still lists parents and parents-in-law for a Dependant Pass, but the 2026 policy is the newer text [E0149, E0014]. ESD's LTSVP page says the pass lasts as long as the principal's pass, while ESD's payment table caps LTSVP approval at 12 months [E0147, E0148]. **Need to know:** which applies, and how long a Dependant Pass lasts. Holders of Category III passes issued before 1 June 2026 still can't bring dependants [E0019].

1. **Get your marriage and birth certificates translated into English and certified** by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka or the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur, with a certification dated in the current year [E0030].
2. **Your employer applies through its ESD account** and pays RM540 per person, then RM90 a year, RM50 processing and the visa fee; it takes about 14 working days [E0034, E0041, E0043, E0029]. **Need to know:** whether an LTSVP for an Employment Pass holder's family needs a personal bond (IMI's bond rate for Bangladeshis is RM750) [E0144].
3. **Can they work?** ESD says a dependant must get their own Employment Pass to work, while IMI's 2021 FAQ says a spouse on a Dependant Pass can work with an Immigration endorsement [E0145, E0038]. **Need to know:** which applies now.
4. **Need to know:** whether a refused Dependant Pass or LTSVP can be appealed, and any official warning about scams in family-pass applications.

### Spouse of a Malaysian citizen

Both spouses go together to an Immigration office with IMI's application forms Imm.12, Imm.38 and Imm.55 (linked for download on IMI's spouse page; Imm.38 and Imm.55 in 2 copies each), a sponsor's statement, a security bond stamped RM10, a sworn declaration, the Malaysian spouse's ID card and birth certificate, the marriage registration certificate, a wedding photo, proof of income and address, and the Malaysian spouse's passport [E0126, E0127, Z0061, Z0062]. IMI's page on extending a spouse's pass says applications can be made at the Immigration office nearest your home address [Z0046]; for a new application, IMI sends only Chinese and African nationals to its headquarters [Z0047]. **Need to know:** which office takes a first application from a Bangladeshi spouse. The LTSVP runs from 6 months to 5 years [E0122], and IMI's client charter gives 25 working days to process an LTSVP [E0057]. Making a false statement to Immigration is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0030, V0103]; IMI's spouse page adds "or both" [E0128]. **Need to know:** the fee for a spouse's LTSVP, whether a refused application can be appealed, and any official warning about marriage or sponsorship scams. To work, apply with your spouse at IMI's Visa, Pass and Permit Division in the state where you will work, according to one part of IMI's page; another part of the same page says Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or the nearest state Immigration office [Z0097, Z0098]. Bring a job offer and a contract stamped RM10 giving the job, period and salary [E0125]; permission to work requires the marriage to be legally registered under Malaysian law [E0129].

**Permanent residence:** IMI's Entry Permit, which lets a foreigner live in Malaysia without time limit, has categories for the wife, the husband and a child under 18 of a citizen, and for fully foreign nationals [Z0050, E0134]; see Other passes for what else is known.

**Residence Pass** (Pas Residen, RM500 for 5 years, which allows work, study and business) [E0131, E0132]: open to relatives of citizens (biological, step or adopted children; a widowed or divorced spouse who has a citizen child; parents and parents-in-law) after 3 years in Malaysia on a valid long-term pass, and to children and spouses of permanent residents after 5 years [Z0018, E0133, Z0049]. A Malaysian citizen aged 21 or over must sponsor the application; submit it at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or any nearby Immigration office, with the sponsor present [Z0049, Z0048]. Bring Form IMM 16A [Z0069], 2 passport photos and a copy of the sponsor's identity card [Z0070], your old and new passports with copies, and the original supporting documents with copies [Z0071]; copies of documents issued in your home country must be attested by its embassy [Z0072]. A multiple-entry visa fee, set by nationality, is charged as well [Z0073]. IMI's page lists no category for a citizen's living spouse [Z0018]. **Need to know:** whether a citizen's spouse can apply, how long an application takes, whether a refusal can be appealed, and any official scam warning.

### Other relatives of Malaysians and of permanent residents

IMI gives a Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP, for a stay of at least 6 months) in several other family categories [C0015]. A foreigner aged 60 or over with no family at home can be sponsored by a child, child-in-law or sibling in Malaysia earning RM2,000 or more [C0016]. An abused, divorced or widowed wife of a Malaysian citizen can apply with a local sponsor earning RM2,000 or more [C0017]; for a widow, the sponsor must be a close relative of her late husband and must attend with her [Z0099, Z0100]; an abused wife also brings a police or hospital report as proof [C0017]. The wife of a Malaysian permanent resident can apply once the marriage is more than 6 months old [C0020]. A child or stepchild of a citizen aged 7 or under can get one on the citizen parent's application [Z0029]. Close family of a foreign student can apply with the student's confirmation letter from the institution and a letter from their embassy proving the relationship [C0019], but see Student visa, Family, for who may bring dependants. Someone having treatment at a government or specialist hospital can get one with a single companion [C0018]. Bring originals of all documents; only complete applications are accepted [C0016]. The checklists include IMI's Forms Imm.12 (or Imm.55) and Imm.38 and the marriage, divorce or birth certificates that apply [Z0074]; the 60-plus category also needs a personal bond stamped RM10 at the tax office (LHDN) [Z0076], and an abused wife a security bond stamped RM10 and the local sponsor's declaration and identity card [Z0075]. IMI's client charter gives 25 working days to process an LTSVP [E0057], and its student-pass page gives RM90 for a dependant's or guardian's LTSVP [S0007]. A false statement to get a pass is an offence [K0031]. **Need to know:** which Immigration office takes these applications, whether these pass holders may work, the fee for the other categories, whether a refusal can be appealed, and any official warning about scams in these applications.

## MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)

A long-stay pass for people with substantial savings, open to citizens of any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia [E0100]; Bangladesh and Malaysia each have a High Commission in the other's capital [B0121, L0032]. The minimum age is 25 for Silver, Gold and Platinum, or 21 for the SEZ/SFZ (Special Economic Zone / Special Financial Zone) category [Z0041, Z0043]. Apply only through an operator licensed by MOTAC (the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture); check its name and licence number at [mm2h.gov.my/agencies](https://www.mm2h.gov.my/agencies) [E0101, E0116].

- **Tiers:** Silver needs a USD 150,000 fixed deposit and a home of at least RM600,000 for a 5-year pass; Gold USD 500,000 and RM1,000,000 for 15 years; Platinum USD 1,000,000 and RM2,000,000 for 20 years [E0106, E0108, E0109, E0114]. SEZ/SFZ needs USD 65,000 (age 21 to 49) or USD 32,000 (age 50 and over) for a 10-year pass, and a home bought in Forest City, Johor [E0106, E0115].
- **Fees:** the MM2H website gives a processing fee of RM5,000 per applicant and RM2,500 per dependant, plus a participation fee of at least RM1,000 (Silver and SEZ/SFZ), RM3,000 (Gold) or RM200,000 (Platinum) [E0107, E0109]. MOTAC's announcement of its MM2H online system, used for new applications from 1 Aug 2025, gives a processing fee of RM500 (plus SST, the sales and service tax) per application [E0118]. **Need to know:** whether the RM500 is a separate system fee or conflicts with the RM5,000.
- **Family:** you may bring a spouse; children under 21 (biological, step or adopted); unmarried, unemployed children up to 34 according to the MM2H website, or up to 35 according to MOTAC's Jan 2025 category guide; disabled children of any age; and parents or parents-in-law [E0104, E0110, E0146, E0111]. Each dependant pays the RM2,500 processing fee [E0107].
- **Rules:** buy the home within a year and keep it 10 years; under 50s must spend 90 days a year in Malaysia; only Platinum holders may work [E0119, E0103, E0112, E0113, Z0019, Z0064]. IMI's older, undated MM2H page still gives guidelines for applying for permission to work part-time [Z0089]. **Need to know:** whether that IMI route still applies.
- **Time and appeal:** IMI's client charter gives 1 working day for its MM2H step [C0047]. Any appeal is a matter for the Ministry of Home Affairs [Z0042]. **Need to know:** the total processing time, how to appeal and by when, and any official warning about MM2H scams. In 2024 MOTAC was reportedly vetting MM2H agents after many complaints from applicants and required all agents to re-register (unofficial, not verified) [E0142].
- **Money from Bangladesh:** Bangladesh Bank issues Bangladesh's foreign-exchange rules, under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act 1947 [Z0103]. In 2024 a Bangladesh Bank spokesperson reportedly said the bank has never approved sending money abroad for MM2H (unofficial, not verified) [N0051]. **Need to know:** Bangladesh Bank's rule.

## DE Rantau Nomad Pass

For remote workers and freelancers with foreign employers or clients, run by MDEC (Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation); open to all nationalities except Israel [E0084]. You need an income of more than USD 36,000 a year in a tech role or USD 60,000 otherwise [E0085]. A remote employee needs an employment contract that started at least 3 months before applying [Z0065]; a freelancer needs signed contracts with foreign clients, at most 3 per application [Z0066, Z0067]. Your passport must be valid more than 14 months when you apply [E0096]. MDEC's Nov 2025 list for remote workers also asks for a Letter of Good Conduct from the relevant authority in your country, your highest education certificate and medical insurance valid in Malaysia [E0097]. Dependants can be a spouse or common-law partner, children (including adopted and stepchildren) under 18, a special-needs child of any age, and parents [E0087]. It lasts up to 12 months at a time and 48 months in all, with no route to permanent residence [E0086, E0098]. Apply at [mdec.my/derantau](https://mdec.my/derantau); MDEC's processing fee is RM1,080, plus RM540 per dependant, and is not refunded even if you are refused [E0091]. At endorsement you also pay Immigration's pass fee (RM90 per 3 months or RM360 a year) and any multiple-entry visa charge [Z0040], and, with MDEC as sponsor, a personal or security bond of RM750 for a Bangladeshi, refunded in full when the pass expires [E0092]. A decision takes 6 to 8 weeks after a complete application [E0089]. There is no appeal against a refusal [E0093]. Neither MDEC nor anyone else can guarantee approval [E0094]. A tourist pass can't be converted to a DE Rantau pass [E0095]. New applications are closed from 1 to 30 Nov 2026 and reopen on 1 Dec 2026 [E0099]. A spouse can't work [E0088]. **Need to know:** any official warning about DE Rantau scams.

## Other passes

- **Residence Pass-Talent:** a 10-year pass for experienced professionals who have worked in Malaysia on an Employment Pass for 3 years, earn at least RM15,000 basic a month and have paid tax for 2 years; the holder can change employer and the spouse can work [E0078, E0079, E0080]. **Need to know:** where and how to apply, the cost, the processing time, whether a refusal can be appealed, any official scam warning, and whether Bangladeshis are eligible.
- **Malaysia Tech Entrepreneur Programme (MTEP):** for tech founders, through MDEC; a 1-year pass for new founders (fee from RM2,700) or a 5-year pass for experienced founders that allows dependants; needs RM50,000 in personal bank statements, a certificate of good conduct and a sponsor [E0081, E0083]. **Need to know:** the processing time, whether a refusal can be appealed, any official scam warning, and whether Bangladeshis are eligible.
- **Investor Pass:** 6 months plus 6, for foreign investors working with Malaysia's investment agencies, with no dependants [E0075, E0076]. Apply on Xpats Gateway while outside Malaysia [Z0108, Z0107]. The fees are RM1,200 (RM1,296 with tax), a RM90 pass fee for 6 months, RM500 processing and the visa fee [E0077]. ESD's FAQ says a complete application is processed within 5 working days, and the ePASS (the electronic pass) appears in Xpats Gateway 1 working day after payment [Z0105, Z0106]. **Need to know:** whether a refusal can be appealed, any official scam warning, and whether Bangladeshis are eligible.
- **Premium Visa Programme (PVIP):** IMI's long-stay visa of up to 20 years for investors, entrepreneurs and foreign talent, with no age limit and permission to work and study [C0034]. You need an offshore income of RM40,000 a month or RM480,000 a year and a RM1 million fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank, and pay a participation fee of RM200,000 for the main applicant and RM100,000 per dependant [C0035]; RM2,000 of it is paid at online registration, and the pass fee is RM2,000 a year [Z0052, Z0053]. Dependants are a spouse, children under 25 (biological, step or adopted), disabled children of any age, and parents and parents-in-law; the main applicant may also bring a domestic helper from home under the domestic helper rules [Z0054, Z0055]. Applications go only through PVIP agencies appointed by IMI [C0036]; an agent may act for you only with your written power of attorney, and agency companies may not be involved in fraud [Z0077, Z0078]. The FAQ states no nationality bar and is open to all ages [C0037]; it shows no date, and its PDF file is dated 4 Sep 2025 [C0034]. **Need to know:** how long a participant's application takes, whether a refusal can be appealed, and any official warning about PVIP scams.
- **PLS@XPATS:** IMI's online service for permission to work for foreign professionals who hold a social visit pass; IMI's charter approves it in 3 working days [C0038, C0048]. ESD's PVP-Expert FAQ says emergency work may be considered through PLS@XPATS, depending on the incident, the actual need and current policy [E0071]. **Need to know:** who is eligible, the fee, whether a refusal can be appealed, and any official scam warning.
- **Permanent residence:** Malaysia's Entry Permit lets a foreigner live in Malaysia without time limit; its categories are the wife, husband or child under 18 of a citizen, and fully foreign nationals [E0134, Z0050]. It can be withdrawn if its conditions are broken [E0135]. IMI has an online Entry Permit points calculator at app.imi.gov.my/epc [E0136], and expatriates and children approved for permanent residence get a Long-Term Social Visit Pass while they wait for the Entry Permit [E0137]. IMI's Oct 2026 Entry Permit guideline for the Returning Expert Programme (REP, run by TalentCorp, Talent Corporation Malaysia Berhad, one of the government agencies IMI's Expatriate Services Division works with [Z0102], to bring highly skilled Malaysians working abroad home for good) covers only the foreign spouse and the biological children under 18 of an REP participant; a wife must have been married at least 3 years and a husband at least 5 [Z0080, Z0081]. Under that guideline the Entry Permit costs RM120 a year, and a refusal can be appealed within 30 days of the refusal letter, by letter to the Home Minister through the Chief Secretary of the Ministry of Home Affairs in Putrajaya; questions about an appeal go to 03-8886 3316, 3319 or 3170 or rayuanpermitmasuk@moha.gov.my; after a refused appeal you can apply again after 1 year [Z0083, Z0082]. IMI's Entry Permit page links its application guideline as an e-book on Google Drive [Z0101]. **Need to know:** for the other categories, the criteria, where to apply, the fee, how long it takes and whether a refusal can be appealed.
- **MACS:** not a visa or pass but a fast immigration clearance lane at two Johor checkpoints (Bangunan Sultan Iskandar in Johor Bahru and Kompleks Sultan Abu Bakar in Tanjung Kupang) for people who travel often between Malaysia and Singapore, set up to support the economic development of Iskandar Malaysia [C0011], the region of the Iskandar Regional Development Authority (IRDA) [Z0063, Z0090]. It does not replace a pass or visa: you still need any visa required for your nationality and the relevant immigration pass [C0011, Z0109]. A foreigner involved in Iskandar Malaysia's development who holds a long-term pass, such as a work, professional visit, student or temporary employment pass, can get a Category B sticker [Z0044]. Apply at the MACS counter, Level 1, Wisma Sunway Big Box, Iskandar Puteri, Johor (+607-2333016), after booking online [Z0045].

## Changing visa type

- **Visit pass to work pass:** a PLKS (temporary work pass) and its VDR are processed while the worker is outside Malaysia, and the worker enters only after approval [S0090, W0045]. A visit pass is for social visits, including tourism, family visits, meetings and business discussions, not for work [S0088, V0104]. An extension is considered only in special cases such as illness, an accident or war in your home country; you must show proof and a ticket home or to a third country [S0089].
- **Visit pass to DE Rantau:** a tourist pass can't be converted to a DE Rantau pass [E0095].
- **Visit pass to student:** you must apply from outside Malaysia [S0002].
- **Visit pass to professional work:** IMI has an online service, PLS@XPATS, for permission to work for foreign professionals who hold a social visit pass (see Other passes) [C0038].
- **Student to a higher level:** a Progression application (for example from bachelor's to master's) must be submitted within 3 months of finishing the previous studies, and you may stay in Malaysia while it is processed [S0037, Z0056].
- **Student to work:** the employer must apply for a work pass such as an Employment Pass [S0100]. Bangladeshis can't get the Graduate Pass [S0044].
- **PLKS to another employer or sector:** not allowed without the Home Ministry's permission [S0091, L0067].
- **Dependant to work:** ESD says a Dependant Pass holder needs their own Employment Pass, while IMI's 2021 FAQ says a spouse can work with an Immigration endorsement [S0093, E0145, E0038]. **Need to know:** which applies now (see Family passes).
- **Family of an Employment Pass holder on an LTSVP:** may not work or study; they must convert to an Employment Pass to work or a Student Pass to study [S0141, E0032].
- **Spouse of a Malaysian:** can work on the LTSVP once permission is endorsed (see Family passes) [E0125].
- **Into an Employment Pass:** ESD's FAQ says that if the applicant is outside Malaysia, the employer applies for a VDR together with the Employment Pass [E0058]. **Need to know:** whether a student or a PLKS worker can switch to an Employment Pass inside Malaysia.

## Asylum

Malaysia is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention and has no national asylum system; UNHCR, the UN refugee agency, decides refugee claims itself [S0101]. The Immigration Act has no provision on refugees or asylum [K0036]. Recognition by UNHCR gives no legal immigration status [S0142]. Refugees and asylum seekers are treated as undocumented migrants: they can be arrested, detained, fined and deported, and have no legal right to work [S0106, S0107, S0108]. UNHCR treats as a refugee only someone forced to flee who faces persecution or serious harm because of race, religion, nationality, social group or political opinion, or who can't return because of war or violence [S0102]. Requests are made only online on UNHCR's portal, with a long wait; priority goes to requests from 2022 or earlier and to vulnerable people [S0109, S0110, S0112]. UNHCR can't interview everyone, so it can't say when any person will be interviewed [S0103]. At the interview you have a duty to tell the truth; if UNHCR later finds you misled it, that can harm your case [S0105]. A refused claim can be appealed once, within 30 days, and then the case is closed [S0104]. Pregnant asylum seekers not yet registered with UNHCR pay the full foreigners' fees for antenatal care and childbirth at government health facilities [S0113]. UNHCR's services are free; paying anyone to send letters doesn't help, and anyone asking money for an interview is lying [S0109, S0143]. In Aug 2026 UNHCR had 225,740 registered refugees and asylum seekers in Malaysia, about 90% from Myanmar; Bangladesh was not among the countries it named [N0044]. A genuine UNHCR card can be checked by scanning its QR code with the free UNHCR Verify Plus app; "Incompatible QR Code" means it is fake [N0046, N0047]. Police broke a Bangladeshi-run ring in 2026 selling fake UNHCR cards and work permits for RM80 to RM250 (unofficial, not verified) [N0049].

## Having a child in Malaysia

1. **Register the birth** at a National Registration Department (JPN) office within 60 days; it is free, and after 60 days it costs RM50 and needs more documents [S0114, S0115, S0119]. Bring form JPN.LM01, the hospital's birth confirmation, the mother's antenatal card, and both parents' passports and marriage documents, originals and copies [S0116, S0117, S0118, Z0024]; JPN's list also includes a police report and an AM80 statutory declaration [Z0024]. **Need to know:** whether the police report and AM80 are needed for a normal hospital birth.
2. **The child is not Malaysian** just by being born there: under the Federal Constitution, a child born in Malaysia is a citizen by law if at least one parent is a Malaysian citizen or permanent resident at the time of birth [K0001, K0002], or if the child is not born a citizen of any other country [Z0051], and JPN decides citizenship from the parents' citizenship [S0123]. A 2025 amendment to the Constitution (Act A1752), not yet in force in the reprint of 30 Nov 2025, will limit this to a citizen parent [K0005]. **Need to know:** whether it has come into force.
3. **Register the birth with the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur,** as Bangladeshi law requires for births abroad [S0127]. Bring the JPN birth certificate attested by Malaysia's Foreign Ministry, both parents' passport copies, the e-apostilled marriage certificate, the printed online form and 2 photos; the child and both parents come in person [S0125, S0126]. **Need to know:** the fee.
4. **Get the child a Bangladeshi passport** at the High Commission. A child under 18 gets a 48-page, 5-year e-passport [Z0023]. Since 19 Jan 2026 that passport costs RM545 (RM485 government fee plus RM60 charged by ESKL, Expat Services (Kuala Lumpur) Sdn. Bhd., the High Commission's outsourced passport company) in the High Commission's "other categories" table, or RM172 in its table for general workers and students [Z0021, Z0022, Z0020]. The 2024 newborn page gave RM545 or RM817 for 64 pages, paid in cash at Maybank [S0128]. **Need to know:** which fee table applies to a child. Book an appointment with ESKL and bring the child, a Bangladeshi birth registration showing Malaysia as the place of birth, both parents' NIDs, and the marriage certificate (kabinnama) attested by the Law Ministry, the Foreign Ministry and the High Commission [S0132, S0131, S0129, S0130, S0133].

## Sending money home

- **Use a licensed remitter.** Check it on the list of licensed money services businesses of Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM, Malaysia's central bank) and their approved agents [B0098]. Report illegal remittance services such as hundi to Bank Negara's contact centre, BNMTELELINK, 1-300-88-5465 [B0099, B0100].
- **Compare costs.** Bangladesh Bank says sending up to USD 200 from Malaysia cost 3.19% on average in Apr-Jun 2026 [B0095]. In the World Bank's mid-2025 survey, sending MYR 610 cost 5.05% with MoneyGram, 5.15% with Western Union, 5.32% with NBL Money Transfer or Agrani Remittance House and 5.49% with Merchantrade, while banks cost more [B0097].
- **To bKash:** from Malaysia you can send to a bKash account through partners including Ria, CBL Money Transfer, Merchantrade, Touch 'n Go (the Touch 'n Go eWallet, an electronic wallet [L0119]), Western Union, MoneyGram and others; give the receiver's bKash number and full registered name [B0102, B0103]. bKash says the family gets the government's 2.5% incentive [B0103]. **Need to know:** the current Bangladesh Bank circular on the incentive.
- **Don't use hundi** [B0007].

## Going home without valid papers

1. **If you have no valid passport, get a Travel Permit** (out pass) from the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur, for anyone whose passport has expired or been lost or damaged [L0001]. Apply in person with a copy of your old passport, a copy of your NID or online birth registration certificate, two colour photos (the High Commission's labour FAQ, written before 31 Jan 2025, said 3) and your phone number [L0003, L0004, L0005, L0014]; without an NID, a citizenship certificate from your UNO (Upazila Nirbahi Officer), or from the Deputy Commissioner if you are from Cox's Bazar, emailed to the labour wing is accepted [L0014, L0015]. It is valid 3 months [L0002]. The fee is RM38 from 16 Jan 2026, set by the High Commission's fee notice [R0003, L0006]. Processing is same-day in one High Commission source and 1 to 2 working days in an older one [L0016, L0012]. Pay by card at the consular counter or into the High Commission's Maybank account 564427102268 [L0007].
2. **Join the repatriation programme.** Malaysia's Program Repatriasi Migran 2.0 (PRM 2.0), for undocumented migrants who want to go home, was due to end on 30 Apr 2026 and now runs from 1 May 2026 to 31 May 2027 [L0125, L0113]; news reports say it began on 19 May 2025 (unofficial, not verified) [L0189]. Walk in at one of 14 Immigration Enforcement offices in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan with a valid passport or travel document and a ticket home dated within 14 days [L0116, L0118]. The compound is RM500 for being without a valid pass, RM500 for overstaying or RM300 for breaking pass conditions [L0120, L0121, L0122], plus RM20 for each Special Pass (Pas Khas), the pass Immigration issues so that a person can leave Malaysia within a set time [L0123, L0013]. The High Commission's older labour FAQ (Jan 2025) gives a different order for going home: pay Immigration's fine and get the Special Pass first, then buy the ticket [L0013]; under PRM 2.0, IMI's poster is the rule: you must already hold a ticket home within 14 days when you attend [L0116]. Pay by debit or credit card or Touch 'n Go eWallet only [L0119]. Children under 18 pay no compound [L0124]. Hotline 03-8880 1269 [L0115]. In Sep 2026 the Bangladesh High Commission reportedly told people that they must come in person and that there is no need to pay any broker (unofficial, not verified) [H0003, H0004]. **Need to know:** whether the flight must be at least 5 days after the Immigration appointment; the High Commission reportedly said so, citing Malaysian guidelines (unofficial, not verified) [H0002], while IMI's poster says only that the ticket must be within 14 days [L0116]. **Need to know:** who is excluded from the programme; news reports say people who registered before but didn't leave, blacklisted people and wanted people (unofficial, not verified) [H0008].
3. **If you lose your passport in Malaysia,** report at once to IMI's Enforcement Division yourself, without middlemen or agents. Bring your passport number or a copy, your new passport or travel document (a Travel Permit, step 1), a police report and a plane, bus or ferry ticket for within 7 days; you get a Special Pass (Pas Khas) for RM100 [L0182, L0183, L0184, L0185, C0040]. **Need to know:** how long the Enforcement Division takes; IMI's charter gives 3 working days only for the Foreign Workers Division's electronic Special Pass (eSP) [C0046].
4. **If you are detained,** the camp arranges the flight when you have a passport and a ticket; without a passport, the camp sends your application and the High Commission issues the Travel Permit to the camp, with no government fee [L0017, L0018].
5. **A close relative in Bangladesh can ask the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare for help** bringing a worker home, where applicable; the Ministry's charter lists this service separately for women and for men workers. Apply with proof of the worker's migrant status, the workplace and home address abroad, the agency's name and address and the employer's mobile number; the ministry acts through the mission, the Welfare Board or BMET within 7 working days of receiving the letter, free [B0052, Z0015].
6. **Bans:** Immigration's Director General can bar a person from entering Malaysia for a stated period or permanently; a person whose pass is cancelled is removed and barred afterwards [K0012, K0013]. The ban order or the cancellation itself can be appealed to the Minister by a written petition within 7 days [K0014], but there is no appeal against removal when you are in Malaysia unlawfully because your pass has expired or been cancelled [K0019]. Coming back unlawfully after removal can bring a fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail and whipping [K0022]. **Need to know:** how long bans last in practice after the programme or deportation. In 2017 Immigration's Director General was reported as saying deported migrants are blacklisted for 1 to 5 years depending on the offence, and for life in serious cases; 2024 and 2026 reports say deported Bangladeshis were blacklisted (unofficial, not verified) [H0012, H0013, H0016, H0018].

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

1. **The employer writes to the High Commission,** appoints a funeral company and sends the papers: its letters, the insurance claim and compensation letters, the hospital letter, the death certificate, the police report and the passport [L0038, B0119]. The family sends a consent letter naming who will receive the body [L0039]. For the High Commission's no-objection certificate (NOC) to send the body home, it also needs a police report and NOC for an abnormal death and the employer's report of the death to PERKESO [L0023, L0024, L0025]; for an undocumented worker the list is the original passport (or, if it can't be found, a police report of the lost passport), the receiver's details, a police report with NOC, the original death certificate, the funeral company's letter and the employer's name and phone number [L0026, L0027].
2. **Who pays:** the Ministry of Human Resources says the employer pays the funeral and the cost of sending the body home [M0148]; bodies normally come home at the employer's cost, and the Welfare Board pays if no employer is found [B0125, L0040]. The family can apply to the Welfare Board (call 16135), which keeps in touch with the High Commission until the body arrives, or to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), which acts within 7 working days, including for undocumented workers [B0074, B0051]. If the family wants burial in Malaysia, the Board passes on its written wish [B0075].
3. **At the airport,** the welfare desk gives the family a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial; bring the worker's passport copy, the heirship certificate, the nominee's photo and NID, the NOC, the death certificate and the air waybill, and collect it within 3 months [B0066, B0067].
4. **The Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant** goes to the family of a worker who had BMET clearance or Board membership and died in the country of the clearance [B0060, B0061]. If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the worker was buried abroad, apply within a year [B0062]. It is paid into the family's bank account, with an SMS [B0065].
5. **Insurance** bought at clearance lasts 5 years and pays Tk 10 lakh for death or total permanent disability, part of that for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if the worker loses the job and comes home within 6 months of going abroad [B0008, B0009]. The Board helps with the claim [B0059].
6. **Malaysian compensation:** for a death, the Labour Department's Form H is sent to the heirs, who fill it in and return it through the High Commission [L0036, B0120]. The labour wing recovers compensation and unpaid wages and the Board pays the heirs [B0136, B0076]. PERKESO's Employment Injury Scheme pays medical care, temporary and permanent disablement benefits, a constant attendance allowance, rehabilitation, a funeral benefit and dependants' benefit, and its Invalidity Scheme pays an invalidity pension or grant, a survivors' pension and a funeral benefit [M0172]. In 2019 PERKESO's funeral benefit was RM6,500 for a worker who died at work and was sent home for burial, or RM2,000 if buried in Malaysia [M0187]. A 2022 PERKESO brochure says that for a death not caused by work, up to RM4,500 of the cost of sending the body home may be reimbursed against the official receipt [M0177]. Claims are made on PERKESO's foreign worker claim form [M0188]. **Need to know:** current benefit amounts and the time limit for a claim.
7. **Injured or sick returnees** with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment if they apply within a year of return (the Board's 2023-24 report said Tk 1.5 lakh) [B0071, B0072, B0129]. The airport desk sends sick returnees to hospital in the Board's ambulance [B0068, B0070].

## Coming home

- **Customs:** duty-free you can bring 100 g of gold jewellery and 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) and 200 cigarettes, and once a year 2 used phones and 1 new phone [B0111, B0113]. One gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) a year is allowed if declared, at Tk 5,000 per tola [B0112]. Bangladeshis may not bring alcohol, and foreign currency over USD 10,000 must be declared to customs on the FMJ form (the foreign currency declaration form) [B0114, B0115].
- **Cash out of Malaysia:** a May 2026 notice of Malaysia's civil aviation authority (its Aeronautical Information Publication) says travellers may carry up to RM1,000 in ringgit notes when leaving, and non-residents may take out foreign currency up to the amount they brought in [L0161, L0162]. **Need to know:** Bank Negara Malaysia's current cash limits.
- **Help at the airport:** the welfare desks help with boarding cards, customs and lost luggage, take complaints from cheated workers and send sick returnees to hospital [B0068, B0069]. Women who return within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [B0038].
- **A place to stay:** the Wage Earners' Centre at Borua, near Shahjalal airport, puts up departing and returning workers; book online at wec.wewb.gov.bd or in person with your clearance or membership proof, passport and ticket [B0080]. The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives free wifi and phone calls and food at up to 30% off; show your clearance or membership proof [B0082].
- **If your family is threatened or its property seized,** apply to the Welfare Board directly or through the district office; it writes to the local administration [B0078, B0132].
- **Business loan:** PKB lends returnees up to Tk 50 lakh (Tk 3 lakh without collateral) at 9% over up to 10 years, decided within 15 working days [B0021, B0022].
- **BRAC** (founded in Bangladesh in 1972, which describes itself as the world's largest southern-led development organisation [Z0104]): its Migration Programme helps returnees at the airport and with counselling, livelihood support and legal help [B0122]. BRAC's head office is at BRAC Centre, 75 Mohakhali, Dhaka-1212; phone 02-2222-81265, email info@brac.net [H0041]. **Need to know:** a direct phone line for the Migration Programme.

## Malaysia as a destination

- **Numbers:** BMET cleared 351,683 workers for Malaysia in 2023, 93,632 in 2024 (BMET's annual table; OEP's live report gives 350,633 and 93,562), 3,451 in 2025 and 1,592 from 1 Jan to 6 Nov 2026 [B0091, B0086, B0087, B0088, B0089]. From 2004 to May 2025 Malaysia took 9.30% of all Bangladeshi workers going abroad, against 34.49% for Saudi Arabia [B0092].
- **Past stops:** BMET's figures fall from 273,201 in 2007 to 919 in 2010, and from 175,927 in 2018 to 545 in 2019 [B0091]. **Need to know:** official statements explaining these falls.
- **Money:** Malaysia was Bangladesh's 5th-largest source of remittances in Jul-Sep 2026, at USD 666.73 million [B0093].
- **Medical travel:** MHTC says close to 70,000 Bangladeshis travelled to Malaysia for health care in 2025 [N0039]. MHTC says visa restrictions have disrupted Bangladeshi patients' travel to India for treatment, and it is working with Bangladeshi healthcare facilitators and travel partners to bring patients to Malaysia [N0041].
- **Tourists and students:** Malaysia reportedly aims to attract 300,000 Bangladeshi tourists in 2026, its Visit Malaysia Year (unofficial, not verified) [N0055]. Malaysian education officials reportedly put Bangladeshi enrolment in Malaysian higher education at over 11,000 in 2026, up 45% in a year (unofficial, not verified) [S0136].
- **Where the jobs are:** foreign workers may work in manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services, and Bangladeshis are allowed in all sectors not frozen [W0041, W0042]; BOESL's Nov 2026 circular is for logistics jobs at DHL Supply Chain at RM1,700 a month [W0001]; Bangladesh signed an agreement on sending workers with CLAB, the Malaysian government's recruiting body for construction, on 7 Aug 2025, and CLAB's first demand was for 500 workers, of whom 255 had visas by 25 Nov 2025 [W0019, D0042]; in Feb 2026 CLAB reportedly said about 1,000 of 3,000 Bangladeshi construction candidates it interviewed were rejected, for reasons including health and suitability (unofficial, not verified) [D0036]; the Budget 2027 speech says the minimum wage will rise from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, with micro, small and medium businesses selling less than RM50 million a year exempted [R0002]. **Need to know:** which sectors are hiring Bangladeshis now.
- **Reopening:** Malaysia sent its criteria for choosing Bangladeshi agencies in Oct 2025, the same as for India, Nepal, Pakistan and Myanmar [W0022, W0023]. **Need to know:** official figures for Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia compared with Indonesia and Nepal. A 2024 memo prepared for Malaysia's Prime Minister reportedly put recruitment costs for Bangladeshis as high as USD 6,600 per worker, far higher than the fees Indonesian or Nepali workers paid (unofficial, not verified) [A0070].
- **Compared with other nationalities (all unofficial, not verified):** in Feb 2026 Immigration's deputy director general reportedly said Bangladeshis were the largest group in the PRM 2.0 repatriation programme, ahead of Indonesians, among more than 155,000 participants, while an Apr 2026 report put Indonesians first and Bangladeshis second among 204,523 who had left [H0007, H0006]. In 2024 activists and researchers estimated that 100,000 to 200,000 of about 800,000 Bangladeshis in Malaysia were jobless, unpaid and in debt [L0191]. In 2024 Bangladeshis held 3,604 MM2H passes, the 5th-largest group after China, Australia, South Korea and Japan [E0141]. In the first half of 2023, 14,973 travellers arriving at KLIA were issued a "Not to Land" order (refused entry and sent back by the airline), mainly from Bangladesh, Pakistan and India [N0016].
