# Malaysia: migration guide

How to go to Malaysia from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step, for Bangladesh passport holders. Citations like \[3\] are official sources; \[N3\] are news, NGO, research or guide sources, listed under "Needs an official source", and a year after one, like \[N3 (2022)\], means it is older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. Each numbered procedure starts with "You'll need:", the documents and details a person must have before starting.

In this doc, Tk means the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000) and RM the Malaysian ringgit. BMET is the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the Bangladesh government agency that registers people who want to work abroad and gives them emigration clearance [B0026, G0001]. IMI (Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia) is the Immigration Department of Malaysia, which runs the eVISA (electronic visa) system on its MyVISA website, malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, and approves work visas [V0005, Z0032, W0045]. Bangladeshis need a visa to enter Malaysia for any purpose, even a short visit [V0001, V0002].

## Work visa: company worker (PLKS)

For factory, construction, plantation, farm and service jobs. The pass is the PLKS (Pas Lawatan Kerja Sementara, Temporary Employment Visit Pass), Malaysia's work pass for foreign workers; it is valid for 12 months at a time and the employer renews it [W0048, L0044]. Foreign workers may work only in manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services, and Bangladeshis are allowed in all of them except sectors where intake is frozen [W0041, W0042]. A worker must be 18 to 45 when the employer applies [W0073, L0064]. The employer pays the levy (a charge the Malaysian government takes from the employer for each foreign worker), the visa, the pass and a deposit [W0054]. Under the memorandum of understanding (MoU) between Bangladesh and Malaysia, the employer pays all costs on the Malaysian side, including the levy and the worker's airfare [W0028, R0009]; the Ministry says the MoU stays in force until 31 December 2026 [Z0156]. **Need to know:** whether the employer still pays the airfare for jobs recruited since October 2026, and what terms apply after the MoU ends.

**How a job reaches you.** A Malaysian employer first gets government approval for a quota of foreign workers [W0041, W0043]. A Bangladeshi recruiting agency on Malaysia's approved list finds the employer and collects its job order, or demand [Z0145]; the Bangladesh High Commission (Bangladesh's embassy) in Kuala Lumpur attests demand letters [A0035, W0038]. The agency that collects the job order selects workers under Bangladesh's rules [Z0147]. BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the only government-owned recruiting agency) or one of 25 private "processing agencies" handles the Malaysian visa paperwork [Z0144, Z0148]. The recruiting agency then gets the worker's BMET clearance under its own licence [Z0149]. On the Malaysian side the employer applies for the worker's entry visa through FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralized Management System, Malaysia's online system for foreign-worker applications, developed by the company Bestinet), and only after the worker has passed the medical check in Bangladesh [W0045, W0009, Z0001].

**Is recruitment open now?** Recruitment restarted in October 2026 through agencies on Malaysia's list [Z0151]. Malaysia listed 338 Bangladeshi agencies: BOESL, 25 processing agencies and 312 recruiting agencies [Z0144]. Malaysia has also agreed in principle to take 10,000 workers at no cost to them, airfare included, with the first batch to go through BOESL [Z0146, Z0154]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment (Bangladesh's ministry for migrant workers, called "the Ministry" below) says a worker must not be made to bear any extra cost for the job [Z0150]. **Need to know:** the most a private agency may take for a Malaysia job since the October 2026 reopening [G0009, G0010].

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP).** OEP is BMET's website for people who want to work abroad. Agencies must pick their workers from BMET's database of registered job-seekers, and BMET gives no clearance to a worker outside it [W0027, W0030].

   You'll need: your NID (national ID card); a mobile phone with a number you can receive codes on; a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-money account with Tk 200; and, for the profile, your passport details, education and work skills [G0035, B0139, G0001, G0037].

   1. Open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) in Google Chrome [B0139, G0035].
   2. Type your name in English exactly as on your NID (leave out any dots and put spaces where the NID has them), your mobile number, your NID number, and your date of birth, division and district as on your NID [G0035, B0139].
   3. Type the numbers in the picture (captcha), tick that you accept the terms, and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [G0035].
   4. Type the code (OTP) sent to your phone and press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue) [B0139].
   5. Fill in the rest of the form correctly, then press "পেমেন্ট করতে এগিয়ে যান" (Proceed to payment), "পেমেন্ট করুন" (Pay), Mobile Banking, and bKash, Nagad or another service [B0139].
   6. Enter your mobile-money account number, the code sent to it and your PIN. The government fee is Tk 200 [B0139, G0001].
   7. Download and print your registration card. It carries your BMET number; you can download the card again later at [oep.gov.bd/employee-card](https://www.oep.gov.bd/employee-card) with that number, without logging in [B0139, G0036, G0038].
   8. Take 2 printed copies of the registration slip to give your fingerprints [G0001]. BMET has fingerprint devices at every DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office), TTC (Technical Training Centre) and IMT (Institute of Marine Technology) [D0064]. BMET lists every DEMO's address, phone and email at [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [G0047]. **Need to know:** whether giving fingerprints costs anything, the office hours, and what you get back.

   **Check it is real:** register on employee.oep.gov.bd, the address BMET's district office gives [G0001]. BMET asks people not to use middlemen and to pay fees only through the proper channel, against a receipt [B0042]. **If it fails:** if "Verify" shows an error, check that your name, NID number and date of birth match the NID exactly [B0139]; for anything else call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [G0036, B0144]. **Need to know:** the hotline's hours and call charge.

2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET's TTCs teach 55 trades [B0037]. BMET lists every TTC, with its principal's name, address, phone and email, at [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6) [G0048, G0049]. TTCs post admission notices with the courses, minimum education, dates and fees; for example the Bangladesh-Korea TTC (BKTTC) in Dhaka posted its July-December 2026 notice at bkttc.dhaka.gov.bd [G0002, G0003]. In the BKTTC example, courses run 3 or 6 months (360 or 720 hours) in trades such as welding, electrical installation, plumbing, automotive mechanics, and refrigeration and air conditioning, and end with an NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) skills certificate [G0006, G0005].

   You'll need: your OEP registration (Step 1.1); the minimum education the course asks for (in the BKTTC example, JSC, the Junior School Certificate, for welding); and a current, clear photo in your OEP profile [G0002, G0005, B0140].

   1. Log in at [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) with your mobile number and the code sent to it [B0140].
   2. From your profile open the training portal (ট্রেনিং পোর্টাল), then Training, then Trade/Skills [B0140, G0002].
   3. Choose your division, district and training centre and press Search [B0140].
   4. Pick the trade and press Apply. Check that your photo matches your face now, press Continue, then "Process to Enroll" and Confirm [B0140].
   5. "My Course" in the training portal shows where you have applied [B0140].
   6. Sit the admission test on the date in the notice; in the BKTTC example the result goes on the TTC's notice board and website and is sent by SMS [G0003].
   7. Pay the course fee against a money receipt: in the BKTTC example Tk 748 for a 3-month course and Tk 1,484 for a 6-month course [G0003, B0037]. Courses run by funded projects are free [B0037]. In the BKTTC example, regular trainees with at least 80% attendance get Tk 150 a day for transport and food when funds are allocated [G0004].

   **Scams:** if anyone offers to get you into a TTC or through its exam for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement officer [B0010]. To complain about a TTC, contact its principal (phone and email on BMET's list) [G0049]; if the problem isn't solved, write to BMET's complaint officer, the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 [B0041]. The government's call centre for migrants, 16135, takes calls about any migration problem [B0083].

   **Construction jobs:** from January 2025 Malaysia requires foreign construction workers to hold a construction skills certificate (SKKP) issued in their own country and checked by CIDB (Malaysia's Construction Industry Development Board), and to finish an online safety and health course (eSICW) [G0007]. BMET's leaflet says the CIDB skill test runs at Khulna TTC (Teligati, Khulna; phone 041-2870047 or 01716966627; ttckhu@gmail.com) and will start in Dhaka, Chattogram and Rajshahi [B0001, G0049]. **Need to know:** whether the CIDB test still runs at Khulna TTC, how to register for it, its fee, and the certificate you get.

3. **Apply for government (BOESL) jobs.** BOESL posts its Malaysia job circulars as notices on [boesl.gov.bd](https://www.boesl.gov.bd/) [Z0003]. Its own application portal, brms.boesl.gov.bd, has been closed for all applications since 11 October 2026 for a technical fault, and BOESL tells applicants to follow its notice board and its Facebook page, [facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd](https://www.facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd/) [W0007, G0059]. Each circular sets its own rules; the example below is its DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia) circular for 112 men at RM1,700 basic pay a month [W0001].

   You'll need: a passport valid for at least 18 to 24 months; your OEP registration; your education and experience details; and, if called to the interview, 4 colour passport photos on a white background, a colour copy of every passport you have held, your original education and experience certificates, your NID and a CV showing your computer skills (MS Excel), numeracy, any driving licence and any overseas or warehouse experience [W0003, G0031, W0006].

   1. Read the circular for the posts, age limit (22 to 35 in the example), education, skills and the application window [W0003, Z0033, W0002].
   2. Apply online through the link in the circular (in the example, [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration)) with your passport number, education and experience, for one post only, within the window [W0001, G0031, W0002]. Incomplete or wrong applications are rejected [W0002].
   3. A software lottery picks a shortlist (300 in the example), which BOESL posts on its website; check the list there [W0004].
   4. If shortlisted, go to the interview with the documents above; the employer's representative tests you and makes the final choice [W0004, W0006].
   5. A valid driving licence and experience in the work count in your favour [G0033].

   In the example circular, workers pay nothing at any stage [W0005]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents and never takes cash; gangs use BOESL's name to promise Malaysia jobs for money [W0068]. Applying, the interview and even selection do not guarantee a job [G0032]. BOESL's office is at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban (5th floor), 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka; phone 02-58311838, 02-48319125, 02-48317515 or 01765411653; email info@boesl.gov.bd [W0006, W0068, G0034]. **Need to know:** which BOESL Malaysia jobs charge the worker, and how much [G0010]; and whether all 10,000 zero-cost places go through BOESL circulars like this one [G0009].

4. **Or go through a private agency.** Only agencies on Malaysia's list can recruit. To see the list, open [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/#recruitment-agencies), choose Recruitment Agencies, then Bangladesh [W0008, Z0144].

   You'll need: the agency's name and RL number (recruiting licence number, which every licensed Bangladeshi agency has) [X0001].

   1. Find the agency on the FWCMS list [W0008].
   2. Open BMET's agency list at [oep.gov.bd/agencies](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies), find the same RL number and check that the licence status is Active (not Expired, Cancelled or Suspended) and the validity date has not passed; the list also gives the agency's registered address and office phone [X0001, X0002, Z0035].
   3. Go to the agency at that registered address, not to a middleman's office [X0001, B0042].
   4. Before going, BMET tells workers to check the demand letter, the contract, the job details and salary, and the migration cost for the country [B0002]; the permission letter for the job is in the next step.

   **What happens in practice:** being on the lists does not stop overcharging. In the last recruitment round, run only through agencies Malaysia had selected, workers paid on average about Tk 5.44 lakh against an official cap of Tk 78,990 [W0072, A0001]. Workers have arrived in Malaysia with valid work permits to find the promised job did not exist [A0095, A0014]. Many left without seeing a contract [A0082]. Some were pressed to sign that they had paid only the official fee [A0061]. **Need to know:** which agencies place Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia in the promised job at a fair cost.

5. **Find and check the permission letter for your job.** For each Malaysia job order the Ministry or BMET issues a permission letter naming the agency and its RL number, the Malaysian employer, the jobs, the salary, the age range, hours, food, housing, airfare, contract length and the most the agency may take from each worker [W0026, W0028, W0029, W0033, W0038].

   You'll need: the agency's RL number, and the employer's name and salary the agency gave you [W0026].

   1. Open [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) and type "Malaysia" (or মালয়েশিয়া) in the search box [W0026].
   2. Entries are titled with the letter's number and the agency's RL number, for example "DN25-225 RL1274"; open the one with your agency's RL number [W0026].
   3. Compare the letter with what the agency told you: the employer's name, the salary and the maximum the agency may take [W0029, W0033, W0038].
   4. Take the demand letter or visa to your DEMO or to BMET's head office, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, and ask them to check it; BMET checks visas and demand letters for free within 3 working days. Bring the visa and, where it is stamped, a copy of the passport page with it [B0005, B0032, B0033, G0026]. If the check shows the paper is fake, complain as set out under "If an agency or broker takes your money" below. **Need to know:** which desk at BMET's head office does the check, its hours, and what you get back.

   **Need to know:** whether BMET posts permission letters for Malaysia jobs from the October 2026 reopening.

6. **Check the employer exists.** Malaysia's Companies Commission (SSM, Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia) sells company profiles online through SSM e-Info [W0067, G0053].

   You'll need: the employer's full company name or Malaysian registration number, as on the demand letter; an email address to open an account; and a credit or debit card, or Malaysian online banking (FPX), to pay [G0055, G0056].

   1. Open [ssm-einfo.my](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/), register for an account and log in [G0055].
   2. Top up your e-Info account; card and FPX payments are approved instantly [G0056].
   3. Search by the company's name or registration number and buy its Company Profile, RM15.40 [G0055, W0067].
   4. Save the PDF at once; it stays in your Transaction History for only 7 days [G0057].
   5. In the profile, check the company status, the registered and business addresses and the nature of business [G0053]. "Existing" means the company is not being wound up, struck off or dissolved [G0054]. Compare the name and address with the demand letter.

   **If it fails:** if you can't get the document, call the e-Info call centre, 1300 30 4636 [G0058]. **Need to know:** what to do if the company can't be found or doesn't match the demand letter.

**Before paying:**

- **Know what you should pay.** The Ministry's last cap for Malaysia, Tk 78,990, covered everything the worker pays inside Bangladesh (passport, medical check, registration, welfare fee, insurance, smart card, pre-departure training, clothing, the agency's service charge and other costs), and the employer paid the security deposit, the pass, insurance and the air tickets [R0008, R0009]. The cap for jobs since the 2026 reopening is not yet set (see "Is recruitment open now?") [G0009, G0012]. The permission letter for your job states the maximum for that job, which in some letters is nothing [W0026, W0034].
- **Pay only by cheque, bank draft or pay order into the agency's own bank account, and keep every receipt** [W0028, B0003]. Agencies and middlemen have started collecting passports from people who want to go [G0011].
- **Get the contract and a Bangla translation before you go.** The agency must sign a contract with each worker before BMET clearance and give each worker a Bangla translation attested by BMET [W0030]. BMET also tells workers to check that the contract is attested by the destination country's embassy [B0002]. **Need to know:** how a worker can check the BMET attestation, and what to do if the agency won't hand over the contract.
- **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years, and decides within 7 working days [B0017]. It lends only once you have a valid visa, and it does not arrange visas [G0039].

  You'll need: the free application form; 3 recent photos; copies of your NID, passport and visa; a copy of your BMET card (BMET's smart card); the contract if you have it; 3 signed MICR cheque leaves from an active bank account of your own, with its statement; and a PKB savings account opened with at least Tk 500 [B0014]. You also need 2 guarantors: one well-off person (3 signed cheque leaves, a bank statement and proof of income) and one family member, each with a photo, NID and proof of address [B0016]. You, and your guarantors, must live permanently in the branch's area; you must be 18 to 60 and have no loan in default [B0013]. **Need to know:** whether the BMET card is needed before the loan is approved, since the card goes with emigration clearance [B0002].

  1. Apply at the PKB branch for your area (PKB's branch list is linked from its loan page on mygov.bd), or online: open the migration loan page on [mygov.bd](https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1726556874) and press "আবেদন করুন" (Apply) [B0019, G0042, G0044]. You can apply only once until the loan is approved or refused [B0019].
  2. On mygov.bd you get an application number and a tracking number by SMS, and can check the status with your mobile number and tracking number [G0043].
  3. Pay 0.5% processing and 0.5% documentation fees plus VAT and Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover; bring 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10, and 3 adhesive stamps of Tk 100 for each guarantor [B0018].
  4. PKB tells you by SMS when the loan is approved [G0039].

  PKB helpline: 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018]. **Need to know:** what to do if the loan is refused, and where to complain about PKB.

**If an agency or broker takes your money and doesn't send you, or sends you to a different job:**

1. **Complain to BMET.** BMET takes complaints from people who were cheated before going, after going or after coming back; it is free and is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0034].

   You'll need: a copy of your NID or passport; every receipt, bank slip or mobile-money record of what you paid; the contract, visa or work permit if you have them; and any letters, messages, audio or video from the agency or broker [G0014, G0023]. Write down the agency's name and RL number, the name and phone number of the person who took the money, the dates and amounts, and the employer's name and address [G0020, G0024].

   1. Fill in BMET's complaint form against a recruiting agent, or write a self-explanatory application to the Director General of BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, with your proof [G0020, B0034, G0026]. Give what the form asks: your name, father's or husband's name, address, phone and email; the agency's RL number, name and address; the type of complaint (for example money misappropriation, or not sending workers abroad); the employer; everyone affected; and the name and phone number of the person who took the money [G0020, G0021, G0024].
   2. Hand it in at BMET's complaint cell at its head office, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, and get the acknowledgement receipt with your complaint ID [B0034, G0026, G0022]. The officer responsible for these complaints is BMET's Director (Employment), 01711111544, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [G0046]. BMET's main number is 02-49357972 [G0026]. **Need to know:** the complaint cell's room and office hours, and whether BMET's online complaint form at ovijog.bmet.gov.bd, which takes evidence files and shows a complaint's status, is in service [G0023].
   3. BMET investigates, calls both sides to a hearing, usually at its Dhaka office, and recommends a decision to the Ministry, which decides [B0034, B0035, G0015]. If the agency is found guilty and refuses to pay, BMET can take the money from the agency's security deposit [G0016].

   **What happens in practice:** it usually takes 4 months to a year, not 30 working days [A0073]. Workers who show full documents get more back [A0077]. Witnesses from the village often cannot come to the hearing in Dhaka [G0017].

2. **If BMET does not settle it,** go up BMET's grievance ladder.

   You'll need: your complaint ID and copies of everything you gave BMET [G0022, B0034].

   1. Write to BMET's Additional Director General (Employment), adgemp@bmet.gov.bd, 02-48321949 or 01720105492, the officer the charter names when the complaint service isn't given [G0046].
   2. Then write to the Secretary of the Ministry, secretary@probashi.gov.bd, who has 1 month [B0041].
   3. Then file on the Cabinet Division's Grievance Redress System (GRS) at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), which has 3 months [B0041].

3. **You can also complain straight to the Ministry.** It is free; action starts within 3 to 5 working days, both sides are heard, and it is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].

   You'll need: an application, documents proving the complaint, your full address, and the agency's RL number and address [B0044].

   1. Send them to the Ministry's Enforcement-2 branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255, or to the Ministry at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Old Elephant Road, Dhaka-1000 [B0044, G0045, G0025].
   2. The same branch runs the Ministry's mobile courts (on-the-spot courts under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013), which give immediate remedies to people cheated before or after going; it holds them on its own initiative after gathering information, free, within 7 working days [B0047, G0045].
   3. For advice on any migration problem, call the government's 24-hour call centre for migrants, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

4. **Courts and NGOs.** You can file a case in court at the same time as a BMET complaint, but many workers cannot keep a case going without money or legal aid [G0017]. With NGOs' legal help, some workers have sued agents or brokers under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 [A0080].

   You'll need: the same papers as for the BMET complaint [G0014].

   1. For free legal advice, call the government legal aid helpline, 16699 (+8809612316699 from abroad), run by NLASO (the National Legal Aid Services Organization), which also has a legal aid office in each district [G0050, G0051, G0052].
   2. A village court can order money owed under a contract, receipt or other document to be repaid, up to Tk 3 lakh [G0018].
   3. Migrant NGOs such as WARBE (Welfare Association for the Rights of Bangladeshi Emigrants) and the Ovibashi Odhikar Forum settle some disputes by village mediation and pass cases against agencies to BMET [A0080]; WARBE does not take on fraud by local sub-agents [G0019].

   **Need to know:** which court and type of case to file against an agency or broker, and its cost; where to file at the village court and its fee; and how to contact WARBE and the Ovibashi Odhikar Forum.

### Step 2: Medical check in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical)

Every worker must pass a medical check (the "Bio-Medical") at a centre in Bangladesh accredited by FWCMS before the employer can apply for the visa in Step 3 [W0009]. FWCMS's accredited centres are registered with the local authorities and audited by Bestinet [W0010]. A medical done before 31 October 2026 is not accepted for immigration clearance [Z0159].

1. **Get the medical slip from your agency and test at an approved centre.** The agency that selected you makes your medical slip, the MiGRAMS slip; MiGRAMS is Bestinet's online system in which a worker is registered before the health screening [Z0166, D0024]. No fee may be charged for the slip [Z0168]. Agencies applying for BMET clearance must give the centre the relevant papers and the VDR visa papers (Step 3) for the test [Z0162]. **Need to know:** which comes first in the current recruitment, the medical or the VDR: FWCMS requires the Bio-Medical before the VDR application [W0009], while the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare requires VDR papers at the medical [Z0162]. **Need to know:** how a worker checks that a MiGRAMS slip is real.

   You'll need: the medical slip and papers from your agency, and Tk 8,000, the fee for general workers [Z0162, Z0164, Z0166]. **Need to know:** whether the centre also asks for your passport, how long the test takes, and what paper you get back.

   1. Open [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/), go to Accredited Medical Centres and choose Bangladesh; the list gives each centre's name and address [I0023, W0009].
   2. Go to any centre on the list. No centre works for a particular agency, and you may test at any approved centre, not only the one the agency names [Z0165].
   3. Pay Tk 8,000; no centre may take more [Z0164]. Get a receipt and keep it: a complaint must come with evidence [Z0163].
   4. If you pass, the employer can apply for your visa [W0009].
   5. If you later move to another agency, the first agency must send your medical report on without delay [Z0166].

   **Check it is real:** don't hand your passport or money to anyone for a medical until you have confirmed the centre is approved [Z0161]. A centre that breaks the Ministry's instructions is removed from its approved list [Z0160]. **Need to know:** where the Ministry posts its own list of approved centres for the 2026 recruitment; until then, the FWCMS list above is the one to check [W0009].

   **Scams:** brokers and dishonest agencies have offered unauthorised medicals to get workers' passports and money [Z0161]. Brokers have charged for medicals for jobs that never came [D0022], so test only after the agency gives you a MiGRAMS slip for a real job [Z0166].

   **If a centre asks for more than Tk 8,000, harasses you, or tests without approval, or a broker took your passport or money for a medical:**

   1. Email the Ministry at secretary@probashi.gov.bd with the date, the place, the names of the people or centre, and your evidence (receipt, photos, messages) [Z0163, Z0167]. A sample: "To the Secretary, Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment. On [date], [centre name and address] charged me Tk [amount] for my Malaysia medical, against the fixed fee of Tk 8,000. My agency is [name, RL number]. The receipt is attached. I ask the Ministry to take action and return the extra money. [Name, phone, NID]." [Z0164, Z0167]
   2. Send the same complaint to the Ministry's enforcement branch (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) with your full address and the agency's RL number and address; it is free, action starts within 3 to 5 working days and the complaint is settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].
   3. If an agency is involved, also complain to the Director General of BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) at BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka; it is free, BMET holds a hearing and the case takes 30 working days [B0034, G0026]. Outside Dhaka, hand it to your District Employment and Manpower Office (DEMO, BMET's district office); BMET publishes the list of DEMOs on [its website](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [G0047, B0005].
   4. If a broker kept your passport, report it to the police through the online GD (general diary) at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/), which takes complaints on any matter (how to file it is in Step 4) [I0045].
   5. For help at any time, call the government's 24-hour migrant call centre, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

   The Ministry also runs mobile courts (on-the-spot courts) under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013; the remedy is free and comes within 7 working days [B0047]. **Need to know:** how a worker asks for a mobile court.

   **Need to know:** how a worker can see their own Bio-Medical result.

### Step 3: The employer gets your visa approval (VDR)

1. **The employer applies; you wait in Bangladesh.** After the employer gets its quota approval and pays the levy, it applies online through FWCMS for a VDR (Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference) for you, which FWCMS calls the Calling Visa; you must stay in Bangladesh until it is approved [W0043, W0045, Z0059]. The application includes the Home Ministry approval letter, the levy receipt, a security deposit or guarantee, and your passport copy and photo [W0055]. The employer pays the deposit, visa fee, pass and levy; none of these is yours to pay [W0054]. **What happens in practice:** some employers deduct the levy from workers' wages, often without the workers knowing [F0082].

   **If the levy is taken from your wages:** complain to the Labour Department (JTK) through the Working for Workers (WFW) app on Android or iOS, which takes complaints from foreign workers in confidence and lets you track them, or call 03-8000 8000 or email jtksm@mohr.gov.my [L0163, L0165, M0118]; then tell the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing (contacts in Step 7, item 2) [L0028] and call 16135 [B0083].

2. **Track the approval yourself.**

   You'll need: the employer's company registration number (or, for an individual employer, their Malaysian identity card number) and the application number [W0065]. **Need to know:** who gives the worker the application number.

   1. Open IMI's [MyIMMs eServices](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main?pra) and choose "Status Permohonan Pembantu Rumah Asing / Pekerja Asing" (application status for foreign maids or workers) [W0065].
   2. Enter the two numbers. BARU means being processed, LULUS approved, BAYAR paid, CETAK visa sticker printed, TOLAK rejected, BATAL cancelled and TANGGUH deferred [W0065].
   3. A complete eVDR (the online VDR) for Peninsular Malaysia is meant to be approved within 5 working days; in Sabah it is 14 working days and in Sarawak 30 [C0045, Z0036, Z0037].

   **If the VDR is refused:** an agency that fails to send workers gets no further permissions from the Ministry until it repays what it owes them [W0036]. To claim your money back, complain to BMET and the Ministry as in Step 2 [B0034, B0044]. **Need to know:** whether and how an employer can appeal a refused VDR.

3. **The entry visa is issued in Dhaka.** Once IMI approves, the High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka issues the VDR [W0064]. Worker visa applications go through the High Commission's authorised visa application agencies, not to the High Commission itself [W0061, W0062]. The visa fee for a Bangladesh passport is Tk 600, and the employer pays it [W0063, W0054]. A processing fee of RM105 (about Tk 2,500) and the agency's own charge, set at its discretion, also apply [I0043, W0063, Z0007]. **Need to know:** who pays the processing fee and the agency's charge, the names and addresses of the authorised agencies for worker visas, and how long they take. To ask, write to the High Commission's visa section at dhaka@imi.gov.my or call +88 02 4108 1918; the High Commission is at House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Dhaka, open Sunday to Thursday, 8.30 to 4.30 [W0061, B0121].

### Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates

1. **Get an e-passport valid long enough.** Your passport must have at least 18 to 24 months left [W0003], and the employer can renew your work pass only while it has more than 12 months left [L0047]. A new e-passport is issued for 5 or 10 years [D0049].

   You'll need: your original NID (compulsory if you are over 20) and, for some jobs, your technical certificates (for example a driving licence) [D0054, D0056].

   1. Open [epassport.gov.bd](https://www.epassport.gov.bd/), choose your present-address district and nearest police station, and the portal names the Regional Passport Office where you enrol [I0032, D0055].
   2. Fill in the form online exactly as on your NID; no papers or photo need attesting [D0053].
   3. Pay online by A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay (card, bKash, Nagad or Rocket), or at a bank through A-Challan [D0051]. A 48-page passport valid 5 years costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express); valid 10 years, Tk 5,750, Tk 8,050 or Tk 10,350 [D0049].
   4. Go to that passport office for your photo and fingerprints (enrolment) with the printed application summary showing your appointment, the printed application form, your original NID, and the payment slip if you paid at a bank [I0033].
   5. New passports based on an online-verified NID are issued without police verification [I0009]. Regular delivery takes up to 15 working days, express 7 and super express 2, counted from enrolment [D0050].

   **Scams:** the official fee for each delivery speed is listed above, and the fees are paid only through the official channels [D0049, D0050, D0051]. The head of TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) has said that from application to delivery there is harassment at every step and nothing moves without money [I0046].

   **To complain:**

   1. Call the passport helpline 16445 (09666716445 from abroad), 24 hours a day [I0024].
   2. Contact your passport office's grievance redress officer, who is named with a phone and email on the office's website (for example, the Secretariat office in Dhaka lists an Assistant Director, 01538855234, rpo_secretariat@passport.gov.bd) [I0025].
   3. File on the government's Grievance Redress System at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), which passport office sites link to [I0026].

   **If it is lost or stolen:** file a GD (general diary) quickly at the nearest police station [D0057], or online at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or the Online GD app: register with your NID number, an active mobile number (you get a code by SMS) and a live photo, then choose what was lost, the district, the police station, and the time and place [I0034, I0039]. The station sends you a digital GD copy with the GD number and the investigating officer's details [I0034]. Help: gd@police.gov.bd, 01320001428 (WhatsApp) [I0034]. Re-apply for the passport with a copy of the old one and the GD copy [D0057]. **Need to know:** whether a GD has a fee.

2. **Police clearance only if asked.** A police clearance certificate is not on IMI's list of VDR documents [W0055] or on BMET's clearance document lists [W0030, B0029]. **Need to know:** whether Malaysian employers or agencies still ask Malaysia-bound workers for one.

   If you are asked for one, apply yourself; you'll need a passport valid at least 3 months, a present address that is one of the addresses in your passport, and a scan of your passport data page attested by a first-class gazetted officer [B0105, B0106].

   1. Apply at [pcc.police.gov.bd](https://pcc.police.gov.bd/) [B0105].
   2. Pay Tk 1,500, either by treasury challan under code 1-7301-0001-2681 at any Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank branch, or online by credit or debit card with a service charge [I0040].
   3. The police verify you at your present address [B0107].
   4. Track it by SMS: type PCC S and your reference number and send it to 26969 [B0107].
   5. Collect it with a copy of your passport or of your application [I0042]. If you are abroad, someone else can collect it with an attested authorisation letter giving their name and NID number [B0108].

   **If there is a problem:** contact the PCC helpdesk, Sunday to Thursday, 9 am to 5 pm: 01320001824, 01320001825, help.pcc@police.gov.bd [I0041]. **Need to know:** how long a police clearance takes.

### Step 5: Check your visa and pass are real

1. **The agency:** find it on FWCMS's list and check its licence status on OEP (Step 1) [W0008, X0001].
2. **The job:** find the BMET permission letter for your job on [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) (Step 1) [W0026].
3. **The visa approval:** check the status on MyIMMs (Step 3) [W0065].
4. **At BMET:** take the visa and a copy of the passport page with the visa stamp to BMET at 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka (phone 02-49357972), or to your DEMO (list on [BMET's website](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c)); BMET checks job visas and demand letters for free within 3 working days [B0005, B0032, B0033, G0026, G0047]. BMET's online visa check doesn't cover Malaysia [B0032].
5. **Your BMET clearance (Step 6):** check it at [portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check) [B0058].
6. **After arrival:** IMI's [ePass check](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid?semakan) is meant for authorities and agencies, but shows the name, nationality, pass type and expiry when you enter the ePass number (for example EP141011C23105313148), passport number and nationality; "Pas Tidak Sah" means the pass is not valid [W0066]. **Need to know:** where a worker finds their ePass number.

**If any check shows a fake visa, pass, job or clearance:**

1. Don't pay any more money and don't hand over your passport [Z0161].
2. Complain to the Director General of BMET at BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka, or through your DEMO, with your passport copy, contract, visa, your correspondence with the agency and proof of payment [B0034, G0014, G0026, G0047].
3. Send the same complaint to the Ministry's enforcement branch (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) [B0044].
4. Ask the High Commission of Malaysia whether the visa is real: dhaka@imi.gov.my, +88 02 4108 1918 [W0061, B0121].
5. Report the person who took your money or passport to the police through the online GD [I0045].
6. Call 16135 for help [B0083].

### Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight

1. **Do the compulsory 3-day pre-departure orientation (PDO).** BMET runs it at its training institutes [B0036]; the list of institutes, with each one's address, phone and email, is on [BMET's website](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6) [G0048, G0049]. It covers how to go legally at low cost, how to avoid fraud by brokers, and what to do after arrival [I0003].

   You'll need: your OEP registration (Step 1) and your passport or NID number [B0012, I0007].

   1. Apply online on OEP for the PDO [B0012].
   2. Download your enrollment card at [training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-enrollment-card](https://training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-enrollment-card) by typing your passport or NID number and the captcha [I0007].
   3. Attend all 3 days and pay the fee against a money receipt [B0036]. At Feni Technical Training Centre, for example, the fee is Tk 200, and the centre tells people to pay no more [I0027].
   4. Download your certificate at [training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-certificate](https://training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-certificate) with your passport or NID number [I0008].

   **Check it is real:** get the certificate only from BMET's portal, training.oep.gov.bd, under your own passport or NID number [I0008]. **Need to know:** whether every institute charges Tk 200.

2. **Check the contract before clearance.** BMET's Malaysia permission letters require the agency to sign a contract with you before BMET clearance and give you a Bangla translation attested by BMET [W0030]. Get it at least 2 days before you go, and check the job, the employer's name and address, the workplace, the length of the contract, the monthly pay, hours, overtime, leave, airfare, medical care, compensation for injury or death, food and housing, and return of the body if you die [I0005]. **Need to know:** whether this condition is in every Malaysia permission letter.

   **If there is no contract, no Bangla translation, or the contract differs from the BMET permission letter for your job:** don't go to clearance; complain to BMET's complaint cell or your DEMO and to the Ministry's enforcement branch as in Step 5, and call 16135 [B0034, B0044, B0083].

3. **BMET clearance (the smart card).** The agency applies after the visa is issued; BMET's emigration branch checks the papers and gives clearance within 3 working days [B0026]. For Malaysia the agency must also submit the demand letter, power of attorney and skill certificates, and may clear only workers from BMET's database [W0030]. The clearance costs are part of what you pay inside Bangladesh [R0008]: a Tk 5,500 Wage Earners' Welfare Board fee (the Bangladesh government's welfare board for migrant workers), a one-time Tk 1,000 insurance premium, a Tk 250 clearance fee, and source income tax of Tk 800 (unskilled) or Tk 1,200 (skilled) on a group visa [B0043, B0008, B0028]. The insurance lasts 5 years and the sum insured is Tk 10 lakh [B0008]. Pay only through the proper channel and against a receipt [B0042].

   **If you got your visa yourself,** apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram DEMO with an application, the visa copy, the contract if any, an attested copy of your OEP registration card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and the original pay order for the welfare fee and insurance [B0029].

   **Check it is real:** check your clearance at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [B0058]. The expatriate welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check every departing worker's BMET smart card, and people with no or fake clearance are stopped [B0038, B0128].

4. **The ticket.** The employer pays your air fare both ways, and BMET gives clearance only once that is ensured [W0036, L0141]. An agency representative must travel with each flight or meet workers at the Malaysian airport [W0031].

   **Check the booking is real (PNR scam):** agencies have handed Malaysia-bound workers fake tickets minutes before the flight, and the workers were turned away inside the airport [I0012]. Get the e-ticket from the agency, then open the airline's own website and type the six-character PNR (booking reference) and your name:

   - Biman: Manage Booking, with the PNR and your last name [I0022].
   - Malaysia Airlines: [Manage Booking](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/ph/en/plan-trip/booking-and-services.html), with the booking code and your last name [I0028].
   - US-Bangla: [Manage Your Booking](https://usbair.com/manage-your-booking), with the booking reference or ticket number [I0029].
   - AirAsia: [Check-in](https://www.airasia.com/check-in/en/gb), with the booking number and your first name or surname [I0030].

   **Scam:** workers have been asked for Tk 50,000 more at the last minute [I0013]; the ticket is the employer's cost [W0036]. **If you are asked for money for the ticket or the ticket is fake:** don't pay; complain to BMET's complaint cell or your DEMO and to the Ministry's enforcement branch with the ticket and the agency's details (Step 5), and call 16135 [B0034, B0044, B0083].

5. **Before you fly,** make sure you have your passport, contract, visa, BMET clearance, medical report, ticket and payment receipts [I0004], and the Bangladesh High Commission's contacts in Kuala Lumpur: No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur; phone 03-2604 0946, 48 or 49; labour wing +60 3 2604 0934 [L0032, L0028]. Leave one set of copies of all your papers at home and carry 3 sets; open a bank account in your name and one in a family member's [B0004]. Keep money and your travel and job papers in your cabin bag, and keep the checked bag within 20 kg [I0006]. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].

6. **Register the MDAC within 3 days before you arrive.** Every foreign national must register the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) within 3 days before arriving [V0058]. Singaporeans, diplomatic and official passport holders, permanent residents, long-term pass holders and some border-pass holders are exempt; none of these applies to a worker arriving on a VDR [V0060]. The MDAC is free [N0057, V0065].

   You'll need: your passport, email address, mobile number, arrival and departure dates and flight number [V0061].

   1. Open [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main) within 3 days of your arrival date (the day you submit counts) [V0059, V0061].
   2. Fill in your name, passport number, date and place of birth, nationality, sex, passport expiry, email (twice), mobile number, arrival and departure dates, flight number, mode of travel and last port of embarkation [V0061].
   3. You are sent a PIN; check your registration under "Check Registration" with your passport number, nationality and the PIN [V0062].
   4. At the airport show the MDAC registration and your passport at the Immigration counter [V0059].

   **Scam:** fake MDAC websites charge for the card; IMI has flagged malaysiacard.com as fake, and sponsored search ads lead to others [V0065, N0057]. Use only the IMI address above [N0030]. **Need to know:** how to get money back from a fake MDAC site, and where to report it.

### Step 7: After arrival

1. **Wait for your employer at the airport.** You may enter only through official entry points with your VDR and visa, and the employer must collect you within 6 hours of arrival [W0046, L0041], take you to the hostel [L0034] and pay your transport to the workplace [M0147]. **What happens in practice:** workers have arrived with valid work permits to find the promised job did not exist [A0095, A0014]. **If no one comes or there is no job:** call the agency representative who must meet the flight [W0031], then the High Commission's labour wing (+603 2604 0934, ministermalaysia@probashi.gov.bd, or +601 2290 3252, counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd) [L0028], and the 16135 call centre (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

2. **FOMEMA medical within 30 days.** FOMEMA (FOMEMA Sdn. Bhd.) is the company that appoints the panel clinics for foreign workers' medical checks in Malaysia [Z0008]. The employer must take you to a FOMEMA-registered clinic within 30 days of arrival and pays for it [L0042, M0144], and FOMEMA urges employers to register workers within 72 hours [Z0128]. The fee is RM207 for men and RM217 for women (which includes a pregnancy test), plus 8% SST (sales and service tax); a payment is valid 30 days, after which the employer must register and pay again [Z0125, Z0126, Z0127]. The check includes a mental health assessment, a doctor's examination, a chest X-ray, urine tests including a drug test for opiates, methamphetamine and cannabis, and blood tests including infectious disease screening [Z0135]. Results usually come within 7 working days [Z0129].

   **If the employer hasn't taken you within 30 days:** the work pass (PLKS) is issued only after FOMEMA certifies you fit [L0043], so complain to the Labour Department through the WFW app or 03-8000 8000 [L0163, L0165, M0118], tell the labour wing (item 1) [L0028] and call 16135 [B0083].

   **Check your own result:** open IMI's [FOMEMA Application Status](https://eservices.imi.gov.my/myimms/FomemaStatus?lang=en), type your passport number and choose BANGLADESH as citizenship [I0001]. The employer can check the result on the FOMEMA portal [I0002]. **What happens in practice:** neither employers nor workers get to see the medical report itself [D0028].

   **If you are found unfit:** an unfit worker gets no PLKS and is sent home with a Check-Out Memo (Memo Periksa Keluar, the employer's application for a foreign worker to leave for home) [L0043, L0071]. Only the employer or agency can appeal, within 2 weeks of the result, on portal.fomema.my; it pays FOMEMA a fee for the appeal, and the case is closed if the extra test results don't arrive within 4 weeks [Z0131, Z0132, I0044]. Confirmed tuberculosis, malaria, pregnancy, syphilis, HIV and hepatitis B can't be appealed [Z0133]. Ask the employer in writing to appeal, and tell the labour wing (item 1) [L0028]. The employer pays the air fare both ways [W0036]. **Need to know:** the appeal fee, and whether recruitment money is refunded to a worker sent home unfit.

   Buying a different passport to sit FOMEMA again is a crime: making or using a false passport carries 2 to 10 years in jail (see What can get you jailed) [L0089].

3. **PLKS sticker and i-Kad.** When IMI approves your PLKS and the employer pays, the PLKS sticker is printed (CETAK on the MyIMMs status) [W0065]. Every PLKS holder gets an i-Kad, the foreign worker identity card, free and without applying; the employer collects it from the Immigration office 7 days after IMI's email [L0126, L0127]. Carry it at all times and show it when asked [L0128]. **If the employer doesn't give you the i-Kad:** complain to the Labour Department through the WFW app or 03-8000 8000 [L0163, L0165, M0118], then the labour wing (item 1) [L0028].

4. **Keep your own passport.** The employer may not keep your passport; you hand it over only for the medical, the PLKS, the i-Kad and renewal, and it must be returned [L0149, L0151, M0143]. Holding someone else's passport without lawful authority is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [K0045, I0031]. **What happens in practice:** employers keeping passports is still widespread [F0084].

   **If your employer keeps your passport:**

   1. Ask for it back in writing and keep a copy [L0151].
   2. Complain to the Labour Department (JTK) through the WFW app, or call 03-8000 8000 or email jtksm@mohr.gov.my [L0163, L0165, M0118].
   3. Tell the High Commission's labour wing (item 1) [L0028].
   4. For unpaid wages too, follow If you aren't paid.

   **Need to know:** whether a report to the Malaysian police or to IMI gets a passport back, and where a worker makes it.

5. **Wages into your own bank account.** Wages must be paid monthly, by the 7th of the next month, into a bank account in your name [L0131, L0132]; cash is allowed only if you ask in writing and the Labour Department approves [L0104, L0105]. The employer is expected to open the account [L0034]. At Maybank, for example, a foreigner opens a savings account at a branch with a passport, may be asked for the work permit, visa and an employer's letter, and needs at least RM250; it can't be opened in the app, which needs a MyKad, the Malaysian identity card [F0011, F0012, F0014, F0015].

6. **SIM card.** Register every prepaid SIM in person at a mobile operator's service centre or authorised dealer with your original passport and work-permit document; copies aren't accepted and nobody can register for you [F0001, F0002, F0003, F0004, F0013]. You can have at most 2 prepaid SIMs with each operator, and you hear within 12 hours that the SIM is active [F0005, F0007]. Don't give your documents or codes to anyone offering to "help"; each operator offers a free check of all numbers registered in your name [F0009, F0010]. **Need to know:** whether the "work permit document" means the i-Kad.

7. **Social security, insurance and the retirement fund.** Your employer must register you with PERKESO (SOCSO, Malaysia's social security organisation) for the Employment Injury Scheme, which covers accidents, work diseases and death at work, and the Invalidity Scheme, which covers critical illness or death in Malaysia before age 60 from any cause [M0166, M0167, M0168]. The employer pays 1.25% of your wages for the first and 0.5% for the second, and you pay 0.5% [M0170, M0171]. Each registered worker gets a 12-digit Foreign Workers Social Security number [M0184]; SOCSO's line is 1-300-22-8000 [M0179]. Failing to register or pay is an offence for the employer [M0174].

   You must also be covered by SPIKPA, the compulsory foreign workers' hospital and surgery insurance, which the employer buys as part of the PLKS application [I0035, F0065]. The premium is RM120 a year; cover starts once you are registered and the premium is paid [I0035]. The employer may pay the premium first and deduct it from your wages, with the Labour Department's permission [I0036, I0021]. At a government hospital you show your valid passport and pay no deposit, and you pay only the part of a bill above the cover limit [I0019, I0020]. An unpaid government hospital bill blocks the renewal of your PLKS [I0020]. **Need to know:** the current annual cover limit.

   Since October 2025 you and your employer each pay 2% of your wages into the EPF (Employees Provident Fund, the retirement fund), and the employer may deduct your share from your pay [I0014, I0015, I0016]. Domestic helpers are not covered [I0016]. EPF registers you automatically; go to any EPF office, without an appointment, to update your details and give your thumbprint [I0017].

   **When you leave Malaysia, take out your EPF savings (Leaving Country Withdrawal)** [J0017]:

   1. Get the Check-Out Memo from the Immigration Department, which is your proof of leaving [J0020, I0037].
   2. Prepare form KWSP 9K (AHL) and its checklist, a copy of your valid passport, and a bank document confirming the account holder's details; have the copies certified by an EPF officer, your High Commission or your employer [J0018, J0019, I0037, J0025].
   3. Apply at the self-service terminal at an EPF office; this withdrawal can't be made online [J0022].
   4. The money is paid into an active account at an EPF panel bank in your name [J0023, J0024]. If you have already left, you can post the application to EPF, Jabatan Pengurusan Transaksi, Menara KWSP, No.1 Persiaran Kwasa Utama, Kwasa Damansara, Seksyen U4, 40150 Shah Alam, Selangor [J0026]. EPF's line is +603-8922 6000 [J0027]. **Need to know:** how long the payment takes.

8. **If you came without BMET clearance,** join the Wage Earners' Welfare Board from Malaysia to get its insurance and help: apply online at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/) with your passport and work permit; the High Commission's labour wing processes it, and the fee is the equivalent of Tk 6,500 in ringgit, life insurance included [B0057]. Workers with BMET clearance are already covered; check at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [B0058].

### Your rights at work

The Employment Act 1955 is the main labour law in Peninsular Malaysia [L0107]. Your contract under the Bangladesh-Malaysia agreement is in Malay, Bangla and English, and if the texts differ the English one counts [L0153]. Some employers replace the contract signed in Bangladesh with a different one on arrival, in a language the worker can't read [J0070]. If any right below is broken, the complaint routes are under If you aren't paid.

**Hours and overtime.** You can't be made to work more than 8 hours a day or 45 hours a week, more than 5 hours in a row without a break of at least 30 minutes, or more than 12 hours a day except in emergencies [M0003, M0004, L0092]. Overtime is only at the employer's request and with your agreement [L0134]. It is paid at least 1.5 times your hourly rate; for a monthly-paid worker the hourly rate is the monthly wage divided by 26, then divided by your normal daily hours [M0001, M0007].

**Rest days and public holidays.** You get one rest day a week [L0135]. Work beyond normal hours on a rest day is paid at least 2 times the hourly rate [M0013]. You get 11 paid public holidays a year [M0014]. If you work on one, you get 2 days' wages on top of the holiday pay, and overtime that day is paid at least 3 times the hourly rate [M0019, M0020].

**Leave.** Paid annual leave is 8 days a year with under 2 years' service, 12 days with 2 to 5 years, and 16 days after 5 years [L0109]. Paid sick leave is 14, 18 or 22 days a year on the same service bands when no hospital stay is needed, and up to 60 days a year in all when a doctor certifies that hospital care is needed [M0023]. Sick leave needs a doctor's certificate, and you must tell the employer within 48 hours; otherwise it counts as absence without permission [M0025]. Annual leave you haven't taken is paid when the contract ends, unless you are dismissed for misconduct [M0022]. Women get 98 days' maternity leave [L0112]. A married man gets 7 days' paid paternity leave for each birth, up to 5 births, after 12 months with the employer; he must tell the employer at least 30 days before the expected birth [L0095].

**Pregnancy.** An employer may not dismiss a pregnant worker except for wilful breach of contract, misconduct or closure of the business [M0240, L0094]. How pregnancy affects the work pass is under Bringing your family.

**Pay.** Wages are paid monthly, by the 7th of the next month, into a bank account in your name [L0131, L0132, M0034]. Cash is allowed only if you ask in writing and the Labour Department approves [L0104, L0105]. Overtime, rest-day and holiday pay is due by the end of the next wage period [M0030]. Your basic wage must meet the minimum wage and be the same as a local worker gets for the same job [L0130, M0152, M0153]. The minimum wage is RM1,700 a month, or RM8.72 an hour; by the day it is RM65.38 for a 6-day week, RM78.46 for a 5-day week and RM98.08 for a 4-day week [Z0137]. It applies to every employer, whatever its size [Z0138, Z0139]. A worker paid only by piece, task, trip or commission must still get at least RM1,700 a month [Z0140]. The rate is set by a Minimum Wages Order made by the Human Resources Minister under the National Wages Consultative Council Act 2011 [J0106]. The government has agreed to raise the minimum to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, with small and medium businesses selling less than RM50 million a year exempted [R0002]. **Need to know:** whether the Minimum Wages Order setting RM2,000 has been made.

**Deductions.** The employer may deduct only what the Employment Act allows [M0044]. Deductions for rent, food or payments to anyone else need your written request and the Labour Department's approval, and the Labour Department approves rent and food deductions only if they benefit you [M0047, M0049]. All deductions in a month together may not exceed half of that month's wages [M0050, L0137]. In Sarawak no deduction may be made for recruitment or repatriation costs, and foreign workers must get the same rate as local workers [L0170, L0169].

**Housing.** The employer gives housing with basic amenities, as the Employees' Minimum Standards of Housing, Accommodations and Amenities Act 1990 (Act 446) requires [L0138, M0146]. The housing must have a Certificate for Accommodation from the Labour Department; men and women are housed separately; and the person in charge of the housing must take a worker who is ill to a clinic or hospital [M0193, M0201, M0203]. Each worker gets a bed of his own with a mattress at least 4 inches thick, a pillow and a blanket, and a lockable cupboard for valuables such as the passport that he can open at any time [J0089]. A bedroom gives at least 3.6 square metres per worker, or 3 square metres of sleeping area per worker in a dormitory, and no more than 6 workers share a bathroom and toilet, or 15 in a dormitory [J0087, J0088]. Rent can be taken from wages only within limits set by regulations [M0197, M0198], and the most an employer may collect is RM100 a month per worker [J0090]. If your job ends with less than 4 weeks' notice, you may stay in the housing for up to 4 weeks [M0205]. The Labour Department can inspect housing at any time without notice and question the workers [M0210, M0212]. **Need to know:** whether the RM100 rent limit has been changed since 2020.

**Your passport.** The employer may not keep your passport [L0149, M0143]. You hand it over only for the medical check, the PLKS (Temporary Employment Visit Pass, the work pass), the i-Kad (the foreign-worker identity card) and the yearly renewal, and it must be given back each time [L0151]. Anyone who holds another person's passport without lawful authority can be fined up to RM10,000, jailed for up to 5 years, or both [K0045, K0046], and this offence may be used to prosecute an employer or agent [J0082]. If the employer keeps your passport, ask for it back [L0151]; if it refuses, complain on the Working for Workers (WFW) app, to the Labour Office or to the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing (see If you aren't paid), or to SUHAKAM (the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia; see route 4 there) [L0163, L0021, J0094].

**Forced labour.** An employer who threatens, deceives or forces you to work and stops you leaving the workplace can be fined up to RM100,000, jailed up to 2 years, or both [L0100, L0101]. If this happens to you, the 24-hour emergency lines of Tenaganita (a Malaysian NGO for migrant workers) take cases of abuse and rescue, and the High Commission's labour wing and SUHAKAM take complaints (contacts under If you aren't paid) [J0010, L0021, J0094].

**Ending the job.** Under the agreement contract, either side gives 2 months' notice or pays 2 months' wages instead [L0144]. After 12 months with the employer, a worker whose job is ended by the employer (except for misconduct) is due termination benefits of at least 10 days' wages for each year of service under 2 years, 15 days for 2 to 5 years and 20 days for 5 years or more, pro rata for part years [J0053, M0056].

**Savings and pension.** Your employer must register you with the EPF (Employees Provident Fund, Malaysia's retirement savings fund) and pay in 2% of your monthly wage, and 2% is taken from your wage, by the 15th of the next month [J0011, J0013, J0014]. You take all of it out when you leave Malaysia for good (see Leaving at the end of the job) [J0017]. PERKESO (SOCSO, the social security organisation) covers you for work accidents, and for critical illness or death in Malaysia before age 60 from any cause; it pays invalidity and survivors' pensions (see Step 7 and If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job) [M0168, M0172]. In Bangladesh, the government's Probash pension scheme is open to any Bangladeshi aged 18 to 50 working abroad [J0071, J0072]:

1. You'll need: a bank account, a mobile number and email address, and a debit or credit card that pays in foreign currency [J0078, J0086, J0073, J0099]. You can name a nominee, the person who gets the money if you die [J0101, J0077]. **Need to know:** the full list of details the registration form asks for, whether registering costs anything, and how a worker without a card can pay.
2. Register online yourself, in your own name, at [upension.gov.bd](https://www.upension.gov.bd/), choosing the Probash scheme; no third person is involved [J0102, J0072]. Your unique pensioner ID, contribution rate and payment dates come by SMS and email [J0085, J0086]. Every Sonali Bank branch in Bangladesh works as the pension authority's front office [J0100]. **Need to know:** what to do if registration or payment fails.
3. Choose a monthly amount: Tk 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500 or 10,000 [J0081]. You can pay 3, 9 or 12 months at once, by card into Sonali Bank's account, and 2.5% is added to what you pay from abroad [J0076, J0073, J0074].
4. What you get: after 10 years of unbroken payments, a monthly pension is paid into your bank account from age 60 without applying; for example Tk 1,000 a month for 20 years gives an estimated Tk 4,927 a month [J0075, J0080]. If you die, everything paid in, with profit, goes to your nominee [J0077].

### How long you can stay

**12 months at a time, up to 10 years.** The PLKS is valid for 12 months [L0044]. Only the employer can renew it; the employer applies from 3 months before expiry and pays any penalty if it is late [L0079, L0139]. For a renewal your passport must be valid for more than 12 months [L0047], you need a FOMEMA (the company that runs foreign workers' medical checks) medical check every year [Z0130], and the employer needs SPIKPA hospital insurance for you, except in plantations [M0220]. A renewal filed after the pass has expired is treated as an overstay [L0045]. In total you can work up to 10 years; a worker registered under the 6P legalisation programme can work only 3 [L0050, M0229, M0157]. **Need to know:** where Bangladeshi workers' renewals are processed now that work-pass applications go through FWCMS (the Foreign Workers Centralised Management System, Immigration's online system) [Z0002].

**If the employer doesn't renew.** Without a valid pass you can be arrested (see What can get you jailed) [K0015].

1. Check your pass on Immigration's ePass check: enter the ePass number, your passport number and nationality, and it shows the pass type and expiry date, or that the pass is not valid (see Step 5) [W0066].
2. If the pass is about to expire and the employer hasn't renewed it, email the High Commission's labour wing, which takes any labour welfare problem, and contact Tenaganita (contacts under If you aren't paid) [L0021, J0007].

**Need to know:** whether the Labour Department takes a complaint about a pass that isn't renewed.

**No family, no marriage, no permanent residence.** PLKS holders can't bring family to live in Malaysia and may not marry a Malaysian or another foreigner while on the PLKS [L0053, L0056, E0138, E0139]. Government policy is that foreign workers can't apply for permanent residence and must go home when the pass ends [J0055, J0056].

### What can get you jailed

**Being without a valid pass or passport.** Entering illegally, or being unable to show a valid passport and pass when an officer asks, carries a fine of up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, and up to 6 strokes of the cane; it can instead be settled with a compound (an out-of-court fine) of up to RM3,000 [L0083, L0084]. Carry your i-Kad at all times [L0128]. Staying after your pass has expired or been cancelled is punished by a fine of at least RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [K0015, R0004], or it can be settled by a compound of up to RM3,000 [L0086]. Breaking any condition of your pass, such as working for another employer, ends the pass at once [K0025, L0055].

**After arrest.** A person whose pass has expired or been cancelled is removed from Malaysia whether or not they are prosecuted, and can be held in custody for as long as the removal takes [K0018, K0020]. There is no appeal against removal in that case [K0019]. People ordered out are held in Immigration's 13 depots (detention centres) [L0181]. Detainees may contact family by phone, letter or visits, with the depot commander's approval [F0055].

1. Family or friends who want to visit need a letter of permission from an Immigration officer first: for the Kuala Lumpur area (Klang Valley) from the Depot and Deportation Management Division at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya, elsewhere from the state Immigration Enforcement Division. Giving the detainee's body number or the police report number helps Immigration find the person [L0179, L0180]. **Need to know:** whether the letter costs anything and how long it takes.
2. Contact the High Commission: its consular line is +603 2604 0949 and the labour wing's emails are under If you aren't paid [B0118, L0021]. Its officials visit detention camps to give consular help [H0023].
3. Going home: a detainee with a valid passport needs only an air ticket, and the camp arranges the flight and the trip to the airport. For one without a passport, the camp sends a travel permit application to the High Commission, which issues it to the camp, free of charge [L0017, L0018].

**False papers.** Making, forging or using a forged visa or pass means a fine of at least RM30,000, 5 to 10 years' jail and up to 6 strokes [L0088]; a false passport, a fine of RM15,000 to RM100,000, 2 to 10 years' jail and up to 6 strokes [L0089].

**Drugs.** Drug trafficking is punished by death or life imprisonment, and anyone not sentenced to death also gets at least 12 strokes of the cane [J0052, K0041, L0155]. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].

**Banned goods.** Obscene pictures or films on any media, daggers and flick knives, and cloth printed with verses of the Quran may not be brought in; declare anything dutiable and expect your bags to be opened [L0158, L0159, L0160, L0156].

### If you aren't paid

**First collect proof.** Workers who complain often have no payslips, contract or proof of the agreed wage, because the employer keeps them [J0068]. Photograph your contract, payslips, attendance records, passport and pass, and the employer's name and address; the Labour Department asks for these [M0113, J0046]. You can find the company's registered name and address on [SSM e-Info](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/), the Companies Commission's search, by company name or registration number; a company profile costs RM15.40 [M0113, W0067]. Where you work for a subcontractor, the main contractor is also liable for up to 3 months' unpaid wages if you complain within 90 days of the wages falling due [M0100].

Use the routes in this order. You can use several at once.

**1. Complain on the Working for Workers (WFW) app** (the Ministry of Human Resources' complaint channel for all workers, local or foreign) [L0163, L0165].

You'll need: a smartphone; your name, mobile number, passport number, gender and nationality; and the company's name, phone number and workplace address (state, district and postcode) [J0003, J0004].

1. Install "WFW" from Google Play or the Apple App Store [L0167]. The app is the Ministry of Human Resources' own; its official download page is [workforworkers.com.my](https://www.workforworkers.com.my/sapn-portal/index) [J0092].
2. Register with the details above [J0003].
3. Choose the type of complaint, such as unpaid wages, late wages, below minimum wage, no overtime pay, unlawful deductions or housing problems, and describe what happened [J0005, L0163].
4. What you get back: the complaint goes to the labour authorities, your identity is kept confidential, and you can follow its status in the app [L0165, L0166]. You can complain at any time, without going to a Labour Office [J0093]. **Need to know:** whether the app costs anything, whether it is in Bangla, how long a complaint takes to be handled, and what to do if it gets no response.
5. Reality check: workers without payslips or contracts struggle to prove their case [J0068]. Workers have been warned by employers' agents not to complain [L0192], and some who complained were falsely reported as absconded or had their visas cancelled [J0066, J0057]; see If your employer reports you absent (lari).

**2. Write to the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing** in Kuala Lumpur (No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur) [B0118].

You'll need: the details on the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's checklist for unpaid wages (see route 5) [B0048].

1. Email counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, ss.lab.kl@gmail.com or fsl.bhc.kl@gmail.com, or call +601 2290 3252 [L0021, B0117]. Workers in a group can send one email together [F0018].
2. What happens: the labour wing calls the employer in, visits the workplace and attends Labour Department hearings for the workers; when the employer ignores it, it takes the case to the Ministry of Human Resources [F0019, F0020, L0193].
3. If the labour wing doesn't act, apply to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (route 5), which writes to the labour wing asking it to act [B0048]. **Need to know:** how long the labour wing takes to reply and whether it charges anything.

**3. File a claim at the Labour Department (the Labour Court).** The Labour Court is the Labour Department's (JTK's) free tribunal for wage claims, with no upper limit on the amount [M0232, M0081, M0093].

You'll need: your passport (or a copy if the employer holds it), the employer's name and address, your payslips, your contract or appointment letter, any termination letter, your arrival date, and the names of any witnesses [M0113, J0046, M0089].

1. Get free advice first if you need it: call 03-8000 8000 or email jtksm@mohr.gov.my [M0118].
2. Go in person to the nearest Labour Office (Pejabat Tenaga Kerja) and give a sworn statement of your claim to an officer [M0112, M0087]. A written complaint by letter or email is also accepted; JTK's email is jtksm@mohr.gov.my [J0036, M0118]. **Need to know:** the official list of Labour Office addresses.
3. The officer sets a mention date (a first short hearing), summons the employer and tells you the date, time and place of the hearing; bring your witnesses [M0114, M0089].
4. Attend every date. If the employer was summoned and doesn't come, the claim can be decided without it [M0091]. Workers with the same claim against one employer can file together and send one or more to represent the rest [M0092]. You can't bring a lawyer, but a trade union can help you [J0037].
5. What you get back: a written order to pay. Unpaid amounts carry interest of up to 8% a year, and if the employer doesn't pay, the Labour Department enforces the order through the courts, for example by seizing and selling property [M0085, M0096, M0116]. Either side can appeal to the High Court within 14 days [M0115].
6. Time: cases settled by negotiation take about a month on average, and cases that go to a hearing about 3 months [J0041]. A dismissal for misconduct must be challenged within 60 days of being told of it [M0083].
7. If your pass ends while the case is running: ask the Labour Office for a letter showing your case is pending, and take it to Immigration to apply for a Special Pass, a one-month pass that can be extended; workers with such a letter usually get the pass and at least two extensions [J0031, J0033]. Immigration's Foreign Workers Division decides a Special Pass application within 3 working days, and the fee is RM100 [C0046, J0091, L0185]. A Special Pass doesn't let you work [J0034]. **Need to know:** whether a worker can apply for the Special Pass himself or needs someone to apply for him, and whether each monthly extension costs RM100 again.
8. Reality check: employers often delay the case until the worker's pass runs out and he has to leave, which is why the pending-case letter and Special Pass in step 7 matter [J0040, J0033]. The Labour Department's complaint pages and hotline are not in Bangla [J0038]. The employer may not stop you from going to the Labour Court [M0099].

**4. Get free help in Malaysia.**

- Tenaganita (a Malaysian NGO that takes individual migrant workers' cases): office at 12 Jalan 6/11, 46000 Petaling Jaya, Selangor, +603 7770 3671 or +603 7770 3691, Monday to Friday 9:30 to 6:00, general@tenaganita.net; 24-hour emergency lines for abuse, rescue, arrest or detention, +6012 335 0512 and +6012 339 5350 [J0007, J0008, J0009, J0010]. NGOs help workers gather the documents a Labour Department claim needs [J0044].
- Bar Council Legal Aid Centres give free legal advice to non-citizens, in every Peninsular state; walk in or call for an appointment and bring your documents [J0047, J0048, J0051]. Kuala Lumpur: Unit 3.03, Level 3, Wisma Badan Peguam Malaysia, 2 Leboh Pasar Besar, 50050 Kuala Lumpur, +603 2691 1121 or 2692 1122, lac.kl@malaysianbar.org.my [J0049, J0050]. They give advice and help negotiate a settlement only; they represent people only occasionally and only in criminal cases, and representation may cost money [J0045, J0047].
- SUHAKAM (the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia) takes complaints from anyone, including non-citizens, about rights abuses such as exploitation or unpaid wages, and can investigate and make recommendations to the authorities [J0094, J0097]. Email aduan@suhakam.org.my, call +603-2612 5635 or 5636, write to 17th Floor, Menara Aras Raya, Jalan Raja Laut, Kuala Lumpur, or click "New Complaint" on suhakam.org.my, fill the starred fields and submit; save the Ticket ID it shows to track the complaint [J0095, J0096].
- The Malaysian Trades Union Congress (MTUC) has run a migrant worker resource centre at 10-5 Jalan USJ 9/5T, Subang Business Centre, 47620 Subang Jaya, Selangor, +603 8024 2953 or +6017 680 9384 [J0098]. **Need to know:** whether this centre still takes migrant workers' cases.

**5. Apply to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare in Bangladesh** (you or your family can do this).

You'll need: what happened; proof that you are a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID number); the employer's name and address; and what you are owed [B0048].

1. Send the application to the Ministry's Welfare-1 branch: saswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030251 [B0048]. It is free [B0048].
2. What you get back: within 7 working days the Ministry writes to the labour wing or the Wage Earners' Welfare Board asking them to act, and tells you by letter [B0048].
3. For advice at any time, the expatriates' call centre is 16135 in Bangladesh (free) or +880 9610102030 from abroad [B0083].

**If you were injured at work,** compensation is claimed through PERKESO; the steps are under If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job [M0168]. To claim from the employer, file a case at the Labour Office with your payslips, contract and attendance record, and give the High Commission a copy [L0037].

### If you want to change employer

**You can't apply yourself.** PLKS holders may not change employer or sector on their own, and the agreement contract bars changing jobs [L0055, W0052, L0146]. Only a registered employer or its appointed company representative may deal with Immigration; using an agent for this is an offence [J0062, J0061].

**When a change is allowed.** A move to a new employer is allowed only if your employer has been declared bankrupt, has closed, has suffered a disaster such as a fire or economic crisis and can't keep its workers, has been restructured or renamed, or the employer has died [M0123, M0124]. The new employer must be in the same sector and allowed to hire foreign workers [M0126]. It is not open to workers in their 10th year or later, workers legalised through 6P, the Labour Recalibration programme (RTK) or any other legalisation programme, or domestic helpers [M0127, Z0009]. Your PLKS must have at least 3 months left [M0130]. **Need to know:** whether a worker can now move to another sector: Immigration says this needs the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission first [J0063], and a 2025 decision to allow cross-sector moves was reported [F0077].

**How it is done** (by the two employers):

1. Both employers register on ePPAx (eppax.gov.my, the Labour Department's online system for employers' foreign-worker applications); the old employer applies and selects the new one, which uploads its documents within 7 days [M0121, Z0010].
2. The Labour Department branch investigates and issues a support letter [M0121, M0128].
3. The old employer (or its heir) fills in the Ministry of Human Resources form, which is free, and submits it with the checklist documents at the counter of the Ministry's foreign workers division: Level 5, Setia Perkasa 3, Kompleks Setia Perkasa, 62546 Putrajaya, 03-8886 6501, Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm, with queue numbers given only from 8.30 am to 12 noon [M0132, M0134, M0142].

**Need to know:** how long a change of employer takes.

### If your employer reports you absent (lari)

**What counts.** You are treated as having run away (lari) if you leave the workplace without telling the employer, intending to escape, or don't come back on the agreed date after a trip home [L0057, L0058, M0213].

**What the employer does.** The employer reports you at an Immigration office with its ID, a copy of your passport, the runaway form and a police report [L0059, M0215]. It must also tell the Labour Department within 14 days [L0099, M0066]. You are blacklisted, and the employer's security bond is forfeited [L0060, L0061, M0216]. When a worker quits, Immigration normally cancels the pass immediately [J0035], and you then have no valid pass (see What can get you jailed).

**False reports.** Some employers falsely report workers as absconded near the end of the contract to avoid paying the fare home, or after the worker complains [J0065, J0066].

**What you can do.**

1. Keep proof that you were working and of any complaint you made: photographs of your pass, payslips and attendance records, and the details of any WFW or Labour Office complaint [M0113, L0166].
2. If you leave because of abuse or unpaid wages, file at the Labour Office and email the High Commission's labour wing (see If you aren't paid) [L0037, L0021]. A Labour Office letter showing a pending complaint usually gets you a Special Pass to stay legally while the case runs [J0033].
3. If you are arrested, family or friends can call Tenaganita's 24-hour emergency line and email the High Commission's labour wing [J0010, L0021].

**Need to know:** how a foreign worker finds out whether they have been reported or blacklisted (Immigration's online blacklist check is for Malaysian citizens only [J0001]), and how to clear a false report.

### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **The employer applies for your Check-Out Memo** (Memo Periksa Keluar, Immigration's approval for a foreign worker to leave for home) with the form, your original passport and a ticket home; you leave on the date and through the exit point on the memo [L0071, L0072, L0073]. Until the employer does this, it stays responsible for you [L0074].
2. **The employer pays your ticket home** when the contract ends, when it ends the contract early (except for your own breach), when you leave because it broke the contract, and after serious work injury or illness [L0141, L0142, M0149]. If you resign or are dismissed for misconduct, you pay [L0143, M0150].
3. **Collect what you are owed.** If you are sent home early, the employer must pay all outstanding wages [L0152]. Untaken annual leave is paid [M0022], and termination benefits are due if the employer ended the job after 12 months (see Your rights at work) [J0053].
4. **Take out your EPF savings before you fly.**

   You'll need: Form KWSP 9K (AHL) with its checklist, a copy of your valid passport, the Check-Out Memo, and a bank document confirming the account holder's details for an active account at an EPF panel bank, in the same passport details as EPF holds [J0018, J0019, J0020, J0024, J0028]. If you don't have your passport, a temporary travel permit from the High Commission is accepted [J0021].

   1. Apply as soon as you get the Check-Out Memo [J0029].
   2. Apply in person at the self-service terminal (SST) at an EPF office [J0022]. Copies must be certified by an EPF officer, the High Commission or your employer [J0025]. EPF's helpline is +603-8922 6000 [J0027]. **Need to know:** EPF's official list of offices, whether the withdrawal costs anything, and how long payment takes.
   3. What you get back: all your EPF savings, paid in ringgit into your Malaysian bank account; keep the account open until the money arrives. If payment to the account fails, EPF issues a banker's cheque [J0017, J0023, J0029, J0105].
   4. If you have already left Malaysia without applying, use EPF's overseas application: post the forms to EPF (Jabatan Pengurusan Transaksi, Menara KWSP, No.1 Persiaran Kwasa Utama, Kwasa Damansara, Seksyen U4, 40150 Shah Alam, Selangor), with copies certified by a Malaysian high commission or a notary; the money is sent by bank transfer [J0103, J0026, J0025, J0023].
   5. Your EPF account is then closed; if you come back to work, you register again [J0016].
5. **To leave Malaysia** you need a passport valid at least 6 months and a valid pass [L0186]. Some employers falsely report workers as absconded near the end of the contract (see If your employer reports you absent) [J0065].

### Going home on leave

Bangladeshi workers get a multiple-entry visa as part of the yearly PLKS renewal, without a separate application [H0027, H0028]. Agree your return date with the employer: not coming back on the agreed date counts as running away (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0058, M0214]. **Need to know:** Immigration's own written rules on the multiple-entry visa, and how a worker checks one has been issued.

### Bringing your family

PLKS workers can't bring family to live in Malaysia [L0053, E0138]. See Family passes for the routes open to professionals, and Having a child in Malaysia for registering a birth [S0114].

**Pregnancy and the work pass.** Women workers' FOMEMA medical includes a pregnancy test, and the medical is now done every year [Z0126, Z0130]. A confirmed pregnancy makes the worker unfit, and that result can't be appealed [Z0134]. A worker found unfit is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [L0043]. The employer still may not dismiss a pregnant worker except for wilful breach of contract, misconduct or closure [M0240]. **Need to know:** what happens to a worker who becomes pregnant between two yearly medicals.

### Scams

**Done to workers.** Report a scam in Bangladesh to BMET: online at ovijog.bmet.gov.bd, naming the agency or person who took the money and uploading receipts and papers, or by application to BMET's Director General; it is free and BMET aims to settle it within 30 working days [G0023, B0034]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's enforcement branch also takes complaints against agencies (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) [B0044]. In Malaysia, email the High Commission's labour wing (see If you aren't paid) [L0021].

- **Fraud in BOESL's name.** BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the state recruiting company) has no agents, takes no money before final selection, and then only by bank pay order [W0068].
- **Agency approvals on social media.** Posts claiming Malaysia has approved particular Bangladeshi agencies have been false [W0021]. **Need to know:** where to check officially which agencies may currently recruit for Malaysia.
- **The fee video.** Agencies have made workers record a video at their Dhaka office saying they paid only the official fee, when they paid far more [J0060]. Keep your own proof of every payment for a BMET complaint [G0014].
- **Fake work passes and "agents" for permits in Malaysia.** Rings sell foreign workers fake e-PLKS (electronic work pass) copies for about RM150 and fake police reports for about RM70 [D0045]. Only your employer can renew or deal with your pass at Immigration; anyone else offering to do it is breaking the law [J0062, J0061, L0079]. Check your pass yourself (see Step 5) [W0066].
- **No job on arrival.** The High Commission makes Malaysian employers sign a commitment that workers will be put to work at once and not left idle [W0059]; if this is broken, email the labour wing [L0021].
- **Pass not renewed.** See How long you can stay.

**Tried by workers, and punishable.** Forged visas, passes and passports carry jail and caning (see What can get you jailed) [L0088, L0089]. Bangladeshi workers who failed the FOMEMA medical have bought forged passports to sit it again; Immigration broke one such ring [D0035, N0035].

## Work visa: domestic worker

Not open to Bangladeshis. Immigration's list of approved source countries for foreign domestic helpers is Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Laos and Nepal, and Bangladesh is not on it [L0194, L0076].

## Student visa (Student Pass)

A Student Pass lets you stay in Malaysia to study full time at an approved institution; it is not a work visa [S0023, S0042, S0013]. Applications go through EMGS (Education Malaysia Global Services, a company under Malaysia's Ministry of Higher Education, KPT), for public universities and private institutions alike [Z0013, S0002, S0060]. The Immigration Department of Malaysia (IMI) gives the final approval [S0003].

### Step 1: Check the institution and the course

Do these checks before you pay anyone, an agent included. Education agents are paid a commission by institutions for each student, so a free consultation is a sales pitch and colleges paying higher commission get recommended first [O0020].

1. **Check the institution may take foreign students.** A private institution needs approval from KPT, an accredited programme, and the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission to take international students; a public university needs no separate check [S0001]. KPT's Department of Higher Education (JPT) publishes the list of private institutions holding that permission and updates it every month [O0001, O0042].
   1. You'll need: the institution's exact name and the address of the campus where you'll study, as the list gives both [O0003].
   2. Go to [jpt.mohe.gov.my, Senarai Daftar dan Statistik IPT Swasta](https://jpt.mohe.gov.my/portal/index.php/ms/ipt/ipt-swasta/25-senarai-daftar-dan-statistik-ipt-swasta) and click the download icon beside "Senarai Daftar IPTS Kelulusan KDN", the list of private institutions with Home Ministry approval [O0001]. It opens a Google Drive folder with one folder per year; open "Tahun 2026" [O0040]. Inside, each month has a numbered pair of files, one marked "AKTIF" (active) and one "TIDAK AKTIF" (inactive); open the AKTIF file with the highest number (in November 2026, file 7, the list up to 31 July 2026, updated 20 August 2026) [O0041, O0003, O0004].
   3. Search the PDF for the institution's name and check the campus address matches the offer letter [O0003].
   4. Also open the TIDAK AKTIF file with the same number, and the page's list of institutions whose establishment approval was cancelled ("Senarai IPTS Yang Telah Diluluskan Tindakan Pembatalan Kelulusan Penubuhan") [O0041, O0005, O0002]. If the institution is on either of these, or missing from the active list, it can't get you a Student Pass: IMI asks for the institution's KDN approval letter, still in force, with every new application [O0006].
2. **Check the course is accredited** on the [Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR)](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr/), run by MQA (the Malaysian Qualifications Agency): type the course name in "Name of Qualification", choose the level (for example Diploma) and press Search [S0087]. Only accredited programmes appear, and only for the campus named; a course run anywhere else is not accredited [S0084, S0085]. "No record" can mean the course has only provisional accreditation, was never accredited or failed [S0086].
3. **For a language or training centre,** check it separately. It needs approval from the Ministry of Education (KPM) and the Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA), plus the Home Ministry's permission to take international students [O0043], and its students can't work part time or bring family [S0012, S0010]. To ask whether a centre is registered, contact KPM's Private Education Division: counter phone 03-8884 9536, email bps@moe.gov.my, Aras 3, Blok E2, Kompleks E, 62604 Putrajaya; its charter promises to acknowledge an enquiry within 3 working days [O0044, O0045]. **Need to know:** a public list of KPM- or KESUMA-approved centres allowed to take international students, and where to check a KESUMA-approved centre.
4. **Check you qualify:** 12 years of schooling and a recognised exam [S0043], and an English test certificate: MUET (Malaysian University English Test), IELTS, TOEFL iBT or TOEFL Essentials, PTE Academic, Cambridge English, Linguaskill, OET or ELS CIEP; without one the application may be delayed and the pass won't cover the whole course [S0050].
5. **Check the offer letter is real.** Agents have given Bangladeshi students fake offer letters [O0021]. The institution's international office can confirm by email or phone whether an offer letter is genuine and the course is running [O0017]. **Need to know:** an official EMGS or KPT channel for checking an offer letter, and where to find the international office's genuine contact details.

### Step 2: Apply from Bangladesh

You must be outside Malaysia when you apply [S0002]. The application can be created by you or by the institution [O0063]. Only an application you create and pay for yourself on the Education Malaysia website can be refunded to you, not one the institution creates [S0078]; you can track it with EMGS without an agent [S0080, O0046].

1. **Get and accept an offer letter** from the institution; the online form can't be submitted without it [S0024, S0047].
2. **Prepare the files.** You'll need [S0045, S0046, S0048, S0050, O0009]:
   1. A photo, 35 x 45 mm, white background.
   2. Your passport's data page, any pages with visas, and the observation page; the passport must be valid at least 18 months (for a pass covering the whole course, EMGS recommends 12 months longer than the course).
   3. The offer letter, all pages scanned as one PDF.
   4. EMGS's Health Declaration Form, filled in.
   5. Your academic certificates and transcripts.
   6. Your English test certificate.

   Keep your passport with you: agents have held students' passports to demand more money [O0018, O0023].
3. **Open an account and apply** on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/) or the EMGS HUB app [S0024]. In the form, choose the institution and course exactly as in the offer letter, type your name exactly as it appears in the two coded lines at the bottom of your passport's data page (the machine-readable zone), and use the passport number shown on the offer letter [O0007, O0008]. EMGS then asks the institution to confirm the application is valid [O0012].
4. **Pay.** You can pay through the institution, by telegraphic transfer (a bank-to-bank international transfer), or by Visa or MasterCard on the EMGS site [O0010, O0049]. A card payment adds a 3% convenience fee; a telegraphic transfer converts the whole fee to US dollars and adds a USD 50 convenience fee [O0049, O0047, O0048]. Tuition money handed to agents has failed to reach the institution [O0022]. EMGS's [fee calculator](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/student-visa/calculate-application-fees) gives, for a new application at a university or private higher education institution [S0060, S0061, S0062, S0063, S0064, V0017]:
   - processing RM1,728;
   - eVAL (electronic Visa Approval Letter, IMI's approval for you to enter as a student) RM159;
   - medical screening in Malaysia RM340 (RM350 in Sarawak);
   - Student Pass RM60;
   - i-Kad (the student identity card) RM50, or RM60.60 if couriered to the institution;
   - health insurance through EMGS RM498 to RM644 depending on the plan, or a policy arranged through your institution;
   - visa RM20.

   An 8% service tax is added to the processing fees [Z0094]. Public-university students may do the medical at their university instead [Z0095]. If a card payment fails but your bank says the money went, email enquiry@emgs.com.my with the bank's confirmation, the transaction ID and your application number [O0011].
5. **Refunds.** The first year's processing fee and the eVAL fee aren't refunded once invoiced, and convenience fees and service tax are never refunded [S0077, O0050, O0051]. If the institution hasn't yet approved or rejected your application, you can get a full refund if you ask within 90 days of creating and paying for it [O0053]. To ask, log in to your account on visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my, from the email address you applied with, and fill in the online refund form; a card payment comes back by telegraphic transfer [O0052, O0058].
6. **Lodge the personal bond** of RM750 (the rate for Bangladeshis) with the institution, which signs a bond with Immigration for you; you get the money back when you finish or withdraw without breaking immigration rules [S0059, S0049]. The bond form can be handed in after you arrive, for the Student Pass sticker [O0059]. **Need to know:** how and when the institution takes the money, what receipt you get, and how to claim it back.
7. **If EMGS returns your application asking for more documents,** send them within 14 working days of the date it was returned [S0020]. If they don't arrive in time, the application is closed, and you can ask for a refund within 6 months of the closing date [O0054, O0055].
8. **Wait.** EMGS checks your academic papers, then IMI's Student Pass unit checks your passport and security and issues the eVAL [S0003]. EMGS's service charter is 14 working days for a complete application [S0026].

**If you're refused.** A refused application can be appealed: EMGS's refund policy lists "eVAL Appeal Rejected" and "Appeal Rejected" among application statuses [O0056]. The institution's international office handles an appeal, or a new application with corrected documents [O0015]. A refund after a refusal must be asked for within 90 days of EMGS's notice [O0057]. **Need to know:** the appeal form, fee and deadline.

### Step 3: eVAL and visa

The eVAL is valid 6 months, and you may not enter Malaysia before it is issued [S0027, S0028]. Bangladeshis then need a Single Entry Visa, applied for on [MyVISA](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/) (IMI's eVISA website) from outside Malaysia [S0004, S0029, S0053, V0007]. On MyVISA students apply under Visa With Reference (VDR), the category that needs an approval letter first, here the eVAL [C0006]. Opening a MyVISA account and paying work as for a tourist (see Tourist visa, Step 2) [V0021, V0022]. The visa fee for a Bangladeshi is RM20 [V0017]. **Need to know:** whether MyVISA's RM105 processing charge also applies to a student's visa.

### Step 4: How to check your application is real

1. **Track it yourself** on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/): open Student Visa, then Track My Application, enter your passport number and nationality, tick the box agreeing to the terms and privacy policy, and press Track My Application [S0080, O0060]. IMI's own status page sends Student Pass checks to EMGS [S0081].
2. **Read the progress figure.** At 70% Immigration has approved your eVAL, and you download it as a PDF from your EMGS account [O0013, O0014]. If the figure stops moving, ask your institution's international office, which must confirm your application, or EMGS through the enquiry form at [educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us](https://educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us) [O0012, O0084].
3. **Need to know:** how to check that an eVAL document itself is genuine (for example a reference or QR check).

### Step 5: Arrival and the pass

1. **Before you fly,** register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) online within 3 days before arriving (see Tourist visa, Step 4) [V0058]. Permanent residents and long-term pass holders are exempt [V0060].
2. **At Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA),** go to EMGS's 24-hour International Student Arrival Centre (ISAC) before immigration: at Terminal 1 in the Level 3 arrival hall, at Terminal 2 on Level 3A after the Skybridge [S0082, S0083]. Its staff help you through immigration clearance, with priority lanes for students, and give tips on transport into town [O0061, O0062]. **Need to know:** which documents ISAC asks to see, and whether it charges anything.
3. **Within 7 working days do the medical** at an EMGS panel clinic; the list is on EMGS's site and the EMGS app shows clinics near you [S0055, S0058]. Without it EMGS can't start your pass [S0056]. If you fail, appeal at the same clinic within 3 days of being told [S0030]; if the appeal also fails, any refund must be asked for within 90 days of EMGS's notice [O0057]. **Need to know:** how to book the medical, and the appeal's cost, documents and possible outcomes.
4. **The institution gets your Student Pass sticker** using your original passport, its own letter, EMGS's support letter, your stamped personal bond and at least 12 months' Malaysian health insurance [S0017]. IMI's client charter gives 5 working days for its Student Pass step [C0047].

### Keeping the pass

The pass usually lasts up to a year at a time; EMGS recommends submitting the renewal as early as 3 months before it expires [S0032, S0033]. The institution or you submit it online through STARS (EMGS's Student Application Registration System) or the EMGS website [O0063, O0064]. You'll need a 35 x 45 mm photo on white, copies of your passport's data page, visa pages and observation page, and your academic certificates and transcripts [O0085]. Renewal needs a CGPA (cumulative grade point average) of at least 2.0 and 80% attendance, or else an explanation letter from the institution [S0051]. EMGS charges RM270 to renew at a private higher education institution, and IMI charges RM60 for the pass [S0060, S0007]. **Need to know:** how long a renewal takes. Breaking any condition can get the pass cancelled [S0016]. To change course, your institution may submit a Variation application once, within 18 months after your first semester, if you have finished at least one semester and study at bachelor's level or above; otherwise the institution shortens your pass with a Check-Out Memo and you must leave Malaysia before applying again [S0034, S0036]. If you entered on an eVAL but have no Student Pass yet, you must leave Malaysia to change course [S0035].

### Can students work?

Only students at universities and private higher education institutions may work, not those at language or training centres [S0012]. Work is allowed only during semester breaks or holidays of more than 7 days, at most 20 hours a week, in restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels or on campus [S0013, S0039]. It is never allowed as a cashier, at a front desk, as a singer, masseur, musician or guest relations officer in a hotel, or as a recruiter of students [S0014, S0040].

To get permission before you start:
1. You'll need: the job offer letter; the institution's letter supporting the application and giving the semester-break dates; your academic results and attendance report; a copy of your passport's data page and your valid Student Pass; and a copy of the employer's approval from the Labour Department (JTK) to employ a foreigner, under section 60K of the Employment Act 1955 [S0015].
2. Apply through your institution: you go in person with a representative of the institution to IMI's Student Pass Unit at Immigration headquarters or to the state Immigration office [S0041, S0015].
3. Immigration decides after checking the documents [S0015]. **Need to know:** the fee and how long a decision takes.

Students who work outside these rules are caught and deported, sometimes fined or jailed [O0019].

### Family

Dependants (spouse, children under 18, disabled children of any age, parents and parents-in-law) are allowed only for master's and PhD students; a Bangladeshi bachelor's student can't bring family, and dependants may not work [S0008, S0009, S0038, S0011]. A master's or PhD student's dependants apply through the institution on STARS, EMGS's Student Application Registration System [S0021, O0064]. You'll need [O0065, O0066]:
1. Forms IMM.12 and IMM.38.
2. The institution's letter requesting the dependant's pass, and its letter confirming your Student Pass.
3. A copy of every page of the dependant's passport, cover included, valid at least 18 months, and a copy of your passport's data page and current pass.
4. A passport photo, 3.5 x 5.0 cm, white background.
5. Health insurance covering at least 12 months.
6. A stamped personal bond (new applications only).
7. Proof you can support the family, such as the last 3 months' bank statements or a scholarship letter.
8. Proof of relationship: the marriage certificate, or the child's birth certificate.

Bring all the originals for checking; only complete applications are processed [O0069]. Documents in Bangla need a certified translation, and if the dependant is already in Malaysia the relationship documents must be certified by the Bangladesh High Commission [O0068, O0067]. EMGS charges RM250 per dependant (RM140 to renew), each dependant buys their own insurance, and IMI charges RM90 for the dependant's Long-Term Social Visit Pass [S0067, S0007]. **Need to know:** how long a dependant's application takes and how the result is shown. IMI's Long-Term Social Visit Pass also has a category for close family of a foreign student [C0019]; **Need to know:** whether it is open to the family of a bachelor's student. For a child born in Malaysia, see Having a child in Malaysia [S0114].

### School pupils

IMI also gives Student Passes to foreign children at pre-school and school [C0024]. The child needs a place at a government or government-aided school under the Ministry of Education (KPM), or at an international, expatriate, private or religious school, or a Chinese independent secondary school, registered with the Ministry's Private Education Division; an international school also needs the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission to take foreign pupils [Z0110, Z0111].

1. **Who applies and where:** the school's appointed representative, a parent or a legal guardian, for a new pass or a renewal, at IMI's Student Pass Unit at Immigration headquarters, the state Visa, Pass and Permit Division, or a branch Immigration office offering Student Passes, according to the school's address [Z0112, Z0113].
2. **You'll need:** Forms IMM.14 and IMM.38 [Z0114]; for a new pass a copy of every page of the child's passport including the cover, or for a renewal a copy of the data page and the current pass, the passport valid at least 18 months [Z0115, Z0124]; a passport photo, 3.5 x 5.0 cm, white background [Z0116]; health insurance covering at least 12 months [Z0118]; a stamped personal bond for a new application [Z0117]; and, for a government or government-aided school, the approval letter of the state or district education office [Z0119]. Bring all the originals for checking [Z0120].
3. **Cost and time:** IMI charges RM60 for the Student Pass, plus the visa fee for the nationality; its client charter gives 5 working days [S0007, C0047].
4. **Companions:** the child's parents, legal guardian or adoptive parents, and siblings under 7, may be considered for a Long-Term Social Visit Pass as the pupil's companions, at RM90, but may not work or run a business [Z0121, Z0122, Z0123, S0007].

**Need to know:** the extra documents IMI lists for international and private schools, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Scams

**Done to students.** The common pattern: an agent promises that a student visa lets you work freely, which it doesn't [S0135, S0013]; sends you to a college that pays a high commission [O0020]; takes tuition money that never reaches the institution [O0022]; or holds your passport to demand more money [O0018, O0023].

**Tried by applicants, and punishable.** Getting a pass for yourself or someone else by a false statement, or using someone else's pass, is an offence with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030]. Breaking any condition can get the Student Pass cancelled [S0016].

**If an agent cheated you or holds your passport,** in this order:
1. **Report it to the police in Bangladesh.** Online, register on [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or the Online GD app with your NID number, a mobile number (for a one-time code) and a live photo; then choose the district and police station, write what happened, when and where, and attach receipts and messages [I0034, I0038]. If the police treat it as a GD (General Diary), you get a digital GD copy with the GD number and the investigating officer's details; if it is a case, you must go to that police station with the printout or the complaint code [O0071, O0070]. You can also call the CID (the police Criminal Investigation Department) hotline, 01320-019999, or its control room, 01320-019998; CID asks to be told when someone demands unusual sums for a visa or holds a passport [O0072, O0033].
2. **Write to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's Complaint Resolution Cell** at minister.expat@gmail.com; it takes problems from Bangladeshis abroad [R0012]. Sample: "My son [name, passport no.] paid [agency, address] Tk [amount] on [dates] for admission to [institution] in Malaysia. The fees were not paid and the agency holds his passport. Receipts attached. Please help recover the passport and money." **Need to know:** whether the Ministry acts on complaints about education agents, which hold no recruiting licence.
3. **If you're already in Malaysia,** email the Bangladesh High Commission's consular wing (Counsellor (Consular), consular.kualalumpur@gmail.com, +603 2604 0946; mission.kualalumpur@mofa.gov.bd) [L0031]; the High Commission is at No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur [L0032]. In an emergency call 999, Malaysia's free emergency number, which reaches the Royal Malaysia Police [O0073, O0074]. **Need to know:** whether the High Commission helps students recover passports held by agents.
4. **Complain about the institution itself** to KPT through SiSPAA, its public complaints system, at [mohe.spab.gov.my](https://mohe.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do) [O0028, O0082]. Register ("Daftar Baharu") and log in, as the complaint page needs a login, then choose "Aduan/Maklum Balas Baharu" (new complaint); "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" shows its progress [O0079, O0083]. Give your full name, passport number, phone and a valid email, and attach evidence such as the offer letter and receipts; KPT may reject a complaint without these, and it doesn't take up private money disputes with an agent [O0080, O0081]. **Need to know:** how long KPT takes to reply.
5. **For free legal help,** OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a Bangladeshi migrants' organisation) gives legal advice and helps file cases for migrant workers, their families and trafficking survivors: helpline 01842773300, 4th Floor, 466 Sawdagar Garden, Dania Post Office Road, Dhaka 1236 [O0076, O0077, O0078]. **Need to know:** whether OKUP takes the cases of cheated students.

## Tourist visa (social visit)

Bangladeshis must get an eVISA to visit Malaysia for tourism, family visits or short business trips, and may stay up to 30 days [V0001, V0003]. For an ordinary visit you get a single-entry visa; a multiple-entry visa is given only for business, medical treatment, Fly & Cruise and wedding tourism [V0009]. A tourist visa never allows work or study (see Don't work) [V0046].

### Step 1: Prepare

You'll need:
1. **A passport** valid more than 6 months from your travel date, with at least 3 blank pages [V0026, V0032].
2. **A studio photo**: plain white background, 35 x 50 mm, taken in the last 6 months, full face; no selfies or scans [V0033, V0034].
3. **Scans of your passport's data page** and of the page showing your previous passport number [V0026].
4. **A confirmed return ticket** [V0026, V0050].
5. **Proof of paid accommodation.** If you'll stay with friends or relatives, upload instead a cover letter to the immigration officer giving your details, the address where you'll stay, and your host's name, Malaysian identity card number and phone number [V0026, V0029].
6. **Proof of money:** cash, card or e-money backed by bank statements for the last 3 months [V0027].
7. **For a child:** the birth certificate [O0039].
8. **A credit card** for payment (MyVISA's only other payment methods are the Alipay and WeChat Pay e-wallets) [O0037].

If you have several documents for one upload field, merge them into one file [V0031].

### Step 2: Apply on MyVISA

1. **Use only [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/),** IMI's only official eVISA website; applications through third parties can't be verified, and you can't apply from inside Malaysia [V0005, V0006, V0007]. If an agent applies for you, only the agent can see the status, and IMI takes no responsibility for problems [O0038].
2. **Open an account:** under Individual click Sign Up, fill in the form and the CAPTCHA (the picture code) and register; a temporary password arrives by email; change it when you first log in [V0021]. Log in with your email, the 6-digit code sent to your email, and your password [V0022]. One account can hold 5 applications at a time, for up to 5 people [V0023].
3. **Fill in the application on a weekday between 9 am and 5 pm Bangladesh time;** the portal accepts applications from Bangladesh only then [V0025]. Write your name exactly as in your passport; if your passport has no surname, leave Surname blank or put your full name in Given Name [V0035]. Apply at least 14 days before you travel [V0013]. A draft left unpaid for 7 working days expires [V0024].
4. **Pay online:** the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, a RM105 processing charge, and a convenience charge of 0.8% by card or 1.7% by e-wallet [V0016, V0017]. Nothing is refunded, even if you're refused [V0019].
5. **Watch the status** in your account [V0040]. If it says "Document Submission or Information Update Required", upload what is asked; if "Interview Requested", confirm and attend the interview, online or in person as the High Commission sets; if you don't respond within 5 days the application is rejected automatically [V0040, O0034, V0041]. A decision takes 2 to 7 working days [V0012]; after you re-upload documents, the 2 to 7 working days start again [O0035].
6. **Download and print the eVISA** (Actions, then Download eVISA) [V0037]. It allows one journey within 6 months of issue; if your travel date changes within those 6 months, you don't need to apply again [R0005, R0006]. If a detail on it is wrong because of your own mistake, you must apply and pay again [O0036].
7. **If refused,** there is no appeal; the decision is final, and no reason need be given [V0042, V0043]. You can apply again and pay again [V0042, V0019]. Questions and complaints go to the High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka: dhaka@imi.gov.my (visa section), +88 02 4108 1918 (visa), House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Sunday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm [V0043, B0121].

### Step 3: Check your eVISA is real

Go to [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa), enter your passport number and the visa sticker number, and fill in the CAPTCHA [V0067]. Only applications made on the official site can be checked [V0006]. **Need to know:** what a genuine eVISA looks like (for example whether it has a QR code) and where its sticker number is printed.

### Step 4: Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)

1. **When:** within 3 days before you arrive, the day you submit included [V0058, V0061].
2. **Where:** only [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main) [V0059]. IMI's instructions mention no fee [V0059], and IMI has flagged malaysiacard.com as a fake site that charged USD 29.99 [V0065]. Fake MDAC sites also appear as sponsored search results [N0057].
3. **You'll need:** your name, passport number, date and place of birth, nationality, sex, passport expiry date, email address, mobile number, arrival and departure dates, flight number, mode of travel and the last airport you leave from [V0061].
4. **Check it:** under "Check Registration" enter your passport number, nationality and PIN [V0062].
5. **On arrival** show the MDAC registration and your passport at the immigration counter [V0059].

### Step 5: At the border

**At Dhaka airport.** Bangladesh's immigration police question departing travellers and stop those whose visa, BMET clearance (the emigration clearance a worker needs) or papers don't match, or whose answers don't match their papers; Malaysia is among the destinations involved [O0031]. The papers that show a visit is genuine are the ones Malaysia checks: the eVISA, a confirmed return ticket, proof of paid accommodation and proof of money [V0050, V0027]. **Need to know:** what to do if Bangladeshi immigration stops you, and whether you can complain or travel later.

**At the Malaysian airport.** The officer checks your passport, your eVISA, your MDAC, a confirmed return ticket and proof of paid accommodation [V0050, V0059]. A visa doesn't guarantee entry: you can be refused if you can't explain your plans, and you must prove you are not a "prohibited immigrant", which includes anyone who can't show means of support [V0051, V0052, V0053, V0054]. Autogates aren't open to Bangladeshis [V0057].

**What happens in practice.** Bangladeshis holding valid tourist visas are regularly turned back at KLIA: for fake hotel bookings, no return ticket, too little money, or no satisfactory reason for the trip [V0109, N0011, N0013]. Travellers are told to carry RM500 to RM1,000, and officers may look at photos on their phones [O0029, O0030]. Refused travellers are put on a "Not to Land" list and sent back, by the airline, within about 24 hours [N0010, N0015].

**If you're refused entry.** The Immigration Act's appeal to the Minister applies only to someone holding a Permit, not a visit pass [V0055, K0011]. **Need to know:** whether a visitor refused entry has any appeal, any official minimum amount of money, and what a "Not to Land" order means for future visits.

### Scams

**Done to travellers.** Fraudsters bring Bangladeshi women on tourist visas promising jobs, though there is no agreement for recruiting Bangladeshi women to Malaysia; a ring has also collected passports and cash for jobs in Sabah [N0028, N0029]. Travel agencies have promised work permits, sent people to Malaysia on visit visas, then held them and demanded ransom from their families [O0032].

**Tried by travellers, and punishable.** Getting a pass by a false statement is an offence, with a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030].

**If someone sold you a "job on a tourist visa", holds your passport, or holds you in Malaysia,** in this order:
1. **If you're held or in danger in Malaysia,** call 999, Malaysia's free emergency number, which reaches the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) [O0073, O0074]. Then call the Bangladesh High Commission's consular wing (+603 2604 0946, consular.kualalumpur@gmail.com) [L0031].
2. **Have your family call the Probashi call centre,** which WEWB (the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, the government body for migrant workers' welfare) runs to solve problems of migrant workers and their families: 16135 toll free in Bangladesh (+880 9610102030 from abroad), open 24 hours [B0083, O0075].
3. **Report the agent to the police in Bangladesh,** online at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or at the police station (see Student visa, Scams), or call the CID hotline, 01320-019999 [I0034, O0070, O0072]. CID asks to be told when someone holds a passport or demands unusual sums for a visa [O0033].
4. **Complain to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment** (Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Old Elephant Road, Dhaka-1000) [G0025]. Against a recruiting agency, send the Ministry's enforcement branch an application with your proof, your full address and the agency's RL (licence) number and address, by email to sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd (phone 02-41030255); it is free, action starts within 3 to 5 working days, and the complaint is settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045]. For a licensed recruiting agency that sent you abroad without permission, send the Secretary a self-explanatory application with your BMET smart card, ticket copy, contract and passport copy; it is free, and settled within 60 working days of BMET's recommendation [B0046]. For any other problem abroad, email the Complaint Resolution Cell at minister.expat@gmail.com [R0012]. Sample: "I, [name, passport no.], paid [agent, address, phone] Tk [amount] on [date] for a job in Malaysia. I was sent on a tourist visa on [date] and [what happened]. Receipts and messages attached."
5. **For free legal help,** OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a Bangladeshi migrants' organisation) gives legal advice and helps file cases for trafficking survivors and migrants' families: helpline 01842773300 [O0076, O0077, O0078].

### Don't work

A visit pass doesn't allow any work, work-related training or study [V0046, V0104]. A pass stops being valid once its conditions are broken [V0100]. Overstaying brings fines and can bar you from entering Malaysia again for a set period [V0047].

## Family visit sponsored by a Malaysian citizen (VDR)

A Malaysian citizen can invite a relative from Bangladesh through a VDR (Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference): the citizen (the sponsor) applies in Malaysia while you stay in Bangladesh, Immigration headquarters approves it, and a Malaysian mission abroad then issues the visa [C0008, V0090, P0010, P0049]. It is open to Bangladeshis [V0089]. The relatives the checklist names, with the proof each needs, are the sponsor's parents or parents-in-law (marriage certificate, birth certificate and passport of the sponsor's husband or wife, and a notarial certificate endorsed by the Malaysian mission abroad and by Wisma Putra, Malaysia's foreign ministry), the sponsor's child under 7 (birth certificate and the sponsor's marriage certificate), and the sponsor's husband or wife (marriage certificate) [P0008]. Other relatives must still prove the relationship with documents [Z0028]. The visit is a social visit: you may not work, train for work or study [V0104].

### Step 1: Send your papers to the sponsor

You'll need: your passport, with every printed page scanned; 2 passport photos, 3.5 x 5.0 cm; the relationship documents the checklist lists for your case (see above); and, for the sponsor's information form, your marital history, your job in Bangladesh with proof, any earlier visits to Malaysia and any work you did there, your husband or wife and children, where you live now and how long you plan to stay [P0010, P0004, P0008, P0012].

1. Check that your passport is valid more than 6 months from your travel date; the eVISA in Step 3 needs this [P0016].
2. **If you are the sponsor's parent or parent-in-law, get the notarial certificate** the checklist asks for, endorsed by the Malaysian mission and by Wisma Putra [P0008]. The High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka is at House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave; visa section dhaka@imi.gov.my, +88 02 4108 1918; Sunday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm [B0121]. Wisma Putra's Consular Division endorses documents walk-in, handed in by the person or a representative with an identity card or passport, usually ready the same or the next working day: Wisma Putra No 1, Jalan Wisma Putra, Precinct 2, 62602 Putrajaya; 03-8000 8000; sah_admin@kln.gov.my; Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4 pm, Friday 8.30 am to 12 noon and 2.45 to 4 pm [P0050, P0051]. A document in a language other than English must be translated into English [P0052]. **Need to know:** what the notarial certificate must state and which notary in Bangladesh may make it; the High Commission in Dhaka's endorsement fee and processing time; Wisma Putra's fee; whether the sponsor can take it to Wisma Putra; and whether the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur must also authenticate it, since Wisma Putra's general rule is that documents from abroad are first authenticated by the issuing country's mission in Malaysia [P0052].
3. Send the sponsor clear copies of all the papers above. The application form (Form Imm.12) asks for your full name in capitals, sex, place and country of birth, date of birth, nationality, and your passport's type, number, place of issue and expiry date, so give the sponsor these exactly as in your passport [P0003].
4. Tell the sponsor which Malaysian mission you want the visa issued at (for Bangladesh, the High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka); the sponsor's form asks for it [P0012, B0121].

### Step 2: The sponsor applies in Malaysia

You'll need (the sponsor): Malaysian identity card (MyKad); 3 months' latest payslips showing RM2,000 a month or more, or, if self-employed, the business registration (SSM, the Companies Commission of Malaysia) and 3 months' bank statements; a confirmation letter from the employer; and the relative's papers from Step 1 [P0010, Z0027].

1. **Download the forms** from IMI's checklist [imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SENARAI-SEMAK-Borang-VDR.pdf](https://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SENARAI-SEMAK-Borang-VDR.pdf): Form Imm.12, the sponsor's declaration (Surat Akuan Penaja), the bond and the sponsor information form (Borang Maklumat Tambahan Penaja) [P0010, P0011, P0012].
2. **Type Form Imm.12** (2 copies) and stick the relative's photo on each [P0004, Z0058].
3. **Swear the sponsor's declaration** before a Commissioner for Oaths at a government court (or a Sessions Court judge, magistrate or notary public). In it the sponsor states their job, employer and monthly income and takes full responsibility for the relative [P0011].
4. **Sign a personal bond and have it stamped RM10** at LHDN (the Inland Revenue Board) [Z0026, P0006]. By signing, the sponsor agrees to forfeit the sum written on it to the Government of Malaysia if the relative breaks the visit's conditions [P0014]. A cash security bond is required only for citizens of China and Nigeria [P0006]. **Need to know:** whether a cash deposit is taken from the sponsor of a Bangladeshi relative; the sponsor form asks whether the sponsor will pay a deposit (wang cagaran) if approved, and IMI's bond rate for Bangladeshis is RM750 [P0048, V0112].
5. **Add a cover letter** (Surat Iringan) and the employer's confirmation letter [P0010]. **Need to know:** what the cover letter must contain.
6. **Submit in person**: the sponsor must attend, and signs the sponsor information form in front of an Immigration officer [Z0025, P0012]. Incomplete applications, or ones without documentary proof, are not accepted [P0009]. VDRs are decided by the Visa, Pass and Permit Division at Immigration headquarters: No. 15, Tingkat 1-7, Blok 2G4, Persiaran Perdana, Presint 2, 62550 Putrajaya; phone 03-8000 8000 [C0008, P0005]. **Need to know:** whether a state Immigration office also takes the application, the counter hours, and whether IMI charges the sponsor any fee for the VDR approval itself.
7. **Wait about 7 working days** for a complete application [V0090, P0010]. The result is a visa approval letter (eVAL) [Z0096, P0016]. **Need to know:** how the approval letter reaches the relative in Bangladesh (by email from IMI, or through the sponsor).

**If it is refused:** **Need to know:** whether a refused family-visit VDR can be appealed.

### Step 3: Apply for the eVISA

Bangladesh is not on MyVISA's list of countries exempted from the eVISA, so a Bangladeshi with a VDR approval must still apply for one [P0015].

You'll need: a scan of your passport's data page (valid more than 6 months from travel); the VDR approval letter; an email address; and a credit card, debit card or e-wallet for about RM125 plus a small card charge [P0016, V0016, V0017].

1. Open an account on [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/) (Individual, then Sign Up; a temporary password comes by email) and choose the Visa With Reference category (see Tourist visa, Step 2) [V0021, C0006, Z0096]. From Bangladesh the portal accepts applications on weekdays from 9 am to 5 pm Bangladesh time [V0025].
2. Fill in the details exactly as on your approval letter. If your details differ from the letter, the office that issued the letter must correct them before you pay [P0017].
3. Pay online: the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, RM105 processing and a 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) charge; nothing is refunded [V0017, P0018, V0019]. If money is taken twice, or the status stays "New Application" after paying, don't pay again; use MyVISA's Live Chat, open 24 hours [P0019].
4. Download and print the eVISA (Actions, then Download eVISA) [V0037]. Check it is real at [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa) with your passport number and the visa sticker number [V0067].

### Step 4: Travel

Register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) within 3 days before you arrive, and carry the papers the border officer checks; if the officer questions you, see Tourist visa, Step 5 for what is checked and why visitors are refused [V0058, V0050].

### Don't work, scams and complaints

A visit pass doesn't allow work; once you break its conditions the pass stops being valid (see What can get you jailed) [V0104, V0100]. Immigration arrests Bangladeshis found working on visit passes in factory and construction-site raids [P0020, P0022]. The sponsor loses the bond if you break the conditions [P0014]. Giving a false statement to get a pass, for yourself or someone else, is an offence (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031]. **Need to know:** scams on this route, such as fake sponsors or brokers charging for a "family VDR".

Immigration matters are handled only at Immigration counters or on its official portal; no officer or third party may handle applications outside them [P0038]. If a sponsor or broker cheats you:

1. **Check before paying:** look up a suspicious phone number, bank account or company name on the Royal Malaysia Police's Semak Mule portal [P0056].
2. **In Malaysia,** make a police report at the nearest police station; in an emergency call 999, which is free [P0055, O0073, O0074]. If you paid online, first call the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) on 997, open 24 hours, within 24 hours of finding out, so the money can be blocked, and tell your bank [P0055].
3. **If an official asked for a bribe,** report it to MACC (the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission): toll-free hotline 1-800-88-6000, online at [portaladuan.sprm.gov.my](https://portaladuan.sprm.gov.my/), email info@sprm.gov.my or SMS +6019-6000 696; headquarters No. 2, Lebuh Wawasan, Presint 7, 62250 Putrajaya [P0053, P0054].
4. **For help as a Bangladeshi in Malaysia,** contact the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur (see Contacts) [L0032, B0117].

## Business visit

A social visit pass covers business meetings, conferences, business discussions, factory inspections, audits, signing agreements, investment surveys and seminars [V0068]. It doesn't cover professional or technical work [V0094]: for up to 30 days of urgent professional work, the Malaysian company gets you PLS@Xpats permission (Step 3); for longer work it needs a Professional Visit Pass or Employment Pass (see those sections) [P0024, V0091]. A business visa never allows a job (see What can get you jailed) [V0094, V0100].

### Step 1: Get the papers

You'll need: your passport, valid more than 6 months, with 3 blank pages; a studio photo; bank statements; proof of paid accommodation; a return ticket; and either your company's official dispatch letter or an invitation letter from a Malaysian government body or government-linked company (GLC) (see Tourist visa, Step 1) [V0026, V0032, V0070].

### Step 2: Apply for the business eVISA on MyVISA

Each traveller needs an application of their own; one MyVISA account can hold applications for up to 5 people, so a husband or wife travelling with you can be added to your account [V0023].

1. Apply on [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/) as for a tourist visa (see Tourist visa, Step 2), choosing the business purpose and uploading the letter and ticket [V0070].
2. **Single or multiple entry.** A business multiple-entry visa is valid 6 months, with up to 30 days per entry, and is limited to 31 eligible nationalities; Bangladesh is one of the 31 nationalities that need an eVISA [V0071, C0007, V0001]. **Need to know:** whether the 31 multiple-entry nationalities are the same 31, so that Bangladeshis can choose multiple entry.
3. **Pay** the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, RM105 processing and the card or e-wallet charge; nothing is refunded [V0017, V0016, V0019].
4. **Wait** 2 to 7 working days [V0012]. If refused, no reasons need be given and no appeal is published; you can apply again and pay again [V0042, V0019].
5. **Download, print and check** the eVISA (see Tourist visa, Step 3) [V0037, V0067].

### Step 3: Short professional work: PLS@Xpats

You'll need (the Malaysian company): its request letter; a copy of every page of your passport, valid 6 months or more; its company registration with SSM; the current work or project schedule; and your return air ticket [P0028].

1. The company applies at [mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats/main](https://mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats/main); it doesn't need to be registered with ESD (the Expatriate Services Division of IMI) [P0028, P0029]. It is free and takes 3 working days [P0027].
2. The approval letter is valid 30 days from approval for one entry, and lets you work for at most 30 days; it can't be extended [P0024, P0025].
3. You still need the eVISA (Step 2) [P0026]. Carry the approval letter: without it you won't be let in for this work [P0030].
4. It applies in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan only, not Sabah or Sarawak, and there is no pass for family [P0031]. It is open to every nationality except Israel, to people aged 18 or over who are not blacklisted [P0029]. Questions go through IMI's enquiry form at eapp.imi.gov.my [P0047].

### Scams and complaints

**Fake invitations.** A syndicate brought Bangladeshis in on fake sports-competition invitation letters, with return tickets and budget-hotel bookings to fool officers, charging RM2,000 to RM5,000 a person [P0040].

**Paying someone to "get" the visa.** The eVISA is applied for and paid only online, for the fees in Step 2 [P0019, V0016]. Immigration matters go only through Immigration counters or its official portal; no officer or third party may handle them outside those channels [P0038].

**If you were cheated:** in Malaysia, use the police, NSRC and MACC route under Family visit (VDR), Don't work, scams and complaints [P0055, P0053]. In Bangladesh, if an agency or broker took money for a "business visa" job, complain to BMET (see Scams under Work visa: company worker (PLKS)) [B0034, B0047].

A false statement to get a pass is an offence (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031].

## Medical visit

The medical eVISA is only for treatment at a hospital that is a member of MHTC (Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council, the Malaysian Health Ministry's agency for patients from abroad) [V0073, P0033].

### Step 1: Get a hospital appointment

You'll need: your medical records and test results from your doctor in Bangladesh, recent scans (X-ray, CT, MRI) in digital form or on CD, a written medical history and a list of your medicines with generic names and doses [P0046].

1. Choose a hospital under "Find A Hospital" on [malaysiahealthcare.org](https://malaysiahealthcare.org/find-hospital-filter); only hospitals on this list qualify for the medical eVISA [P0035, V0073]. MHTC's concierge, on WhatsApp at +60 18 870 0100, helps choose a hospital and can arrange the appointment, hotel and transport [P0035, P0059].
2. Send the hospital your records and ask for an appointment letter in English with your treatment details and personal information [P0034]. Ask for a written cost estimate covering treatment, stay and follow-up care [P0046]. **Need to know:** whether a hospital or MHTC charges for reviewing records or issuing the appointment letter, how long it takes, and how to check a letter is genuine.
3. The hospital also gives you an MHTC support letter for the visa [V0076].

### Step 2: Apply for the medical eVISA

You'll need: a passport valid at least 6 months; the appointment letter; the MHTC support letter; a return ticket; proof of accommodation; and a passport photo [V0076].

1. Apply on [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/) at least 14 working days before the appointment (see Tourist visa, Step 2 for the account) [V0073]. **Need to know:** whether Bangladeshis must still go through MHTC's authorised partners, as the High Commission in Dhaka says, or can apply themselves; which companies are MHTC's authorised partners in Bangladesh, what they do and what they charge [V0075].
2. Pay the RM20 visa fee for Bangladesh, RM105 processing and the card or e-wallet charge; nothing is refunded [V0016, V0017, V0019].
3. It takes 3 to 5 working days [V0076]. If refused, no reasons need be given and no appeal is published [V0042].
4. The approval comes by email. Print it and keep a copy on your phone [P0036]. A medical multiple-entry visa, where given, is valid 6 months [V0071].

### Step 3: Companions and arrival

Up to 2 companions may come: a husband or wife, parent, child, grandparent, brother or sister, nurse or domestic helper [V0074]. At the airport show your passport, eVISA, the eVAL printout, the hospital appointment letter and your companions' details [V0074]. The special airport lane is only for MHTC member-hospital patients with an MHTC authorisation letter or a Meet and Greet package [P0037]. Meet and Greet costs RM189 per companion, and the hospital must request it at least 3 working days ahead [V0077].

### Longer treatment

For treatment at a government or specialist hospital, IMI gives a Long-Term Social Visit Pass, with one companion only [C0018]. The patient, the hospital's representative or the companion applies, with Form Imm.38 (IMI's visa application form) and Form Imm.12, a copy of every printed passport page, the hospital's support letter, 3 months' bank statements, proof that the companion is close family certified by the Malaysian High Commission, the MHTC letter, and proof of payment [P0013, P0063]. A Long-Term Social Visit Pass takes 25 working days [E0057]. **Need to know:** which IMI office takes a medical application, the pass fee, what document you get, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Don't work, scams and complaints

A visa holder may not take unauthorised work [V0046]. A false statement to get a pass, or using someone else's pass, is an offence (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031]. **Need to know:** scams on the Malaysia medical-visa route.

If something goes wrong:

1. **With the hospital** (care, staff or the bill): complain to the Health Ministry's Private Medical Practice Control Section (CKAPS) online at [myckaps.spab.gov.my](https://myckaps.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do), by phone 03-8883 1362, by email ckaps.aduan@moh.gov.my, or by letter to the Deputy Director CKAPS, Level 3, Block E1, Complex E, 62590 Putrajaya. A family member can complain for the patient with the patient's written permission. The hospital must explain to you within 14 days of CKAPS's letter, and simple cases take 30 days; a refund or bill reduction is up to the hospital, or a lawyer or civil court [P0060, P0061, P0062].
2. **With an agent or broker who took money:** use the police, NSRC and MACC route under Family visit (VDR), Don't work, scams and complaints [P0055, P0053].

## Professional Visit Pass

The Professional Visit Pass (PVP) is for a professional sent by an overseas company to work on a project or train with a Malaysian company for up to 12 months; it isn't for general workers or operators [V0091, V0093]. You may work only for the company named on the pass, and family can't come on a Dependant Pass [V0091]. You can't apply yourself: a registered Malaysian company, organisation or institution applies while you stay in Bangladesh [V0092, V0094].

### Step 1: The Malaysian company prepares

1. The company registers with ESD [V0091].
2. It gets the approval of JTKSM (the Labour Department of Peninsular Malaysia) to employ a foreigner (under section 60K of the Employment Act) and an acknowledgement that it advertised the job on MYFutureJobs, the job portal of PERKESO (the Social Security Organisation) [E0050, Z0012]. Both are done on Xpats Gateway, the online platform between employers and government agencies for Employment Pass and Professional Visit Pass applications [Z0011]. For a training programme the organiser applies for the pass before you travel [V0097].
3. **Check the company is real.** Look it up on SSM's e-Info portal ([ssm-einfo.my](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/)): register, search by company name or registration number and buy its company profile, which shows its status, addresses, business and directors; "existing" status means it is not being wound up, struck off or dissolved [G0055, G0053, G0054]. Help: SSM's call centre, 1300 30 4636 [G0058]. **Need to know:** how a worker can check that a company is registered with ESD, or that a PVP offer is genuine.

### Step 2: Send your documents; the company applies

You'll need: a passport copy; your appointment or assignment letter; the invitation or support letter; the work or training schedule; contract or project papers; your academic or professional certificates; and proof of work experience [P0041]. Certificates follow the same rules as for the Employment Pass (see Employment Pass, Step 2) [P0065].

1. The company applies through its ESD account and pays RM1,200 (plus tax), then RM90 for each quarter of the pass [V0091, V0095].
2. For Bangladeshis the security bond is RM750, with a bank guarantee addressed to the Director General of Immigration [V0112, Z0017].
3. The period approved depends on the task and schedule; the full 12 months is not automatic [P0042]. **Need to know:** how long a standard PVP decision takes.
4. If refused, only the company can appeal, within 14 days in ESD Online; the appeal is decided within 30 working days [E0063].

### Step 3: Bangladesh clearance

**Need to know:** whether a Bangladeshi going to Malaysia on a PVP needs BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) emigration clearance, as a worker with an individually obtained visa does (see Employment Pass, Step 3) [B0029].

### Step 4: Enter and get the ePASS

1. After approval you get a VDR to enter Malaysia [V0096, P0045].
2. The company submits and pays for the endorsement in ESD Online. You then enter and go to the Expatriate Satellite Centre at KLIA Terminal 1 or 2, without an appointment, or the company has the pass endorsed at the MYXpats Centre [P0045]. The MYXpats Centre, run jointly by TalentCorp (Talent Corporation Malaysia) and Immigration, processes and issues Employment Passes and related passes: Level 4, Surian Tower, No 1, Jalan PJU 7/3, Mutiara Damansara, 47810 Petaling Jaya, Selangor; 9 am to 5 pm, Monday to Friday [P0057, P0058].
3. Your pass is an ePASS, which you must always carry with your passport [P0043]. Check it is real by its QR code or at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan) [E0060]. If it has a mistake, the company has it corrected through the MYXpats helpdesk (+603-7839 7171, helpdesk@myxpats.com.my) in 3 to 5 working days [P0044, P0058].

**Scams:** fake ePASSes exist; in 2025 a work pass sold through a middleman for RM5,000 had a tampered QR code, and the passport had no work-pass record in Immigration's system [P0038]. Always check the pass as in item 3. **Need to know:** any case of a "PVP" sold to general workers as a work visa. False information can lead to rejection, cancellation, withdrawal of the pass, immigration action and blacklisting [E0070].

IMI also handles PVPs outside ESD for jockeys, volunteers and students on exchange, mobility or industrial-training programmes (at most 6 months for students); foreign artistes and film crews apply through PUSPAL, the Communications Ministry's centre for foreign filming and performances [C0021, C0022].

## Transit

1. **Same flight, staying in the airport:** no transit visa [V0080].
2. **Changing planes:** with all flights on one booking (PNR) and staying in the transit hall, no visa is needed. You need a visa if your bookings are separate and you must go through immigration to check in or collect bags, if you change between KLIA and KLIA2 (Kuala Lumpur International Airport 2) through immigration, or if you leave the airport [V0081, Z0060]. **Need to know:** whether this applies to Bangladeshis as it does to Indian travellers.
3. **Leaving the airport on a layover:** apply on MyVISA for a transit or visit eVISA, give the airport as your address and upload a cover letter explaining the layover [V0079]. The transit visa fee for Bangladeshis is RM20, plus the RM105 processing charge; a decision takes 2 to 7 working days, and a refusal comes without reasons and with no published appeal [V0017, V0016, V0012, V0042].
4. Malaysia's 120-hour transit without visa for travellers from the Indian subcontinent is suspended [V0082].

**Ship crew:** a seaman signing on to a ship at a Malaysian port needs a passport valid at least 6 months from entry, a Seaman Book, a Letter of Guarantee issued by IMI at the port, and, since Bangladeshis need a visa, a transit visa [C0032, C0033, V0002]. **Need to know:** who requests the Letter of Guarantee (the shipping agent or the crew member), at which IMI office, its cost and how long it takes.

## Employment Pass

For professionals hired by a Malaysian company at a basic salary of at least RM5,000 a month who meet ESD's qualification rule (see Step 1) [E0011, E0022]. It is valid for one employer, and only the employer can apply, never the worker [E0002, E0006]. Some passes are approved by other bodies: manufacturing by MIDA (the Malaysian Investment Development Authority); ICT (information and communications technology) companies with MSC Malaysia status by the Multimedia Development Corporation; banking, finance and insurance by Bank Negara Malaysia, the central bank; the capital market by the Securities Commission; government contract posts by the Public Services Department; and jobs in the East Coast Economic Region by ECERDC (the East Coast Economic Region Development Council) and in Iskandar Malaysia by IRDA (the Iskandar Regional Development Authority) [E0007, C0013, Z0063, Z0090]. **Need to know:** what MSC Malaysia status is and how a company gets it.

### Step 1: Check you qualify

Counting basic salary only: Category I is RM20,000 a month or more and Category II RM10,000 to RM19,999, each for up to 10 years; Category III is RM5,000 to RM9,999 (RM7,000 to RM9,999 in manufacturing) for up to 5 years; the maximum counts time with the same employer, in the same position and category [E0008, E0009, E0010, E0011, E0018, P0068]. You need a degree and 3 years' relevant experience, a diploma and 5 years, or a technical certificate and 7 years [E0022]. All three categories may bring family: a Dependant Pass for a husband or wife and children under 18, and a Long-Term Social Visit Pass for parents, parents-in-law and children aged 18 to 25 (see Family passes, Family of an Employment Pass holder) [E0009, E0010, E0011, P0068].

### Step 2: The employer applies

You'll need: a passport valid more than 12 months with at least 6 empty pages, the whole booklet including both covers scanned in colour; a photo 3.5 x 5.0 cm on a light blue background, 99 x 142 pixels, not edited; your highest educational certificate (and any professional certificate) in colour; a detailed resume in colour with your total work experience; and the employment contract, stamped by the Inland Revenue Board and signed and dated by you and the employer [E0025, P0064, P0066, P0067]. For certificates, while you are in Bangladesh: an English certificate must be a certified true copy from the embassy or high commission in your country, from one in Malaysia, or from the employer's HR manager; a certificate not in English must be translated into English and certified by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka [P0065]. **Need to know:** the High Commission in Dhaka's fee and time for certifying certificates.

1. **The employer registers with ESD** (5 working days), then gets JTKSM's approval to employ a foreigner and the MYFutureJobs advertisement acknowledgement on Xpats Gateway (see Professional Visit Pass, Step 1), which takes 10 working days [E0016, E0050, E0051]. Hiring without that approval carries a fine of up to RM100,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [E0052].
2. **Send the employer** the documents above [E0025].
3. **The employer applies on ESD Online and pays RM2,160**; the decision takes 2 to 5 working days [E0040, E0016, E0054]. If refused, only the employer can appeal, within 14 days [E0063]; if it doesn't appeal, it can ask for 75% of the processing fee back within 14 days [E0048, Z0039].
4. **The employer applies for your VDR** with the pass; after approval it pays for the endorsement: RM200 a year, RM125 processing and RM20 a year visa fee [E0058, E0042, E0045].

### Step 3: Emigration clearance in Bangladesh

You'll need: your own application; a copy of the visa; the contract if any; an attested copy of your OEP (Overseas Employment Platform) registration card (see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 1 for registering); a declaration on a Tk 300 non-judicial stamp; and a pay-order for the fees [B0029].

1. Apply yourself at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka, or at the District Employment and Manpower Office in Chattogram; clearance takes 3 working days [B0029]. BMET's head office is at 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000 [G0026]. The fees and the BMET smart card checked at the airport are as for a worker who got a visa on their own (see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 6) [B0030, B0038]. Questions or problems: Director (Emigration), +8802222222415, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [B0031]. **Need to know:** the emigration branch's opening hours, where to buy the stamp paper, which bank the pay-order is drawn on, and what to do if clearance is refused.

### Step 4: Fly and get the ePASS

1. Register the MDAC within 3 days before you arrive [V0058].
2. On arrival go to the Expatriate Satellite Centre at KLIA Terminal 1 or 2, without an appointment, to get your ePASS and i-KAD (the card given to Employment Pass holders) [E0059, P0044]. Problems with the pass go to the MYXpats helpdesk (see Professional Visit Pass, Step 4) [P0058].

### Step 5: Check it is real

The ePASS carries a QR code that authorities and the company can scan; banks and embassies can check it at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan) [E0060]. Fake ePASSes have been sold (see Professional Visit Pass, Scams) [P0038]. Before you accept an offer, check the employer on SSM's e-Info portal (see Professional Visit Pass, Step 1) [G0055]. If you are cheated, use the police, NSRC and MACC route under Family visit (VDR), Don't work, scams and complaints [P0055, P0053]. A false statement to get a pass, or using someone else's pass, is an offence (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031].

### Problems with the employer

Unpaid wages and other breaches of the Employment Act go through the Working for Workers app, the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing in Kuala Lumpur and the Labour Court, as for any worker (see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), If you aren't paid) [L0163, L0021, M0232]. JTKSM gives free advice on 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my [M0118]. **Need to know:** what an Employment Pass holder can do if the employer cancels the pass or doesn't renew it.

## Family passes

Which pass a family member gets depends on who brings them: an Employment Pass holder, a Malaysian citizen, a permanent resident, or a student. A PLKS (temporary work pass) holder can't bring anyone; see Workers on PLKS below.

### Family of an Employment Pass holder

**Who gets which pass.** A spouse and children under 18 get a Dependant Pass. Parents, parents-in-law, children aged 18 to 25 and a common-law spouse get a Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP, a pass for a stay of 6 months or more) [E0014, C0015]. A Dependant Pass lasts as long as the Employment Pass holder's pass, or until the dependant's passport expires if that comes first [E0034]. Holders of a Category III Employment Pass issued before 1 June 2026 (when Category III covered a basic salary of RM3,000 to RM4,999) still can't bring dependants [E0019, E0012]. **Need to know:** how long an LTSVP for these relatives is approved for at a time.

1. **Get the family documents translated and attested.**

   You'll need: the marriage certificate (in Bangladesh, the Muslim marriage registration certificate) for a spouse; the birth certificate of each child; each person's passport; and, for a common-law spouse, an affidavit or acknowledgement letter from the Employment Pass holder's embassy [E0030, E0031].

   1. Have each certificate translated into English if it isn't already [E0030]. In Bangladesh a translation is attested by taking the original and the translation, both carrying the translation agency's and the translator's seal and signature, to an approved notary public and then to the Ministry of Law [Q0084].
   2. Each certificate must be certified as a true copy by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka or by the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur, and the certification must be dated in the current year [E0030].
   3. The Bangladesh High Commission attests a marriage certificate only after it has been attested in Dhaka by both the Ministry of Law and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs [Q0079]. Have it attested by a notary public and the Ministry of Law first, then hand in two sets at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dhaka (Consular wing, rooms 201/205; 02-47123389; asconsular2@mofa.gov.bd) between 8 am and noon, with a copy of your NID or passport; they are returned the same day between 2 and 3 pm, free [Q0082]. A birth certificate must be one issued through BDRIS (Bangladesh's online birth and death registration system) that can be verified online, attested by a notary public and the Ministry of Law in the same way [Q0083]; for a birth certificate the High Commission also accepts proof of online verification instead of the Dhaka attestation [Q0080].
   4. At the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur (No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur), bring the attested originals, copies of every paper, copies of your passport and visa, and one extra set of copies [Q0079, Q0080, L0032]. Attesting a certificate or affidavit costs RM42 (RM63 urgent) plus RM8.40 for each extra page. Pay at the counter with a card through the Maybank Merchant Card Service, or pay into the High Commission's Maybank account 564427102268 and bring the original deposit slip [Q0062, Q0063].

   **Need to know:** the Bangladesh High Commission's counter hours for attestation and how long it takes; and the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka's procedure and fee for certifying family documents.

2. **The employer applies.** Only the Employment Pass holder's company can apply, through its account on ESD Online (the online system of the Expatriate Services Division, the part of Immigration that handles professional passes) [Q0002, E0034]. It can apply for dependants together with the Employment Pass or add them after the Employment Pass is approved [Q0065].

   You'll need: the attested documents from item 1; a colour scan of each family member's passport; and, for an LTSVP, a personal bond form stamped by the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN, Malaysia's tax authority) [E0031]. Give these to the employer.

   1. The employer pays RM540 per person when it applies, then RM90 a year for the pass, RM50 processing and the visa fee, which is RM20 a year for a Bangladeshi [E0041, E0043, E0045].
   2. For an LTSVP, the personal bond is a written promise that the relative will obey Malaysia's immigration law, backed by a Malaysian company representative's pledge of a sum set by nationality [Q0086]. For a Bangladeshi the sum is RM750 [E0144]. **Need to know:** whether the RM750 is paid as a deposit, and whether and when it is returned.
   3. A decision takes about 14 working days [E0029].
   4. An approved family member gets an ePASS (electronic pass) [Q0066].

   **Check it is real:** an ePASS can be checked by scanning its QR code or at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan) [E0060]. Getting a pass by a false statement is an offence [K0031]. **If it is refused:** ESD's online appeal covers only Employment Pass and Professional Visit Pass refusals [Q0064]. **Need to know:** whether a refused Dependant Pass or LTSVP can be appealed. For questions, the employer or family can contact the MYXpats Centre, which processes Employment Pass applications [E0001], on +603-7839 7171 or helpdesk@myxpats.com.my [Q0013].

3. **Work and study.** A Dependant Pass holder who wants to work must get their own Employment Pass, through an employer [Q0001, E0145]. From 1 January 2027 the spouse of a Category I Employment Pass holder (RM20,000 a month or more) may work on the Dependant Pass, under conditions the government will set [Q0003, E0009]. **Need to know:** those conditions, once published. A spouse on a Dependant Pass applies for Permission to Study online, through the Sub-Product menu in ESD Online; applications on paper are no longer accepted at Immigration or MYXpats Centre counters [Q0005, Q0078]. **Need to know:** the fee for Permission to Study, how long it takes and what the holder receives. Relatives on an LTSVP may not work or study; they must switch to an Employment Pass to work or a Student Pass to study [S0141, E0032].

**Need to know:** any official warning about scams in family-pass applications.

### Spouse of a Malaysian citizen

**Before you marry.** A worker on a PLKS may not marry a Malaysian or another foreign worker in Malaysia [E0139]; one who does has the work permit cancelled and is sent home [Q0010]. For any other foreigner, the Malaysian partner first asks Immigration to confirm the foreigner's pass, on IMI's (the Immigration Department of Malaysia's) form "Permohonan Pengesahan Pas bagi Tujuan Perkahwinan Warganegara Asing" (application to confirm a pass for marriage to a foreign national), [downloadable here](https://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Borang-Pengesahan-Pas-Bagi-Tujuan-Perkahwinan-Dengan-Warganegara-Asing.pdf) [Q0087]. Fill it in two copies and hand it in at any state Immigration headquarters with a copy of the Malaysian partner's identity card, two passport-size photos of each of you, and a copy of the foreigner's passport including the pages with passes; an Immigration officer certifies the pass and its expiry date on the form [Q0088]. For a Bangladeshi marrying a Malaysian, the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur asks for an unmarried certificate from the Deputy Commissioner's office, attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dhaka, and the parents' affidavit or consent, attested by the Ministry of Law and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, with the consular fee receipt [Q0081]. **Need to know:** whether the pass-confirmation form has a fee and how long it takes. The marriage must be legally registered under Malaysian law before the spouse can be given permission to work [E0129], and registered in Malaysia before the spouse can later apply for permanent residence [Q0038].

1. **Apply for the spouse's LTSVP.**

   You'll need, as originals with copies: IMI's [Form Imm.12](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/IM.12.pdf), [Form Imm.38](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/IM38.pdf) (2 copies), [Form Imm.55](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Imm55.pdf) (2 copies), the [sponsor's statement form](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Borang_Kenyatan_Penaja.pdf), the [security bond form](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Security-Bond.pdf) and the [sworn declaration form](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Surat_Akuan_Sumpah.pdf), all listed on IMI's [new spouse application page](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/permohonan-baru-isteri-atau-suami-kepada-warganegara/); the Malaysian spouse's identity card (MyKad), birth certificate and passport (all pages); the marriage registration certificate; a divorce or death certificate if either of you was married before; a postcard-size wedding photo; children's birth certificates; the Malaysian spouse's employer letter (or business licence if self-employed); proof of income (last 3 months' payslips, bank book or tax Form J); and proof of address (a utility bill) [E0126, Z0061, Z0062, Q0006, Q0007, Q0009, Q0111].

   1. Get the security bond form stamped with a RM10 revenue stamp [E0126], and the sworn declaration, with your photo, stamped by a Commissioner for Oaths [Q0008].
   2. Go together to the Immigration office nearest your home address; the Malaysian spouse must come with you [Z0046, E0127]. Only Chinese and African nationals must apply at Immigration headquarters [Z0047].
   3. Bring every original document: Immigration checks them and accepts only complete applications [Q0006].
   4. IMI's client charter gives 25 working days to decide [E0057]. The pass is issued for 6 months to 5 years at a time [E0122].

   **Need to know:** the fee for a spouse's LTSVP, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

   **What happens in practice.** Immigration will not approve an LTSVP if its checks suggest the marriage was only for immigration status, and it looks closely at large age gaps [Q0011, Q0012]. It may inspect: visit your registered address, observe, interview people and check your documents, and it uses what it finds to decide whether to grant or extend the pass [Q0089]. Anyone selling approval of a spouse pass or marriage registration is a syndicate [Q0011, Q0012]; report them on IMI's SISPAA or to its Integrity Division, as under "If Immigration mishandles any family-pass application" below [Q0046, Q0068]. Making a false statement in a spouse application can bring a fine of up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail, or both [E0128].

2. **Get permission to work.** The spouse's LTSVP allows work, business or a profession without changing to an Employment Pass or PLKS, once permission is endorsed in the passport [E0122, S0095].

   You'll need: the Malaysian spouse (who must come with you), their MyKad, your passport and pass, a sworn statement, the marriage certificate, the job offer letter, and an employment contract stamped with a RM10 revenue stamp that states the job, the period and the monthly pay [E0125]; IMI's [Applicant, Citizen Spouse and Employer Information Form](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Borang_Maklumat.pdf); and the employer's latest company registration form printed from MyData SSM (the online records service of SSM, the Companies Commission of Malaysia) [Q0090, Q0091].

   1. Apply at the Visa, Pass and Permit Division of the Immigration office in the state where you will work, together with the LTSVP application or any time after you get a job offer [Z0097, S0099].

   **Need to know:** whether the work permission can also be applied for at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or the nearest state office; its fee and how long it takes.

**Permanent residence.** A wife can apply for an Entry Permit after 3 years of marriage and more than 12 months on the spouse LTSVP; a husband after 5 years of marriage and 5 continuous years on the spouse LTSVP [Q0016, Q0018, Q0017]. See Other passes, Permanent residence, for the procedure.

**Residence Pass** (Pas Residen: RM500 for 5 years; it allows work, study and business) [E0131, E0132]. It is open to relatives of citizens (biological, step or adopted children; a widowed or divorced spouse who has a citizen child; parents and parents-in-law) after 3 years in Malaysia on a valid long-term pass, and to children and spouses of permanent residents after 5 years [Z0018, E0133, Z0049].

   You'll need: Form IMM 16A [Z0069]; 2 passport photos and a copy of the sponsor's identity card [Z0070]; your old and new passports with copies, and the original supporting documents with copies [Z0071]; copies of Bangladeshi documents attested by the Bangladesh High Commission [Z0072].

   1. A Malaysian citizen aged 21 or over must sponsor you [Z0049].
   2. Submit at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or any nearby Immigration office, with the sponsor present [Z0048].
   3. Pay RM500 for 5 years, plus a multiple-entry visa fee set by nationality [E0131, Z0073].
   4. Check progress on IMI's online status page for Residence Pass applications (myOnlinePasResiden) at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main) [E0136].

   **Need to know:** how long a decision takes, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Other relatives of Malaysians and of permanent residents

IMI gives an LTSVP in several other family categories [C0015]:

- A foreigner aged 60 or over with no family at home, sponsored by a child, child-in-law or sibling in Malaysia earning RM2,000 or more [C0016]; this needs a personal bond stamped RM10 at LHDN [Z0076].
- An abused, divorced or widowed wife of a Malaysian citizen, with a local sponsor earning RM2,000 or more [C0017]. A widow's sponsor must be a close relative of her late husband and must come with her [Z0099, Z0100]. An abused wife also brings a police or hospital report, a security bond stamped RM10, and the sponsor's declaration and identity card [C0017, Z0075].
- The wife of a Malaysian permanent resident, once the marriage is more than 6 months old [C0020].
- A child or stepchild of a citizen aged 7 or under, on the citizen parent's application [Z0029].
- Close family of a foreign student, with the student's confirmation letter from the institution and a letter from their embassy proving the relationship [C0019]; see Student visa, Family, for who may bring dependants.
- A person having treatment at a government or specialist hospital, with one companion [C0018].

   You'll need: originals of all documents; Forms Imm.12 (or Imm.55) and Imm.38; and the marriage, divorce or birth certificates that apply [C0016, Z0074]. Only complete applications are accepted [C0016].

   1. Apply at the Immigration office nearest your home address [Z0046].
   2. IMI's client charter gives 25 working days to decide [E0057].
   3. A dependant's or guardian's LTSVP costs RM90 [S0007].
   4. A false statement to get a pass is an offence [K0031].

   **Need to know:** whether these pass holders may work, the fee for the other categories, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

**If Immigration mishandles any family-pass application.**

1. File a complaint on IMI's SISPAA (public complaints system) at [imi.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do](https://imi.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do): choose "Aduan/Maklum Balas Baharu" (new complaint), fill in every field, and later use "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" to check it [Q0046]. A short text could read: "I applied for a [pass] at [office] on [date], reference [number]. I have had no decision after [N] working days, beyond the processing time in IMI's client charter (14 working days for a Dependant Pass, 25 for an LTSVP). Please tell me the status." [E0029, E0057]
2. If an Immigration officer asks for money or misbehaves, report it to IMI's Integrity Division complaints unit: Tingkat 4 (Menara), Persiaran Perdana, Presint 2, 62550 Putrajaya; aduanintegriti@imi.gov.my; 03-8880 1246. A person who gives their name and contact to the complaints officer is protected under Malaysia's Whistleblower Protection Act 2010 [Q0068, Q0069, Q0070].
3. Immigration headquarters: No. 15, Persiaran Perdana, Presint 2, 62550 Putrajaya; 03-8000 8000 [Q0067].

**Need to know:** how long SISPAA complaints take to answer, and whether the Bangladesh High Commission helps with family-pass cases.

## MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)

A long-stay pass for people with substantial savings, run by MOTAC (the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture) [E0101]. It is open to citizens of any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia [E0100]. The minimum age is 25 for the Silver, Gold and Platinum tiers, or 21 for the SEZ/SFZ (Special Economic Zone / Special Financial Zone) tier [Z0041, Z0043].

**Tiers.** Silver needs a USD 150,000 fixed deposit and a home of at least RM600,000, for a 5-year pass; Gold USD 500,000 and RM1,000,000, for 15 years; Platinum USD 1,000,000 and RM2,000,000, for 20 years [E0106, E0108, E0114]. SEZ/SFZ needs USD 65,000 (age 21 to 49) or USD 32,000 (age 50 and over) for a 10-year pass, and a home bought in Forest City, Johor [E0106, E0115].

**Family.** You may bring a spouse; children under 21 (biological, step or adopted); unmarried, unemployed children aged 21 to 34; disabled children of any age; and parents or parents-in-law [E0104, E0110].

1. **Check the money can legally leave Bangladesh.** Bangladesh Bank sets Bangladesh's foreign-exchange rules under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act 1947 [Z0103]. Bangladesh Bank has not approved sending money abroad for MM2H [N0051]. A Malaysian MM2H licence does not by itself mean an agent meets Bangladesh's rules [Q0050]. **Need to know:** Bangladesh Bank's written rule on whether a resident may send money abroad for a fixed deposit or home under MM2H, and how to ask it for permission.

2. **Choose a licensed agent.** Every application must go through an MM2H operator licensed by MOTAC [E0101].

   1. Open [mm2h.gov.my/agencies](https://www.mm2h.gov.my/agencies) and check that the company's name, licence number (MM2H followed by digits) and licence dates match what the agent shows you [E0116].
   2. No agent can guarantee approval. Some agents have taken tens of thousands of ringgit, then stopped answering, and applicants could not recover the money [Q0047, Q0048].

3. **The agent applies.** Every application goes through the One Stop Centre MM2H (OSC MM2H), using MOTAC's online MM2H system [Q0041, E0118]. Final approval is by the Ministry of Home Affairs, through Immigration [Q0041].

   1. Fees: a processing fee of RM5,000 for the main applicant and RM2,500 per dependant, plus a participation fee of RM1,000 (Silver and SEZ/SFZ), RM3,000 (Gold) or RM200,000 (Platinum) [E0107, E0109]. MOTAC's online system also lists a processing fee of RM500 plus SST (sales and service tax) per application [E0118].
   2. Applicants need a Certificate of Good Conduct (in Bangladesh, a police clearance certificate; see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 4, for how to get one) and Malaysian police security screening [Q0049].

   **Need to know:** whether the RM500 system fee is on top of the RM5,000; the full document list; and how long a decision takes.

4. **After approval.**

   1. The main applicant and every dependant have a medical check-up at a clinic or hospital on MOTAC's panel [Q0039].
   2. Open the fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank; after approval you may withdraw up to half of it for a home, education, medical care or tourism in Malaysia [Q0040].
   3. Buy a home within a year of the pass being endorsed, and keep it for 10 years unless you sell it to buy a dearer one [E0119, E0103].
   4. Immigration's client charter gives 1 working day for its part (endorsing the pass) [C0047].

5. **Living on MM2H.** If you are under 50, you must spend at least 90 days a year in Malaysia [E0103]. Only Platinum holders may work or run a business; Gold, Silver and SEZ/SFZ holders may not [E0112, E0113, Z0019, Z0064]. Renewing the pass costs RM500 a year plus a visa fee of RM0 to RM50 by nationality [Q0042]. Years on MM2H don't count toward permanent residence, and an MM2H holder can't apply for it as a fully foreign national [Q0015].

**If it is refused.** Any appeal is a matter for the Ministry of Home Affairs [Z0042]. **Need to know:** how to appeal and by when.

**If an agent cheats you.**

1. File a complaint on MOTAC's SISPAA at [motac.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do](https://motac.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do): choose "Aduan/Maklum Balas Baharu", give the agent's name and licence number, what you paid and when, and attach the receipts; use "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" to follow it [Q0045]. A short text could read: "I paid RM[amount] on [date] to [agent], licence MM2H[number], for an MM2H application. The agent has not submitted my application or answered since [date]. Receipts attached. I ask MOTAC to investigate and help me recover the money."
2. You can also call the MM2H office: +603 8891 7568 or +603 8891 7434; MOTAC, No. 2, Tower 1, Jalan P5/6, Presint 5, 62200 Putrajaya [Q0043, Q0044].

**Need to know:** whether MOTAC has ever recovered money for an applicant cheated by an agent.

## DE Rantau Nomad Pass

For remote workers and freelancers paid by employers or clients outside Malaysia. It is run by MDEC (the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation) and open to all nationalities except Israel; the main applicant must be over 18 [E0084]. You need an income of at least USD 36,000 a year in a tech role, or USD 60,000 in other roles [E0085, E0098]. It lasts up to 12 months at a time and up to 48 months in all [E0086]; holders who reach their two-year limit will get two more years, under conditions to be set [Q0004]. It gives no route to permanent residence [E0098]. New applications are closed until 30 November 2026 and reopen on 1 December 2026 [E0099].

**Family.** A spouse or common-law partner, children under 18 (including adopted and stepchildren), a special-needs child of any age, and parents can come as dependants [E0087]. A spouse can't work [E0088].

1. **Prepare the documents.** Everything must be in English or have a certified English translation, as clear PDF files, with the same name and income figures across all documents [Q0051, Q0074].

   You'll need, as a remote employee: a passport with at least 6 empty pages, valid more than 14 months [Q0072, E0096]; a CV; a signed employment contract that shows remote work, job title, duration and salary, with an employer not registered in Malaysia and a start date at least 3 months before you apply [Q0072, Z0065]; the last 3 months' payslips and bank statements showing the same deposits [Q0073]; an income statement or tax return; a Letter of Good Conduct from the authority in your country (in Bangladesh, a police clearance certificate; see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 4, for how to get one); the personal bond form; and your highest education certificate [Q0072, E0097]. You also need a medical insurance enrolment certificate valid in Malaysia that covers your dependants and is valid at least 3 months; it can be handed in after approval, before the pass is issued [Q0109]. **Need to know:** the minimum cover the insurance must give, and which insurers MDEC accepts.

   You'll need, as a freelancer: the same passport, CV, Letter of Good Conduct, bond form, education certificate and insurance; signed client contracts showing scope, duration and payment terms (at most 3 per application); invoices from the last 3 months; proof of payment (PayPal, Stripe, Wise or bank records); and 3 months' bank statements above the income threshold [Q0075, Q0076, Z0067]. **Need to know:** whether contracts with Malaysian clients count.

2. **Apply online** at [mdec.my/derantau](https://mdec.my/derantau) [E0089].

   1. Pay MDEC's processing fee of RM1,080, plus RM540 per dependant, online. It is not refunded, even if you are refused [E0091].
   2. A decision takes 6 to 8 weeks after a complete application [E0089]. Check the status at [malaysiadigital.mdec.my](https://malaysiadigital.mdec.my) [E0089].
   3. There is no appeal; you can apply again later [E0093]. Neither MDEC nor any agent can guarantee approval [E0094].

3. **After approval: enter Malaysia and get the pass endorsed.**

   1. The approval letter is valid for 6 months and can't be extended; finish every step below within that time [Q0052].
   2. Get an eVISA (a visa applied for online) or, if your approval letter says so, a VDR (visa with reference) [Q0053]. Apply only on IMI's own site, [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/); applications through third parties or agents can't be verified [V0005, V0006]. For a Bangladeshi an eVISA costs the RM20 visa fee, a RM105 processing charge and a 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) charge, none of it refunded, and takes 2 to 7 working days; see Tourist visa, Step 2, for how to apply [V0016, V0017, V0019, V0012]. If you are in Malaysia on a tourist or social visit pass, leave and re-enter with that visa; a tourist pass can't be converted [Q0054, E0095]. **Need to know:** the cost, documents and processing time of a VDR for DE Rantau.
   3. On arrival, use a normal Immigration counter, not the automatic gate, so your passport gets an entry stamp [Q0053].
   4. ## Asylum

Asylum gives no legal right to stay or work in Malaysia [S0142, S0107]. Malaysia is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention and has no national asylum system; UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) decides refugee claims under its own mandate [S0101]. The Immigration Act has no provision on refugees or asylum [K0036]. Recognition by UNHCR gives no legal immigration status [S0142]. Refugees and asylum seekers are treated as undocumented migrants: they can be arrested, detained, fined and deported, and have no legal right to work [S0106, S0107]. UNHCR cannot grant legal status or work permits, or stop an arrest [S0108]. A UNHCR document is not a visa or permit; at most it may reduce the risk of arrest and detention [S0111].

**Who counts as a refugee.** UNHCR treats as a refugee only someone forced to flee who faces persecution or serious harm because of race, religion, nationality, social group or political opinion, or who can't return because of war or serious public disorder; someone who left for work or study is a migrant, not a refugee [S0102].

**What the process is like.** Requests go only through UNHCR's My Services self-service portal, reached from UNHCR Malaysia's help site (help.unhcr.org/malaysia, "Registering with UNHCR"): you create a personal account, submit the request and get a reference number to quote in every later contact; letters are not accepted [S0109, T0050, T0051]. You then wait for UNHCR to contact you for an interview; don't go to the UNHCR Reception Centre without an appointment [T0069]. The wait is long; UNHCR is dealing first with requests made in or before 2022 and with particularly vulnerable people [S0110, S0112]. UNHCR can't interview everyone, so it can't say when any person will be interviewed [S0103]. At the interview you must tell the truth; if UNHCR finds you were coached or misled it, that harms the case [S0105]. A refused claim can be appealed once, within 30 days; after that the case is closed and UNHCR's help and documents end [S0104]. A pregnant asylum seeker not yet registered with UNHCR pays the full foreigners' fees for antenatal care and childbirth at government health facilities [S0113].

**Scams.** All UNHCR services are free; anyone who asks for money for a UNHCR interview or to send letters to UNHCR is lying [S0109, S0143]. Fake UNHCR cards and work permits are sold [N0049]. A real UNHCR card can be checked by scanning its large QR code with the free UNHCR Verify Plus app (Google Play or App Store); "Incompatible QR Code" means the card is not genuine [N0046, N0047].

**Contact.** UNHCR Malaysia, 570 Jalan Bukit Petaling, 50460 Kuala Lumpur, phone 03 2118 4800; detention hotline +6012 630 5060 [N0048].

## Having a child in Malaysia

**PLKS workers.** A worker on a PLKS may not marry in Malaysia or bring family, and the government may revoke the pass of a worker who marries [S0092, M0159, L0148]. The FOMEMA medical check (the compulsory medical check for foreign workers in Malaysia), taken within 30 days of arrival, includes a pregnancy test for women; a confirmed pregnancy can't be appealed, and a worker found unfit at that check is sent home by the employer [M0223, Z0126, Z0134]. An employer may not dismiss a pregnant worker except for wilful breach of contract, misconduct or closure of the business [M0240, L0094]. **Need to know:** what happens to a PLKS worker who becomes pregnant after the arrival FOMEMA check, including at the renewal check.

The steps below are for parents who are lawfully in Malaysia with their family, such as an Employment Pass holder with dependants or the spouse of a Malaysian [E0035, S0095].

1. **Register the birth with JPN** (Jabatan Pendaftaran Negara, the National Registration Department) within 60 days. It is free; after 60 days it is a late registration, which costs RM50 [S0114, S0115, S0119].

   You'll need: form JPN.LM01 filled in (original; take the form at the JPN office, because the copy on JPN's website is only a guide); the hospital's birth confirmation form, or a doctor's letter for a home birth (original); the mother's antenatal card (original and copy); both parents' passports (original and copy); the parents' marriage certificate (original and copy); an AM80 statutory declaration if the mother had no antenatal care; and a utility bill if you live at a different address from the one on your documents [S0116, S0117, S0118, Z0024, T0026, T0028, T0043].

   1. Find the nearest JPN office in JPN's branch directory, [jpn.gov.my/hubungi-kami/direktori-cawangan](https://www.jpn.gov.my/hubungi-kami/direktori-cawangan/) (choose your state) [T0040]. Counters open Monday to Friday, 8 am to 4.45 pm, in Johor, Melaka, Negeri Sembilan, Pahang, Penang, Perak, Perlis, Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya; in Kedah, Kelantan and Terengganu they open Sunday to Wednesday, 8 am to 4.45 pm, and Thursday, 8 am to 3.15 pm [T0041, T0042].
   2. Go there with the documents. The mother, the father, the child's guardian, or someone who knew of or saw the birth can register it [T0027].
   3. Hand in the documents. You get the child's birth certificate [S0114, S0116].

   A child born before the parents' marriage was registered takes the mother's status [S0124].

   **Need to know:** whether a police report is needed for a hospital birth; how long JPN takes to issue the certificate; and how to check that a certificate is genuine.

   **The child is not Malaysian.** A child born in Malaysia is a Malaysian citizen only if at least one parent is a citizen or permanent resident at the time of the birth, or if the child would otherwise be born stateless [K0001, K0002, Z0051]; JPN decides citizenship from the parents' status [S0123]. A constitutional amendment (Act A1752) not yet in force will limit this to a citizen parent [K0005]. **Need to know:** whether Act A1752 has come into force.

2. **Register the birth with Bangladesh,** which Bangladeshi law requires for a child born abroad [S0127].

   You'll need: the JPN birth certificate attested by Wisma Putra (Malaysia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs); copies of both parents' passports; the parents' marriage certificate (kabinnama), e-apostilled (authenticated online by Bangladesh's Ministry of Foreign Affairs), or for an older certificate attested by the Law Ministry and then the Foreign Ministry in Dhaka; the printed and signed online application; and 2 recent passport-size colour photos with a white background [S0125, T0025].

   1. **Get the JPN certificate attested** at the Consular Division, Wisma Putra No 1, Jalan Wisma Putra, Precinct 2, 62602 Putrajaya, 03-8000 8000, sah_admin@kln.gov.my. It is walk-in, Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4 pm and Friday 8.30 am to 12 pm and 2.45 to 4 pm; go yourself or send someone, with an identity card or passport (and an authorisation letter if needed). You usually collect it the same day or the next working day [T0044, T0045]. **Need to know:** the attestation fee.
   2. **Get the marriage certificate e-apostilled** online through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' e-Apostille service on MyGov, [mygov.bd](https://www.mygov.bd/services?category=sector&data=service-sector-67). It takes up to five days, and the e-Apostille carries a QR code for checking [T0046, T0047]. **Need to know:** the e-Apostille fee.
   3. **Apply online** at [bdris.gov.bd](https://bdris.gov.bd/) (BDRIS, Bangladesh's birth and death registration system). Give your permanent address in Bangladesh, not your address abroad, and upload the child's foreign birth certificate and the father's or mother's Bangladeshi passport or NID (national identity card); an application without the uploads is rejected [T0020, T0021, T0022, S0125].
   4. Print and sign the application. The child and both parents go in person to the Bangladesh High Commission, No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur, with the documents [S0126, L0032].
   5. Pay the fee of RM5 by card at the consular counter or by deposit to the High Commission's Maybank account 564427102268 [T0024].
   6. **Check it is real:** you can check at [everify.bdris.gov.bd](https://everify.bdris.gov.bd/) whether a birth certificate is digital (17 digits) [T0023].

   **Need to know:** how long the High Commission takes to issue the birth registration and how it is collected.

3. **Get the child a Bangladeshi passport.** Every applicant under 18 gets a 48-page e-passport valid for 5 years [Z0023].

   You'll need: the child (who must come in person); the JPN birth certificate (original, plus a copy attested by JPN); the child's Bangladeshi birth registration in English, showing Malaysia as the place of birth, with its online verification copy; both parents' NID numbers, NID copies and online-verified NID copies; copies of both parents' passports and visas; the marriage certificate attested by the Law Ministry, the Foreign Ministry and the High Commission; a photo of each parent; and, for a child under 6, a 3R or 4R lab-printed photo with a grey background [S0129, S0130, S0131, S0133, T0029, T0033, T0034].

Bhd., the company the High Commission uses for passports) at [expatservicesmy.com/#/book-appointment](https://www.expatservicesmy.com/#/book-appointment) or on its helpline, 03-92120267; an appointment is compulsory [S0132, T0032].

## Sending money home

**Use a licensed remitter, never hundi.** Send money only through legal channels, never through hundi (sending money through an unlicensed agent) [B0007]. A shop that sends money must display a copy of its licence from Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM, Malaysia's central bank), or its certificate of appointment as an agent of a licensed company; a shop that can't show one is not authorised [T0061]. Licensed remitters such as MoneyGram, Western Union, NBL Money Transfer, Agrani Remittance House, Merchantrade and CBL Money Transfer cost about 5% to 5.5% to send RM610; banks cost about 9% to 16% [B0097].

You'll need: the receiver's bKash number (bKash is a Bangladeshi mobile-money account) and full name exactly as registered with bKash [B0103]; and, for the Touch 'n Go eWallet (a Malaysian mobile wallet app) remittance service, a passport valid at least 12 more months and a valid Malaysian work visa [T0016].

1. **Check the remitter is licensed.** Search the company's name in BNM's List of MSB (money services business) Licensees, [bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/msb-licensees](https://www.bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/msb-licensees), and, for a shop acting as an agent, in the List of Approved MSB Agents, [bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/approved-msb-agents](https://www.bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/approved-msb-agents) [T0060, B0098]. The licensee list also shows each company's head office and branches; for example, it shows Agrani Remittance House licensed for remittance, with its head office at No. 33 Jalan Tun Tan Siew Sin, Kuala Lumpur [B0101].
2. **Choose how to send.** To a bKash account you can send through Ria, CBL Money Transfer, Merchantrade, NBL Money Transfer, WorldRemit, Wise, Touch 'n Go eWallet, Western Union, MoneyGram and others [B0102]. The Touch 'n Go eWallet remittance service is open to Bangladeshi workers and needs no Malaysian bank account [T0016].
3. **Send.** At a partner bank branch, exchange house or money transfer agent, give the receiver's bKash number and registered full name and hand over the cash [T0068, B0103]. Up to Tk 250,000 can go in one transfer to bKash, as many times as you want [T0066].
4. **What the family gets.** The receiver gets the government's 2.5% cash incentive on money sent through legal channels [B0103, B0104], and can cash it out from BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs at Tk 7 per thousand [T0067].

**Need to know:** what the remitter's receipt shows (such as a reference number to track the transfer) and how long the money takes to reach the bKash account.

**If the money doesn't arrive.**

1. Complain first to the remitter's or bank's own Complaints Unit: by phone (note the officer's name, the date and time), then in writing with your name, account number and copies of the receipts; keep a copy. BNM doesn't accept a complaint that hasn't gone to the Complaints Unit first [T0017, T0062].
2. If there is no reply after 14 days, take it to BNMLINK, BNM's customer service centre: online at [bnmlink.bnm.gov.my](https://bnmlink.bnm.gov.my/), in person at Ground Floor, D Block, Bank Negara Malaysia, Jalan Dato' Onn, 50480 Kuala Lumpur (9 am to 5 pm, Monday to Friday), or by phone to BNMTELELINK, its call centre, 1-300-88-5465 [T0063, T0065, T0018]. For a bank, you can also go to ABMConnect, the complaint line of the Association of Banks in Malaysia, 1-300-88-9980 [T0064, T0017].
3. Report an unlicensed remittance business (hundi) to BNMTELELINK, 1-300-88-5465 [B0099, B0100].

**Need to know:** whether BNMLINK takes complaints against a licensed remitter; the Bangladesh Bank circular that sets the 2.5% incentive for this year; and how a worker without a valid pass can send money legally.

## Going home without valid papers

Someone without a valid pass can go home under Malaysia's repatriation programme, PRM 2.0 (Program Repatriasi Migran 2.0), which runs from 1 May 2026 to 31 May 2027 at Immigration offices in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan [L0113, L0118]. You pay a compound (a fixed fine paid instead of being prosecuted) and are not prosecuted [L0120, T0039]. The alternative is arrest, detention in an immigration depot for as long as removal takes, and deportation [K0020, F0089].

### Step 1: Get a passport or Travel Permit

The Ministry of Home Affairs is at Blok D1 & D2, Kompleks D, Pusat Pentadbiran Kerajaan Persekutuan, 62546 Putrajaya, 03-8886 8000 [T0057]. ## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Help comes from both countries. In Malaysia, PERKESO (Pertubuhan Keselamatan Sosial, Malaysia's social security organisation, also called SOCSO) pays for injuries, disability and death, and the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing chases compensation from employers [U0001, U0011, B0136]. In Bangladesh, the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the government body for migrant workers' welfare, called the Welfare Board below; Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka) pays grants and handles the migrant workers' insurance bought at BMET clearance, which Jiban Bima Corporation, the state life insurer, pays out [U0099, B0060, B0059, U0026].

### If you are injured or fall ill at work

1. **Get treatment.** PERKESO's medical benefit is free treatment at a PERKESO panel clinic (a private clinic PERKESO has appointed) or at a government clinic or hospital [U0003]. For an accident at the workplace, a panel clinic gives free emergency treatment and checks your cover itself on PERKESO's SEHATi system [U0062]. PERKESO publishes its panel clinics by state with address and phone; the Selangor list is at perkeso.gov.my/images/Klinik_Panel/Senarai_Klinik/SENARAI_KLINIK_PANEL_SELANGOR.pdf, and PERKESO answers questions on 1-300-22-8000 [U0063, U0062]. If you are treated at a clinic that is not on the panel, you or your employer can claim the cost back from PERKESO [U0061]. At Ministry of Health hospitals, SPIKPA (Skim Perlindungan Insurans Kesihatan Pekerja Asing, the hospital and surgical insurance employers must buy for foreign workers) is used without a deposit; show your valid passport at admission [I0019]. Ask the doctor for a medical certificate, and tell your employer about the sick leave within 48 hours, or it counts as absence without permission [M0025].
   - **Need to know:** a current list of PERKESO panel clinics for each state.
2. **Make sure the accident is reported to PERKESO and the claim is filed.** The employer must report it immediately, and at the latest 30 days after hearing of it; PERKESO can fine or prosecute an employer that reports late [U0021, U0059]. Accidents at work, on the way between home and work, during an emergency, and work diseases are covered, and since 1 June 2026 so are accidents outside work [U0001, U0002]. The claim is made on Form 34-PA (PERKESO's foreign worker notice and benefit claim form), which you can download at perkeso.gov.my/images/borang/Borang34/290626-BORANG_34-PA.pdf [U0068]. The worker or dependant signs it as the claimant, and the employer fills in its sections if you still work there [U0069].
   - You'll need: Form 34-PA, a copy of your passport and of your work pass (the PLKS, Malaysia's temporary work pass), your medical certificate or sick-leave certificate, and for a road accident the original police report or a copy certified by the police station that issued it [U0015, U0016]. Quote your 12-digit Foreign Workers Social Security (FWSS) number in every dealing with PERKESO [M0184].
   - Where: hand the form in at a PERKESO office counter and keep the stamped acknowledgement slip, or post it to the nearest PERKESO office; the form asks which PERKESO office you want to deal with [U0022, U0060, U0071]. PERKESO's head office is Menara PERKESO, 281 Jalan Ampang, 50538 Kuala Lumpur [U0074].
   - Deadline: within 12 months after the benefit became due; PERKESO's appeal board can allow longer for good reason [U0064]. PERKESO can reject a claim that is incomplete, unclear or late [U0022].
   - Cost: free. PERKESO tells workers not to use agents or middlemen who charge [U0020]. A false claim is punished by up to 2 years' jail, a fine of up to RM10,000 or both [U0023].
   - To check progress: call PERKESO on 1 300 22 8000 or email perkeso@perkeso.gov.my [U0024].
   - If PERKESO refuses or underpays: a dispute about your right to a benefit or its amount goes to the Social Security Appellate Board, within 3 years; a Medical Board's disability assessment can be appealed to the Appellate Medical Board [U0065, U0066, U0067].
   - **Need to know:** whether PERKESO accepts Form 34-PA without the employer's sections when the employer refuses to sign; the address of the nearest PERKESO branch; how long PERKESO takes to decide; and how to file an appeal to the Social Security Appellate Board.
3. **What you can get.** Temporary Disablement Benefit is paid for medical leave of at least 4 days, counting the day of the accident [U0004]. If you are left with a permanent disability, a Medical Board in Malaysia assesses it; up to 20% is paid as a lump sum, and above 20% you can take one fifth as a lump sum and the rest monthly for life [U0005]. A worker 100% disabled who needs constant care also gets RM500 a month, and PERKESO pays for physiotherapy, artificial limbs, wheelchairs and similar aids [U0006, M0172]. Payments can go to a bank account outside Malaysia: give the account holder, bank, address, branch, account number and SWIFT code on the form, with a bank verification letter [U0070, U0018].
4. **If the employer won't report the accident or pay you while you are off work:**
   1. File a case at the nearest Labour Department office (JTKSM, the Labour Department of Peninsular Malaysia; office addresses and phones by state at jtksm.mohr.gov.my/ms/hubungi-kami/alamat-ibu-pejabat-dan-cawangan) with your payslips, employment contract and attendance record, and give a copy to the High Commission's labour wing [U0075, L0037]. The Labour Court of the Labour Department hears wage and contract claims free of charge; you go in person and give a sworn statement to an officer [U0029, U0030]. The court sets a date, summons the employer, hears the case and makes an Order; either side can appeal to the High Court within 14 days, and if the employer ignores the Order the Labour Department can seize and sell its property, take the money from its bank (garnishment) or prosecute it [U0076]. Free advice: 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my [U0031].
      - **Need to know:** how long a Labour Court case takes.
   2. Contact the labour wing: counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, +60 12-290 3252 [B0117]. It pursues unpaid wages and compensation claims [F0029]. Sample message: "I am [name], passport [number], PLKS with [employer, address]. I was injured at work on [date] at [place]. My employer has not reported it to PERKESO. Please help me claim."
   3. Tenaganita, a Malaysian non-governmental organisation (NGO), takes individual migrant workers' cases (case management and legal help): 12 Jalan 6/11, 46000 Petaling Jaya, Selangor, +603 7770 3671, Monday to Friday 9:30am to 6pm [J0007, J0008]. The Bar Council's Legal Aid Centres give non-citizens free legal advice, and representation may cost money; in Kuala Lumpur, Unit 3.03, Wisma Badan Peguam Malaysia, 2 Leboh Pasar Besar, 50050 Kuala Lumpur, +603 2691 1121 [J0047, J0049]. The ILO (International Labour Organization) does not take complaints from individual workers [G0027].
      - **Need to know:** whether Tenaganita charges workers for its help.
5. **Going home injured.** On your or your family's application, the Welfare Board asks the High Commission to bring an injured or sick worker home; it needs proof of BMET clearance or Board membership and a passport copy [B0073]. At Dhaka and Chattogram airports the Board's ambulance is free on request [B0070].
6. **Treatment money in Bangladesh.** A returnee with BMET clearance or Board membership who is injured, sick or disabled can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment [B0071, B0072].
   - Where: the family applies to the Board's Director General through the Board's web portal, the district probashi kallyan (migrant welfare) centre, or directly at the Board [U0092].
   - You'll need: medical reports and prescriptions, a bank statement, a copy of the arrival stamp in your passport, a passport copy, proof of BMET clearance or Board membership, and one 6R (6 by 8 inch) colour photo of the worker [B0071, U0092].
   - What happens: a Board committee checks the case by a WhatsApp video call with the worker and recommends an amount, and the Director General approves it [B0071, U0092].
   - When: apply within 1 year of coming home; the Board takes up to 60 working days after it has all the papers [B0071, U0092]. Cost: free [U0092].
   - **Need to know:** how the money is paid; the application asks for a bank statement [B0071].
7. **Insurance.** The migrant workers' insurance bought at clearance pays Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability and 10% to 90% of that for partial permanent disability [B0009]. See "Claiming the insurance" below.

### If a worker dies

These steps are for the family in Bangladesh.

1. **Tell the Welfare Board and ask it to bring the body home.** Call the Probashi call centre on 16135 (from abroad +8809610102030) [B0083]. Then apply in one of these ways; all are free:
   - Online on myGov, the government's online services portal, with the service "Application to bring home a migrant worker's body" at https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638167134 [U0049]. You need a myGov account, made with your mobile number (register at idp-v2.live.mygov.bd/registration) [U0091, U0051]. You'll need the worker's passport and, if the worker had them, the BMET clearance or Board membership [U0049]. You get an application and tracking number by SMS and can follow the case on myGov; the service takes up to 35 working days [U0051, U0049].
   - Or to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), which also handles undocumented workers. Send an application describing what happened, the cause of death, and proof the person was a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID, death certificate) [B0051].
   - A family can apply even if the worker had no BMET clearance or Board membership [F0074].
   - Sample: "My [relation], [name], passport [number], worked for [employer, phone] in [town], Malaysia, and died on [date]. We want the body brought home to [district]. The receiver is [name, phone]."
2. **Choose: home or burial in Malaysia.** The Board sends the family's wish to the High Commission either way [B0074, B0075]. The family also sends the High Commission a claim or consent letter naming who will receive the body and that person's phone number [L0039].
   - For burial in Malaysia, apply on myGov with the service "Application for permission to bury a migrant worker's body in the destination country" at https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638246958. You'll need a copy of the worker's passport or visa, the BMET clearance or Board membership if any, the district office's investigation report, and a certificate signed by all heirs on the union parishad chairman's letterhead (the union parishad is the elected rural local council). It is free and takes 3 working days [U0083].
3. **The employer's part in Malaysia.** The employer writes to the High Commission, appoints a funeral company and sends the papers: its letters, the insurance claim and compensation letters, the hospital letter, the death certificate, the Malaysian police report and the passport [L0038, B0119]. The employer pays the funeral and the cost of sending the body home [M0148]. For its no-objection certificate (NOC) the High Commission also needs a police report with NOC if the death was abnormal (hanging, a fall, an accident, murder) and the employer's report of the death to PERKESO [L0023, L0024, L0025]. For an undocumented worker it needs the original passport (or a police report of the lost passport), the receiver's details, a police report with NOC, the original death certificate, the funeral company's letter and the employer's name and phone number [L0026, L0027].
   - If no employer is found or it refuses, the Welfare Board pays [B0125, L0040]. PERKESO also pays back up to RM4,500 of the cost of sending a body home, against the official receipt, even when the death is not covered by any of its schemes [U0010]. Whoever paid claims it: a person (Malaysian or not), a company, or the High Commission, on PERKESO's Funeral Repatriation Cost Claim Form (PPJ), on which the employer states whether it paid [U0072]. With the form go a certified copy of the death certificate, a certified copy of the claimant's ID or company registration, the original receipt, proof of the bank account, and proof the body was sent home (the Ministry of Health's export permit or the airline's air waybill); hand them in at a PERKESO counter or post them [U0073].
   - Time: the Board forwards the request to the High Commission within 3 to 5 working days, and the labour wing takes about 30 working days [U0050].
   - In practice: a documented worker's body usually comes home within a reasonable time, an undocumented worker's can take much longer [F0073]. Malaysian authorities trace the family through the permanent address and the emergency contact written in the worker's passport, so give a full name and a working phone number there: in 2026 a body stayed 15 days in a Kuala Lumpur morgue, at RM75 a day, because the passport's emergency phone field held only "0088" [U0088, F0068, F0069].
   - If the family doubts the cause of death, it can apply to the Welfare Board for legal help abroad, on myGov at https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638179893, directly at the Board, or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office (the district administration) or the district employment and manpower office (DEMO). It needs proof of BMET clearance or Board membership and a passport or travel permit copy with the arrival and departure stamps; it is free, the Board sends the case to the labour wing within 1 to 2 days, and the service takes up to 20 working days [U0085, B0077].
   - **Need to know:** whether a family can ask the Malaysian police for a post-mortem or an inquest into the death, and how.
4. **At the airport.** The Welfare Board's desk gives the family a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, and the Board's ambulance is free [B0066, B0070].
   - You'll need: the worker's passport copy; a heirship (warish) certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor; the NID (national identity card) of the wife, father or mother; the death certificate; the NOC; the original air waybill; proof of BMET clearance or Board membership; and one photo and a copy of the NID or passport of the family member named to receive the cheque, and of the person collecting it if that is someone else [B0067, B0066].
   - Deadline: collect the cheque within 3 months of the body arriving [B0066].
5. **The Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant.** It goes to the family of a worker who had BMET clearance or Board membership and died in Malaysia, the country of the clearance [B0060, B0061]. If the family took the airport cheque, the Board writes asking for the papers and no separate application is needed; if the worker was buried in Malaysia, apply within 1 year of the death to the Board's Director General or through the district office [B0062].
   - You'll need: a family-members certificate on the official letterhead of the union parishad chairman, municipal mayor or city councillor, countersigned by the UNO (upazila nirbahi officer, the government's chief executive officer of a sub-district) or, in a city corporation, by the regional executive officer; an indemnity, undertaking and authorisation on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper, countersigned the same way (samples on wewb.gov.bd); a copy of the High Commission's NOC or the death certificate; a bank certificate or the original statement for the receiver's account with the branch routing number (minor children's own account where they apply); a colour photo of each family member, attested by the chairman and by the district welfare centre; and a copy of the dead worker's passport [B0064, U0096].

   - PERKESO pays the dependants a Dependants' Benefit when a death is work-related [U0007]. ### If you lose the job

1. **What the employer must do.** It must tell the Labour Department within 30 days that your job has ended [M0065]. Under the standard employment contract attached to the 2021 Bangladesh–Malaysia memorandum of understanding (MoU), published by the High Commission, it gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages, pays all wages and other money still owed, and pays your fare home unless you broke the contract [L0144, L0152, L0142]. It sends you home with a Check Out Memo, Immigration's exit document for foreign workers [M0229]. If the job ends with less than 4 weeks' notice, you may stay in the employer's housing for up to 4 weeks [M0205].
2. **You can't simply move to another employer.** A change of employer is allowed only in set cases, such as the employer closing or going bankrupt; see "If you want to change employer" [M0123].
3. **To claim money owed,** go to the Labour Court as in step 4 of "If you are injured or fall ill at work", and see "If you aren't paid" [U0029, U0030].
   - You'll need: your name and address (bring your passport), the workplace's name and address, your income details and payslips, your appointment letter and terms of service, and the termination letter if you have one [M0113].
4. **If you were dismissed without just cause,** you can ask to be reinstated. Any worker, in a union or not, can make a written representation to the Director General of Industrial Relations, filed at the Industrial Relations Department (JPP) office nearest the workplace, within 60 days of the dismissal (or within 60 days of the end of the notice period) [U0077, U0078]. File the form by hand and keep the acknowledgement of receipt [U0081]. The Department first tries to settle the case; if that fails, it sends it to the Industrial Court for an award [U0079]. You can represent yourself [U0080]. The Industrial Court can award compensation instead of reinstatement, but only where reinstatement was asked for [U0082]. A letter showing you are pursuing a complaint at the Labour Department or the Industrial Relations Department usually gets you a Special Pass from Immigration, renewable to about 3 months, so you can stay while the case runs [J0033].
   - **Need to know:** the address of the nearest JPP office, whether the representation form can be filed online, and whether there is a fee.
5. **Insurance if you come home early.** If you lose the job and come home within 6 months of going, the insurance pays Tk 50,000 [B0009, B0085]. See "Claiming the insurance" below.

2. **Apply.** For a death, no application is needed if the body came home through the airport desk; if the worker was buried in Malaysia or died after coming home, the family applies online at portal.wewb.gov.bd or at the Board [U0095]. For disability or a lost job, apply to the Board's welfare section: Deputy Director, +88 02 48317085, 01321-158354, dd.welfare2@wewb.gov.bd (disability); Assistant Director, 01712-107301, jahanwewb@gmail.com (lost job) [U0094, U0093].

For disability, add copies of the medical papers and one 6R colour photo of the disabled part of the body [U0094]. For a death, also the papers listed under step 5 of "If a worker dies".
4. **Cost and time:** free; the Board's service time is 30 working days [U0093, U0094, U0095].

## Coming home

1. **Cash out of Malaysia.** You may carry foreign currency of any amount and ringgit up to the equivalent of USD 10,000 [U0032]. If you carry more than USD 10,000 in total, declare it on Customs form No. 22, available before the customs checkpoint; not declaring can mean a fine of up to RM1 million, up to a year in jail or both [U0032, U0033, U0034].
2. **Customs in Bangladesh.** Free of duty you may bring up to 65 kg of baggage, up to 2 used mobile phones and, once a year, 1 new phone, and, once a year and if you declare it, either 100 g of gold jewellery or 200 g of silver jewellery, with no more than 12 pieces of one type [U0039, U0035, U0036]. A gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) or a silver bar of up to 234 g is allowed once a year if you declare it and pay duty; for a gold bar that is Tk 5,000 per tola, and a bigger or hidden bar is confiscated [U0037, B0112]. Bangladeshi passport holders may not bring alcohol [U0038]. Foreign currency over USD 10,000 must be declared on the FMJ form (the foreign currency declaration form) [B0115]. Airport customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 [B0116].
   - **Need to know:** whether BMET card holders who have been abroad at least 6 months may bring 2 new phones a year duty-free.
3. **Help at the airport.** The Welfare Board's Probashi Kallyan desks help with boarding cards, customs and lost luggage, take complaints from workers who were abused or cheated, and send sick returnees to hospital in the Board's ambulance [B0068, B0069]. Women who come back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [B0038]. Dhaka desk: +8802 8901040, 01404 413388 [B0070].
4. **A place to stay.** The Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport (Lonjonipara, Borua, Khilkhet, Dhaka-1229) puts up departing and returning workers for Tk 100 admission and Tk 200 a night; book online at wec.wewb.gov.bd or in person with proof of clearance or membership, a passport copy and the ticket; its 24-hour numbers are 01310 350555 and 01754 715720 [B0080]. The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives free wifi and phone calls and food at up to 30% off when you show proof of clearance or membership [B0082].
5. **If your family is threatened or its property seized,** apply to the Welfare Board for legal help through the local administration: on myGov at https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638181581, directly at the Board, or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office (the district administration office) or the district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [U0084, B0078]. Send the worker's passport copy and BMET clearance or Board membership if you have them; it is free and the service takes up to 3 working days; the Board writes to the local administration, such as the Deputy Commissioner or the Superintendent of Police [U0084, B0078, B0132].
6. **A business loan.** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB, the government bank for migrants) lends returnees who start a project up to Tk 50 lakh, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral, at 9% simple interest over up to 10 years [B0021, B0022, U0055].
   - Where: the PKB branch in the area of the business, on the bank's own form, which is free [U0052].
   - You'll need: 3 recent passport-size photos; passport and NID copies; a certificate of your present and permanent address from the pourashava (municipality) or union parishad; an up-to-date trade licence; the project's details with 2 years' income and expenses; the lease or ownership papers for the premises; a declaration of your own investment and of any other loans; copies of all papers about your return; 3 signed cheque leaves from the business's bank account; and a guarantor (a parent, sibling, spouse, close relative or anyone able to repay) with 2 photos, ID copy, address certificate, a clean CIB report (a credit report from the Credit Information Bureau of Bangladesh Bank, showing no unpaid loans) and 3 signed cheque leaves [U0053, U0054, U0056].
   - Fees: 0.50% processing fee (at most Tk 15,000) and 0.50% documentation fee (at most Tk 15,000), each plus VAT, paid when you take the loan; there is no service charge [U0090].
   - Time: a decision within 15 working days of a complete application [B0022].
   - Problems: PKB helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018].

## Malaysia as a destination

- **Bangladeshis are the largest foreign workforce.** At the end of 2025, 798,973 of Malaysia's 2,125,587 PLKS holders (37.59%) were Bangladeshi, ahead of Indonesians (551,337) and Nepalis (323,601) [U0057]. In 2025, 130,603 undocumented migrants registered to go home under Malaysia's repatriation programme and 51,100 foreigners were arrested for immigration offences [U0058].
- **Departures from Bangladesh:** BMET cleared 351,683 workers for Malaysia in 2023, 93,632 in 2024, 3,451 in 2025 and 1,592 from 1 January to 6 November 2026 [B0091, B0089, B0088]. From 2004 to May 2025 Malaysia took 9.30% of all Bangladeshi workers going abroad, against 34.49% for Saudi Arabia [B0092].
- **Closures:** clearances fell from 273,201 in 2007 to 919 in 2010, and from 175,927 in 2018 to 545 in 2019 [B0091]. Malaysia closed its market to Bangladeshi workers in 2009 over syndicate irregularities, reopened it at the end of 2016, closed it again from September 2018 over syndication and corruption allegations, and reopened it in 2022 [U0041]. In March 2024 it announced that new intake would stop on 31 May 2024 [U0042].
- **The 2026 reopening:** on 28 August 2026 the Ministry said Malaysia's Cabinet had approved 25 Bangladeshi agencies (as for Nepal; India, Pakistan and Myanmar got 10 each) [Z0155]. On 15 October it said Malaysia had agreed to list 338 Bangladeshi agencies in all: BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government's recruiting agency), those 25 private agencies as processing and service agencies, and 312 recruiting agencies [Z0144]. Malaysia's FWCMS system lists the 312 as associates: 10 under each of the 25 principal agencies and 62 under BOESL [W0008]. No demand letter is accepted without the High Commission's attestation [U0044]. BOESL's first circular, of 5 November 2026, recruits at zero migration cost; apply online at employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration [W0001]. See "Is recruitment open now?" under the work visa.
- **Where the jobs are:** foreign workers may work only in manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services, and Bangladeshis are allowed in every sector that is not frozen [W0041, W0042]. BOESL's November 2026 circular is for logistics jobs at DHL Supply Chain at RM1,700 a month [W0001]. Bangladesh signed an agreement on 7 August 2025 with CLAB (Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad, the Malaysian government's recruiting body for construction workers), whose first demand was for 500 workers [W0019, D0042]. CLAB interviews candidates and screens their health and suitability, and those who pass get 12 days of basic construction training before the Bangladesh government issues a certificate [D0036]. Malaysia's investment minister told parliament in November 2026 that foreign hiring should be limited mainly to dirty, dangerous and difficult (3D) jobs [U0046, U0045].
- **Pay:** the minimum wage is to rise from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, except at micro, small and medium businesses selling less than RM50 million a year [R0002].
- **Cost compared with other nationalities:** recruitment costs for Bangladeshis have reached USD 6,600 per worker, far above what Indonesian or Nepali workers pay [A0070].
- **Money home:** Malaysia was Bangladesh's 5th-largest source of remittances in July to September 2026, at USD 666.73 million [B0093].
