# W: Work route (PLKS) to arrival, scams, document checks

# Findings, part W: Work visa (PLKS, Temporary Employment Visit Pass), Bangladesh to arrival, scams and checks

Researched 6 Nov 2026. Ledger ids are in `ledger-parts/W.csv` (W0001-W0073). Every Bangla image PDF was read page by page from rendered images (text layers garbled); those rows carry `--no-quote-check` with a note.

Key terms (to explain inline in the doc): **PLKS** = Pas Lawatan (Kerja Sementara), Temporary Employment Visit Pass, Malaysia's work pass for semi-skilled foreign workers. **VDR / eVDR** = Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference (the "calling visa"): approved by the Immigration Department of Malaysia (IMI/JIM) for the employer, then issued as an entry visa by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka. **FWCMS** = Foreign Workers Centralized Management System, run by Bestinet Sdn Bhd for Malaysia; employers, source-country agencies and medical centres all work through it. **Bio-Medical** = the pre-departure medical in Bangladesh at an FWCMS-accredited centre. **FOMEMA** = Malaysia's post-arrival foreign-worker medical check company. **OSC** = One Stop Centre (Pusat Kelulusan Setempat) that approves employers' quotas. **KDN** = Ministry of Home Affairs; **KSM** = Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA); **JTK** = Labour Department. **i-Kad** = foreign worker ID card issued with the PLKS. **BOESL** = Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the state recruiting company. **CLAB** = Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad (Malaysian government body that recruits construction workers).

## 1. Current status (Nov 2026)

- **Timeline from official sources:**
  - 19 Dec 2021: Bangladesh-Malaysia MoU signed; employer bears airfare, levy and all Malaysia-side costs; no one goes without BMET database registration [W0027].
  - 2023-early 2025: recruitment permissions issued under this MoU at an agency maximum of Tk 78,990 (and lower for some job orders) [W0037, W0033, W0034, W0035, W0028].
  - HC Kuala Lumpur labour FAQ (undated, before 31 Jan 2025): only plantation open, entry deadline 31 Jan 2025; all other sectors closed [W0024].
  - BMET's newest Malaysia recruitment permission posted is dated 27 Feb 2025 (plantation); one more approval, for construction, is dated 21 Sep 2025 [W0026, W0028, W0038].
  - Sep 2025: BOESL warns "Malaysia's regular labour market has not yet opened". Only the 7,873 listed workers left behind on 31 May 2024 may go, through BOESL only, in construction or tourism, at Tk 1,62,500 [W0020].
  - 29 Oct and 18 Nov 2025: Malaysia sends selection criteria for Bangladeshi recruiting agencies (the same criteria for BD, India, Nepal, Pakistan and Myanmar); the Ministry invites agencies to apply [W0022, W0023].
  - 25 Nov 2025: sending of left-behind workers starts through BOESL. 7,873 of 17,777 were assigned to BOESL; a CLAB agreement was signed 7 Aug 2025; CLAB demanded 500 workers and 255 had visas [W0018, W0019].
  - 1 Dec 2025: Ministry warns that the news of "25 agencies approved" was false at that time [W0021].
  - Now (undated FWCMS page, content matches Oct 2026): FWCMS's registered-agency list shows 338 Bangladeshi entries: 25 principal agencies with 10 associates each, plus BOESL with 62 associates [W0008]. News of a Ministry release of 15 Oct 2026 matches this: recruitment restarted, 10,000 workers at zero cost agreed in principle, and 338 agencies [W0011-W0014, lead].
  - 5 Nov 2026: first BOESL zero-cost Malaysia circular, for 112 DHL Supply Chain jobs, applications 6-8 Nov 2026 [W0001-W0006].
- **So, as of 6 Nov 2026:** recruitment is reopening in a limited form. Only the 25 + BOESL principal agencies and their 312 associates on FWCMS can recruit [W0008]. BOESL runs zero-cost government-channel circulars [W0001, W0005]. No new BMET Malaysia permission letter under the reopened system has been posted yet [W0026].
- Whether the agency maximum fee of Tk 78,990 still applies to private-agency job orders after reopening is not given by any official 2026 source. The 2026 news says "zero cost" for the 10,000 [W0011, lead].

## 2. The labour agreement (MoU) and G2G

- MoU signed 19 Dec 2021 [W0027]. Its text is not online (blocked list). News says the Tk 78,990 cap covered passport, health check, registration, welfare, insurance, ID card, clothing and agency fees [W0071, lead].
- Prothom Alo dates the MoU 18 Dec 2022 and says Malaysia's Human Resources Ministry selects agencies [W0015, lead]. This conflicts with BMET's 19 Dec 2021 date [W0027] and is probably a news error.
- Agency selection is by Malaysia, using criteria it sent on 28 Oct 2025 [W0022, W0023]. Criteria: 5 years' operation; 3,000 workers in the last 3 years (the 29 Oct version said 5 years); 3 destination countries; a certificate of good conduct; a clean record; its own training centre; 5 employer testimonials; and an office of at least 10,000 sq ft [W0023].
- **G2G / BOESL:** BOESL now recruits for Malaysia at zero cost through circulars. Workers apply online via OEP (employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration), because BOESL's own portal brms.boesl.gov.bd has been closed since 11 Oct 2026 [W0001, W0007]. Selection is by software lottery, then the employer's test; selected workers pay nothing [W0004, W0005]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents, and its fees are paid only by bank pay order, never cash [W0068].

## 3. Employer side (how a real Malaysia job is created)

1. The employer gets quota approval from the One Stop Centre (OSC) and pays the levy [W0041, W0043, W0044]. One IMI page puts the OSC under KDN [W0043]; another puts it under KSM [W0044]. This is a conflict.
2. Sectors allowed: manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services [W0041]. Bangladesh is allowed in all sectors except frozen ones [W0042].
3. Levy amounts are only in a lead: RM1,850 for manufacturing, construction and services, and RM750 for plantation and agriculture [W0069]. The levy is always the employer's cost [W0054, W0027, W0028].
4. The employer applies for the VDR online via FWCMS; the worker waits in Bangladesh [W0045]. The documents include the KDN approval letter, the levy receipt, a security bond and the Bio-Medical report from Bangladesh [W0055]. The worker's visa fee is RM20 and the bond RM500 [W0051].
5. From 1 Feb 2025 all new ePLKS applications and insurance purchases go through FWCMS [W0056].
6. BMET requires the demand letter to carry the Malaysian KSM/KDN/FWCMS approval number [W0039]. Example format: "KSM/FWCMSBW490000267/QA03/1 Date: 26/07/2023" [W0037].
7. The Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing attests the demand. The employer must submit a commitment letter, notarised and attested by Malaysia's Foreign Ministry: enough work, reception at KLIA, housing, no idle time, regular salary [W0059]. Permission letters cite the attestation, e.g. block visa "BHC-LW-36" or "embassy attestation letter no. 158, 29/08/2025" [W0033, W0038].

## 4. Bangladesh side, Malaysia-specific

- **BMET/Ministry permission letters (নিয়োগানুমতি) for Malaysia** are posted on bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos. Search "Malaysia" (English) or মালয়েশিয়া typed with the precomposed য় character; the decomposed spelling returns nothing. There is also a menu category "মালয়েশিয়ায় কর্মানুমতি" [W0026].
  - Each letter gives: agency and RL number; the employer and its Malaysian approval reference; posts; salary; age range; hours; food, housing and airfare; contract length; and the agency maximum [W0028-W0038].
  - From 2025 there is also an English "Recruitment Permission Letter" page with a permission ID (e.g. MY-RL1274-2025-0000883), block visa number, "Attested: Yes" and the Ministry reference [W0032].
- **Agency maximums seen:** Tk 78,990 [W0037, W0038], Tk 78,740 [W0033], Tk 72,040 [W0035], Tk 41,240 [W0028], Tk 38,710 and zero [W0034]. All are payable only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, after visa and flight are confirmed [W0028].
- **Conditions in every letter** [W0030, W0031, W0036]:
  - workers are selected from the BMET database (Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 s.19(3)) [W0030];
  - the contract is signed before clearance, and each worker gets a BMET-attested Bangla translation [W0030];
  - the employer pays both-way airfare [W0036];
  - an agency representative travels with each flight or meets workers at the Malaysian airport [W0031];
  - the flight schedule goes to the High Commission and BMET [W0031];
  - an agency gets no new permissions until it repays the debts owed to workers it failed to send [W0036].
- **Job terms seen:** RM1,500 (2023-25) and RM1,700 (Sep 2025, Nov 2026) [W0029, W0038, W0001]; 3-year renewable contracts; 8 hours a day and 6 days a week (one letter: 7.5 hours; another: 45 hours a week) [W0029, W0034, W0035]; food usually at the worker's cost, housing and medical free [W0033, W0035, W0037]; 90-day probation [W0035].
- **Ages seen:** 18-35, 18-40, 18-45, 23-45, and 22-35 for BOESL/DHL [W0029, W0034, W0033, W0038, W0003]. IMI's limit is 18-45 [W0073].
- **BMET database registration for Malaysia (2022 notice):** Tk 200 at a District Employment and Manpower Office (DEMO) or TTC, or via the Ami Probashi app (+Tk 100); valid 2 years; registration does not guarantee a job [W0040]. Registration has since moved to OEP (generic part).
- **Recruitment flow 2026 (lead):** the agency collects the job order and selects workers; BOESL or a processing agency handles the calling visa and e-visa and issues an NOC; the recruiting agency gets BMET clearance in its own name [W0012, W0014].

## 5. Steps the worker does

- **Medical in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical):** only at an FWCMS-accredited centre (51 in Bangladesh on the FWCMS list); it is mandatory before the eVDR [W0009, W0010].
  - Lead: the Ministry fixed the fee at Tk 8,000 on 31 Oct 2026; there is no fee for the MiGRAMS slip; the worker may use any authorised centre; if the worker changes agency, the old agency must pass on the report; overcharging can be reported to secretary@probashi.gov.bd [W0016, W0017].
  - Lead: Malaysia removed "controversial" centres, and a joint Malaysian team will inspect the rest [W0013].
- **Visa (VDR) issuance:** lodged through the Malaysian High Commission Dhaka's authorised Visa Application Agencies; there is no direct submission to the High Commission [W0061, W0062]. Fees for Bangladeshi passports: visa BDT 600 (RM20), processing RM105 (about BDT 2,500), plus the agency's own fee [W0063]. For workers, IMI says the employer pays visa, pass and levy [W0054]. The VDR is issued only after IMI approval [W0064].
- **Police clearance:** IMI's VDR/PLKS document list doesn't include one [W0055]. See Need to know.
- **Pre-departure in Bangladesh:** the contract is signed after training and before departure; the worker joins the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB) [W0060, W0030].
- **Arrival:**
  - entry only through gazetted entry points with the VDR and visa [W0046];
  - the employer must collect the worker within 6 hours [W0046];
  - the employer moves the worker to the hostel [W0060];
  - the FOMEMA exam must be done within 30 days per IMI [W0047] (3 days per IMI slides, lead W0069);
  - the PLKS is issued only if the worker is fit; an unfit worker is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [W0047];
  - the PLKS is valid for 12 months and renewable [W0048];
  - the i-Kad comes free with the PLKS [W0053];
  - a bank account in the worker's name, with wages paid by bank [W0060].
- **PLKS conditions:** no family, no change of employer or sector, no marriage [W0052]. The maximum stay is 10 years per one IMI page and 5 per another: a conflict [W0049, W0050].
- **Cost in practice (unofficial, not verified):** Tk 5.44 lakh (Verité survey, 2024 report) and Tk 4.5-5 lakh, against the Tk 78,990 cap [W0070, W0072].

## 6. How to check each document

- **Agency:**
  - It must be on FWCMS's "Registered Recruitment Agencies" list, Bangladesh section: fwcms.com.my/affiliates/#recruitment-agencies [W0008].
  - It must also be Active on OEP. Of the 25 principal agencies, 20 matched an Active RL in BMET's 28 Oct 2026 list by exact name; 5 didn't match by name and need checking by RL (not ledgered; researcher's cross-check).
  - For BOESL jobs, only BOESL circulars count (boesl.gov.bd notice board) [W0001, W0068].
- **BMET permission letter:** search bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos (method above) and compare the employer, salary, maximum fee and Malaysian reference [W0026, W0032].
- **Demand letter:** it must carry the KSM/KDN/FWCMS reference [W0039] and the High Commission's attestation number, which appears in the permission letter [W0033, W0038].
- **VDR/PLKS application status:** IMI MyIMMs eServices → "Inquiry Application Status for Foreign Maid/Foreign Worker" (imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main?pra) [W0065].
  - It needs the employer's IC or company registration number plus the application number, so the worker has to ask the employer or agency for them.
  - Statuses: BARU (being processed), LULUS (approved; ready for payment and sticker; do FOMEMA), BAYAR (paid), CETAK (sticker printed), TOLAK (rejected), BATAL (cancelled), TANGGUH (deferred) [W0065].
- **Pass after arrival:** IMI "Semakan Maklumat ePass" (imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid?semakan). Enter the ePass number, passport number and nationality; it shows name, nationality, pass type and expiry, or "Pas Tidak Sah" (pass not valid). The page says it is meant for authorities and agencies [W0066].
- **Employer exists:** SSM e-Info (ssm-einfo.my); search by name or registration number; a company profile costs RM15.40 [W0067].
- **FOMEMA result:** blocked (see Blocked).

## 7. Scams (official warnings)

- Paying anyone before the government announces recruitment (BMET 2021) [W0027].
- False news of agency approvals (Ministry, Dec 2025) [W0021].
- Claims that "the market is open" used to collect money (BOESL, Sep 2025) [W0020].
- Fraud using BOESL's name: BOESL has no agents, takes no fee before selection, and takes no cash [W0068].
- Medical centre overcharging and brokers for the medical (lead) [W0016].
- In practice (unofficial): syndicate fees [W0070, W0072]. Workers arriving to no job is implied by the High Commission's employer commitment letter ("No workers will have to sit idle") [W0059].
- Applicant-side: the BMET database rule bars clearance for workers outside the database [W0030]; no official Malaysia-specific warning on fake ages or documents was found (Need to know).

## 1. After arrival

- **Collection and medical.** The employer must collect the worker within 6 hours of arrival [L0041]. The employer then has 30 days to get a FOMEMA medical at a FOMEMA-registered clinic [L0042]. The PLKS is issued only if FOMEMA certifies the worker fit; an unfit worker is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [L0043]. The older IMI page says all arrival steps, including the PLKS sticker, must be done within one month of entry [L0080].
- **Who pays.** The employer pays the deposit, visa, pass and levy [L0068]. For a Bangladeshi worker the visa is RM 20 and the security bond RM 500 [L0052]. Payments to Immigration are made only after approval, against an official receipt [L0081].
- **FOMEMA itself.** Need to know: the fee, who pays it, and how a worker checks the result. The FOMEMA site was unreachable and its domain is misconfigured. News leads give RM207 for men and RM217 for women after Dec 2023, but these are unverified and conflicting, so they are not in the ledger. A MOHR policy PDF reportedly says the employer pays (blocked).
- **i-Kad.** Every PLKS holder gets an i-Kad (the foreign worker ID card), valid for as long as the PLKS. It is free and grey, and the worker does not apply for it [L0062, L0063, L0126]. The employer collects it at the Immigration office where the PLKS was applied for, 7 days after the notification email [L0127]. The worker must carry it at all times and show it to the authorities on request [L0128]. The High Commission's process also says the employer must ensure an employment visa and i-Kad for every worker [L0035].
- **Checking your pass.** IMI's MyIMMs ePass check (imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid?semakan) asks for the ePass number, document number and nationality. It shows the name, nationality, pass type and expiry date. The page says it is "for authorities or agencies" [L0129]. Need to know: whether workers can use it and where the ePass number is printed.
- **Passport.** Under the MoU contract the employer may not keep the worker's passport [L0149]. The worker hands it over only for the medical, the PLKS, the i-Kad and renewal, and gets it back afterwards [L0151]. The employer pays if it is lost while in its custody [L0154]. The worker carries the passport, a copy of the PLKS or an ID document [L0150]. IMI says foreigners should carry their passport at all times [L0085]. Workers report employers not returning passports [L0190, unofficial]. Need to know: the Malaysian legal offence for withholding a passport (Passports Act 1966 s.12(1)(f); ATIPSOM). The LOM text is blocked.
- **Bank account.** The MoU contract says wages go into a bank account in the worker's name [L0132]. The High Commission's process says the employer opens the account [L0034]. The Employment Act as amended requires wages to be paid into an account at a financial institution; cash needs the worker's written request plus Director General approval [L0104, L0105]. Need to know: which ID banks accept (passport plus i-Kad?), from BNM or the banks. BNM's basic savings account was for citizens and permanent residents in 2005; that is a lead only, not logged.
- **SIM card.** Need to know. News leads say MCMC Determination No. 1 of 2026 requires non-Malaysians to register with the original passport on a biometric passport reader, with a limit of 2 prepaid SIMs per telco. No official MCMC page was retrieved.
- **Health insurance and SOCSO.** Renewal needs the SPIKPA slip (except plantation) and the SPPA (foreign workers compensation scheme) slip [L0046, L0048]. The contract insures the worker under the PERKESO Employment Injury Scheme and, where applicable, SPIKPA [L0136]. Need to know: SPIKPA cover and premium, and PERKESO registration, contribution and benefits (blocked). The leads say the employer pays 1.25%, and that from 2026 there is an invalidity scheme with 0.5% employer and 0.5% worker. EPF (KWSP) 2% + 2% for foreign workers from October 2025 wages is also a lead; the MOHR PDF is blocked.

## 2. Rights at work

- **Hours.** At most 45 normal hours a week in Peninsular Malaysia [L0108, L0092]. The contract says 8 hours a day [L0133]. Overtime only if the worker agrees, paid under labour law [L0134]. Need to know: the overtime rate (1.5x on normal days, 2x on rest days, 3x on public holidays under s.60/60A/60D) from the Act text (blocked).
- **Rest day.** 1 a week [L0135].
- **Annual leave.** 8, 12 or 16 days by length of service [L0109].
- **Sick leave.** 14, 16 or 22 days by length of service, plus 60 days' hospitalisation leave [L0110].
- **Maternity.** 98 days [L0112, L0093]. A pregnant worker cannot be dismissed except for misconduct, wilful breach or closure [L0094].
- **Paternity.** 7 days [L0095].
- **Public holidays.** Need to know: the number of paid public holidays (the contract only says "per labour law").
- **Minimum wage.** Need to know (official). The contract says the basic wage must meet the Malaysian minimum wage [L0130]. Sarawak requires the minimum wage and the same rate as local workers [L0169]. Leads: RM1,700 under P.U.(A) 376/2024 from 1 Feb 2025 (1 Aug 2025 for employers with fewer than 5 workers), and a rise to RM2,000 from June 2027 announced in Budget 2027 on 9 Oct 2026 [L0188, lead].
- **Pay.** Monthly, by the 7th of the next month [L0131]. Deductions are capped at 50% a month, to recover advances [L0137]. Sarawak bans deductions for recruitment or repatriation costs [L0170]. Need to know: whether levy may be deducted in Peninsular Malaysia. The IMI page makes the employer responsible for the levy [L0068]; the Act's s.24 text is blocked.
- **Housing.** The employer provides reasonable accommodation with basic amenities [L0138]. Need to know: the Act 446 minimum standards (blocked). The employer commitment letter to BDHC KL also promises "good accommodation" (seen in `bdhckl-employer-notice-2023-pdf`, not logged by me).
- **Forced labour.** Under s.90B, an employer who threatens, deceives or forces a worker and stops them leaving the workplace faces a fine of up to RM100,000 or 2 years' jail [L0100, L0101]. The Director General of Labour can decide discrimination disputes [L0102]. A court can order a convicted employer to pay what it owes, by seizing its property [L0103].
- **Contract.** The MoU contract is in Malay, Bangla and English, and English prevails [L0153]. Notice is 2 months on either side [L0144, L0145]. The worker may not change employment or run another business [L0146]. Marriage in Malaysia can lead to the PLKS being revoked [L0148, L0056].

## 3. How long you can stay

- **Per issue.** The PLKS lasts 12 months and is renewed from 3 months before expiry [L0044]. The employer renews it and pays any penalty if it is late [L0139, L0140]. Renewals are filed by the employer only [L0079]. Bangladeshi renewals are done only at Immigration HQ in Putrajaya (2021 FAQ) [L0174]. A renewal filed after expiry is treated as an overstay case [L0045]. Renewal needs a passport valid more than 12 months [L0047], and a FOMEMA medical for the 2nd and 3rd year renewals [L0049].
- **Total.** Up to 10 years in total, or 3 years for workers registered under 6P [L0050]. This **conflicts** with "not more than 5 years" on the older PLKS page [L0066].
- **Age and family.** Age 18-45 at application [L0064]. No family can come [L0053].
- **Permanent residence.** Need to know: no official statement that PLKS never leads to permanent residence was found.

## 4. What can get you jailed

- **Illegal entry or no valid document (s.6(3)).** Fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail or both, and up to 6 strokes of the cane; or a compound of up to RM3,000 [L0083, L0084].
- **Overstay (s.15(4)).** Fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years or both; or a compound of up to RM3,000 [L0086, L0087].
- **Forged visa or pass (s.55D).** At least RM30,000, 5-10 years and up to 6 strokes [L0088].
- **False passport (Passports Act s.12B).** RM15,000-100,000, 2-10 years and up to 6 strokes [L0089].
- **Drugs.** Banned from import and export; "the punishment for drug smuggling is the death penalty" (CAAM, May 2026) [L0155]. Need to know: the current s.39B text after the Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act 2023 (lead: death is now discretionary, with life imprisonment and caning as the alternative).
- **Customs.** Declare all dutiable or prohibited goods [L0156]; false declarations carry stiff penalties [L0157]. Prohibited imports include obscene material on any media, daggers and flick knives, and cloth printed with Quran verses [L0158-L0160]. Need to know: an official warning about carrying parcels for others (none found on a Malaysian site).
- **Using an agent for Immigration matters** is an offence (employer side) [L0175].

## 5. If you aren't paid, or a dispute

- **Official route found.** The Working for Workers (WFW) app from the Ministry of Human Resources. It is for all workers, local or foreign, and takes complaints about unpaid wages, unfair conditions or any Employment Act breach, 24/7, without visiting an office [L0163-L0165]. It keeps the complaint confidential and lets the worker track its status [L0166]. It is on Android and iOS; the portal is workforworkers.com.my [L0167].
- **Labour Court.** Need to know: the Labour Court claim under s.69 (where to file, documents, statutory declaration, fee, time limit, how long it takes), the forced labour complaint line, and Industrial Relations Department unfair-dismissal representations. All are on JTK or MOHR pages that are blocked. The 2022 amendment raised the fine for not obeying a Labour Court order to RM50,000 [L0106].
- **BD High Commission KL labour wing.** For any labour welfare problem, email ss.lab.kl@gmail.com, fsl.bhc.kl@gmail.com or counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd [L0021]. The labour officers listed in Oct 2026 are:
  - Minister (Labour) Md. Siddiqur Rahman, ministermalaysia@probashi.gov.bd, +603 2604 0934 [L0029].
  - Minister (Labour) Syed Shariful Islam, counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, +601 2290 3252 [L0028].
  - Counsellor (Labour), +603 2604 0929 [L0030].

  The mission is at No 8 Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 KL, PABX 603-2604-0946/48/49 [L0032, L0033]. For injury compensation, a working worker files a case at the labour office with payslips, the contract and attendance records, and gives the High Commission a copy [L0037].
- **What happens in practice.** In 2023 the mission took a case to the Human Resources Ministry, which summoned the employer [L0193, unofficial]. Workers report employers' agents warning them not to complain [L0192, unofficial]. Activists estimate 100,000-200,000 Bangladeshis in Malaysia are jobless or unpaid [L0191, unofficial].
- **Sabah and Sarawak.** Their worker applications fall under state jurisdiction [L0065]. Sarawak uses its own Labour Ordinance (Cap. 76) licence for non-resident workers [L0168]. That licence requires the minimum wage and pay equal to locals, bans recruitment or repatriation deductions, requires PERKESO, makes the licence holder responsible for repatriation, and requires a police report if a worker absconds [L0169-L0173]. Need to know: Sabah (the JTK Sabah site is unreachable).

## 6. Changing employer

- **Status.** PLKS holders may not change employer or sector [L0055]. The older IMI page says it is possible with Home Ministry (KDN) permission [L0067, L0176]. Outsourcing companies may move workers only within the same sector [L0082]. The contract also bars changing jobs [L0146]. For domestic helpers, Immigration can move an abused helper to a new employer without the first employer's consent [L0078].
- **Need to know.** The JTK/BPPA "Pertukaran Majikan" (PTM) process. A lead from a search snippet of the BPPA form dated 7 Jun 2024 says it is allowed when the employer is bankrupt or has closed; not for workers in their 10th year or later; the PLKS must have at least 3 months left; a JTK support letter comes first; and the original employer files it. The form is blocked; no ledger row (no saved raw).

## 7. Reported absent (lari) or pass cancelled

- **What counts.** Leaving the workplace without telling the employer, or not returning on the agreed date after going home, counts as "lari" [L0057, L0058].
- **What the employer does.** It reports at Immigration with its ID, a copy of the passport, the runaway form and a police report [L0059]. To cancel the PLKS it brings the worker's original passport and a certified police report [L0075]. It must also tell the Director General of Labour within 14 days (s.60KA) [L0099]. In Sarawak it must report to the police [L0173].
- **What happens to the worker.** Workers reported as lari are blacklisted [L0060, L0178]. The employer's bond is forfeited [L0061]. Prohibited immigrants include people removed from any country whom the Director General deems undesirable [L0091].
- **Need to know.** How a worker checks whether he has been reported (the ePass check [L0129] may show the pass status), and the official remedy for a false report. The labour route is WFW [L0163]; Immigration appeal procedure not found.

## 8. Leaving at the end of the job

- **Check-Out Memo.** The employer applies for the Check-Out Memo (Memo Periksa Keluar) with the form, the worker's original passport and a ticket [L0071, L0072]. The worker leaves on the date and through the exit point in the memo [L0073]. Without a memo the employer stays responsible for the worker [L0074]. Workers must be sent home when the job ends, after dismissal, when the pass expires or is cancelled, or when they are found unfit [L0069]. The bond is refunded only after a proper exit with the memo [L0051]. The employer tells JTK within 30 days of termination (s.60KA) [L0098].
- **Ticket home.** The employer pays the fare home on completion of the contract [L0141]. It also pays when it ends the contract (except for the worker's breach), when the worker quits because the employer broke the contract, and after serious work injury or illness [L0142]. Otherwise the worker pays [L0143].
- **Wages owed.** On early repatriation the employer pays all outstanding wages [L0152].
- **Leaving the country.** You need a passport valid at least 6 months and a valid pass [L0186, L0187].
- **Lost passport.** Report to Immigration Enforcement with the police report, the new passport and a ticket within 7 days; the Special Pass costs RM100 [L0182-L0185, L0177].

## 9. Going home on leave

- Not returning on the agreed date counts as running away [L0058].
- **Need to know.** Whether the PLKS sticker allows re-entry, and whether any permit or employer letter is needed. No official IMI text on PLKS re-entry was found. The leave entitlement is annual leave [L0109].

## 10. Undocumented workers going home

- **Programme Repatriasi Migran 2.0 (PRM 2.0).** Runs 1 May 2026 to 31 May 2027. Walk-in at one of 14 Immigration Enforcement offices in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan; hotline 03-8880 1269 [L0113-L0118]. Bring a valid passport or travel document and a ticket home for a date within 14 days [L0116, L0117]. Compounds are RM500 for no valid pass, RM500 for overstay and RM300 for breaking pass conditions, plus RM20 for each special pass [L0120-L0123]. Pay by debit or credit card or Touch 'n Go eWallet only [L0119]. Children under 18 pay no compound but need the RM20 pass [L0124]. The earlier phase ended 30 April 2026 [L0125]. News adds that participants are exempt from prosecution subject to conditions [L0189, lead]. Need to know: whether Sabah and Sarawak are covered (the poster says Peninsula and Labuan) and the exclusions (news: PRM 1.0 no-shows, blacklisted people, people with warrants).
- **Order of steps per the High Commission.** First pay Immigration's fine and get a Special Pass, then buy a ticket and leave on time. Without a valid passport, get a Travel Permit from the High Commission first [L0013].
- **Travel Permit (out pass).**
  - **Who can apply:** Bangladeshis whose passport has expired, been lost or damaged [L0001].
  - **What to bring:** apply in person, with a copy of your past passport and a copy of your NID or online birth registration certificate [L0003-L0005]. The older pages add the form, 3 photos and an interview. Instead of a passport copy, a letter from the UNO (or from the DC for Cox's Bazar residents), emailed to the labour wing, is accepted [L0009, L0010, L0014, L0015].
  - **Validity:** 3 months [L0002].
  - **Fee:** **conflict**. RM 38 (bdhckl.gov.bd, Aug 2026; and the Jan 2026 fee chart) [L0006] against RM 44 at a Maybank counter (2023 page and form) [L0008].
  - **Processing time:** **conflict**. 1-2 working days [L0012] against same day after 4 pm [L0016].
  - **Payment:** by card at the counter, or a Maybank deposit to account 564427102268 [L0007].
- **In detention.** Detainees with a passport only need a ticket, and the depot handles the rest. For detainees without a passport, the camp sends the TP application and the High Commission issues the TP to the camp, with no government fee [L0017, L0018]. There are 13 depots, 11 in the Peninsula and 2 in Sarawak [L0181]. To visit a detainee you need an Immigration permission letter; the detainee's body number or police report number helps find them [L0179, L0180].
- **Bans.** Need to know: the official ban length after PRM or deportation. "Prohibited immigrant" status applies to people removed and deemed undesirable [L0091].
- **Program Rekalibrasi (RTK 2.0).** The IMI FAQ page (2023) is empty. Need to know: its end date. It is believed closed; no official status found.

## 11. If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

- **NOC to send a body home (High Commission).** For a documented worker:
  - the passport;
  - the receiver's details in Bangladesh;
  - a police report and NOC for abnormal deaths;
  - the original death certificate;
  - the funeral company's letter;
  - the employer's letter;
  - a copy of the employer's PERKESO accident/illness notice for foreign workers (form PA-F101R1) [L0023-L0025].

  For an undocumented worker: the passport or a police report that it was lost, the receiver's details, a police report and NOC, the death certificate, the funeral company's letter, and the employer's name and contact [L0026, L0027].
- **Sending the body home (labour wing page, 2024).** The company writes to the High Commission and appoints a casket company. Papers go to the High Commission: the company letter, insurance claim, compensation letter, hospital letter, death certificate, police report and passport. The family sends a claim/consent letter naming the receiver. Inform the Probashi Kallyan desk at the airport [L0038, L0039]. The Welfare Board pays for the body to be sent home if no employer is found [L0040]. Under the contract the employer pays repatriation after serious work injury or illness [L0142]. Need to know: who pays to send a body home under Malaysian rules (the PERKESO funeral benefit reportedly includes repatriation; blocked).
- **Compensation.** The employer notifies the insurer, and the insurer and Labour Department investigate. For a death, Labour Department Form H is sent to the heirs in Bangladesh, completed and returned through the High Commission [L0036]. Need to know: PERKESO Employment Injury Scheme benefits, dependants' benefit, how to claim and time limits (blocked). The Welfare Board benefits listed by the High Commission: Tk 1 lakh for treatment, Tk 35,000 at the airport, a Tk 3 lakh grant, and recovery of compensation and unpaid wages [L0020]. Joining the Welfare Board from Malaysia costs RM 155 [L0019]. This overlaps with the Bangladesh-side part.

## 12. Domestic workers

Bangladesh is **not** an approved source country for foreign domestic helpers (PRA). The approved countries are Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam and Laos on the PLKS page; the PRA page also lists Nepal and still not Bangladesh [L0076, L0194]. Helpers must be women aged 21-45 [L0077].

## Draft steps

### After arrival
1. **At KLIA, wait for your employer.** It must collect you within 6 hours [L0041]. The High Commission expects the employer to take you to the hostel [L0034].
2. **Do the FOMEMA medical within 30 days** at a clinic the employer takes you to. The employer pays the Immigration fees, levy and bond (Bangladesh: visa RM20, bond RM500) [L0042, L0068, L0052]. If you fail, you are sent home with a Check-Out Memo [L0043]. Need to know: the FOMEMA fee, who pays it, and how to see your result.
3. **Get your PLKS sticker and i-Kad.** The employer collects the i-Kad free, 7 days after Immigration's email. Carry it always [L0126-L0128].
4. **Get your passport back.** The employer may hold it only for the medical, the PLKS, the i-Kad and renewal [L0149, L0151]. If it is kept, complain on WFW [L0163]. Need to know: the Malaysian offence for withholding a passport.
5. **Make sure wages go into a bank account in your name, by the 7th of each month** [L0131, L0132, L0104]. Need to know: the ID banks accept, and how to get a SIM.
6. **Check you are covered** by PERKESO's Employment Injury Scheme and SPIKPA [L0136, L0046]. Need to know: the benefits and how to check.

### If you aren't paid
1. **Complain on the WFW app** (Android or iOS, or workforworkers.com.my): it takes unpaid-wage complaints from foreign workers, is confidential, and lets you track the case [L0163-L0167].
2. **Email the High Commission labour wing** (counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, ss.lab.kl@gmail.com, fsl.bhc.kl@gmail.com) or call +601 2290 3252 / +603 2604 0934 [L0021, L0028, L0029].
3. **Keep payslips, the contract and attendance records** [L0037].
4. Need to know: the Labour Court (s.69) filing steps, time limit and cost.

### End of job or going home
1. **The employer applies for the Check-Out Memo** with your passport and ticket. Leave on the date and through the exit point on the memo [L0071-L0073].
2. **The employer pays your ticket** at the end of the contract, if it ends the contract, if you leave because it broke the contract, or after serious work injury [L0141, L0142]. If you resign, give 2 months' notice and pay your own ticket [L0145].
3. **Collect all unpaid wages before you leave** [L0152].

### Undocumented
1. **If you have no valid passport, get a Travel Permit** at the High Commission:
   - Go in person with a copy of your old passport and your NID or online birth certificate. Without these, a UNO letter emailed to the labour wing.
   - Bring photos.
   - Pay RM38 (or RM44, see conflict) by card or by Maybank deposit to account 564427102268.
   - It is valid 3 months [L0001-L0007, L0014, L0015].
2. **Walk in to an Immigration Enforcement office** (14 in the Peninsula and Labuan) under PRM 2.0 before 31 May 2027:
   - Bring your passport or TP and a ticket for a date within 14 days.
   - Pay the RM500 or RM300 compound plus RM20 by card or Touch 'n Go.
   - Hotline 03-8880 1269 [L0113-L0124].
3. **If detained,** the depot arranges the flight. The High Commission issues a free TP to the camp [L0017, L0018].

### Death
1. **The employer writes to the High Commission and reports the death to PERKESO (form PA-F101R1)** [L0023, L0024, L0038].
2. **The family sends a consent letter** naming who will receive the body in Bangladesh [L0039].
3. **The High Commission issues the NOC** once it has the passport, death certificate, police report and NOC (for abnormal deaths), funeral company letter and employer letter [L0023-L0027].
4. **For compensation, the heirs fill Form H** and return it through the High Commission [L0036].

# Part V findings: short-stay entry for Bangladesh passport holders (tourist/social visit, MDAC, border, business, medical, transit, PVP, checks, visas not open, scams)

Researched 6 Nov 2026. Ledger ids V0001-V0114 (ledger-parts/V.csv). Official facts only; news rows are `lead` / `unofficial-practice`.
Key official sources: MyVISA FAQ (last updated 6 Oct 2026) and Terms & Conditions (6 Oct 2026) at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my (both Firecrawl, SPA); MyVISA home announcements (18 and 28 Sep 2026); IMI (imi.gov.my, BM and EN pages, all undated); IMI MEV notice image (Sep 2026); MDAC site and infographics; High Commission of Malaysia Dhaka (kln.gov.my/web/bgd_dhaka, undated; its "Last Updated" footer shows the access date on every KLN page, so treated as undated); ESD (PVP); MHTC (malaysiahealthcare.org); Immigration Act 1959/63 reprint (2006) hosted on imi.gov.my.

## 1. Tourist / social visit (Visa Without Reference, VTR, via eVISA)

**Do Bangladeshis need a visa?** Yes. Bangladesh is one of 31 nationalities for which an eVISA is compulsory for social visits (tourism, family visits, short business trips) [V0001]; IMI lists Bangladesh as visa-required, by any entry point (no air-only mark) [V0002]. Ordinary passport: visa needed, stay not more than 30 days [V0003]. Diplomatic/official passports are visa-exempt for social visits under a 2014 bilateral agreement [V0004].

**Which eVISA:** Single Entry Visa (SEV) for social visits. From 1 Sep 2026 the Multiple Entry Visa (MEV) is restricted to four purposes (Fly & Cruise, Business, Medical Treatment, Wedding Tourism) for the eligible visa-required nationalities [V0009, V0010, V0011]. eNTRI does not apply (see table). News lead: Bangladeshi tourists will now generally get single-entry visas [V0108].

**Where:** only malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my (also MyVISA mobile app) [V0005, V0006]. Not from inside Malaysia [V0007]; from the country of residence [V0008]. Bangladesh-specific: the portal shows "Visa applications from Bangladesh can only be submitted between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM (Bangladesh Time) on weekdays" [V0025] (text in portal code; seen only after login).

**Account:** Individual > Sign Up, fill form, CAPTCHA (case sensitive), Register; temporary password by email; change it at first login [V0021]. Login = email + 6-digit OTP + password [V0022]. One account: 5 applications at a time, max 5 people; apply yourself, not via someone else [V0023]. Gmail/Hotmail/Yahoo/QQ recommended if confirmation email doesn't arrive (FAQ). From 1 Oct 2026 drafts in "New Application"/"Pending Payment" for >7 working days expire [V0024].

**Documents (VTR):** studio photo; passport bio page valid >6 months from travel; page with previous passport number; round-trip ticket/proof of journey; proof of paid accommodation; proof of financial support; birth certificate for minors; other supporting documents [V0026]. Proof of funds = cash, card, traveller's cheques or BNM-recognised e-money, backed by last 3 months' bank statements, plus return ticket [V0027]. HC Dhaka list instead says 6 months' bank statement with a solvency letter, 2 photos, hotel reservation, invitation letter if any [V0028] (CONFLICT). Staying with friends/relatives: cover letter to the Immigration Officer with your details, Malaysian address and host's name, IC number, phone; upload in the accommodation field [V0029]. No invitation letter: English PDF cover letter explaining why [V0030]. Merge several files into one per upload field [V0031]. Passport needs 3 blank pages [V0032]. Photo: studio, plain white background, <6 months old, full face, no headgear (religious exception), 35 x 50 mm, head 30-35 mm, no scans/selfies [V0033, V0034]. Name exactly as in passport; no surname -> leave Surname blank or full name in Given Name [V0035].

**Fee:** visa fee by nationality + RM105 processing charge + convenience charge 0.8% (card) / 1.7% (e-wallet) [V0016]. Bangladesh visa fee RM20 (IMI table) [V0017]; HC Dhaka: RM20 ≈ BDT 600, processing RM105 ≈ BDT 2,500, agencies' fee at their discretion [V0018]. Online only (credit card, Alipay/WeChat Pay) [V0020]. Non-refundable even if refused [V0019].

**Processing:** 2-7 working days, excluding queries/interviews/incomplete files [V0012]. Apply at least 14 days ahead (FAQ and T&C) [V0013, V0015] vs at least 30 days (HC Dhaka post, 15 Feb 2026) [V0014] (CONFLICT). Statuses: New, eVISA Under Review, Application Successful, Application Denied, Document Submission or Information Update Required, Interview Requested [V0040]; auto-rejected if documents/interview not done within 5 days of request [V0041].

**Result:** email with download link; download from account (Actions > Download eVISA); print A4 or show digital eVISA Note [V0036, V0037]. Validity: single journey within 6 months of issue (MyVISA T&C) [V0038] vs 3 months (HC Dhaka; IMI Types of Visas) [V0039] (CONFLICT). Stay: up to 30 days [V0003]. A new eVISA cancels any earlier active one [V0045]. Mismatch with passport = invalid; re-apply and pay again [V0044].

**Refusal / appeal:** mission may refuse without reasons; FAQ only advises re-applying with all requirements [V0042]. HC Dhaka: decision at Immigration Department's discretion and final; enquiries/complaints dhaka@imi.gov.my [V0043]. No appeal route found.

**Can't work:** no unauthorised employment, school, or foreign-media work [V0046, V0104]. Overstay: fines and possible later entry bans [V0047].

**Paper/agency route via HC Dhaka:** HC Dhaka FAQ says applications go through Government-appointed "Authorised Visa Application Agencies", no direct submission to the High Commission [V0048]; the "Agencies" page lists only Tourism Malaysia's Dhaka office [V0049]. The visa-requirement page now points to eVISA online. Applicants with travel documents other than passports must ask a mission about a "Normal Paper Visa" (FAQ).

**Leads (history):** tourist eVISA suspended for Bangladeshis until 30 Aug 2024 [V0105]; MyVISA paused for Bangladesh from 10 Feb 2026 "until further notice" [V0106, V0107]; resumption date not found (the portal shows BD-specific hours [V0025], so it has resumed).

## 2. MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card)

All foreign nationals must register within 3 days before arrival; passport valid 6 months from entry [V0058]. Mandatory since 1 Jan 2024; exemptions (Singaporeans, diplomatic/official passports, PR/long-term pass holders, border-pass holders) don't cover ordinary Bangladeshi visitors [V0060]. Steps: fill at https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main, then on arrival show MDAC registration and passport at the counter [V0059]. Form fields: name, passport no., date of birth, nationality, place of birth, sex, passport expiry, email x2, country code, mobile; arrival and departure dates, flight/vessel no., mode of travel (air/land/sea), last port of embarkation; trip must be within 3 days including the submission day [V0061] (rest of form not captured: blocked). Check registration with passport no. + nationality + PIN [V0062]; "Check Visit Pass" shows last 3 months' movement records [V0063]. Enquiries: eapp.imi.gov.my [V0064]. Fake site: IMI flagged malaysiacard.com (charged USD 29.99, "SuperFast" +14.99) [V0065, V0066]. **Need to know:** whether MDAC is free (no official page states a fee or "free"; IMI's fake-site warning implies paying is a sign of a fake).

## 3. At the border

Documents checked: passport (6 months), eVISA printout or eVISA Note, boarding pass/ticket, confirmed return ticket, proof of paid accommodation, other supporting documents (T&C adds financial support) [V0050]. eVISA doesn't guarantee entry [V0051]; refusals happen when the traveller isn't the visa holder or can't explain planned activities [V0052]. Prohibited immigrants include anyone who can't show means of support [V0053]; burden of proof is on the traveller (s.8(4)) [V0054]; the s.8(6) appeal to the Minister applies only to Permit holders [V0055]. General entry conditions: passport >6 months, visa, sufficient funds, not on Suspect List / lost-stolen document list, not prohibited [V0056]. Autogate for foreigners: only 10 countries, not Bangladesh [V0057].
Practice (unofficial): AKPS (Malaysian Border Control and Protection Agency) turned back 98 Bangladeshis on 15 Aug 2025 for fake hotel bookings, no return tickets, insufficient funds [V0109]; 123 Bangladeshis on tourist visas put on a "Not to Land" (NTL) list in July 2025 [V0110]; AKPS cited visitors claiming a month's stay with about RM500 [V0111].
**Need to know:** official minimum funds (none published; searched IMI, MyVISA, HC Dhaka, AKPS); official definition/consequences of a "Not to Land" notice (NTL is issued under s.8(3) per news; AKPS has no official site found); whether airline or traveller pays the return flight.

## 4. Business visit
Allowed on short-term social visit pass: meetings/conferences, business discussions, factory inspection, auditing, signing agreements, investment surveys, seminars, sports [V0068]. Business eVISA documents: company dispatch letter or invitation from Malaysian Government/GLC, return ticket [V0070]. Business MEV: 6 months validity, 30 days per entry [V0071]. Older pages (IMI Types of Visas; HC Dhaka FAQ) still describe MEV 3-12 months [V0072, V0114] (CONFLICT with 2026 rules). Professional/technical work needs PVP, not SVP [V0094]. Extension only for illness/accident/war at home, Form IMM.55 in person [V0069].

## 5. Medical visit
Medical eVISA only for treatment at an MHTC member hospital; get appointment letter first; apply at least 14 working days before [V0073]. At entry: passport, eVISA Note + eVAL printout, MHTC hospital appointment letter, companion details; max 2 companions (spouse, parent, child, grandparent, sibling, nurse, domestic helper) [V0074]. HC Dhaka: Bangladeshis apply as VTR through MHTC's authorised partners with an MHTC-hospital appointment letter [V0075]. MHTC steps: appointment letter -> MHTC support letter from hospital -> eVISA with passport, letters, return ticket, accommodation, photo -> 3-5 working days -> print [V0076]. Meet and Greet RM189/person, hospital books 3 working days ahead [V0077]. MHTC 2022: GD Assist Limited named MHTC's referral partner and official representative in Bangladesh [V0078] (possibly outdated). Medical MEV: 6 months [V0071]. **Need to know:** current list of MHTC authorised partners in Bangladesh; companion visa fee.

## 6. Transit
Short layover leaving the airport: apply on MyVISA, give airport address + cover letter [V0079]. No transit visa if staying airside on the same flight [V0080]; Malaysian mission (New Delhi): single PNR in the transit hall needs no visa; separate PNRs or KLIA-KLIA2 change need a visa [V0081]. TWOV (120-hour transit without visa for Indian-subcontinent citizens at KLIA/KLIA2) "remain currently suspended" [V0082, V0083]. Transit visa fee for Bangladesh RM20 [V0017]. VOA only for PRC and India [V0083]. **Need to know:** an IMI or HC Dhaka statement on TWOV for Bangladeshis (only Malaysian missions in India state it).

## 7. Professional Visit Pass (PVP)
For foreign talent with professional qualifications/skills to provide services or practical training with a Malaysian company on behalf of an overseas company, up to 12 months; company applies through its ESD account; work only for named company; no Dependant Pass [V0091]. Only a registered Malaysian sponsor can apply [V0092]; not for general workers/operators [V0093]; applicant stays abroad until approval; SVP can't be used for professional work [V0094]. Fees: ESD application RM1,200 (RM1,296 with SST) + PVP fee RM90 per quarter [V0095]. PVP-Expert categories: technical expert, research, internship, volunteers, exhibition participants; enter with VDR after approval [V0096]. Training programmes need the organiser to apply for a professional pass, then VDR [V0097]. Bond for Bangladesh RM750 [V0112]. **Need to know:** any nationality restriction on PVP for Bangladeshis (none found on IMI/ESD pages); ESD processing time.

## 1. Student Pass (feeds the "Student visa" H2)

### Who it is for and which institutions count
- A Student Pass is an Immigration pass to stay legally to study at an approved institution [S0023].
- Eligible: an offer from a public university (UA), or from a private higher education institution (IPTS) that KPT (Ministry of Higher Education) approved, whose programme MQA (Malaysian Qualifications Agency) accredited, and that has KDN (Home Ministry) permission to take international students; language, training/skills and accredited centres need KPM/KESUMA approval plus KDN permission [S0001].
- Entry level: 12 years of schooling and a recognised exam [S0043]. Full-time only (except mobility programmes), one degree at a time [S0042].
- Public universities go through the same EMGS/STARS route (IMI's rule covers UA and IPTS together; the fee calculator has "New Application for IPTA" RM1,728) [S0002, S0060]. Public-university students can do the medical at the university clinic [S0066].
- **Checking the course:** the MQR (Malaysian Qualifications Register, www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr) lists only accredited programmes, tied to the campus named; "no record" can mean provisional accreditation only (check www2.mqa.gov.my/pasp), never accredited, or failed; search by qualification name and type [S0084-S0087].
- **Need to know:** a public, searchable official register of IPTS registered with KPT and holding KDN permission to recruit international students (jpt.mohe.gov.my "IPT Swasta" page is empty; MOHE links only an internal directory app). Searched mohe.gov.my, jpt.mohe.gov.my, educationmalaysia.gov.my; Malay "senarai IPTS berdaftar", "semakan status IPTS".

### Steps
1. Offer letter, accepted [S0024, S0047].
2. Apply from **outside Malaysia only**, online via EMGS STARS (visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my, EMGS HUB app) or by the institution [S0002, S0024].
3. EMGS academic screening and "Approval to Study"; complete files go to Immigration HQ's Student Pass Unit for eVAL with travel-document and security checks [S0003]. Missing documents within 14 working days of EMGS returning the file [S0020]. Stages list [S0025].
4. eVAL (electronic Visa Approval Letter) issued; valid 6 months; no entry before it [S0027, S0028]. EMGS charter: 14 working days for a complete application [S0026].
5. Single Entry Visa: Bangladesh on EMGS's SEV list; apply on eVISA (malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my), not possible from inside Malaysia; EMGS lists Dhaka as the mission [S0004, S0005, S0029, S0053, S0054]. RM20 [S0065].
6. Arrive: EMGS ISAC counter (24 hours) at KLIA T1 (Level 3 arrival hall, before immigration) and T2 (Level 3A after the Skybridge) [S0082, S0083].
7. Report to the institution and do the post-arrival medical at an EMGS panel clinic within 7 days (EMGS says 7 working days); without it EMGS cannot start the endorsement; failed medical can be appealed at the same clinic within 3 days [S0006, S0055, S0056, S0030]. Panel clinic list on EMGS site and app [S0058].
8. Institution applies for the sticker with: original passport, its request letter, EMGS sticker support letter, stamped Personal Bond, 12-month Malaysian insurance [S0017]; then the pass is issued at the immigration office for the institution's address [S0006].

### Documents
Photo 35x45mm white background; passport data page, visa pages, observation page; passport valid 18+ months (EMGS recommends 12 months beyond the course for a one-off pass; IMI: copy of all pages incl. cover); offer letter; Health Declaration Form; certificates and transcripts; English certificate (MUET, IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, Cambridge, Linguaskill, OET, ELS CIEP; without it the pass will not cover the whole course) [S0045-S0048, S0050, S0019].

### Costs
- EMGS fee calculator: new application RM1,728 (IPTS or IPTA; RM1,600 + 8% service tax), short course RM864, private education institute (IPS, e.g. language centre) RM540, IPTS renewal RM270; medical RM340 (Sarawak RM350); Student Pass RM60; eVAL RM159; i-Kad RM50 (self-collect) or RM60.60 (courier); insurance RM498-644 a year or own policy via the institution; multiple-entry visa for Bangladesh RM20; course fees not included; 8% tax on processing fees since 1 Mar 2024 [S0060-S0066]. IMI: Student Pass RM60 [S0007].
- Personal bond, Bangladesh band: RM750, lodged with the institution, returned at completion or withdrawal if no immigration breach [S0059, S0049].
- EMGS schedule of fees, updated 23 Jun 2026 [S0068, S0069].
- Refunds: first-year processing and eVAL fees not refundable once invoiced; students can claim only for applications they created themselves; immigration fees refunded only if the pass was not issued [S0077-S0079].

### Insurance
Compulsory and bought locally; EMGS insurance covers from date of entry; top up if it ends before the pass [S0031, S0075, S0076].

### Validity, renewal, attendance, stopping study
- Usually up to 1 year at a time; renew before expiry, ideally 3 months before [S0032, S0033].
- Renewal needs CGPA 2.0 and 80% attendance, else an explanation letter from the institution; the renewal file includes results and attendance record [S0051, S0018]. No new medical for renewal [S0057].
- Changing course: Variation once, within 18 months after the first semester, Bachelor's level or above, with a release letter from the old institution; otherwise the institution shortens the pass with a Check Out Memo and the student must leave before reapplying; if no Student Pass yet, the student must leave [S0034-S0036, S0052]. Progression within 3 months of finishing [S0037].
- Breaking any condition: Immigration may cancel the Student Pass [S0016].
- **Need to know:** the official penalty for working or otherwise breaching pass conditions (Immigration Regulations 1963 reg. 39; employer offence Immigration Act s.55B). Blocked; see below.

### Can students work?
- IMI: only students at public universities and IPTS; not language, training or accredited centres [S0012]. Semester and festive breaks only, max 20 hours a week, at restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels, or on campus [S0013]; no front-desk work; never act as a student-recruitment agent [S0014].
- EMGS: 20 hours a week "ONLY during semester breaks or holidays of more than 7 days", with prior Immigration approval; not as cashiers; in hotels not as singers, masseurs, musicians or GROs (guest relations officers); nothing immoral [S0039, S0040].
- How: through the institution; the student goes in person with an institution representative to Immigration (Student Pass Unit at HQ or a state office) with the job offer, the institution's letter with semester-break dates, academic and attendance reports, passport and valid pass, and the employer's Labour Department (JTK) approval under section 60K of the Employment Act [S0041, S0015].

### Family of students
- IMI: only Master's or PhD students at UA/IPTS can bring dependants (spouse; children under 18 incl. step and adopted; disabled children of any age; parents; parents-in-law) on a Long-Term Social Visit Pass; language/training-centre students cannot; dependants may not work or do business [S0008-S0011]. Fee RM90 (IMI) [S0007]; EMGS processing RM250 new, RM140 renewal; dependant buys own insurance [S0067].
- EMGS FAQ: postgraduates can; Bachelor's students only from 17 listed countries, and Bangladesh is not one [S0038].
- **Conflict S0008 / S0038** (Master's/PhD vs "postgraduate" plus an undergraduate country list). For a Bangladeshi Bachelor's student both mean no.
- Applications go through STARS; relationship documents attested by the applicant's own embassy in Malaysia; foreign-language documents need a certified translation [S0021, S0022].

### How to check it is real
- EMGS Track My Application (visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my, menu Student Visa > Track My Application): enter passport (travel document) number and nationality, tick the terms box, press Track My Application [S0080]. IMI's own "Semakan Online" page sends Student Pass status checks to EMGS [S0081].
- **Need to know:** how to verify an eVAL PDF itself (QR/reference check), and whether IMI's pass-validity checker (imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid) shows Student Pass stickers. Not on any EMGS/IMI page fetched.

### Scams (leads and unofficial practice only)
- Agents charging BDT 6-8 lakh or more and steering students to barely functioning colleges [S0134]; students told a student visa lets them work freely [S0135]. KPT reportedly closed colleges and tightened scrutiny of Bangladeshi applications; Bangladeshi enrolment reportedly up 45% to 11,000+ [S0136, S0137 leads]. (Prothom Alo op-ed, Sept 2026.)

### Graduate Pass (not open to Bangladeshis)
Social Visit Pass for 12 months after a Bachelor's or higher, work in permitted sectors without an employer; Malaysian sponsor earning RM1,500+ needed; RM600 plus RM600 per dependant plus MEV; cannot be extended; only 32 nationalities and **Bangladesh is not one** [S0044, S0070-S0074].

## 2. Changing visa type

- **Visit pass to work:** a short-term Social Visit Pass covers a list of social activities, none of them work, and is extended only for illness, accident or war [S0088, S0089]. A PLKS worker must be outside Malaysia while the employer's VDR/PLKS is processed and enters only after approval [S0090]. **Need to know:** an explicit official sentence that a Social Visit Pass cannot be converted to a work pass (not found in those words on IMI/ESD pages).
- **Visit pass to student:** the Student Pass applicant must be outside Malaysia when applying [S0002].
- **Student to work:** no automatic post-study route; the employer must apply for a work pass such as an Employment Pass [S0100]; Graduate Pass excludes Bangladesh [S0044]. **Need to know:** whether a Student Pass holder can switch to an Employment Pass inside Malaysia; any graduate route into RP-T (Residence Pass-Talent).
- **PLKS to anything else:** no change of sector or employer without KDN permission [S0091]; PLKS workers may not marry locals or other foreign workers [S0092]. **Need to know:** whether a PLKS worker can ever move to an Employment Pass.
- **Dependant to work:** Dependant Pass holders must get their own Employment Pass [S0093, S0094]; LTSVP family of EP holders may not work or study without converting [S0141]; student dependants may not work [S0011].
- **Spouse of a Malaysian citizen:** LTSVP up to 5 years; may work or run a business without changing pass, once the permission is endorsed in the passport; apply together with the Malaysian spouse at the Visa, Pass and Permit Division of the state where working, with documents including an RM10-stamped contract [S0095-S0099].

## 3. Asylum

- Malaysia is not party to the 1951 Refugee Convention and has no national asylum system; UNHCR does refugee status determination [S0101]; UNHCR recognition gives no legal immigration status [S0142].
- Refugees are treated as undocumented migrants: may be arrested, detained, fined, deported; no legal right to work formally; UNHCR cannot grant status or work permits or stop arrests [S0106-S0108].
- Leaving for work or study does not make someone a refugee [S0102].
- Process: request on UNHCR's My Services portal; no letters; paying anyone does not help; long wait [S0109, S0110]. A UNHCR card is not a visa [S0111]. Registration priority: requests from 2022 or earlier and vulnerable people [S0112]. Interviews only for priority cases; no timeline [S0103]. One appeal within 30 days, then the case closes [S0104]. Must tell the truth [S0105]. Services are free; anyone asking money is lying [S0143]. Unregistered pregnant women pay full foreigner fees [S0113].
- **Need to know:** UNHCR Malaysia figures by nationality (any Bangladeshis recognised); Immigration Act text (blocked).

## 4. Children born in Malaysia

- Register within 60 days at a JPN (National Registration Department) office; free [S0114, S0115]. Who may report [S0121]. Documents include form JPN.LM01 and the hospital birth confirmation; parents' ID or passports and marriage documents (original and copy) [S0116-S0118].
- Late (after 60 days): RM50 and many more documents; sponsors and witnesses may identify with a passport or UNHCR card [S0119, S0120]. Replacement extract RM5 [S0122].
- Citizenship: JPN confirms the status of a person born in Malaysia from the parents' citizenship [S0123]; a child born before the marriage was registered follows the mother [S0124]. **Need to know:** the Federal Constitution text (Second Schedule Part II: born in Malaysia is a citizen only if a parent is a citizen or permanent resident) — blocked; the non-citizen certificate marking (news lead only).
- Bangladesh side: a birth abroad must be registered with the mission (Births and Deaths Registration Act 2004) [S0127]. At the BD High Commission KL: JPN certificate attested by Malaysia's Foreign Ministry; parents' passport copies; e-apostilled marriage certificate; printed online form; 2 photos; child and both parents in person [S0125, S0126]. **Need to know:** the fee (blank on the page).
- Child's Bangladeshi e-passport: RM545 (48 pages, 5 years) or RM817 (64 pages, 5 years) in cash at Maybank, receipt in the child's name; Bangladeshi birth registration must show Malaysia as birthplace; parents' NIDs; marriage certificate attested by the Law Ministry, Foreign Ministry and High Commission; child must come; ESKL appointment compulsory [S0128-S0133] (page dated 28 May 2024, possibly outdated).
- **Need to know:** official position on children of undocumented parents (can JPN register them, does registration trigger enforcement, can the child get a pass). No official page found.

Context for the doc: none of these passes is a route for an ordinary Bangladeshi worker. PLKS (factory, construction, plantation) workers **cannot bring family and cannot marry in Malaysia** [E0138, E0139, E0140]. The passes below are for graduates and professionals earning RM5,000+ a month, for wealthy retirees or investors, or for remote workers earning USD36,000+ a year from abroad. Bangladeshis are not excluded from any of them: MM2H is open to any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia [E0100], and DE Rantau to every country except Israel [E0084]. A Bangladeshi needs a Visa with Reference (VDR), and the visa costs RM20 a year [E0045, E0046, E0047, E0058].

## 1. Employment Pass (EP)

**What:** a work pass for an "expatriate", the Malaysian term for a foreign professional or skilled worker, valid for one employer [E0004, E0005, E0006]. Issued for 1-60 months, multiple entry [E0015]. Valid only in Peninsular Malaysia; Sabah and Sarawak have their own immigration and ESD units [E0005; see IMI charter E0056 for Sabah/Sarawak units].

**Who decides:** the Expatriate Committee (EC), or a sector approving agency: MIDA (manufacturing), MDEC (ICT/MSC), Bank Negara (banking), Securities Commission, the Public Services Department, and the ECERDC and IRDA regions [E0004, E0007]. Paying the fee doesn't guarantee approval [E0049].

**Categories from 1 June 2026** (basic salary only, taxable in Malaysia; allowances, bonuses and pay outside Malaysia don't count) [E0008, E0013, E0017]:
- Cat I: RM20,000+ a month, up to 10 years, dependants allowed [E0009].
- Cat II: RM10,000-19,999, up to 10 years (succession plan required from 1 Jan 2027), dependants allowed [E0010].
- Cat III: RM5,000-9,999, up to 5 years with the same employer (succession plan from 1 Jan 2027), dependants now allowed [E0011]. In manufacturing and manufacturing-related services under MIDA the range is RM7,000-9,999 [E0018]. Before June 2026 Cat III was RM3,000-4,999, 12 months per application and no dependants [E0012]. Cat III holders issued before 1 June 2026 still can't bring dependants [E0019].
- Minimum qualification: degree + 3 years' relevant experience, or diploma + 5 years, or technical certificate + 7 years [E0022, E0023] (guidebook dated April 2025).
- The employer must be registered with ESD. Paid-up capital: RM250,000 if 100% local, RM350,000 for a joint venture, RM500,000 if 100% foreign, RM1m for foreign-owned trade companies [E0024].

**Who applies:** only the employer, never the worker [E0002, E0001]. The process from the booklet [E0016]: (1) company registers on ESD Online, about 5 working days; (2) JTKSM (Department of Labour) prior approval under Section 60K of the Employment Act, plus a MYFutureJobs (PERKESO) job advert acknowledgement, both on Xpats Gateway, about 10 working days [E0050, E0051, E0052, E0053]; (3) support letter from the approving agency if the sector is regulated; (4) EP application on ESD Online, 2 working days fast track or 5 normal [E0016, E0054, E0055]; (5) VDR for visa-required nationals [E0058]; (6) entry and ePASS endorsement, 1 working day, at the Expatriate Satellite Centre (ESC) at KLIA T1/T2 with no appointment, or at the MYXpats Centre [E0059]. An employer that hires without JTKSM approval faces a fine of up to RM100,000 and/or 5 years in jail [E0052].

**Documents:** colour scan of the full passport (more than 12 months' validity, 6 empty pages), photo, attested certificates, contract and job description; for renewals, the last 3 months' payslips, tax forms and receipts [E0025, E0026].

**Fees:** MYXpats application fee RM2,000 + 8% SST = RM2,160 [E0040]. Immigration endorsement: RM200 a year (RM300 for a MIDA key post) + RM125 processing + visa (Bangladesh RM20 a year) [E0042, E0045]. For a 12-month EP for a Bangladeshi that is RM200 + RM125 + RM20 = RM345 immigration fees plus the RM2,160 application fee (my arithmetic from E0040/E0042/E0045; the doc should show the parts, not a total). A rejected application gets 75% of the processing fee back unless the employer appeals [E0048].

**Appeal:** the employer appeals within 14 days in ESD Online; the EC-Appeal decides within up to 30 working days; after that the case can be referred to MOHA [E0063, E0064, E0021].

**Renewal:** can be filed 3 months before expiry, under the revised salary rules [E0020]. A new passport requires a Transfer of Endorsement, or the holder can be stopped from leaving Malaysia [E0065].

**Check it is real:** the ePASS has a QR code; anyone can check it at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan, or email esdhelpdesk@imi.gov.my (1 working day) [E0060, E0061, E0062]. MYXpats helpdesk: +603-7839 7171, helpdesk@myxpats.com.my, Level 4 Surian Tower, Mutiara Damansara [E0066].

## 2. Dependant Pass and LTSVP for EP holders' families

- From 1 June 2026: the **Dependant Pass (DP)** covers a spouse and children under 18 (step and adopted included; disabled children of any age). The **LTSVP** (Long-Term Social Visit Pass) covers parents, parents-in-law, children aged 18-25 and a common-law spouse [E0014, E0027].
- The company applies through its ESD account, together with the EP or later [E0034, E0037]. Fees: RM540 application (RM500 + SST) [E0041], plus RM90 a year, RM50 processing and the visa fee [E0043]. The LTSVP needs a personal bond: RM750 for Bangladeshis [E0144]. Charter time 14 working days [E0029].
- Documents: photo, colour passport (12+ months), and a marriage or birth certificate translated into English and certified (CTC) by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka or the Bangladesh High Commission in KL, with a current-year CTC for new applications [E0030]. A common-law spouse needs an affidavit from the embassy; children 18-25 need a statutory declaration and embassy confirmation at every renewal [E0031].
- Validity follows the principal's pass, capped by the dependant's passport [E0034]. **Conflict** on the LTSVP maximum: 12 months [E0148] vs following the principal [E0147].
- **Can dependants work?** **Conflict:** the ESD guidebook and DP page say no, the dependant must get their own EP [E0028, E0035, E0145]; the older IMI FAQ (2021) says a DP spouse can work with an Immigration work endorsement [E0038]. LTSVP holders can't work or study (they need an EP or a Student Pass) [E0032].
- Domestic maid: EP holder earning RM5,000+ (1 maid), RM10,000+ (2), RM15,000+ (3) [E0033].

## 3. Can PLKS workers bring family?

No. "Pekerja Asing tidak dibenarkan membawa keluarga masuk dan tinggal di negara ini" (foreign workers are not allowed to bring their family to live in Malaysia) [E0138, E0140]. They also can't marry a local or another foreign worker while on PLKS [E0139]. A Bangladeshi worker's only official family route is a family member's own pass (a visit visa, which is another part's topic) or the worker moving to a professional pass after qualifying.

## 4. LTSVP for a foreign spouse of a Malaysian citizen

- Minimum 6 months, up to 5 years. The holder may work or run a business without an EP or PLKS once Immigration endorses permission in the passport [E0122, E0123, E0125]. The marriage must be registered under Malaysian law [E0129]. Immigration may visit the home or workplace to check (naziran) [E0124].
- New application: both spouses attend the state Immigration office with Imm.12, Imm.38 (2 copies), Imm.55 (2 copies), a sponsor statement, a security bond stamped RM10, a sworn declaration with photo, the spouse's IC and birth certificate, the marriage registration, a wedding photo, proof of income and address, and the spouse's Malaysian passport [E0126, E0127]. Charter: 25 working days [E0057].
- Work endorsement: at the Visa, Pass and Permit Division in the state of work, with the spouse present. Bring the spouse's MyKad copy, passport and pass, a sworn declaration, the marriage certificate, an offer letter, a contract stamped RM10 (stating position, period and salary), the information form and the SSM company printout [E0125].
- A false statement is an offence under s.56(1)(f): fine up to RM10,000 and/or 5 years in jail [E0128].
- After 3 years on a long-term pass, family of citizens can get the **Pas Residen** (Residence Pass): RM500 for 5 years; holders may work, study and run a business [E0131, E0132, E0133].

## 5. MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home), MOTAC

- Open to any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia, which includes Bangladesh. Age 25+ (Silver/Gold/Platinum) or 21+ (SEZ/SFZ) [E0100].
- **Apply only through a MOTAC-licensed MM2H operator** [E0101]. Final approval and appeals rest with MOHA/Immigration [E0102]. The licensed list, with licence numbers (MM2Hxxx) and validity dates, is at mm2h.gov.my/agencies [E0116]. Since 1 Aug 2025 applications go through the system at mm2h.gov.my/portal (helpdesk 03-8891 7207), and from 1 Oct 2025 by online appointment [E0118].
- Tiers [E0106-E0109, E0114]:

| | Fixed deposit | Pass | Min. property | Participating fee | Renewal fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platinum | USD 1,000,000 | 20 yrs | RM2,000,000 | RM200,000 | RM5,000 |
| Gold | USD 500,000 | 15 yrs | RM1,000,000 | RM3,000 | RM3,000 |
| Silver | USD 150,000 | 5 yrs | RM600,000 | RM1,000 | RM1,500 |
| SEZ/SFZ | USD 65,000 (21-49) / USD 32,000 (50+) | 10 yrs | Forest City, Johor only | RM1,000 | RM300 |

  Processing fee: RM5,000 per principal and RM2,500 per dependant [E0107]. Under 50s must spend 90 days a year in Malaysia [E0103]. The property must be bought within 1 year of endorsement and not sold for 10 years [E0119, E0103]. Up to 50% of the fixed deposit can be withdrawn for a house, education, medical or tourism costs [E0106]. Police may interview applicants at random [E0120].
- **Work:** Platinum only; Gold, Silver and SEZ can't work or run a business [E0112, E0113].
- Family: spouse, children under 21 (21-34 if single and unemployed), disabled children, parents and parents-in-law; maid for Platinum only [E0104]. **Conflict:** maximum child age 34 [E0110, E0146] vs 35 [E0111].
- Renewal: RM500 a year pass fee + RM0-50 visa [E0105].
- Sarawak runs its own S-MM2H. **Lead only:** from 2025, age 30+, RM500,000 fixed deposit in a Sarawak bank, 30 days a year in Sarawak [E0143]. The official MTCP pages were blocked.

## 6. DE Rantau Nomad Pass (MDEC)

- A type of Professional Visit Pass for remote workers and freelancers with foreign employers or clients. Open to all nationalities except Israel; age 18+ [E0084].
- Income: tech roles more than USD36,000 a year (raised from USD24,000 for applications from 1 Dec 2026), non-tech USD60,000 [E0085, E0098]. The contract must have started at least 3 months before applying; up to 3 contracts [E0085].
- Duration: 3-12 months, then up to three 12-month renewals, 48 months maximum. No route to PR [E0086, E0098].
- Apply at mdec.my/derantau. Processing 6-8 weeks; the e-Pass comes within 1 week of endorsement; check status at malaysiadigital.mdec.my [E0089, E0090]. Bangladeshis then get a VDR or eVISA and enter for endorsement [E0089].
- Fees: RM1,080 for the main applicant and RM540 per dependant (non-refundable), plus the RM360 a year pass fee and visa. Refundable bond for Bangladeshis: RM750 [E0091, E0092].
- Family: spouse or common-law partner, children under 18, special-needs children, parents [E0087]. **The spouse can't work** [E0088].
- No appeal [E0093]. Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan only; a tourist pass can't be converted [E0095]. Passport needs 14+ months' validity [E0096]. Documents include a letter of good conduct from the home country [E0097].
- **New applications are closed 1-30 Nov 2026 and reopen on 1 Dec 2026** [E0099].
- "Agents" can't guarantee approval [E0094].

## 7. Other passes

- **Professional Visit Pass (PVP-Expert):** short assignments of up to 12 months for an overseas company; no Dependant Pass [E0067]. Not for general workers or operators; the applicant waits abroad; false documents lead to blacklisting [E0068, E0070]. A Social Visit Pass does not allow work [E0069].
- **PLS@Xpats:** permission to work on a Social Visit Pass, for emergency or critical professional work, applied for by the company at mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats [E0071, E0072]; charter 3 working days [E0056]. **Need to know:** the duration (news says 30 days), sectors and fees; the FAQ page is empty (see blocked).
- **Investor Pass:** 6 months + 6, for foreign investors working through MIDA and other investment agencies, applied from abroad on Xpats Gateway with a business email. No dependants. RM1,296 + RM90 + RM500 + visa [E0075, E0076, E0077, E0073].
- **Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T, TalentCorp):** 10 years. Requires 3 consecutive years in Malaysia on an EP, RM15,000 basic salary, 2 years of tax paid, a qualification and 5 years' experience. The holder can change employer; the spouse can work [E0078, E0079, E0080].
- **MTEP (MDEC):** for tech founders. 1-year PVP (fee from RM2,700) or 5-year Resident Pass (from RM5,400, dependants allowed). Needs RM50,000 in bank statements, a good-conduct certificate and a sponsor [E0081, E0082, E0083].
- **PVIP (Premium Visa Programme):** listed on the IMI home page [E0074]. **Need to know:** official terms. No public official page was found; news reports a RM200,000 fee, RM40,000 a month offshore income, 20 years and appointed agents only.

## 8. Permanent residence

The Entry Permit (Permit Masuk) gives indefinite residence. Its categories are the wife, husband or child under 18 of a citizen, and "Warga Asing Sepenuhnya" (fully foreign nationals) [E0134]. It can be revoked [E0135]. Expatriates approved for PR get an LTSVP while waiting [E0137]. Status checks and a points calculator are at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices and app.imi.gov.my/epc [E0136]. DE Rantau gives no PR route [E0098]. PLKS rules (no family, no marriage, 5 years maximum) mean an ordinary worker can't build up the qualifying stay [E0138, E0139]. **Need to know:** the official criteria for "fully foreign" PR (the e-book is on Google Drive; see blocked).

## 9. Scams and checks

- EP/DP: the worker never applies; any "EP for sale" offer to an individual is outside the official process, because only a registered employer can apply [E0002]. Verify any ePASS by its QR code or at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan [E0060]. PVP-Expert misuse and false information lead to blacklisting [E0070].
- MM2H: only MOTAC-licensed operators may submit; check the name and licence number at mm2h.gov.my/agencies [E0101, E0116]. Lead: MOTAC re-vetted all agents in 2024 after complaints [E0142].
- DE Rantau: no agent can guarantee approval [E0094].
- Practical lead: 3,604 Bangladeshis held active MM2H passes on 31 Jan 2024, 5th-largest nationality [E0141]. This needs the parlimen.gov.my answer (11 March 2024).

## Draft steps

### Employment Pass (for a Bangladeshi graduate or skilled professional)
1. **Check you qualify:** a degree + 3 years' experience, diploma + 5 years or technical certificate + 7 years, and a job offer of at least RM5,000 basic salary a month (RM7,000 in manufacturing) [E0022, E0011, E0018].
2. **Get a written offer from a registered Malaysian company.** It, not you, applies [E0002]. It must first be registered on ESD (paid-up capital RM250,000-1m) [E0024] and get JTKSM Section 60K approval and a MYFutureJobs advert acknowledgement on Xpats Gateway (about 10 working days) [E0050, E0051, E0016].
3. **Send the company your documents:** a colour scan of every passport page (12+ months valid), a photo, attested certificates and the signed contract [E0025].
4. **The company applies on ESD Online and pays RM2,160.** Decision in 2-5 working days; the approval letter is printed from the system [E0040, E0016, E0054].
5. **Get your Visa with Reference (VDR).** The company applies with the EP and collects it at MYXpats [E0058].
6. **Fly in and get the ePASS endorsed** at the ESC at KLIA T1/T2 (no appointment) or MYXpats. The company pays the immigration fees: RM200 a year + RM125 + RM20 visa [E0059, E0042, E0045].
7. **Check the ePASS** by its QR code or at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan [E0060].
- If refused: only the company can appeal, within 14 days (decision in up to 30 working days) [E0063].
- Changing employer means a new application [E0006].
- Bringing family: see below.

### Bringing your family (EP holder)
1. Get your marriage and children's birth certificates translated into English and certified by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka (or the Bangladesh High Commission in KL), dated the current year [E0030].
2. Your company applies on ESD Online for a DP (spouse, children under 18) or an LTSVP (parents, in-laws, children 18-25, common-law spouse) and pays RM540 per person [E0014, E0041]. For an LTSVP, you sign a personal bond (RM750 for Bangladeshis) [E0144, E0031].
3. Decision in about 14 working days; then endorsement at RM90 a year + RM50 + visa [E0029, E0043].
4. Your family can't work on a DP or LTSVP unless they get their own EP (ESD); IMI's older FAQ mentions a work endorsement for a DP spouse (conflict) [E0028, E0038].

### PLKS workers
- You can't bring your family or marry in Malaysia while on PLKS [E0138, E0139].

### Married to a Malaysian
1. Register the marriage under Malaysian law [E0129].
2. Both go to the state Immigration office with the forms and documents in E0126; LTSVP of 6 months to 5 years; 25 working days [E0122, E0123, E0057].
3. To work, apply for the work endorsement with the offer letter and RM10-stamped contract [E0125].
4. After 3 years, you can apply for a Pas Residen (RM500 for 5 years) [E0131, E0133].

### MM2H
1. Pick a licensed operator from mm2h.gov.my/agencies and check its licence number and validity [E0116, E0101].
2. Have the fixed deposit (Silver USD150,000 and up) and money for the property (Silver RM600,000 and up) [E0106, E0108].
3. Pay a RM5,000 processing fee plus the participating fee; police may interview you; medical check at a MOTAC panel clinic [E0107, E0109, E0120].
4. Buy the home within 1 year; stay 90 days a year if under 50; only Platinum may work [E0119, E0103, E0112].

### DE Rantau
1. Check you earn USD36,000+ (tech) or USD60,000+ (non-tech) a year from foreign employers or clients, with contracts that started 3+ months ago [E0085].
2. Apply at mdec.my/derantau (closed 1-30 Nov 2026, reopens 1 Dec 2026); pay RM1,080 + RM540 per dependant [E0099, E0091].
3. Wait 6-8 weeks and check malaysiadigital.mdec.my [E0089, E0090].
4. Get a VDR or eVISA, enter, and endorse (RM360 a year pass fee + visa + RM750 refundable bond) [E0089, E0091, E0092].

## Answers to the open "Need to know" items

**Overtime rates and public holidays (doc step "Hours").** Overtime beyond normal daily hours: at least 1.5x the hourly rate [M0001, M0002]. Rest day: daily-rated get 1 day's wages (up to half normal hours) or 2 days' (more) [M0011]; monthly-rated get half a day's or 1 day's pay [M0012]; beyond normal hours on a rest day at least 2x hourly [M0013]; no one can be forced to work a rest day except shift work or emergencies [M0010]. Public holiday worked: 2 days' wages on top of holiday pay [M0019]; overtime on a public holiday at least 3x hourly [M0020]. Paid public holidays: 11 gazetted a year, 5 fixed (National Day, King's Birthday, state Ruler's Birthday or FT Day, Workers' Day, Malaysia Day) [M0014, M0015]; substitute day if it falls on a rest day [M0016]; employer posts the other six [M0017]; absence the day before/after loses holiday pay without a reasonable excuse [M0018]. Hourly rate = (monthly wage / 26) / normal daily hours [M0007, M0008, M0234, M0235]. Limits: 8 h/day, 45 h/week, 30-min break after 5 h [M0003]; never more than 12 h a day [M0004]; 45 h excludes free breaks [M0237]; wages can't be cut for the 48-to-45 change [M0061]. Workers earning over RM4,000 (except manual workers) are excluded from OT/rest-day/holiday pay and termination benefits [M0058, M0109]. Still not answered: the monthly overtime cap (set by regulations, not in the Act) [M0005].

**Leave.** Annual 8/12/16 days [M0021, M0022]; sick 14/18/22 + 60 hospitalisation (74-82 total) [M0023, M0024, M0025, M0239]; maternity 98 days [M0026]; paternity 7 days [M0027]; pregnant worker protection [M0240].

**How and when wages are paid (s.19, s.25).** Wage period max 1 month [M0028]; pay by the 7th day after it ends [M0029]; OT, rest-day and holiday pay by the last day of the next wage period [M0030]; into a bank account in the worker's own name [M0034, M0035]; cash/cheque only on the worker's written request plus JTKSM approval [M0036, M0037]; on termination with notice, pay by the last day [M0032]; if the worker quits without notice (s.13 or s.14(3)), within 3 days [M0033]; employer can't dictate how wages are spent [M0038]; late pay is an offence [M0040] and a breach of contract by the employer [M0041]; partial-month formula [M0043].

**Deductions (s.24); levy and recruitment costs.** Only deductions the Act allows [M0044]: overpayment, notice indemnity, interest-free advances, deductions authorised by other written law [M0045, M0046]; third-party payments, insurance and accommodation/food need the worker's written request plus the Director General's prior written permission [M0108, M0047, M0048], and accommodation/food only if for the worker's benefit [M0049]; max 50% of monthly wages [M0050, M0051]; unauthorised deduction is an offence [M0040]. Levy/recruitment costs: none of these files names them; the Act's closed list does not include them. **Still not answered:** an explicit official statement that the levy or recruitment fees may not be deducted (look in JTKSM/KESUMA levy circulars, the "Employer Mandatory Commitment" for levy, or the Minimum Standards of Housing regulations 2020 for rent caps).

**Termination and lay-off benefits.** Notice 4/6/8 weeks or as in the contract [M0052]; pay in lieu [M0053]; misconduct dismissal only after due inquiry [M0054]; worker can leave without notice if in danger [M0055]; foreign workers retrenched before locals in a similar job [M0057]; locals can't be replaced by foreigners [M0069]; untaken annual leave paid out [M0022]; return ticket: employer pays at contract end or when it terminates, worker pays if dismissed for misconduct or resigns [M0149, M0150]. **Still not answered:** the amounts of termination/lay-off benefits: s.60J leaves them to the Employment (Termination and Lay-Off Benefits) Regulations 1980, not in these files [M0056].

**Labour Court claim.** Where: JTKSM (Labour Department) office; the Director General of Labour hears wage/payment disputes without upper limit [M0081, M0082]. Free advice: 03-8000 8000, jtksm@mohr.gov.my, nearest Labour Office [M0118]. How: go in person and give a sworn statement [M0087, M0088, M0112]; bring passport, employer name/address, payslips, appointment letter/contract, termination letter [M0113]. Fee: free [M0232]; no fee for summonses [M0093]; free copy of decision [M0095]; the Act allows filing fees by regulation (none named) [M0094]. ## Citizenship of a child born in Malaysia (doc: "having a child"; family sections per visa)

- Born on/after Malaysia Day = citizen by operation of law only if a qualification in Second Schedule Part II applies [K0001].
- Rule: citizen if **at least one parent is, at the time of birth, a Malaysian citizen or permanent resident** (Sch.2 Pt II s.1(a)) [K0002]. A child of two Bangladeshi parents who are neither citizens nor PRs does not qualify under this rule.
- Only other route: child "not born a citizen of any country" (s.1(e)) [K0003]; any citizenship acquired within one year after birth counts as citizenship at birth for this rule (s.2(3)) [K0004].
- Pending change: Act A1752 (2025, not yet in force at the reprint) will make s.1(a) "a citizen" only, i.e. a PR parent will no longer be enough [K0005].
- Citizenship decisions of the Federal Government cannot be appealed or reviewed in court [K0006].

## What can get you jailed: entry, overstay, removal, bans (doc: "what can get you jailed"; "going home without valid papers")

- Entering without a valid Entry Permit or Pass (or s.55 exemption) [K0007]: fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years or both, plus up to 6 strokes of whipping (s.6(3)) [K0008].
- Prohibited immigrants: no means of support or no definite job awaiting [K0009]; unlawful entrants, people without valid or with forged travel documents, family of prohibited immigrants [K0010]. Appeal to the Minister (final) for a permit holder refused entry on arrival [K0011]. The Act sets no money amount for "means of support".
- Overstay: may not remain after the pass expires or is cancelled [K0016]; without reasonable cause: fine **not less than** RM10,000 or up to 5 years or both (s.15(4)) [K0015].
- Removal (deportation): after conviction under s.5/6/8/9 [K0017]; overstayers removed whether or not prosecuted [K0018], with no appeal against removal where the pass expired or was cancelled [K0019]; any non-citizen unlawfully entering or remaining is liable to removal [K0037]. Detention until removal can be arranged [K0020]; arrest without warrant and up to 30 days' detention while removal is decided [K0021]. Failing to produce a pass on demand = presumed unlawful [K0023].
- Bans: the Director General can bar a person from entry for a stated period or permanently [K0012]; when a pass is cancelled the holder is removed and "thereafter" prohibited from entering [K0013]; appeal to the Minister within 7 days by written petition [K0014]. Coming back after removal: fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years or both, up to 6 strokes, and removal again (s.36) [K0022].
- Working in breach of a pass: the pass "shall cease to be a valid Pass" when any of its conditions is broken (s.55B(2)) [K0025]. Employers of workers without a valid pass: RM10,000-50,000 or up to 12 months or both, per worker [K0024]; more than five at once: 6 months-5 years and whipping [K0026]. The worker-side offence for breaching pass conditions is regulation 39 of the Immigration Regulations 1963 (referred to in s.55E(7)) [K0029]; that regulation is not in raw/.
- Landlords/occupiers who let an illegal immigrant (including an overstayer) stay: RM5,000-30,000 or up to 12 months per person [K0029]. Harbouring: RM10,000-50,000 per person [K0033]. Smuggling people in: RM10,000-50,000 and 2-5 years and whipping [K0027].
- Forged papers: making, forging or altering a visa, permit or pass: RM30,000-100,000 **and** 5-10 years **and** up to 6 strokes (s.55D) [K0028]. False statements, using someone else's pass, getting a pass by false statement, using or holding a forged pass: fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years (s.56(1)) [K0030, K0031, K0032]. Attempts and abetment punished like the offence [K0034]. General penalty: up to RM10,000 or 5 years or both [K0035].

## Refugees and asylum (doc: "asylum", written to discourage)

- The Immigration Act (2006 reprint) contains no refugee or asylum provision: 0 matches for "refugee"/"asylum"; every non-citizen needs a valid permit, pass or exemption to enter [K0036]. The Federal Constitution reprint also has 0 matches (noted in K0036).

## Passports (doc: "rights at work" passport withholding; "scams"; "what can get you jailed")

- Holding another person's passport without lawful authority is an offence for any person (s.12(1)(f)): fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years or both [K0045, K0046]. The Act does not mention employers or consent; "lawful authority" is not defined.
- A holder who lets someone else possess their passport intending or knowing it may be used by another person also offends (s.12(1)(e)) [K0047].
- Forged or altered passport, or using/holding one: s.12(1)(a) [K0048]; false statement for one's own passport: s.12(1)(c) [K0049]; both up to RM10,000 or 5 years [K0046]. Getting a passport/visa for someone else by false statement: RM10,000-50,000 and 1-5 years (s.12A) [K0050]. Making a false passport: RM15,000-100,000 and 2-10 years and up to 6 strokes (s.12B) [K0051].
- Text is an unofficial consolidation to 1997 [K0052].

## Drugs and the death penalty (doc: "what can get you jailed")

- Act 846 abolishes the **mandatory** death penalty across seven laws, including the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952 [K0039]; commencement by gazette notification, date not in the file [K0040].
- Drug trafficking (DDA s.39B): Act 846 only lowers the minimum whipping (for those not sentenced to death) from 15 to 12 strokes and deletes s.39B(2A)-(2B); it does not remove death [K0041]. Applies to anyone convicted after commencement even for earlier offences [K0042].
- Murder (Penal Code s.302): death or 30-40 years, plus at least 12 strokes if not sentenced to death [K0043]. "Imprisonment for natural life" replaced by 30-40 years in several laws [K0044].

Loans are mostly informal and paid straight to a third party, with no record [A0066]; families sell land or borrow [A0015, A0106]. Recruiters themselves proposed PKB instalment migration loans [A0013]. Nothing found on PKB loan use for Malaysia in 2025-26 (the doc's PKB facts come from official B0013-B0019).

**What helps (evidence).**
- BOESL government channel: zero-cost in principle. Track record: about 1,381-1,500 workers sent at no cost in 2022-24 against demand for 10,000+ [A0059 (2024), A0030]. But the stranded-worker scheme cost Tk 1,62,500 (official W0020), reportedly Tk 1 lakh of it a "visa purchase fee" [A0056]; BOESL sent only 190 in about six months [A0057]; workers complained of harassment [A0055]; UN experts reported BOESL recruits paying more than five times the limit [A0060]. So BOESL's zero-cost circulars (W0001-W0005) are the official route, but there is no outside evidence yet on how the 2026 zero-cost scheme works in practice.
- Fair-recruitment pilots: none found for Malaysia. ILO's only Bangladesh pilot cited is Qatar construction 2018-21 (92% cost drop) [A0092]. No agency in the Malaysia corridor has outside evidence of low fees; **do not name any agency**. The 25 FWCMS agencies are listed (official W0008), but news gives no evidence about their fees.
- Employer fee repayment in Malaysia happens only under pressure: Mediceram agreed in 2025 to repay RM22,500 each after workers went to a rights group [A0090], paid on 27 Oct 2025, then terminated all of them on 31 Oct 2025 and kept passports [A0108, A0109].
- Malaysian labour office claims with an NGO can work: 74 workers won a RM1.54 million labour office order against Meranti Binamas through Tenaganita (Jun 2025); 33 are suing in the Shah Alam High Court for RM1.71 million including their RM25,000 fees [A0086, A0087]. **Complaints to BMET / the Ministry: what actually happens.** Official route and 30-working-day timeline are in B0034, B0035, B0041, B0044, B0045. In practice (2023 data, newest found): victims say claims take 4 months to a year (conflict row A0073 vs B0034/B0045); BMET resolved about one-third of 2,380 complaints in 2023, awarding Tk 4.87 crore [A0074]; most get under half their loss [A0075]; in Aug 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 against Tk 3.5-5 lakh costs [A0076]. Documents decide the outcome: those who can show proof get more; most paid middlemen with no written agreement [A0077, A0079]. A BMET official says an agency's licence is suspended if a complaint stays unresolved for six months through its fault [A0078]. OKUP: BMET both licenses and arbitrates, and there are no compensation standards [A0083]. NGO routes: Ovibashi Odhikar Forum and WARBE do local arbitration, and refer recruiter cases to BMET; complex cases go to court under the 2013 Act or the 2012 Trafficking Act [A0080]. 2013 Act amended (Nov 2024) to cover sub-agents [A0093]. For workers in Malaysia, the HC and labour department did not help the Mediceram workers [A0091, A0109]; HC services were reported "inaccessible and slow" [A0111].

**Enforcement against agencies.** 49 licences cancelled [A0012]; ACC cases against 64 people from 25 agencies over about Tk 2,288 crore [A0085, A0084].

## If you aren't paid (complaint routes in practice)

**Official JTK route (JTKSM leaflet "Masalah di tempat kerja", printed Oct 2017, possibly outdated).** Free advice: 03-8000 8000, jtksm@mohr.gov.my, or the nearest labour office [F0093]. A complaint is made in writing to the labour office with your name and address, the employer's name and address, and the issues and documents [F0094]. JTK investigates; if valid it issues a correction notice to the employer, then prosecution or a compound if ignored; you are told the result [F0095]. It covers no contract, late/unpaid wages, below-minimum pay, no payslip, unpaid overtime, illegal deductions, dismissal, excess hours, no rest, no leave [F0096]. A wage claim (Labour Court, s.69) is made in person with a sworn statement before an officer [F0097, F0103]; bring passport, employer's name and address (from a Companies Commission search), payslips, appointment letter and termination letter if any [F0104, F0098]. Steps: statement, mention date, notice to you and summons to the employer, hearing, order [F0100]. The Labour Court is free [F0099]. Appeal to the High Court within 14 days of the decision [F0101]. If the employer ignores the order, JTK can use a judgment debtor summons, garnishment, prosecution and a writ of seizure and sale [F0102]. - 96 on 11 Jul (V0111)
- 123 on 23 Jul (N0013)
- 80 on 25 Jul (N0011)
- 40 on 12 Aug (N0009)
- 98 on 15 Aug (V0109)

Reasons given: fake hotel bookings, no return ticket, too little money or foreign currency, falsified documents, a vague purpose, and failing to give "satisfactory justification". Some people admitted they came for jobs (N0007, N0008, N0011, N0013). AKPS says entrants must show a valid passport and visa, enough money and credible accommodation (N0012). More than 200 Bangladeshis were deported after refusal in 2025 (N0014). In 2023, most NTL notices went to Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Indian travellers (N0016 (2023)).

**What happens after an NTL.** Refused travellers are deported within 24 hours (N0010). They are held in a designated airport area until the airline flies them back (N0015 (2023), N0020 (2022)). Since a July 2023 Cabinet decision, airlines and not third parties handle the return (N0015). A traveller who doesn't understand the questions can ask for a translator (Indian High Commission advisory, by analogy: N0019 (2022)). An NTL means you cannot enter until you meet the entry conditions, and blacklisting is one listed reason for an NTL (N0017, N0018 (2023, NST explainer)).

**Need to know (still open):** whether an NTL is recorded against future visa applications or entries, and whether the traveller is ever charged for the return flight. No official IMI or AKPS page found.
