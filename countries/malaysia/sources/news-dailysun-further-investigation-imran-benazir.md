# Court orders further investigation against Imran, Benazir and 101 others

Source: https://www.daily-sun.com/post/837457 (Daily Sun; Firecrawl scrape, 7 Nov 2026)

Daily Sun Report, Dhaka. Published: 02 Nov 2025, 11:11 PM

A Dhaka court on Sunday ordered further investigation against former expatriates' welfare and overseas employment minister Imran Ahmad, former inspector general of police Benazir Ahmed, and 101 others in a case filed over fraud, extortion, and human trafficking.

Judge Md Abdullah Al Mamun of the Dhaka Human Trafficking Prevention Tribunal passed the order after accepting the "naraji" (no-confidence) petition and directed the Detective Branch of police to submit the investigation report by 7 January, confirmed state prosecutor of the court, Mostofa Kamal Khan.

Court sources said that on 15 July, the investigating officer of the case, CID Inspector Md Rasel, submitted the final report to the court, stating that the allegations were false and recommended that the accused be discharged. The IO also sought action against the plaintiff, Altab Khan, for filing the case.

However, Altab said the investigating officer submitted the final report despite having sufficient information, evidence, and witnesses against the accused, which prompted him to file the petition.

According to the case documents, the plaintiff alleged that the accused had formed an organised human trafficking syndicate that extorted large sums of money from migrant workers under the guise of overseas employment, accumulating vast assets and laundering money abroad.

He also alleged that former secretary Ahmed Munirus Saleheen exploited his official power to support individuals involved in the trafficking network, and that former minister Imran Ahmad approved the "Ami Probashi" app in breach of regulations, registering it under the names of his family members.

According to the case details, when the Malaysian labour market reopened on 1 October 2022, the plaintiff—who holds a valid licence—approached the ministry to initiate job recruitment. However, he was informed that only 100 selected agencies would be allowed to carry out recruitment.

Later, when Altab tried to process workers' visas through his agency, which was not listed, the alleged syndicate demanded Tk1.5 lakh for each migration aspirant, despite the fact that the Bangladesh-Malaysia G2G agreement set the cost of visa processing at Tk78,990 per worker.

As he declined to pay the amount, Saleheen allegedly threatened to obstruct his business activities in the Malaysian labour market.

However, Altab reportedly paid a total of Tk12.56 crore between 11 November 2022 and 8 May 2024 to different parties, as he faced pressure from the workers to complete their visa processing. He also claimed to have incurred an additional loss of Tk20 crore in the process, bringing his total alleged damages to Tk32.56 crore.

He accused the defendants of orchestrating a scheme to traffic workers abroad, where they were subjected to abuse and forced labour, which is a violation of Bangladesh's Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking Act.
