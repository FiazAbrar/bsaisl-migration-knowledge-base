Malaysia clears entry of 800 Bangladeshi workers

By Asila Jalil
February 2, 2026 @ 8:01am

KUALA LUMPUR: Malaysia has accepted about 800 workers from Bangladesh between December 2025 and January this year following a stringent vetting process, according to Construction Labour Exchange Centre Bhd (CLAB).

Its chief executive officer Datuk Abdul Rafik Abdul Rajis said the centre had been entrusted with managing the entry of these workers as the majority are employed in the construction sector.

CLAB is a subsidiary of the Construction Industry Development Board (CIDB), which was established under the Construction Industry Development Act 1994 (Act 520) to develop the capacity and capability of the construction industry.

The intake of workers from Bangladesh follows the government's move to resume recruitment from the South Asian nation after many workers were left stranded when Malaysia imposed a hiring freeze in June 2024.

In May last year, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said the government would recruit nearly 8,000 stranded Bangladeshi workers following discussions with the Bangladesh government.

"CLAB was entrusted with the programme because about 80 per cent of these workers are from the construction industry.

"Of the 3,000 workers we interviewed, about 1,000 were rejected for various reasons, including health conditions and their suitability to work in Malaysia," Abdul Rafik told Business Times.

He said the 2,000 workers who passed the vetting process were placed at a training centre for 12 days, during which they received basic construction skills training.

"We want them to have basic construction knowledge and, upon completion of the training, the Bangladesh government will issue a certificate," he said.

Upon arrival in Malaysia, CLAB will further train the workers to obtain official accreditation under the Construction Skills Competency Certificate, which is issued by CIDB to foreign workers employed in the country.

Abdul Rafik added that the entry of foreign workers would continue to depend on industry demand.

"We are trying to minimise the intake of foreign workers through the accreditation programme. If the government maintains a hiring freeze, at least we will have a pool of accredited workers, which can help reduce dependence on foreign labour in the future," he said.
