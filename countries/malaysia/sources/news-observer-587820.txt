Bangladeshi workers unpaid for months in Malaysia 

Published : Thursday, 13 August, 2026 at 10:39 AM

Observer Online Report 

X

Collected Photo
Around 250 migrant workers at Mentakab Veneer and Plywood Sdn. Bhd. in Pahang, Malaysia, reported that they have not received salaries for over three months.
This unpaid status left both the workers and their families in severe financial distress.

A report by 
Free Malaysia Today
 on August 12 stated the workers hail from Bangladesh, Myanmar, Indonesia, and Nepal. They alleged that actual working conditions differ significantly from their employment contracts.
A spokesperson representing 126 Bangladeshi workers said they struggle to cover daily living expenses in Malaysia. Their families at home face difficulties paying off loans and housing installments.
He stated that each worker spent nearly 16,000 to 18,000 Ringgit to travel to Malaysia. Much of this money came from bank loans, land mortgages, or family debts.
He urged Malaysian authorities to address the issue urgently.

The company exports to the United States, South Africa, and Gulf nations. Workers claimed their contracts specified eight-hour shifts, but they work up to 12 hours daily for a 65 Ringgit wage. They also alleged they are denied regular weekly off days.
The spokesperson further alleged that the company confiscated their passports, violating employment contract terms.
Additionally, the company reportedly demands a 2,500 Ringgit deposit from workers taking annual leave to ensure their return.
Workers said they reported the matter to the Bangladesh High Commission and the Labor Department. However, no officials have visited the site to investigate yet.
Meanwhile, a company spokesperson admitted to payment delays, citing operational difficulties. He stated that a portion of the arrears had been cleared, though he withheld specific details.
The representative assured that all outstanding dues would be cleared as soon as possible. He added that negotiating with workers had become difficult, describing their behavior as aggressive.
The official claimed worker demands keep rising, complicating the situation. He dismissed other allegations as exaggerated pressure tactics.
-SA
