# Govt yet to select medical test centres for Malaysia-bound workers

Tk 8,000 test fee per person, total migration cost not fixed yet

Staff Correspondent . Dhaka \

The government has failed to start medical examinations for Bangladeshi workers seeking employment in Malaysia as scheduled on Saturday because it could not finalise the list of authorised medical centres, despite fixing the examination fee at Tk 8,000 per person.

The Ministry of Expatriates’ Welfare and Overseas Employment announced on October 28 that medical examinations would begin on October 31 at authorised centres and that a list of the centres would soon be published on its website.

However, the list remained unpublished on Saturday, leaving prospective migrants uncertain about where to undergo the mandatory tests.

Several ministry officials said they could not finalise the list and therefore could not publish it.

The expatriates’ welfare minister did not respond to calls or text messages seeking an explanation for why the government had failed to finalise the list by the scheduled launch date. 

The ministry’s Public Relations Officer Md Shariful Islam told New Age that officials were working to identify suitable medical centres. ‘But, it will take some more days,’ he said.

Meanwhile, in a notification issued on Saturday, the ministry fixed the medical examination fee at Tk 8,000 and warned that no additional charges could be collected.

The ministry has advised prospective migrants to report any centre demanding more than the prescribed fee or subjecting them to harassment, unnecessary delays or other irregularities. Complaints can be sent to [\[email protected\]](/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection).

Bangladesh is preparing to resume sending workers to Malaysia after the labour market remained closed to Bangladeshi recruitment since May 2024 amid allegations of excessive migration costs and irregularities.

The government announced the reopening of the market on October 15. 

Under the new arrangement, Malaysia has agreed, in principle, to recruit 10,000 Bangladeshi workers at zero cost, while 25 private processing agencies, 312 associate recruiting agencies and the state-owned Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited will be involved in the process.

However, the overall cost of migration has yet to be finalised. 

The government had fixed the migration cost at Tk 78,990 in 2022, but the rate was not followed in practice, with many workers paying substantially more.

Migration experts and recruiting agency representatives have expressed concern that the new recruitment process could again lead to high costs if effective monitoring and safeguards are not ensured.
