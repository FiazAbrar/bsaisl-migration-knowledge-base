MALAYSIA LABOUR MARKET 

New recruiters’ list stokes fresh syndication fears

Malaysia okays 25 agencies

Rashad Ahamad
23 August, 2026, 00:30
Print

Malaysia has published a list of 25 Bangladeshi recruiting agencies ahead of the possible reopening of its labour market, raising concerns among migration experts and rights activists that limiting recruitment to a small number of agencies – many of which have been flagged as suspicious – could once again lead to syndication.

Syndication in manpower exports has long been blamed for corruption, inflated migration costs, market closures and worker exploitation.

The development comes as Bangladesh and Malaysia prepare to resume new worker recruitment, which was suspended by Malaysia on May 31, 2024. 

The list, published on Malaysia’s Foreign Workers Centralised Management System, includes agencies that have been largely inactive in overseas recruitment.

FWCMS is a Malaysian government recognised online foreign workers hiring platform, which was previously used for manpower recruitment from Bangladesh.

Migration experts warn that a handful of agencies managing recruitment could revive the practices that previously sparked controversies and led Malaysia to suspend worker recruitment from Bangladesh.

‘This is clearly a case of market syndication. Different players may be involved this time, but corruption and irregularities could again affect the labour market,’ said Shariful Hasan, associate director and head of the Migration Programme and BRAC Youth Platform.

An analysis of the selected agencies also raised questions about their recent experience, ownership and connections.

The 25 agencies are: Bangladesh One Overseas Ltd, Bengal Human Resources Development, General Trading Company, M/S United Gulf Services, Annesa Air International, Att-Taqwa Overseas Ltd, Earth-Smart Bangladesh Ltd, Khan Jahan Ali Overseas, M/S Al-Shupto Overseas, Motherland Overseas Ltd, Rifa International, Vely Trade International, A-Plus International, Al-Hayat Overseas, Bhaluka Overseas, Brothers Trading & Contracting Ltd, Chandpur International, Country Employment Agency, Goodness Services Limited, Jewel Rinku Enterprise, M/S Satkhira International, Mangrove Career Link, Neighbour Associates Ltd, Tania Trade International and The Anchor Care Global Migration.

Among them, Mangrove Career Link is managed by Sabiha Sultana, the wife of Juba Dal central committee vice-president Md Atiqur Rahman Biswas, who also owns another recruiting agency, Al Aqaba Associates.

Atiqur denied having any business relationship with Mangrove Career Link.

‘I do not have any business link with Mangrove Career Link, although there is a family relationship,’ he said.

Both agencies, however, use the same office address.

Hajj Agencies Association of Bangladesh secretary general Farid Ahmed Mazumder, who is also the proprietor of M/S Al-Shupto Overseas, said that his agency had been selected for the first time. He said that he was primarily involved in Hajj management.

Asked why his agency had been selected, Farid said that he was unaware of the criteria used for the selection.

At a press conference on August 19, the general members of the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies, alleged that the cost of securing a place in the Malaysian recruitment syndicate had increased to about Tk 17.5 crore this time from around Tk 5 crore previously.

They feared that the additional cost would ultimately be passed on to migrant workers, pushing the cost of migration to between Tk 8 lakh and Tk 10 lakh per worker.

Lawmaker Hasnat Abdullah, in a Facebook post, alleged that Awami League lawmakers and ministers were involved in the previous syndicate.

‘Earlier, recruiting agencies owned by Awami League ministers and MPs were directly included in the syndicate. This time, perhaps, agencies directly owned by ministers or MPs have not been included. But allegations have emerged that their dummy or proxy companies have been brought in,’ he said.

Hasnat also questioned the selection of a number of relatively little-known or controversial agencies.

Expatriates’ welfare ministry officials said that Bangladesh had little involvement in the preparation of the list and that Malaysia selected the agencies.

State minister for expatriates’ welfare and overseas employment Mohammad Nurul Haque also said that he was unaware of how the list had been finalised.

‘I am not involved in the Malaysia negotiations. You should speak to the minister or the adviser,’ he said.

Asked whether the new arrangement could again create a recruitment syndicate similar to previous systems, he said that such a possibility could not be ruled out.

Expatriates’ welfare minister Ariful Haque Choudhury and special assistant Mahdi Amin were contacted for comments but did not respond to calls and messages.

Bangladesh currently has more than 2,600 licensed recruiting agencies. Earlier, the government prepared and sent Malaysia a list of 432 agencies that it considered eligible to handle recruitment.

Malaysia had earlier proposed eligibility criteria including large office space, owned training facilities and a record of sending at least 5,000 workers abroad in the previous three years.

Bangladesh objected to some of the conditions and subsequently submitted its own list of 432 recruiting agencies.

However, sector insiders said that several agencies included in the list of 25 did not appear to have the office capacity or recent migration record envisaged under the earlier Malaysian criteria.

They also alleged that politically connected people had influence over some of the selected agencies. 

New Age could not independently verify the allegations.

The latest list of 25 has also revived memories of the previous recruitment arrangement, which also began with only 25 Bangladeshi agencies in 2022 before being expanded to around 100.

That system was widely criticised as a syndicate, with allegations that a small group controlled access to the Malaysian labour market and charged other agencies substantial amounts to process workers.

Migration rights activists alleged that the syndicate system eventually pushed up migration costs far beyond the government-approved rate, leaving many workers heavily indebted even before leaving Bangladesh.

Malaysia is one of Bangladesh’s largest overseas labour markets. 

According to the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, nearly 13 lakh Bangladeshis have migrated to Malaysia since 2009.

The market has, however, been suspended several times amid allegations of corruption and irregularities.

Malaysia stopped recruiting Bangladeshi workers in 2009. The market reopened in late 2016 but was suspended again in September 2018. Recruitment resumed in 2022 before being halted again in 2024.

Migration experts warned that unless the selection process is made transparent and recruitment is opened to all qualified agencies, the latest arrangement could again create a closed syndicate.

They said that the ultimate victims of such a system would be migrant workers, who could once again be forced to pay several times more than the officially fixed migration cost to secure jobs in Malaysia.
