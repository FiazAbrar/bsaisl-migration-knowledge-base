                                          Achievements and Challenges

    Introduction: 2023 is a momentous year for migration from Bangladesh as it is the country's
    national election year. This year, significant changes in policies have occurred, and migration-
    related issues have been included in the election manifestos. Like every year, this year also,
    RMMRU has published the annual migration report titled- “Labour Migration from
    Bangladesh 2023: Achievements and Challenges”.

    The report is divided into 7 sections. Section 1 presents statistics on migration and the flow of
    remittances in 2023. Section 2 focuses on the important events that took place that year. In
    section 3, the role of service providers has been evaluated. Section 4 highlights the major law
    and policy reform. Section 5 concentrates on changes in international laws, and section 6
    highlights the role of civil society organisations with respect to the establishment of migrant
    rights. The report concludes with major conclusions and recommendations.


    1. Labour Migration from Bangladesh 2023

     1.1 Flow of Migrants

    According to BMET information, in 2023, a total of 13,05,453 Bangladeshi workers migrated
    to different countries around the world for employment.1 This is 13 percent higher in
    comparison to the previous year. A total of 11,35,873 Bangladeshi workers had migrated in
    2022.2 Analysing the migration data from 1976 to 2023, it is evident that this year Bangladesh
    has had the highest rate of increase in migration in the past 48 years.
    From 2020 to 2021, Bangladesh's migration sector was at risk due to the COVID-19
    pandemic. However, since 2022, the rate of labour migration from Bangladesh has increased
    again. This increase can be attributed to the fact that migrants who were unable to travel
    abroad during the COVID-19 pandemic have migrated in 2022 and 2023. the reopening of
    business establishments in labour- receiving countries after COVID-19 also contributed to
    such an increase. The increase of quota from 25 percent to 40 percent for Bangladeshi migrant
    workers in all Saudi institutions has also contributed to the rise in migration.3 This trend of
    increased migration has been observed not only in Bangladesh but also in other South Asian
    countries since 2022. Migration from Nepal increased 300 times in 2022 compared to the

    1.2 Female Migration

    In 2023, a total of 76,519 female workers have migrated abroad for employment. In 2022, this
    number was 105,466. It is observed that compared to 2022, the flow of female migration
    decreased by 27.45 percent this year.6 Of the total international migration, 5.86 percent were
    female workers this year. Last year, it was 9.3 percent. From 2015 to 2019, more than 100,000
    annually migrated. After COVID-19, the number of female migrants is reducing. From 1991
    to 2023, a total of 11,820,030 female workers have migrated abroad for employment.

    1.5 Skill Composition

    BMET divides Bangladeshi migrant workers into four categories: professional, skilled, semi-
    skilled, and less-skilled. This year, 4.14 percent of total migrant workers are professionals,
    24.77 percent are skilled, 21.01 percent are semi-skilled, and 50 percent belong to the less-
    skilled category. Compared to last year, this year, the rate of professionals, skilled workers,
    and semi-skilled workers has increased by 3.8 percent, 2.04 percent, and 17.36 percent,
    respectively. The percentage share of less-skilled workers has decreased by 23 percent.9

 2.4 Commencement of National Diaspora Day
 Although the United Nations has declared December 18 as International Migrants' Day for all
 groups of migrants, the government of Bangladesh has declared December 30 as the National
 Migrants' Day for long-term international migrants. Based on the importance of observance,
 the day is listed as category B by the Cabinet Division, while on 18th December, the UN
 International Migrants Day, is listed as category C. Since 1996, the civil society has been
 celebrating International Migrants Day. Since 2007, the government has taken over the
 responsibility of observing the day. 18th December is listed as Category C. Category C has
 less entitlement than Category B.

 2.7 Regularisation of Irregular Workers
     2.7.1 Opportunities for Regularisation of Irregular Migrants in Malaysia

 The Malaysian government undertook the Workforce Recalibration (RTK 2.0) programme in
 January this year, which provided an opportunity to obtain regular visa status from irregular.21
 This opportunity was offered till 31st December 2023. More than 700,000 applications were
 received, including those from Bangladeshi workers.22 Bangladesh High Commission in
 Kuala Lumpur issued passports to the irregular migrants after verifying their Bangladeshi
 citizenship. Passports were transferred through the Post Office, Passport Service Center of
 Bangladesh High Commission in KL, and CBL Money Transfer House.


 2.7.2 Regularisation of Bangladeshi Workers in Greece
 From January to 30th November 2023, the government of Greece implemented a
 regularization scheme.23 The application was contingent on having a Bangladeshi passport
 with two years validity, proof of residence in Greece before 9 February 2022, and proof of
 work permit if regular. So far, more than 10,000 Bangladeshi migrants have registered, and
 6,000 are awaiting residence permits.

 2.9 Human Trafficking, Smuggling, and Migration

Every year, approximately 5000 people from Bangladesh attempt to reach developed countries
through unsafe means.26 But many of those are cheated and experience great financial and
untimely returns. Recently, 140 people arrested in Libya have been sent back to the country. In
2022, a United Nations report on human trafficking revealed that Bangladesh's Dhaka, Khulna,
and Sylhet divisions have the highest number of labour smuggling/trafficking cases.27
Recently, the countries of the European Union have reached a consensus on tougher migration
laws.

 3.4 Labour Wing


 At present Bangladesh Embassies located in 27 countries have a total of 30 Labour Welfare
 Wings working to serve the migrant workers in the countries. The main responsibility of the
 Labour Welfare Wing is to protect the rights and security of the migrant workers. The WEWB
 provides legal assistance in handling cases in different courts, recovery of compensation,
 arrears and other benefits, bringing dead bodies, running safe homes for runaway/deserter
 women workers through these Labour Welfare Wings

 5. Protection of Migrant Workers in the International Law


 5.1 Stricter Migration Regime in France


 In the recently passed Bill, there is an indication of stricter migration policies and further
 toughening of immigration laws in France. In the controversial bill, it has been suggested that
 the process of deciding asylum applications be expedited.48 Additionally, it has been
 suggested that the time limit for appeals be reduced in case of application rejection. There is a
 recommendation to make the family reunion visa process complicated, which will make
 migration for individuals who have family members in France difficult. There is also a
 possibility of making visa applications for medical treatment in France more difficult. A
 possible change is likely to occur regarding sending back those under 13 years of age.


 5.2 New Law to Control Irregular Migration in the United Kingdom

 To control the influx of irregular migrants using small boats to the United Kingdom, the

 5.3 Stricter Laws on Migration by the European Union

 The countries of the European Union are moving towards consensus on enforcement of laws
 aimed at controlling irregular migrants. Consequently, it is expected that EU migration laws
 will become even stricter. Providing shelter to irregular migrants in Europe, expedited
 verification and selection processes, rapid repatriation of individuals whose asylum
 applications are rejected, and the establishment of detention at borders are some major issues
 that have been focused on here. Under this process, several asylum seekers will be relocated
 to various countries within the European Union. If any asylum seeker is denied shelter by a
 certain country, they are expected to contribute financially or through infrastructure.


 5.4 Positive Changes in Migration Law in Germany


 The government of Germany has proposed amendments to the Citizenship law aimed at
 solving the skilled worker crisis and making citizenship more attractive for migrants. As a
 result of these amendments, obtaining citizenship may become easier for migrants.50 One
 major change in the draft law is the reduction of the residency requirement for citizenship
 applications from eight years to five years. This means that if someone resides regularly in
 Germany for five years, they can apply for citizenship. Individuals with skills, voluntary
 work, language proficiency, and financial independence for themselves and their families may
 be granted approval after three years under special consideration.
