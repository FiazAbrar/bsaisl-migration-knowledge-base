## Changing visa type

- **Visit pass (tourist or family visit) to PLKS (company worker's pass):** No. The employer gets the VDR and PLKS while you are outside Malaysia; you enter only after approval [S0090, W0045]. See Work visa: company worker (PLKS).
- **Visit pass to Longer visit pass:** Only for illness, an accident or war at home, with proof and a ticket home or onward [S0089].
- **Visit pass to DE Rantau Nomad Pass:** No. A tourist pass can't be converted [E0095]. See DE Rantau Nomad Pass.
- **Visit pass to Student Pass:** No. You must be outside Malaysia when the application is submitted [S0002]. See Student visa (Student Pass).
- **Visit pass to Professional job up to 30 days:** Only arranged before you travel. The company gets PLS@Xpats approval (see Business visit, Step 3) [P0024]; without the approval letter you won't be let in for this work [P0030]. Longer: see Professional Visit Pass.
- **Student Pass to Student Pass for a higher level:** Yes. Apply for Progression (for example bachelor's to master's) within 3 months of finishing; you may stay while it is processed [S0037, Z0056].
- **Student Pass to Work pass:** Only through an employer. The employer applies, for example for an Employment Pass [S0100]. Bangladeshis can't get the Graduate Pass [S0044].
- **PLKS to Another employer or sector:** Only with the Home Ministry's permission. See If you want to change employer [S0091, L0067].
- **Dependant Pass to Work:** Only as an Employment Pass. The employer applies for an Employment Pass for the dependant [E0028, E0035]. See Family of an Employment Pass holder.
- **Long-Term Social Visit Pass, family of an Employment Pass holder to Work or study:** Only by converting. An employer applies for an Employment Pass, or you get a Student Pass [S0141, E0032]. See Employment Pass; Student visa (Student Pass).
- **Long-Term Social Visit Pass, spouse of a Malaysian to Work:** Yes. Get the work endorsement in your passport; see Spouse of a Malaysian citizen [S0095, E0125].
- **Any pass, applicant outside Malaysia to Employment Pass:** Yes. The employer applies for the VDR with the Employment Pass [E0058].

## Asylum

Asylum gives no right to stay or work in Malaysia [S0142, S0107]. There is no national asylum system and the Immigration Act has no provision on refugees; UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) decides claims under its own mandate, and its recognition gives no legal immigration status [S0101, K0036, S0142]. Refugees and asylum seekers are treated as undocumented migrants: they can be arrested, detained, fined and deported, and may not work [S0106, S0107]. UNHCR can't grant work permits or stop an arrest; its document is not a visa and at most may reduce the risk of arrest [S0108, S0111].

**Who counts.** Only someone who fled persecution or serious harm because of race, religion, nationality, social group or political opinion, or who can't return because of war or serious disorder; someone who left for work or study is a migrant [S0102].

**The process.**

1. Apply only on UNHCR's My Services portal, reached from help.unhcr.org/malaysia ("Registering with UNHCR"): create a personal account and submit the request; you get a reference number to quote in every contact, and letters are not accepted [S0109, T0050, T0051].
2. Wait to be called for an interview; don't go to the Reception Centre without an appointment [T0069]. UNHCR deals first with requests from 2022 or earlier and the most vulnerable, and can't say when anyone will be interviewed [S0110, S0112, S0103].
3. At the interview you must tell the truth; being coached or misleading UNHCR harms the case [S0105].
4. A refusal can be appealed once, within 30 days; then the case closes and UNHCR's help and documents end [S0104].

An unregistered pregnant asylum seeker pays full foreigners' fees for antenatal care and childbirth at government facilities [S0113].

**Scams.** All UNHCR services are free; anyone asking money for an interview or letters is lying [S0109, S0143]. Fake UNHCR cards and work permits are sold [N0049]. Scan a card's large QR code with the free UNHCR Verify Plus app (Google Play or App Store); "Incompatible QR Code" means it is not genuine [N0046, N0047].

**Contact.** UNHCR Malaysia, 570 Jalan Bukit Petaling, 50460 Kuala Lumpur, 03 2118 4800; detention hotline +6012 630 5060 [N0048].

   - A 100% disabled worker needing constant care gets RM500 a month more, and PERKESO pays for physiotherapy, artificial limbs, wheelchairs and similar aids [U0006, M0172].
   - A worker certified invalid who hasn't paid Invalidity Scheme contributions long enough gets a one-time Invalidity Grant of both sides' contributions with interest [U0013].

   Payments can go to a bank outside Malaysia: give the account holder, bank, address, branch, account number and SWIFT code on the form, with a bank verification letter [U0070, U0018].
4. **If the employer won't report it or pay you while you are off work,** file a case at the nearest Labour Department office (offices by state at jtksm.mohr.gov.my/ms/hubungi-kami/alamat-ibu-pejabat-dan-cawangan) with your payslips, contract and attendance record, and give a copy to the labour wing (counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, +60 12-290 3252), which pursues compensation claims [U0075, L0037, B0117, F0029]. The Labour Court works as in route 3 of "If you aren't paid"; if the employer ignores its Order, the Labour Department can seize and sell its property, take the money from its bank or prosecute it [U0029, U0030, U0076]. Sample message to the labour wing: "I am [name], passport [number], PLKS with [employer, address]. I was injured at work on [date] at [place]. My employer has not reported it to PERKESO. Please help me claim." Tenaganita and the Bar Council's Legal Aid Centres help with cases (see route 4 of "If you aren't paid") [J0007, J0047]; the ILO (International Labour Organization) does not take complaints from individual workers [G0027].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does Tenaganita charge workers for its help?

5. **Going home injured.** On your or your family's application, with proof of BMET clearance or Board membership and a passport copy, the Welfare Board asks the High Commission to bring you home [B0073]. The Board's ambulance at Dhaka and Chattogram airports is free on request [B0070].
6. **Treatment money in Bangladesh.** A returnee with BMET clearance or Board membership who is injured, sick or disabled can get up to Tk 2 lakh [B0071, B0072]. Within 1 year of return, the family applies to the Board's Director General via its web portal, the district probashi kallyan (migrant welfare) centre or the Board [B0071, U0092].

   You'll need [B0071, U0092]:
   - medical reports and prescriptions;
   - a bank statement;
   - a passport copy and a copy of its arrival stamp;
   - proof of clearance or membership;
   - one 6R (6 by 8 inch) colour photo of the worker.

   A Board committee checks the case by WhatsApp video call and recommends an amount; free, up to 60 working days after all papers are in [B0071, U0092].
7. **Insurance.** It pays Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability and 10% to 90% of that for partial permanent disability [B0009]; see "Claiming the insurance".

### If a worker dies

These steps are for the family in Bangladesh.

1. **Ask the Welfare Board to bring the body home.** Call 16135 (from abroad +8809610102030) [B0083]. Then apply in one of these ways, even if the worker had no BMET clearance or Board membership [F0074]:
   - on myGov (the government's online services portal), service "Application to bring home a migrant worker's body", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638167134, with a myGov account (register with your mobile number at idp-v2.live.mygov.bd/registration), the worker's passport and any clearance or membership; a tracking number comes by SMS and you can follow the case on myGov; free; up to 35 working days [U0049, U0091, U0051]; or
   - to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, which also handles undocumented workers, describing what happened and the cause of death, with proof the person was a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID, death certificate); free [B0051].

   Sample: "My [relation], [name], passport [number], worked for [employer, phone] in [town], Malaysia, and died on [date]. We want the body brought home to [district]. The receiver is [name, phone]."
2. **Home or burial in Malaysia.** The Board passes the family's choice to the High Commission, and the family sends the High Commission a claim or consent letter naming the receiver and their phone number [B0074, B0075, L0039]. For burial in Malaysia, apply on myGov with the service "Application for permission to bury a migrant worker's body in the destination country", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638246958, free, 3 working days [U0083].

   You'll need [U0083]:
   - the worker's passport or visa copy;
   - any clearance or membership;
   - the district office's investigation report;
   - a certificate signed by all heirs on the union parishad (rural local council) chairman's letterhead.
3. **The Malaysian side.** The employer writes to the High Commission, appoints a funeral company and sends its letters, the insurance claim and compensation letters, the hospital letter, the death certificate, the Malaysian police report and the passport [L0038, B0119]. It pays the funeral and repatriation [M0148]. For its no-objection certificate (NOC) the High Commission also needs [L0023, L0024, L0025, L0026, L0027]:
   - the employer's PERKESO death report;
   - for an abnormal death (hanging, fall, accident, murder), a police report with NOC;
   - for an undocumented worker, the original passport (or a police report of its loss), the receiver's details, a police report with NOC, the original death certificate, the funeral company's letter and the employer's name and phone.

   The Board forwards the request within 3 to 5 working days, and the labour wing takes about 30 [U0050]. An undocumented worker's body can take much longer to come home [F0073].

   If there is no employer or it refuses, the Welfare Board pays [B0125, L0040]. PERKESO repays whoever paid (a person, company or the High Commission) up to RM4,500 of repatriation, even if the death isn't otherwise covered, on its PPJ (Funeral Repatriation Cost Claim) form, on which the employer states whether it paid; hand it in at a PERKESO counter or post it, with [U0010, U0072, U0073]:
   - a certified copy of the death certificate and of the claimant's ID or company registration;
   - the original receipt;
   - bank proof;
   - proof the body was sent home (the Ministry of Health's export permit or the airline's air waybill).

   Malaysian authorities trace the family through the permanent address and emergency contact in the worker's passport, so write a full name and a working phone number in the passport's emergency contact field; one body waited 15 days in a Kuala Lumpur morgue, at RM75 a day, because that field held only "0088" [U0088, F0068, F0069]. If no family is found, the worker may be buried in Malaysia as an unclaimed person [F0070].

   Death papers usually give the cause as illness or natural death [YB0023]; migrants say deaths are recorded as stroke or heart attack to avoid workplace-accident compensation, and Bangladesh doesn't check the cause [F0076, H0037]. If the family doubts it, apply to the Welfare Board for legal help abroad: myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638179893, at the Board, or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office (the district administration) or the DEMO, with proof of clearance or membership and a passport or travel permit copy with arrival and departure stamps; free; sent to the labour wing within 1 to 2 days; up to 20 working days [U0085, B0077].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Can a family ask the Malaysian police for a post-mortem or inquest?
   > - How does the family make that request?

4. **At the airport.** The Board's desk gives a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, collectable within 3 months, and a free ambulance [B0066, B0070]. You'll need [B0067, B0066]:
   - the worker's passport copy;
   - a heirship (warish) certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor;
   - the NID of the wife, father or mother;
   - the death certificate;
   - the NOC;
   - the original air waybill;
   - proof of clearance or membership;
   - a photo and NID or passport copy of the named receiver, and of the collector if different.
5. **The Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant,** for the family of a worker with clearance or membership who died in Malaysia, the country of the clearance [B0060, B0061]. If the family took the airport cheque, no application is needed: the Board writes asking for the papers; after burial in Malaysia, apply within 1 year of the death to the Board's Director General or through the district office [B0062]. The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents; for an unmarried worker with no living parents, to full siblings [B0063].

   You'll need [B0064, U0096]:
   - a family-members certificate on the letterhead of the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor, countersigned by the UNO or, in a city corporation, the regional executive officer;
   - an indemnity, undertaking and authorisation on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper, countersigned the same way (samples on wewb.gov.bd);
   - the NOC or death certificate copy;
   - a bank certificate or original statement for the receiver's account with the routing number (minor children's own account where they apply);
   - each family member's colour photo, attested by the chairman and the district welfare centre;
   - the worker's passport copy.

   Paid by bank transfer (BEFTN), with an SMS, within 60 working days of all papers reaching the Board [B0065, U0096].
6. **The insurance:** Tk 10 lakh for a death within 5 years of the smart card date, for workers who left with clearance on or after 1 January 2023 [B0085, B0009]. Cover for those who left between 19 December 2019 and 31 December 2022 lasted 2 years and has ended [U0087]. See "Claiming the insurance".

   > **Need to know**
   > - Are workers who left between 1 January and 11 March 2023 covered, since BMET's leaflet gives the start as 12 March 2023 [B0008]?

7. **Malaysian compensation.** PERKESO pays:
   - a Dependants' Benefit for a work-related death [U0007];
   - a funeral benefit against receipts of up to RM7,500 for burial in Bangladesh (up to RM4,500 to whoever paid repatriation, RM3,000 to the dependants) or up to RM3,000 for burial in Malaysia [U0008, U0009];
   - if the worker died in Malaysia before 60 from any cause, a Survivors' Pension of at least RM550 a month, if Invalidity Scheme contributions were paid for at least 24 of the 40 consecutive months before the claim or two-thirds of the months since they became due [U0011, U0012, U0089].

   Claim within 12 months [U0064]. You'll need [U0015, U0017, U0018, U0019]:
   - Form 34-PA;
   - the worker's passport and work pass copies;
   - the death certificate or burial permit;
   - proof of relationship (marriage certificate, widow's ID and children's birth certificates; or, with no wife or child, the worker's birth certificate and parents' IDs);
   - bank proof;
   - Bangladeshi papers translated into English by an accredited translator and certified by the High Commission or a Bangladesh authority [U0019].

   For an accident, the Labour Department sends its Form H to the heirs, who fill it in and return it through the High Commission [L0036, B0120].

   How it works: when the High Commission reports that compensation may be due, the Welfare Board asks the family for a power of attorney and the papers, with free help from its legal cell, sends them to the High Commission and follows the case; the labour wing recovers compensation and unpaid wages, and the Board pays them to the heirs within 60 working days of having the papers and the money [B0076, U0097, B0136]. The family can also ask the Ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), which writes to the High Commission and the Board within 7 working days [B0050].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does PERKESO take to pay a death claim filed through the High Commission?

### If you lose the job

1. **The employer must** tell the Labour Department within 30 days [M0065]. Under the MoU's standard contract it gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages, pays all money owed and pays your fare home unless you broke the contract [L0144, L0152, L0142]. It sends you home on a Check-Out Memo [M0229]; with under 4 weeks' notice you may stay in its housing up to 4 weeks [M0205]. You can't simply move to another employer (If you want to change employer) [M0123].
2. **To claim money owed,** go to the Labour Court ("If you aren't paid") [U0029, U0030, M0113] with:
   - your passport;
   - the workplace's name and address;
   - payslips and income details;
   - appointment letter and terms of service;
   - any termination letter.
3. **If you were dismissed without just cause,** file a written representation to the Director General of Industrial Relations, by hand at the Industrial Relations Department (JPP) office nearest the workplace, within 60 days of the dismissal or the end of the notice period, and keep the acknowledgement; union membership isn't needed and you can represent yourself [U0077, U0078, U0081, U0080]. JPP tries to settle, then sends the case to the Industrial Court, which can award compensation only if you asked for reinstatement [U0079, U0082]. A letter showing the case is pending usually gets you a Special Pass (see route 3 of "If you aren't paid") [J0033].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How does a worker find the address of the JPP office nearest the workplace?
   > - Can the representation be filed online?
   > - Is there a fee to file it?

4. **Insurance:** coming home within 6 months of going after losing the job pays Tk 50,000 [B0009, B0085]; see "Claiming the insurance".

## Coming home

- **Leaving Malaysia with cash.** You may carry any amount of foreign currency, and ringgit up to the equivalent of USD 10,000; above USD 10,000 in total, declare it on Customs form No. 22, available before the customs checkpoint, or face a fine of up to RM1 million, up to a year in jail or both [U0032, U0033, U0034].
- **Customs in Bangladesh.**
  - Duty-free: 65 kg of baggage, 2 used phones and 1 new phone a year, and once a year, if declared, 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [U0039, U0035, U0036].
  - Once a year you may bring a declared gold bar up to 117 g (10 tola; duty Tk 5,000 per tola) or silver bar up to 234 g; a bigger or hidden bar is confiscated [U0037, B0112].
  - No alcohol on a Bangladeshi passport [U0038].
  - Declare foreign currency over USD 10,000 on the FMJ form [B0115].
  - Customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 [B0116].

  > **Need to know**
  > - May BMET card holders who have been abroad at least 6 months bring 2 new phones a year duty-free?

- **Airport help.** The Welfare Board's desks help with boarding cards, customs and lost luggage, take complaints of abuse or cheating, and send sick returnees to hospital by ambulance; Dhaka desk +8802 8901040, 01404 413388 [B0068, B0069, B0070]. Women returning within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 cash [B0038].
- **A place to stay.**
  - The Wage Earners' Centre (Lonjonipara, Borua, Khilkhet, Dhaka-1229, near Shahjalal airport): Tk 100 admission, Tk 200 a night; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd or in person with proof of clearance or membership, a passport copy and the ticket; 01310 350555, 01754 715720 (24 hours) [B0080].
  - The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives free wifi and phone calls and food at up to 30% off when you show proof of clearance or membership [B0082].
- **If your family is threatened or its property seized,** apply to the Welfare Board for legal help (myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638181581, the Board, the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office or the DEMO) with the worker's passport copy and any clearance or membership; free, up to 3 working days; the Board writes to the local administration, such as the Deputy Commissioner or Superintendent of Police [U0084, B0078, B0132].
- **A business loan.** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB, the government bank for migrants) lends returnees who start a project up to Tk 50 lakh, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral, at 9% simple interest over up to 10 years; apply at the PKB branch for the business's area on its free form [B0021, B0022, U0055, U0052].

  You'll need [U0053, U0054, U0056]:
  - 3 passport photos;
  - passport and NID copies;
  - a certificate of your present and permanent address from the pourashava (municipality) or union parishad;
  - a current trade licence;
  - project details with 2 years' income and expenses;
  - the premises' lease or ownership papers;
  - a declaration of your own investment and other loans;
  - copies of your return papers;
  - 3 signed cheque leaves from the business account;
  - a guarantor (a relative or anyone able to repay) with 2 photos, ID copy, address certificate, a clean CIB report (Bangladesh Bank's credit report showing no unpaid loans) and 3 signed cheque leaves.

  Fees: 0.50% processing and 0.50% documentation, each capped at Tk 15,000, each plus VAT, paid when you take the loan; no service charge [U0090]. Decision within 15 working days of a complete application [B0022]. Helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018].
- **BRAC's help.** BRAC's Migration Programme gives returnees and trafficking survivors airport help at Dhaka through its Migration Welfare Centre, counselling, money or materials to start earning, and legal help [B0122, U0047].

  > **Need to know**
  > - How does a returnee contact BRAC's Migration Programme?

- **Children's scholarships.** The Welfare Board pays migrants' children scholarships (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [B0131].
