# Malaysia: migration guide

How to go to Malaysia from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step, for Bangladesh passport holders. \[3\] is an official source; \[N3\] is a news, NGO, research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like \[N3 (2022)\], marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000); RM is the Malaysian ringgit. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is the Bangladesh government agency that registers people who want to work abroad and gives them emigration clearance [B0026, G0001]. IMI (Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia, the Immigration Department of Malaysia) approves work visas and runs the eVISA (electronic visa) system on its MyVISA website, malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my [V0005, Z0032, W0045]. Bangladeshis need a visa to enter Malaysia for any purpose, even a short visit [V0001, V0002].

## Background

**How recruitment works.** Bangladeshis go to Malaysia mainly as company workers on the PLKS (Pas Lawatan Kerja Sementara, Temporary Employment Visit Pass), Malaysia's work pass for factory, construction, plantation, farm and service jobs [U0057, W0041]. A Malaysian employer first gets a government quota of foreign workers and pays a levy (a per-worker charge) for each [W0041, W0054]. Malaysia caps foreign workers at 2.5 million and wants them cut from about 15% of the workforce to 10% by 2030 [YA0070]. The employer applies for the worker's visa through FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralised Management System), the online system built by the private company Bestinet, which Malaysia has used for visa applications from Bangladesh since 2013 [W0045, Z0001, YA0051]; Bestinet's MiGRAMS module registers each worker before the medical check [D0024]. Recruitment has run in two ways: government-to-government (G2G), through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government-owned recruiting agency), and through private agencies on a short list that Malaysia chooses [YA0008, Z0084, YA0017]. Construction workers can also go through CLAB (Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad, Malaysia's government construction recruiter), which signed with Bangladesh on 7 August 2025 and by November 2025 had sent a demand for 500 workers [W0019, D0042]; it interviews and health-screens recruits and trains those who pass for 12 days, after which the Bangladesh government issues a certificate [D0036].

**The syndicate.** In this corridor the "syndicate" is the short list of agencies that alone may process Malaysian recruitment, so every other agency must pay one of them to send workers [YA0023, A0006]. Whenever private agencies were allowed, Malaysia limited them to such a list: 10 agencies under the 2016-18 "G2G Plus" scheme, 25 rising to about 100 in 2022-24, and 25 "processing agencies" in 2026, as for Nepal, against 10 each for India, Pakistan and Myanmar [YA0001, YA0016, Z0155]. Its criteria require an agency to have run satisfactorily for 5 years, sent 3,000 workers in the last 3 years and worked in 3 destination countries in 5 years [W0023]. In 2022-24 Bangladesh asked for all 1,520 licensed agencies to be allowed; Malaysia chose 101, and every other agency had to process its job orders through one of them [YA0023, A0006]. Agencies reportedly paid the syndicate about Tk 1.07 to 1.52 lakh per worker, plus Tk 1.6 to 2 lakh to middlemen for the demand letter (the employer's job order), and recouped it from workers [A0002, A0028, A0107, YA0032, W0070]; workers who didn't pay a syndicate fee of about USD 1,350 were allegedly denied their passports [A0069]. Of a Tk 1.07 lakh charge against an official registration fee of Tk 2,700, Tk 1 lakh reportedly went to Bestinet through illegal channels; Bestinet says it charges no syndicate fee [YA0048, YA0049, YA0050]. UN human rights experts wrote in 2024 that the chain reportedly begins with bribes in Malaysia's Human Resources and Home ministries for fake quotas for bogus employers, then reaches the Bangladesh High Commission (embassy) in Kuala Lumpur and the listed agencies, and that high-level officials in both governments were reported to be involved [YA0025, YA0026, YA0060].

**The people named and the cases.** The Daily Star reports that the 2022-24 syndicate of 101 agencies was led by Aminul Islam Abdul Nor ("Amin"), a Bangladesh-born Malaysian citizen who founded Bestinet, and Ruhul Amin Swapan, owner of Catharsis International [YA0054, YA0047, YA0053]. Swapan, a former secretary general of BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies, the agencies' trade body), also ran one of the 10 G2G Plus agencies [YA0007, YA0042]. In October 2024 Bangladesh police asked Malaysia, through Interpol, to arrest and extradite both over alleged money laundering, extortion and trafficking of workers [YA0045]; Malaysian police are handling the request, but by April 2026 Amin had not been extradited or charged [YA0053, YA0055]. Both deny the allegations [YA0046, YA0056]. Malaysia's anti-corruption agency, the MACC (Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission), questioned Amin in a 2022 investigation of Bestinet over the selection of 25 Bangladeshi agencies, and in 2024 closed it with no further action, saying it found no evidence of crime [YB0022, YB0021]. In August 2025 a Dhaka court attached about Tk 500 crore of Swapan's property on the application of the CID (Criminal Investigation Department of Bangladesh Police) [YA0043].

Agencies of former ministers and MPs were among the listed ones: Orbital Enterprises and Orbital International (wife and daughter of former finance minister AHM Mustafa Kamal), Snigdha Overseas (former MP Nizam Uddin Hazari), Five M International (former MP Lt Gen (retd) Masud Uddin Chowdhury) and Ahmed International (former MP Benazir Ahmed) [YA0020]. The ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) filed 12 cases against them and others in March 2025, alleging they took Tk 1,128 crore above the Tk 78,990 cap [YA0033, YA0034]; with 13 more agencies in September 2025, its cases covered 64 people from 25 agencies over about Tk 2,288 crore, including fees up to five times the cap [YA0037, A0084, A0085]. It filed 6 more cases in November 2025 and decided in December to sue 60 more agencies over Tk 4,545 crore [YA0039, YA0038]. The CID filed a money-laundering case against 33 people, including Swapan and the former MPs, in August 2025 [YA0041, YA0042]. In July 2026 Bangladesh's Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment ("the Ministry") cancelled the licences of 49 agencies, including all of these and Catharsis [YA0019, YA0020, YA0021, A0012]. A separate trafficking case against former expatriates' welfare minister Imran Ahmad and the 101 agencies was closed by the CID in July 2025, three months after Malaysia's Human Resources Ministry asked Bangladesh to review or withdraw the cases; in November 2025 a Dhaka tribunal ordered the police Detective Branch to investigate again [YA0027, YA0028, YA0031, YA0030].

**What workers pay.** Every round has had an official cap and a far higher reported price: agents charged over Tk 2 lakh against Tk 84,000 in 2006-09 [YA0009]; the 10 G2G Plus agencies were alleged to take Tk 3-4 lakh against Tk 37,000 [YA0002]; and workers paid an average of Tk 5.44 lakh against Tk 78,990 in 2022-24, by survey and press estimates [R0008, YA0022, W0070]. A 2024 memo for Malaysia's prime minister put costs as high as USD 6,600 per worker, far more than Indonesian or Nepali workers pay [A0070]. The ACC alleges the extra was charged as fees for passports, medical checks and other services [YA0036]. UN experts said workers were pressed to sign that they had paid only the official fee [A0061], and the CID officer who closed the trafficking case cited those signatures [YA0029]. In a 2024 survey of 240 Malaysia workers, 96% had borrowed to pay, and 73% spent half or more of their monthly pay on repayments [A0064]. For 2026 (cap: see "Is recruitment open now?") the expatriates' welfare minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, expects private-agency costs of Tk 2.5-3 lakh, the range the agencies proposed [YA0066]. Agency owners say a Malaysian employer's demand letter now sells for RM8,000 to 10,000 (formerly RM2,000 to 3,000) and that workers will pay at least Tk 5 lakh [G0013, G0008].

**Bans and closures.** About 400,000 Bangladeshis went in 2007-08; many found no job and became undocumented, and in 2009 Malaysia froze hiring over reported exploitation and alleged syndicate irregularities [YA0011, YA0010, U0041]. BMET clearances fell from 273,201 in 2007 to 919 in 2010, and from 175,927 in 2018 to 545 in 2019 [B0091]. Under the 2012 G2G deal, 1.4 million applied for about 10,000 places [G0029, YA0008]. G2G Plus ran from March 2016 until Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad's new government ended it on 1 September 2018 as monopolistic [YA0001, YA0003, YA0004]. The 2021 MoU (memorandum of understanding) reopened the market in 2022 [W0027, YA0017]; on 21 March 2024 Malaysia announced that new intake would stop on 31 May 2024 [U0042], after tens of thousands of Bangladeshis had reportedly paid up to USD 5,000 for jobs that did not exist [YA0052, YA0062]. A Ministry probe blamed the agencies for the 17,777 BMET-cleared workers who could not fly by the deadline, and ordered refunds [A0045, A0054]. From 25 November 2025 BOESL began sending the 7,873 of them it was allotted, at Tk 1,62,500 each, of which BOESL's managing director said Tk 1 lakh was for buying visas [W0018, W0020, A0058, A0056].

**Where things stand.** In 2026 Malaysia's high-level committee again chose the agencies without the Bangladesh government, the Ministry said [YA0063, A0029]; a press analysis of BMET data found 17 of the 25 had never sent a worker to Malaysia [A0027]. Malaysia lists 338 Bangladeshi agencies: BOESL, the 25, and 312 associates (10 under each of the 25, 62 under BOESL) [Z0144, W0008]. Of the 25, Mangrove Career Link is managed by the wife of a central vice-president of Juba Dal (the BNP's youth wing), who denies a business link, and M/S Al-Shupto Overseas belongs to the secretary general of the Hajj Agencies Association of Bangladesh [YB0024, YB0030, YB0025, YB0026]; a lawmaker alleged that proxy companies of former ministers and MPs were brought in, and it is alleged that Amin and Swapan decided who joined [YB0027, YB0028]. Former BAIRA leaders alleged each agency was asked for Tk 17 crore to join a new syndicate [A0011]; BAIRA members say workers face a RM5,500 syndicate fee plus RM8,000-9,000 to buy each visa, Tk 6 to 7 lakh in all, and the state minister said a new syndicate could not be ruled out [A0026, YA0064]. On 5 November 2026 the expatriates' welfare minister said he wants all licensed agencies to take part, that departures should start in December with the first two or three flights free, and that a new MoU will be signed on 31 December, when the current one ends [YA0067, YA0065, YA0068, Z0156]. Recruitment restarted in October 2026, and Malaysia has agreed in principle to take 10,000 workers at no cost to them ("Is recruitment open now?") [Z0151, Z0146].

**Irregular routes.** In 2026 a ring charged up to RM15,000 a person to smuggle Bangladeshis in through a third country and Kelantan (a Malaysian state bordering Thailand); those caught were held in an immigration depot [F0052, F0053]. In 2023 the MACC arrested an enforcement officer at the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka and a labour-supply company owner on suspicion of approving visas for bribes of US$300 to 500 each since 2021, and tourist visas were reportedly issued to people going to work [N0025, N0024]. Malaysia refuses Bangladeshi visa applications at among the highest rates, which travel operators blame on fake documents, overstaying and tourist visas used to work [N0026].

**Numbers.** At the end of 2025, 798,973 of Malaysia's 2,125,587 PLKS holders (37.59%) were Bangladeshi, ahead of Indonesians (551,337) and Nepalis (323,601) [U0057]. Activists and researchers estimate that 100,000 to 200,000 of about 800,000 Bangladeshis in Malaysia are jobless, unpaid and in debt [L0191]. BMET cleared 351,683 workers for Malaysia in 2023, 93,632 in 2024, 3,451 in 2025 and 1,592 from 1 January to 6 November 2026 [B0091, B0088, B0089]. Malaysia was Bangladesh's 5th-largest remittance source in July to September 2026, at USD 666.73 million [B0093]. Malaysia's investment minister wants foreign hiring limited mainly to dirty, dangerous and difficult (3D) jobs [U0046, U0045].

## Work visa: company worker (PLKS)

The PLKS is valid 12 months at a time and renewed by the employer [W0048, L0044]. Bangladeshis may work only in manufacturing, construction, agriculture, plantation and services, except sectors where intake is frozen [W0041, W0042]. Jobs in Sabah and Sarawak fall under those state governments [L0065]. You must be 18 to 45 when the employer applies [W0073, L0064]. The employer pays the levy, the visa, the pass and a deposit [W0054]. Under the Bangladesh-Malaysia MoU, in force until 31 December 2026, the employer pays all Malaysian-side costs, including your airfare [W0028, R0009, Z0156].

**How a job reaches you.** A Bangladeshi recruiting agency on Malaysia's approved list collects the employer's job order (demand) [Z0145], which the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur attests [A0035, W0038], and selects workers under Bangladesh's rules [Z0147]. BOESL or one of the 25 processing agencies handles the Malaysian visa paperwork [Z0144, Z0148]; the recruiting agency gets your BMET clearance under its own licence [Z0149]. The employer applies for your visa through FWCMS only after you pass the medical check in Bangladesh [W0045, W0009, Z0001].

**Is recruitment open now?** Recruitment restarted in October 2026 through agencies on Malaysia's list [Z0151]. In their 8 April 2026 joint statement both governments said employers will bear the full cost of recruitment [A0097]. Malaysia has agreed in principle to take 10,000 workers at no cost to them, airfare included, the first batch through BOESL [Z0146, Z0154]; on 28 October the state minister estimated that batch would cost each worker Tk 1.55 to 1.60 lakh [U0043]. The Ministry says no worker may be made to bear extra cost for the job [Z0150]. **Need to know:** the most a private agency may take since the October 2026 reopening [G0009, G0010], whether the employer still pays the airfare, and what terms the new MoU due on 31 December (Background) will set.

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP),** BMET's website for job-seekers. Agencies must pick workers from BMET's database of registered job-seekers, and BMET clears no one outside it [W0027, W0030].

   You'll need: your NID (national ID card); a phone that can receive codes; a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-money account with Tk 200; and your passport details, education and work skills [G0035, B0139, G0001, G0037].

   1. In Google Chrome, open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) [B0139, G0035].
   2. Type your name in English exactly as on your NID (no dots; spaces where the NID has them), your mobile and NID numbers, and your date of birth, division and district as on the NID [G0035, B0139]. Type the captcha numbers, accept the terms and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [G0035].
   3. Type the code (OTP) sent to your phone, press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue) [B0139].
   4. Fill in the form, then press "পেমেন্ট করতে এগিয়ে যান" (Proceed to payment), "পেমেন্ট করুন" (Pay), Mobile Banking and your service; enter your account number, the code and your PIN to pay the Tk 200 government fee [B0139, G0001].
   5. Download and print your registration card, which carries your BMET number; you can download it again at [oep.gov.bd/employee-card](https://www.oep.gov.bd/employee-card) with that number, without logging in [B0139, G0036, G0038].
   6. Take 2 printed copies of the registration slip to give your fingerprints [G0001] at any DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office), TTC (Technical Training Centre) or IMT (Institute of Marine Technology) [D0064]. DEMO addresses, phones and emails: [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [G0047]. **Need to know:** whether fingerprinting costs anything, the hours, and what you get back.

   **Check it is real:** register at employee.oep.gov.bd, the address BMET's district office gives [G0001]; use no middlemen and pay fees only through the proper channel, against a receipt [B0042]. **If it fails:** if "Verify" shows an error, check your name, NID number and date of birth match the NID exactly [B0139]; otherwise call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [G0036, B0144].

2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET's TTCs teach 55 trades [B0037]; every TTC, with its principal's name, address, phone and email, is listed at [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6) [G0048, G0049]. Each TTC posts admission notices with courses, minimum education, dates and fees; the Bangladesh-Korea TTC (BKTTC) in Dhaka, for example, posts them at bkttc.dhaka.gov.bd [G0002, G0003]. BKTTC's 3- or 6-month courses (360 or 720 hours), in trades such as welding and plumbing, end with an NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) skills certificate [G0006, G0005].

   You'll need: your OEP registration; the course's minimum education (at BKTTC, JSC, the Junior School Certificate, for welding); and a current, clear photo in your OEP profile [G0002, G0005, B0140].

   1. Log in at [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) with your mobile number and the code sent to it [B0140].
   2. Open the training portal (ট্রেনিং পোর্টাল), then Training, then Trade/Skills; choose your division, district and centre and press Search [B0140, G0002].
   3. Pick the trade, press Apply, check your photo matches your face now, then press Continue, "Process to Enroll" and Confirm; "My Course" shows your applications [B0140].
   4. Sit the admission test on the date in the notice; BKTTC posts results on its notice board and website and sends them by SMS [G0003].
   5. Pay the fee against a money receipt (BKTTC: Tk 748 for 3 months, Tk 1,484 for 6 months); courses run by funded projects are free [G0003, B0037]. At BKTTC, regular trainees with at least 80% attendance get Tk 150 a day for transport and food when funds are allocated [G0004].

   **Scams:** if anyone offers a TTC place or exam pass for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement officer [B0010]. Complain about a TTC to its principal (phone and email on BMET's TTC list above) [G0049], then to BMET's Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 [B0041]. The government's migrant call centre, 16135, takes calls on any migration problem [B0083].

   **Construction jobs:** Malaysia requires foreign construction workers to hold a construction skills certificate (SKKP) from their own country, checked by CIDB (Malaysia's Construction Industry Development Board), and to finish an online safety and health course (eSICW) [G0007]. The CIDB skill test runs at Khulna TTC (Teligati, Khulna; 041-2870047 or 01716966627; ttckhu@gmail.com) and is to start in Dhaka, Chattogram and Rajshahi [B0001, G0049]. **Need to know:** whether the test still runs at Khulna TTC, how to register, the fee, and the certificate you get.

3. **Apply for government (BOESL) jobs.** BOESL posts Malaysia job circulars on [boesl.gov.bd](https://www.boesl.gov.bd/) [Z0003]. Its portal, brms.boesl.gov.bd, has been closed since 11 October 2026 for a technical fault; follow its notice board and [facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd](https://www.facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd/) [W0007, G0059]. Each circular sets its own rules; the example below is its 5 November 2026 zero-cost circular for DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia), for men, at RM1,700 basic pay a month [W0001].

   You'll need: a passport valid for at least 18 to 24 months; your OEP registration; your education and experience details; and, for the interview, 4 colour passport photos on white, colour copies of every passport you have held, original education and experience certificates, your NID, and a CV showing computer skills (MS Excel), numeracy, any driving licence and any overseas or warehouse experience [W0003, G0031, W0006].

   1. Check the circular's posts, age limit (22 to 35 in the example), education, skills and window [W0003, Z0033, W0002].
   2. Apply online through the circular's link (in the example, [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration)) with your passport number, education and experience, for one post only, within the window; incomplete or wrong applications are rejected [W0001, G0031, W0002].
   3. A software lottery picks a shortlist, which BOESL posts on its website; check your name there [W0004].
   4. If shortlisted, bring the documents above to the interview, where the employer's representative tests you and chooses [W0004, W0006]; a driving licence and work experience count in your favour [G0033].

   In the example, workers pay nothing at any stage [W0005]. Even selection does not guarantee a job [G0032]. BOESL's places are few: only 1,381 went through it at no cost from October 2022 to May 2024 [A0059, Z0084], and UN experts said thousands recruited through it paid over five times the official fees [YB0015, A0060]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents, never takes cash and takes money only after final selection, by bank pay order; gangs use its name to sell Malaysia jobs [W0068]. Office: Probashi Kallyan Bhaban (5th floor), 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka; 02-58311838, 02-48319125, 02-48317515 or 01765411653; info@boesl.gov.bd [W0006, W0068, G0034]. **Need to know:** which BOESL Malaysia jobs charge the worker, and how much [G0010]; whether all 10,000 zero-cost places go through BOESL circulars [G0009].

4. **Or go through a private agency.** Only agencies on Malaysia's list can recruit: open [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/#recruitment-agencies), choose Recruitment Agencies, then Bangladesh [W0008, Z0144].

   You'll need: the agency's name and RL number (the recruiting licence number every licensed Bangladeshi agency has) [X0001].

   1. Find the agency on the FWCMS list [W0008].
   2. On BMET's list, [oep.gov.bd/agencies](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies), check the same RL number shows status Active (not Expired, Cancelled or Suspended) and an unexpired validity date; note its registered address and office phone [X0001, X0002, Z0035].
   3. Go to the agency at that address, not to a middleman's office [X0001, B0042].
   4. Check the demand letter, contract, job details, salary and migration cost [B0002] against the permission letter (item 5).

   **What happens in practice:** being on both lists does not stop overcharging (Background, What workers pay) [W0072, A0001]. Some companies bring in more workers than they have work for, or promise jobs that don't exist, and supply them to other companies, hiding it with fake papers [A0071, D0046, D0047]; in 2023 service employers brought in 142,204 foreign workers against 20,000 needed, some on quotas obtained with fake documents [YB0016]. Some arrived with valid work permits to find no job (Step 7, item 1) [A0095, A0014]; many left without seeing a contract [A0082]. **Need to know:** which agencies place Bangladeshis in the promised job at a fair cost.

   **If a relative in Malaysia offers a job or a "visa".** A Malaysian job reaches a Bangladeshi only through an employer's approved quota and an agency on Malaysia's list [W0041, Z0145, W0008]; check the offer as in items 4 to 6, whoever makes it. **Need to know:** how often relatives abroad resell calling visas, and what happens to the workers they send.

5. **Find and check the permission letter for your job.** For each Malaysia job order the Ministry or BMET issues a permission letter naming the agency and its RL number, the employer, the jobs, salary, age range, hours, food, housing, airfare, contract length and the most the agency may take from each worker [W0026, W0028, W0029, W0033, W0038].

   You'll need: the agency's RL number, and the employer's name and salary the agency gave you [W0026].

   1. Open [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) and search "Malaysia" (or মালয়েশিয়া) [W0026].
   2. Open the entry with your agency's RL number; titles give the letter number and RL number, like "DN25-225 RL1274" [W0026].
   3. Compare its employer, salary and maximum fee with what the agency told you [W0029, W0033, W0038]. Its English "Recruitment Permission Letter" page shows a permission ID, the block visa number (Malaysia's approval reference for the group of visas), the visa issue date and the employer's name and city; check they match your visa and demand letter [W0032, Z0005].
   4. Take the demand letter or visa (if stamped, with a copy of the passport page) to your DEMO or BMET's head office, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [B0005, B0032, B0033, G0026]. If it is fake, complain as under "If an agency or broker takes your money" below.

   As of 7 November 2026 BMET's list had no Malaysia permission letter from the 2026 reopening; the newest was dated 27 February 2025 [G0030]. **Need to know:** whether BMET will post them.

6. **Check the employer exists** through SSM e-Info, the online shop of Malaysia's Companies Commission (SSM, Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia) [W0067, G0053].

   You'll need: the employer's full name or Malaysian registration number from the demand letter; an email address; and a credit or debit card or Malaysian online banking (FPX) [G0055, G0056].

   1. Register and log in at [ssm-einfo.my](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/) [G0055].
   2. Top up your account; card and FPX payments clear instantly [G0056].
   3. Search the name or number and buy the Company Profile, RM15.40 [G0055, W0067].
   4. Save the PDF at once; it stays in Transaction History for only 7 days [G0057].
   5. Check the status, registered and business addresses and nature of business [G0053]; "Existing" means not being wound up, struck off or dissolved [G0054]. Compare name and address with the demand letter.

   **If it fails:** if you can't get the document, call the e-Info call centre, 1300 30 4636 [G0058]. **Need to know:** what to do if the company can't be found or doesn't match the demand letter.

**Before paying:**

- **Know what you should pay.** The Ministry's last Malaysia cap, Tk 78,990, covered everything the worker pays in Bangladesh (passport, medical, registration, welfare fee, insurance, smart card, pre-departure training, clothing, agency service charge and other costs); the employer paid the security deposit, pass, insurance and air tickets [R0008, R0009]. Your job's permission letter states its maximum, in some letters nothing [W0026, W0034]. The agency must display in writing at its office the migration cost fixed for your destination and the monthly wage [YB0003]; compare both with your permission letter (item 5).
- **Pay only by cheque, bank draft or pay order into the agency's own bank account, and keep every receipt** [W0028, B0003]. Agencies and middlemen have started collecting passports from people who want to go [G0011]; on 10 September 2026 a BMET mobile court sealed Triple A Overseas (RL-2630), fined it Tk 50,000 and seized passports it had collected with promises of Malaysian visas [A0043]. The Ministry has warned against paying anything before the Malaysia process is finalised, or handing over a passport or money without confirming approval [A0031, Z0161].
- **If a local broker brings you the job.** An agency may use only a sub-agent registered with BMET, and must list its registered sub-agents on its own website [YB0001, YB0002]. A registered sub-agent has an office with a signboard showing his name, registration number and date and the agency's name and licence number, and his BMET registration certificate on display inside [YB0005, YB0006]. The agency and the sub-agent are both liable for what he does, and a worker cheated by a sub-agent is compensated from the Tk 3 lakh security he lodges with the agency [YB0004, YB0007]. **Need to know:** whether BMET, any DEMO or any agency has yet published a list of registered sub-agents [YB0006].
- **Get the contract and a Bangla translation before you go.** The agency must sign a contract with you before BMET clearance and give you a Bangla translation attested by BMET [W0030]; check the contract is attested by the destination country's embassy [B0002]. Keep your copy: a wage claim in Malaysia asks for it [M0113], and some employers swap it on arrival for one the worker can't read [J0070]. **Need to know:** how to check the BMET attestation, and what to do if the agency won't hand over the contract.
- **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years, and decides within 7 working days [B0017]. It lends only once you have a valid visa and does not arrange visas [G0039]. Informal loans are usually paid straight to a third party, leaving no record of what was paid [A0066].

  You'll need: the free form; 3 recent photos; copies of your NID, passport, visa and BMET card (BMET's smart card); the contract if you have it; 3 signed MICR cheque leaves from your own active bank account, with its statement; and a PKB savings account opened with at least Tk 500 [B0014]. Two guarantors: one well-off person (3 signed cheque leaves, a bank statement, proof of income) and one family member, each with a photo, NID and proof of address [B0016]; many applicants can't get the loan for lack of guarantors [G0040]. You and they must live permanently in the branch's area; you must be 18 to 60 with no loan in default [B0013].

  1. Apply at your area's PKB branch (list linked from PKB's loan page on mygov.bd), or online on the [mygov.bd](https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1726556874) migration loan page with "আবেদন করুন" (Apply) [B0019, G0042, G0044]. You can apply only once until the loan is approved or refused [B0019].
  2. Online, you get application and tracking numbers by SMS and can check status with your mobile and tracking numbers [G0043].
  3. Pay 0.5% processing and 0.5% documentation fees plus VAT and Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover; bring 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10, and 3 adhesive stamps of Tk 100 per guarantor [B0018].
  4. PKB sends an SMS when the loan is approved [G0039].

  PKB helpline: 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018]; loans department: DGM, Loans and Advances, 01716-175560, migration@pkb.gov.bd [B0055]. To complain, write to PKB's grievance officer, GM (Admin and Accounts), +88-02-48321195, gm2@pkb.gov.bd (30 working days), then the DMD, dmd@pkb.gov.bd (20), then the MD, md@pkb.gov.bd (60) [B0056]. **Need to know:** whether the BMET card, which comes with emigration clearance, is needed before approval [B0002], and what to do if the loan is refused.

**If an agency or broker takes your money and doesn't send you, or sends you to a different job:**

1. **Complain to BMET.** It takes complaints from people cheated before going, after going or after coming back; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0034].

   You'll need: a copy of your NID or passport; every receipt, bank slip or mobile-money record of what you paid; the contract, visa or work permit if you have them; any letters, messages, audio or video from the agency or broker [G0014, G0023]; and written down, the agency's name and RL number, the name and phone of whoever took the money, dates and amounts, and the employer's name and address [G0020, G0024].

   1. Fill in BMET's complaint form against a recruiting agent, or write an application to BMET's Director General, with your proof [G0020, B0034, G0026]. Give your name, father's or husband's name, address, phone and email, the type of complaint (for example money misappropriation, or not sending workers abroad), everyone affected, the agency's address, and the details above [G0020, G0021, G0024].
   2. Hand it in at BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, and get the acknowledgement receipt with your complaint ID [B0034, G0026, G0022]. The responsible officer is BMET's Director (Employment), 01711111544, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [G0046]; BMET's main number is 02-49357972 [G0026]. **Need to know:** whether the online form at ovijog.bmet.gov.bd, which takes evidence files and shows status, is in service [G0023].
   3. BMET investigates, hears both sides, usually in Dhaka, and recommends a decision to the Ministry, which decides [B0034, B0035, G0015]. If a guilty agency won't pay, BMET can take the money from its security deposit [G0016].

   **What happens in practice:** it usually takes 4 months to a year [A0073]; workers with full documents get more back [A0077]. In 2023 BMET resolved about a third of 2,380 complaints and most victims got back less than half their loss; in August 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 against Tk 3.5 to 5 lakh paid [A0074, A0075, A0076]. Workers who paid middlemen with no written agreement have only their testimony, passport and BMET card to show [A0079]. A complaint proved against a registered sub-agent can cost him his registration [YB0008].

2. **If BMET does not settle it,** go up the ladder.

   You'll need: your complaint ID and copies of everything you gave BMET [G0022, B0034].

   1. Write to BMET's Additional Director General (Employment), adgemp@bmet.gov.bd, 02-48321949 or 01720105492 [G0046].
   2. Then write to the Ministry's Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd, who has 1 month [B0041].
   3. Then file on the Cabinet Division's Grievance Redress System at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), which has 3 months [B0041].

3. **Or complain straight to the Ministry.** It is free; action starts within 3 to 5 working days, both sides are heard, and it is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].

   You'll need: an application, documents proving the complaint, your full address, and the agency's RL number and address [B0044].

   1. Send them to the Ministry's Enforcement-2 branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255, or to the Ministry at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Old Elephant Road, Dhaka-1000 [B0044, G0045, G0025].
   2. The branch also holds mobile courts (on-the-spot courts under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013) on its own initiative, free, within 7 working days, giving immediate remedies to people cheated before or after going [B0047, G0045].
   3. For advice, call the 24-hour migrant call centre, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

4. **Courts and NGOs.** You can go to court alongside a BMET complaint, but many workers can't keep a case going without money or legal aid [G0017]; with NGO legal help, some have sued agents or brokers under the 2013 Act [A0080], which since a 2024 amendment also covers sub-agents and other intermediaries [A0093].

   You'll need: the same papers as for the BMET complaint [G0014].

   1. Free legal advice: the government legal aid helpline, 16699 (+8809612316699 from abroad), run by NLASO (National Legal Aid Services Organization), which has a legal aid office in each district [G0050, G0051, G0052].
   2. A village court can order money owed under a contract, receipt or other document repaid, up to Tk 3 lakh [G0018].
   3. Migrant NGOs such as WARBE (Welfare Association for the Rights of Bangladeshi Emigrants) and the Ovibashi Odhikar Forum settle some disputes by village mediation and pass agency cases to BMET [A0080]; WARBE does not take fraud by local sub-agents [G0019].

   **Need to know:** which court and case type to use against an agency or broker, and its cost; where to file at the village court and its fee; how to contact WARBE and the Ovibashi Odhikar Forum.

### Step 2: Medical check in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical)

Every worker must pass a medical check (the "Bio-Medical") at a Bangladesh centre accredited by FWCMS before the employer can apply for the visa in Step 3 [W0009]. A medical done before 31 October 2026 is not accepted for immigration clearance [Z0159].

1. **Get the medical slip from your agency and test at an approved centre.** Your agency makes your MiGRAMS slip; no fee may be charged for it [Z0166, Z0168]. The agency gives the centre your papers and VDR visa papers (Step 3) [Z0162]. **Need to know:** which comes first, the medical or the VDR (FWCMS requires the medical before the VDR application [W0009]; the Ministry requires VDR papers at the medical [Z0162]), whether the centre asks for your passport, and how to check a MiGRAMS slip is real.

   You'll need: the medical slip and papers from your agency, and Tk 8,000, the fee for general workers [Z0162, Z0164, Z0166].

   1. Find a centre at [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/), under Accredited Medical Centres, Bangladesh (name and address of each) [I0023, W0009]. You may use any centre on the list, not only the one the agency names [Z0165].
   2. Pay Tk 8,000, no more, and keep the receipt: a complaint needs evidence [Z0164, Z0163].
   3. If you pass, the employer can apply for your visa [W0009]. If you move to another agency, the first must send your medical report on without delay [Z0166].

   **Check it is real and scams:** brokers and dishonest agencies offer unauthorised medicals to get passports and money, and have charged for medicals for jobs that never came [Z0161, D0022]; BAIRA and its members say that in the last round at least five times as many people did the Malaysia medical as went, and agencies took Tk 11,000 against the fixed Tk 6,500 [D0022, D0021]. Hand over nothing until the centre is confirmed on the FWCMS list, and test only after the agency gives you a MiGRAMS slip for a real job [Z0161, W0009, Z0166]. The list is not enough: Human Diagnostic and Medical Centre, Box Culvert Road, Paltan, Dhaka, was still on it in November 2026 after a mobile court sealed it on 1 October for testing Malaysia-bound workers without approval [D0019, D0017, D0015]; the government says it will check listed centres and close those with irregularities [D0020]. **Need to know:** where to check that an FWCMS-listed centre is also approved by the Ministry. A centre that breaks the Ministry's instructions loses its approval [Z0160].

   **If a centre overcharges, harasses you or tests without approval, or a broker took your passport or money for a medical:**

   1. Email the Ministry at secretary@probashi.gov.bd with the date, place, names of the people or centre, and evidence (receipt, photos, messages) [Z0163, Z0167]. For example: "To the Secretary, Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment. On [date], [centre, address] charged me Tk [amount] for my Malaysia medical, against the fixed Tk 8,000. My agency is [name, RL number]. Receipt attached. Please take action and return the extra money. [Name, phone, NID]." [Z0164, Z0167]
   2. Send the same to the Ministry's enforcement branch (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) with your full address and the agency's RL number and address; free, action within 3 to 5 working days, settled within 30 [B0044, B0045].
   3. If an agency is involved, also complain to the Director General of BMET at BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka, or your DEMO, as in "If an agency or broker takes your money" (Step 1); free, BMET holds a hearing, 30 working days [B0034, G0026, G0047, B0005].
   4. If a broker kept your passport, file an online GD (general diary) at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) (Step 4) [I0045].
   5. Call the 24-hour migrant call centre, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

   The Ministry's mobile courts (on-the-spot courts under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013) give a free remedy within 7 working days [B0047].

### Step 3: The employer gets your visa approval (VDR)

1. **The employer applies; you wait in Bangladesh.** After quota approval and paying the levy, the employer applies online through FWCMS for your VDR (Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference; FWCMS calls it the Calling Visa) with the Home Ministry approval letter, levy receipt, a security deposit or guarantee, and your passport copy and photo; you stay in Bangladesh until it is approved [W0043, W0045, Z0059, W0055]. The deposit, visa fee, pass and levy are the employer's costs, but some deduct the levy from wages, often secretly [W0054, F0082].

   **If the levy is taken from your wages:** complain to the Labour Department (JTK) through the Working for Workers (WFW) app (Android or iOS; confidential, trackable), 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my [L0163, L0165, M0118]; tell the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing (Step 7, item 1) [L0028]; call 16135 [B0083].

2. **Track the approval.** You'll need the employer's company registration number (or an individual employer's Malaysian identity card number) and the application number [W0065]. On IMI's [MyIMMs eServices](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main?pra) choose "Status Permohonan Pembantu Rumah Asing / Pekerja Asing" and enter both: BARU means processing, LULUS approved, BAYAR paid, CETAK visa sticker printed, TOLAK rejected, BATAL cancelled, TANGGUH deferred [W0065]. A complete eVDR (online VDR) should be approved within 5 working days for Peninsular Malaysia, 14 for Sabah, 30 for Sarawak [C0045, Z0036, Z0037].

   **If the VDR is refused:** an agency that fails to send workers gets no further Ministry permissions until it repays them [W0036]; claim your money back through BMET and the Ministry as in Step 2 [B0034, B0044].

3. **The entry visa is issued in Dhaka.** After IMI approves, the High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka issues the VDR [W0064]. Worker visa applications go to its authorised visa application agencies, not to the High Commission itself [W0061, W0062]. The Tk 600 visa fee is the employer's; an RM105 (about Tk 2,500) processing fee and the agency's own charge also apply [W0063, W0054, I0043, Z0007]. **Need to know:** who gives the worker the VDR application number, who pays the processing fee and agency charge, and which agencies handle worker visas; ask the visa section, dhaka@imi.gov.my, +88 02 4108 1918, House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Dhaka, Sunday to Thursday, 8.30 to 4.30 [W0061, B0121].

### Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates

1. **Get an e-passport valid long enough:** at least 18 to 24 months left [W0003]; the work pass can be renewed only while your passport has more than 12 months left [L0047]. E-passports are issued for 5 or 10 years [D0049].

   You'll need: your original NID (compulsory over age 20) and, for some jobs, technical certificates such as a driving licence [D0054, D0056].

   1. On [epassport.gov.bd](https://www.epassport.gov.bd/) choose your present-address district and police station to get your Regional Passport Office, and fill in the form exactly as on your NID; nothing needs attesting [I0032, D0055, D0053]. A Bangladeshi abroad can correct the age on a reissued passport by at most 8 years, on the basis of the birth registration [YB0020]. **Need to know:** whether brokers alter ages or names on passports for Malaysia jobs, and what that does to PLKS renewal and EPF withdrawal.
   2. Pay only through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay (card, bKash, Nagad or Rocket), or at a bank through A-Challan [D0051]. A 48-page passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express) for 5 years; Tk 5,750, Tk 8,050 or Tk 10,350 for 10 years [D0049].
   3. Enrol (photo and fingerprints) at that office with the printed appointment summary and form, your original NID, and the bank slip if you paid at a bank [I0033].
   4. With an online-verified NID there is no police verification; delivery takes up to 15, 7 or 2 working days from enrolment (regular, express, super express) [I0009, D0050].

   **To complain:** passport helpline 16445 (09666716445 from abroad, 24 hours) [I0024]; then the office's grievance redress officer, listed on its website (for example the Dhaka Secretariat office: 01538855234, rpo_secretariat@passport.gov.bd) [I0025]; then [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/) [I0026].

   **If it is lost or stolen:** file a GD quickly at the nearest police station [D0057], or at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or the Online GD app with your NID number, a mobile number (for an SMS code) and a live photo, entering what was lost, the district, the police station, and the time and place [I0034, I0039]. You get a digital GD copy with its number and the investigating officer's details (help: gd@police.gov.bd, 01320001428 on WhatsApp) [I0034]. Re-apply with copies of the old passport and the GD [D0057].

2. **Police clearance only if asked;** it is not on IMI's VDR list or BMET's clearance lists [W0055, W0030, B0029]. **Need to know:** whether Malaysian employers or agencies still ask for one.

   If asked, you'll need a passport valid at least 3 months, a present address that is one of your passport's addresses, and a scan of the passport data page attested by a first-class gazetted officer [B0105, B0106]. Apply at [pcc.police.gov.bd](https://pcc.police.gov.bd/) [B0105]; pay Tk 1,500 by treasury challan, code 1-7301-0001-2681, at any Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank branch, or online by card with a service charge [I0040]. The police verify you at your present address; track it by texting PCC S and your reference number to 26969 [B0107]. Collect it with a copy of your passport or application, or, if you are abroad, through someone with an attested authorisation letter giving their name and NID number [I0042, B0108]. Helpdesk: Sunday to Thursday, 9 am to 5 pm, 01320001824, 01320001825, help.pcc@police.gov.bd [I0041].

### Step 5: Check your visa and pass are real

1. **Agency, job and visa approval:** as in Step 1 (FWCMS list, OEP, BMET permission letter) and Step 3 (MyIMMs) [W0008, X0001, W0026, W0065].
2. **At BMET:** take the visa and a copy of the stamped passport page to BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka (02-49357972), or your DEMO; BMET checks job visas and demand letters free within 3 working days, as BMET's online visa check doesn't cover Malaysia [B0005, B0032, B0033, G0026, G0047].
3. **Your BMET clearance (Step 6):** at [portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check) [B0058].
4. **After arrival:** IMI's [ePass check](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/checkEpassValid?semakan) shows name, nationality, pass type and expiry when you enter the ePass number (like EP141011C23105313148), passport number and nationality; "Pas Tidak Sah" means not valid [W0066]. **Need to know:** where a worker finds their ePass number.

**If any check shows a fake visa, pass, job or clearance:** stop paying and keep your passport [Z0161]. Complain to BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka, or your DEMO, with your passport copy, contract, visa, messages with the agency and proof of payment [B0034, G0014, G0026, G0047], and send the same to the Ministry's enforcement branch (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) [B0044]. Ask the High Commission of Malaysia whether the visa is real (dhaka@imi.gov.my, +88 02 4108 1918) [W0061, B0121]. Report whoever took your money or passport through the online GD [I0045], and call 16135 [B0083].

### Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight

1. **Do the compulsory 3-day pre-departure orientation (PDO)** at a BMET training institute (list with contacts on [BMET's website](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6)): going legally at low cost, avoiding broker fraud, and what to do after arrival [B0036, G0048, G0049, I0003]. You'll need your OEP registration and passport or NID number [B0012, I0007]. Apply on OEP, then download the enrollment card at [training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-enrollment-card](https://training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-enrollment-card) with your passport or NID number and the captcha [B0012, I0007]. Attend all 3 days and pay against a money receipt (Tk 200 at Feni Technical Training Centre, which says to pay no more) [B0036, I0027]. The only real certificate is the one you download at [training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-certificate](https://training.oep.gov.bd/pdo-certificate) with your passport or NID number [I0008].

2. **Check the contract before clearance.** BMET's Malaysia permission letters require the agency to sign a contract with you before clearance and give you a BMET-attested Bangla translation [W0030]. Get it at least 2 days before you go and check the job, employer and address, workplace, length, monthly pay, hours, overtime, leave, airfare, medical care, injury and death compensation, food and housing, and return of the body [I0005]. **If there is no contract or translation, or it differs from your permission letter:** don't go to clearance; complain to BMET and the Ministry as in Step 5, and call 16135 [B0034, B0044, B0083].

3. **BMET clearance (the smart card).** After the visa, the agency applies with, for Malaysia, the demand letter, power of attorney and skill certificates, for workers from BMET's database only; BMET's emigration branch clears within 3 working days [B0026, W0030]. Your costs [R0008]: Tk 5,500 to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the government's welfare board for migrant workers), a one-time Tk 1,000 insurance premium (5 years' cover, Tk 10 lakh), a Tk 250 clearance fee, and source tax of Tk 800 (unskilled) or Tk 1,200 (skilled) on a group visa, paid only through the proper channel against a receipt [B0043, B0008, B0028, B0042]. **Need to know:** whether the Tk 250 fee still applies; the Ministry reportedly abolished the smart card fee in December 2024 [D0059].

   **If you got your visa yourself,** apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram DEMO with an application, the visa copy, any contract, an attested copy of your OEP card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and the original pay order for the welfare fee and insurance [B0029].

   **Check it is real** at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check; airport welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet stop workers with no or a fake smart card [B0058, B0038, B0128].

4. **The ticket.** The employer pays your air fare both ways, and BMET clears you only once that is ensured; an agency representative must fly with you or meet you in Malaysia [W0036, L0141, W0031].

   **Check the booking is real:** agencies have handed workers fake tickets minutes before the flight [I0012]. Enter the six-character PNR (booking reference) from your e-ticket on the airline's site: Biman's Manage Booking, with your last name [I0022]; Malaysia Airlines' [Manage Booking](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/ph/en/plan-trip/booking-and-services.html), with your last name [I0028]; US-Bangla's [Manage Your Booking](https://usbair.com/manage-your-booking), or use the ticket number [I0029]; AirAsia's [Check-in](https://www.airasia.com/check-in/en/gb), with your first name or surname [I0030].

   **Scam:** last-minute demands, such as Tk 50,000, for the ticket, which is the employer's cost [I0013, W0036]. **If asked for ticket money or the ticket is fake:** don't pay; complain to BMET and the Ministry as in Step 5 with the ticket and agency details, and call 16135 [B0034, B0044, B0083].

5. **Before you fly,** carry your passport, contract, visa, BMET clearance, medical report, ticket and receipts [I0004], and the Bangladesh High Commission's contacts: No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur; 03-2604 0946, 48 or 49; labour wing +60 3 2604 0934 [L0032, L0028]. Leave one set of copies at home and carry 3, and have a bank account in your name and one in a family member's [B0004]. Keep money and papers in your cabin bag and the checked bag within 20 kg [I0006]. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].

6. **Register the free MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card)** within 3 days before arrival (the day you submit counts) at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main); workers on a VDR are not exempt [V0058, V0060, N0057, V0065, V0059, V0061]. The form asks your name, passport number and expiry, date and place of birth, nationality, sex, email, mobile, arrival and departure dates, flight number, mode of travel and last port of embarkation [V0061]; you get a PIN, and check the registration under "Check Registration" with your passport number, nationality and PIN [V0062]; show it with your passport at the Immigration counter [V0059]. **Scam:** fake MDAC sites charge for the card (IMI has flagged malaysiacard.com; sponsored search ads lead to others), so use only the IMI address [V0065, N0057, N0030].

### Step 7: After arrival

1. **Wait for your employer at the airport.** Enter only through official entry points with your VDR and visa; the employer must collect you within 6 hours, take you to the hostel and pay your transport to the workplace [W0046, L0041, L0034, M0147]. **If no one comes or there is no job:** call the agency representative who must meet the flight [W0031], then the High Commission's labour wing (+603 2604 0934, ministermalaysia@probashi.gov.bd, or +601 2290 3252, counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd) [L0028], and 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

   **If there is no job.** In one case 161 Bangladeshis promised RM1,500 construction jobs waited seven months without work or pay while the employer held their passports and asked RM6,000 for them [YB0018]. Don't take other work, which ends the pass (What can get you jailed) [K0025]; file at the Labour Office (If you aren't paid, route 3) and get free help (route 4): in that case the Labour Department told the employer to pay wages owed since arrival and either send the workers home or help them find new employers [YB0019]. In December 2023, 171 Bangladeshis were arrested in Kota Tinggi, Johor, when they went to the police to report their agent [YB0017]. **Need to know:** whether a worker whose pass was never issued can report to the police without being arrested.

2. **FOMEMA medical within 30 days.** FOMEMA appoints the clinics for foreign workers' medical checks [Z0008]. The employer must take you to a FOMEMA clinic within 30 days of arrival and pay the fee (RM207 for men, RM217 for women (which includes a pregnancy test), plus 8% sales and service tax) [L0042, M0144, Z0125, Z0126]. It includes a chest X-ray, blood tests and a urine drug test for opiates, methamphetamine and cannabis; results usually come within 7 working days [Z0135, Z0129]. Check yours on IMI's [FOMEMA Application Status](https://eservices.imi.gov.my/myimms/FomemaStatus?lang=en) with your passport number and BANGLADESH [I0001]; the employer checks it on the FOMEMA portal, and neither of you sees the report itself [I0002, D0028].

   **If the employer hasn't taken you within 30 days:** without a FOMEMA fit result there is no work pass (PLKS) [L0043]; complain to the Labour Department (WFW app or 03-8000 8000), tell the labour wing (item 1) and call 16135 [L0163, L0165, M0118, L0028, B0083].

   **If you are found unfit:** no PLKS; employers complain that FOMEMA rejects workers even for treatable conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol [D0027]. You are sent home with a Check-Out Memo (the employer's application for a foreign worker to leave), and the employer pays the fare [L0043, L0071, W0036]. Only the employer or agency can appeal, for a fee, on portal.fomema.my within 2 weeks; the case closes if extra test results don't arrive within 4 weeks [Z0131, Z0132, I0044]. Confirmed tuberculosis, malaria, pregnancy, syphilis, HIV and hepatitis B can't be appealed [Z0133]. Ask the employer in writing to appeal, and tell the labour wing [L0028]. Using a false passport to sit FOMEMA again carries 2 to 10 years' jail (What can get you jailed) [L0089].

3. **PLKS sticker and i-Kad.** The sticker is printed once IMI approves your PLKS and the employer pays (CETAK on MyIMMs) [W0065]. Every PLKS holder gets an i-Kad (foreign worker identity card), free and without applying; the employer collects it from Immigration 7 days after IMI's email [L0126, L0127]. Carry it always and show it when asked [L0128]. If you don't get it, complain to the Labour Department (WFW app or 03-8000 8000), then the labour wing [L0163, L0165, M0118, L0028].

4. **Keep your own passport.** Your employer may not keep your passport; hand it over only for the medical, PLKS, i-Kad and renewal, and get it back [L0149, L0151, M0143]. Holding someone else's passport without lawful authority carries a fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both [K0045, I0031]. **If your employer keeps it:** ask for it back in writing and keep a copy [L0151]; complain to the Labour Department (WFW app, 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my) and tell the labour wing [L0163, L0165, M0118, L0028]; for unpaid wages, see If you aren't paid. **Need to know:** whether a report to the Malaysian police or IMI gets a passport back, and where to make it.

5. **Wages into your own bank account,** monthly by the 7th of the next month; cash only if you ask in writing and the Labour Department approves [L0131, L0132, L0104, L0105]. The employer is expected to open it [L0034]. At Maybank, for example, open it at a branch (the app needs a Malaysian identity card) with your passport and at least RM250, and perhaps your work permit, visa and an employer's letter [F0011, F0012, F0014, F0015].

6. **SIM card.** Register every prepaid SIM yourself, in person, at an operator's service centre or authorised dealer with your original passport and work-permit document [F0001, F0002, F0003, F0004, F0013]. The limit is 2 prepaid SIMs per operator; activation is confirmed within 12 hours [F0005, F0007]. Don't use a SIM that was active before you registered it [F0006]. Don't give documents or codes to anyone offering to "help"; each operator checks free which numbers are in your name [F0009, F0010].

7. **Social security, insurance and the retirement fund.** The employer must register you with PERKESO (SOCSO, Malaysia's social security organisation) for the Employment Injury Scheme (accidents, work diseases, death at work; employer pays 1.25% of wages) and the Invalidity Scheme (critical illness or death in Malaysia before 60 from any cause; employer 0.5%, you 0.5%) [M0166, M0167, M0168, M0170, M0171]. You get a 12-digit social security number; SOCSO: 1-300-22-8000 [M0184, M0179]. Not registering or paying is an offence for the employer [M0174].

   SPIKPA, the compulsory hospital and surgery insurance, costs RM120 a year; the employer buys it with the PLKS application and may deduct it from wages with Labour Department permission, and cover starts once you are registered and it is paid [I0035, F0065, I0036, I0021]. At a government hospital show your valid passport; you pay no deposit and only the part of a bill above the cover limit [I0019, I0020]. An unpaid government hospital bill blocks your PLKS renewal [I0020].

   You and your employer each pay 2% of your wages into the EPF (Employees Provident Fund, the retirement fund); the employer may deduct your share, and domestic helpers are not covered [I0014, I0015, I0016]. EPF registers you automatically; visit any EPF office, no appointment, to update your details and give your thumbprint [I0017].

   **When you leave Malaysia, take out your EPF savings (Leaving Country Withdrawal)** [J0017]: get the Check-Out Memo from Immigration as proof of leaving [J0020, I0037]; prepare form KWSP 9K (AHL) and its checklist, a passport copy and a bank document confirming the account holder, with copies certified by an EPF officer, your High Commission or your employer [J0018, J0019, I0037, J0025]; apply at an EPF office's self-service terminal, as it can't be done online [J0022]. The money goes into an active account in your name at an EPF panel bank [J0023, J0024]. After leaving, post the application to EPF, Jabatan Pengurusan Transaksi, Menara KWSP, No.1 Persiaran Kwasa Utama, Kwasa Damansara, Seksyen U4, 40150 Shah Alam, Selangor [J0026]. EPF: +603-8922 6000 [J0027].

8. **If you came without BMET clearance,** join the Wage Earners' Welfare Board for its insurance and help: apply at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/) with your passport and work permit; the labour wing processes it, for the ringgit equivalent of Tk 6,500, life insurance included [B0057]. Workers with BMET clearance are already covered (check at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check) [B0058].

### Your rights at work

Your terms may be no worse than the Employment Act 1955 (the main labour law in Peninsular Malaysia): a worse term is void and replaced by the Act, and no contract may stop you joining a registered trade union [M0154, M0071, M0072]. The employer or its agent must explain the terms when you are selected, the contract you sign must carry the same terms, and you are entitled to your own copy from the employer named on your work permit [M0155, M0156]. Under the Bangladesh-Malaysia agreement the contract is in Malay, Bangla and English, and the English counts [L0153]. These rights are Peninsular Malaysia's and Labuan's; Sabah and Sarawak have their own Labour Ordinances [M0060]. For any broken right, use the routes under If you aren't paid.

**Hours and overtime.** At most 8 hours a day and 45 a week, no more than 5 hours in a row without a 30-minute break, and never over 12 hours a day except in emergencies [M0003, M0004, L0092]. Overtime is only at the employer's request with your agreement, paid at least 1.5 times the hourly rate (for monthly pay: the monthly wage divided by 26, then by your normal daily hours) [L0134, M0001, M0007].

**Rest days and holidays.** One rest day a week, on which you can't be made to work except in continuous shift work or emergencies [L0135, M0010]. Work on it within normal hours pays, on monthly pay, half a day's pay for up to half the normal hours and a full day's for more, on top of the monthly wage, and on daily pay one or two days' wages; beyond normal hours, at least 2 times the hourly rate [M0012, M0011, M0013]. 11 paid public holidays a year; working one earns 2 extra days' wages, and overtime that day at least 3 times the hourly rate [M0014, M0019, M0020]. Absence without consent on the working day before or after a holiday loses that holiday's pay unless you have a reasonable excuse [M0018].

**Leave.** Paid annual leave is 8, 12 or 16 days a year (under 2, 2 to 5, over 5 years' service) [L0109]; paid sick leave 14, 18 or 22 days on the same bands, plus up to 60 days if a doctor certifies hospital care, so 74, 78 or 82 days in all [M0023, M0239]; it needs a doctor's certificate and notice to the employer within 48 hours, or it counts as absence [M0025]. Maternity leave is 98 days [L0112]. A married man gets 7 days' paid paternity leave per birth, up to 5 births, after 12 months' service, if he tells the employer at least 30 days before the expected birth [L0095].

**Pregnancy.** A pregnant worker can't be dismissed except for wilful breach of contract, misconduct or closure of the business [M0240, L0094]; for the work pass, see Bringing your family.

**Pay.** Wages are paid monthly by the 7th of the next month into a bank account in your name, in cash only if you ask in writing and the Labour Department approves; overtime, rest-day and holiday pay by the end of the next wage period [L0131, L0132, M0034, L0104, L0105, M0030]. Your basic wage must meet the minimum wage and equal a local worker's for the same job [L0130, M0152, M0153]. The employer may not dictate where, how or with whom you spend it [M0038]. The minimum is RM1,700 a month or RM8.72 an hour (by the day: RM65.38 on a 6-day week, RM78.46 on 5 days, RM98.08 on 4) [Z0137], for every employer, including for piece, task, trip or commission pay [Z0138, Z0139, Z0140]. The government has agreed to raise it to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, with small and medium businesses selling under RM50 million a year exempted [R0002]. **Need to know:** whether the Minimum Wages Order setting RM2,000 has been made.

**Deductions.** Only what the Employment Act allows [L0107, M0044]. Rent, food or payments to anyone else need your written request and Labour Department approval, given for rent and food only if they benefit you [M0047, M0049]. All deductions together may not exceed half a month's wages [M0050, L0137]. In Sarawak nothing may be deducted for recruitment or repatriation, and foreign workers get local workers' rate [L0170, L0169].

**Housing.** Employer housing, under Act 446 (the Employees' Minimum Standards of Housing, Accommodations and Amenities Act 1990), needs a Labour Department Certificate for Accommodation, men and women live separately, and the person in charge must take ill workers to a clinic or hospital [L0138, M0146, M0193, M0201, M0203]. Each worker gets his own bed with a 4-inch mattress, pillow, blanket and lockable cupboard [J0089], at least 3.6 square metres in a bedroom (3 in a dormitory), and no more than 6 share a bathroom and toilet (15 in a dormitory) [J0087, J0088]. On estates and other workplaces outside city, municipal and Federal Territory areas, employer housing must have free piped water and electricity, and no rent may be charged [M0207, M0208, M0209]. In practice many employers still house workers in hostels that break Act 446, and some leave workers to buy their own beds and kitchen equipment [F0060, F0062]. Elsewhere rent can be taken from wages only within limits set by regulations [M0197, M0198]; the most is RM100 a month per worker [J0090]. If the job ends with under 4 weeks' notice, you may stay up to 4 weeks [M0205]. The Labour Department can inspect at any time without notice [M0210, M0212]. **Need to know:** whether the RM100 limit has changed since 2020.

**Your passport.** The employer may not keep it (Step 7, item 4) [L0149, K0045]; if it is kept, you can also complain to SUHAKAM (the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia, If you aren't paid, route 4) [J0094].

**Forced labour.** An employer who threatens, deceives or forces you to work and stops you leaving can be fined up to RM100,000, jailed up to 2 years, or both [L0100, L0101]. Call the 24-hour emergency line of Tenaganita (a Malaysian NGO for migrant workers), the labour wing or SUHAKAM (If you aren't paid) [J0010, L0021, J0094].

**Sexual harassment.** Report it to the Labour Department's Director General, who can direct the employer to inquire [M0078]. A woman worker or a close relative in Bangladesh can also apply free to the Ministry, aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, with passport, smart card and residence-permit copies, the agency's details and the accused person's contacts; it acts within 7 working days [B0049]. **Need to know:** how a worker files a complaint with the Director General.

**Ending the job.** Under the agreement contract, either side gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages [L0144]. Not paying wages counts as the employer breaking the contract; either side may end it without notice for a wilful breach by the other, and you may leave at once if you or your dependants face immediate violence or disease you didn't agree to risk [M0041, M0053, M0055]. Being absent more than 2 working days in a row without leave or a reasonable excuse told to the employer counts as you breaking it [M0042]. Dismissal for misconduct needs an inquiry first; suspension during it lasts at most 2 weeks on at least half pay [M0054]. In a redundancy, foreign workers in a similar job go before any local worker [M0057]. After 12 months, if the employer ends the job (except for misconduct), you get at least 10 days' wages per year of service under 2 years, 15 days for 2 to 5 years, 20 days for 5 or more, pro rata [J0053, M0056].

**Savings and pension.** The employer registers you with EPF (the retirement fund) and pays 2% of your wage, with 2% deducted from you, by the 15th of the next month; you withdraw it all when you leave for good (Leaving at the end of the job) [J0011, J0013, J0014, J0017]. PERKESO (social security, see Step 7) covers work accidents, and critical illness or death in Malaysia before 60 from any cause, and pays invalidity and survivors' pensions (If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job) [M0168, M0172]. Bangladesh's Probash pension is open to Bangladeshis aged 18 to 50 working abroad [J0071, J0072]:

1. You'll need: a bank account, mobile number, email and a foreign-currency card [J0078, J0086, J0073, J0099]. You can name a nominee, who gets the money if you die [J0101, J0077].
2. Register yourself, in your own name, at [upension.gov.bd](https://www.upension.gov.bd/) under Probash, with no third person; your ID, rate and payment dates come by SMS and email, and any Sonali Bank branch is the front office [J0102, J0072, J0085, J0086, J0100].
3. Pay Tk 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500 or 10,000 a month, 3, 9 or 12 months at a time, by card to Sonali Bank; 2.5% is added from abroad [J0081, J0076, J0073, J0074].
4. What you get: after 10 years of unbroken payments, a monthly pension into your bank from age 60 without applying (Tk 1,000 a month for 20 years gives about Tk 4,927 a month); if you die, all you paid plus profit goes to your nominee [J0075, J0080, J0077]. On returning home you can switch to the Progoti or Surokkha scheme [J0079].

**Need to know:** the full list of details the form asks for, whether registering costs anything, how to pay without a foreign-currency card, and what to do if registration or payment fails.

### How long you can stay

**12 months at a time, up to 10 years.** The PLKS lasts 12 months [L0044]. Only the employer renews it, from 3 months before expiry, and pays any late penalty [L0079, L0139]. Renewal needs a passport valid over 12 months, a FOMEMA medical every year, and SPIKPA hospital insurance (except in plantations) [L0047, Z0130, M0220]. A new 48-page, 5-year e-passport from the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur costs a worker RM172 (RM140 fee plus a RM32 ESKL charge for the appointment, enrolment and delivery) [Z0020]; Bangladesh missions charge workers USD 50 for 10 years (express USD 45 for 5 years, USD 75 for 10) [D0052]. A renewal filed after expiry counts as overstay [L0045]. The limit is 10 years in total, or 3 for workers registered under the 6P legalisation programme [L0050, M0229, M0157].

**If the employer doesn't renew,** you risk arrest [K0015]. Check your pass on Immigration's ePass check (Step 5) [W0066]; if it is near expiry and unrenewed, email the labour wing and contact Tenaganita (If you aren't paid) [L0021, J0007]. **Need to know:** whether the Labour Department takes a complaint about a pass that isn't renewed, and whether Bangladeshi workers' renewals are still done only at Immigration Putrajaya [L0174] now that work-pass applications go through FWCMS [Z0002].

**No family, marriage or permanent residence.** PLKS holders can't bring family, may not marry a Malaysian or another foreigner while on the pass, can't apply for permanent residence, and must go home when the pass ends [L0053, L0056, E0138, E0139, J0055, J0056].

### What can get you jailed

**No valid pass or passport.** Entering illegally, or failing to show a valid passport and pass when asked: fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, and up to 6 strokes of the cane, or a compound (out-of-court fine) up to RM3,000 [L0083, L0084]. Carry your i-Kad at all times [L0128]. Overstaying an expired or cancelled pass: fine of at least RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, or a compound up to RM3,000 [K0015, R0004, L0086]. Breaking a pass condition, such as working for another employer, ends the pass at once [K0025, L0055]; PLKS holders may not work as frontliners (customer-facing staff) [L0054].

**After arrest.** Someone whose pass has expired or been cancelled is removed whether or not prosecuted, held as long as removal takes, with no appeal [K0018, K0020, K0019]. Detainees in Immigration depots may contact family by phone, letter or visit with the commander's approval [L0181, F0055]. On entering a depot your biometrics are taken and you are blacklisted [H0015]. Depots have held more people than their capacity (22,045 against 21,530), 465 detainees died in them in 2021 to 2025, and former detainees describe medical care denied or delayed [F0056, F0054, F0092].

1. Visitors first need an Immigration permission letter, from the Depot and Deportation Management Division at Immigration headquarters, Putrajaya, for Klang Valley (the Kuala Lumpur area), or the state Immigration Enforcement Division elsewhere; give the detainee's body or police report number [L0179, L0180]. **Need to know:** whether the letter costs anything and how long it takes.
2. Call the High Commission's consular line, +603 2604 0949, or email the labour wing; its officials visit camps [B0118, L0021, H0023].
3. Going home needs only an air ticket if the passport is valid, and the camp arranges the flight and the trip to the airport; otherwise the camp gets a free travel permit from the High Commission [L0017, L0018].

**False papers.** Making, forging or using a forged visa or pass: fine of at least RM30,000, 5 to 10 years' jail, up to 6 strokes [L0088]. A false passport: RM15,000 to RM100,000, 2 to 10 years, up to 6 strokes [L0089]. Letting someone else hold your passport, intending or knowing it may be used by another person, is also an offence [K0047].

**Drugs.** Trafficking means death or life imprisonment, plus at least 12 strokes if not sentenced to death [J0052, K0041, L0155]. Never carry a packet whose contents you don't know [B0006].

**Banned goods.** Obscene pictures or films on any media, daggers, flick knives and cloth printed with Quran verses may not be brought in; declare anything dutiable and expect your bags to be opened [L0158, L0159, L0160, L0156].

### If you aren't paid

**First collect proof.** Photograph your contract, payslips, attendance records, passport and pass, and note the employer's name and address [M0113, J0046]. Find the company's registered name and address on [SSM e-Info](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/), the Companies Commission's search, by name or registration number; a profile costs RM15.40 [M0113, W0067]. Without a written contract you are still presumed an employee if the other person controls your work or hours, provides tools, or pays you regularly as most of your income [M0111]. If you work for a subcontractor, the main contractor is also liable for up to 3 months' unpaid wages if you complain within 90 days of the due date [M0100]. If the employer seems about to run off to avoid paying, complain to a Magistrate, who can require a bond or order arrest [M0110]; **Need to know:** how a worker reaches a Magistrate. Use the routes below in order; several at once is fine.

**1. Working for Workers (WFW) app**, the Ministry of Human Resources' complaint channel [L0163, L0165].

You'll need: a smartphone; your name, mobile, passport number, gender and nationality; the company's name, phone and workplace address (state, district and postcode) [J0003, J0004].

1. Install "WFW" from Google Play or the App Store (official page: [workforworkers.com.my](https://www.workforworkers.com.my/sapn-portal/index)) and register [L0167, J0092, J0003].
2. Choose the complaint type (unpaid, late or below-minimum wages, no overtime pay, unlawful deductions, housing) and describe what happened [J0005, L0163].
3. What you get back: the complaint goes to the labour authorities, your identity is kept confidential, and you track it in the app; no Labour Office visit is needed [L0165, L0166, J0093]. At launch the ministry said complaints would be dealt with within 3 to 7 days [J0006].
4. Reality check: claims without payslips are hard to prove; complainers have been warned off, falsely reported absent or had visas cancelled (If your employer reports you absent (lari)) [J0068, L0192, J0066, J0057].

**Need to know:** whether the app costs anything or works in Bangla, and what to do if a complaint gets no response.

**2. The High Commission's labour wing**, No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur [B0118]. You'll need: the details on the route 5 checklist [B0048].

1. Email counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, ss.lab.kl@gmail.com or fsl.bhc.kl@gmail.com, or call +601 2290 3252; a group can send one email [L0021, B0117, F0018].
2. What happens: it calls the employer in, visits the workplace and attends Labour Department hearings, and takes ignored cases to the Ministry of Human Resources [F0019, F0020, L0193].
3. If it doesn't act, apply to the Ministry (route 5), which tells it to [B0048].
4. Reality check: it says it settled over 330 of about 450 complaints since 2022 by mediation [F0022, F0023], recovered RM11.42 million on 2,857 compensation claims in 2025 and RM5.89 million on 1,381 claims in January to June 2026 [F0025, YB0029], and gave about 400 workers consular and legal help in January to June 2026 [H0026]; other workers say complaints to it and the Labour Department brought nothing until a migrant rights group stepped in [A0090, A0091, F0034].

**Need to know:** how long the labour wing takes to reply and whether it charges anything.

**3. Labour Court claim**, the Labour Department's (JTK's) free wage tribunal, with no upper limit [M0232, M0081, M0093].

You'll need: your passport (or a copy), the employer's name and address, payslips, contract or appointment letter, any termination letter, arrival date and witnesses' names [M0113, J0046, M0089].

1. For free advice call 03-8000 8000. Then give a sworn statement to an officer at the nearest Labour Office (Pejabat Tenaga Kerja), or complain by letter or to jtksm@mohr.gov.my [M0118, M0112, M0087, J0036].
2. The officer sets a mention date (a first short hearing), summons the employer and tells you when and where; bring your witnesses [M0114, M0089].
3. Attend every date; an absent employer can lose by default [M0091]. Workers with the same claim can file together through representatives [M0092]. No lawyers, but a trade union can help [J0037].
4. What you get back: a written order to pay, with up to 8% yearly interest, enforced through the courts (for example by seizing property) if unpaid [M0085, M0096, M0116]. Either side can appeal to the High Court within 14 days [M0115].
5. Time: about a month if settled, about 3 months if heard [J0041]. Challenge a misconduct dismissal within 60 days of being told of it [M0083].
6. If your pass ends mid-case: take a Labour Office letter showing the pending case to Immigration's Foreign Workers Division for a Special Pass (one month, extendable; usually granted with at least two extensions; decided within 3 working days; RM100). It doesn't let you work [J0031, J0033, C0046, J0091, L0185, J0034].
7. Reality check: about half of claims settle at the mention and about 85% of those heard are decided for the worker, but 38% are withdrawn or dismissed for non-attendance, usually because the pass expired [J0042, J0043]; employers often stall until the pass runs out, hence step 6 [J0040, J0033]. An order is not money: in one case a tribunal ordered over RM3 million in wages, but the workers got only RM251,000 after the employer withheld pay for months and closed [F0044, F0046]. JTK's complaint pages and hotline are not in Bangla [J0038]. The employer may not stop you going to the Labour Court [M0099].

**Need to know:** the official list of Labour Office addresses, whether a worker can apply for the Special Pass himself, and whether each extension costs RM100.

**In Sabah and Sarawak** complain to the state's own Labour Department, under its Labour Ordinance [YB0013, YB0012].

- **Sarawak.** Complain to the labour office nearest the workplace: in writing or on its Borang Aduan Pekerja form, by post, fax or email; in person; by phone; on SISPAA (the Malaysian government's online complaints system); or on the WFW app [YB0009]. Give your and the employer's name, address and phone, the issues, and your contract and payslips [YB0010]. A Labour Court claim is made in person by sworn statement, with your passport, the employer's name and address, contract, pay details and the amount claimed [YB0012]. Headquarters: Tingkat 13, Bangunan Sultan Iskandar, Jalan Simpang Tiga, 93532 Kuching; 082-242261; jtknsarawak@mohr.gov.my [YB0011].
- **Sabah.** Complain to JTK Sabah, jtksabah.gov.my [YB0013]. **Need to know:** JTK Sabah's office addresses and phone numbers, and whether it takes WFW complaints.

**4. Free help in Malaysia.** Representation matters: unrepresented workers in the 2024 Pengerang mediation settled for about half their RM2.1 million claim [A0088, A0089], while 74 workers helped by Tenaganita won a Labour Office order for RM1.54 million [A0086]. The Welfare Board has no safe house in Malaysia [B0079].

- Tenaganita (an NGO that takes migrant workers' cases): 12 Jalan 6/11, 46000 Petaling Jaya, Selangor, +603 7770 3671 or 3691, Monday to Friday 9:30 to 6:00, general@tenaganita.net; 24-hour emergency lines for abuse, rescue, arrest or detention +6012 335 0512 and +6012 339 5350 [J0007, J0008, J0009, J0010]. NGOs help workers gather the documents a Labour Department claim needs [J0044].

Kuala Lumpur: Unit 3.03, Level 3, Wisma Badan Peguam Malaysia, 2 Leboh Pasar Besar, 50050 Kuala Lumpur, +603 2691 1121 or 2692 1122, lac.kl@malaysianbar.org.my [J0049, J0050]. They advise and help negotiate; representation is rare, criminal only, and may cost money [J0045, J0047].
- SUHAKAM investigates complaints from anyone, including non-citizens, about exploitation or unpaid wages, and recommends action to the authorities [J0094, J0097]. Email aduan@suhakam.org.my, call +603-2612 5635 or 5636, write to 17th Floor, Menara Aras Raya, Jalan Raja Laut, Kuala Lumpur, or use "New Complaint" on suhakam.org.my and keep the Ticket ID [J0095, J0096].
- MTUC (the Malaysian Trades Union Congress) has run a migrant worker centre at 10-5 Jalan USJ 9/5T, Subang Business Centre, 47620 Subang Jaya, Selangor, +603 8024 2953 or +6017 680 9384 [J0098]. **Need to know:** whether it still takes cases.

**5. Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, Bangladesh** (you or your family). You'll need: what happened; proof you are a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID number); the employer's name and address; what you are owed [B0048].

1. Send it free to the Welfare-1 branch: saswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030251 [B0048].

### If you want to change employer

**You can't apply yourself.** PLKS holders may not change employer or sector on their own, and the agreement contract bars it [L0055, W0052, L0146]. Only the employer or its appointed company representative deals with Immigration; using an agent is an offence [J0062, J0061].

**When it is allowed.** Only if the employer is bankrupt, closed, has suffered a disaster such as a fire or economic crisis and can't keep its workers, has been restructured or renamed, or has died [M0123, M0124]; the new employer must be in the same sector and allowed foreign workers [M0126]; your PLKS must have 3 months left [M0130]. Not open in your 10th year or later, to workers legalised through 6P, RTK (Labour Recalibration) or other programmes, or to domestic helpers [M0127, Z0009]. **Need to know:** whether cross-sector moves are now possible: Immigration says they need Home Ministry (KDN) permission [J0063], but they have been reported as allowed [F0077].

**How the employers do it.**

1. Both employers register on ePPAx (eppax.gov.my, the Labour Department's employer system); the old employer applies naming the new one, which uploads its documents within 7 days [M0121, Z0010].
2. The Labour Department investigates and issues a support letter [M0121, M0128].
3. The old employer (or heir) submits the free Ministry of Human Resources form and documents at the foreign workers division, Level 5, Setia Perkasa 3, Kompleks Setia Perkasa, 62546 Putrajaya, 03-8886 6501, Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm, queue numbers 8.30 am to noon only [M0132, M0134, M0142].

**Need to know:** how long a change of employer takes.

### If your employer reports you absent (lari)

**What counts.** Leaving the workplace without telling the employer, intending to escape, or not returning on the agreed date after a trip home [L0057, L0058, M0213].

**What follows.** The employer reports you to Immigration with its ID, your passport copy, the runaway form and a police report, and tells the Labour Department within 14 days [L0059, M0215, L0099, M0066]. You are blacklisted and its security bond is forfeited [L0060, L0061, M0216]. Immigration normally cancels a quitting worker's pass at once, leaving you without a valid pass [J0035]. False reports are filed near the contract's end to avoid the fare home, or after a complaint [J0065, J0066].

**What you can do.**

1. Keep photos of your pass, payslips and attendance records, and details of any WFW or Labour Office complaint [M0113, L0166].
2. If you leave over abuse or unpaid wages, file at the Labour Office and email the labour wing [L0037, L0021]; the pending-case letter usually gets you a Special Pass (If you aren't paid, route 3) [J0033].
3. If you are arrested, family can call Tenaganita's 24-hour line and email the labour wing [J0010, L0021].

**Need to know:** how a worker finds out about a report or blacklisting (Immigration's online check is for citizens only [J0001]), and how to clear a false one.

### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Check-Out Memo** (Memo Periksa Keluar, Immigration's approval to leave for home). The employer applies to Immigration with the form, your original passport and a ticket; you leave on the date and through the exit point on the memo, and the employer is responsible for you until then [L0071, L0072, L0073, L0074]. The Home Minister announced in May 2025 that employers must also be present at the exit point [H0029].
2. **Ticket home.** The employer pays when the contract ends, when it ends the contract early (unless you breached it), when you leave because it broke the contract, or after serious work injury or illness; you pay if you resign or are dismissed for misconduct [L0141, L0142, M0149, L0143, M0150].
3. **What you are owed:** wages by the last day of the job, or within 3 days if you end it without notice [M0032, M0033]; all outstanding wages if sent home early, untaken annual leave (unless dismissed for misconduct), and termination benefits if the employer ended the job after 12 months (Your rights at work) [L0152, M0022, J0053].
4. **Withdraw your EPF before you fly.** You'll need: Form KWSP 9K (AHL) and checklist, a copy of your valid passport (or a High Commission travel permit), the Check-Out Memo, and a bank document for an active account at an EPF panel bank in the passport details EPF holds [J0018, J0019, J0020, J0024, J0028, J0021].
   1. Apply as soon as you have the memo [J0029].
   2. Apply in person at the self-service terminal in an EPF office, with copies certified by an EPF officer, the High Commission or your employer; helpline +603-8922 6000 [J0022, J0025, J0027]. **Need to know:** EPF's official list of offices, whether withdrawal costs anything, and how long payment takes.
   3. You get all your savings in ringgit into that account, so keep it open, or a banker's cheque if payment fails [J0017, J0023, J0029, J0105].
   4. Already left? Use EPF's overseas application: post the forms, certified by a Malaysian high commission or notary, to EPF, Jabatan Pengurusan Transaksi, Menara KWSP, No.1 Persiaran Kwasa Utama, Kwasa Damansara, Seksyen U4, 40150 Shah Alam, Selangor; the money is sent by bank transfer [J0103, J0026, J0025, J0023].
   5. The EPF account then closes; returning workers register again [J0016].
5. **To leave** you need a passport valid 6 months and a valid pass [L0186].

### Going home on leave

The yearly PLKS renewal includes a multiple-entry visa for Bangladeshi workers, with no separate application [H0027, H0028]. Agree your return date with the employer; missing it counts as running away [L0058, M0214]. **Need to know:** how a worker checks the multiple-entry visa was issued.

### Bringing your family

PLKS workers can't bring family [L0053, E0138]. See Family passes for professionals' routes and Having a child in Malaysia for registering a birth [S0114].

**Pregnancy and the work pass.** The yearly FOMEMA medical includes a pregnancy test for women [Z0126, Z0130]. Confirmed pregnancy means unfit, with no appeal, and the worker is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [Z0134, L0043]. **Need to know:** what happens to a worker who becomes pregnant between medicals.

### Scams

**Done to workers.** In Bangladesh, complain free to BMET at ovijog.bmet.gov.bd (name who took the money, upload receipts and papers) or by application to its Director General; it aims to settle within 30 working days [G0023, B0034]. The Ministry's enforcement branch also takes agency complaints: sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255 [B0044]. In Malaysia, email the labour wing [L0021].

- **Social media posts on Malaysia-approved agencies** have been false [W0021]. **Need to know:** where to check officially which agencies may recruit for Malaysia now.
- **The fee video.** Agencies make workers record a video at their Dhaka office saying they paid only the official fee when they paid far more [J0060]; keep your own proof of every payment [G0014].
- **Fake passes and permit "agents".** Rings sell fake e-PLKS (electronic work pass) copies for about RM150 and fake police reports for about RM70 [D0045]; police in Kulim, Kedah, broke a Bangladeshi-run ring selling fake work permits and fake UNHCR cards for RM80 to 250 in 2026 [N0049]. Only your employer deals with your pass; anyone else offering is breaking the law [J0062, J0061, L0079]. Check it yourself (Step 5) [W0066].
- **Sabah jobs.** There is no agreement for recruiting Bangladeshis to Sabah; rings have taken passports and cash for Sabah jobs [N0029, A0102].
- **No job on arrival.** Employers sign a High Commission commitment to put workers to work at once; if broken, email the labour wing [W0059, L0021].

**Tried by workers.** Forged visas, passes and passports carry jail and caning (What can get you jailed), including passports bought to resit a failed FOMEMA medical [L0088, L0089, D0035, N0035].

## Work visa: domestic worker

Not open to Bangladeshis. Immigration's approved source countries for domestic helpers are Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Laos and Nepal [L0194, L0076].

## Student visa (Student Pass)

A Student Pass lets you stay in Malaysia to study full time at an approved institution; it is not a work visa [S0023, S0042, S0013]. You apply through EMGS (Education Malaysia Global Services, a company under Malaysia's Ministry of Higher Education, KPT), for public and private institutions alike [Z0013, S0002, S0060]; the Immigration Department of Malaysia (IMI) gives final approval [S0003].

### Step 1: Check the institution and the course

Check before you pay anyone: agents earn commission from institutions and push the colleges paying most [O0020].

1. **Check the institution may take foreign students.** A private institution needs KPT approval, an accredited programme and the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission to take international students; a public university needs no separate check [S0001]. KPT's Department of Higher Education (JPT) lists private institutions with that permission, updated monthly [O0001, O0042]. On [jpt.mohe.gov.my, Senarai Daftar dan Statistik IPT Swasta](https://jpt.mohe.gov.my/portal/index.php/ms/ipt/ipt-swasta/25-senarai-daftar-dan-statistik-ipt-swasta), click the download icon beside "Senarai Daftar IPTS Kelulusan KDN" [O0001], open "Tahun 2026", then the "AKTIF" (active) file with the highest number (in November 2026, file 7, the list up to 31 July 2026, updated 20 August 2026) [O0040, O0041, O0004]. Search it for the institution's name; the campus address must match the offer letter [O0003]. If the institution is missing, is in the "TIDAK AKTIF" (inactive) file of the same number, or is on the page's list of cancelled approvals ("Senarai IPTS Yang Telah Diluluskan Tindakan Pembatalan Kelulusan Penubuhan"), it can't get you a Student Pass, since IMI needs its KDN approval letter, in force, with every new application [O0041, O0005, O0002, O0006].
2. **Check the course is accredited** on the [Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR)](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr/) of MQA (the Malaysian Qualifications Agency): type the course in "Name of Qualification", choose the level and press Search [S0087]. Only accredited programmes appear, and only for the campus named; a course run anywhere else is not accredited [S0084, S0085]. "No record" can mean provisional accreditation only, never accredited, or failed [S0086].
3. **A language or training centre** needs approval from the Ministry of Education (KPM) and the Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA), plus KDN's permission to take international students [O0043], and its students can't work part time or bring family [S0012, S0010]. To check one, contact KPM's Private Education Division: 03-8884 9536, bps@moe.gov.my, Aras 3, Blok E2, Kompleks E, 62604 Putrajaya; it acknowledges enquiries within 3 working days [O0044, O0045]. **Need to know:** a public list of KPM- or KESUMA-approved centres allowed to take international students, and where to check a KESUMA-approved centre.
4. **Check you qualify:** 12 years of schooling and a recognised exam [S0043], and an English certificate (MUET, the Malaysian University English Test; IELTS; TOEFL iBT or Essentials; PTE Academic; Cambridge English; Linguaskill; OET; or ELS CIEP), without which the application may be delayed and the pass won't cover the whole course [S0050].
5. **Check the offer letter is real.** Agents have given Bangladeshi students fake ones [O0021]. The institution's international office can confirm by email or phone that it is genuine and the course is running [O0017]. **Need to know:** an official EMGS or KPT channel for checking an offer letter, and where to find the international office's genuine contacts.

### Step 2: Apply from Bangladesh

Apply from outside Malaysia [S0002]. You or the institution can create the application [O0063], but only one you create and pay for yourself on the Education Malaysia website can be refunded to you [S0078]. You can track it without an agent [S0080, O0046].

1. **Get and accept an offer letter;** the form can't be submitted without it [S0024, S0047].
2. **You'll need:** a 35 x 45 mm photo on white; your passport's data, visa and observation pages, the passport valid at least 18 months (for a pass covering the whole course, EMGS recommends 12 months beyond the course); the offer letter, all pages, as one PDF; EMGS's Health Declaration Form; your certificates and transcripts; and your English certificate [S0045, S0046, S0048, S0050, O0009]. Keep your passport: agents have held students' passports to demand more money [O0018, O0023].
3. **Open an account and apply** on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/) or the EMGS HUB app [S0024]. Enter the institution and course exactly as in the offer letter, your name exactly as in the two coded lines at the bottom of your passport's data page, and the passport number on the offer letter [O0007, O0008]. EMGS then asks the institution to confirm [O0012].
4. **Pay** through the institution, by telegraphic transfer (bank-to-bank international transfer: converted to US dollars, plus USD 50), or by Visa or MasterCard on the EMGS site (plus 3%) [O0010, O0049, O0047, O0048]. Tuition handed to agents has failed to reach institutions [O0022]. EMGS's [fee calculator](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/student-visa/calculate-application-fees) gives, for a new application at a university or private higher education institution: processing RM1,728; eVAL (electronic Visa Approval Letter, IMI's approval to enter as a student) RM159; medical screening in Malaysia RM340 (RM350 in Sarawak); Student Pass RM60; i-Kad (student identity card) RM50, or RM60.60 couriered; EMGS health insurance RM498 to RM644, or a policy through your institution; visa RM20 [S0060, S0061, S0062, S0063, S0064, V0017]. 8% service tax is added to processing fees [Z0094]. Public-university students may do the medical at their university [Z0095]. If a card payment fails but your bank took the money, email enquiry@emgs.com.my with the bank's confirmation, transaction ID and application number [O0011].
5. **Refunds.** The first year's processing and eVAL fees aren't refunded once invoiced; convenience fees and service tax never are [S0077, O0050, O0051]. Before the institution decides, you get a full refund if you ask within 90 days of paying [O0053], through the online refund form in your account, logged in with the email you applied with; card payments come back by telegraphic transfer [O0052, O0058].
6. **Lodge the RM750 personal bond** (the Bangladeshi rate) with the institution, which signs a bond with Immigration for you; it is returned when you finish or withdraw without breaking immigration rules [S0059, S0049]. The form can be handed in after arrival, for the Student Pass sticker [O0059]. **Need to know:** how and when the institution takes the money, the receipt, and how to claim it back.
7. **If EMGS asks for more documents,** send them within 14 working days of the return date [S0020], or the application closes, and a refund must be asked for within 6 months of closing [O0054, O0055].
8. **Wait.** EMGS checks your academic papers, then IMI checks your passport and security and issues the eVAL [S0003]; EMGS's charter is 14 working days for a complete application [S0026].

**If you're refused,** you can appeal [O0056]; the institution's international office handles the appeal or a new application with corrected documents [O0015]. Ask for any refund within 90 days of EMGS's notice [O0057]. **Need to know:** the appeal form, fee and deadline.

### Step 3: eVAL and visa

The eVAL is valid 6 months; you may not enter before it is issued [S0027, S0028]. Then apply on [MyVISA](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/), from outside Malaysia, for a Single Entry Visa under Visa With Reference (VDR), the category needing an approval letter (the eVAL) [S0004, S0029, S0053, V0007, C0006]. Account and payment work as in Tourist visa, Step 2 [V0021, V0022]; the fee is RM20 [V0017]. **Need to know:** whether MyVISA's RM105 processing charge applies to students.

### Step 4: How to check your application is real

1. **Track it** on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/): Student Visa, Track My Application, enter passport number and nationality, tick the terms box, press Track My Application [S0080, O0060]. IMI's own status page sends Student Pass checks to EMGS [S0081].
2. **At 70%** Immigration has approved your eVAL; download it as a PDF from your EMGS account [O0013, O0014]. If progress stops, ask the institution's international office, which must confirm your application, or EMGS via [educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us](https://educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us) [O0012, O0084].
3. **Need to know:** how to check that an eVAL document itself is genuine.

### Step 5: Arrival and the pass

1. **Register the MDAC** (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) within 3 days before arriving (see Tourist visa, Step 4) [V0058]; long-term pass holders are exempt [V0060].
2. **At Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA),** go to EMGS's 24-hour International Student Arrival Centre (ISAC) before immigration (Terminal 1: Level 3 arrival hall; Terminal 2: Level 3A after the Skybridge); its staff help you through immigration, with priority lanes for students [S0082, S0083, O0061, O0062]. **Need to know:** which documents ISAC asks to see, and whether it charges anything.
3. **Within 7 working days do the medical** at an EMGS panel clinic, listed on EMGS's site and app; without it EMGS can't start your pass [S0055, S0058, S0056]. If you fail, appeal at the same clinic within 3 days of being told [S0030]; if that fails, ask for any refund within 90 days of EMGS's notice [O0057]. **Need to know:** how to book the medical, and the appeal's cost, documents and possible outcomes.
4. **The institution gets your pass sticker** with your original passport, its letter, EMGS's support letter, your stamped personal bond and at least 12 months' Malaysian health insurance [S0017]; IMI's client charter gives 5 working days [C0047].

### Keeping the pass

The pass usually lasts up to a year; renew as early as 3 months before expiry [S0032, S0033], yourself or through the institution, on STARS (EMGS's Student Application Registration System) or the EMGS website [O0063, O0064]. You'll need a 35 x 45 mm photo on white, copies of your passport's data, visa and observation pages, and your certificates and transcripts [O0085]. You need a CGPA (grade point average) of at least 2.0 and 80% attendance, or an explanation letter from the institution [S0051]. Renewal costs RM270 to EMGS (private institution) and RM60 to IMI [S0060, S0007]. **Need to know:** how long a renewal takes. Breaking any condition can get the pass cancelled [S0016].

**Changing course:** the institution may file a Variation application once, within 18 months after your first semester, if you finished at least one semester at bachelor's level or above; otherwise it ends your pass with a Check-Out Memo and you leave Malaysia before applying again [S0034, S0036]. With an eVAL but no pass yet, you must leave to change course [S0035].

### Can students work?

Only university and private higher education students may work, not language or training centre students [S0012]: in semester breaks or holidays over 7 days, at most 20 hours a week, in restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels or on campus [S0013, S0039]; never as a cashier, front desk worker, hotel singer, masseur, musician or guest relations officer, or student recruiter [S0014, S0040]. Students working outside these rules are deported, sometimes fined or jailed [O0019]. There is no agreement giving Bangladeshi students graduate passes to work; Malaysia's Higher Education Minister called that claim false [N0034].

To get permission first: you'll need the job offer letter, the institution's supporting letter with the break dates, your results and attendance report, copies of your data page and Student Pass, and the employer's Labour Department (JTK) approval to employ a foreigner under section 60K of the Employment Act 1955 [S0015]. Go in person with an institution representative to IMI's Student Pass Unit at headquarters or the state Immigration office, which decides after checking the documents [S0041, S0015]. **Need to know:** the fee and how long a decision takes.

### Family

Only master's and PhD students may bring dependants (spouse, children under 18, disabled children of any age, parents and parents-in-law); a Bangladeshi bachelor's student can't, and dependants may not work [S0008, S0009, S0038, S0011]. The institution applies on STARS [S0021, O0064]. You'll need: Forms IMM.12 and IMM.38; the institution's letters requesting the pass and confirming your Student Pass; a copy of every page of the dependant's passport, cover included (valid 18 months or more) and of your data page and pass; a 3.5 x 5.0 cm photo on white; 12 months' health insurance; a stamped personal bond (new applications); proof of support, such as 3 months' bank statements or a scholarship letter; and the marriage or birth certificate [O0065, O0066]. Bring the originals; incomplete applications aren't processed [O0069]. Bangla documents need a certified translation; a dependant already in Malaysia needs relationship documents certified by the Bangladesh High Commission [O0068, O0067]. EMGS charges RM250 per dependant (RM140 to renew), each dependant buys insurance, and IMI charges RM90 for the Long-Term Social Visit Pass [S0067, S0007]. **Need to know:** how long a dependant's application takes and how the result is shown. That pass has a category for close family of a foreign student [C0019]; **Need to know:** whether it covers a bachelor's student's family. For a child born in Malaysia, see Having a child in Malaysia [S0114].

### School pupils

IMI gives Student Passes to foreign pre-school and school children [C0024] with a place at a government or government-aided school under KPM, or at an international, expatriate, private or religious school or Chinese independent secondary school registered with KPM's Private Education Division; an international school also needs KDN's permission [Z0110, Z0111]. The school's representative, a parent or a legal guardian applies, new or renewal, at IMI's Student Pass Unit at headquarters, the state Visa, Pass and Permit Division, or a branch offering Student Passes, by the school's address [Z0112, Z0113]. You'll need: Forms IMM.14 and IMM.38 [Z0114]; a copy of every page of the child's passport, cover included (renewal: data page and current pass), valid 18 months or more [Z0115, Z0124]; a 3.5 x 5.0 cm photo on white [Z0116]; 12 months' health insurance [Z0118]; a stamped personal bond (new) [Z0117]; for a government or aided school, the state or district education office's approval letter [Z0119]; and the originals [Z0120]. It costs RM60 plus the visa fee [S0007]; IMI's client charter gives 5 working days [C0047]. The child's parents, legal guardian or adoptive parents, and siblings under 7 may get a Long-Term Social Visit Pass as companions, at RM90, with no work or business [Z0121, Z0122, Z0123, S0007]. **Need to know:** the extra documents for international and private schools, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Scams

**Done to students.** Besides commission-driven college choices, fake offer letters, lost tuition and held passports (Steps 1 and 2) [O0020, O0021, O0022, O0018, O0023], agents claim a student visa lets you work freely; it doesn't [S0135, S0013]. Agents have charged families Tk 6 to 8 lakh or more for admission and a visa, steering students to barely functioning colleges [S0134]; agencies have left EMGS applications stuck for months and, after arrival, moved students from the promised university into a Bangladeshi-owned English language centre, whose students can't work [O0025, O0026].

**Tried by applicants.** Getting a pass by a false statement, or using someone else's, carries a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030].

**If an agent cheated you or holds your passport,** in this order:
1. **Bangladesh police.** Register on [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or the Online GD app with your NID, a mobile number (for a one-time code) and a live photo; pick the district and station, describe what happened, when and where, and attach receipts and messages [I0034, I0038]. A GD (General Diary) comes back as a digital copy with its number and the investigating officer's details; for a case, take the printout or complaint code to that station [O0071, O0070]. Or call the CID (Criminal Investigation Department) hotline, 01320-019999, or control room, 01320-019998, which wants to hear of anyone holding passports or demanding unusual visa sums [O0072, O0033].
2. **The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's Complaint Resolution Cell,** minister.expat@gmail.com [R0012]. Sample: "My son [name, passport no.] paid [agency, address] Tk [amount] on [dates] for admission to [institution] in Malaysia. The fees were not paid and the agency holds his passport. Receipts attached. Please help recover the passport and money." **Need to know:** whether the Ministry acts on complaints about education agents, which hold no recruiting licence.
3. **In Malaysia,** the Bangladesh High Commission's consular wing: consular.kualalumpur@gmail.com, +603 2604 0946, mission.kualalumpur@mofa.gov.bd, No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur [L0031, L0032]. In an emergency call 999 (free) for the Royal Malaysia Police [O0073, O0074]. **Need to know:** whether the High Commission helps students recover passports held by agents.
4. **Against the institution,** KPT's SiSPAA complaints system, [mohe.spab.gov.my](https://mohe.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do) [O0028, O0082]: register ("Daftar Baharu"), log in, choose "Aduan/Maklum Balas Baharu"; "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" shows progress [O0079, O0083]. Give full name, passport number, phone and email, and attach the offer letter and receipts, or KPT may reject it; it doesn't take private money disputes with agents [O0080, O0081]. **Need to know:** how long KPT takes to reply.
5. **Free legal help:** OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a Bangladeshi migrants' organisation) advises and files cases for migrant workers, families and trafficking survivors: helpline 01842773300, 4th Floor, 466 Sawdagar Garden, Dania Post Office Road, Dhaka 1236 [O0076, O0077, O0078]. **Need to know:** whether OKUP takes the cases of cheated students.

## Tourist visa (social visit)

Bangladeshis need an eVISA to visit for tourism, family or short business, and may stay up to 30 days [V0001, V0003]. Ordinary visits get a single-entry visa; multiple-entry is only for business, medical treatment, Fly & Cruise and wedding tourism [V0009]. It never allows work or study (see Don't work) [V0046].

### Step 1: Prepare

You'll need: a passport valid over 6 months from travel, with at least 3 blank pages [V0026, V0032]; a studio photo, 35 x 50 mm, white background, full face, under 6 months old, no selfies or scans [V0033, V0034]; scans of the data page and the page with your previous passport number [V0026]; a confirmed return ticket [V0026, V0050]; proof of paid accommodation, or, staying with friends or relatives, a cover letter to the immigration officer with your details, the address, and the host's name, Malaysian identity card number and phone [V0026, V0029]; proof of money (cash, card or e-money backed by 3 months' bank statements) [V0027]; for a child, the birth certificate [O0039]; and a credit card, or Alipay or WeChat Pay, to pay [O0037]. Merge several files for one upload field into one [V0031].

### Step 2: Apply on MyVISA

1. **Use only [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/),** IMI's only official eVISA site; you can't apply from inside Malaysia, and third-party applications can't be verified [V0005, V0006, V0007]. If an agent applies, only the agent sees the status, and IMI takes no responsibility [O0038]; agents in Bangladesh charge Tk 5,000 to 8,000 for an eVISA you can apply for yourself [N0056]. MyVISA has shut for weeks at a time (from 10 February 2026, and again in July 2026), suspending all applications and approvals until a notice says it is back [N0003, N0005, N0006].
2. **Open an account:** under Individual, Sign Up, fill in the form and CAPTCHA (picture code); change the emailed temporary password at first login [V0021]. Log in with email, the emailed 6-digit code and password [V0022]. One account holds 5 applications, for up to 5 people [V0023].
3. **Apply on a weekday, 9 am to 5 pm Bangladesh time** (the only hours open to Bangladesh) [V0025], at least 14 days before travel [V0013]. Write your name exactly as in your passport; with no surname, leave Surname blank or put the full name in Given Name [V0035]. Unpaid drafts expire after 7 working days [V0024].
4. **Pay** RM20 visa fee, RM105 processing and 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) convenience charge; nothing is refunded, even on refusal [V0016, V0017, V0019]. Visa agencies add their own fee [V0018].
5. **Watch the status** [V0040]. "Document Submission or Information Update Required": upload what is asked. "Interview Requested": confirm and attend, online or in person as the High Commission sets. No response in 5 days means automatic rejection [V0040, O0034, V0041]. A decision takes 2 to 7 working days, restarting after any re-upload [V0012, O0035].
6. **Download and print the eVISA** (Actions, Download eVISA) [V0037]. It allows one journey within 6 months of issue, whatever the travel date in that window [R0005, R0006]; it cancels any earlier visa still active [V0045]. A wrong detail from your own mistake means applying and paying again [O0036].
7. **If refused,** there is no appeal, and no reason need be given; you can reapply and pay again [V0042, V0043, V0019]. Questions and complaints: High Commission of Malaysia, Dhaka, dhaka@imi.gov.my (visa section), +88 02 4108 1918 (visa), House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave, Sunday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm [V0043, B0121].

### Step 3: Check your eVISA is real

At [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa) enter your passport number, the visa sticker number and the CAPTCHA [V0067]; only official-site applications can be checked [V0006]. **Need to know:** what a genuine eVISA looks like and where its sticker number is printed.

### Step 4: Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)

1. **When:** within 3 days before arrival, the submission day included [V0058, V0061].
2. **Where:** only [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main), with no fee mentioned [V0059]. Fake sites charge, such as malaysiacard.com (USD 29.99), and appear as sponsored search results [V0065, N0057].
3. **You'll need:** name, passport number, date and place of birth, nationality, sex, passport expiry, email, mobile, arrival and departure dates, flight number, mode of travel and last airport of departure [V0061].
4. **Check it** under "Check Registration" with passport number, nationality and PIN [V0062]; show it with your passport at immigration [V0059].

### Step 5: At the border

**At Dhaka airport,** immigration police stop travellers (Malaysia included) whose visa, BMET clearance (a worker's emigration clearance), papers or answers don't match [O0031]. Carry what Malaysia checks: eVISA, return ticket, paid accommodation and proof of money [V0050, V0027]. **Need to know:** what to do if Bangladeshi immigration stops you, and whether you can complain or travel later.

**At the Malaysian airport,** the officer checks passport, eVISA, MDAC, return ticket and paid accommodation [V0050, V0059]. A visa doesn't guarantee entry: you can be refused if you can't explain your plans, or can't prove you aren't a "prohibited immigrant", which includes lacking means of support [V0051, V0052, V0053, V0054]. Autogates aren't open to Bangladeshis [V0057]. Bangladeshis holding valid tourist visas are regularly turned back at KLIA for fake hotel bookings, no return ticket, too little money or no satisfactory reason [V0109, N0011, N0013]; travellers are told to carry RM500 to RM1,000, and officers may check phone photos [O0029, O0030]. Those refused go on a "Not to Land" list and the airline returns them within about 24 hours [N0010, N0015].

**If refused entry:** the Immigration Act's appeal to the Minister covers only Permit holders, not visitors [V0055, K0011]. **Need to know:** whether a refused visitor can appeal, any official minimum sum of money, and what "Not to Land" means for future visits.

**Done to travellers.** Fraudsters bring Bangladeshi women on tourist visas promising jobs, though no agreement exists to recruit Bangladeshi women for Malaysia [N0028]. Rings in Kuala Lumpur sell Bangladeshis forged European visas for up to RM20,000 [D0043]; a man promised Italy was sent with fake Malaysian and French visas and returned from the Malaysian airport [D0048]. Travel agencies have promised work permits, sent people on visit visas, held them and demanded ransom from families [O0032].

**Tried by travellers.** Getting a pass by a false statement carries a fine up to RM10,000 or up to 5 years' jail [K0031, K0030].

**If someone sold you a "job on a tourist visa", holds your passport, or holds you in Malaysia,** in this order:
1. **Held or in danger in Malaysia:** call 999 for the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) [O0073, O0074], then the Bangladesh High Commission's consular wing, +603 2604 0946, consular.kualalumpur@gmail.com [L0031].
2. **Family calls the Probashi call centre** of WEWB (the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, the government body for migrant workers' welfare), for problems of migrants and families: 16135 toll free in Bangladesh, +880 9610102030 from abroad, 24 hours [B0083, O0075].
3. **Bangladesh police:** online at [gd.police.gov.bd](https://gd.police.gov.bd/) or at the station (see Student visa, Scams), or the CID hotline, 01320-019999, for anyone holding passports or demanding unusual visa sums [I0034, O0070, O0072, O0033].
4. **Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment** (Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Old Elephant Road, Dhaka-1000) [G0025]. Against a recruiting agency, email the enforcement branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd (02-41030255), with your proof, full address and the agency's RL (licence) number and address; free, action within 3 to 5 working days, settled within 30 [B0044, B0045]. Against a licensed agency that sent you abroad without permission, apply to the Secretary with your BMET smart card, ticket, contract and passport copies; free, settled within 60 working days of BMET's recommendation [B0046]. Anything else: the Complaint Resolution Cell, minister.expat@gmail.com [R0012]. Sample: "I, [name, passport no.], paid [agent, address, phone] Tk [amount] on [date] for a job in Malaysia. I was sent on a tourist visa on [date] and [what happened]. Receipts and messages attached."
5. **Free legal help:** OKUP (see Student visa, Scams) helps trafficking survivors and migrants' families, helpline 01842773300 [O0076, O0077, O0078].

### Don't work

A visit pass allows no work, work training or study [V0046, V0104] and lapses once its conditions are broken [V0100]. Overstaying brings fines and can bar re-entry for a set period [V0047]. A visit pass is extended only for illness, an accident or war at home, with evidence and a return ticket, by applying in person on Form IMM.55 [V0069]. A directive of 8 June 2026 ordered a nationwide multi-agency crackdown on people working on tourist, student and other short passes [P0021].

## Family visit sponsored by a Malaysian citizen (VDR)

A Malaysian citizen (the sponsor) can invite a Bangladeshi relative through a VDR (Visa Dengan Rujukan, visa with reference): the sponsor applies in Malaysia, Immigration headquarters approves, and a Malaysian mission issues the visa [C0008, V0090, P0010, P0049, V0089]. Proof of relationship: for parents or parents-in-law, the sponsor's spouse's marriage certificate, birth certificate and passport, plus a notarial certificate endorsed by the Malaysian mission and Wisma Putra (Malaysia's foreign ministry); for a child under 7, the birth certificate and the sponsor's marriage certificate; for a spouse, the marriage certificate [P0008]. Other relatives also need documentary proof [Z0028]. No work, work training or study [V0104].

### Step 1: Send your papers to the sponsor

You'll need: a passport valid more than 6 months from travel, every printed page scanned; 2 photos, 3.5 x 5.0 cm; the relationship documents above; and, for the sponsor's form, your marital history, job in Bangladesh with proof, earlier visits to and work in Malaysia, spouse and children, address and planned length of stay [P0010, P0004, P0008, P0012, P0016].

1. **Parents or parents-in-law: get the notarial certificate endorsed** [P0008]. High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka: House 19, Road 6, Baridhara Diplomatic Enclave; dhaka@imi.gov.my, +88 02 4108 1918; Sunday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm [B0121]. Wisma Putra's Consular Division takes documents walk-in from the person or a representative with ID, usually ready the same or next working day: Wisma Putra No 1, Jalan Wisma Putra, Precinct 2, 62602 Putrajaya; 03-8000 8000; sah_admin@kln.gov.my; Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4 pm, Friday 8.30 to 12 and 2.45 to 4 pm [P0050, P0051]. Non-English documents need an English translation [P0052]. **Need to know:** what the certificate must state and which notary may make it; the fees and times in Dhaka and Putrajaya; whether the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur must authenticate it first (Wisma Putra's general rule for documents from abroad) [P0052]; and whether the sponsor can take it to Wisma Putra.
2. **Send the sponsor copies of everything,** with your name in capitals, sex, place and country of birth, date of birth, nationality and passport details exactly as in your passport, for Form Imm.12 [P0003]. Ask the sponsor to name the High Commission in Dhaka as the issuing mission [P0012, B0121].

### Step 2: The sponsor applies in Malaysia

You'll need (the sponsor): MyKad (Malaysian identity card); the 3 latest payslips showing RM2,000 a month or more, or, if self-employed, the SSM (Companies Commission of Malaysia) business registration and 3 months' bank statements; an employer's confirmation letter; and the papers from Step 1 [P0010, Z0027].

1. **Download the forms** from [IMI's VDR checklist](https://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SENARAI-SEMAK-Borang-VDR.pdf): Form Imm.12, the sponsor's declaration (Surat Akuan Penaja), the bond and the sponsor information form (Borang Maklumat Tambahan Penaja) [P0010, P0011, P0012].
2. **Type Form Imm.12** in 2 copies, with the relative's photo on each [P0004, Z0058].
3. **Swear the declaration** (job, employer, monthly income and full responsibility for the relative) before a Commissioner for Oaths at a government court, a Sessions Court judge, magistrate or notary public [P0011].
4. **Sign a personal bond, stamped RM10 at LHDN** (the Inland Revenue Board); the sponsor forfeits its sum if the relative breaks the visit's conditions [Z0026, P0006, P0014]. A cash security bond applies only to citizens of China and Nigeria [P0006].
5. **Add a cover letter** (Surat Iringan) and the employer's letter [P0010].
6. **Submit in person;** the sponsor signs the sponsor form before an Immigration officer [Z0025, P0012]. VDRs are decided by the Visa, Pass and Permit Division, Immigration headquarters, No. 15, Tingkat 1-7, Blok 2G4, Persiaran Perdana, Presint 2, 62550 Putrajaya; 03-8000 8000 [C0008, P0005]. Incomplete applications or ones without documentary proof are not accepted [P0009].
7. **Wait about 7 working days;** the result is a visa approval letter (eVAL) [V0090, P0010, Z0096, P0016].

**Need to know:** whether a Bangladeshi relative's sponsor pays a cash deposit (the form asks about one, wang cagaran, and IMI's bond rate for Bangladeshis is RM750) [P0048, V0112]; what the cover letter must contain; whether a state Immigration office also takes the application, and the counter hours; any IMI fee for the approval; how the approval reaches the relative; and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Step 3: Apply for the eVISA

Bangladeshis still need an eVISA with the VDR approval [P0015]. You'll need: a scan of your passport's data page, the approval letter, an email address, and a card or e-wallet [P0016, V0016, V0017].

1. Apply on [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/) as in Tourist visa, Step 2, choosing Visa With Reference, with details exactly as on the approval letter; any difference must be corrected by the issuing office before you pay [V0021, C0006, Z0096, P0017]. From Bangladesh the portal takes applications on weekdays, 9 am to 5 pm Bangladesh time [V0025].
2. Pay RM20 visa fee, RM105 processing and 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet); nothing is refunded [V0017, P0018, V0019]. If charged twice, or the status stays "New Application", don't pay again; use MyVISA's 24-hour Live Chat [P0019].
3. Download and print the eVISA (Actions, Download eVISA) and check it at [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/evisa/check-evisa) with your passport number and the visa sticker number [V0037, V0067].

### Step 4: Travel

Register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) within 3 days before arrival and carry the papers the border officer checks (see Tourist visa, Step 5) [V0058, V0050].

### Don't work, scams and complaints

Working ends a visit pass (see What can get you jailed); Immigration arrests Bangladeshis working on visit passes, and the sponsor loses the bond [V0104, V0100, P0020, P0022, P0014]. False statements are an offence (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031]. **Need to know:** scams on this route, such as fake sponsors or brokers charging for a "family VDR". 3. **If an official asked for a bribe,** report to the MACC: 1-800-88-6000 (free), [portaladuan.sprm.gov.my](https://portaladuan.sprm.gov.my/), info@sprm.gov.my, SMS +6019-6000 696; No. 2, Lebuh Wawasan, Presint 7, 62250 Putrajaya [P0053, P0054].
4. **For help as a Bangladeshi,** contact the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur (see Contacts) [L0032, B0117].

## Business visit

A social visit pass covers meetings, conferences, inspections, audits, signing agreements, investment surveys and seminars, not professional or technical work or a job [V0068, V0094, V0100]. For up to 30 days of urgent professional work the Malaysian company gets PLS@Xpats permission (Step 3); longer work needs a Professional Visit Pass or Employment Pass [P0024, V0091].

### Step 1: Get the papers

You'll need: a passport valid more than 6 months with 3 blank pages; a studio photo; bank statements; proof of paid accommodation; a return ticket; and your company's dispatch letter or an invitation from a Malaysian government body or government-linked company (see Tourist visa, Step 1) [V0026, V0032, V0070].

### Step 2: Apply for the business eVISA on MyVISA

1. Apply as in Tourist visa, Steps 2 and 3, choosing the business purpose and uploading the letter and ticket; one account holds up to 5 travellers' applications [V0070, V0023, V0037, V0067].
2. A business multiple-entry visa (6 months, up to 30 days per entry) is limited to 31 eligible nationalities; Bangladesh is one of the 31 nationalities that need an eVISA [V0071, C0007, V0001]. **Need to know:** whether these are the same 31, so that Bangladeshis can choose multiple entry.
3. Pay RM20 visa fee, RM105 processing and the card or e-wallet charge, non-refundable; the decision takes 2 to 7 working days, and a refusal has no reasons or published appeal, though you can reapply and pay again [V0017, V0016, V0019, V0012, V0042].

You'll need (the company): its request letter; a copy of every page of your passport, valid 6 months or more; its SSM registration; the work or project schedule; and your return ticket [P0028].

1. The company applies free at [mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats/main](https://mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats/main), without ESD (IMI's Expatriate Services Division) registration; it takes 3 working days, for anyone 18 or over, not blacklisted and not Israeli [P0028, P0029, P0027].

### Scams and complaints

Fake invitations: a syndicate sold Bangladeshis fake invitation letters (such as to sports competitions), with return tickets and hotel bookings to fool officers, for RM2,000 to RM5,000 [P0040]. Don't pay anyone to "get" the visa: it is applied for and paid only online, for the Step 2 fees, and no officer or third party may handle it outside official channels [P0019, V0016, P0038]. ## Employment Pass

For professionals hired by a Malaysian company at a basic salary of at least RM5,000 a month who meet ESD's qualification rule (Step 1); valid for one employer, and only the employer applies [E0011, E0022, E0002, E0006]. A pass issued through ESD is valid only in Peninsular Malaysia, and changing company means a new application [E0005]. Some passes are approved by other bodies: manufacturing by MIDA (Malaysian Investment Development Authority); ICT companies with MSC Malaysia status by the Multimedia Development Corporation; banking, finance and insurance by Bank Negara Malaysia (the central bank); the capital market by the Securities Commission; government contract posts by the Public Services Department; the East Coast Economic Region by ECERDC (East Coast Economic Region Development Council); and Iskandar Malaysia by IRDA (Iskandar Regional Development Authority) [E0007, C0013, Z0063, Z0090]. **Need to know:** what MSC Malaysia status is and how a company gets it.

### Step 1: Check you qualify

By basic salary: Category I RM20,000 a month or more and Category II RM10,000 to RM19,999, each up to 10 years; Category III RM5,000 to RM9,999 (RM7,000 to RM9,999 in manufacturing), up to 5 years, counting time with the same employer, position and category [E0008, E0009, E0010, E0011, E0018, P0068]. Only basic salary taxable in Malaysia counts, not allowances, bonuses or pay outside Malaysia [E0013, E0017]. You need a degree and 3 years' relevant experience, a diploma and 5, or a technical certificate and 7 [E0022]. All categories may bring family: a Dependant Pass for a spouse and children under 18, and a Long-Term Social Visit Pass for parents, parents-in-law and children 18 to 25 (see Family passes, Family of an Employment Pass holder) [E0009, E0010, E0011, P0068].

### Step 2: The employer applies

You'll need: a passport valid more than 12 months with 6 empty pages, the whole booklet and both covers scanned in colour; an unedited photo, 3.5 x 5.0 cm, light blue background, 99 x 142 pixels; your highest educational (and any professional) certificate and a detailed resume with your total work experience, in colour; and the contract, stamped by the Inland Revenue Board and signed and dated by both sides [E0025, P0064, P0066, P0067]. From Bangladesh, English certificates must be certified true copies from an embassy or high commission (in Bangladesh or Malaysia) or the employer's HR manager; others must be translated into English and certified by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka [P0065]. **Need to know:** the High Commission in Dhaka's fee and time for certifying certificates.

1. **The employer registers with ESD** (5 working days), then gets JTKSM approval and the MYFutureJobs acknowledgement (see Professional Visit Pass, Step 1), in 10 working days; hiring without approval risks a fine up to RM100,000, 5 years' jail or both [E0016, E0050, E0051, E0052].
2. **Send the employer** the documents above [E0025].
3. **The employer applies on ESD Online and pays RM2,160;** the decision takes 2 to 5 working days [E0040, E0016, E0054]. Only the employer can appeal a refusal, within 14 days, or else claim 75% of the processing fee back within 14 days [E0063, E0048, Z0039].
4. **The employer applies for your VDR** with the pass; after approval it pays the endorsement: RM200 a year, RM125 processing and RM20 a year visa fee [E0058, E0042, E0045].

### Step 3: Emigration clearance in Bangladesh

You'll need: your application; a visa copy; the contract if any; an attested copy of your OEP registration card (see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 1); a declaration on Tk 300 non-judicial stamp paper; and a pay-order for the fees [B0029].

1. Apply yourself at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka (BMET head office: 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000) or the District Employment and Manpower Office in Chattogram; it takes 3 working days [B0029, G0026]. Fees and the smart card checked at the airport are as in Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 6 [B0030, B0038]. Problems: Director (Emigration), +8802222222415, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [B0031]. **Need to know:** the emigration branch's opening hours, where to buy the stamp paper, which bank the pay-order is drawn on, and what to do if clearance is refused.

### Step 4: Fly and get the ePASS

Register the MDAC within 3 days before arrival [V0058]. On arrival go, no appointment needed, to the Expatriate Satellite Centre at KLIA Terminal 1 or 2 for your ePASS and i-KAD (the Employment Pass holder's card) [E0059, P0044]; pass problems go to the MYXpats helpdesk (see Professional Visit Pass, Step 4) [P0058].

### Step 5: Check it is real

Check the ePASS by QR code or at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan), since fakes are sold (see Professional Visit Pass, Scams), and check the employer on SSM e-Info before accepting [E0060, P0038, G0055]. If you get a new passport, the company must transfer the pass endorsement to it, or Immigration may stop you leaving [E0065]. If cheated, use the route under Family visit (VDR), Don't work, scams and complaints [P0055, P0053]. False statements and using another's pass are offences (see Tourist visa, Scams) [K0031].

### Problems with the employer

Unpaid wages and other Employment Act breaches go through the Working for Workers app, the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing and the Labour Court (see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), If you aren't paid) [L0163, L0021, M0232]. JTKSM advises free on 03-8000 8000 or jtksm@mohr.gov.my [M0118]. **Need to know:** what an Employment Pass holder can do if the employer cancels or doesn't renew the pass.

## Family passes

The pass depends on who brings the family member: an Employment Pass holder, a Malaysian citizen, a permanent resident or a student. A PLKS (temporary work pass) holder can't bring anyone.

### Family of an Employment Pass holder

**Who gets which pass.** A spouse and children under 18 get a Dependant Pass; parents, parents-in-law, children aged 18 to 25 and a common-law spouse get a Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP, a pass for a stay of 6 months or more) [E0014, C0015]. A Dependant Pass lasts as long as the Employment Pass, or until the dependant's passport expires if sooner [E0034]. Holders of a Category III Employment Pass issued before 1 June 2026 (when Category III covered a basic salary of RM3,000 to RM4,999) still can't bring dependants [E0019, E0012]. **Need to know:** how long an LTSVP for these relatives is approved for at a time.

1. **Get the family documents translated and attested.**

   You'll need: for a spouse, the marriage certificate (in Bangladesh, the Muslim marriage registration certificate); each child's birth certificate; each person's passport; and, for a common-law spouse, an affidavit or acknowledgement letter from the Employment Pass holder's embassy [E0030, E0031].

   1. Translate each certificate into English if needed [E0030]. Take the original and translation, both with the translation agency's and translator's seal and signature, to an approved notary public and then the Ministry of Law [Q0084].
   2. Each certificate must then be certified as a true copy, dated in the current year, by the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka or the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur [E0030].
   3. The Bangladesh High Commission attests a marriage certificate only after it has been attested in Dhaka by both the Ministry of Law and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs [Q0079]. Have it attested by a notary public and the Ministry of Law first, then hand in two sets at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' Consular wing in Dhaka (rooms 201/205; 02-47123389; asconsular2@mofa.gov.bd) between 8 am and noon with a copy of your NID or passport, and collect them free the same day, 2 to 3 pm [Q0082]. A birth certificate must be issued through BDRIS (Bangladesh's online birth and death registration system), verifiable online, and attested by a notary and the Ministry of Law [Q0083]; for a birth certificate the High Commission also accepts proof of online verification instead of the Dhaka attestation [Q0080].
   4. At the Bangladesh High Commission (No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur), bring the attested originals, copies of every paper and of your passport and visa, and one extra set of copies [Q0079, Q0080, L0032]. Attesting a certificate or affidavit costs RM42 (RM63 urgent) plus RM8.40 per extra page, paid by card at the counter (Maybank Merchant Card Service) or into the High Commission's Maybank account 564427102268 with the original deposit slip [Q0062, Q0063].

   **Need to know:** the Bangladesh High Commission's counter hours for attestation and how long it takes; and the Malaysian High Commission in Dhaka's procedure and fee for certifying family documents.

2. **The employer applies.** Only the Employment Pass holder's company can apply, on ESD Online (the system of Immigration's Expatriate Services Division, which handles professional passes), with the Employment Pass or after it is approved [Q0002, E0034, Q0065].

   You'll need, to give the employer: the attested documents; a colour scan of each family member's passport; and, for an LTSVP, a personal bond form stamped by the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN, Malaysia's tax authority) [E0031].

   1. The employer pays RM540 per person on applying, then RM90 a year for the pass, RM50 processing and the visa fee (RM20 a year for a Bangladeshi) [E0041, E0043, E0045].
   2. The LTSVP bond is a promise that the relative will obey immigration law, backed by a Malaysian company representative's pledge of RM750 for a Bangladeshi [Q0086, E0144].
   3. A decision takes about 14 working days; the family member gets an ePASS (electronic pass) [E0029, Q0066].

   **Check it is real:** scan the ePASS QR code or check it at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/epass?semakan) [E0060]. A false statement to get a pass is an offence [K0031]. **If refused:** ESD's online appeal covers only Employment Pass and Professional Visit Pass refusals [Q0064]. Questions: MYXpats Centre, which processes Employment Pass applications [E0001], +603-7839 7171, helpdesk@myxpats.com.my [Q0013]. **Need to know:** whether the RM750 is a deposit and is returned, and whether a refused Dependant Pass or LTSVP can be appealed.

3. **Work and study.** To work, a Dependant Pass holder needs their own Employment Pass [Q0001, E0145]. From 1 January 2027 the spouse of a Category I Employment Pass holder (RM20,000 a month or more) may work on the Dependant Pass, under conditions still to be set [Q0003, E0009]. A spouse on a Dependant Pass applies for Permission to Study online through the Sub-Product menu in ESD Online; paper applications are no longer accepted at Immigration or MYXpats Centre counters [Q0005, Q0078]. LTSVP relatives may not work or study without switching to an Employment Pass or Student Pass [S0141, E0032]. **Need to know:** the conditions for Category I spouses once published; the fee, processing time and what a Permission to Study holder receives.

**Need to know:** any official warning about scams in family-pass applications.

### Spouse of a Malaysian citizen

**Before you marry.** A PLKS worker may not marry a Malaysian or another foreign worker in Malaysia; one who does loses the work permit and is sent home [E0139, Q0010]. For other foreigners, the Malaysian partner first has Immigration confirm the foreigner's pass on IMI's (the Immigration Department of Malaysia's) pass-confirmation form for marriage, [Permohonan Pengesahan Pas bagi Tujuan Perkahwinan Warganegara Asing](https://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Borang-Pengesahan-Pas-Bagi-Tujuan-Perkahwinan-Dengan-Warganegara-Asing.pdf) [Q0087]: hand in two copies at any state Immigration headquarters with a copy of the partner's identity card, two passport photos of each of you and a copy of the foreigner's passport with the pass pages; an officer certifies the pass and its expiry on the form [Q0088]. The Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur asks a Bangladeshi for an unmarried certificate from the Deputy Commissioner's office attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dhaka, and the parents' affidavit or consent attested by the Ministry of Law and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, with the consular fee receipt [Q0081]. **Need to know:** whether the pass-confirmation form has a fee and how long it takes. The marriage must be registered under Malaysian law before the spouse can get permission to work or apply for permanent residence [E0129, Q0038].

1. **Apply for the spouse's LTSVP.**

   You'll need, as originals with copies: IMI's [Form Imm.12](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/IM.12.pdf), [Form Imm.38](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/IM38.pdf) (2 copies), [Form Imm.55](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Imm55.pdf) (2 copies), [sponsor's statement](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Borang_Kenyatan_Penaja.pdf), [security bond](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Security-Bond.pdf) and [sworn declaration](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Surat_Akuan_Sumpah.pdf), listed on IMI's [new spouse application page](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/permohonan-baru-isteri-atau-suami-kepada-warganegara/); the Malaysian spouse's identity card (MyKad), birth certificate and passport (all pages); the marriage registration certificate; any divorce or death certificate from an earlier marriage; a postcard-size wedding photo; children's birth certificates; the Malaysian spouse's employer letter (or business licence if self-employed); proof of income (3 months' payslips, bank book or tax Form J); and a utility bill [E0126, Z0061, Z0062, Q0006, Q0007, Q0009, Q0111].

   1. Stamp the security bond with a RM10 revenue stamp [E0126]; have the sworn declaration, with your photo, stamped by a Commissioner for Oaths [Q0008].
   2. Go with the Malaysian spouse to the Immigration office nearest your home [Z0046, E0127]. Immigration checks every original and accepts only complete applications [Q0006].
   3. A decision takes 25 working days under IMI's client charter; the pass runs 6 months to 5 years at a time [E0057, E0122].

   **Need to know:** the fee, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

   **Checks and scams.** Immigration refuses a marriage made only for immigration status and looks closely at large age gaps [Q0011, Q0012]; it may visit your address, interview people and check documents before granting or extending the pass [Q0089]. Anyone selling a spouse pass or marriage registration is a syndicate [Q0011, Q0012]; report them as under "If Immigration mishandles any family-pass application" [Q0046, Q0068]. A false statement in the application can bring a fine of up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail, or both [E0128].

2. **Get permission to work.** Once endorsed in the passport, it lets the spouse work, run a business or practise a profession on the LTSVP [E0122, S0095].

   You'll need: the Malaysian spouse in person with their MyKad; your passport and pass; a sworn statement; the marriage certificate; the job offer; an employment contract with a RM10 revenue stamp stating the job, period and monthly pay [E0125]; IMI's [Applicant, Citizen Spouse and Employer Information Form](http://www.imi.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Borang_Maklumat.pdf); and the employer's latest company registration form from MyData SSM (the online records of the Companies Commission of Malaysia) [Q0090, Q0091].

   1. Apply at the Visa, Pass and Permit Division of Immigration in the state where you'll work, with the LTSVP application or any time after a job offer [Z0097, S0099].

   **Need to know:** whether the work permission can also be applied for at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or the nearest state office; its fee and how long it takes.

**Permanent residence.** A wife can apply for an Entry Permit after 3 years of marriage and over 12 months on the spouse LTSVP; a husband after 5 years of marriage and 5 continuous years on it [Q0016, Q0018, Q0017]. See Other passes, Permanent residence.

**Residence Pass** (Pas Residen: RM500 for 5 years; allows work, study and business) [E0131, E0132]. For relatives of citizens (biological, step or adopted children; a widowed or divorced spouse with a citizen child; parents and parents-in-law) after 3 years on a valid long-term pass, and children and spouses of permanent residents after 5 [Z0018, E0133, Z0049].

   You'll need: Form IMM 16A [Z0069]; 2 passport photos and a copy of the sponsor's identity card [Z0070]; old and new passports and original supporting documents, with copies [Z0071]; copies of Bangladeshi documents attested by the Bangladesh High Commission [Z0072].

   1. A Malaysian citizen aged 21 or over sponsors you and comes with you to Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya or a nearby Immigration office [Z0049, Z0048].
   2. Pay RM500 plus a multiple-entry visa fee set by nationality [E0131, Z0073].
   3. Track it on myOnlinePasResiden at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main) [E0136].

   **Need to know:** how long a decision takes, and whether a refusal can be appealed.

### Other relatives of Malaysians and of permanent residents

- A foreigner aged 60 or over with no family at home, sponsored by a child, child-in-law or sibling in Malaysia earning RM2,000 or more [C0016], with a personal bond stamped RM10 at LHDN [Z0076].
- An abused, divorced or widowed wife of a citizen, with a local sponsor earning RM2,000 or more [C0017]; a widow's sponsor is a close relative of her late husband who comes with her [Z0099, Z0100]; an abused wife adds a police or hospital report, a security bond stamped RM10, and the sponsor's declaration and identity card [C0017, Z0075].

   1. Apply at the Immigration office nearest your home; only complete applications are accepted, and the charter gives 25 working days [Z0046, C0016, E0057].
   2. A dependant's or guardian's LTSVP costs RM90 [S0007]. A false statement is an offence [K0031].

## MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)

A long-stay pass for people with substantial savings, run by MOTAC (the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture), for citizens of any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia [E0101, E0100], aged 25 or over (21 for the SEZ/SFZ, Special Economic Zone / Special Financial Zone, tier) [Z0041, Z0043].

**Tiers.** Silver: USD 150,000 fixed deposit and a home of at least RM600,000, 5 years. Gold: USD 500,000 and RM1,000,000, 15 years. Platinum: USD 1,000,000 and RM2,000,000, 20 years [E0106, E0108, E0114]. SEZ/SFZ: USD 65,000 (age 21 to 49) or USD 32,000 (50 and over) and a home in Forest City, Johor, 10 years [E0106, E0115].

**Family.** A spouse; children under 21 (biological, step or adopted); unmarried, unemployed children aged 21 to 34; disabled children of any age; parents and parents-in-law [E0104, E0110].

**Sarawak's S-MM2H.** Sarawak runs its own scheme, reportedly on these terms since 2025: age 30 or over, RM500,000 fixed deposit in a Sarawak bank, 30 days a year in Sarawak, a 5-year pass, applied for through the state's one-stop centre or licensed agents [E0143].

1. **Check the money can legally leave Bangladesh.** Bangladesh Bank sets foreign-exchange rules under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act 1947 [Z0103] and has not approved sending money abroad for MM2H [N0051]; a Malaysian MM2H licence doesn't by itself mean an agent meets Bangladesh's rules [Q0050]. **Need to know:** Bangladesh Bank's written rule on this, and how to ask it for permission.

2. **Choose a licensed agent.** Every application goes through an MM2H operator licensed by MOTAC [E0101]. Check at [mm2h.gov.my/agencies](https://www.mm2h.gov.my/agencies) that the company name, licence number (MM2H plus digits) and licence dates match what the agent shows [E0116]. No agent can guarantee approval; some have taken tens of thousands of ringgit, then stopped answering, and applicants couldn't recover it [Q0047, Q0048].

3. **The agent applies** through the One Stop Centre MM2H on MOTAC's online system; the Ministry of Home Affairs approves through Immigration [Q0041, E0118]. Fees: processing RM5,000 for the main applicant and RM2,500 per dependant, plus participation RM1,000 (Silver, SEZ/SFZ), RM3,000 (Gold) or RM200,000 (Platinum) [E0107, E0109]; MOTAC's system also lists RM500 plus SST (sales and service tax) per application [E0118]. You need a Certificate of Good Conduct (in Bangladesh, a police clearance certificate; see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 4) and Malaysian police screening [Q0049]. **Need to know:** whether the RM500 is extra, the full document list and the processing time.

4. **After approval.** Everyone has a medical check at a MOTAC panel clinic or hospital [Q0039]. Open the fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank; up to half can later be withdrawn for a home, education, medical care or tourism in Malaysia [Q0040]. Buy a home within a year of endorsement and keep it 10 years unless selling to buy a dearer one [E0119, E0103]. Immigration's client charter gives 1 working day to endorse the pass [C0047].

5. **Living on MM2H.** Under 50, spend at least 90 days a year in Malaysia [E0103]. Only Platinum holders may work or run a business [E0112, E0113, Z0019, Z0064]. Renewal is RM500 a year plus a visa fee of RM0 to RM50 by nationality [Q0042]. MM2H years don't count toward permanent residence [Q0015].

**If refused.** Any appeal is for the Ministry of Home Affairs [Z0042]. **Need to know:** how and by when.

**If an agent cheats you.**

1. Complain on MOTAC's SISPAA at [motac.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do](https://motac.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do): choose "Aduan/Maklum Balas Baharu", give the agent's name and licence number, what you paid and when, attach receipts, and track it under "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" [Q0045]. For example: "I paid RM[amount] on [date] to [agent], licence MM2H[number], for an MM2H application. The agent has not applied or answered since [date]. Receipts attached. Please investigate and help me recover the money."
2. Or call the MM2H office: +603 8891 7568 or +603 8891 7434; MOTAC, No. 2, Tower 1, Jalan P5/6, Presint 5, 62200 Putrajaya [Q0043, Q0044].

**Need to know:** whether MOTAC has ever recovered money for an applicant cheated by an agent.

## DE Rantau Nomad Pass

For remote workers and freelancers paid from outside Malaysia, run by MDEC (the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation), for all nationalities except Israel, main applicant over 18 [E0084]. Minimum income: USD 36,000 a year in tech, USD 60,000 otherwise [E0085, E0098]. Up to 12 months at a time and 48 in all [E0086]; holders at the two-year limit will get two more years under conditions to be set [Q0004]. No route to permanent residence [E0098]. New applications are closed until 30 November 2026 and reopen on 1 December 2026 [E0099].

**Family.** A spouse or common-law partner, children under 18 (including adopted and stepchildren), a special-needs child of any age, and parents [E0087]. A spouse can't work [E0088].

1. **Prepare the documents** in English or with a certified translation, as clear PDFs, with the same name and income figures throughout [Q0051, Q0074].

   You'll need, as a remote employee: a passport with 6 empty pages, valid over 14 months [Q0072, E0096]; a CV; a signed contract showing remote work, title, duration and salary, with an employer not registered in Malaysia, starting at least 3 months before you apply [Q0072, Z0065]; 3 months' payslips and bank statements showing the same deposits [Q0073]; an income statement or tax return; a Letter of Good Conduct (police clearance; see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 4); the personal bond form; your highest education certificate [Q0072, E0097]; and a medical insurance enrolment certificate valid in Malaysia for at least 3 months, covering dependants; it can be handed in after approval, before the pass is issued [Q0109].

   You'll need, as a freelancer: the passport, CV, Letter of Good Conduct, bond form, certificate and insurance; up to 3 signed client contracts showing scope, duration and payment terms; 3 months' invoices; proof of payment (PayPal, Stripe, Wise or bank records); and 3 months' bank statements above the threshold [Q0075, Q0076, Z0067].

   **Need to know:** the minimum insurance cover and accepted insurers, and whether Malaysian clients count.

2. **Apply** at [mdec.my/derantau](https://mdec.my/derantau) and pay RM1,080 plus RM540 per dependant online, not refunded [E0089, E0091]. A decision takes 6 to 8 weeks after a complete application; track it at [malaysiadigital.mdec.my](https://malaysiadigital.mdec.my) [E0089]. No appeal, but you can reapply; no one can guarantee approval [E0093, E0094].

3. **After approval: enter and get the pass endorsed.** The approval letter is valid 6 months, not extendable; finish every step within it [Q0052].

   1. Get an eVISA (a visa applied for online) or, if the letter says so, a VDR (visa with reference) [Q0053]. Apply only on IMI's own site, [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/); applications through third parties or agents can't be verified [V0005, V0006]. For a Bangladeshi an eVISA costs the RM20 visa fee, a RM105 processing charge and a 0.8% (card) or 1.7% (e-wallet) charge, none of it refunded, and takes 2 to 7 working days; see Tourist visa, Step 2, for how to apply [V0016, V0017, V0019, V0012]. A tourist or social visit pass can't be converted: leave and re-enter [Q0054, E0095]. **Need to know:** the cost, documents and processing time of a VDR for DE Rantau.
   2. Use a normal Immigration counter, not the automatic gate, to get an entry stamp [Q0053].
   3. Within 1 month, upload on the DE Rantau portal, under your approved application: the personal bond with a RM10 revenue stamp, endorsed by LHDN; all passport pages; the entry stamp; the eVISA or VDR; Malaysian health insurance; and an LHDN tax registration slip from mytax.hasil.gov.my [Q0057, Q0055, Q0058].
   4. Pay Immigration RM90 per 3 months or RM360 a year, any multiple-entry visa charge, and a RM750 bond for a Bangladeshi, refunded when the pass expires [Z0040, E0092].
   5. Stay in Malaysia until the e-Pass is issued, about 1 week; leaving can cancel the application [Q0056, E0089].

**Where you can live.** Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan; Sabah or Sarawak need a tourist pass [E0095].

**If something goes wrong.** Contact MDEC's Expats Service Centre (expatctr@mdec.com.my, 03-8315 3106 / 3157) [Q0097] or the form at [mdec.my/contact-us](https://mdec.my/contact-us), enquiry type "Foreign Knowledge Workers" [Q0098]. For a visa or endorsement problem, complain on IMI's SISPAA at [imi.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do](https://imi.spab.gov.my/eApps/system/index.do) [Q0046].

**Need to know:** any official warning about DE Rantau scams.

## Other passes

**Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T).** A 10-year pass for experienced professionals from TalentCorp (Talent Corporation Malaysia, a government agency for skilled workers) [E0078]. You need 3 consecutive years' work in Malaysia and an Employment Pass with over 3 months left; at least RM15,000 basic a month; Malaysian income tax paid for 2 years; and a degree, diploma or professional certificate with 5 years' experience [E0078, E0079, Q0059]. The holder can change employer, and the spouse can work without an Employment Pass [E0080].

1. Register an account on the RP-T Portal at [rpt.talentcorp.com.my](https://rpt.talentcorp.com.my/), complete the application and upload the documents yourself; no agent may apply for you [Q0100]. Name a local contact: a Malaysian citizen aged 21 or over working and living in Malaysia, with a copy of both sides of their MyKad [Q0110].
2. Pay TalentCorp online RM540 per person on applying, not refunded; if approved, RM5,400 for you or your spouse and RM4,860 per other dependant; then Immigration RM500 per pass (RM90 for an LTSVP) plus the visa fee [Q0099].
3. Approval takes 21 working days from a complete application, the result follows within 10, endorsement takes 7, and Pos Malaysia delivers the pass within 7 more [Q0101].
4. No appeal; reapply after 1 year [Q0102]. Questions: RPTCare@myxpats.com.my, 03-7839 7171 [Q0102]; TalentCorp +603 7839 7000, info@talentcorp.com.my [Q0061].

**Malaysia Tech Entrepreneur Programme (MTEP).** For tech founders, through MDEC, at [mdec.my/mtep](https://mdec.my/mtep): 1 year for new founders (from RM2,700) or 5 years for experienced founders (from RM5,400, dependants allowed). You need RM50,000 in personal bank statements, a certificate of good conduct and a sponsor [E0081, E0082, E0083, Q0103]: for 1 year, a Malaysia Digital Hub or MTEP recognised partner, which signs a personal bond and gives a letter of offer or tenancy agreement; for 5 years, a Malaysian sponsor or individual who signs a declaration [Q0103]. **Need to know:** the processing time, whether a refusal can be appealed, and whether any nationality restriction applies to Bangladeshis.

**Investor Pass.** For investors working with Malaysia's investment agencies; 6 months, extendable by 6, no dependants [E0075, E0076]. Apply from outside Malaysia on Xpats Gateway ([xpatsgateway.com.my](https://xpatsgateway.com.my/), the platform for professional passes), after the company is fully registered in ESD Online [Z0108, Z0107, Q0107]. Fees: RM1,200 (RM1,296 with tax), a RM90 pass fee for 6 months, RM500 processing and the visa fee [E0077]. A complete application is processed within 5 working days, and the ePASS appears in Xpats Gateway 1 working day after payment [Z0105, Z0106]. **Need to know:** whether a refusal can be appealed, and whether any nationality restriction applies to Bangladeshis.

**Premium Visa Programme (PVIP).** IMI's visa of up to 20 years for investors, entrepreneurs and talent, no age limit, allowing work and study [C0034]. You need offshore income of RM40,000 a month or RM480,000 a year and a RM1 million fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank; the participation fee is RM200,000, plus RM100,000 per dependant [C0035], RM2,000 of it paid at online registration; the pass is RM2,000 a year [Z0052, Z0053]. Dependants: a spouse, children under 25 (biological, step or adopted), disabled children of any age, parents and parents-in-law, plus a domestic helper under the domestic helper rules [Z0054, Z0055]. Only IMI-appointed PVIP agencies apply, on [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/eservices/main) [C0036, Q0104], with your written power of attorney [Z0077]; check the agency is registered with IMI before paying [Q0105]. PVIP years don't count toward permanent residence [Q0015]. **Need to know:** where IMI publishes the list of appointed PVIP agencies, how long an application takes and whether a refusal can be appealed.

**PLS@XPATS.** IMI's online permission to work for professionals on a social visit pass, at [mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats](https://mtp.imi.gov.my/plsXpats); IMI's charter approves it in 3 working days [C0038, Q0106, C0048]. Emergency work may be considered by incident, need and policy [E0071]. **Need to know:** who is eligible, the fee and whether a refusal can be appealed.

**Permanent residence (Entry Permit).** Lets a foreigner live in Malaysia without time limit and work, study and run a business without another pass [E0134, Q0033]; it can be cancelled for harm to public order, security, health or morality [Q0037].

**Who can apply.** A Malaysian citizen's wife, husband or biological child under 18, and "fully foreign" nationals [Z0050, Q0016]: investors, experts and professionals in fields recognised by a Malaysian regulating agency, with 5 continuous years on an Employment, Student, Professional Visit or Residence Pass and no break over 6 months [Q0019, Q0020]. Check your points first at [app.imi.gov.my/epc](https://app.imi.gov.my/epc) [E0136]. PLKS, MM2H and PVIP holders can't apply [Q0014, Q0015].

   You'll need, as originals with copies: a passport valid 12 months; Form IM.4 (any Immigration office); old and new passports (data page and endorsed pages); the sponsor's MyKad and declaration; 2 white-background passport photos each of you and the sponsor [Q0016, Q0096]. A spouse adds the information form, the marriage certificate registered in Malaysia, a declaration that the marriage continues, MyKad copies of 2 Malaysian referees, children's birth certificates (and MyKad from 12), proof of address (a tenancy or purchase agreement or a utility bill) and the spouse LTSVP deposit receipt; a child under 18 adds the foreign parent's passport, foreign birth certificate, any Malaysian birth registration, parents' marriage certificates, proof of address and the sponsor's proof of income; a fully foreign applicant adds the information form and 2 referees' MyKad copies [Q0096]. Copies of Bangladeshi documents (passport, marriage and birth certificates) need attestation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the Bangladesh High Commission, translation into English or Malay, then attestation by a Malaysian mission or Malaysia's Foreign Ministry [Q0035].

   1. Download IMI's Entry Permit guideline e-book from [imi.gov.my/index.php/perkhidmatan-utama/permit-masuk](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/perkhidmatan-utama/permit-masuk/) [Z0101].
   2. Go with your sponsor and all originals to the nearest Immigration office [Q0022, Q0021]. A fully foreign Bangladeshi pays a RM750 deposit, refundable after 2 years on the permit [Q0093, Q0031, Q0032].

## Asylum

Asylum gives no legal right to stay or work in Malaysia [S0142, S0107]. There is no national asylum system and the Immigration Act has no provision on refugees; UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) decides claims under its own mandate [S0101, K0036], and its recognition gives no legal immigration status [S0142]. Refugees and asylum seekers are treated as undocumented migrants: they can be arrested, detained, fined and deported, and may not work [S0106, S0107]. UNHCR can't grant status or work permits or stop an arrest; its document is not a visa and at most may reduce the risk of arrest [S0108, S0111].

**Who counts.** Only someone who fled persecution or serious harm because of race, religion, nationality, social group or political opinion, or who can't return because of war or serious disorder; someone who left for work or study is a migrant [S0102].

**The process.** Apply only on UNHCR's My Services portal, reached from help.unhcr.org/malaysia ("Registering with UNHCR"): create a personal account and submit the request; you get a reference number to quote in every contact, and letters are not accepted [S0109, T0050, T0051]. Wait to be called for an interview; don't go to the Reception Centre without an appointment [T0069]. UNHCR deals first with requests from 2022 or earlier and the most vulnerable, and can't say when anyone will be interviewed [S0110, S0112, S0103]. At the interview you must tell the truth; being coached or misleading UNHCR harms the case [S0105]. A refusal can be appealed once, within 30 days; then the case closes and UNHCR's help and documents end [S0104]. An unregistered pregnant asylum seeker pays full foreigners' fees for antenatal care and childbirth at government facilities [S0113].

**Scams.** All UNHCR services are free; anyone asking money for an interview or letters is lying [S0109, S0143]. Fake UNHCR cards and work permits are sold [N0049]. Scan a card's large QR code with the free UNHCR Verify Plus app (Google Play or App Store); "Incompatible QR Code" means it is not genuine [N0046, N0047].

**Contact.** UNHCR Malaysia, 570 Jalan Bukit Petaling, 50460 Kuala Lumpur, 03 2118 4800; detention hotline +6012 630 5060 [N0048].

   - You'll need: Form 34-PA, passport and PLKS copies, your medical or sick-leave certificate, and for a road accident the original police report or a copy certified by the issuing police station [U0015, U0016]. Quote your 12-digit FWSS (Foreign Workers Social Security) number in every dealing with PERKESO [M0184].
   - Where and when: hand it in at a PERKESO counter and keep the stamped slip, or post it to the nearest PERKESO office (the form asks which office you want to deal with); head office: Menara PERKESO, 281 Jalan Ampang, 50538 Kuala Lumpur [U0022, U0060, U0071, U0074]. File within 12 months of the benefit falling due (the appeal board can extend this for good reason); incomplete, unclear or late claims can be rejected [U0064, U0022].
   - Cost: free; don't use agents or middlemen who charge [U0020]. A false claim can bring up to 2 years' jail, a fine of up to RM10,000 or both [U0023]. Progress: 1 300 22 8000 or perkeso@perkeso.gov.my [U0024].
   - If PERKESO refuses or underpays, appeal to the Social Security Appellate Board within 3 years; a Medical Board's disability assessment goes to the Appellate Medical Board [U0065, U0066, U0067].
   - **Need to know:** whether PERKESO accepts Form 34-PA without the employer's sections when the employer refuses to sign; the nearest PERKESO branch's address; how long PERKESO takes to decide; how to file an appeal to the Social Security Appellate Board.
3. **What you can get.** Temporary Disablement Benefit for medical leave of at least 4 days, counting the accident day [U0004]. For permanent disability, assessed by a Medical Board in Malaysia: up to 20% is paid as a lump sum; above 20%, one fifth can be taken as a lump sum and the rest monthly for life [U0005]. A 100% disabled worker needing constant care gets RM500 a month more, and PERKESO pays for physiotherapy, artificial limbs, wheelchairs and similar aids [U0006, M0172]. A worker certified invalid who hasn't paid Invalidity Scheme contributions long enough gets a one-time Invalidity Grant of both sides' contributions with interest [U0013]. Payments can go to a bank outside Malaysia: give the account holder, bank, address, branch, account number and SWIFT code on the form, with a bank verification letter [U0070, U0018].
4. **If the employer won't report it or pay you while you are off work,** file a case at the nearest Labour Department office (offices by state at jtksm.mohr.gov.my/ms/hubungi-kami/alamat-ibu-pejabat-dan-cawangan) with your payslips, contract and attendance record, and give a copy to the labour wing (counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, +60 12-290 3252), which pursues compensation claims [U0075, L0037, B0117, F0029]. The Labour Court works as in route 3 of "If you aren't paid"; if the employer ignores its Order, the Labour Department can seize and sell its property, take the money from its bank or prosecute it [U0029, U0030, U0076]. Sample message to the labour wing: "I am [name], passport [number], PLKS with [employer, address]. I was injured at work on [date] at [place]. My employer has not reported it to PERKESO. Please help me claim." Tenaganita and the Bar Council's Legal Aid Centres help with cases (see route 4 of "If you aren't paid") [J0007, J0047]; the ILO (International Labour Organization) does not take complaints from individual workers [G0027]. **Need to know:** whether Tenaganita charges workers for its help.
5. **Going home injured.** On your or your family's application, with proof of BMET clearance or Board membership and a passport copy, the Welfare Board asks the High Commission to bring you home [B0073]. The Board's ambulance at Dhaka and Chattogram airports is free on request [B0070].
6. **Treatment money in Bangladesh.** A returnee with BMET clearance or Board membership who is injured, sick or disabled can get up to Tk 2 lakh [B0071, B0072]. Within 1 year of return, the family applies to the Board's Director General via its web portal, the district probashi kallyan (migrant welfare) centre or the Board, with medical reports and prescriptions, a bank statement, a passport copy and a copy of its arrival stamp, proof of clearance or membership, and one 6R (6 by 8 inch) colour photo of the worker [B0071, U0092]. A Board committee checks the case by WhatsApp video call and recommends an amount; free, up to 60 working days after all papers are in [B0071, U0092].
7. **Insurance.** It pays Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability and 10% to 90% of that for partial permanent disability [B0009]; see "Claiming the insurance".

### If a worker dies

These steps are for the family in Bangladesh.

1. **Ask the Welfare Board to bring the body home.** Call 16135 (from abroad +8809610102030) [B0083]. Then apply in one of these ways, even if the worker had no BMET clearance or Board membership [F0074]:
   - on myGov (the government's online services portal), service "Application to bring home a migrant worker's body", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638167134, with a myGov account (register with your mobile number at idp-v2.live.mygov.bd/registration), the worker's passport and any clearance or membership; a tracking number comes by SMS and you can follow the case on myGov; free; up to 35 working days [U0049, U0091, U0051]; or
   - to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, which also handles undocumented workers, describing what happened and the cause of death, with proof the person was a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID, death certificate); free [B0051].
   - Sample: "My [relation], [name], passport [number], worked for [employer, phone] in [town], Malaysia, and died on [date]. We want the body brought home to [district]. The receiver is [name, phone]."
2. **Home or burial in Malaysia.** The Board passes the family's choice to the High Commission, and the family sends the High Commission a claim or consent letter naming the receiver and their phone number [B0074, B0075, L0039]. For burial in Malaysia, apply on myGov with the service "Application for permission to bury a migrant worker's body in the destination country", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638246958, with the worker's passport or visa copy, any clearance or membership, the district office's investigation report, and a certificate signed by all heirs on the union parishad (rural local council) chairman's letterhead; free, 3 working days [U0083].
3. **The Malaysian side.** The employer writes to the High Commission, appoints a funeral company and sends its letters, the insurance claim and compensation letters, the hospital letter, the death certificate, the Malaysian police report and the passport [L0038, B0119]. It pays the funeral and repatriation [M0148]. For its no-objection certificate (NOC) the High Commission also needs a police report with NOC for an abnormal death (hanging, fall, accident, murder) and the employer's PERKESO death report; for an undocumented worker, the original passport (or a police report of its loss), the receiver's details, a police report with NOC, the original death certificate, the funeral company's letter and the employer's name and phone [L0023, L0024, L0025, L0026, L0027]. The Board forwards the request within 3 to 5 working days, and the labour wing takes about 30 [U0050]. A documented worker's body usually comes home in reasonable time; an undocumented worker's can take much longer [F0073].
   - If there is no employer or it refuses, the Welfare Board pays [B0125, L0040]. PERKESO repays whoever paid (a person, company or the High Commission) up to RM4,500 of repatriation, even if the death isn't otherwise covered, on its PPJ (Funeral Repatriation Cost Claim) form, on which the employer states whether it paid, with a certified copy of the death certificate and of the claimant's ID or company registration, the original receipt, bank proof, and proof the body was sent home (the Ministry of Health's export permit or the airline's air waybill); hand in at a PERKESO counter or post [U0010, U0072, U0073].
   - Malaysian authorities trace the family through the permanent address and emergency contact in the worker's passport, so write a full name and a working phone number in the passport's emergency contact field; in 2026 a body stayed 15 days in a Kuala Lumpur morgue, at RM75 a day, because that field held only "0088" [U0088, F0068, F0069]. If no family is found, the worker may be buried in Malaysia as an unclaimed person [F0070].
   - NGO and research officials say papers from the destination country usually give the cause as illness or natural death [YB0023]; Bangladesh doesn't check it independently, and migrants say deaths are recorded as stroke or heart attack to avoid workplace-accident compensation [F0076, H0037]. If the family doubts the cause of death, apply to the Welfare Board for legal help abroad: myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638179893, at the Board, or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office (the district administration) or the district employment and manpower office (DEMO), with proof of clearance or membership and a passport or travel permit copy with arrival and departure stamps; free; sent to the labour wing within 1 to 2 days; up to 20 working days [U0085, B0077]. **Need to know:** whether and how a family can ask the Malaysian police for a post-mortem or inquest.
4. **At the airport.** The Board's desk gives a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, collectable within 3 months, and a free ambulance [B0066, B0070]. You'll need: the worker's passport copy; a heirship (warish) certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor; the NID of the wife, father or mother; the death certificate; the NOC; the original air waybill; proof of clearance or membership; and a photo and NID or passport copy of the named receiver, and of the collector if different [B0067, B0066].
5. **The Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant,** for the family of a worker with clearance or membership who died in Malaysia, the country of the clearance [B0060, B0061]. If the family took the airport cheque, no application is needed: the Board writes asking for the papers; after burial in Malaysia, apply within 1 year of the death to the Board's Director General or through the district office [B0062]. The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents; for an unmarried worker with no living parents, to full siblings [B0063].
   - You'll need: a family-members certificate on the letterhead of the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor, countersigned by the UNO (upazila nirbahi officer, the sub-district's chief officer) or, in a city corporation, the regional executive officer; an indemnity, undertaking and authorisation on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper, countersigned the same way (samples on wewb.gov.bd); the NOC or death certificate copy; a bank certificate or original statement for the receiver's account with the routing number (minor children's own account where they apply); each family member's colour photo, attested by the chairman and the district welfare centre; and the worker's passport copy [B0064, U0096].
   - Paid by bank transfer (BEFTN), with an SMS, within 60 working days of all papers reaching the Board [B0065, U0096].
6. **The insurance:** Tk 10 lakh for a death within 5 years of the smart card date, for workers who left with clearance on or after 1 January 2023 [B0085, B0009]. Cover for those who left between 19 December 2019 and 31 December 2022 lasted 2 years and has ended [U0087]. See "Claiming the insurance". **Need to know:** whether those who left between 1 January and 11 March 2023 are covered, since BMET's leaflet gives the start as 12 March 2023 [B0008].
7. **Malaysian compensation.** PERKESO pays a Dependants' Benefit for a work-related death [U0007]; a funeral benefit against receipts of up to RM7,500 for burial in Bangladesh (up to RM4,500 to whoever paid repatriation, RM3,000 to the dependants) or up to RM3,000 for burial in Malaysia [U0008, U0009]; and, if the worker died in Malaysia before 60 from any cause, a Survivors' Pension of at least RM550 a month, if Invalidity Scheme contributions were paid for at least 24 of the 40 consecutive months before the claim or two-thirds of the months since they became due [U0011, U0012, U0089].
   - You'll need: Form 34-PA, the worker's passport and work pass copies, the death certificate or burial permit, proof of relationship (marriage certificate, widow's ID and children's birth certificates; or, with no wife or child, the worker's birth certificate and parents' IDs) and bank proof, with Bangladeshi papers translated into English by an accredited translator and certified by the High Commission or a Bangladesh authority; claim within 12 months [U0015, U0017, U0018, U0019, U0064]. For an accident, the Labour Department sends its Form H to the heirs, who fill it in and return it through the High Commission [L0036, B0120].
   - How: when the High Commission reports that compensation may be due, the Welfare Board asks the family for a power of attorney and the papers, with free help from its legal cell, sends them to the High Commission and follows the case; the labour wing recovers compensation and unpaid wages, and the Board pays them to the heirs within 60 working days of having the papers and the money [B0076, U0097, B0136]. The family can also ask the Ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), which writes to the High Commission and the Board within 7 working days [B0050].
   - **Need to know:** how long PERKESO takes to pay a death claim filed through the High Commission.

### If you lose the job

1. **The employer must** tell the Labour Department within 30 days [M0065]. Under the standard contract attached to the 2021 Bangladesh–Malaysia memorandum of understanding (MoU), it gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages, pays all money owed and pays your fare home unless you broke the contract [L0144, L0152, L0142]. It sends you home with a Check Out Memo, Immigration's exit document for foreign workers [M0229]; with under 4 weeks' notice you may stay in its housing up to 4 weeks [M0205]. You can't simply move to another employer; see "If you want to change employer" [M0123].
2. **To claim money owed,** go to the Labour Court ("If you aren't paid") with your passport, the workplace's name and address, payslips and income details, appointment letter and terms of service, and any termination letter [U0029, U0030, M0113].
3. **If you were dismissed without just cause,** file a written representation to the Director General of Industrial Relations, by hand at the Industrial Relations Department (JPP) office nearest the workplace, within 60 days of the dismissal or the end of the notice period, and keep the acknowledgement; union membership isn't needed and you can represent yourself [U0077, U0078, U0081, U0080]. JPP tries to settle, then sends the case to the Industrial Court, which can award compensation only if you asked for reinstatement [U0079, U0082]. A letter showing the case is pending usually gets you a Special Pass (see route 3 of "If you aren't paid") [J0033]. **Need to know:** the nearest JPP office's address, whether the form can be filed online, and any fee.
4. **Insurance:** coming home within 6 months of going after losing the job pays Tk 50,000 [B0009, B0085]; see "Claiming the insurance".

2. **Apply.** For a death, nothing is needed if the body came through the airport desk; after burial in Malaysia or death after return, apply at portal.wewb.gov.bd or at the Board [U0095]. For disability or a lost job, apply to the Board's welfare section: Deputy Director, +88 02 48317085, 01321-158354, dd.welfare2@wewb.gov.bd (disability); Assistant Director, 01712-107301, jahanwewb@gmail.com (lost job) [U0094, U0093].

Free; 30 working days [U0093, U0094, U0095]. **Need to know:** how Jiban Bima Corporation pays the money (bank transfer or cheque).
4. **If it stalls** (families have waited months, with delays blamed on Jiban Bima Corporation and the Board [U0025]), complain to the Board's grievance officer, +88 02 55138267, 01684-501891, azizwelfare@gmail.com (30 working days); then the Ministry's Joint Secretary (Administration), +88 02 41030261, jsadmin@probashi.gov.bd (20 working days); then grs.gov.bd, the Cabinet Division's grievance system [U0098]. The Ministry's complaint cell also takes email at minister.expat@gmail.com [R0012].

## Coming home

1. **Leaving Malaysia with cash.** You may carry any amount of foreign currency, and ringgit up to the equivalent of USD 10,000; above USD 10,000 in total, declare it on Customs form No. 22, which is available before the customs checkpoint, or face a fine of up to RM1 million, up to a year in jail or both [U0032, U0033, U0034].
2. **Customs in Bangladesh.** Duty-free: 65 kg of baggage, 2 used phones and 1 new phone a year, and once a year, if declared, 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [U0039, U0035, U0036]. Once a year you may bring a declared gold bar up to 117 g (10 tola; duty Tk 5,000 per tola) or silver bar up to 234 g; a bigger or hidden bar is confiscated [U0037, B0112]. No alcohol on a Bangladeshi passport [U0038]. Declare foreign currency over USD 10,000 on the FMJ form [B0115]. Customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 [B0116]. **Need to know:** whether BMET card holders who have been abroad at least 6 months may bring 2 new phones a year duty-free.
3. **Airport help.** The Welfare Board's desks help with boarding cards, customs and lost luggage, take complaints of abuse or cheating, and send sick returnees to hospital by ambulance; Dhaka desk +8802 8901040, 01404 413388 [B0068, B0069, B0070]. Women returning within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 cash [B0038].
4. **A place to stay.** The Wage Earners' Centre (Lonjonipara, Borua, Khilkhet, Dhaka-1229, near Shahjalal airport): Tk 100 admission, Tk 200 a night; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd or in person with proof of clearance or membership, a passport copy and the ticket; 01310 350555, 01754 715720 (24 hours) [B0080]. The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives free wifi and phone calls and food at up to 30% off when you show proof of clearance or membership [B0082].
5. **If your family is threatened or its property seized,** apply to the Welfare Board for legal help (myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638181581, the Board, the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office or the DEMO) with the worker's passport copy and any clearance or membership; free, up to 3 working days; the Board writes to the local administration, such as the Deputy Commissioner or Superintendent of Police [U0084, B0078, B0132].
6. **A business loan.** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB, the government bank for migrants) lends returnees who start a project up to Tk 50 lakh, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral, at 9% simple interest over up to 10 years; apply at the PKB branch for the business's area on its free form [B0021, B0022, U0055, U0052].
   - You'll need: 3 passport photos; passport and NID copies; a certificate of your present and permanent address from the pourashava (municipality) or union parishad; a current trade licence; project details with 2 years' income and expenses; the premises' lease or ownership papers; a declaration of your own investment and other loans; copies of your return papers; 3 signed cheque leaves from the business account; and a guarantor (a relative or anyone able to repay) with 2 photos, ID copy, address certificate, a clean CIB report (Bangladesh Bank's credit report showing no unpaid loans) and 3 signed cheque leaves [U0053, U0054, U0056].
   - Fees: 0.50% processing and 0.50% documentation, each capped at Tk 15,000, each plus VAT, paid when you take the loan; no service charge [U0090]. Decision within 15 working days of a complete application [B0022]. Helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018].
7. **BRAC's help.** BRAC's Migration Programme gives returnees and trafficking survivors airport help at Dhaka through its Migration Welfare Centre, counselling, money or materials to start earning, and legal help [B0122, U0047]. **Need to know:** how a returnee contacts it.
8. **Children's scholarships.** The Welfare Board pays migrants' children scholarships (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [B0131]. **Need to know:** how to apply.
