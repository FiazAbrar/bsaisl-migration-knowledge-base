# Findings, part D: tests and documents (practical pass)

Researched 6 Nov 2026. Ledger ids are in `ledger-parts/D.csv` (D0001-D0066). 30 sources registered; 15 rows official, 51 news/guide. Every news row is practice context, "unofficial, not verified", and is never the basis of a fee, eligibility, document, processing-time or legal rule. Official rows: Bangladesh e-passport portal (DIP, D0049-D0058), CIDB (D0040-D0042), FWCMS (D0019), BMET (D0064).

Key terms (explain inline in the doc): **MiGRAMS** = Bestinet's online registration portal for the Malaysia pre-departure health screening; the "MiGRAMS slip" (written "Migram slip" or "medical slip" in news) is the registration slip an agency makes before the worker can be screened [D0002, D0024]. **FOMEMA** = the company that runs the post-arrival medical for Malaysia's Health Ministry. **CLAB** = Construction Labour Exchange Centre Bhd, CIDB's subsidiary that handles foreign construction workers [D0036, D0042]. **SKKP** = Sijil Kecekapan Kemahiran Pembinaan, CIDB's Construction Skills Competency Certificate [D0038].

## Step 2: Medical check in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical)

**Current round (Oct-Nov 2026), all from news reports of Ministry statements (no official text found online):**
- Medicals for Malaysia started 31 Oct 2026 at approved centres; any medical done before 31 Oct is not accepted and not processed for clearance [D0007, D0013].
- The ministry said its own list of approved centres would be on its website and could change with compliance and capacity [D0007]. **Not found on probashi.gov.bd** on 6 Nov 2026 (notice search for "মেডিকেল" returns only 2022-2023 notices). Jugantor (6 Nov) reports FWCMS now shows 51 accredited Bangladeshi centres with addresses [D0014], consistent with official W0009 (51 on fwcms.com.my/affiliates).
- The 15 Oct release (Jugantor): Malaysia's existing list was used after removing "controversial" centres; Bangladeshi authorities will regularly check the listed centres and close any with irregularities; new centres added in phases [D0020; same release as W0013].
- **Fee:** Tk 8,000 per worker, no more; no fee for the MiGRAMS slip; don't use brokers [D0001, D0002, D0006]. Jugantor says the notice was posted on the Ministry's Facebook page [D0004]. Doc must write this as "Need to know ... reported unofficially" (fee = exact fact; no official source).
- **Choice of centre and switching agency:** no centre works under a particular agency; a selected worker may test at any approved centre; if the worker moves to another agency, the agency that made the slip must send the report without delay, no excuse accepted [D0002, D0003, D0005].
- **Complaints:** email secretary@probashi.gov.bd with the date, place, names of people/organisations and evidence (overcharging, harassment, delay, unauthorised medicals) [D0003, D0008, D0012]. Penalties: centre closure, suspension of the agency's recruitment, cancellation of approval [D0011].
- **Order of steps (conflict):** the 28 Oct release says agencies applying for BMET clearance send the documents and the VDR papers to the approved centre for the test [D0009], which implies the medical comes after VDR approval. Official FWCMS pages put the Bio-Medical before the VDR (W0009, W0058). D0009 marked conflict. Need to know: the actual order in 2026.
- **Fake/unauthorised medicals:** brokers and agencies were collecting passports and money promising medicals before approval; workers told not to hand over passport or money without confirming official approval [D0008]. On 1 Oct 2026 a mobile court sealed Human Diagnostic and Medical Center (Box Culvert Road, Paltan), which had tested and issued reports to 14 Malaysia-bound people without government authorisation and used two signboards [D0017, D0018]. **The same centre is on the FWCMS accredited list** (102/1/A New Paltan) [D0019, D0015]: FWCMS listing alone did not mean Bangladesh authorisation. Doc implication: check the centre is on the Ministry's list (when published), not only FWCMS.
- **What happened last round (practice):** BAIRA says the fixed fee was Tk 6,500 but agencies took Tk 11,000; at least 5 times as many people did the medical as actually went [D0021, D0022] (TBS, Sep 2025). Gulf-centre practice of "unfit first, then bribe for fit" [D0023] (not Malaysia-specific; context only).
- **MiGRAMS (background, 2023):** the worker must be registered on MiGRAMS (RM100, paid by the agency via a top-up e-wallet) before the screening; the health report is needed before the calling visa; re-screening meant paying RM100 again [D0024, D0025]. Whether the RM100 still applies in 2026 is unknown (the 2026 notice only says the slip is free to the worker, D0002).
- **Agencies' pressure:** on 5 Nov 2026 agencies asked the minister to speed medical approvals and set a "reasonable" BOESL processing fee [D0016].

## Step 3: VDR / calling visa, visa sticker, and fake visas

- Real route is official (W0045, W0061-W0065). Practice:
- **Fake visas:** a Narsingdi man promised Italy (Tk 21.98 lakh) was sent in June 2025 with fake Malaysian and French visas pasted in his passport; Malaysian airport immigration detected them and sent him back within 4 days [D0048] (PBI case, Oct 2026). A Bangladeshi couple in KL sold forged European visas (up to RM20,000) to clients in Bangladesh, with fake immigration stamps and forged immigration slips [D0043, D0044].
- **How to check:** official checks already in W0065 (MyIMMs status), W0066 (ePass). No news source gives a worker-facing way to check a VDR sticker. Need to know stays.

## Step 4: Police clearance, passport, certificates

- **Police clearance:** no Malaysia-specific practice found (searched English and Bangla news, guides). Only generic visa-guide sites list a PCC (not used). Need to know remains: whether Malaysia requires a PCC for PLKS workers.
- **Passport (official, DIP, verified):** apply online; no attestation of papers or photo [D0053]. Fill it per NID or online BRC (English): under 18 BRC; 18-20 NID or BRC; over 20 NID compulsory (BRC only at a mission abroad) [D0054]. Apply at the divisional/regional passport office for your present address, or a mission abroad [D0055]; bring original NID/BRC and any technical certificates [D0056]. Fees inside Bangladesh, 48 pages incl. VAT: 5 years Tk 4,025 / 6,325 / 8,625 (regular / express / super express); 10 years Tk 5,750 / 8,050 / 10,350 [D0049]. Pay online (A-Challan, DGePay, ShurjoPay: cards, bKash, Nagad, Rocket) or at any bank via A-Challan [D0051]. Delivery 15 / 7 / 2 working days from biometric enrolment [D0050]. Lost passport: GD at the nearest police station, then re-apply with old passport photocopy and GD [D0057]. At missions, workers and students pay USD 30/45 (5 yrs) or USD 50/75 (10 yrs) for 48 pages [D0052]. Under-18s get only a 5-year, 48-page passport [D0058].

## Age (doctoring)

- IMI age 18-45 (W0073); job-order ranges 18-35, 22-35 etc. (W0029, W0003). The passport's birth date comes from the NID/BRC (D0054), so age is set by the NID/BRC.
- Practice: Perak Labour Department rescued four Bangladeshi boys aged 14-16 who had worked in a factory for over a year; employer detained under ATIPSOM Act s.14 for employing underage workers [D0065, D0066] (Malay Mail, 2024). No source found on how they entered or on age-doctoring for the Malaysia corridor.
- **Need to know:** any official Bangladeshi or Malaysian warning on falsified ages/NIDs for Malaysia (searched Bangla "বয়স জালিয়াতি", "জন্মনিবন্ধন জালিয়াতি", English "fake age passport Malaysia"; nothing Malaysia-specific).

## Step 6: BMET registration, clearance and smart card

- The Tk 250 smart card fee and Tk 250 modification fee were abolished (Ministry letter 1 Dec 2024, as reported); QR-code exit clearance is free and online [D0059, D0060]. Doc: "Need to know: official confirmation" or cite B-part official source if one exists.
- Fingerprints: BMET (Apr 2023, official) let Ami Probashi supply biometric fingerprint devices to all TTCs, IMTs and DEMOs for digital clearance [D0064]. A 2026 travel guide says fingerprinting is done in person there, not online; status/card download by passport number; no card = no exit at the airport [D0061, D0062, D0063] (consistent with official B0038).

## Step 7: After arrival: FOMEMA

- Official: within 30 days at a FOMEMA clinic; unfit = sent home (L0042, L0043, W0047). Renewal: fit result for 2nd and 3rd year (L0049).
- **Frequency conflict:** employer groups say since 16 Dec 2023 FOMEMA requires a yearly screening [D0026, conflict with L0049].
- **Fee (unofficial):** RM207 men / RM217 women since Dec 2023 (news 2024-2025) [D0026, D0030]; an employer guide gives RM191.40 / RM201.40 [D0032]. Refund request RM27 [D0031]. FOMEMA's site is blocked (other parts' blocked lists). Need to know: official fee.
- **Who pays:** employer groups describe the fee as an employer cost (about RM414 million a year) [D0029, D0030]; an employer guide says the employer must pay [D0032]. No source found on deductions from Bangladeshi workers' wages for FOMEMA.
- **Tests added Dec 2023:** filariasis, hepatitis C, methamphetamine on top of HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis, pregnancy, opiates, cannabis [D0030]. FOMEMA rejects workers with diabetes, hypertension or high cholesterol (employer group's complaint) [D0027].
- **Results:** FOMEMA gives no copies of reports to workers or employers [D0028, D0029]; employer sees status on the FOMEMA portal in 5-7 working days [D0032]. Worker brings original passport and the printed FOMEMA form [D0033]. Unfit = employer must repatriate [D0034].
- **Appeal:** no source found (searched "Fomema appeal", "rayuan Fomema", Bangla "ফোমেমা আনফিট"). Need to know.
- **Fraud tried by migrants:** a syndicate sold 200-300 altered Bangladeshi passports a month to Bangladeshis who had failed FOMEMA so they could re-sit it; 22 seized (Feb 2026) [D0035].

## CIDB skill test / construction

- Official BMET notice (B0001, possibly outdated): CIDB test at Khulna TTC, to expand. No 2026 Bangladesh-side CIDB test news found.
- Practice 2025-26 (CLAB, NST Feb 2026): of 3,000 left-behind construction workers interviewed, about 1,000 rejected (health, suitability); the 2,000 passed got 12 days' basic construction training, then a Bangladesh government certificate; after arrival CLAB trains them for CIDB's SKKP [D0036-D0039].
- Official CIDB: LKK skills training open to local and foreign construction workers aged 18-55 at Akademi Binaan Malaysia [D0040, D0041]; foreign construction worker applications go through CLAB [D0042].

## Scams with documents (both ways) and What can get you jailed

- Fake e-PLKS copies sold for RM150 and fake police reports for RM70 in KL (27 Bangladeshis held, Sep 2026) [D0045].
- "A-to-B" quota fraud: quota approved with fake company papers; workers brought on one employer's PLKS and supplied to other firms; 265 Bangladeshi passports seized (Oct 2026) [D0046, D0047]. Doc implication: a worker who ends up with a different employer is working illegally (link to L-part rules).
- Forged FOMEMA passports [D0035]; fake visas sold in Bangladesh [D0043, D0048].

## Draft steps (for the doc writer)

**Step 2 (medical in Bangladesh):**
1. Wait until your agency (or BOESL) has selected you and made your MiGRAMS ("medical") slip; the slip is free (unofficial, not verified) [D0002].
2. Go only to a centre on the Ministry's approved list (not yet found online: Need to know) and on fwcms.com.my/affiliates (W0009); being on FWCMS alone isn't enough: a listed centre was sealed for unauthorised Malaysia tests in Oct 2026 [D0017, D0019]. **Step 4 (passport):** apply online at epassport.gov.bd per your NID (over 20), pay Tk 4,025 (5 years) or Tk 5,750 (10 years) for 48 pages regular delivery online or at a bank via A-Challan, attend biometrics at the passport office for your present address with original NID, collect in 15 working days [D0049-D0056].
