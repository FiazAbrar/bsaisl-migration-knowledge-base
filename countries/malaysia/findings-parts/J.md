## Reality checks (detail behind the doc's lines)

- **Labour Court in practice** (Bar Council guide, 2021) [J0036-J0046]: complaints free at any state Labour Office, by letter, email, in person or phone (Telekerja); no lawyers; documents demanded (passport, contract, payslip, arrival card, termination letter) though the Act only needs a statement; worker must attend every date; employers delay until the pass expires; MOHR figures: ~50% settled at mention, ~20% go to hearing, ~85% of those won by the worker, 38% withdrawn or dismissed for non-attendance; average 34 days (negotiated) and 84 days (hearing). Complaints page and hotline not in Bengali. NGOs help gather documents.
- **SUHAKAM** (Malaysia's human rights commission, submission to UNGA 80th session, undated, uses Sep 2024 data) [J0065-J0070]: false absconding reports near contract end and after complaints; 40% of WFW grievances about unpaid wages/illegal deductions; workers lack payslips/contracts; passport confiscation routine; contract substitution on arrival.
- **Fortify Rights / Migrant Welfare Network, 30 Sep 2026** [J0057-J0060]: visa cancelled after a wage protest; permit not renewed then employer denied employing the worker, 46 days in Muar depot; RM100/month housing deduction and RM500 for uniforms; agency videos falsely recording the official fee (Tk 79,000) when workers paid ~Tk 5.5-5.7 lakh.
- **High Commission** (Jagonews, Mar 2026; Jugantor, Sep 2026; Jagonews Aug 2026) [F0018-F0025]: Esstar Vision case: complaint by email 16 Jan 2026, first Labour Department hearing 28 Jan with a High Commission officer present, company meeting 11 Feb, visit 8-9 Mar, still unpaid mid-March. HC says 330+ of ~450 complaints since 2022 resolved; RM11.42 million recovered on 2,857 claims in 2025. NSU policy brief: no dedicated helpline, HC slow (A0111).
- **WFW**: launched May 2021; minister said complaints handled in 3-7 days; 17 complaint categories; registration with passport number (AJobThing, May 2021) [J0003-J0006]. No independent data on WFW resolution rates for migrants found; SUHAKAM figure above is the only one.
- **Tenaganita**: handled ~125 unpaid-wage/forced-labour cases in 2025 (Instagram snippet only, not saved); helped 74 Bangladeshi workers win RM1.54 million at the KL labour office (A0086).
- **Bar Council Legal Aid Centres** [J0045-J0051]: free advice to non-citizens, settlement help, rarely court representation; all state centres in raw/malaysianbar-legal-aid-pamphlet-2026.txt (Johor +607 223 5698, Selangor +603 5510 7007, Penang, etc.).
- **ILO/IOM**: searched ILO site for individual complaints; ILO procedures are for states and organisations, not individual workers (no quotable page saved). No IOM Malaysia individual-case channel found. Kept as "Need to know" in the doc. Bar guide lists the people who actually advise migrants: NGO staff, legal-aid volunteers, embassy labour officers, unions [J0083].
