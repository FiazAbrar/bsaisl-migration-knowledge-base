## Work visa (PLKS), Step 7: After arrival

**SIM card (official, verified, MCMC FAQ on Determination No. 1 of 2026, PDF dated 26 Feb 2026).** Every prepaid SIM user must register, any nationality [F0001]. A foreign worker brings the original passport plus the work permit identification document; photocopies, photos or scans are refused [F0002, F0003]. Registration is done in person at a telco service centre or authorised dealer, on a passport reader with facial recognition [F0013]; nobody else may register for you [F0004]. Limit: 2 prepaid SIMs per telco for non-Malaysians [F0005]. Do not use a SIM that is already active before you register [F0006]. Activation status comes within 12 hours [F0007]. Never give your passport photo, ID or OTP to anyone offering to "help" register; each telco has a free check of all SIMs registered in your name [F0009, F0010]. Tourists' SIMs end after 3 months [F0008]. This closes the L part's SIM "Need to know" (its news lead on 2 SIMs and the biometric passport reader is now confirmed officially). Need to know: whether the "work permit identification document" means the i-Kad or the PLKS sticker in the passport (the FAQ doesn't say).

**Bank account (Maybank's own product page, undated, rates effective 1 June 2026, so possibly outdated).** Foreigners open a savings account at a Maybank branch with a passport; a valid work permit, valid visa and an employer confirmation letter may be required [F0011]; you can also open one at any Maybank branch [F0012]. Minimum initial deposit RM250 [F0014]. Online opening in the MAE app needs a MyKad scan, so it is not open to foreign workers [F0015]. Official rule already in doc: wages must go into an account in the worker's name and the High Commission expects the employer to open it [L0132, L0104, L0034]. Need to know: Bank Negara Malaysia's rule on which ID banks must accept from PLKS holders and any basic-account product for foreign workers (searched bnm.gov.my via WebSearch; no page found; BSN, CIMB and RHB pages not found). The employer letter in [F0011] means the worker in practice depends on the employer to open the account.

**Health care.** Official: renewal needs the SPIKPA slip [L0046]. Practice (news): public hospitals take a deposit from foreigners before treatment [F0063]; a worker covered by the government foreign workers' health insurance scheme may be exempt from the deposit [F0066]; the emergency "Lampiran A" form only delays payment, it does not waive it [F0067]; employers must pay SPIKPA in the PLKS application and can have bank accounts cut off if they leave workers' hospital bills unpaid [F0065, F0064]. Need to know: an official MOH page on foreigner deposits and SPIKPA cover (not searched on moh.gov.my in this pass; the L part lists SPIKPA as a gap).

**Passport.** Official rule: the employer may not keep it [L0149]. Practice: passport retention "remains widespread" and only 15 people were convicted for it in the US TIP 2026 period [F0084]; workers at Mentakab (Aug 2026) and Kawaguchi say their passports were kept [F0032, F0045]; Aerofoam workers got passports back after complaining to the authorities [F0042]. UN experts (Nov 2025) list confiscated passports among common abuses [F0086].

## Your rights at work (what happens in practice)

**Real pay, hours and deductions.** Mentakab workers (Aug 2026) say they work up to 12 hours a day for RM65 a day against 8-hour contracts, with no regular weekly day off [F0031]; they say they spent RM16,000-18,000 each to get there [F0035]; the company is said to demand a RM2,500 deposit before annual leave [F0033]. Aerofoam workers report flight-ticket deposits, hostel deductions and no proper payslips [F0039]. The US TIP 2026 says some employers deduct the levy from wages, often without telling the worker [F0082] (official rule: the employer pays the levy [L0068]; deductions capped at 50% only to recover advances [L0137]). These are workers' claims, not rulings.

**Housing.** Official standard: Act 446 (text in raw/lom-act446, other part). Practice: many employers still use non-compliant "hostels" six years after the amendments [F0060]; a PPR flat meant for 5 can hold 8-10 workers [F0061]; some employers rent empty units and leave workers to buy beds and kitchen items [F0062]; about 35 Aerofoam workers lived in shipping containers [F0040].

**Fees and debt.** US TIP 2026: some Bangladeshis paid up to about USD 7,200 before arrival [F0085]; UN experts: some were pressured to sign false declarations that they paid only official fees, some were asked for extra payments or moved to other jobs without consent [F0087, F0088].

## If you aren't paid (complaint routes in practice)

**Official JTK route (JTKSM leaflet "Masalah di tempat kerja", printed Oct 2017, possibly outdated).** Free advice: 03-8000 8000, jtksm@mohr.gov.my, or the nearest labour office [F0093]. A complaint is made in writing to the labour office with your name and address, the employer's name and address, and the issues and documents [F0094]. JTK investigates; if valid it issues a correction notice to the employer, then prosecution or a compound if ignored; you are told the result [F0095]. It covers no contract, late/unpaid wages, below-minimum pay, no payslip, unpaid overtime, illegal deductions, dismissal, excess hours, no rest, no leave [F0096]. A wage claim (Labour Court, s.69) is made in person with a sworn statement before an officer [F0097, F0103]; bring passport, employer's name and address (from a Companies Commission search), payslips, appointment letter and termination letter if any [F0104, F0098]. Steps: statement, mention date, notice to you and summons to the employer, hearing, order [F0100]. The Labour Court is free [F0099]. Appeal to the High Court within 14 days of the decision [F0101]. If the employer ignores the order, JTK can use a judgment debtor summons, garnishment, prosecution and a writ of seizure and sale [F0102]. Note: the leaflet's RM2,000-5,000 wage bands predate the 2022 amendment; check against the Act text (raw/jtksm-employment-act-1955-act265-2023, other part).

**What happens in practice.**
- Esstar Vision, Johor (2026): 85 workers emailed the High Commission on 16 Jan [F0018]; they filed with JTK and a High Commission officer attended the hearing on 28 Jan [F0019]; the High Commission met the company, which promised instalments, and visited the site [F0020]; in mid-March, after media reports, JTK found wages delayed Sep 2025-Feb 2026 and opened 14 case files worth RM636,414.65 [F0016, F0017]; workers asked for a release letter to find other work [F0021].
- Aerofoam (Jul 2026): 109 workers filed JTK claims with legal help; passports returned after complaints; but permits expired while the claims ran, leaving them open to arrest [F0038, F0043, F0042, F0041].
- Mentakab (Aug 2026): workers told the High Commission and JTK; no official had visited [F0034]; a complaint to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry said some workers with valid permits were sent home against their will and food was stopped [F0036, F0037].
- Kawaguchi (2025): a labour tribunal ordered over RM3 million, but workers had got RM251,000 [F0044]; wages withheld up to 8 months before closure [F0046].
- Mediceram (Nov 2025): about 180 workers who protested visa non-renewal were sacked; 10 were deported after company executives took them to the airport; 152 hired lawyers and asked to be transferred [F0047, F0048, F0049].
- High Commission figures: about 450 complaints since 2022, over 330 resolved by mediation [F0022, F0023]; RM11.42 million recovered on 2,857 claims in 2025 and RM5.89 million on 1,381 claims Jan-Jun 2026, mostly work-injury (PERKESO-type) benefits [F0025, F0026, F0027, F0028]. Its labour minister says many workers do not know their compensation rights [F0029]; community leaders cite language, paperwork and middlemen [F0030]; a worker says many don't know where to go [F0024].
- US TIP 2026: labour courts resolved 28,992 disputes and ordered RM70 million in back wages in the period [F0083].
- Existing rows: agents warn workers not to complain [L0192]; in 2023 the mission took a case to the HR ministry, which summoned the employer [L0193].

Pattern for the doc (stated as reports, not advice): complaints that reached JTK with the High Commission involved led to hearings and case files; payment came slowly or partly; permits can expire during the case; protest can lead to dismissal, deportation or arrest [F0048, F0058].

## If you want to change employer

**Conflict.** Official IMI pages (undated) say PLKS holders may not change employer or sector [L0055, W0052], or only with Home Ministry permission [L0067]. News (May 2025) reports the Home and HR ministries decided to allow cross-sector transfers [F0077]; before that, transfers were allowed within the same sector on restructuring, closure, expansion or a JTK committee's approval [F0078]; the HR minister said guidelines would cover a minimum period, worker consent and five formal sectors [F0080]; the US TIP 2026 says the policy exists but written consent of all parties limits it [F0081]. Status `conflict` on F0077, F0078, F0080, F0081 with notes naming L0055/W0052/L0067. Official process document already in raw: `raw/jtksm-proses-kerja-ptm-pdg` (JTKSM "Pertukaran Majikan" flow: both employers register on ePPAx, the original employer applies and picks the new employer, the new employer uploads documents within 7 days, the branch labour office investigates, JTKSM decides, the new employer finishes at the One Stop Centre). Not logged by me (other part). Need to know: the official 2025 cross-sector transfer guideline (searched news only; jtksm.mohr.gov.my not reached in this pass).

In practice, workers stuck with non-paying employers ask for a "release letter" [F0021] or a transfer [F0049].

## If your employer reports you absent (lari)

Practice: workers who entered legally, were cheated and left the employer have been detained as illegal migrants (171 in Johor, Dec 2023) [F0057 (2023)]. Need to know: an official way to check or challenge a false absconding report (searched English and Bangla news; none found; the Indian High Commission's page shows a mission-side practice of putting a worker's emergency-certificate on hold when an employer reports absconding, saved as raw/hcikl-absconding-workers but not logged, as it concerns Indian workers).

## What can get you jailed: raids and depots

Practice: night raids on worker hostels check papers and detain those without valid documents or permits [F0050] and send them to depots such as Belantik (Kedah) [F0051]; people smuggled in via a third country and Kelantan paid up to RM15,000 and were held at the Putrajaya depot under the anti-smuggling law [F0052, F0053]. Home Minister: 465 depot deaths in 2021-2025 out of 349,856 admissions, Bangladeshis among the smaller groups [F0054]; detainees may contact family by phone, letter or visit with the depot commander's approval [F0055]. Fortify Rights: depots over capacity (22,045 against 21,530), 140 deaths since 2024 [F0056]; a Bangladeshi former detainee says people are afraid to report sick [F0092]. HRW: about 18,000 held in immigration detention [F0089]. Existing official rows on depots: L0180, L0181; release by Travel Permit through the camp: L0017, L0018.

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Practice: Malaysia had the second-highest number of Bangladeshi worker deaths in 2026 after Saudi Arabia [F0072]. Bodies of documented workers come home in reasonable time; undocumented workers' can take long [F0073]. Families can apply to the Welfare Board even without clearance or membership [F0074] (official: B0051, B0052). Families report repeated visits to the Board [F0075]; causes of death are usually recorded as natural and not verified [F0076]. A Kuala Lumpur case (Aug 2026): a body stayed 15 days in a morgue at RM75 a day because the passport's emergency contact held only "0088" and no family could be traced; if none is found he may be buried in Malaysia as unclaimed; the labour wing searched his past workplaces [F0068, F0069, F0070, F0071]. High Commission recoveries include dependants' benefit, funeral and repatriation costs [F0028]. This supports telling workers to keep a working family phone number in the passport and with the High Commission (the doc should phrase this as what the case shows, not our advice).

## Malaysia as a destination

Jun 2026: the two prime ministers agreed to clean up recruitment and cut middlemen; hiring of Bangladeshis restricted since 2024 [F0091]. Aug 2026: a state minister said Malaysia would take about 200,000 workers over six months, 10,000 at no cost (in raw/news-bss-419282; only F0090 logged).

## Draft steps (for the doc writer)

Step 7 additions:
1. **Get a SIM in your own name.** Go yourself to a telco service centre or authorised dealer with your original passport and work permit ID; the dealer scans your passport and face; no copies; up to 2 prepaid SIMs per telco; you hear within 12 hours; don't accept a SIM that is already active; never hand your passport photo or OTP to a "helper"; check free with your telco which numbers are in your name [F0001-F0010, F0013].
2. **Open your own bank account.** At a bank branch with your passport; Maybank may ask for your work permit, visa and an employer confirmation letter; RM250 opening deposit at Maybank [F0011, F0012, F0014, F0015]; wages must go into your account [L0132, L0104].
3. **Hospital.** Public hospitals take a deposit from foreigners; insured foreign workers may be exempt (unofficial, not verified) [F0063, F0066].

If you aren't paid:
1. Write down hours, keep payslips, contract and appointment/termination letters [F0104].
2. Complain on WFW [L0163] or go to the nearest labour office / call 03-8000 8000; give a sworn statement for a Labour Court claim; it is free; appeal within 14 days; JTK can enforce an ignored order [F0093-F0102].
3. Tell the labour wing at the High Commission (contacts L0021, B0117); in 2026 cases its officers attended JTK hearings and called employers in [F0019, F0020]; it reports about 330 of 450 complaints resolved [F0023].
4. Practice warnings, labelled unofficial: cases take months and payment may be partial [F0016, F0044]; permits can expire meanwhile, so ask who is renewing yours [F0041]; protests have led to dismissal, deportation and arrest [F0047, F0048, F0058].
