## PERKESO (Malaysian social security) for foreign workers
- Live perkeso.gov.my blocks curl and Firecrawl (tunnel error). Wayback copies used: the foreign-worker page (capture 6 Jun 2026; page itself undated but describes the Non-Employment Injury Scheme from 1 Jun 2026) and the Form 34-PA checklist (file dated 10 Jul 2025, capture 17 Oct 2026).
- Funeral benefit is now RM7,500 (RM4,500 repatriation + RM3,000 dependants) or RM3,000 if buried in Malaysia (U0008, U0009). The old draft's 2019 figures (RM6,500 / RM2,000, M0187) are superseded; PP.U.(A) 181/2024 amended the funeral benefit amount. RM4,500 repatriation reimbursement for non-covered deaths is still current (U0010, same as M0177 from 2022).
- Employer must report a work accident immediately or within 30 days (U0021). Secondary sources (dataon.com, HR forums, an MBSJ PERKESO slide) say 48 hours; those apply to local-worker forms and were not saved. Not used.
- Claim deadline: search results show PERKESO's local-worker scheme page and brochure saying 12 months from the last day of temporary disablement, and the MBSJ slide says 12 months from the last sick-leave certificate for permanent disablement. Neither the live page nor a Wayback copy could be saved, and nothing confirms it for foreign workers, so it stays a Need to know.
- Survivors' and Invalidity pensions need at least 24 monthly contributions (U0012). The Invalidity Scheme started 1 Jul 2024, so few Bangladeshi workers can qualify before mid-2026.
- Form 34-PA acknowledgement slip is addressed to the employer or the insured worker (U0060), so a worker can hand the claim in. No source says PERKESO takes a claim from a worker whose employer refuses to sign; the Labour Court and labour wing route is given instead.

## Reality checks
- Insurance (Bonik Barta, reprinted by Gulf Bangla, 27 Jul 2026): settlement rate 56.11% against premiums to April 2026; about 2,100 claims worth Tk 69.73 crore unpaid by June 2026; the Welfare Board stopped paying premiums to Jiban Bima Corporation Feb-Jun 2026 over a Tk 22.81 crore dispute; software (MIS) mismatch caused a backlog. WEWB DG said it would be fixed "this week" (U0025-U0028). Settlement-rate and premium figures kept here only.
- Bodies: documented workers' bodies come home in reasonable time, undocumented take long (F0073); morgue stays 15-18 days at RM75/day, community-funded RM7,000 (F0068, F0069, H0033); 13 at government cost and 584 paid by employers, companies and relatives Jan-Jun 2026 (H0024). Cause of death usually recorded as illness; Bangladesh does not verify (F0076, H0037).
- Compensation: HC labour wing recovered RM11.42m on 2,857 claims in 2025, mostly temporary disablement (F0025, F0027); many workers don't know their rights (F0029).
- Job loss: group dismissals after protests (Mediceram, F0047, A0108); 2024 estimate of 100,000-200,000 jobless and unpaid Bangladeshis (L0191, dropped from doc as an old estimate that changes no action).
- Who takes individual cases: Tenaganita yes (case management, J0007); Bar Council Legal Aid Centres give non-citizens free advice, representation limited (J0045, J0047); ILO representation procedure is for associations only (G0027). IOM: no evidence found that it takes individual compensation cases in Malaysia; not mentioned. BRAC: encourages returnees to file cases and gives legal help (B0122).
- J-rows (Tenaganita, Bar Council) come from the parallel part J and were checked against tenaganita-contact.md and the legal-aid pamphlet sources.

## Malaysia as a destination
- Official comparison found: Malaysian Parliament written answer No. 64, 10 Feb 2026: 2,125,587 PLKS holders at 31 Dec 2025; Bangladesh 798,973 (37.59%), Indonesia 551,337, Nepal 323,601, Myanmar 166,259, India 100,662 (U0057). Replaces the old draft's news-only PRM 2.0 comparisons (H0006, H0007), which disagreed with each other. Same answer: 130,603 registered for the repatriation programme and 51,100 arrested in 2025 (U0058).
- Closures explained (news only; no official Malaysian statement saved): 2009 closure over syndicate irregularities, reopened end of 2016, closed Sept 2018, reopened 2022 (U0041, Daily Star 2024 citing Prothom Alo); 21 Mar 2024 announcement of the 31 May 2024 stop (U0042). The Edge (via andyjhall.org) says the Home Minister suspended intake in May 2024 over security concerns and workers arriving without jobs; not saved, not used.
- 2026 reopening: State Minister (Kaler Kantho, 28 Oct 2026): BOESL first at an estimated Tk 1.55-1.60 lakh, then private agencies; demand letters need High Commission attestation (U0043, U0044). He also said sending might start in the first week of November; not in the doc, because it is a forecast.
- Malaysia's investment minister (Jugantor, 4 Nov 2026): about 2.6 million foreign workers; hiring to be limited mainly to 3D jobs; employers applying for non-3D posts must state pay and qualifications (U0045, U0046). "Which sectors are hiring Bangladeshis now" is answered by W0041/W0042, the BOESL DHL circular and CLAB. The old Need to know was dropped. No list of 2026 job orders by sector has been published (BMET permission list has no 2026 Malaysia entries, G0030).
- The old draft's NTL (2023) and PRM 2.0 nationality-ranking lines were dropped (old or superseded by Hansard). The 2025 OEP live-report figures for 2023/2024 (B0086, B0087) conflict slightly with BMET's annual table (B0091); the annual table is used for 2023 and 2024.
