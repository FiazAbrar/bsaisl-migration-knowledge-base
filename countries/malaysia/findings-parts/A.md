## Where things stand (context for the "Is recruitment open now?" paragraph)

- **The 2026 arrangement.** 338 entities: 25 principal agencies chosen by Malaysia from Bangladesh's list of 423, 312 associate agencies (10 per principal) and BOESL with 62 associates; associates can send workers only with a principal agency's or BOESL's approval [A0005, A0028; matches official W0008]. FWCMS published the 25 on 21 Aug 2026 [A0017]. Reported mechanism: employers holding approvals choose one of the 25; other agencies reportedly pay one of them a Tk 6,000 processing fee [A0019]; employers are expected to nominate agencies of their choice [A0007]. Prothom Alo found 17 of the 25 had never sent a worker to Malaysia [A0027]; the experience, office and training-centre criteria were relaxed at Bangladesh's request [A0010].
- **Zero-cost 10,000.** Per a Ministry release of 28 Aug 2026 (not online), Malaysia agreed to take 10,000 workers at zero cost, including airfare, first batch via BOESL on chartered flights [A0033, A0034]. In Oct 2026 employers had started issuing demand letters and paying levies on FWCMS; the High Commission in KL had begun attesting demands (first example: 28 workers for NSK Trade City under Goodness Services Ltd) [A0035]. BOESL says the employer alone picks among applicants [A0036]. The medical process was "not yet approved" in early Oct 2026 [A0038]; a Paltan centre doing unapproved Malaysia medicals was sealed on 1 Oct 2026 [A0039]. This matches the BOESL DHL circular (official W0001-W0006).
- **Policy statements.** Apr 2026 joint statement: employers bear the full cost, zero cost to workers, under the ILO Employer Pays Principle; Malaysia plans an AI-based / direct-hiring platform (TURAP, by Bestinet, FWCMS's developer) [A0097, A0022, A0104, A0100]. Jun 2026 PMs' meeting: fewer intermediaries, lower costs [A0103, A0105]. The 2021 MoU runs to Dec 2026 and must be renewed [A0018, A0025]. Gradual reopening: about 800 vetted workers entered construction in Dec 2025-Jan 2026 [A0023]; the "last week of August 2026" start date did not happen [A0021].

## Step 1: Find a real job. What workers report

**What workers actually paid in 2022-24 (unofficial, not verified).** Average about Tk 5.44 lakh against the official Tk 78,990 [A0001, A0098 (Tk 5-6 lakh)]; Verité: about USD 5,000, usually through several loans [A0072 (2024)]; NSU/UniSZA: RM17,000-25,000 [A0068]; NSU policy brief: Tk 3-4 lakh, mostly borrowed [A0110]; FMT: up to RM20,000 [A0024]; OKUP case study: every male worker paid above the legal limit, up to six times for Malaysia [A0081]; costs as high as USD 6,600 per a 2024 memo to Malaysia's PM [A0070]. This is consistent with existing rows W0070, W0072, B0124.

**Where the money goes (who gets it).** In 2022-24, job orders were routed only to 101 listed agencies; other agencies had to process through them [A0006], paying the listed agency about Tk 1.07-1.52 lakh per worker as a "syndicate fee" [A0002, A0028] and Tk 1.6-2 lakh to middlemen for the demand letter [A0002]. Bloomberg: a "syndicate fee" of about USD 1,350; workers who did not pay cash allegedly did not get their passports back [A0069]. Agencies paid to join the syndicate and recouped it from workers [A0107 (2024)]. For 2026, BAIRA members allege a RM5,500 (Tk 1.68 lakh) syndicate fee plus RM8,000-9,000 to buy each visa (over Tk 4.3 lakh per worker), with total worker costs of Tk 6-7 lakh [A0026]; others say costs could pass Tk 6 lakh and visas could cost about RM7,000 [A0003, A0004]; an alleged Tk 17 crore entry payment per agency and RM5,000 per worker [A0011]. All allegations, not verified.

**Agency practices to warn about.** Collecting passports before any official go-ahead: a BMET mobile court sealed Triple A Overseas (RL-2630) on 10 Sep 2026, fined it Tk 50,000 and seized three boxes of workers' passports [A0043, A0044]. Workers pressured to sign or record false declarations that they paid only the official fee [A0061]. Extra payments demanded after the fact and jobs changed without consent [A0062]. No contract before departure (75% in OKUP's cases) [A0082] (contrast the official rule W0030). Jobs that do not exist on arrival; employers over-recruit or recruit to subcontract illegally [A0014, A0016, A0071, A0095]. Even BOESL-recruited workers reportedly paid more than five times the official limit (UN experts, Nov 2025) [A0060].

**Ministry warnings (official text not online).** Don't pay, do medicals or hand over passports until an official notice [A0033, A0031]; agencies must not sign contracts, collect passports, arrange medicals or take money [A0040, A0008]. Complaints: Expatriate Call Center 16135 (+880 961-010-2030 from abroad); Malaysia focal point Md. Hedayetul Islam Mondal, +880 241-030-241, dsemployment1@probashi.gov.bd, +880 171-604-8115 [A0041, A0042]; need the official notice before the doc uses these. The HC in KL warned of a fraud ring taking passports and cash for "Sabah" jobs; there is no Sabah agreement [A0102].

**Loans and debt.** 96% of surveyed Malaysia workers took at least one loan for recruitment, 82% two or more, and 73% spent 50-100% of monthly pay on repayments [A0064, A0065 (2024)]. Loans are mostly informal and paid straight to a third party, with no record [A0066]; families sell land or borrow [A0015, A0106]. Recruiters themselves proposed PKB instalment migration loans [A0013]. Nothing found on PKB loan use for Malaysia in 2025-26 (the doc's PKB facts come from official B0013-B0019).

**What helps (evidence).**
- BOESL government channel: zero-cost in principle. Track record: about 1,381-1,500 workers sent at no cost in 2022-24 against demand for 10,000+ [A0059 (2024), A0030]. But the stranded-worker scheme cost Tk 1,62,500 (official W0020), reportedly Tk 1 lakh of it a "visa purchase fee" [A0056]; BOESL sent only 190 in about six months [A0057]; workers complained of harassment [A0055]; UN experts reported BOESL recruits paying more than five times the limit [A0060]. So BOESL's zero-cost circulars (W0001-W0005) are the official route, but there is no outside evidence yet on how the 2026 zero-cost scheme works in practice.
- Fair-recruitment pilots: none found for Malaysia. ILO's only Bangladesh pilot cited is Qatar construction 2018-21 (92% cost drop) [A0092]. No agency in the Malaysia corridor has outside evidence of low fees; **do not name any agency**. The 25 FWCMS agencies are listed (official W0008), but news gives no evidence about their fees.
- Employer fee repayment in Malaysia happens only under pressure: Mediceram agreed in 2025 to repay RM22,500 each after workers went to a rights group [A0090], paid on 27 Oct 2025, then terminated all of them on 31 Oct 2025 and kept passports [A0108, A0109].
- Malaysian labour office claims with an NGO can work: 74 workers won a RM1.54 million labour office order against Meranti Binamas through Tenaganita (Jun 2025); 33 are suing in the Shah Alam High Court for RM1.71 million including their RM25,000 fees [A0086, A0087]. Without representation, the 2024 Pengerang workers settled for about half [A0088, A0089 (2024)]. (Part L may use these for "If you aren't paid".)
- ILO definition (benchmark for what is a recruitment fee): any fee or cost to get the job, wherever and whenever charged [A0096]; under the Employer Pays Principle, workers pay only for their passport [A0099].

**Complaints to BMET / the Ministry: what actually happens.** Official route and 30-working-day timeline are in B0034, B0035, B0041, B0044, B0045. In practice (2023 data, newest found): victims say claims take 4 months to a year (conflict row A0073 vs B0034/B0045); BMET resolved about one-third of 2,380 complaints in 2023, awarding Tk 4.87 crore [A0074]; most get under half their loss [A0075]; in Aug 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 against Tk 3.5-5 lakh costs [A0076]. Documents decide the outcome: those who can show proof get more; most paid middlemen with no written agreement [A0077, A0079]. A BMET official says an agency's licence is suspended if a complaint stays unresolved for six months through its fault [A0078]. OKUP: BMET both licenses and arbitrates, and there are no compensation standards [A0083]. NGO routes: Ovibashi Odhikar Forum and WARBE do local arbitration, and refer recruiter cases to BMET; complex cases go to court under the 2013 Act or the 2012 Trafficking Act [A0080]. 2013 Act amended (Nov 2024) to cover sub-agents [A0093]. For workers in Malaysia, the HC and labour department did not help the Mediceram workers [A0091, A0109]; HC services were reported "inaccessible and slow" [A0111].

**Enforcement against agencies.** 49 licences cancelled [A0012]; ACC cases against 64 people from 25 agencies over about Tk 2,288 crore [A0085, A0084].

## The 2024 stranded workers (17,777) and refunds

- Cause: agencies sent workers late, causing ticket shortages; fares jumped from Tk 20,000-25,000 to over Tk 1 lakh; some employers were blacklisted [A0054, A0052 (2024), A0051].
- Refund process (2024): the ministry ordered agencies to refund by 18 Jul 2024; workers sign a receipt only after getting the full amount; BAIRA reports the list to the ministry; agencies that don't refund face legal action [A0045, A0046]. Reported progress: 70% (Jul 2024, unconfirmed) [A0047], 81% of agencies (Jan 2025) [A0048]. Workers without visa or BMET card were told to reclaim passport and money from the agency, with ministry help through local police stations [A0049]. High Court writ: reports every three months, and asked why refunds should not carry interest [A0053]. Official W0036: an agency gets no new permissions until it repays the workers it failed to send.
- Going later: BOESL took 7,873 (official W0018/W0020); the first 60 flew on 25 Nov 2025 [A0058]; about 120-150 were still waiting in Oct 2026 [A0037].

## Draft steps (for Step 1, "Before paying", and Scams)

1. **Don't pay anyone, hand over a passport or do a medical for Malaysia until the Ministry or BOESL publishes a notice** (official: W0020, W0021, W0027; practice: agencies were caught collecting passports in Sep 2026 [A0043]; the Ministry repeated the warning in 2026 [A0033, A0040], unofficial until the notice is found).
2. **Prefer BOESL's zero-cost circulars** (W0001-W0005). Unofficial, not verified: BOESL's past Malaysia programmes were small, and the stranded-worker scheme cost Tk 1,62,500 [A0059, A0056].
3. **Know what workers really paid** (unofficial, not verified): Tk 3-6 lakh in 2022-24 against Tk 78,990 [A0001, A0110, A0098], mostly through loans [A0064]. Agents have alleged 2026 costs of Tk 6-7 lakh [A0026]. **Need to know:** the official maximum for private-agency jobs under the 2026 system.
4. **Never sign a paper saying you paid only the official fee if you paid more**; keep proof of every payment. Workers were pressured to do this [A0061], and proof decides BMET compensation [A0077, A0079].
5. **If cheated:** BMET / Ministry complaint (official B0034-B0045). Unofficial, not verified: it often takes months and awards are usually under half the loss [A0073-A0076]; NGOs (WARBE, Ovibashi Odhikar Forum) can help [A0080]. In Malaysia, an NGO such as Tenaganita can help with labour office claims [A0086].
