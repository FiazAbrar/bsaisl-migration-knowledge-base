Tokyo Narita and Haneda Transit: What Changed in 2026

Japan stopped issuing transit visas on July 1, 2026. Staying airside at one airport still needs no visa, but a Narita overnight or any Narita to Haneda switch means entering Japan.

Written by Kenji Tanaka
Published Nov 17, 2026 11 min read
32 0
Transit Visa
Ended July 1, 2026
Replaced By
Temporary Visitor visa
Narita Airside
Closed midnight to 6 a.m.
Haneda Hours
24 hours

Japan stopped issuing airport transit visas on July 1, 2026. If you only change planes at Narita or Haneda and never pass immigration, nothing about that changes: you still need no visa, whatever your passport. What changed is the fallback. Anyone who has to enter Japan during a layover, even for a night or to cross town between airports, now applies for a regular Temporary Visitor visa, or travels visa-free if their country is on Japan’s exemption list.

That matters most for travellers whose passport is not visa-exempt, including Indian, Pakistani, Filipino and Nigerian passport holders, who may route through Tokyo between Asia and North America. A plan that worked in 2025, such as a transit visa and a hotel near Narita, no longer exists as a product. Embassy pages now tell these applicants to apply for a short-stay visa instead.

Narita (NRT) and Haneda (HND) are two separate airports on opposite sides of Tokyo, and they behave differently. Haneda runs round the clock and has an international terminal with 24-hour lounges. Narita does not let passengers stay airside between midnight and 6:00 a.m. That one difference decides whether an overnight connection is easy or needs a visa.

The second trap is the airport switch. A ticket that lands at Narita and departs from Haneda, or the reverse, is a self-transfer between two airports. You have to enter Japan, collect your bags, and check in again. Japan’s embassies say a visa is required for that unless your passport is exempt, and an airline will not let you board without it.

This guide follows the order an airline agent checks at your first desk: your route, your passport, the airport and the hours, and what happens if you need to land. The rules and amounts below come from Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA), the Embassy of Japan network, and the English text of the regulations that enforce Japan’s Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act. Details are current as of November 2026.

One caveat on sourcing: mofa.go.jp and the embassy sites returned access errors to our automated tools, so the embassy wording quoted here comes from the live consulate pages as surfaced in search results, and the law text comes from the government’s Japanese Law Translation database. Confirm the final answer for your passport on the embassy page that serves your country before you book.

1

Know What Changed on July 1, 2026

MOFA amended the Cabinet Order on consular fees, and the new schedule took effect on July 1, 2026. Two things happened at once. Visa fees rose, and the transit visa category was closed. Our earlier news report on the Japan visa fee hike and the end of the transit visa covered both; the consulate pages we rechecked for this guide confirm its main claims.

Consulates in Seattle, Houston and Montreal state that acceptance of transit visa applications stopped on June 30, 2026 and that no transit visas are issued from July 1. The Seattle consulate adds that a transit visa already issued stays usable until it expires or reaches its entry limit. People who would have applied for one are told to request a single-entry or double-entry Temporary Visitor visa. Houston adds that a single-entry visa for transit purposes can be requested through Japan’s online eVISA system.

MOFA says the amount can differ by nationality and by mission. The figures above are examples from MOFA’s reference level and from consulate fee pages; use the table of the embassy that will receive your application. Visa centre service charges are separate.
2

Work Out Whether You Stay Airside

The embassy of Japan in Canada puts the rule plainly: if you transit within the same airport on the same day without leaving the secure transit area, no visa is needed, and that applies to all nationalities. If you must go through immigration for any reason, such as switching airports, picking up luggage and checking in again, or staying overnight for the next flight, you need a visa unless your passport is exempt.

Consulates serving visa-exempt travellers add conditions for visa-free transit: a confirmed onward ticket, the same airport, staying in the gate area, and the same calendar day. Chicago and Nashville list these; Nashville adds that stand-by (non-revenue) passengers need a visa. Airlines can also require immigration clearance on their own, so confirm with your carrier.

Tokyo connections by situation
SituationEnter Japan?What you need
Same airport, same day, bags checked through, stay airsideNoNo visa, any nationality
Haneda, next-day flight, stay in the 24-hour international terminalNot necessarilyNo visa if you stay airside; showers and the terminal hotel are landside

Everything in the “Yes” rows means the same thing in law: you are landing in Japan. A visa-exempt traveller gets a short-stay entry stamp; everyone else needs the visa before boarding the flight that brings them to Japan.

Warning
The airline decides at the origin airport. If your itinerary forces you to enter Japan and you hold no visa, the first carrier will usually refuse to check you in, not the Japanese officer at Tokyo.
3

Check Whether Your Passport Is Visa-Exempt

Whether you need a visa to land at all depends on your passport. MOFA publishes a single list of countries and regions that have visa exemption arrangements for short stays. The version we could read lists 74 countries and regions, most of them with 90 days on landing, 30 days for Brunei and Qatar, and 15 days for Indonesia and Thailand. Some entries carry conditions, such as an electronic passport or a specific passport type, so read the row for your country.

MOFA: Exemption of Visa (Short-Term Stay)
The official list of countries and regions, with stay lengths and conditions

US, Canadian, UK, Australian, Korean, Singaporean and most European passport holders are on the list, so a layover that puts them in Tokyo is straightforward. Indian, Pakistani, Filipino and Nigerian passport holders are not, which is why the end of the transit visa hits them. For a wider picture of requirements by nationality, see our Japan visa requirements guide.

A visa-exempt passport does not remove the need for a confirmed onward ticket and a passport valid for your next destination. Japan’s enforcement regulations for landing in transit require both, and an officer can still refuse landing.

4

Compare Narita and Haneda Hours

The Seattle consulate’s FAQ spells out the difference. Haneda, Osaka Kansai and Nagoya Chubu operate 24 hours a day, and at those airports you can stay in the international departure lounge without passing immigration. At Narita you cannot stay at the airport between midnight and 6:00 a.m., so a connection into a next-morning flight means leaving the airside area, which means entering Japan.

Narita vs Haneda for connecting passengers
Narita (NRT)Haneda (HND)
TerminalsTerminals 1, 2 and 3Terminal 3 is international; 1 and 2 mainly domestic
Airside overnightNo (closed midnight to 6:00 a.m.)Yes, 24-hour operation
Next-morning connectionRequires entering JapanPossible airside
Hotels and showersLandside, so immigration firstLandside at the terminal; some lounges are 24 hours

Narita’s three terminals are linked by shuttle buses, and a connection between terminals can mean extra security screening and, for some airline pairings, immigration rather than staying airside. Published transfer times run from about 4 minutes between Terminals 2 and 3 to about 25 minutes between Terminals 3 and 1. Ask your airline whether your two flights share a transit area.

Tip
If your passport needs a visa and your flights are the same day, Haneda is the lower-risk hub. A late arrival and a morning departure at Haneda can be handled airside; at Narita they cannot.
5

Plan Any Switch Between Narita and Haneda

The consulates are consistent here: transferring between Narita and Haneda requires entering Japan, and a visa is required unless you are visa-exempt. The two airports share no airside corridor. You clear immigration, collect your bags, travel by road or rail, and check in again as a new passenger. That is exactly the self-transfer problem described in our guide to self-transfer layovers and when you need a visa.

Give the connection real time. JAL publishes minimum connection times of three hours when it connects its own flights across the two airports, and three and a half hours to another airline’s international flight. A late last bus to the other airport can leave you paying for a taxi, so build in a buffer, and do not rely on any airline’s minimum connection if the first flight is delayed.

Before you book a Narita-Haneda switch
  • A visa for Japan, or a passport on the MOFA exemption list
  • At least three hours between landing and the next departure
  • A confirmed onward ticket and a passport valid for the next country
  • A plan for the last bus or train between the airports
  • Baggage rules for the second airline, since you will re-check
6

Understand the Shore Pass and the 72-Hour Landing Permission

Japan’s immigration law has two permissions for people landing while travelling onward. Landing in transit under Article 15 covers a passenger who lands at one port and leaves from another, with a period of up to 3 days for an aircraft passenger under the enforcement regulations. The permission for landing at a port of call (the shore pass, kikochi joriku kyoka) applies to a passenger who comes back to the same port, for a period under 72 hours, within the municipality of the port in principle.

Both require a confirmed ticket to travel on after leaving Japan, or a letter of guarantee in its place, and a valid passport that lets you enter the next destination. Both are granted by an immigration inspector at the airport on a written application, and the inspector can refuse.

Do not plan around it
The shore pass is a discretionary permission, not a product you can book or rely on at check-in. Since July 1, 2026, consulates direct travellers who must pass immigration to a Temporary Visitor visa. We found no embassy page that offers the shore pass as the substitute for the abolished transit visa.
7

Apply for the Visa if You Need to Land

Apply for a Temporary Visitor visa, single-entry unless you plan to pass through Japan more than once. Many missions use a visa application centre, and some nationalities can use Japan’s eVISA system, which issues the visa electronically. Eligibility for eVISA depends on your country and on where you apply, so check the embassy that handles your passport.

MOFA: Visas for visiting Japan
Visa types, application procedure and the exemption list

Apply before you buy non-refundable tickets. Our news report notes that processing times vary with application volume and that the process cannot be expedited. If your Tokyo connection is a one-night stopover, that can cost more than the fare itself, so check the visa timeline first.

A visa for the United States, Canada or Japan can exempt you from a Schengen airport transit visa on the other end of a trip, so Japan visas are worth knowing about when you also change planes in Europe. Our Schengen airport transit visa country list explains which documents exempt you.

8

Watch for JESTA, but Do Not Wait for It

Japan plans an electronic travel authorization, widely called JESTA, for visa-exempt visitors. Reports put the target at fiscal year 2028, which runs from April 2028 to March 2029. No portal, fee or validity period had been published in the coverage we found, and we could not confirm the date on a MOFA or Immigration Services Agency page.

Until it launches, 2026 travel follows the existing rules above. JESTA would apply to travellers who already enter visa-free, not to visa-required nationalities, so it will not replace the transit visa that was abolished.

1
Planning on a transit visa that no longer exists

Older guides, agent sites and forums still describe a transit visa. Applications closed on June 30, 2026, and the fee schedule in force now has no such category.

2
Booking a next-morning flight out of Narita

You cannot stay airside at Narita between midnight and 6:00 a.m., so an overnight connection means entering Japan.

3
Mixing Narita and Haneda on one itinerary

Different airports mean immigration, baggage recheck and a visa for non-exempt passports, with the airline refusing boarding at the origin if you lack it.

4
Treating the shore pass as a loophole

It is a discretionary landing permission with a ticket requirement, not an alternative to a visa.

Before You Book a Tokyo Connection

Run through the checks in order. Is it one airport and one day? Then you need no visa. Is it Haneda with a long gap? Stay airside. Is it Narita overnight, or any NRT and HND switch? Confirm you hold a visa or a passport on the exemption list. If you are weighing other hubs, compare how Europe handles the same questions at Amsterdam Schiphol, Paris CDG and Istanbul Airport.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a transit visa for Narita or Haneda in 2026?

No transit visa exists any more. Japan stopped issuing them on July 1, 2026. If you change planes at one airport on the same day and stay airside, you need no visa. If you must pass immigration, you need a Temporary Visitor visa unless your passport is visa-exempt.

What replaced the Japan transit visa?

Consulates tell travellers who must clear immigration to apply for a Temporary Visitor visa, single or double entry. Houston’s consulate says a single-entry visa for transit purposes can be requested through the online eVISA system. Check the embassy that serves your passport.

Can I stay overnight at Narita airport without a visa?

No. Embassy guidance says you cannot stay at Narita between midnight and 6:00 a.m., so a next-morning connection means leaving the airside area and entering Japan. That needs a visa unless your passport is on Japan’s visa exemption list.

Can I sleep airside at Haneda on a long layover?

Haneda operates 24 hours, and Japanese consulates say you can remain in the international departure lounge there without passing immigration. Hotels and shower rooms at the terminal are landside, so using them means entering Japan.

Do I need a visa to go from Narita to Haneda?

Yes, unless your passport is visa-exempt. The airports are separate, so you must clear immigration, collect your bags and check in again. The Seattle consulate states that a Temporary Visitor visa is required to transfer between Narita and Haneda.

Which passports can enter Japan without a visa?

MOFA publishes the list. The version we could read shows 74 countries and regions, mostly with 90 days on landing, 30 days for Brunei and Qatar, and 15 days for Indonesia and Thailand. Indian, Pakistani, Filipino and Nigerian passport holders are not on it.

What is the Japan shore pass?

It is a landing permission at a port of call under Japan’s immigration law, for a period under 72 hours within the municipality of the port in principle. It needs a confirmed onward ticket and valid passport, is discretionary, and is not a substitute for a visa.

Does JESTA apply to Tokyo layovers now?

No. JESTA, Japan’s planned electronic travel authorization for visa-exempt visitors, is reported for fiscal year 2028, running April 2028 to March 2029. No fee or portal had been published in the sources we found, so 2026 travel follows the current rules.
