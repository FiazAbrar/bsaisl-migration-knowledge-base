## 1 Start here

If you have never been abroad, read this first. Every point is set out in full further down.

<a id="can-you-go-now"></a>

### 1.1 Can you go now?

Yes, but only through BOESL, Bangladesh's state-owned recruiting company, and few go: BMET cleared 1,726 workers for Brunei from 1 January to 7 November 2026 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [3](#o3)</sup>. Its 2026 jobs are for men aged 25 to 45 with trade experience, on two-year contracts <sup>[N2](#n2), [4](#o4)</sup>. Working on a visit visa is a crime <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.

<a id="your-first-five-steps"></a>

### 1.2 Your first five steps

1. Register on OEP, BMET's online system, and get an e-passport, Tk 4,025 for 5 years ([Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass), [Step 1: Check you qualify and get your papers ready](#step-1-check-you-qualify-and-get-your-papers-ready)) <sup>[6](#o6), [7](#o7)</sup>.
2. Apply for a BOESL Brunei job on brms.boesl.gov.bd, paying the Tk 100 fee by bKash ([Step 2: Apply through BOESL](#step-2-apply-through-boesl)) <sup>[8](#o8), [4](#o4)</sup>.
3. If picked, take the medical at a Brunei-approved centre in Dhaka, at your cost ([Step 3: Medical examination in Dhaka](#step-3-medical-examination-in-dhaka)) <sup>[9](#o9), [4](#o4)</sup>.
4. Check the company is not on the Bangladesh High Commission's blacklist of 29 Brunei companies ([Step 4: The employer gets the licence and your visa](#step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa)) <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.
5. Get the visa endorsed in Dhaka, do the 3-day orientation and get BMET clearance, the smart card ([Step 5: Before you fly](#step-5-before-you-fly)) <sup>[11](#o11), [12](#o12), [13](#o13)</sup>.

<a id="what-it-really-costs"></a>

### 1.3 What it really costs

Through BOESL you pay Tk 56,350 if skilled or Tk 44,850 if not, plus the visa fee and the Dhaka medical, by pay order only: BOESL takes no cash <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. The employer pays the air fare and housing; you pay your own food out of BND 20 to 22 a day (about Tk 1,900 to 2,100) <sup>[4](#o4), [2](#o2)</sup>. Before the BOESL route, men paid brokers Tk 3 to 5 lakh, often for no job <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="three-scams-to-watch-for"></a>

### 1.4 Three scams to watch for

- *A "free visa" or company visa from a broker:* only BOESL may send workers to Brunei, and it has no agents or sub-agents <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [4](#o4)</sup>.
- *A broker who keeps your passport and takes a monthly cut:* a Bangladeshi was jailed and caned in Brunei for this in 2025 <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- *A remittance counter that gives fake receipts:* in 2024, 116 Bangladeshi workers lost BND 0.72 million this way through a Western Union agent <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

<a id="who-to-call"></a>

### 1.5 Who to call

- In Bangladesh: BOESL, +88-02-48319125 or 01765-411653, Sunday to Thursday, 9am to 4pm <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>; Probash-Bondhu, 16135, free, or +8809610102030 from abroad <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>.
- In Brunei: the Department of Labour's complaints hotline, 7298989, also on WhatsApp <sup>[15](#o15)</sup>.
- The Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing in Bandar Seri Begawan: First Secretary (Labour), +6732342460 <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>.

## 2 Background

<a id="in-one-minute"></a>

### 2.1 In one minute

Brunei is a small, steady market for Bangladeshi workers: BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the Bangladesh government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared 1,681 workers for Brunei in 2025 and 1,726 from 1 January to 7 November 2026, against an average of about 5,000 a year before COVID-19 by BMET's count <sup>[17](#o17), [3](#o3), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. Most Bangladeshis there work in construction, cleaning, restaurants, and oil and gas, on renewable two-year contracts <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N8](#n8)</sup>. A job starts when a Brunei employer gets a Foreign Worker Licence, the LPA (Lesen Pekerja Asing), from Brunei's Labour Department after failing to find a local through JobCentre Brunei; the licence names how many foreigners the employer may bring and for which jobs <sup>[18](#o18), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Bangladesh's High Commission in Brunei and RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion, a Bangladesh police force) said in 2019 and 2020 that Bangladeshis with Bruneian partners had set up companies with no real work and traded visas through them; men paid Tk 3 to 5 lakh for these visas, and thousands landed with no job <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9), [N10 (2020)](#n10), [N11 (2019)](#n11), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>. After Brunei's Sultan intervened in 2019 and both governments signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) in October 2022, Brunei's Labour Department announced that from 1 February 2024 Bangladeshis would be recruited only through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the state-owned recruiting agency), with the Bangladesh High Commission checking each employer before attesting its visas <sup>[N11 (2019)](#n11), [N1 (2024)](#n1), [20](#o20)</sup>. Through BOESL a worker pays Tk 44,850 to 56,350 plus the visa and medical fees, and the employer pays the recruitment fee, the air ticket and housing <sup>[N2](#n2), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. The work pass ties the worker to one employer and one job; leaving that employer, working elsewhere or staying past the pass's expiry leads to arrest, and an overstay of more than 90 days carries prison and at least three strokes of the cane, a sentence Brunei's courts gave several Bangladeshis in 2026 <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5), [N12](#n12), [N13](#n13), [N14](#n14)</sup>.

<a id="is-it-open-now-and-who-can-go"></a>

### 2.2 Is it open now, and who can go?

Brunei is open to Bangladeshi men through BOESL, in small numbers: Bangladesh cleared 1,726 workers for Brunei from 1 January to 7 November 2026, and BOESL advertised 172 jobs with several Brunei companies in August 2026 and 148 with one employer in its circular of 29 September 2026 <sup>[3](#o3), [N15](#n15), [N2](#n2), [4](#o4)</sup>. In 2018 and 2019, by the High Commission's count, three in four went through illegal channels, many on visas from companies with no work for them <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="status"></a>

#### 2.2.1 Status

Open, through BOESL <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2](#n2)</sup>. On 29 July 2026 Bangladesh's state minister for foreign affairs asked Brunei's High Commissioner in Dhaka for more jobs for Bangladeshis, and on 3 November 2026 Bangladesh's acting foreign minister offered Brunei more skilled, semi-skilled and professional workers; Brunei's second minister of finance said he was optimistic about continued recruitment <sup>[N16](#n16), [N17](#n17)</sup>. On 18 November 2026 Prime Minister Tarique Rahman told Parliament that several countries had suspended or slowed recruitment from Bangladesh because of syndicates, without naming them <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.

In January 2024 Brunei's Labour Department announced that from 1 February 2024 Bangladeshi workers would be recruited only through BOESL, and the Bangladesh High Commission in Bandar Seri Begawan attests (checks and stamps) each employer's demand letter and visas only after seeing its JobCentre approval, licence, contracts, projects, staff house and three months' wage receipts of its existing Bangladeshi workers <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [22](#o22), [20](#o20)</sup>. In September 2025 Brunei's deputy minister for security and law, citing Brunei's first conviction under its Trafficking and Smuggling of Persons Order, of a Bangladeshi convicted on 11 August 2025 of deceptively recruiting six compatriots for forced labour, said countries of origin must screen their own citizens <sup>[N19](#n19)</sup>. The US State Department's 2026 trafficking report says Bangladeshis recruited under the MoU are still at risk when employers or intermediaries break the rules <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

Brunei's Minister of Home Affairs said in August 2026 that the Labour Department is now known as the Department of Manpower and Employment (DME) and enforces the Employment Act, Chapter 278; a November 2026 report still has the DME enforcing the Employment Order 2009 (see [Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass)) <sup>[15](#o15), [N21](#n21)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - From what date did the Employment Act, Chapter 278 replace the Employment Order 2009, and did JobCentre Brunei merge into the DME?
> - Is BOESL still the only channel in November 2026, or may private Bangladeshi agencies again send workers to Brunei employment agencies that have signed with BOESL?

<a id="the-channels"></a>

#### 2.2.2 The channels

- *BOESL (the government channel).* BOESL signs a memorandum of co-operation with a licensed Brunei employment agency (BEA), receives the employer's demand letter after the High Commission has attested it, advertises the jobs, shortlists and arranges interviews <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. BOESL's general manager said in 2024 that Brunei employers hire through their local agencies, not directly, and that BOESL had MoUs with 36 such agencies, 10 of which had started recruiting <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. Applicants apply on brms.boesl.gov.bd for Tk 100 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- *Workers named by the employer.* The High Commission's October 2026 checklist lets a demand letter include at most two "recommended workers" whom the employer has already chosen, with the worker's passport, contact number and any earlier Brunei employment pass <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>.
- *Private agencies.* Under the 2022 MoU private Bangladeshi agencies may not send workers to Brunei, and a former secretary general of BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) objected to this in 2024 <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
- *Irregular "visa" routes.* Before 2024 many men went on visas bought from brokers, often from companies with no projects, and officials said in 2020 and 2021 that most were sent through Dhaka airport by "body contract" (collusion with airport officials to let a worker board without the High Commission's attestation or proper clearance) <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9), [N4 (2020)](#n4), [N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - BMET cleared 1,681 workers for Brunei in 2025 and 1,726 by 7 November 2026, but only 25 had been hired under the government-to-government arrangement by September 2024 <sup>[17](#o17), [3](#o3), [N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>: through which channel did the rest go?

<a id="who-qualifies"></a>

#### 2.2.3 Who qualifies

- *Sex.* Men: BOESL's circular of 29 September 2026 was for 148 male workers, and its 2024 trades were masonry, painting, carpentry, construction labour, chefs, waiters and kitchen assistants <sup>[N2](#n2), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
- *Age.* 25 to 45 in BOESL's circular of 29 September 2026 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- *Skill.* At least two to three years' experience in the trade, with no education requirement, in the same circular <sup>[N2](#n2), [4](#o4)</sup>. BOESL's agreement with Brunei agencies covers construction, oil and gas, garments, care giving, nursing, hospitality, bakery, agriculture and other sectors <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
- *Health.* A medical test in Bangladesh before departure and a Ministry of Health medical examination in Brunei after arrival <sup>[N2](#n2), [18](#o18)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Do Bangladeshi women go to Brunei as domestic workers or cleaners, through which channel, and how many did BMET clear in 2025-26? (The US State Department's 2026 trafficking report says Brunei penalised four agencies for overcharging domestic workers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.)

<a id="where-the-workers-work"></a>

#### 2.2.4 Where the workers work

Brunei had 64,100 non-local workers in 2025, 29.1% of everyone employed in the country <sup>[23](#o23)</sup>. Foreign workers rose from 65,579 in 2019 to 75,402 in 2023; in April 2024 construction employed 21,515 foreigners, 83% of its workforce <sup>[N24](#n24)</sup>. Bangladeshis work in construction, cleaning, restaurants, and oil and gas, and the High Commission said in 2019 that some own businesses <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N25 (2019)](#n25)</sup>. Most lower-skilled workers in Brunei come from Bangladesh, Indonesia and the Philippines <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>; the Financial Express reported in 2024 that most foreign workers are Indonesian, with Filipinos and Bangladeshis next <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>. Brunei had labour recruitment MoUs with two foreign governments in the US State Department's 2026 reporting period <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

<a id="the-size-of-the-flow"></a>

#### 2.2.5 The size of the flow

Labour migration to Brunei began in 1992 <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. BMET counted 77,958 Bangladeshis who went from 1992 to January 2024, and 64,974 from 2004 to May 2025, 0.47% of all Bangladeshi overseas employment in that time <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [24](#o24)</sup>. Estimates of how many are there now run from 13,000 to 25,000: 13,000 (2021), 15,000 (the High Commission, 2021), about 20,000 (a High Commission official, 2022; again in 2024) and about 25,000 (unofficial, 2024) <sup>[N23 (2021)](#n23), [N3 (2021)](#n3), [N26 (2022)](#n26), [N6 (2024)](#n6), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. Workers who went to Brunei, by year (BMET's yearly table to 2024, its clearance report for 2025 and 2026):
- 2014: 6,633 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2015: 6,354 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2016: 5,836 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2017: 8,587 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2018: 4,480 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2019: 3,628 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2020: 530 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2021: 12 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2022: 1,850 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2023: 1,081 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2024: 205 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>
- 2025: 1,681 <sup>[17](#o17)</sup>
- 2026, to 7 November: 1,726 <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>

From 1 January to 7 November 2026 BMET cleared about as many workers for Brunei (1,726) as for Japan (1,863) or Malaysia (1,592), and more than for South Korea (1,342), against 334,770 for Saudi Arabia and 66,657 for Singapore <sup>[3](#o3)</sup>.

Official counts understate the real flow: the High Commission told the Ministry that 34,000 Bangladeshis went to Brunei in 2018 and 2019, 25,573 of them through illegal channels, while BMET recorded 8,108 <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9), [24](#o24)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - BMET's yearly table gives 1,081 for 2023 and 205 for 2024 (matching BMET's figure of 3,473 from January 2020 to January 2024), while its online clearance report gives 5,959 and 3,058 <sup>[24](#o24), [N7 (2024)](#n7), [25](#o25), [26](#o26)</sup>: what does the clearance report count that the table does not, and are its 2025 and 2026 figures departures?

<a id="what-has-closed-it-before"></a>

#### 2.2.6 What has closed it before

- *Late 2015: projects end.* Demand fell when the Temburong Bridge and the first phase of the oil refinery were finished, and malpractice grew <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>.
- *2019: the Sultan's crackdown.* After an unscheduled visit by the Sultan to the immigration and labour departments on 8 October 2019, Brunei blacklisted 31 companies under Bruneian-Bangladeshi joint ownership and deported Bangladeshi brokers; Bangladesh suspended the passports of five citizens suspected of running a trafficking ring <sup>[N11 (2019)](#n11), [N4 (2020)](#n4), [N25 (2019)](#n25)</sup>. The High Commission's own list of blacklisted Brunei companies had 29 names in October 2026 <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. Brunei's government had wanted to stop recruiting from Bangladesh altogether, and did not after the High Commission asked it not to <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>.
- *2020-2021: COVID-19.* Departures fell from 3,628 in 2019 to 530 in 2020 and 12 in 2021 <sup>[24](#o24)</sup>.
- *2022-2024: the switch to government-to-government recruitment.* Brunei said in 2022 it wanted Bangladeshi workers only government to government; BOESL became the only channel from 1 February 2024 and its process began on 1 March 2024; only 950 Bangladeshis went from January 2020 to August 2022 <sup>[N26 (2022)](#n26), [N1 (2024)](#n1), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.

<a id="where-it-may-grow"></a>

#### 2.2.7 Where it may grow

BOESL's deputy general manager said in 2024 that 2,000 to 3,000 workers a year could go, after 400 job demands came from 100 employers <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22)</sup>. In 2020 the High Commissioner expected at least 40,000 jobs for foreign workers from a tourism hub in Temburong and the second phase of the oil refinery <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="how-a-job-reaches-you-and-who-takes-a-cut"></a>

### 2.3 How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A Brunei job starts as a place on an employer's Foreign Worker Licence, which the Labour Department grants only after the employer has advertised the job to locals; High Commission sources said in 2020 that visa traders used fake companies with no real demand for workers to obtain and sell these visas <sup>[18](#o18), [N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>. The choke point is now the High Commission's attestation and BOESL: only BOESL may send workers, and the High Commission attests an employer's demand letter and visas only after checking its licence, projects, housing and wage records <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [22](#o22), [20](#o20)</sup>.

<a id="the-licence-and-the-quota"></a>

#### 2.3.1 The licence and the quota

The Labour Department controls how many foreigners each employer may hire, through the licence; Immigration issues the passes <sup>[N8](#n8), [N24](#n24)</sup>. The employer or its agency advertises the job to locals on JobCentre Brunei (Brunei's government job portal), gets JobCentre's clearance letter, applies to the Labour Department for the licence and pays a security deposit for each worker, then applies to the Immigration and National Registration Department (INRD) for a visa for each worker the licence names ([Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass), [Step 4: The employer gets the licence and your visa](#step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa)) <sup>[18](#o18), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Since 2022 a construction employer must also sign a declaration that it is responsible for its workers' whereabouts and well-being once its projects end <sup>[N27 (2022)](#n27)</sup>.

The Labour Department inspects within 3 to 6 months of approval, and inspects construction firms both before and after <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>. The employer pays the agency's recruitment fee, which Brunei's Employment Agencies Order 2004 caps at BND 2,000 per worker, and observers cited in the US State Department's 2026 trafficking report say fees can run higher and employers pass part of them on to workers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

<a id="visa-trading-and-the-free-visa"></a>

#### 2.3.2 Visa trading and the "free visa"

A "free visa" is a work visa sold with no job behind it: the buyer is left to find his own work, which Brunei law forbids (see [What ties you to the employer](#what-ties-you-to-the-employer)) <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N25 (2019)](#n25), [N12](#n12)</sup>. How it worked, as officials and workers described it from 2019 to 2021:
- *Fake companies.* High Commission sources said in 2020 that Bangladeshis with Bruneian partners, and some with Indians, had set up companies that had no projects and existed only to trade visas <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>. RAB said in 2020 that about 3,000 companies in Brunei were Bangladeshi-owned and only 1% of them legitimate <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10)</sup>.
- *Over-issue.* The Financial Express reported in 2020 that about 22,000 Brunei visas were issued in the second half of 2019, while High Commission sources said the mission certified 3,300 <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.
- *The airport.* The newspaper reported that the rest were sent through "body contract" with airport officials, and officials said in 2021 that most workers went this way <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.
- *On arrival.* Workers said brokers or employers took their passports on arrival and left them without work, and one was told to find his own job and hand over part of his wage or be deported <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10), [N23 (2021)](#n23), [N25 (2019)](#n25)</sup>.

The High Commission began attesting visas in August 2017 because of this trafficking <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10)</sup>. In a January 2020 letter to the Ministry, the High Commission said it had cancelled the attestation of 41 visas from two companies with no projects, Motiar Rahaman Sdn Bhd and Omar Sdn Bhd, but the companies still got BMET smart cards and sent workers <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>. In 2022 its standard operating procedure barred employers from using their existing Bangladeshi workers as brokers and visa traders <sup>[N28 (2022)](#n28)</sup>.

<a id="who-controls-it"></a>

#### 2.3.3 Who controls it

The networks described by officials and courts:
- *A deported ringleader.* The High Commission and RAB named a Bangladeshi as the ringleader of the Brunei trafficking racket in 2019 and 2020; accused in about 20 cases, he had his passport suspended in 2019 and was deported from Brunei, and in 2020 the High Commission said he was running the business from Bangladesh with his brother in Brunei <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10), [N4 (2020)](#n4), [N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>. Mission officials said in 2020 that none of the ringleaders had been brought to book <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.
- *Mahedi Hasan, 44.* Sentenced in Brunei in November 2025 to five years, a BND 50,000 fine and six strokes for trafficking in 2018 and 2019; his victims had their passports taken, paid monthly fees and worked for little or no pay <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- *A Dhaka recruiter, 2020.* RAB arrested a man in Dhaka whom it accused of taking Tk 3 crore from 400 people in one year for Brunei jobs <sup>[N10 (2020)](#n10)</sup>.
- *Four companies, 2021.* The High Commission named MB SND BHD, Hj Momin Snd Bhd, Haja Monwara Snd Bhd and Hj Bashar Snd Bhd, run by three Bangladeshi men with Bruneian partners; 49 workers claimed BND 323,890 from them, and workers filed 34 cases with the High Commission and 24 with Brunei police; Haja Monwara Sdn Bhd is on the High Commission's blacklist in October 2026 <sup>[N29 (2021)](#n29), [10](#o10)</sup>.
- *Convictions.* Brunei convicted two labour traffickers under its Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act in the US State Department's 2026 reporting period, the first convictions under that law since 2016 <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- *Officials.* Brunei detained four of its immigration officials in the 2026 reporting year for allegedly using counterfeit and official stamps to help foreigners break immigration rules <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

<a id="down-the-chain"></a>

#### 2.3.4 Down the chain

In the brokered route, the High Commission said in 2021, rackets of brokers based in both Brunei and Bangladesh recruited men for false jobs at fake companies run by Bangladeshis with Bruneian or Malay partners <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>. In 2020 it said traffickers forced many stranded workers to act as middlemen themselves <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>. In 2015, workers said, two agencies, Rabbi International and Estema International, took Tk 4.5 to 5 lakh each for Brunei gardening jobs; 30 of the 34 men sent returned home <sup>[N30 (2015)](#n30)</sup>. Some traffickers promised Brunei and sent the men illegally to nearby countries instead, the High Commission said in 2021 <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>.

<a id="what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep"></a>

### 2.4 What you pay, and what you earn and keep

Through BOESL a worker pays Tk 56,350 if skilled or Tk 44,850 if semi-skilled or unskilled, plus the visa fee and the medical test in Bangladesh; the employer pays the recruitment fee, Brunei visa, employment pass, attestation, Brunei medical, insurance, air ticket and housing <sup>[N2](#n2), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Men who went through brokers paid Tk 3 to 5 lakh, often for no job <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="what-people-really-pay"></a>

#### 2.4.1 What people really pay

- *2026, BOESL.* Tk 56,350 (skilled) or Tk 44,850 (semi-skilled and unskilled) for the service charge, registration and incidentals, plus the visa fee and medical test <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- *2021.* Tk 3 to 4 lakh, said the High Commissioner, for low-paid jobs <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>.
- *2019.* About US$4,000 per worker, said the High Commissioner; BND 5,000, said workers and High Commission sources in 2020 <sup>[N25 (2019)](#n25), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.
- *2019 cases.* Tk 5 lakh for a "free visa" that brought no job, Tk 3.8 lakh for 21 days of work in seven months, Tk 4.5 lakh for a promised airport cleaning job that never existed <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.
- *2017 case.* Tk 11.13 lakh in all, by the High Commission's 2021 account: Tk 3.5 lakh for a driving job that never came, then Tk 7.35 lakh more handed to the employer's men <sup>[N29 (2021)](#n29)</sup>.
- *2015.* Tk 4.5 to 5 lakh for gardening jobs <sup>[N30 (2015)](#n30)</sup>.

<a id="how-they-pay"></a>

#### 2.4.2 How they pay

In 2020 a worker borrowed his Tk 3.8 lakh and still owed it after returning; another family sold land and a relative's gold for Tk 3.2 lakh, then borrowed Tk 1.2 lakh from NGOs to pay brokers to send him home <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N10 (2020)](#n10)</sup>. Mission officials said in 2020 that about 90% of Bangladeshi workers had gone to Brunei on verbal contracts only <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="what-the-job-pays"></a>

#### 2.4.3 What the job pays

There is no minimum wage for construction or domestic work in Brunei: the minimum wage covers only banking, finance and ICT (information and communications technology) from July 2023 and, from 1 April 2025, nine sub-industries including accommodation and food services <sup>[N20](#n20), [N31](#n31)</sup>. Reported pay:
- *BOESL, 2026.* BOESL's circular of 29 September 2026 for Unity Land Sdn Bhd pays BND 20 a day for carpenters, construction labourers and masons and BND 22 a day for electricians, drivers, operators, plumbers and welders, for 8 hours' duty a day on a two-year renewable contract, with housing paid by the company, the air fare by the employer and food by the worker ([Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass), [Step 2, item 1: Find the circular](#step-2-apply-through-boesl-item-1)) <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. BOESL's August 2026 circular gave BND 20 to 21 a day or BND 600 to 1,000 a month, depending on the job <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
- *BOESL, 2024.* About Tk 50,000 a month, said BOESL's managing director; the High Commission said it aimed for a minimum of BND 600 a month <sup>[N22 (2024)](#n22), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
- *2021.* About US$200 a month, less than other foreign workers; the High Commission's labour attaché said Filipino workers earn more because they are skilled and recruited under a better process <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>.
- *2015.* BND 254 a month as day labourers, against US$400 promised <sup>[N30 (2015)](#n30)</sup>.

Workers of the four companies named in 2021 claimed over BND 6,600 each in deductions for "Sultan tax", insurance fees, staff house rent and a forged medical fee <sup>[N29 (2021)](#n29)</sup>. Unlawful deductions are an offence under section 44 of the Employment Act <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. Overtime is due beyond 8 hours a day or 44 a week, and may not exceed 72 hours a month <sup>[N21](#n21)</sup>.

<a id="how-long-the-debt-takes"></a>

#### 2.4.4 How long the debt takes

In 2021 officials said workers paid about Tk 4 lakh each to migrate for jobs in which they could hardly earn US$200 a month <sup>[N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>. Workers who went on "free visas" often never earned it back: one worked 21 days in seven months, and another said he had sent home Tk 35,000 in five months <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N25 (2019)](#n25)</sup>.

<a id="remittances-from-brunei"></a>

#### 2.4.5 Remittances from Brunei

Bangladesh Bank recorded US$74.2 million sent from Brunei in the 2023-24 financial year, US$87.4 million in 2024-25 and US$77.4 million in 2025-26 <sup>[27](#o27)</sup>. In July and August 2026 Brunei sent US$9.96 million, and it was 30th among source countries in August <sup>[28](#o28)</sup>. In 2024 the High Commission said 116 workers had sent BND 720,000 through Remittance @ Seconds, a Western Union agent, which gave them fake receipts while an employee allegedly kept the money; it took the case up with BDCB (Brunei Darussalam Central Bank) (see [Sending money home](#sending-money-home)) <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

<a id="what-ties-you-to-the-employer"></a>

### 2.5 What ties you to the employer

You are tied to one employer for everything: the employment pass names the employer you may work for and the job you may do, and the employer must report you to Immigration as soon as you leave <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5), [N12](#n12)</sup>.

- **Entering.** The employer's licence must name you before Immigration issues your visa <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>.
- **Renewing.** Contracts last two years and are renewed only if both sides agree <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21), [N8](#n8)</sup>. In a case the High Commission reported in 2021, an employer's men demanded more than BND 2,100 for a "visa renewal" and never renewed the visa <sup>[N29 (2021)](#n29)</sup>.
- **Quitting.** You must work in the job on your pass for as long as the pass lasts (Immigration Regulation 15(3)), and the employer must tell the Controller of Immigration at once if you leave <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
- **Changing employer.** Working for anyone else is an offence under Regulation 15(2) <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Brunei used to publish newspaper and website notices seeking "runaway workers", and has since told state news agencies not to use the term <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Running away from the employer or working for another brings a BND 600 compound fine (a fine paid instead of going to court); a second offence brings cancellation of the pass, expulsion and possible blacklisting <sup>[N33 (2022)](#n33)</sup>.
- **Leaving.** Workers need the employer's approval before leaving Brunei to avoid a BND 600 fine, a rule observers say exposes them to extortion <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. The employer pays the ticket home unless the worker absconded or was convicted of a crime <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

*Which law covers you.* The Employment Order 2009, which a September 2026 report calls the Employment Act, Chapter 278, covers wages, hours, leave and contracts; the Immigration Act, Chapter 17, covers passes and overstaying <sup>[N34](#n34), [N32](#n32), [N35 (2024)](#n35)</sup>.

*Your passport.* Some employers and agencies still keep workers' passports <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Without it a worker loses legal status, and one worker whose passport his employer kept was given only a special pass by Immigration <sup>[N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.

*An expired or cancelled pass.* Overstaying up to 90 days carries a fine of up to BND 4,000, up to six months' prison, or both; more than 90 days carries 3 months to 2 years' prison and at least three strokes of the whip <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>. In 2026 Brunei's courts gave four Bangladeshi overstayers four to six months and three strokes of the cane <sup>[N13](#n13), [N14](#n14)</sup>. This applies also to workers who fall out of status through their employer's negligence <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.

<a id="what-goes-wrong-most-and-what-people-wrongly-believe"></a>

### 2.6 What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The biggest harm in Brunei is arriving with no job: in 2019 the High Commissioner said about 6,000 Bangladeshis recruited without its attestation in one year were all jobless <sup>[N11 (2019)](#n11)</sup>. Unpaid and docked wages, seized passports and arrest for overstaying follow from it <sup>[N29 (2021)](#n29), [N23 (2021)](#n23), [N13](#n13)</sup>.

*What goes wrong.*
- *No job on arrival.* The US State Department's 2026 trafficking report says some recruiters take thousands of dollars and find no job, and the workers turn to "freelancing", which is illegal <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. In January 2020 the High Commission received at least 25 complaints a day <sup>[N9 (2020)](#n9)</sup>.
- *Unpaid wages.* In 2025 two owners of Qharrof Sdn Bhd were fined BND 1,200 each for not paying two foreign workers, who were each still owed BND 1,000 <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>. Labour inspections in 2026 found unpaid overtime, unlawful deductions, no written contracts and workers placed in jobs not on their licence <sup>[N36](#n36), [N32](#n32)</sup>.
- *Raids and jail.* Immigration's "Operasi Kakas" raids on rented houses, markets and business premises in 2026 detained foreigners suspected of overstaying, working on visit passes and working for someone other than their employer <sup>[N36](#n36), [N12](#n12)</sup>. In December 2023 a raid in Mukim Berakas detained 18 Bangladeshis <sup>[N37 (2023)](#n37)</sup>.
- *Deaths.* A Bangladeshi worker who died in Brunei in 2022 was undocumented, so his employer did not pay his Tk 16 lakh hospital bill and no insurance claim could be made, and his body stayed in the hospital for two and a half years, it was reported in April 2024 (see [What happens in practice](#what-happens-in-practice)) <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38)</sup>. Bringing a body home from Brunei costs about Tk 4.5 lakh <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38)</sup>. In 2022 the High Commissioner said bodies lay in the RIPAS hospital morgue for months with no one taking responsibility <sup>[N28 (2022)](#n28)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis die in Brunei each year, and of what causes?

*"A free visa lets you work for any employer."* Working for anyone other than the employer on your pass is an offence, and a second offence ends in expulsion <sup>[N12](#n12), [N33 (2022)](#n33)</sup>.

*"Brunei pays like Singapore."* A former BAIRA secretary general said in 2024 that wages in Brunei are comparable to Singapore's; Bangladeshis reported earning about US$200 a month in 2021, below other foreign workers <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N3 (2021)](#n3)</sup>.

*"The government channel is the same as an agent."* Through BOESL the worker pays about Tk 45,000 to 56,000 in Bangladesh, and the Brunei side may not charge him or deduct anything from his wage <sup>[N2](#n2), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

*"If I am caught, I will just be sent home."* Overstaying more than 90 days carries prison and caning before removal, and coming back after removal without permission carries a BND 6,000 fine and one to three years' prison <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.

*How the irregular routes end.*
- *Deportation.* In late 2019 Brunei deported nine Bangladeshis in a month, and many jobless workers who took other work were deported <sup>[N11 (2019)](#n11)</sup>.
- *Prison and caning for overstaying* (see [What ties you to the employer](#what-ties-you-to-the-employer)) <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
- *Returning penniless.* Of 34 men sent by two agencies in 2015, 30 came home; they received Tk 5,000 each from the Wage Earners' Welfare Fund <sup>[N30 (2015)](#n30)</sup>.

<a id="visas-not-open-to-bangladeshis"></a>

## 3 Visas not open to Bangladeshis

Brunei sorts countries into visa categories; Bangladesh's ordinary passport is in Category B, which needs a visa applied for through a Brunei mission (embassy or high commission) before travel <sup>[29](#o29)</sup>. These routes therefore do not apply to most Bangladeshis:

- **Visa-free entry:** only Bangladeshi diplomatic and official passports are in Category D3, which allows up to 14 days without a visa <sup>[29](#o29), [30](#o30)</sup>. An ordinary passport needs a visa <sup>[29](#o29)</sup>.
- **Visa on arrival (VOA):** Brunei gives a 14-day visa on arrival, for BND 20 or BND 30, only to Category C nationalities <sup>[29](#o29), [31](#o31)</sup>. Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka says Bangladesh's immigration does not accept a Brunei VOA, so a Bangladeshi must have the visa endorsed in the passport before leaving Bangladesh <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
- **E-visa:** there is no online visa for Bangladeshis. A Category B application is handed in, in person, at the Brunei mission <sup>[33](#o33), [29](#o29)</sup>. The E-Arrival Card filled in on imm.gov.bn before travel is not a visa or a pass <sup>[N39 (2023)](#n39), [32](#o32)</sup>.
- **Transit visa:** a 72-hour transit visa at Brunei International Airport is given only to nationals of China, Australia, Russia, Tajikistan, Mongolia, India, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and Taiwan, and to holders of residence permits from Australia, New Zealand, the US, Japan and the UK; Bangladesh is not on the list (see [Transit](#transit)) <sup>[34](#o34)</sup>.

<a id="work-visa-employment-pass"></a>

## 4 Work visa: employment pass

Every foreign worker in Brunei must hold a valid work visa and work pass, issued by the Immigration and National Registration Department (JIPK in Malay, "DINR" in English papers) <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. The pass is called an Employment Pass: it lets you work only in the job written in it, for up to 5 years at a time, and it can name the one employer you may work for <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>. Before Immigration will issue it, your employer must hold a Foreign Worker Licence (Lesen Pekerja Asing, LPA) from the Department of Labour (Jabatan Buruh), which names you, your job and your workplace <sup>[11](#o11), [19 (2024)](#o19), [36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Since 1 February 2024 Bangladeshi workers can be recruited for Brunei only through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government-owned recruiting company), under a memorandum of understanding (MoU) the two governments signed on 16 October 2022; private Bangladeshi agencies may not send workers <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. On the Brunei side BOESL works only with Brunei employment agencies licensed by the Department of Labour that have signed a Memorandum of Co-operation (MoC) with it <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>. Jobs are mostly for men in construction and related trades, on 2-year renewable contracts <sup>[N2](#n2), [N15](#n15), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

The Department of Labour is now known as the Department of Manpower and Employment (Jabatan Tenaga Manusia dan Pekerjaan), and the Employment Order 2009 it enforces is now cited as the Employment Act (Chapter 278); this doc calls it the Department of Labour <sup>[15](#o15), [N32](#n32), [N21](#n21)</sup>.

<a id="who-pays-what"></a>

### 4.1 Who pays what

<a id="what-the-employer-pays"></a>

#### 4.1.1 What the employer pays

Under the MoC every Brunei agency signs with BOESL, the employer pays <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>:
- the Brunei agency's recruitment fee and the Foreign Worker Licence;
- the employment visa fee and the employment pass fee;
- the High Commission's visa attestation fee: BND 15 a worker (written "B$" in the source) <sup>[20](#o20)</sup>;
- the medical examination in Brunei;
- medical insurance and workmen's compensation insurance <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>;
- local transport;
- an open round-trip air ticket, Dhaka to Brunei and back <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>;
- free food or a food allowance, free housing and medicine <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

BOESL's circular of 29 September 2026 says the company pays for housing and the employer for the air fare, but the worker pays for his own food <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - On BOESL's 2026 Brunei jobs, does the employer give any food allowance, as the MoC requires, or does the worker pay all his food?

The Brunei agency may not charge you any money or deduct anything from your salary <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Under Brunei's Employment Agencies Order 2004 the government lets agencies charge a recruitment fee of up to BND 2,000 for a migrant worker and requires the employer to pay it; the US State Department's 2026 trafficking report says observers found fees above this cap, with employers shifting part of them onto workers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

**Security deposit.** When the employer collects the approved licence it pays a security deposit for each worker, in cash, by banker's guarantee or by JITPA (Jaminan Insurans/Takaful Pekerja Asing, an insurance guarantee bought instead of a cash deposit) <sup>[18](#o18), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. In 2022 Bangladesh's commerce minister said Bangladeshi workers' deposit was US$1,600 each against US$500 for Malaysians <sup>[N40 (2022)](#n40)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What is the Department of Labour's current security deposit for a Bangladeshi worker, and is any of it ever taken from the worker's salary?

**Going home.** The employer pays your repatriation; you pay it yourself only if you abscond or are convicted of a crime <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21), [N41](#n41)</sup>. If you die or are disabled at work, the employer pays to send you or your body home <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

<a id="what-you-pay"></a>

#### 4.1.2 What you pay

Under the MoC, BOESL may take from you only the officially declared migration cost: its service charge, government tax and VAT, the Wage Earners' Welfare Board fee, the pre-exit processing and training fee, insurance, the fingerprint and data-entry fee, the smart card fee, the medical examination in Bangladesh and the immigration clearance fee <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. If the job is advertised as "zero migration cost", the employer pays all of it <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

In BOESL's Brunei circular of 29 September 2026 (148 jobs at Unity Land Sdn Bhd), a worker finally selected by the employer who passes the medical pays <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>:
- BOESL: Tk 56,350 if skilled, or Tk 44,850 if semi-skilled or unskilled, covering BOESL's service charge, VAT, AIT (advance income tax), insurance, the Wage Earners' Welfare fee, registration and incidental costs;
- the visa fee set by the embassy;
- the medical test fee in Bangladesh.

The application fee is Tk 100, paid by bKash <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. When BOESL started the G2G (government-to-government) route in March 2024 it gave the total cost as Tk 44,000 to Tk 56,000 <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.

<a id="the-bangladesh-maximum"></a>

#### 4.1.3 The Bangladesh maximum

The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment's ceiling on what a recruiting agent may charge a male worker for Brunei Darussalam is Tk 1,20,780, covering the air fare, passport, health fees and related costs <sup>[37 (2019)](#o37)</sup>. PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) sets its migration loan ceiling by this country cost ([Step 5, item 5: If you need a loan](#step-5-before-you-fly-item-5)) <sup>[37 (2019)](#o37)</sup>. Before the G2G route, recruiters charged about BND 5,000 to bring each worker, according to workers and High Commission sources in 2020, and workers said they paid Tk 4.5 lakh to Tk 5 lakh in 2019 for jobs that did not exist (see [Done to workers](#done-to-workers)) <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4), [N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.

<a id="how-to-pay"></a>

#### 4.1.4 How to pay

Pay BOESL's charge by pay order in the name "BOESL, Dhaka", made at Sonali Bank's Moghbazar branch, and hand the original pay order to BOESL <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. BOESL takes no cash, has no agent, sub-agent, representative or branch office anywhere in Bangladesh, and says anyone asking for money beyond its set service charge is a fraud <sup>[4](#o4), [N2](#n2)</sup>. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) tells workers to pay any recruiter only through the bank, into its own account, and to keep every receipt <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>.

### 4.2 Step 1: Check you qualify and get your papers ready

1. <a id="step-1-check-you-qualify-and-get-your-papers-ready-item-1"></a>**Check what the job asks for.** BOESL's 2026 Brunei circulars were for men only <sup>[N2](#n2), [N15](#n15)</sup>. The 29 September 2026 circular asked for age 25 to 45 and at least 2 to 3 years' practical experience in the trade, with no schooling required; employers sometimes prefer men who speak English or Malay <sup>[N2](#n2), [4](#o4)</sup>. Your passport must be valid at least 6 months for the visa and pass <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-1-check-you-qualify-and-get-your-papers-ready-item-2"></a>**Get a passport.**

   You'll need: your original NID (national ID card); applicants over 20 must use the NID <sup>[39](#o39)</sup>.

   1. Fill in the e-passport application online, exactly as on your NID <sup>[39](#o39)</sup>.
   2. Pay online through A-Challan (the government's online payment-slip system) or the gateways DGePay or ShurjoPay (card, bKash or Nagad), or at a bank <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>. A 48-page e-passport for 5 years costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express); for 10 years Tk 5,750, Tk 8,050 or Tk 10,350, VAT included <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>.
   3. Take the original NID to the divisional or regional passport office for your present address <sup>[39](#o39)</sup>. Delivery after biometrics is within 15 working days (regular), 7 (express) or 2 (super express) <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>.

3. <a id="step-1-check-you-qualify-and-get-your-papers-ready-item-3"></a>**Register on OEP.** OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform, the online system of BMET, the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is where you apply for the pre-departure orientation and emigration clearance <sup>[38](#o38), [40](#o40)</sup>.

   You'll need <sup>[6](#o6), [41](#o41)</sup>:
   - your NID;
   - a mobile phone that can receive codes;
   - a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-banking account for the Tk 200 government fee.

   1. In Google Chrome open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp and press "অনুগ্রহ করে এখানে ক্লিক করুন" (Please click here) twice <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>.
   2. Type every detail exactly as on your NID and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>.
   3. Type the OTP (one-time code) sent to your phone, press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue), and fill in the form <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>.
   4. Press "পেমেন্ট করতে এগিয়ে যান" (Proceed to payment), then "পেমেন্ট করুন" (Pay), and pay by mobile banking <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>.
   5. Download your Registration Card <sup>[6](#o6)</sup>.
   6. Take 2 copies of the registration slip to your DEMO (district employment and manpower office) to give your fingerprints <sup>[41](#o41)</sup>.

   **If it fails:** call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 <sup>[42](#o42)</sup>, or Probash-Bondhu, the 24-hour call centre of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>.

<a id="step-2-apply-through-boesl"></a>

### 4.3 Step 2: Apply through BOESL

How a job reaches BOESL: a Brunei employer goes to a licensed Brunei employment agency, which fills in a demand letter with the employer and takes it to the Bangladesh High Commission in Bandar Seri Begawan for attestation, then sends the attested demand letter and a copy of the employment contract to BOESL <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. BOESL then advertises the jobs in newspapers, on its website and notice board, or from BMET's database <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

1. <a id="step-2-apply-through-boesl-item-1"></a>**Find the circular.** BOESL posts its Brunei jobs on BRMS (BOESL Recruitment Management System), brms.boesl.gov.bd, and the circulars are carried by newspapers <sup>[8](#o8), [N2](#n2)</sup>. Each circular names the Brunei company, the trades, the number of workers, the wage, the deadline and the cost <sup>[N2](#n2), [N15](#n15), [4](#o4)</sup>. In 2026 Brunei circulars paid BND 20 to 22 a day or BND 600 to 1,000 a month, depending on the job; at Unity Land Sdn Bhd, carpenters, construction labourers and masons get BND 20 a day and electricians, drivers, operators, plumbers and welders BND 22 a day, for 8 hours' duty <sup>[N15](#n15), [4](#o4)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-2-apply-through-boesl-item-2"></a>**Register and apply on BRMS.** Register free on brms.boesl.gov.bd, choose the job and submit the application with your passport number, schooling and experience; pay the Tk 100 application fee by bKash and enter the transaction ID, or the application is rejected <sup>[8](#o8), [4](#o4)</sup>. You can see the application's status on BRMS, and updates come by email and phone <sup>[8](#o8)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-2-apply-through-boesl-item-3"></a>**Interview.** BOESL sends the applications to the company, which draws up a shortlist; shortlisted men are called by SMS to a final interview with the employer or its representative, online or in person <sup>[4](#o4), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Bring your original passport, an English CV and your experience certificates <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. The employer's choice is final <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-2-apply-through-boesl-item-4"></a>**Medical in Dhaka** ([Step 3: Medical examination in Dhaka](#step-3-medical-examination-in-dhaka)).

**Check it is real:** BOESL is the only Bangladeshi body allowed to send workers to Brunei, and it has no agents or sub-agents <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7), [N1 (2024)](#n1), [4](#o4)</sup>. A Brunei company may name at most 2 "recommended" (pre-selected) workers with its demand letter, filing a pre-selection declaration, each worker's passport copy and Bangladesh phone number, and his old employment pass if he has worked in Brunei before <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>. BOESL's office is at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban (5th floor), 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka-1000, open Sunday to Thursday, 9am to 4pm: +88-02-48319125, 01765-411653, info@boesl.gov.bd <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker whose relative's Brunei company wants him by name get onto a BOESL demand letter, and does he still pay the full BOESL charge?

<a id="step-3-medical-examination-in-dhaka"></a>

### 4.4 Step 3: Medical examination in Dhaka

1. Selected candidates take a medical test in Bangladesh; if you are unfit, BOESL restarts recruitment for that place <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
2. Brunei's Ministry of Health requires new workers to have a full medical examination in their home country before arriving, including blood tests for communicable diseases and a chest X-ray <sup>[43](#o43)</sup>. It approves three centres in Bangladesh, all in Dhaka <sup>[9](#o9)</sup>:
   - Fairways Medical Centre, House 39, Road 11, Block H, Banani;
   - International Health Centre, House 103, Road 4, Block B, Banani;
   - Modern Diagnostic Centre (Unit 3), House 8, Road 7, Dhanmondi.
   The ministry's list, updated 22 June 2026, still prints approval periods for these centres that ended in July 2017 (Modern Diagnostic Centre) and July 2020 (the other two) <sup>[9](#o9)</sup>.
3. You pay for this medical yourself <sup>[4](#o4)</sup>. The report goes with your passport for the visa endorsement ([Step 5, item 1: Visa endorsement](#step-5-before-you-fly-item-1)) <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
4. BOESL then emails a confirmation letter, with the demand letter attached, to the Brunei agency, copied to the Department of Labour and the High Commission <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which of the three Dhaka centres does BOESL send Brunei workers to, are their Brunei approvals still current, what do they charge, and how long is the report valid?

<a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa"></a>

### 4.5 Step 4: The employer gets the licence and your visa

1. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-1"></a>**Local hiring first.** Before applying for a licence, the employer must advertise the job on JobCentre Brunei for at least 2 weeks; if no local applies, JobCentre issues a Clearance Letter within 3 working days <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-2"></a>**The Foreign Worker Licence (LPA).** The Brunei agency applies to the Department of Labour through NLMS (the National Labour Management System, its online system) with BOESL's confirmation letter and the signed MoC; new applications must go through a registered employment agent <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21), [18](#o18), [36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. The Labour Department's published time is 7 working days, or 18 for construction; the BOESL process note allows about 14 to 28 working days, depending on security vetting <sup>[18](#o18), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Construction employers are inspected before and after approval, others within 3 to 6 months after <sup>[18](#o18)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-3"></a>**The deposit** is verified and stamped on the licence at the Department of Labour <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-4"></a>**The employment visa.** The agency applies to Immigration with the licence, the job order from the Department of Labour, your passport copy, the visa form, Form 23, photos and its agency representative card; it takes about 5 working days <sup>[11](#o11), [21 (2024)](#o21), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Immigration issues a work visa approval letter and faxes it to Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
5. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-5"></a>**The High Commission's visa attestation.** The Brunei agency takes the visa to the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing for attestation with <sup>[44](#o44)</sup>:
   - an application letter on agency and company letterhead;
   - BOESL's confirmation letter;
   - the visa and the licence (LD), originals and copies;
   - the insurance, original and copy;
   - a copy of the attested demand letter;
   - the bank payment receipt;
   - your passport copy;
   - for more than 5 workers, the licences of all the company's workers and 3 months' wage receipts of all its Bangladeshi workers.

   The fee, B$15 a worker, goes to the High Commission's account at Bank Islam Brunei Darussalam (BIBD), 00-001-01-0072146 <sup>[20](#o20), [44](#o44)</sup>. For the demand letter the High Commission asks for the agency's and company's demand letters, the worker's contract with company seal and signature, a valid JobCentre approval and the company's registration papers <sup>[22](#o22)</sup>. For demand letters and visas it also checks the company's bank statements, its list of existing workers, its projects and the address, capacity and photos of its staff house (labour camp) <sup>[22](#o22), [20](#o20)</sup>. BOESL's demand letter form asks the employer to fill in the salary, overtime, allowances, contract period, housing, food, medical care, insurance, air fare, leave, working hours, transport and death compensation <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
6. <a id="step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-6"></a>**The agency sends the approved licence and visa to BOESL** <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

**Check it was done:** check the company is not on the High Commission's list of 29 blacklisted Brunei companies, on bandarseribegawan.mofa.gov.bd under "Blacklisted Companies" <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>.

> **Need to know**

<a id="step-5-before-you-fly"></a>

### 4.6 Step 5: Before you fly

1. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-1"></a>**Visa endorsement.** You take your passport, the medical report and related papers to Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka, which endorses the work visa in your passport <sup>[11](#o11), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

   > **Need to know**
   > - In practice, does BOESL take the passports to Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka, or must each worker go in person, and how long does endorsement take?

2. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-2"></a>**The pre-departure orientation (PDO).** BOESL's process includes registration and fingerprints, the BMET smart card and the PEPT (pre-exit process and training) or PDO <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. The compulsory 3-day PDO is given at 70 training institutes; book it on OEP and pay the institute's fee against a money receipt <sup>[12](#o12), [38](#o38)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-3"></a>**BMET clearance (the smart card).** The smart card is your emigration clearance; with it you need no separate Welfare Board membership, your family gets a Tk 3 lakh grant from the Board if you die in Brunei, and you are insured ([Step 5, item 4: Know your insurance](#step-5-before-you-fly-item-4)) <sup>[13](#o13), [45](#o45), [38](#o38)</sup>. The recruiting body applies after the visa; BMET's emigration branch clears within 3 working days <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. Fees:
   - welfare fee: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 <sup>[46](#o46)</sup>;
   - insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>;
   - clearance (smart card) fee: Tk 250 <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>;
   - source income tax: Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>.

   **Check it is real** at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>. Welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards before departure <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. **If it goes wrong:** BMET's Director (Emigration), +8802222222415, 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>.

4. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-4"></a>**Know your insurance.** Workers who leave with BMET clearance are insured by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC) for Tk 10 lakh, aged 18 to 55, for the one-time Tk 1,000, for 5 years from the card's date <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47), [38](#o38)</sup>. It pays Tk 10 lakh for death or total permanent disability, 10% to 90% for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going, but not if you went entirely at the employer's cost <sup>[38](#o38), [47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>. The Welfare Board checks claims and JBC pays through it <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>.
5. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-5"></a>**If you need a loan,** PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank, the government bank for migrant workers) lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period <sup>[48](#o48)</sup>. You must be 18 to 60, not in default, and you and your guarantors must live in the branch's area <sup>[48](#o48)</sup>.

   You'll need <sup>[48](#o48)</sup>:
   - the free application form and 3 photos;
   - copies of your NID, passport, visa and BMET card;
   - 3 signed MICR (machine-readable) cheque leaves from your own active bank account, with its statement;
   - a PKB savings account opened with at least Tk 500;
   - two guarantors: one financially solvent person with 3 signed cheques, a statement and proof of income, and one family member.

   1. Apply at the PKB branch for your permanent address, or online on mygov.bd; you pay nothing when you submit <sup>[49 (2019)](#o49)</sup>.
   2. After approval, pay processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each plus VAT, and Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover, plus stamps <sup>[48](#o48)</sup>.

   **If it goes wrong:** PKB helpline 16238, +880 9677-787878 from abroad <sup>[48](#o48)</sup>; loans department 01716-175560, migration@pkb.gov.bd <sup>[50](#o50)</sup>.

6. <a id="step-5-before-you-fly-item-6"></a>**The flight.** BOESL must send you within 15 working days of receiving the visa; it tells the Brunei agency you are ready, and the air ticket is bought by the agency or the employer <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Carry <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>:
   - a valid passport;
   - the visa;
   - the medical certificate;
   - the contract and job details;
   - BOESL's name, address and phone;
   - the BMET smart card;
   - the air ticket.

   Keep one set of copies of all papers at home and at least 3 with you, and open at least 2 bank accounts before going, one in your own name and one in a family member's <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>.

<a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks"></a>

### 4.7 Step 6: Arrival and your first weeks

1. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-1"></a>**At the airport** the work visa is stamped and an employment pass valid for 3 months is endorsed, to give time for the medical and insurance; the Brunei agency picks you up <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-2"></a>**Register at the High Commission within 15 days** of arrival, for its records <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-3"></a>**Medical in Brunei.** You are registered on Bru-HIMS (the health ministry's patient system) and BruHealth (its app), and have a blood test and X-ray <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. The Ministry of Health's Foreign Worker Health Screening issues the medical fitness certificate Immigration needs for your pass; centres are in Berakas, Tutong, Belait and Temburong <sup>[43](#o43)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-4"></a>**Insurance.** The employer buys your medical insurance and workmen's compensation insurance; Immigration will not endorse the pass without the medical insurance <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>.
5. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-5"></a>**Sign the written contract** with the employer <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. The Department of Labour gives foreign workers a compulsory briefing on their labour rights when they sign <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. It must be in writing and signed by both of you, and show at least your job, start date, contract length, daily hours, working days, rest day, basic salary, deductions, overtime rate, leave, medical benefits, probation, notice period and repatriation <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
6. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-6"></a>**The 2-year pass.** The agency or employer applies to Immigration to extend the pass, endorsed in your passport for 2 years or as the licence allows <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. Immigration needs the application letter, registration form, Form 23, visa approval, licence copy, the medical check and your passport valid at least 6 months <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
7. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-7"></a>**Green IC.** You then apply for a green identity card (the card Brunei issues to foreign residents) at Immigration with your passport and approved pass; every foreigner holding a pass for more than 3 months must register for one <sup>[18](#o18), [21 (2024)](#o21), [15](#o15)</sup>.
8. <a id="step-6-arrival-and-your-first-weeks-item-8"></a>**SIM card and bank account.** Register a prepaid SIM with your green IC, or with your passport and employment pass; DST's Easi SIM pack costs nothing and is activated with a BND 10 recharge within 30 days <sup>[51 (2024)](#o51), [N42](#n42)</sup>. To open a BIBD (Bank Islam Brunei Darussalam) General Saver account you need a valid work permit, your identity card or passport and proof of employment, such as your latest salary slip or employment letter, and must keep at least BND 50 in it <sup>[N43](#n43)</sup>.

<a id="work-visa-scams"></a>

### 4.8 Work visa scams

<a id="done-to-workers"></a>

#### 4.8.1 Done to workers

**"Free visa" with no job.** Before 2024, recruiters charged about BND 5,000 to bring each worker, many without a confirmed job; workers and High Commission sources said in 2020 that of about 22,000 visas issued in late 2019 the mission certified only 3,300, and recruiters sent the rest through "body contact" with dishonest airport officials <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>. One worker paid Tk 5 lakh for a construction "free visa", found no job and came home after five months <sup>[N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>. Recruiters still take thousands of dollars and then find workers no job on arrival <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. **How to spot it:** any Brunei job not offered through BOESL ([Step 2: Apply through BOESL](#step-2-apply-through-boesl)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

**Company visas from blacklisted or shell firms.** Brunei blacklisted at least 31 fake companies in 2019 for visa trading and harassing Bangladeshi workers, and the High Commission rejected attestation for fake company visas <sup>[N23 (2021)](#n23), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>. Its current blacklist has 29 companies <sup>[10](#o10)</sup>. **How to spot it:** a company on that list, or one whose visa the High Commission has not attested ([Step 4, item 5: The High Commission's visa attestation](#step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa-item-5)).

**The broker who keeps your passport and takes a monthly cut.** In 2025 a Bangladeshi, Mahedi Hasan, got 5 years' jail, a BND 50,000 fine and six strokes for trafficking workers in 2018-19: he lured them with false job promises, took their passports, made them pay monthly "legal" or "repatriation" fees and placed them in jobs with little or no pay <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. In 2019 another worker said his agent dropped him after a week's work and threatened to have him deported unless he handed over part of his monthly salary <sup>[N44 (2019)](#n44)</sup>.

**Rule-breaking on the official route.** Workers recruited under the MoU are still exploited when employers or middlemen break the recruitment rules or the contract <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Brunei penalised six unlicensed recruiters and sub-brokers with fines of BND 1,000 to 2,000, and made four licensed agencies refund fees they had overcharged domestic workers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

**Brokers who go unpunished at home.** In 2019 Bangladesh suspended the passports of five suspected traffickers in Brunei to force their deportation; Brunei deported at least seven, but none of the ringleaders had been punished in Bangladesh by 2020 <sup>[N44 (2019)](#n44), [N4 (2020)](#n4)</sup>.

**Visa traders inside the company.** The recruitment SOP (standard operating procedure) the High Commission set out in 2022 forbids employers to use their existing workers as manpower brokers and visa traders <sup>[N28 (2022)](#n28)</sup>.

**Fake job adverts and "illegal visa" offers online.** The High Commission tells workers not to get involved with fake job adverts, illegal visas, quick-money offers or suspicious cryptocurrency schemes <sup>[52](#o52)</sup>.

**Remittance agent who pockets the money.** In September 2024 the High Commission said 116 Bangladeshi workers had complained that a Western Union agent took BND 0.72 million from them, gave fake receipts and never sent the money (see [Use a licensed remitter or bank](#use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank)) <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

**In Bangladesh:** taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, or keeping a migrant's passport, is a crime under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013, with up to 5 years' jail and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh; these offences are non-bailable <sup>[53](#o53)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - In 2026, are brokers still selling Brunei "company visas" outside BOESL, and at what price?
> - Are visit visas being sold in Bangladesh as Brunei jobs, and how do those workers end up?

<a id="tried-by-workers"></a>

#### 4.8.2 Tried by workers

**Going on a visit visa to look for work.** A visit pass holder may not do any work, paid or unpaid, without Immigration's written consent: a fine of up to BND 2,000, up to 6 months' jail or both <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.

**Freelancing.** Working for anyone other than the employer on your pass is illegal <sup>[N20](#n20), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. In February 2026 Immigration detained a foreign worker suspected of overstaying and working for someone other than his sponsor <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>.

**False papers.** Getting a pass by a false statement, or using a forged or altered pass, carries a fine of up to BND 4,000, up to a year in jail or both <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.

**Returning after deportation.** Coming back after removal without Immigration's written authority carries a BND 6,000 fine, 1 to 3 years' jail and removal again <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.

<a id="your-rights-at-work"></a>

### 4.9 Your rights at work

<a id="which-law-covers-you"></a>

#### 4.9.1 Which law covers you

The Employment Order 2009 covers private-sector workers below managerial level; it does not cover domestic workers (except some provisions) or seamen <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. An employer who breaks its rules faces a compound fine (an out-of-court penalty) of up to BND 1,000, or on conviction a fine of up to BND 3,000 (for contracts, pay and rest-day pay) or BND 1,600 (for hours, rest days, public holidays, annual and sick leave), a year's jail or both <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Did the revision of the Employment Order 2009 into the Employment Act (Chapter 278) change any of the rights below?

<a id="pay"></a>

#### 4.9.2 Pay

1. <a id="pay-item-1"></a>**No minimum wage for most Bangladeshi jobs.** Brunei's minimum wage (BND 500 a month, BND 2.62 an hour part-time) covers local and foreign workers only in selected industries: banking, finance and ICT since July 2023, and nine sub-industries in financial services, health, education, professional, administrative and accommodation and food services since April 2025 <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36), [N31](#n31)</sup>. It does not cover domestic workers, and it had not been extended to construction <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36), [N20](#n20)</sup>. Where it applies, it is the base salary before contributions and allowances, and no contract can lower it <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. In 2024 the High Commission said it aimed for Bangladeshi workers to earn at least BND 600 a month <sup>[N7 (2024)](#n7)</sup>.
2. <a id="pay-item-2"></a>**When you must be paid.** Salary at least once a month, within 7 days after the end of the salary period; overtime within 14 days <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Not paying salary is a breach of contract by the employer <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
3. <a id="pay-item-3"></a>**What may be deducted:** absence, damage or loss of goods or money, meals supplied at your request, amenities and services, advances or loans, income tax, and others the Minister approves <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. The Brunei agency may not deduct anything <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

<a id="hours-rest-and-leave"></a>

#### 4.9.3 Hours, rest and leave

- **Hours:** at most 8 hours a day or 44 a week; never more than 12 hours a day including overtime <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Overtime:** 1.5 times your hourly basic rate, at most 72 hours a month <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Rest day:** 1 a week, unpaid, Sunday or a day the employer announces before the month <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Working on a rest day or public holiday at the employer's request earns 1 day's pay for up to half a day, 2 days' pay for more, and overtime beyond normal hours <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Public holidays:** 11 paid days, including 23 February (National Day), 15 July (the Sultan's birthday), Hari Raya Aidilfitri and Aidiladha, the first day of Ramadhan and 25 December <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Annual leave:** after 3 months' service, at least 7 days in the first year, rising by a day a year to 14 <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Maternity leave:** a foreign woman employee gets 9 weeks (4 before the birth and 5 after), 8 of them paid, after more than 180 days' service <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
- **Sick leave:** after 6 months' service, 14 days' outpatient sick leave a year and up to 60 days in hospital (including the 14), if a doctor certifies you and you tell the employer within 48 hours <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

<a id="housing-food-and-medical-care"></a>

#### 4.9.4 Housing, food and medical care

Under the BOESL MoC the employer provides free housing, free food or a food allowance, and medicine <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. BOESL's 2026 circulars say the employer pays for housing, but the September 2026 circular makes the worker pay for his food (see [What the employer pays](#what-the-employer-pays)) <sup>[N15](#n15), [4](#o4)</sup>. The High Commission checks a company's staff house address, capacity and photos before attesting visas <sup>[20](#o20)</sup>. Medical insurance and workmen's compensation are the employer's to buy <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does Brunei set minimum standards for foreign workers' staff houses (space per worker, toilets), and who inspects them?

<a id="your-passport"></a>

#### 4.9.5 Your passport

Some employers and agencies in Brunei keep workers' passports <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Workers whose passports were kept have lost their legal status, and one was given only a special pass by Immigration <sup>[N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>. In Bangladesh, keeping a migrant's passport without lawful reason is a crime under the 2013 Act <sup>[53](#o53)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which Brunei law, if any, makes it an offence for an employer to keep a worker's passport, and has anyone been charged?

<a id="changing-your-job-inside-the-company"></a>

#### 4.9.6 Changing your job inside the company

You must do only the job written in your pass and licence, at the licensed address; the employer may not move you to other work or another workplace <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19), [36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. In 2026 the Department of Labour found licensed "construction workers" making furniture and a "general labourer" doing office work, and investigated both as suspected licence breaches by the employer <sup>[N32](#n32), [N21](#n21)</sup>. Breaking licence conditions can cost the employer a compound of up to BND 1,000, or a fine of BND 6,000 to 10,000, 6 months to 3 years in jail, or both <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

<a id="your-work-pass-and-stay"></a>

### 4.10 Your work pass and stay

- **How long:** you arrive on a 3-month pass, which is then extended for 2 years or as the licence allows <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. An employment pass can run up to 5 years and be extended up to 5 years at a time <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
- **Keep it valid:** Immigration can act against you under the Immigration Act if the pass in your passport lapses <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>.
- **Renewal:** renewing the pass needs a new medical check <sup>[11](#o11), [43](#o43)</sup>. The contract is 2 years and renewable by agreement <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
- **Going home on leave:** you need a re-entry visa from Immigration's Work Pass Section to come back <sup>[11](#o11), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Single visas cost BND 20 and multiple visas BND 30 to 50 <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>.

<a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported"></a>

### 4.11 What can get you jailed or deported

1. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-1"></a>**Overstaying.** Staying after your pass expires or is cancelled: up to 90 days, a fine of up to BND 4,000, up to 6 months' jail or both; over 90 days, 3 months to 2 years in jail and at least 3 strokes of the cane <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>. The offence can also be settled by a compound payment of up to BND 600 <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>. In April 2026 two Bangladeshis got 5 and 6 months' jail and three strokes each <sup>[N45](#n45)</sup>. In 2025 the High Commission recorded 19 Bangladeshis in prison, and 30 by October 2026 <sup>[54](#o54)</sup>.
2. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-2"></a>**Entering without a valid pass:** 3 months to 2 years in jail and at least 3 strokes <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
3. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-3"></a>**Not taking up, or leaving, the job on your pass:** a fine of up to BND 2,000, up to 6 months' jail or both; the employer must report you to Immigration at once <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
4. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-4"></a>**Working on a visit pass, freelancing, false papers, returning after removal** (see [Tried by workers](#tried-by-workers)) <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
5. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-5"></a>**Absence:** being away from work for more than 2 days without leave, a reasonable excuse or telling the employer is a breach of contract by you <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
6. <a id="what-can-get-you-jailed-or-deported-item-6"></a>**What you post online.** The High Commission tells workers not to post their own or others' passport, visa or bank details, or photograph workplaces, airports, ports or government and security sites <sup>[52](#o52)</sup>.

<a id="if-something-goes-wrong"></a>

### 4.12 If something goes wrong

<a id="if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law"></a>

#### 4.12.1 If you aren't paid or your employer breaks the law

1. <a id="if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law-item-1"></a>**Report at the nearest police station** and get the official police report <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law-item-2"></a>**Go in person to the Department of Labour's Labour Enforcement Division** (or its nearest branch) to make a formal complaint; no one can complain for you <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

   You'll need <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>:
   - the police report;
   - your identity card;
   - your passport;
   - your contract;
   - your job card or punch card;
   - any other relevant papers.

   Contacts: hotline 7298989 (also WhatsApp) or 2381848 (working hours only), led@buruh.gov.bn or info.buruh@buruh.gov.bn; Department of Labour, Jalan Dewan Majlis, Bandar Seri Begawan BS3910 <sup>[15](#o15), [36 (2024)](#o36), [N34](#n34)</sup>. Your details are kept confidential as far as the case allows <sup>[15](#o15)</sup>. You can also complain online on nlms.gov.bn under "Aduan Pelanggaran Undang-Undang Perburuhan" <sup>[N31](#n31)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law-item-3"></a>**What it can do:** mediation, a warning letter, a compound fine, prosecution or revoking the employer's foreign worker licence <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

**What actually happens:** an employer found at fault can settle each offence with a compound fine of up to BND 1,000, and goes to court only if it does not pay <sup>[N32](#n32)</sup>. In August 2025 two company owners who had not paid two foreign workers for five months were fined BND 1,200 each, and BND 1,000 per worker was still unpaid <sup>[N34](#n34)</sup>. Raids in 2026 found unpaid overtime, unlawful deductions, no rest days and no written contracts at 10 companies in one operation <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Department of Labour order unpaid wages to be paid to the worker, and can a worker stay and work elsewhere while the case runs?

<a id="help-from-the-high-commission"></a>

#### 4.12.2 Help from the High Commission

The labour wing of the Bangladesh High Commission takes complaints on its complaint sheet (your name, passport and IC number, phone, company and agent details, and what happened) <sup>[55](#o55)</sup>. It records the complaint, calls both sides for mediation and, if that fails, refers the case to the Department of Labour, Immigration or the Royal Brunei Police Force <sup>[56](#o56)</sup>. It received 54 complaints from January to early September 2026 <sup>[56](#o56)</sup>. Unsettled complaints from workers or their families must also be reported by the Brunei agency to the Department of Labour and the High Commission <sup>[21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>. A High Commission officer visits Immigration every Tuesday to help Bangladeshi workers, and the mission provides interpreters in court cases <sup>[57](#o57), [58](#o58)</sup>.

**Labour wing:** First Secretary (Labour), +6732342460, fsbrunei@probashi.gov.bd <sup>[16](#o16)</sup>; Administrative Officer (Labour), 2342420 extension 104, labourwingbrunei@gmail.com <sup>[59](#o59)</sup>.

<a id="if-you-are-trafficked"></a>

#### 4.12.3 If you are trafficked

Brunei's Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act 2019 carries 4 to 30 years' jail and fines of BND 10,000 to 1 million <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Brunei gives identified and possible trafficking victims a work permit while the case runs <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Victims are not prosecuted for illegal entry, unlawful stay or false papers that resulted directly from being trafficked <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. The Labour Department also takes trafficking and labour tips by hotline and online, and in the 2026 reporting period these led to 250 screenings for signs of trafficking <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Report illegal immigration activity to Immigration's hotline, 8734888 or 8753888 (Bandar Seri Begawan) and 8984111 (Kuala Belait) <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>.

<a id="complaining-in-bangladesh"></a>

#### 4.12.4 Complaining in Bangladesh

Complain about a recruiter to BMET's Director General with proof (free, 30 working days) <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>, or to the Ministry's enforcement branch with your application, proof, full address and the agency's details, settled within 30 working days <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>. If BMET's officer does not resolve it, complain to BMET's complaint officer, the Director (Employment), 01711111544, diremi@bmet.gov.bd, and then appeal to the Secretary of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, secretary@probashi.gov.bd <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>.

<a id="changing-employer-or-leaving"></a>

### 4.13 Changing employer or leaving

<a id="changing-employer"></a>

#### 4.13.1 Changing employer

Your pass and licence tie you to one employer; your employer may not transfer you to another without the consent of the Commissioner of Labour <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5), [36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. A "transfer of contract" is a licence application made through NLMS <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Immigration then endorses the new pass with the Department of Labour's contract transfer form (Borang Pertukaran Kontrak), the registration form, Form 23, the licence copy, a medical check and your new and old passports <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. If your company is restructured and your contract moves to another company, the new employer takes it over on the same terms, and you must be told and consulted <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Working for a new employer before this is done is an immigration offence <sup>[N36](#n36)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does a worker need his current employer's release letter to transfer, and can he transfer if the employer has not paid him?

<a id="resigning-and-notice"></a>

#### 4.13.2 Resigning and notice

Notice is what your contract says, and must be the same for both sides; if none is set, the Department of Labour's guideline applies: 1 day under 26 weeks' service, 1 week up to 2 years, 2 weeks up to 5 years, 4 weeks after that <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. You may leave without notice if you are mistreated or threatened with violence or disease <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Before dismissing you for misconduct, the employer should hold an inquiry where you hear the charge and can answer it <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

<a id="leaving-brunei"></a>

#### 4.13.3 Leaving Brunei

1. The employer cancels your pass at Immigration with cancellation forms, your passport, the licence copy and a copy of your air ticket <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
2. The employer pays your ticket home at the end of the contract, unless you absconded or were convicted <sup>[N41](#n41), [21 (2024)](#o21)</sup>.
3. Leave with your employer's approval: workers who leave without it face a BND 600 fine <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What is the BND 600 "exit fine" in law, and how does a worker whose employer refuses approval leave legally?

<a id="work-visa-domestic-worker"></a>

## 5 Work visa: domestic worker

Brunei issues a Domestic Service Work Pass (pas kerja perkhidmatan rumah tangga) for foreign domestic helpers, handled by the Work Pass Section of the Immigration and National Registration Department (JIPK, Jabatan Imigresen dan Pendaftaran Kebangsaan) <sup>[11](#o11), [19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Few Bangladeshi women go to Brunei for work: BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) counted 152 from 2004 to May 2025, including 9 in 2024 and 50 in the first five months of 2025 <sup>[61](#o61)</sup>. Most of the employer's steps are the same as for a company worker ([Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass), [Step 4: The employer gets the licence and your visa](#step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa)).

**What makes this pass different:**

- **Fewer legal protections.** The Employment Order 2009, Brunei's main labour law, does not cover domestic workers except for some provisions <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. The minimum wage has not been extended to domestic work <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- **Recruitment fees.** Brunei agencies may charge up to BND 2,000 per migrant worker, and the employer must pay it; observers say fees can run higher, with employers passing part of them on to workers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. In the period covered by the US State Department's 2026 trafficking report, Brunei acted against four licensed agencies that charged domestic workers more than the limit; they got a warning letter and had to refund the excess <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- **The risks reported.** Migrant domestic workers in Brunei face deceptive recruitment, contract switching, debt, unauthorised deductions, unpaid wages, passport retention and being kept indoors <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. In a 2015 study of Indonesian maids in Brunei, most first-timers had paid most of their own costs before coming; one paid about BND 200 for her work permit, and another borrowed from her future Brunei employer and lost about half her pay each month until the debt was cleared <sup>[N46 (2015)](#n46)</sup>.
- **Insurance.** Since 1 July 2025, domestic service work pass holders must have medical insurance valid for the whole pass <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>.

<a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers"></a>

### 5.1 Step 1: Check Bangladesh's rules for women domestic workers

1. <a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers-item-1"></a>**Age and guardian's consent.** For BMET emigration clearance, a woman going abroad as a domestic worker must show proof that she is aged 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers-item-2"></a>**The housekeeping course.** BMET makes it compulsory for women going to Hong Kong and the Middle East for domestic work, at 41 TTCs (Technical Training Centres) <sup>[12](#o12)</sup>. It lasts one month (24 working days) and is residential <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>. BMET posts the admission notice online; apply online, pay the fee, and choose the centre and date <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers-item-3"></a>**Who can enrol:** women aged 25 to 45 at enrolment (returnees up to 50) who passed class 5 (relaxable in special cases) and are physically and mentally fit <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>. A woman whose youngest child is under 6, who is pregnant, or who has a contagious or incurable disease cannot enrol <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers-item-4"></a>**Papers for the course:** proof of class 5, a no-objection letter from your legal guardian, and a photocopy of your birth certificate, NID (national identity card) or passport <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>.
5. <a id="step-1-check-bangladeshs-rules-for-women-domestic-workers-item-5"></a>**Certificate validity:** 2 years for a first departure; between 2 and 3 years after issue you take a 3-day refresher, and after 3 years you repeat the full course <sup>[62](#o62)</sup>.

> **Need to know**

<a id="step-2-the-employer-applies-in-brunei"></a>

### 5.2 Step 2: The employer applies in Brunei

1. <a id="step-2-the-employer-applies-in-brunei-item-1"></a>**The licence.** The employer gets a Foreign Worker Licence from the Department of Labour, as for other foreign workers ([Work visa: employment pass](#work-visa-employment-pass), [Step 4: The employer gets the licence and your visa](#step-4-the-employer-gets-the-licence-and-your-visa)) <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-2-the-employer-applies-in-brunei-item-2"></a>**The work pass.** For a domestic worker, Immigration endorses the pass with the domestic work pass form (Borang Penyambungan Pas Kerja, Perkhidmatan Rumah Tangga), a copy of the visa approval, the licence application form to recruit and employ a foreign worker, a medical check (blood test and X-ray, original and copy) and your passport, valid for at least 6 more months <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.

**Check it was done:** Brunei Immigration sends the work visa approval to its High Commission in Dhaka, which needs a copy of the Visa Advice from Brunei Immigration to issue your employment visa <sup>[11](#o11), [32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-3-employment-visa-at-bruneis-high-commission-in-dhaka"></a>

### 5.3 Step 3: Employment visa at Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka

You'll need <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>:

- the visa application form and two photos;
- copies of your NID and birth certificate, and of your passport, previous visas and endorsed pages;
- a passport valid at least 6 months on entry, with four blank pages;
- a copy of the Visa Advice from Brunei Immigration, valid six months from issue;
- a medical test from an enlisted medical centre (original and copy), still valid when you enter Brunei;
- a confirmed air ticket (original and copy);
- printed medical insurance bought by the employer, with at least BND 100,000 cover for the whole pass;
- Tk 2,100 visa fee in cash, non-refundable.

Processing normally takes five working days, not counting the day you submit <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Fill in the E-Arrival Card on imm.gov.bn before you fly (see [Visit visa](#visit-visa), [Step 5: E-Arrival Card](#step-5-e-arrival-card)) <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-4-after-you-arrive"></a>

### 5.4 Step 4: After you arrive

Soon after arrival you must give Immigration a medical fit certificate from Brunei's Ministry of Health <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. A worker who arrives without valid insurance gets only a two-week pass until the papers are produced <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>. You may work only in the job written in your passport endorsement <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>. Keep the pass valid: an expired pass is an offence under the Immigration Act, Chapter 17 <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19)</sup>.

**Scams to watch for:**
- *An agency fee beyond the legal limit.* Brunei made four licensed agencies refund what they had overcharged domestic workers, and fined six unlicensed recruiters and sub-brokers <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- *Working on a visit visa.* A visit pass holder may not work at all, paid or unpaid (see [Tried by workers](#tried-by-workers)) <sup>[5 (2024)](#o5)</sup>.
- *Passport kept, kept indoors.* Both are among the abuses reported against migrant domestic workers in Brunei (see [Your passport](#your-passport)) <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

**Where to get help:** the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing in Bandar Seri Begawan (see [Help from the High Commission](#help-from-the-high-commission)), or Probash-Bondhu, 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad <sup>[14](#o14)</sup>.

<a id="visit-visa"></a>

## 6 Visit visa

A Brunei visit visa (social visit visa) is for social visits and holidays only <sup>[63](#o63), [32](#o32)</sup>. You can get one in two ways:

- **Yourself, at Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka,** for a tourist trip <sup>[33](#o33), [32](#o32)</sup>.
- **Through a sponsor in Brunei** (a relative or friend), who applies to Immigration in Brunei; the approval is then stamped at the High Commission <sup>[64](#o64), [32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="cost-time-and-stay"></a>

### 6.1 Cost, time and stay

- **Fee:** Tk 2,100 for a single entry, in cash, not refunded if refused <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
- **Processing:** usually five working days, sometimes longer; apply at least two weeks before travel <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. The High Commission does not speed up a file because you already hold a ticket <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
- **Validity:** the visa is valid for up to three months <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
- **Length of stay:** with valid insurance, Immigration gives a visit pass matching the approved visa; without it, only a two-week pass, which can be extended by showing an insurance certificate to Immigration <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many days of stay does the High Commission in Dhaka usually grant on a tourist visit visa: 14, 30 or more?
> - How does a visitor extend a visit pass inside Brunei, at which counter, and for what fee?

<a id="step-1-prepare-the-papers"></a>

### 6.2 Step 1: Prepare the papers

You'll need <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>:

- one visa application form per person and two recent passport photos;
- copies of your NID and birth certificate;
- a colour copy of your passport, previous visas and endorsed pages; if old visas are in an old passport, bring the old passport too;
- a passport valid at least six months beyond your entry date, with at least four blank pages;
- two copies of a confirmed flight booking, and a confirmed hotel booking if needed.

**Proof of money.** Bring the original bank statement for the last six months, a bank solvency certificate, your cheque book and ATM card, and your TIN (Tax Identification Number) certificate with income tax returns <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. A business account needs a certified copy of the trade licence with an official English translation <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. The balance must be at least Tk 300,000, kept steadily over the six months <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Recent large deposits are not accepted even if they reach the minimum, nor are one-time large deposits withdrawn the same or next day, even if repeated over several months <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Credit card statements, SOD (secured overdraft) and loan-linked accounts do not count <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

**If invited:** an invitation letter from your sponsor, authenticated by Brunei Immigration or a Brunei court, and a certificate from your employer or business associate, if asked <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

**Translations:** any document not in English needs a notarised English translation, or the file is returned unprocessed <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does the High Commission accept a visit visa file from someone with no sponsor in Brunei, on a hotel booking alone?

<a id="step-2-buy-medical-insurance"></a>

### 6.3 Step 2: Buy medical insurance

Every visitor needs printed travel health insurance, shown both when applying and at the Brunei border; digital copies and confirmation emails are refused <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. For a visit visa the cover must be at least BND 10,000 per person, for the whole stay, including emergency treatment and hospitalisation <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. For a tourist visit under 14 days, a local or international insurer is accepted; for a social visit approved by Brunei Immigration, the insurance must come from a Brunei insurer, arranged by the sponsor <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - For a tourist visit under 14 days, does "local" insurer mean a Bangladeshi insurer, and which insurer does the High Commission require for a tourist visit of 14 days or more?

<a id="step-3-if-a-sponsor-in-brunei-applies-for-you"></a>

### 6.4 Step 3: If a sponsor in Brunei applies for you

The sponsor applies to Brunei Immigration: its Visa, Visit and Diplomatic Section is on the 2nd floor of Immigration headquarters, Jalan Menteri Besar, Bandar Seri Begawan, and it also has branch offices <sup>[63](#o63)</sup>. The sponsor brings <sup>[64](#o64)</sup>:

- an application letter, the visa form and Form 8 (application for a temporary stay, which names the sponsor and the purpose, length and address of the stay) <sup>[65](#o65)</sup>;
- a copy of your passport, valid six months, and two photos;
- the sponsor's smart identity card, original and copy;
- if the sponsor is a foreign worker: passport and work pass valid at least six more months, labour licence, BUR 500/555 (Department of Labour forms), licence renewal receipt if expired, salary slip, and the employer's permission letter if the sponsor lives with the employer;
- proof of relationship, such as a marriage or birth certificate;
- two sets of papers for non-ASEAN nationals such as Bangladeshis.

Incomplete files are not accepted, and papers not in Malay or English need an official translation from the relevant embassy in Brunei <sup>[64](#o64)</sup>. Once approved, take the approval with the papers in [Step 1: Prepare the papers](#step-1-prepare-the-papers) to the High Commission in Dhaka <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-4-submit-at-the-high-commission-in-dhaka"></a>

### 6.5 Step 4: Submit at the High Commission in Dhaka

1. <a id="step-4-submit-at-the-high-commission-in-dhaka-item-1"></a>**Go in person** to the High Commission of Brunei Darussalam, House 26, Road 6, Baridhara, Dhaka-1212 <sup>[66](#o66), [33](#o33)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-4-submit-at-the-high-commission-in-dhaka-item-2"></a>**Hand in the file between 9.30 and 11 am,** Sunday to Thursday <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Pay the Tk 2,100 in cash <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-4-submit-at-the-high-commission-in-dhaka-item-3"></a>**Be ready for an interview,** in person or by phone <sup>[32](#o32), [33](#o33)</sup>. The High Commission may ask for more documents <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-4-submit-at-the-high-commission-in-dhaka-item-4"></a>**Collect the passport between 2.30 and 3 pm** on the day you are told <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-5-e-arrival-card"></a>

### 6.6 Step 5: E-Arrival Card

1. <a id="step-5-e-arrival-card-item-1"></a>**Fill it in after the visa is issued and before you arrive** <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-5-e-arrival-card-item-2"></a>**Go to www.imm.gov.bn,** click "Register Now" if you are new, and sign in with the email and password you registered on Immigration's online portal (Brunei INRS) <sup>[67 (2023)](#o67)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-5-e-arrival-card-item-3"></a>**Go to "Travel Cards", then "Create Travel Card",** fill in identity, general, arrival and departure details, and press "Create"; you get a reference number <sup>[67 (2023)](#o67)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-5-e-arrival-card-item-4"></a>**It is free.** Immigration charges nothing and has appointed no person or company to collect a fee for it <sup>[N39 (2023)](#n39)</sup>. It is compulsory at air, land and sea entry points <sup>[N39 (2023)](#n39)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many days before arrival can the E-Arrival Card be filled in at the earliest?

<a id="step-6-at-the-brunei-border"></a>

### 6.7 Step 6: At the Brunei border

Show your passport with the visa and your printed insurance <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Without valid insurance you get only a two-week pass <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>.

<a id="can-you-work"></a>

### 6.8 Can you work?

No. The visit visa is for social and holiday visits only <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Every foreign worker in Brunei must hold a valid visa and work pass <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. Working in a job other than the one endorsed in your passport is an offence under Regulation 15(3) of the Immigration Regulations, and Immigration's "Operasi Kakas" raids find such workers <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19), [N48](#n48)</sup>. Overstaying is an offence under section 15(1) of the Immigration Act: Bangladeshis convicted in 2024 to 2026 got five or six months' jail and three strokes of the cane <sup>[N49 (2024)](#n49), [N50](#n50), [N48](#n48)</sup>.

<a id="if-you-are-refused"></a>

### 6.9 If you are refused

The fee is not refunded <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. If any document is found false, the application is cancelled at once <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does the High Commission give a reason for refusal, and how long must an applicant wait before applying again?

<a id="visit-visa-scams"></a>

### 6.10 Visit visa scams

<a id="done-to-visitors"></a>

#### 6.10.1 Done to visitors

- **"Visit visa, then a job."** The visit visa is for holidays only, and a business visit visa may not be used to wait for a labour quota or to work <sup>[32](#o32), [63](#o63)</sup>. Some recruiters take thousands of dollars in fees and then find workers no job in Brunei; those workers, most of them in debt, often turn to "freelancing", which is illegal <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.
- **Paid E-Arrival Card sites.** The card is free on imm.gov.bn; anyone charging for it is not appointed by Immigration <sup>[N39 (2023)](#n39), [67 (2023)](#o67)</sup>.
- **Fake insurance certificates.** The applicant alone is responsible for the insurance certificate being real <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>; for a sponsored visit only a Brunei insurer counts <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="tried-by-visitors"></a>

#### 6.10.2 Tried by visitors

- **Borrowed bank balances.** Putting money in shortly before applying, or depositing and withdrawing it, is spotted and not accepted <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
- **False documents.** A false document cancels the application, and the fee is kept <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Brunei's own forms warn that all documents must be original and not falsified <sup>[68](#o68)</sup>.

<a id="where-to-get-help"></a>

### 6.11 Where to get help

- **Brunei High Commission, Dhaka, visa section:** +8802222261837 or +880258813304, 9 am to 1 pm and 2 to 4 pm; consular@bruneihcbd.com or dhaka.bangladesh@mfa.gov.bn <sup>[32](#o32), [66](#o66)</sup>.
- **Immigration headquarters, Brunei:** Jalan Menteri Besar, Bandar Seri Begawan BB3910; +673 729 9550; info@immigration.gov.bn <sup>[63](#o63), [69 (2024)](#o69)</sup>.
- **Bangladesh High Commission, Bandar Seri Begawan:** mission.bandarseribegawan@mofa.gov.bd <sup>[70](#o70)</sup>.

<a id="business-visit"></a>

## 7 Business visit

A business visit visa (BVV) is for investment or business: board meetings, expos, investing or exploring opportunities <sup>[63](#o63), [33](#o33)</sup>. It is not for waiting for a Department of Labour quota or for working with the company <sup>[63](#o63)</sup>. A professional visit visa is for professional work or expertise on a project awarded in Brunei <sup>[33](#o33)</sup>.

<a id="step-1-the-brunei-company-applies"></a>

### 7.1 Step 1: The Brunei company applies

1. <a id="step-1-the-brunei-company-applies-item-1"></a>**Who applies.** A registered business or government-linked company in Brunei applies on your behalf to the Visa and Diplomatic Section of Immigration <sup>[33](#o33)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-1-the-brunei-company-applies-item-2"></a>**What it files** <sup>[68](#o68), [71](#o71)</sup>:
   - an application letter from the company, the visa form and Form 8;
   - a copy of your passport (valid 6 months) and two photos;
   - the company's licences (Form 16 and 17, Form X or certificate of incorporation);
   - your trade licence or business certificate from Bangladesh, with an English or Malay translation certified by the consulate;
   - papers for the purpose: financial statements, business plan and company profile if you are starting a business, a meeting agenda, or permission for an expo;
   - for a professional visit: your CV and the project offer or appointment letter.
3. <a id="step-1-the-brunei-company-applies-item-3"></a>**Time.** About 3 working days at Immigration headquarters or Kuala Belait, 5 at Temburong, longer if checked further <sup>[33](#o33)</sup>.

<a id="step-2-get-the-visa-stamped-in-dhaka"></a>

### 7.2 Step 2: Get the visa stamped in Dhaka

Take the approval letter (Visa Advice, valid three months) to Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka with the visa form, photos, NID and birth certificate copies, passport copies and a confirmed air ticket <sup>[33](#o33), [32](#o32)</sup>. Printed insurance must cover at least BND 10,000 for visits under 90 days and BND 100,000 for longer ones <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. The fee is Tk 2,100, in cash and not refunded, and processing takes five working days <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

**If you are refused:** the fee is kept, and a false document cancels the application at once (see [If you are refused](#if-you-are-refused)) <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

**Scams to watch for:** a business visit visa sold as a way into a job; it may not be used to wait for a Labour Department quota or to work for the company, and working without a work pass is an offence (see [Tried by workers](#tried-by-workers)) <sup>[63](#o63), [11](#o11)</sup>.

**Where to get help:** Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka and Immigration headquarters in Brunei (see [Where to get help](#where-to-get-help)) <sup>[32](#o32), [63](#o63)</sup>.

<a id="long-term-business-and-professional-passes"></a>

### 7.3 Long-term business and professional passes

Brunei also has a long-term business visit pass for company owners and foreign investors, and a long-term professional visit pass for experts whose skills Brunei needs <sup>[72](#o72)</sup>. Applications go to the Long Term Pass Secretariat, 3rd floor, Immigration headquarters <sup>[63](#o63)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What capital, salary or qualification does Immigration require for a long-term business or professional pass?

<a id="student-visa"></a>

## 8 Student visa

Bangladeshis can study at Brunei's universities on a student pass, either fee-paying or on the Brunei Darussalam Government Scholarship (BDGS) <sup>[73](#o73), [74](#o74)</sup>. A student pass holder may not work at all <sup>[75 (2023)](#o75)</sup>.

<a id="the-scholarship-bdgs"></a>

### 8.1 The scholarship (BDGS)

Brunei's Ministry of Foreign Affairs offers scholarships for diplomas, bachelor's and master's degrees at Universiti Brunei Darussalam (UBD), Universiti Islam Sultan Sharif Ali (UNISSA), Universiti Teknologi Brunei (UTB), Kolej Universiti Perguruan Ugama Seri Begawan (KUPU SB) and Politeknik Brunei <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.

- **Who can apply:** age up to 25 for diploma and bachelor's, up to 35 for master's, on 1 July of the starting year; English at GCE O level credit 6, IGCSE grade C, IELTS 6.0 or TOEFL 550 <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>. Anyone who studies or studied at a Brunei university is not eligible <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.
- **When:** the 2026/2027 round closed on 15 February 2026 <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.
- **How:** apply online on the BDGS form, uploading your ID, certified copies of certificates and transcripts, certified English translations, birth certificate, passport and two references <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>. For UBD and UTB, also apply on the university's own portal (apply.ubd.edu.bn, apply.utb.edu.bn) <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>. You may be interviewed and given a written test in English, Malay or Arabic <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>. Queries: queriesBDGS2025@mfa.gov.bn <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.
- **What it pays:** free tuition, an economy return ticket, BND 500 a month personal allowance, BND 150 a month for meals, BND 600 a year for books, and a room at the university residence <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.
- **Rules:** no family allowance or family housing; no paid work; leave Brunei at once when the course ends <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - When does the 2027/2028 BDGS round open, and how many Bangladeshis received the scholarship in recent rounds?

<a id="fees-for-paying-students"></a>

### 8.2 Fees for paying students

- **UTB:** international undergraduate degrees cost, for example, BND 24,000 (business) to BND 27,000 (architecture, product design) per three-year programme; you pay one semester (half a year's fee) in advance to accept the offer <sup>[74](#o74)</sup>.
- **UBD:** undergraduate fees are charged yearly in Brunei dollars, with new fees from the 2026/2027 intake; on top, a BND 100 acceptance fee, not refunded if you fail to register, and a BND 250 refundable programme deposit <sup>[76 (2024)](#o76)</sup>.
- **Insurance:** at least BND 5,000 for the whole student pass, from a Brunei insurer arranged by the sponsor <sup>[32](#o32), [N47](#n47)</sup>.
- **Emergency money:** UBD tells students to bring at least BND 1,000 <sup>[77 (2022)](#o77)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What is UBD's current yearly tuition for an international undergraduate?
> - Do agents in Bangladesh sell Brunei university places or "study and work" packages, and what do they charge?

<a id="step-1-the-university-gets-your-visa-approval"></a>

### 8.3 Step 1: The university gets your visa approval

1. <a id="step-1-the-university-gets-your-visa-approval-item-1"></a>**BDGS scholars at UTB:** the Registrar's Office writes to Immigration for your student visa approval and emails you a copy of Immigration's approval letter <sup>[35](#o35)</sup>. UTB says the scholar then shows that letter at Brunei International Airport to get a single-entry visa for BND 20 <sup>[35](#o35)</sup>. Bangladesh Immigration does not accept a Brunei visa on arrival, so a Bangladeshi must have the visa stamped in the passport before departure ([Student visa](#student-visa), [Step 2: The student visa in Dhaka](#step-2-the-student-visa-in-dhaka)) <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-1-the-university-gets-your-visa-approval-item-2"></a>**Fee-paying students at UTB** pay a BND 1,500 visa deposit, refunded when the course ends and the pass is cancelled; Immigration keeps the passport for a few days <sup>[35](#o35)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does UTB or UBD send a Visa Advice that Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka accepts, so a Bangladeshi student can get the visa stamped and board in Dhaka?

<a id="step-2-the-student-visa-in-dhaka"></a>

### 8.4 Step 2: The student visa in Dhaka

At Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka bring the visa form, two photos, NID and birth certificate copies, passport copies, the Visa Advice from Brunei Immigration (valid three months), a medical test from an enlisted centre, a confirmed air ticket and printed insurance; the fee is Tk 2,100 and it takes five working days <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-3-student-pass-after-arrival"></a>

### 8.5 Step 3: Student pass after arrival

1. <a id="step-3-student-pass-after-arrival-item-1"></a>**At UBD,** email office.isu@ubd.edu.bn for an appointment with the International Student Unit 3 to 5 days after arrival; bring your passport and a copy, a photo, the stamped visa letter, the student pass form and your offer letter <sup>[77 (2022)](#o77)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-3-student-pass-after-arrival-item-2"></a>**At UTB,** for BDGS scholars the university processes the student pass, multiple-entry visa and Brunei identity card within two weeks of arrival; you fill in Student Pass Form 10, the visa form and the green identity card form, and pay BND 50 for the multiple-entry visa <sup>[35](#o35)</sup>.

**If you are refused:** the Tk 2,100 visa fee is not refunded, and a false document cancels the application at once <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

**Scams to watch for:** any offer of a Brunei student visa as a way to work; a student pass holder may not take any job <sup>[75 (2023)](#o75)</sup>.

**Where to get help:** UBD's International Student Unit, office.isu@ubd.edu.bn; the scholarship office, queriesBDGS2025@mfa.gov.bn; Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka (see [Where to get help](#where-to-get-help)) <sup>[77 (2022)](#o77), [73](#o73), [32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="transit"></a>

## 9 Transit

A Bangladeshi cannot get Brunei's transit visa: it is issued only at Brunei International Airport, to the nationalities listed under Visas not open to Bangladeshis <sup>[34](#o34)</sup>. Passengers who only transit at the airport and do not pass immigration do not fill in the E-Arrival Card <sup>[N39 (2023)](#n39)</sup>. To leave the airport during a stopover, a Bangladeshi needs a visit visa from Dhaka (see [Visit visa](#visit-visa)) <sup>[29](#o29), [32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="family-visas"></a>

## 10 Family visas

<a id="dependant-pass-for-a-workers-spouse-and-children"></a>

### 10.1 Dependant pass for a worker's spouse and children

A dependant visa is for the wife and children under 18 of a man working in Brunei <sup>[34](#o34)</sup>. The worker applies on Form 25 as the holder of an employment pass, giving his pass number and how he will provide security (a guarantee) <sup>[78](#o78), [63](#o63)</sup>. The Work Pass Section at Immigration handles dependant passes, their re-entry visas, extensions and cancellations <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.

**Who can bring family.** Only professional, technical or managerial staff earning more than BND 1,500 a month, who meet conditions such as their own accommodation, are considered <sup>[N51 (2005)](#n51)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does Immigration still apply a salary threshold for a dependant pass (BND 1,500 a month or another figure), and what security deposit does Form 25 require?

<a id="step-1-the-worker-applies-in-brunei"></a>

#### 10.1.1 Step 1: The worker applies in Brunei

The worker files Form 25 at the Work Pass Section with his employment pass details <sup>[78](#o78), [11](#o11)</sup>. He must also arrange Brunei-based medical insurance: BND 10,000 for each dependant <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>.

<a id="step-2-the-dependant-visa-in-dhaka"></a>

#### 10.1.2 Step 2: The dependant visa in Dhaka

Each family member takes to Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka the visa form, two photos, NID and birth certificate copies, passport copies, the Visa Advice from Brunei Immigration (valid three months), a medical test from an enlisted centre (babies under 2 need the checks but no X-ray), a confirmed air ticket and the printed insurance <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. The fee is Tk 2,100 each, in cash and not refunded if refused, and it takes five working days <sup>[32](#o32)</sup>. Brunei's compulsory medical insurance for foreigners covers private-sector workers' dependants from 1 January 2026, the Borneo Bulletin reported in July 2025 <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>.

**Where to get help:** Immigration's Work Pass Section handles dependant passes; Brunei's High Commission in Dhaka handles the visa (see [Where to get help](#where-to-get-help)) <sup>[11](#o11), [32](#o32)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi worker on a BOESL contract been given a dependant pass, and are brokers selling "family visas" for Brunei in Bangladesh?
> - Can a refused dependant application be appealed, and to whom?

<a id="children-at-school"></a>

#### 10.1.3 Children at school

A worker's child needs a student pass (Form 10) to attend school, with a parent's letter, the visa form, permission from the Ministry of Education's Department of Schools, the birth certificate, the child's passport, the parents' IDs and passes, BUR 500/555 from the Department of Labour and the parent's salary slip <sup>[79](#o79)</sup>.

<a id="family-visits"></a>

### 10.2 Family visits

A worker who cannot get a dependant pass can sponsor a relative for a visit (see [Visit visa](#visit-visa), [Step 3: If a sponsor in Brunei applies for you](#step-3-if-a-sponsor-in-brunei-applies-for-you)) <sup>[64](#o64)</sup>.

<a id="marriage-to-a-bruneian"></a>

### 10.3 Marriage to a Bruneian

Marriage does not give residence quickly <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69), [80](#o80)</sup>:

- **Long-term social visit pass:** for foreigners with family ties to a Bruneian or permanent resident (PR) who do not yet qualify for PR; apply at the Visa, Visit and Diplomatic Section <sup>[72](#o72), [63](#o63)</sup>.
- **PR for a foreign wife:** after 15 years of marriage, if it is the Bruneian husband's first marriage to a foreign wife; the husband applies and must be alive when Immigration accepts the application; the wife must live in Brunei and hold a valid passport and medical insurance <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69)</sup>.
- **PR for a foreign husband:** after 15 years of marriage, if it is the Bruneian wife's first marriage to a foreign husband and he has no other marriage; the wife applies <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69)</sup>.
- **Citizenship for a foreign wife:** she must live with her Bruneian husband in Brunei as a PR, and apply at least 5 years after a first marriage and at least 3 years after her residence or entry permit was issued (see [Permanent residence and citizenship](#permanent-residence-and-citizenship)) <sup>[80](#o80)</sup>. Muslim couples married abroad need the marriage confirmed by Brunei's Syariah Court <sup>[80](#o80)</sup>.

<a id="permanent-residence-and-citizenship"></a>

## 11 Permanent residence and citizenship

For a Bangladeshi worker, neither is realistic: the PR categories published on 31 December 2024 include none for workers, and citizenship needs 20 years as a PR <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69), [80](#o80)</sup>. Those categories cover children, stepchildren and adopted children of citizens and PRs, spouses of citizens and PRs, and people of proven Bruneian descent <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69)</sup>. Applicants with a foreign nationality must have valid medical insurance <sup>[69 (2024)](#o69)</sup>. Once PR is approved, the entry permit card costs BND 5 for Commonwealth nationals <sup>[81](#o81)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Brunei's insurance rules mention PRs "under the economic and professional categories" <sup>[N47](#n47)</sup>: what are these categories, and can a long-serving foreign worker apply?

Citizenship by naturalisation for someone born abroad needs a residence or entry permit (PR) for at least 20 of the 25 years before applying, the last 2 years continuously in Brunei, good conduct, and a written Malay test for those 18 and over <sup>[80](#o80)</sup>. The 46th citizenship ceremony on 12 November 2026 gave certificates to 710 people: residents who passed the Malay test, children and adopted children of citizens, and wives of Bruneian men <sup>[82](#o82)</sup>.

**Citizenship section:** 4th floor, Immigration headquarters; +673 2383106 extension 130/133; Monday to Thursday and Saturday, 8 to 11.45 am and 1.30 to 3.45 pm <sup>[80](#o80)</sup>.

<a id="changing-visa-type"></a>

## 12 Changing visa type

**Visit to work: not allowed in practice.** A work visa approval is sent to the Brunei mission abroad, and the worker collects the visa there with passport and medical report <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>. A business visit visa may not be used to wait for a labour quota or to work <sup>[63](#o63)</sup>. Working on a visit pass, or in a job not endorsed in the passport, is an offence (see [Can you work?](#can-you-work)) <sup>[19 (2024)](#o19), [N48](#n48)</sup>.

**Changing employer (transfer).** A transfer needs the Department of Labour's change-of-contract form, endorsed on the work pass by Immigration (see [Changing employer](#changing-employer)) <sup>[11](#o11)</sup>.

**Student to work.** A BDGS scholar must leave Brunei as soon as the course ends <sup>[73](#o73)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Can a visit pass holder in Brunei be switched to a work pass without leaving the country if an employer gets a licence?
> - Can a dependant pass holder take a job, and which pass is needed?

<a id="asylum"></a>

## 13 Asylum

Brunei is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol, and it has no system to decide who is a refugee <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83)</sup>. UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) has recommended that Brunei join them, but in 2024 it had still not signed <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83)</sup>. Trafficking victims who need protection but lack papers may avoid the authorities for fear of detention and deportation <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83)</sup>. Brunei has no formal way for foreign trafficking victims to avoid removal to a country where they face hardship <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>.

There is no asylum route to staying in Brunei: entering or staying without a valid pass is an offence, and overstaying brings jail and caning (see [What overstaying costs](#what-overstaying-costs)) <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83), [N48](#n48)</sup>. Identified and potential trafficking victims can get work permits for as long as the investigation and court case last <sup>[N20](#n20)</sup>. Unpaid wages or a broken contract are complained about in person at the Department of Labour's Labour Enforcement Division, with a police report (see [If you aren't paid or your employer breaks the law](#if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law)) <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.

<a id="having-a-child-in-brunei"></a>

## 14 Having a child in Brunei

<a id="register-the-birth-in-brunei"></a>

### 14.1 Register the birth in Brunei

1. <a id="register-the-birth-in-brunei-item-1"></a>**Who registers.** Under the Births and Deaths Registration Act and Regulations (Chapter 79), the father, the mother, or a family member who knows of the birth registers it with the Immigration and National Registration Department, preferably within 14 days <sup>[84](#o84)</sup>. The law provides that all children born in Brunei should have their births registered <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83)</sup>.
2. <a id="register-the-birth-in-brunei-item-2"></a>**What to bring, originals and copies:** the birth registration form, the hospital or clinic's birth notification, both parents' passports and the marriage certificate (with an English translation if needed), and, for a couple married abroad, a confirmation letter from Brunei's Syariah Court if it applies <sup>[84](#o84)</sup>.
3. <a id="register-the-birth-in-brunei-item-3"></a>**Cost:** free <sup>[84](#o84)</sup>.

<a id="the-childs-status"></a>

### 14.2 The child's status

A child born in Brunei to two Bangladeshi parents does not become Bruneian: Brunei gives citizenship automatically by descent from a Bruneian father or by belonging to listed indigenous groups, and everyone else must apply by registration or naturalisation <sup>[83 (2024)](#o83), [N52 (2019)](#n52)</sup>. The child is a Bangladeshi citizen by descent if the father or mother is a Bangladeshi citizen at the birth; if that parent is a citizen by descent only, the birth must be registered at a Bangladesh mission <sup>[85](#o85)</sup>. The child needs its own Brunei pass to stay, usually a dependant pass (see [Dependant pass for a worker's spouse and children](#dependant-pass-for-a-workers-spouse-and-children)) <sup>[34](#o34)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How long may a baby born in Brunei stay before a dependant pass is issued, and what happens if the parent does not qualify for one?

### 14.3 Bangladeshi birth certificate and passport

1. <a id="bangladeshi-birth-certificate-and-passport-item-1"></a>**Birth certificate at the Bangladesh High Commission, Bandar Seri Begawan.** Fill in the BR application on bdris.gov.bd, print and sign it, and fill in the mission's Form J with a photo <sup>[86 (2024)](#o86)</sup>. A new certificate costs BND 7, a reissue or copy BND 14 <sup>[86 (2024)](#o86)</sup>. Submit 9.30 am to 1 pm on working days; it is closed on Saturday, Sunday and public holidays <sup>[86 (2024)](#o86)</sup>.
2. <a id="bangladeshi-birth-certificate-and-passport-item-2"></a>**E-passport.** Bring the printed online application and appointment slip, the child's original English birth registration certificate (17 digits, verifiable online), copies of the parents' NIDs, proof of legal status in Brunei, and, for a child under 6, a 3R photo on a white background <sup>[87](#o87)</sup>.

<a id="sending-money-home"></a>

## 15 Sending money home

Remittance from Brunei to Bangladesh was USD 18.3 million in FY2016-17 and rose to USD 74.2 million in FY2023-24 and USD 87.4 million in FY2024-25, before falling to USD 77.4 million in FY2025-26 (a fiscal year runs July to June) <sup>[27](#o27)</sup>. In July 2026 Brunei ranked 29th among Bangladesh's top 30 remittance source countries <sup>[88](#o88)</sup>.

<a id="use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank"></a>

### 15.1 Use a licensed remitter or bank

Bangladesh's government asks workers to send money only through legal channels, not through hundi (informal transfer through an agent who pays the family in taka from money held in Bangladesh) or other unlicensed agents <sup>[38](#o38)</sup>. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with a further 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps the government incentive <sup>[N53](#n53), [89](#o89)</sup>.

In Brunei, only a remitter licensed by BDCB (Brunei Darussalam Central Bank, the central bank and financial regulator) may take money to send abroad, under the Money-Changing and Remittance Businesses Act, Cap 174 <sup>[90](#o90), [91 (1999)](#o91)</sup>. Running a remittance business without a licence carries a fine of up to BND 10,000, up to one year in jail, or both <sup>[91 (1999)](#o91)</sup>. A person who uses an unlicensed operator loses the protection of Brunei's financial laws and can be charged as abetting the operator <sup>[92](#o92)</sup>.

You'll need:
- For a bKash wallet: the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered <sup>[N53](#n53)</sup>.
- For a bank account: your own or a relative's account in Bangladesh <sup>[93](#o93)</sup>.

1. <a id="use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank-item-1"></a>**Check the remitter's licence.** BDCB's "List of BDCB Licensees" (bdcb.gov.bn, Regulatory, List of BDCB Licensees; filter Finance Providers and Money Services, then Money Remitters) shows 17 licensed money remitters <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>: Alrame, Cendana, Darul Trustee, K & H, Kemuda, LBC Mabuhay, LR, Magnajaya, Maji, Remittance @ Seconds, Siammali, Thanee, Wadi Shazana, Easternunion, Estetika, Jernik and MoneyMatch Brunei <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>. BDCB's Alert List (bdcb.gov.bn, Consumer, BDCB Alert List) lists companies and individuals that may be wrongly seen as licensed by BDCB, 332 of them as of 29 September 2026, and running a remittance business without a licence is one of its categories <sup>[92](#o92)</sup>. Questions about a remitter's licence: BDCB money services, 2381370, infomcr@bdcb.gov.bn <sup>[90](#o90)</sup>.
2. <a id="use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank-item-2"></a>**Choose the channel.**
   - To a bKash wallet: bKash's partners in Brunei are KHRS Brunei, Western Union and MoneyGram <sup>[N53](#n53)</sup>. BDCB lists K & H Remittance Services at No. 5, Ground Floor, Bangunan Haji Sulaiman, Kampong Sengkurong, 426 1436 <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>.
   - To a Nagad wallet: Nagad's partner in Brunei is Magnajaya Money Remittance, and Western Union transfers also reach Nagad wallets with the 2.5% incentive <sup>[N54](#n54)</sup>. Magnajaya: Unit 27, Ground Floor, Jalan Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien, Bandar Seri Begawan, 224 1369 <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>.
   - Other licensed remitters near where workers live: in the Abdul Razak Complex, Gadong, Alrame (242 1129), Easternunion (244 3399) and Jernik (The Mall, 244 7999) <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>; in Seria Plaza, Kuala Belait district, Maji Remittance Services (322 4560) <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>; MoneyMatch Brunei, 33 Jalan Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien, 222 2012, moneymatch.com.bn <sup>[94](#o94)</sup>.
   - To a bank account: use a remitter or bank that has a "drawing arrangement" (a correspondent agreement) with a Bangladeshi bank; banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most <sup>[93](#o93)</sup>.
3. <a id="use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank-item-3"></a>**Send, and keep the receipt until the family has the money.** For bKash, give the partner the receiver's number and registered name and hand over the cash; up to Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer, as many times as you like <sup>[N53](#n53)</sup>. According to a High Commission press statement reported on 19 September 2024, 116 Bangladeshi workers handed BND 0.72 million (about Tk 7 crore at BND 1 = Tk 97) to Remittance @ Seconds, a Western Union agent, between 31 July and 27 August 2024, and were given fake receipts while the money was never deposited <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6), [2](#o2)</sup>. The High Commission met BDCB and the remitter, but by then Western Union had given no assurance of a refund <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>; on 4 October 2024 BDCB ordered Remittance @ Seconds to stop taking money from the public for remittance <sup>[N55 (2024)](#n55)</sup>.
4. <a id="use-a-licensed-remitter-or-bank-item-4"></a>**The family collects.** bKash money can be cashed out at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATM booths for Tk 7 per thousand <sup>[N53](#n53)</sup>.

<a id="carrying-cash-out-of-brunei"></a>

### 15.2 Carrying cash out of Brunei

Anyone leaving Brunei with cash or traveller's cheques worth BND 15,000 or more must fill in a CBNI (cash and bearer negotiable instruments) declaration (form S37) and give it to the Immigration Officer before departing <sup>[97](#o97)</sup>. Not declaring, or a false declaration, can bring a fine of up to BND 50,000, up to 3 years in jail, or both <sup>[97](#o97)</sup>.

<a id="saving-and-pension-in-bangladesh"></a>

### 15.3 Saving and pension in Bangladesh

Brunei's own National Retirement Scheme, SPK (Skim Persaraan Kebangsaan), run since July 2023, covers only Brunei citizens and permanent residents <sup>[N56](#n56)</sup>.

<a id="nrcta-non-resident-convertible-taka-account"></a>

#### 15.3.1 NRCTA (Non-Resident Convertible Taka Account)

The NRCTA is a taka savings, current or fixed-deposit account for Bangladeshis abroad, funded by remittances, at a scheduled bank's AD (Authorised Dealer, licensed for foreign exchange) branch, under Bangladesh Bank's Circular 41 of 8 October 2026 <sup>[98](#o98)</sup>. It earns market interest, and principal and interest can be sent abroad freely, but money paid into an NRCTA generally gets no 2.5% incentive <sup>[98](#o98)</sup>. A worker can open one before departure with no initial deposit <sup>[98](#o98)</sup>. A relative can operate it under a power of attorney, and the account can be kept after coming home for good <sup>[98](#o98)</sup>. Banks may lend in taka against it <sup>[98](#o98)</sup>.

<a id="wage-earner-development-bond-wedb"></a>

#### 15.3.2 Wage Earner Development Bond (WEDB)

The WEDB is a 5-year government bond <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.
- Sizes: Tk 25,000, 50,000, 1 lakh, 2 lakh, 5 lakh, 10 lakh and 50 lakh <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.
- Who buys: a legal wage earner, in taka, with remittance sent through legal channels, in his own name or a named person's <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.
- Profit at maturity: 12% for holdings up to Tk 15 lakh, 11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh and 9% above <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.
- Cashing early pays less: for holdings up to Tk 15 lakh, 8.70% after 6-12 months, rising to 11.20% after 24-60 months <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.
- Other terms: a nominee, loans against the bond and a 40-50% death-risk benefit <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Does any remitter or bank in Brunei sell the WEDB <sup>[99](#o99)</sup>?

<a id="the-probash-universal-pension"></a>

#### 15.3.3 The Probash universal pension

The National Pension Authority's "Probash" scheme is for Bangladeshis working abroad: you pay in foreign currency and the pension is paid in taka <sup>[100](#o100)</sup>. You join at 18 to 50; over 50, only under special consideration and with 10 years of unbroken payments <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>. You pay until 60 and the pension is paid for life <sup>[100](#o100)</sup>. The monthly contribution is Tk 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500 or 10,000 <sup>[102](#o102)</sup>. From Brunei you pay in foreign currency by card, exchange house or a legal bank channel, not in taka, and contributions may never be paid in cash to any person <sup>[103](#o103), [101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>. A missed contribution can be paid without penalty for one more month; after that a late fee of 1% per day keeps the account active <sup>[103](#o103)</sup>. You can also open a Surakkha account (the scheme for self-employed and informal workers) for a family member aged 18 or over in Bangladesh <sup>[100](#o100)</sup>.

You'll need <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>:
- Your NID number (or passport number).
- Your Bangladesh bank account details.
- Your nominee's ID number.

1. <a id="the-probash-universal-pension-item-1"></a>**Register** at www.upension.gov.bd: click Registration, choose Probash, and type your NID or passport number, date of birth and mobile number <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>.
2. <a id="the-probash-universal-pension-item-2"></a>**Fill in** occupation, annual income and permanent address, the bank account name, number and routing number, and the nominee's ID number, relationship and share; submit <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>.
3. <a id="the-probash-universal-pension-item-3"></a>**Pay** the first contribution, download the payment slip and keep the email that gives your pensioner ID <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101)</sup>.
4. <a id="the-probash-universal-pension-item-4"></a>**If you registered with a passport,** submit your NID once you have one <sup>[103](#o103)</sup>.

**Help:** National Pension Authority, 43 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000; 16131 or +88 09610 900800, 9 am-5 pm Bangladesh time; support@upension.gov.bd <sup>[101 (2024)](#o101), [102](#o102), [100](#o100)</sup>.

<a id="going-home-without-valid-papers"></a>

## 16 Going home without valid papers

Overstaying a pass in Brunei is a crime, and long overstays are punished with jail and caning <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>. A worker without a valid passport goes home on a Travel Permit (TP, a travel document issued in place of a passport) from the High Commission, which asks for Immigration's letter when the person is in jail or being deported <sup>[105](#o105)</sup>.

<a id="what-overstaying-costs"></a>

### 16.1 What overstaying costs

Under section 15(1) of the Immigration Act (Cap 17), as listed in the offences sheet INRD (the Immigration and National Registration Department) posted in December 2025:
- Overstay of up to 90 days: a fine of up to BND 4,000, up to 6 months in jail, or both <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>.
- Overstay of more than 90 days: 3 months to 2 years in jail and at least 3 strokes of the cane <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>.
- The offence can be settled by a compound (an out-of-court payment) of up to BND 600 <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>.
- Leaving Brunei other than through a declared immigration checkpoint: a fine of BND 2,000 or 6 months in jail, or a compound of up to BND 600 <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>.

In April 2026 two Bangladeshi men who overstayed were jailed for 5 and 6 months, each with three strokes of the cane <sup>[N45](#n45)</sup>. Anyone who employs or harbours an overstayer can be fined BND 3,000-6,000, jailed up to 2 years, or both (BND 6,000-12,000 or up to 4 years for a repeat offence); where an overstayer is found at a workplace, the penalty is BND 6,000-12,000 or up to 1 year for each one found <sup>[104](#o104)</sup>.

### 16.3 Help from Bangladesh

A close relative in Bangladesh can ask the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare to help bring a worker home where the case qualifies: the relative gives proof that the person is a migrant worker, the workplace and residence address in Brunei, the recruiting agency's name and address and the employer's mobile number; free, within 7 working days <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>. <a id="step-1-get-a-passport-or-travel-permit"></a>

### 16.4 Step 1: Get a passport or Travel Permit

With a valid passport, go to [Step 2: Settle your case with Immigration](#step-2-settle-your-case-with-immigration). Without one, apply at the High Commission for a TP. A new e-passport at a Bangladesh mission costs workers USD 30 regular or USD 45 express <sup>[7](#o7)</sup>.

You'll need <sup>[105](#o105)</sup>:
- The TP form, from the High Commission's website or its counter.
- A photocopy of the passport.
- If the passport was lost, the original police report.
- If you are in jail or being deported, Immigration's letter.
- A copy of the Special Pass (a pass from Immigration; a worker whose passport was held has been given one in its place) <sup>[N23 (2021)](#n23)</sup>.
- A letter from the company or employer.
- 3 passport-size photos.

1. <a id="step-1-get-a-passport-or-travel-permit-item-1"></a>**Fill in the form** in English block letters, with a 2x2 inch photo glued on and 2 more stapled; it asks for your parents' names, date and place of birth, permanent address in Bangladesh, present passport number, a reference, height, eye and hair colour, marks, occupation and reason, and you declare you hold no other Bangladesh passport <sup>[107](#o107)</sup>. The address box is headed "Address in the United States", copied from another mission's form <sup>[107](#o107)</sup>.
2. <a id="step-1-get-a-passport-or-travel-permit-item-2"></a>**Submit in person** at the High Commission, any working day, 9:30 am-1:00 pm; closed Saturday, Sunday and public holidays <sup>[105](#o105)</sup>.
3. <a id="step-1-get-a-passport-or-travel-permit-item-3"></a>**Pay** BND 19 ordinary (ready after 7 days) or BND 38 urgent (after 3 days) <sup>[105](#o105)</sup>.
4. <a id="step-1-get-a-passport-or-travel-permit-item-4"></a>**Collect in person** between 2:30 and 4:30 pm <sup>[105](#o105)</sup>.

<a id="if-a-worker-dies-is-injured-or-loses-the-job"></a>

## 17 If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Brunei's Workmen's Compensation Act (Cap 74) makes the employer pay for work injuries and deaths, and the Labour Department administers it <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>. Every employer must insure against this liability, and may not deduct the cost from wages <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>. The High Commission states that no compensation is paid to illegal workers or workers without insurance <sup>[109](#o109)</sup>.

<a id="check-you-are-covered"></a>

### 17.1 Check you are covered

WEWB's death grant needs BMET emigration clearance or Board membership, and death in the country the clearance was for <sup>[45](#o45)</sup>. Check your clearance or membership at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check; if you left without clearance, you can join from Brunei by applying online at portal.wewb.gov.bd through the labour wing, with your passport and proof of legal work, for the equivalent of Tk 6,500 including life insurance <sup>[13](#o13)</sup>. Workers cleared from 1 January 2023 also have the JBC (Jiban Bima Corporation, the state life insurer) migrant insurance: Tk 10 lakh, 5 years, for a Tk 1,000 one-time premium <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>.

<a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill"></a>

### 17.2 If you are injured or fall ill

1. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-1"></a>**Tell the employer within 7 days** and claim within 6 months of the accident <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>. The employer may, and if the Labour Commissioner directs must, offer a free medical examination within 5 days of the notice <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-2"></a>**Compensation in Brunei.** Under the 1984 text of Cap 74, permanent total incapacity pays 60 months' earnings or BND 36,000, whichever is less, but at least BND 12,000; permanent partial incapacity a proportionate share; temporary incapacity, a periodic payment of two-thirds of monthly earnings, capped at BND 400 a month, less whatever the worker can still earn <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>. An insurer's 2021 policy sheet still cites the 1984 edition and also covers medical expenses up to BND 10,000 per worker, at government hospitals and clinics only <sup>[N57 (2021)](#n57)</sup>. The money is paid into the Labour Commissioner's hands, not straight to the worker <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-3"></a>**How the claim runs.** The company reports to the Labour Department; the Labour Department gets a medical report from RIPAS (Raja Isteri Pengiran Anak Saleha Hospital, the main government hospital) or the treating hospital and assesses it; the High Commission helps; and the money is sent to the beneficiary in Bangladesh through Brunei's Foreign Ministry and its High Commission in Dhaka <sup>[109](#o109)</sup>.
4. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-4"></a>**Hospital visits.** The High Commission made 30 hospital visits to Bangladeshis in 2024, 25 in 2025 and 6 in 2026 to October <sup>[110](#o110)</sup>.
5. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-5"></a>**Going home sick or injured.** On the worker's or family's application, WEWB asks the High Commission to bring the worker home <sup>[111](#o111)</sup>. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports takes sick or injured returnees on request <sup>[112](#o112)</sup>.
6. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-6"></a>**Treatment aid in Bangladesh.** Returnees with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment <sup>[113](#o113)</sup>.
7. <a id="if-you-are-injured-or-fall-ill-item-7"></a>**The JBC insurance.** It pays the full Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability, and for partial disability a share, for example 90% for a whole arm or leg and 50% for one eye <sup>[38](#o38), [47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>. WEWB checks the claim papers and recommends it to JBC <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Have Cap 74's compensation amounts been raised since the 1984 edition, given that the Act caps death compensation at BND 28,000 but an insurer's 2021 sheet says BND 28,800 <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108), [114](#o114)</sup>?
> - How does an undocumented worker's family pay or get a waiver of a RIPAS hospital bill?

<a id="if-a-worker-dies"></a>

### 17.3 If a worker dies

1. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-1"></a>**Death compensation.** For a death at work, on the road or by killing, the legal heirs give the High Commission a power of attorney; the mission's enlisted lawyer takes the case to court; the cheque is paid into the mission's account and sent to WEWB, which pays the heirs <sup>[109](#o109)</sup>. For a government Workmen's Compensation case, the Labour Department process in If you are injured or fall ill, item 3, applies <sup>[109](#o109)</sup>. Under the 1984 text of Cap 74, a worker's wholly dependent family gets 48 months' earnings or BND 28,000, whichever is less, but at least BND 9,600; the employer also pays reasonable burial and medical expenses <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>. A death claim must be made within 6 months of the death <sup>[108 (1984)](#o108)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-2"></a>**Body home or burial in Brunei.** The family applies to WEWB, which takes the family's choice (body home or local burial) to the High Commission <sup>[115](#o115), [116](#o116)</sup>. The Ministry also brings home the bodies of irregular, undocumented and destitute workers: the next of kin applies with what happened, the cause of death, proof of status and the death certificate; free, 7 working days <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>. Bringing a body home from Brunei costs around Tk 4.5 lakh, the High Commission's First Secretary (Labour) said in April 2024 <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38)</sup>. The High Commission repatriated 14 bodies in 2024, 16 in 2025 and 8 in 2026 to 22 October <sup>[N57 (2021)](#n57)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-3"></a>**At the airport: Tk 35,000.** For every body that arrives, the airport welfare desk gives the family a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, collectable within 3 months <sup>[12](#o12), [117](#o117)</sup>. You'll need <sup>[118](#o118)</sup>:
   - The deceased's passport copy.
   - A heirship certificate from the local chairman or commissioner.
   - The NID of the wife, father or mother.
   - The death certificate.
   - The NOC (no-objection certificate).
   - The original airway bill.
   - BMET clearance or membership proof, if any.
4. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-4"></a>**The Tk 3 lakh grant.** If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the body was buried in Brunei, the family applies to WEWB within 1 year of the death <sup>[45](#o45)</sup>. It goes to the spouse, children and parents, or, for an unmarried worker with no living parents, to full siblings, and is paid into a bank account by BEFTN (electronic transfer) <sup>[45](#o45)</sup>. The family applies with a family-members certificate countersigned by the UNO (Upazila Nirbahi Officer), an undertaking on Tk 400 stamp paper, the NOC or death certificate, a bank statement, photos and the deceased's passport copy <sup>[45](#o45)</sup>.
5. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-5"></a>**The JBC insurance: Tk 10 lakh.** It pays in full for natural death, and for accidental death during the cover or within 90 days after it ends <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>. WEWB helps the family claim, free, on 16135 <sup>[119](#o119)</sup>.
6. <a id="if-a-worker-dies-item-6"></a>**Money owed by the employer.** The next of kin can apply to the Ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) with proof of status, employer details and amounts owed; the Ministry writes to the High Commission and WEWB within 7 working days <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>. In 2023-24 Bangladesh's labour wings recovered Tk 127.6 crore in 1,852 cases of death compensation, wages and benefits <sup>[120 (2024)](#o120)</sup>.

<a id="what-happens-in-practice"></a>

#### 17.3.1 What happens in practice

A Bangladeshi worker who died in 2022 while being treated at RIPAS, undocumented, left an unpaid hospital bill of about Tk 16 lakh; the employer paid nothing, no insurance claim was possible, and his body stayed in the hospital morgue for more than two years <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38), [N58 (2022)](#n58)</sup>. After Ain O Salish Kendra (a Bangladeshi human rights organisation) appealed to the Ministry, the High Commission got the bill waived and the Ministry paid to fly the body home in April 2024 <sup>[N38 (2024)](#n38)</sup>. In 2022 his widow had written to WEWB's director general and was told only that the High Commission had been asked to look into it <sup>[N58 (2022)](#n58)</sup>.

<a id="if-you-lose-the-job"></a>

### 17.4 If you lose the job

1. <a id="if-you-lose-the-job-item-1"></a>**Complain in Brunei.** Unpaid wages and repatriation are among the complaints Brunei's Labour Department handles; file a police report first, then complain in person or online (see [If you aren't paid or your employer breaks the law](#if-you-arent-paid-or-your-employer-breaks-the-law)) <sup>[36 (2024)](#o36)</sup>.
2. <a id="if-you-lose-the-job-item-2"></a>**Tell the High Commission's labour wing,** which takes complaints on its complaint sheet and mediates (see [Help from the High Commission](#help-from-the-high-commission)) <sup>[55](#o55), [56](#o56)</sup>.
3. <a id="if-you-lose-the-job-item-3"></a>**From Bangladesh,** you or your family can apply to the Ministry about unpaid wages, allowances or insurance; free, 7 working days <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>.
4. <a id="if-you-lose-the-job-item-4"></a>**Job-loss insurance.** Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going, but not if you went entirely at the employer's cost <sup>[47 (2024)](#o47)</sup>. Claim through WEWB with proof of clearance or membership and a passport copy <sup>[119](#o119)</sup>.
5. <a id="if-you-lose-the-job-item-5"></a>**If you can't get home,** a relative in Bangladesh can ask the Ministry for help (see [Help from Bangladesh](#help-from-bangladesh)) <sup>[60](#o60)</sup>.

## 18 Coming home

<a id="what-you-can-bring-home"></a>

### 18.1 What you can bring home

Bangladesh's Non-tourist Passenger Baggage Rules 2025 set what a returning worker may bring <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- Baggage: up to 65 kg duty-free for a passenger aged 12 or over, plus more for clothes and personal items <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- One of each listed item duty-free, such as a laptop, TV up to 29 inches, rice cooker or fan <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- Phones: 2 used and 1 new mobile phone, the new one once a year <sup>[124](#o124)</sup>.
- Jewellery: up to 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery, declared, once a year, no more than 12 pieces of one type <sup>[123](#o123), [124](#o124)</sup>.
- Gold bar: one bar up to 117 g (10 tola) once a year, declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; more is confiscated <sup>[124](#o124)</sup>.
- With duty: TV 30-36 inches Tk 10,000, refrigerator Tk 5,000, split AC Tk 15,000 and others <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- Not allowed: alcohol, for a Bangladeshi passport holder <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- Declaring: fill in the baggage declaration form (Schedule 1); with nothing to declare, use the green channel <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>. A missed declaration can still be made within 30 days <sup>[123](#o123)</sup>.
- Money: up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency without declaring; above that, declare it to customs on the FMJ currency declaration form <sup>[125 (2020)](#o125), [124](#o124)</sup>. Customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 <sup>[124](#o124)</sup>.

<a id="support-after-return"></a>

### 18.2 Support after return

1. <a id="support-after-return-item-1"></a>**PKB rehabilitation loan.** Returnees starting a project can borrow up to Tk 50 lakh (up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral) from Probashi Kallyan Bank, with a 0.50% processing fee and a 0.50% documentation fee, each at most Tk 15,000 <sup>[126](#o126)</sup>. You need an updated trade licence, project details, a guarantor and the papers of your return <sup>[126](#o126)</sup>. In 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees got PKB's special reintegration loan, and a returnee said its conditions put him off <sup>[N59 (2021)](#n59)</sup>. PKB helpline: 16238, +880 9677-787878 from abroad <sup>[126](#o126)</sup>.
2. <a id="support-after-return-item-2"></a>**WEWB reintegration help.** WEWB's World Bank-funded RAISE (Recovery and Advancement of Informal Sector Employment) project gave returnees cash incentives, self-employment help and training until June 2026 <sup>[127](#o127), [120 (2024)](#o120)</sup>.

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which RAISE services continue after June 2026 <sup>[127](#o127)</sup>?
3. <a id="support-after-return-item-3"></a>**BRAC Migration Programme.** BRAC, a development organisation founded in Bangladesh in 1972 and the organisation behind this guide, gives returnees airport help, counselling, help to start earning and legal assistance <sup>[128](#o128), [129](#o129)</sup>.
4. <a id="support-after-return-item-4"></a>**Children's scholarships.** WEWB pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children: Tk 27,500 a year at SSC (Secondary School Certificate) level and Tk 34,000 at HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) level <sup>[120 (2024)](#o120)</sup>.
5. <a id="support-after-return-item-5"></a>**If your family is threatened** or its property seized, apply to WEWB, which acts through the local administration <sup>[120 (2024)](#o120), [130](#o130)</sup>.
