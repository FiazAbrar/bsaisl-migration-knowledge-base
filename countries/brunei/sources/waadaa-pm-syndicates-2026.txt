Prime Minister Tarique Rahman has accused the previous government of deliberately allowing the country's healthcare system to deteriorate, forcing people to travel to a particular foreign country for medical treatment.
He made the remarks during the question-and-answer session in Parliament on Wednesday, the final day of the fourth session.
Tarique said the previous government had failed to develop the country's healthcare infrastructure to ensure proper medical services for its citizens.
He told Parliament that the tender process for expanding healthcare facilities across all upazilas would begin within the next one to two weeks.
Under the plan, existing 50-bed upazila health complexes would be upgraded to 150-bed facilities over the next five years, with an additional 100 beds at each hospital, he said.
Highlighting the shortage of hospital beds, the prime minister said patients at government hospitals were often forced to sleep on floors in corridors and other spaces due to a lack of beds.
He cited statistics showing that around four million people faced such conditions, describing the situation as deeply distressing.
Despite the country's economic difficulties, the government had managed to add around 3,500 hospital beds across Bangladesh over the past few months, he added.
Overseas employment and remittances
The prime minister also said several countries had suspended or slowed the recruitment of Bangladeshi workers because of syndicates that operated under the previous government.
He said fraud and exploitation in overseas recruitment had become widespread under the previous regime, with syndicates taking money from aspiring migrant workers by promising to send them abroad.
"Several countries had almost stopped recruiting workers from Bangladesh because of these syndicates. Some had suspended recruitment, while others had slowed it down," he said.
Tarique said the government had successfully resolved recruitment-related problems with Malaysia through negotiations, paving the way for around 10,000 Bangladeshi workers to migrate there without paying recruitment costs, which would be covered by the government.
Of these workers, around 1,000 would be from families affected by enforced disappearances and killings under the previous government, as well as those who were killed or injured during the 2024 mass uprising, he said.
The government was also holding discussions with several Middle Eastern countries to expand employment opportunities for Bangladeshi workers. Some countries had expressed interest in recruiting workers from Bangladesh in various sectors, including driving, he added.
He said the government was working to identify syndicates involved in exploiting aspiring migrant workers and embezzling their money, and bring those responsible to justice.
The prime minister also highlighted the importance of remittances, describing them as one of Bangladesh's major sources of foreign currency earnings.
He said the government had undertaken policy reforms to increase remittance inflows through formal channels, curb hundi, expand transaction options and ensure faster transfers.
The government had facilitated arrangements between domestic scheduled banks and overseas exchange houses and money transfer operators to introduce new remittance channels, he said.
These measures have expanded the options available to expatriate Bangladeshis and reduced transfer costs through increased competition, Tarique added.
He also said the government was providing cash incentives on remittances sent through formal channels to encourage expatriate Bangladeshis to use legal channels.
