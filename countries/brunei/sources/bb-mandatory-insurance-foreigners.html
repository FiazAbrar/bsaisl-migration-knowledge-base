      Mandatory insurance policy for foreign nationals enforced

      July 10, 2025
      Share
      Facebook
      Twitter
      Pinterest
      WhatsApp
      Telegram

        Foreign nationals entering or residing in Brunei will now be required to have valid medical insurance under a new policy officially in force as of July 1, 2025, the Ministry of Home Affairs announced in a joint press conference with the Ministry of Health (MoH) on Thursday.

        According to the Minister of Home Affairs Dato Seri Setia Awang Haji Ahmaddin bin Haji Abdul Rahman during the press conference at the MoH building, the Medical Insurance Policy for Foreign Nationals aims to ensure that foreigners have access to healthcare services while reducing the burden on Brunei’s public health system. The policy is being rolled out in two phases and will apply to various immigration pass holders, including domestic workers, business travellers, dependents, and foreign students.

        Phase One, already in effect, applies to: Holders of Domestic Service Work Passes; Social Visit Visa holders; Professional and Business Visit Pass holders; And Permanent Residents (PRs) who are foreign nationals contributing to the nation’s development and economy.

        Phase Two, starting January 1, 2026, will cover: Private sector foreign workers; Dependents (spouse and children not enrolled in school); And foreign students.

        Speaking at the event, the Minister of Home Affairs said the initiative reflects the government’s commitment to enhancing the country’s immigration and healthcare policies.

        “This step ensures that foreign nationals who reside or enter the country do so with adequate medical protection, while also helping maintain the sustainability of our national healthcare services,” he said.

        Minimum coverage requirements

        Insurance coverage must be valid for the entire duration of the immigration pass and meet minimum coverage amounts, including:

        • BND100,000 for Work Pass holders and long-term business or professional visits

        • BND10,000 for Social Visit and Dependent Pass holders

        • BND5,000 for foreign students

        • BND100,000 for eligible Permanent Residents under the economic and professional categories

        Insurance can be purchased from local insurers or international companies offering worldwide coverage that includes Brunei.

        Entry procedures updated

        Under the updated entry procedures:

        • Visitors with valid insurance will receive visit passes based on their approved visa durations.

        • Those without insurance will be granted a two-week pass only, with extensions possible upon presenting a valid insurance certificate to the Immigration and National Registration Department.

        Foreign nationals arriving under Work Passes, including returning workers with renewed contracts, must also show proof of valid medical insurance. Those without coverage upon arrival will also be issued a two-week pass pending further documentation.

        The policy also applies to foreign students, who are now required to maintain valid insurance throughout their studies in Brunei.

        However, the policy does not apply to foreign nationals directly employed under agreements with the Government of His Majesty the Sultan and Yang Di-Pertuan of Brunei Darussalam or with statutory bodies.

        Encouraging personal responsibility

        Foreign nationals, especially spouses and family members of Bruneian citizens and PRs, are encouraged to voluntarily obtain insurance to safeguard their health while in the country.

        The Minister emphasised that the policy not only reinforces personal responsibility but also ensures fairness in access to healthcare.
