                             Introduction

The Department of Economic Planning and Statistics (JPES) conducted the Labour Force Survey
(LFS) 2025 from 3 to 30 November 2025. The main objective of the LFS 2025 was to collect
latest information on the size, structure, distribution and characteristics of the labour force,
employment, unemployment and other economic characteristics of the population, which will
be used to assist in the planning, research, policy-making and management concerning the
labour force in the country.

The survey covered about 3,200 households using stratified, clustered, and two-stage sampling
design. The data from the selected households were extrapolated according to the sampling
weights and calibrated to represent the total population living in regular households in Brunei
Darussalam.

This Preliminary Report of the Labour Force Survey (LFS) 2025 presents the findings of the
main indicators of LFS 2025 on economic activity of the population of Brunei Darussalam in
2025, which provide information on the status of economic activity of the population, labour
force participation rate and unemployment. It provides information on the labour force
profile for population aged 18 years and over, taking into account the accepted definition
of working age in Brunei Darussalam. Information on the labour force profile for
population aged 15 years and over is also provided as a reference and recommendation from
International Labour Organization (ILO).

The collection and compilation of labour statistics for Brunei Darussalam through the Labour
Force Survey (LFS) 2025 are in line with the international standards, adopted by
the 21st International Conference of Labour Statisticians (21st ICLS) in 2023.

More detailed information on the labour force such as employment status, types of
economic activity, occupation, employment sector and income from employment will be
released in the detailed Report of the Labour Force Survey (LFS) 2025.

                             Key Findings

Working Age Population and Labour Force

In 2025, the working age population (aged 18 years and over) was 348,700 persons, comprising
187,900 males (53.9 per cent) and 160,800 females (46.1 per cent).
Out of this working age population, 66.5 per cent (232,000 persons) were in the labour force
and the remaining 33.5 per cent (116,700 persons) were outside labour force.
The labour force participation rate decreased from 67.4 per cent in 2024 to 66.5 per cent in
2025. This decrease was due to a decrease in the number of employed persons, particularly in
the Government sector.
The labour force participation rate for males (74.6 per cent) being higher than females (57.1
percent) by 17.5 per cent.

Employed

In 2025, the total number of employed persons decreased by 0.9 per cent (1,900 persons) to
220,400 persons compared to 222,300 persons in 2024.
The employed person in 2025 comprising 134,600 males (61.1 per cent) and 85,800 females
(38.9 per cent).
The majority of employed persons were local workers, totaling of 156,300 persons (70.9 per
cent), while non-local workers accounted for 64,100 (29.1 per cent).

                                                                                                                                 2
Unemployed

Out of the total population aged 18 years and over, around 11,600 persons were not working
and actively looking for a job (unemployed), consisting of 6,000 females (51.7 per cent) and
5,600 males (48.3 per cent).
The unemployment rate in 2025 was 5.0 per cent compared to 4.7 per cent in 2024. While the
youth unemployment rate for persons aged 18 to 24 years old was 18.0 per cent, the rate for
females (24.2 per cent) was higher compared to males (14.5 per cent).

Outside Labour Force

The total number of persons in the outside labour force was 116,700 persons, including 47,700
males (40.9 per cent) and 69,000 females (59.1 per cent).

Concepts and Definitions

The main concepts and definitions used in the survey are as follows:

A household is defined as a group of persons who live together and make common provision
for food and other essentials for living. The people in the group may be related or unrelated or
a combination of both. A household may consist of only one person or several persons.

This refers to the week preceding the date of the survey interview.

a) Local persons are the persons in relation to the immigration laws of the country. The
    categories are:
    i) Brunei citizens, i.e. holder of Brunei Darussalam yellow identity card;
    ii) Permanent resident, i.e. holder of Brunei Darussalam purple identity card.

b) Non-local persons are the persons in relation to the immigration laws of the country which
    are Temporary resident, i.e. holder of Brunei Darussalam green identity card; and Others.

Persons in the working age population consist of working age population aged 15 years and
over; and 18 years and over. Working age population 15 years and over is according to the
recommendations of the International Labour Organization (ILO). While working age
population 18 years and over is the accepted definition of working age in Brunei Darussalam.

Persons in the labour force are those who were either employed or looking for job
(unemployed) during the reference period.

                                                                                                                                 4
The starting point of the international standards on statistics of work, employment and labour
underutilization is the concept of work defined as:
 "Any activity performed by persons of any sex and age to produce goods or to provide

    services for use by others or for own use" in line with the General production boundary
    defined in the System of National Accounts 2008.
 Work is defined "irrespective of its formal or informal character or the legality of the
    activity."
 It excludes "activities not involving production of goods or services (begging, stealing), self-
    care (personal grooming, hygiene) and activities that cannot be performed by another
    person on one's own behalf (sleeping, learning, own recreation)."

The international standards recognize different forms of work:
 Own-use production work (production of goods and services for own final use); -

    employment (work performed for others in exchange for pay or profit);
 unpaid trainee work (work performed for others without pay to acquire workplace

    experience or skills);
 volunteer work (non-compulsory work performed for others without pay); and - other

    forms of work (not defined at this time by the international standards, e.g., community
    work by prisoners, ordered by court or similar authority).

Employment is a particular form of work. Persons in employment are defined as all those with
working age population, during a short reference period, who were engaged in any
activity to produce goods or provide services for pay or profit. It excludes persons
engaged wholly in activities to produce goods or services for own final use such as producing
agricultural, fishing and gathering products for own-consumption or cleaning,
decorating, gardening and maintaining one's own dwelling or premises, durables and
other goods.

Persons in employment comprise:
    employed persons "at work," i.e., who worked in a job for at least one hour; and
    employed persons "not at work" due to temporary absence from a job, or to working-
        time arrangements (such as shift work, flexi-time and compensatory leave for overtime).

                                                                                                                                 5
Persons in unemployment are defined as all those with working age population during the
reference period who:
a) were not in employment;
b) carried out activities to seek employment during a specified recent period; and
c) were currently available to take up employment given a job opportunity.

The definition of unemployment provides an exception in the case of future starters. They are
considered as unemployed even if they did not carry out activities to seek employment during
the specified recent period, criterion (b) as long as they satisfy the availability condition.

Persons in the outside labour force are those who were not working, did not have job and were
not actively seeking for work during the reference period.

Persons who are categorised as outside labour force include homemakers, students,
pensioners and others such as disabled persons.
