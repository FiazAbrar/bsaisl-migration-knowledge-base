## Resolved conflicts

- **H-1B fee: yearly or once, employer or worker.** Commerce Secretary Lutnick said on 19 September 2025 it would be paid every year of the three-year visa, details still under consideration [XU0165]; the White House spokesperson said on 20 September that it is a one-time fee on the application only [XU0167], and a 10 October 2025 article still calls it "annual". The White House clarification, given as the rule took effect, wins; the doc gives both statements with dates because the first was widely reported. The September 2026 article says skilled workers must pay it; the September 2025 reports of the order itself say US companies pay it [XU0164]; the reports of the order win.

- **5% remittance tax vs 1% cash-only tax:** May 2025 articles report a proposed 5% tax on all remittances by non-citizens with no minimum [XU0036]. The January 2026 article reports the law as signed on 4 July 2025: 1% on cash, money order, cashier's cheque and similar only, none on bank, card or digital transfers [XU0055, XU0057]. The later report of the enacted law wins; the 5% figure was a proposal and is left out of the doc.
- **US rank for remittance:** top in January-March 2025 with about 18% [XU0039]; first until July 2025 and fifth by January 2026 (Standard Express CEO) [XU0059]; fourth for calendar 2025 with 11% [XU0097]. Different periods, not a conflict; all three kept with their periods.
- **First-nine-months FY2024-25 remittance:** USD 3.94 billion [XU0038] vs USD 4.271 billion for July-April [XU0035]: different periods; the doc keeps the July-April figure and the quarterly figure. XU0038 uncited as duplicate.
- **Deportation totals:** 34 (6 Mar-21 Apr 2025), 39 (2 Aug 2025), 30 (5 Sep 2025), total 187 by September 2025, about 500 by March 2026 [XU0032, XU0044, XU0048, XU0050, XU0099]. Consistent progression; 34+39+30 is less than 187 because other flights were not reported in this set.
- **Visa bond: who pays.** The 8 January 2026 article says citizens "applying to enter" must give the bond [XU0060], but the same article says it applies to those found eligible for B1/B2 [XU0064]. The doc uses the specific B1/B2 statement; the global Illegal routes doc's embassy notice confirms it applies to approved applicants and not to visas issued before 21 January 2026.
- **Fulbright Visiting Scholar deadline:** the November 2025 article gives "20 December 2026" for the "2026-27" session (published Nov 2025, so almost certainly a misprint for 20 December 2025); the October 2026 article gives 3 October 2026 extended to 31 October 2026 for a programme starting September 2027, while still calling it "2026-27". The doc uses only the October 2026 article's dates and gives no session label.
- **Nahida Sultana Brishti's departure date:** 12 August 2025 (25 April article) vs 25 August 2025 (26 April article). Not used in the doc.
- **Visa bond countries:** six in August 2025, seven later, then Bangladesh and 24 others on 6 January 2026, total 38 (6+7+25). Consistent.

## Official research pass, 23-24 November 2026

**Searched:** travel.state.gov (student, visitor, fees, visa bond, immigrant visa pause), the Federal Register (visa bond final rule, H-1B weighted selection rule, EAD auto-extension rule), uscis.gov (H-1B, OPT, asylum, I-589, VAWA, CSPA, hold-policy court order, fee alerts), ICE SEVIS fee, Study in the States, eCFR 22 CFR 62.14 (J-1 insurance), DHS Entry/Exit Overstay Report FY2024, US Embassy Dhaka (visas, nonimmigrant, immigrant, family-based, fraud warning), the Bangladesh Embassy in Washington (contact, dead body repatriation and its PDF), the Consulates General in New York and Los Angeles, BMET statistics. 29 sources registered, 127 facts logged (OU0001 to OU0127).

**Found and changed in the doc:**
- Visa bond: made permanent by a final rule effective 3 August 2026 (91 FR 48757); amounts now USD 10,000, 15,000 (default) or 20,000; visas valid three to 12 months; entry and exit through any commercial airport (the three designated airports of the pilot are gone); refund rules, breach (including claiming asylum), no waiver application, appeal of breach. The Embassy's visas page (16 Nov 2026) says "up to $20,000" for Bangladeshis, so Bangladesh got no B-1 exemption.
- Immigrant visa pause: ended 21 August 2026 under the court order in CLINIC v. Rubio (State Department page, Last Updated 28 August 2026, read from the copy AILA posted as Doc. 26090106 because travel.state.gov returns 403 and the Wayback copy predates the change). The plaintiffs' 26 August release reports a new global pause on immigrant visa appointments; whether Dhaka is issuing again is a Need to know.
- Dhaka immigrant visa process: two-visit processing since 1 June 2026, documents, November 2026 priority dates, fees (I-130 USD 675, IV USD 325), DNA collection by USCIS Dhaka.
- H-1B: the USD 100,000 payment's guidance was vacated by the District of Massachusetts on 8 June 2026; the First Circuit denied a stay on 24 July 2026; DHS says it complies but would collect if the order is lifted. Weighted selection (wage level I to IV entered 1 to 4 times) effective 27 February 2026 for the FY2027 round. Portability and return-fare rules added.
- Asylum: PM-602-0192 hold enjoined on 24 August 2026 (Red Eagle Law v. Edlow, N.D. Cal.); one-year deadline, 150-day EAD wait, Annual Asylum Fee.
- Student visa: I-20, SEVIS fee USD 350, visa fee USD 185, DS-160 7-day rule, on-campus 20 hours, off-campus hardship rule, OPT 12 months plus STEM 24 months, 60-day departure rule.
- Overstay rates FY2024 (DHS): Bangladeshi B1/B2 5.73% vs 2.33% all non-VWP; Bangladeshi students 9.09% vs 3.23% all and 4.32% India.
- VAWA self-petition and the Domestic Violence Hotline; CSPA age rules; J-1 insurance minimums; Bangladesh missions' contacts; body repatriation documents.

The final rule wins. The three designated airports (XU0066, XU0067) were replaced in the State Department's May 2026 list by all commercial airports, confirmed by the final rule.
