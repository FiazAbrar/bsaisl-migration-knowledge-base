# United States: migration guide

How Bangladeshis go to the United States, visa by visa, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. Built from Prothom Alo reporting and from US and Bangladesh government sources. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

USD is the US dollar; Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh). Prothom Alo converted at USD 1 = Tk 122.31 in January 2026 [XU0060].

## Background

### In one minute

Bangladeshis go to the United States as students, exchange scholars, visitors, family immigrants, or irregularly overland from South America [XU0013, XU0007, XU0093, XU0112]. More than 17,000 Bangladeshi students were in US higher education in 2023-24, a record [XU0013]. A Bangladeshi found eligible for a business or tourist visa must post a bond of up to USD 20,000 before the visa is issued, and may enter and leave only through commercial airports; the bond rule began for Bangladesh on 21 January 2026 and was made permanent on 3 August 2026 [OU0001, OU0022, OU0003, OU0009]. From 21 January to 21 August 2026 the US issued no immigrant (green card) visas to Bangladeshis; a court order ended that pause [OU0026]. Brokers in Bangladesh sell the irregular route through Brazil and Mexico, and in one case a young man paid a broker ring Tk 25 lakh and never left [XU0114, XU0112, XU0045]. People without valid papers are being sent back: about 500 by March 2026, with about 5,000 more in process [XU0099]. USCIS (US Citizenship and Immigration Services, which decides immigration applications) put all asylum decisions on hold in late 2025 until a court ordered normal processing in August 2026, and the US had revoked more than 300,000 visas of all kinds by October 2026 [XU0187, OU0091, XU0213]. For skilled work, one Bangladeshi engineer's route was a funded US master's and then a US job; staying on to work needs an employer to sponsor an H-1B visa, whose USD 100,000 payment for workers hired from abroad had its guidance vacated by a court in June 2026, with DHS saying it would comply, and never applied to a former student switching status inside the US [XU0148, XU0150, XU0152, OU0072, OU0074, OU0076].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open for students, exchange visitors, visitors (with a bond) and family immigrants, all of whom must be interviewed in person [XU0062, OU0001, OU0026].

#### Status

- **January 2025:** within three days of President Trump's inauguration the US began arresting migrants without papers [XU0158].
- **February 2025:** the US Embassy in Dhaka told the Bangladesh government by diplomatic note that the US would send back Bangladeshis who had become irregular (see Deportation to Bangladesh) [XU0017].
- **June 2025:** the US imposed a full travel ban on 12 countries; Bangladesh is not one of them [XU0084].
- **September 2025:** a USD 100,000 payment on certain new H-1B work visa petitions took effect on 21 September (see Work visa: H-1B) [OU0075, XU0166].
- **30 October 2025:** work permits (EADs, Employment Authorization Documents) stopped being extended automatically while a renewal is pending (see Work permits) [OU0117].
- **November 2025:** the State Department told visa officers to weigh applicants' health and ability to pay for their own care (see Health and means checks); on 27 November President Trump wrote that he would permanently pause immigration from all third-world countries; on 28 November USCIS halted all asylum decisions (see Asylum) [XU0184, XU0189, XU0187].
- **21 January 2026:** the visa bond started for Bangladeshi visitors (see Visit visa and the visa bond), and the US paused immigrant visas for citizens of 75 countries including Bangladesh (see Family immigrant visa: the green card) [OU0022, OU0026].
- **March 2026:** Bangladesh planned to ask for B-1 business travellers to be exempted from the bond [XU0100].
- **May 2026:** people in the US on temporary visas were told they must go home to apply for a green card (see Applying from inside the US) [XU0199].
- **June and July 2026:** a federal court vacated the guidance for the USD 100,000 H-1B payment, an appeals court refused to pause that ruling on 24 July, and DHS (the Department of Homeland Security) said it would comply [OU0072, OU0073].
- **3 August 2026:** the visa bond became permanent, with amounts of USD 10,000, 15,000 or 20,000 [OU0004, OU0005].
- **21 August 2026:** the immigrant visa pause for the 75 countries ended under a court order [OU0026].
- **24 August 2026:** a court ordered USCIS to decide held applications, including asylum, in the normal way (see Asylum) [OU0091].
- **September 2026:** the H-1B payment order was extended for another year [XU0209].
- **October 2026:** the State Department said more than 300,000 visas had been revoked since January 2025 [XU0213].

#### The channels

- **Student visa:** the US Embassy urges students to apply early, and the US Ambassador says the US encourages Bangladeshi students (see Student visa) [XU0042, XU0109].
- **Exchange visitor (J-1) through Fulbright:** for secondary school teachers and for experienced academics and professionals (see Exchange visitor visa: Fulbright) [XU0008, XU0052].
- **Visit visa (B1/B2):** business and tourism, with a bond (see Visit visa and the visa bond) [OU0001].
- **Family immigrant visa: the green card:** for relatives of US citizens and permanent residents, sponsored by a petition (see Family immigrant visa: the green card) [XU0086].
- **Work visa: H-1B:** used by US companies to hire skilled foreign workers (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0082].
- **Not a legal route:** the overland route through South America and Mexico (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0112].

#### Size

- **US visas issued to Bangladeshis, US fiscal 2023 (October 2022 to September 2023):** about 59,254, of them 44,674 non-immigrant and 14,580 immigrant [XU0093].
- **Bangladeshis granted US permanent residence:** 14,890 in 2019, 9,010 in 2020, 6,180 in 2021, 10,140 in 2022 and 18,240 in 2023 [XU0094, XU0095].
- **Overstays, US fiscal 2024:** 5.73% of Bangladeshi business and tourist visitors due to leave stayed on (2,213 of 38,590), against 2.33% for all visitors from countries that need a visa; 9.09% of Bangladeshi students and exchange visitors (674 of 7,413), against 3.23% for all students and 4.32% for Indians [OU0098, OU0099, OU0100, OU0101, OU0102].
- **Bangladeshis living in the US:** 40,000 in 2000 and 270,000 in 2023, according to a 2025 Pew Research Center report; a US-based Bangladeshi put Bangladeshi immigrants at more than 4 lakh, and a December 2025 estimate put people of Bangladeshi origin at about 5 lakh or more [XU0195, XU0161, XU0194].
- **Income:** in 2023 a Bangladeshi household in the US earned USD 78,400 a year on average, against USD 105,600 for Asian households; 14% of Bangladeshis were below the poverty line, against 10% of Asians [XU0196, XU0197].
- **Students:** 4,802 in 2013-14 and 17,099 in 2023-24, up 26% in a year, making Bangladesh the 8th-largest sender of students to the US (13th a year before) [XU0015, XU0013, XU0014].
- **Waiting family:** some Bangladeshi families have 15 to 16 immigration applications pending, and US immigration lawyers put those waiting at several lakh [XU0096]. In November 2026 brothers and sisters of US citizens were being reached with petitions filed before 22 October 2012 (see Step 1: The petition and the queue) [OU0032].
- **Remittance:** see Sending money home [XU0039].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

The reported ways in are a university's admission, a Fulbright grant or a relative's immigrant petition, and brokers in Bangladesh sell the irregular route (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0013, XU0052, XU0086, XU0114]. In a US Justice Department case, migrants paid large sums to brokers in Bangladesh, and a Bangladeshi ring then housed them in São Paulo, Brazil, and in Tapachula and Monterrey, Mexico, before telling them how to cross the Rio Grande river into the US [XU0114, XU0112, XU0113]. A young man from Chatkhil, Noakhali, paid Tk 25 lakh to a broker ring from Lakshmipur and Feni to go to the US and had not gone two and a half years later [XU0045]. For a skilled job, one reported route is a US degree, then a US employer that agrees to sponsor an H-1B visa, which is usually the only realistic way for a skilled foreigner to work long-term in the US and later get a green card (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0152, XU0178].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **Visa fees:** USD 185 for a visitor, student or exchange visa, USD 205 for an H-1B; students also pay a USD 350 SEVIS fee, for the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (see Step 1: Get admission and the I-20) [OU0061, OU0062, OU0064].
- **The visa bond:** USD 10,000, 15,000 or 20,000 for a visit visa, set by the officer, usually 15,000; it is returned without interest if the conditions are kept (see Visit visa and the visa bond) [OU0005, OU0006, OU0012].
- **A family immigrant visa:** USD 675 for the relative's I-130 petition and USD 325 per person for the visa application (see Family immigrant visa: the green card) [OU0039, OU0038].
- **The H-1B payment:** USD 100,000 on certain new petitions for workers outside the US, paid by the employer; a court vacated the guidance for it in June 2026 and DHS said it would comply; not charged when a former US student changes status inside the US (see Work visa: H-1B) [OU0075, OU0125, OU0072, OU0074, OU0076].
- **A first graduate job:** one Bangladeshi engineer put starting pay for a master's graduate at USD 80,000-100,000 a year in big cities and 70,000-90,000 elsewhere, with 15-20% tax in the first five years and one-bedroom rent of USD 2,500 a month in a big city and up to 1,500 in a small one (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0153, XU0154, XU0155].
- **The irregular route:** Tk 25 lakh paid to a broker ring in one 2025 case [XU0045].
- **Health care:** a US-based Bangladeshi wrote that a two-day hospital stay for a birth billed over USD 50,000, of which he paid a USD 6,000 insurance deductible (see Health care and its cost) [XU0023].
- **Sending money home in cash:** 1% tax since 1 January 2026 (see Sending money home) [XU0055].

### What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** on-campus work only, up to 20 hours a week in term; off campus only with approval after a full year and a qualifying economic hardship, or through OPT (Optional Practical Training) (see Can you work?) [OU0066, OU0067, OU0068].
- **Visitors:** the bond is forfeited if the holder takes unauthorised work or overstays [OU0018, OU0017].
- **H-1B:** the employer files the petition and must pay at least a minimum salary set for the area and occupation; the worker can move to a new H-1B employer once that employer files a new petition (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0172, OU0080].
- **Work permits (EAD):** since 30 October 2025 a worker whose permit expires before renewal is decided may have to stop work (see Work permits) [OU0117, XU0182].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are removal of people who lost their immigration cases, visas cancelled over the holder's conduct or posts, money lost to brokers, family petitions frozen by the 2026 pause, and violent deaths of students and delivery workers [XU0033, XU0020, XU0022, XU0216, XU0045, XU0087, XU0198, XU0205].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Come on a visitor visa and switch to asylum."** Bangladeshis post this on Facebook; a US-based columnist warned that such posts make visas harder for ordinary Bangladeshis, and the State Department treats a bond holder who claims asylum as a possible bond breach (see Asylum) [XU0025, XU0026, OU0023].
- **"A student can openly find restaurant or gas-station work."** A student's visa was cancelled after he asked on Facebook for help finding such work; off-campus work needs approval (see Can you work?) [XU0020, OU0067].
- **"Paying the bond gets me the visa."** It does not; if the officer then refuses the visa, the bond is cancelled and returned [OU0025, OU0011].
- **"The immigrant visa pause stops all US visas."** It never applied to tourist, student or business visas, and it ended on 21 August 2026 [XU0081, OU0026].
- **"Once in the US on a student or visit visa, I can apply for a green card there."** Since May 2026 people on temporary visas must go home to apply, except some "dual intent" visa categories (see Applying from inside the US) [XU0199, XU0203].
- **"Only criminals lose their visas."** The US has revoked visas en masse of foreigners who joined pro-Palestine protests, and over social media comments on the killing of the commentator Charlie Kirk [XU0216, XU0217].
- **"Blank boxes on a form must be filled with something."** A US-based columnist reported parents whose immigration form had a date entered under "date of death", who then had to prove they were alive [XU0027].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **Overland through Brazil and Mexico: for the smugglers it has ended in US prison.** Two Bangladeshi smugglers were each sentenced to 46 months, and a third Bangladeshi accused, extradited from Brazil, faces 5 to 15 years if convicted (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0115, XU0110, XU0116].
- **Staying without papers: it ends in removal to Dhaka**, sometimes in handcuffs on the plane (see Deportation to Bangladesh) [XU0051].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Visa-free or bond-free visit visas:** a Bangladeshi found eligible for a B1/B2 visa must post the bond, and there is no procedure to apply for a waiver [OU0001, OU0019].

## Student visa

In July 2026 the US Ambassador, Brent Christensen, said the US encourages Bangladeshi students in US higher education [XU0109]. Some go on full scholarships for a PhD, as a Bangladeshi student did to the University of South Florida [XU0105].

### Step 1: Get admission and the I-20

1. Write to EducationUSA, the US Embassy's education advising service, at EducationUSA-Bangla@state.gov for information on applying, programmes and funding [XU0016].
2. Apply to a school certified by SEVP (the Student and Exchange Visitor Program). A school that accepts you registers you in SEVIS (the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System) and sends you a Form I-20 [OU0122].
3. Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee of USD 350 once you have the I-20 [OU0122, OU0064].

> **Need to know**
> - Where is the EducationUSA advising centre in Dhaka, what are its hours, and is advising free?

### Step 2: Apply for the visa

1. Apply as soon as you have admission: the Embassy said on 31 July 2025 that qualified students should apply quickly so there is enough time for processing. A new student's visa can be issued up to 365 days before the course starts [XU0042, OU0057].
2. Fill in the DS-160 online application and create a profile and book the interview at www.ustraveldocs.com/bd/en or through its call centre (+880-9610202040). Submit the DS-160 more than 7 calendar days before the interview; changes within 7 days cancel the appointment, and the DS-160 barcode (starting AA) must match the one used to book [OU0047, OU0052, OU0044, OU0045].
3. Pay the USD 185 visa fee at an Eastern Bank (EBL) branch, by BEFTN (Bangladesh Electronic Funds Transfer Network) or EBL Skybanking transfer, or by card [OU0061, OU0046].
4. Set all your social media accounts to "public": the Embassy instructs student visa applicants to, so they can be vetted [OU0055]. The US has revoked visas over protest participation and posts, and since August 2025 USCIS vetting of immigration applications includes searches for "anti-American activity" [XU0216, XU0217, XU0071].
5. Go to the interview in person at the US Embassy, Madani Avenue, Baridhara, Dhaka, with your passport (valid six months beyond your stay), the fee receipt, the DS-160 confirmation page, the appointment letter and the I-20 [XU0062, OU0054, OU0046, OU0056].
6. Enter the US no earlier than 30 days before your course starts [OU0058].

> **Need to know**
> - How long is the wait for a student visa interview in Dhaka in late 2026, and what share of Bangladeshi student applicants are refused?

### Can you work?

- **On campus:** up to 20 hours a week while school is in session, for example in a university bookstore or cafeteria [OU0066].
- **Off campus:** only after a full academic year, with an economic hardship the school's designated school official (DSO) accepts and a work permit (EAD) from USCIS; you cannot start while the application is pending [OU0067].
- **OPT:** up to 12 months of work permission during or after the degree, recommended by the DSO on your I-20 and approved by USCIS on Form I-765; a graduate in a listed STEM (science, technology, engineering and mathematics) subject working for an employer enrolled in E-Verify (the US government's online check of a worker's right to work) can get 24 months more. Work starts only after the EAD arrives [OU0068, OU0071, OU0069, OU0070].

### Family and leaving

- **Spouse and children:** each needs their own I-20 from your school and their own visa, but pays no SEVIS fee [OU0060].
- **Leaving:** you must leave within 60 days after the programme end date on your I-20, including any practical training [OU0059].

### After graduation: getting a US job

Syed Tahmid Hossain, a CUET (Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology) graduate, went to Georgia Southern University in 2022 with full funding for a master's in civil engineering, did a three-month paid internship during it, and got an engineering job in April 2024 right after finishing [XU0148, XU0149, XU0150]. His account, from a May 2024 interview:
1. From the start of the master's, go to the university's career fairs and ask company engineers whether they take international students and would sponsor a visa later; leaving a CV at a career fair got him the most responses [XU0152, XU0220].
2. Keep LinkedIn, Indeed and Glassdoor profiles updated and apply through them too, starting at the beginning of the final year [XU0220, XU0221].
3. He applied to 28 companies, was called by six, and got two offers after several interview rounds [XU0151].

**What the job pays, by his account:** USD 80,000-100,000 a year to start for a master's graduate in New York, Chicago or Los Angeles, and 70,000-90,000 in smaller cities; 25-35% of pay is withheld as tax, but international students pay no Medicare or Social Security for their first five years, so it comes to 15-20%; a one-bedroom flat costs USD 2,500 a month in a big city and up to 1,500 in a small one; hours over 40 a week are paid at 1.5 to 2 times the rate; paid leave is 10-15 days in the first year [XU0153, XU0154, XU0155, XU0156, XU0157].

**The visa:** staying on to work needs an employer to sponsor an H-1B visa, and the USD 100,000 payment does not apply when a student inside the US is granted a change to H-1B status (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0152, OU0076]. The employer must pay a former student at least a minimum set by area and occupation: USD 136,000 for a new computer science graduate in San Francisco County, and USD 164,000 for a software developer in Las Vegas, where a landscape architect earning USD 97,000 would have a better chance [XU0172, XU0173].

### Scams and mistakes

#### Done by students

- A student's visa was cancelled after he posted on Facebook, on getting it, asking for help finding restaurant or gas-station work in the US [XU0020].
- A student was sent home with his visa cancelled after allegedly trying to forcibly kiss a woman at the gas station where he worked, a columnist reported [XU0022].
- The US has revoked visas en masse of foreigners who joined pro-Palestine protests, and over social media comments on the killing of Charlie Kirk; the State Department spokesperson said in October 2026 there would be no tolerance for a visa holder who breaks the law, commits fraud or undermines national security [XU0216, XU0217, XU0215].

#### Done to students

- Calls, emails and letters asking for "visa fees": the US Embassy never emails or calls to ask for a visa payment, never takes visa fees by bKash, Nagad, Rocket or similar, and scammers can make its phone number appear on your screen; a visa email from an address not ending in ".gov" is suspect. Report it to DhakaFraud@state.gov [OU0048, OU0049, OU0050, OU0053].

## Exchange visitor visa: Fulbright

Fulbright grants take Bangladeshi teachers, academics and professionals to the US on exchange; the TEA programme gives J-1 visa support [XU0007, XU0052, XU0008]. J-1 applicants on official US government exchanges pay no visa application fee [OU0063]. Every J-1 exchange visitor must be insured for at least USD 100,000 per accident or illness, USD 25,000 for sending the body home and USD 50,000 for medical evacuation, with deductibles of no more than USD 500 [OU0103].

### Fulbright Teaching Excellence and Achievement (TEA)

A six-week exchange in the US for secondary school teachers, run through the US Embassy's American Center in Dhaka [XU0007].

**Who qualifies:** full-time secondary teachers of English, EFL (English as a foreign language), maths, science, foreign languages, social studies, journalism or special education, with a bachelor's degree and at least five years of full-time teaching [XU0009]. Ministry officials, education administrators, full-time teacher trainers, university teachers and private English tutors cannot apply [XU0010].

**What it covers:** J-1 visa support, a pre-departure orientation in Dhaka, return and domestic US airfare, programme fees, housing (usually shared) and meals, and accident and health insurance [XU0008].

**How to apply:**
1. Apply online with an institutional support and reference form (ISRF) filled in by your supervisor and a leave approval form (LAF) from https://fulbright.irex.org/ [XU0012].
2. If shortlisted, take a paper-based IELTS (International English Language Testing System) test (score 6.0 or more, or equivalent), which the American Center arranges, and an interview in English [XU0011].

> **Need to know**
> - When does the TEA call open in 2027, and does it run every year?

### Fulbright Visiting Scholar

A fully funded grant for one academic year of research or teaching at a US institution [XU0052].

**Who qualifies:** Bangladeshi public and private professionals, university teachers and researchers with 10 or more years of professional experience, holding a PhD or equivalent professional degree and proposing research and teaching in their field, with English good enough to work professionally at a US university [XU0052, XU0120, XU0119].

**What it covers:** return airfare, academic fees, a monthly stipend for room and living costs, a book allowance, health and accident insurance, and travel and excess-baggage allowances [XU0053].

**How to apply:** online, after creating a login account; questions go to dhakausexchanges@state.gov [XU0054]. For the round starting in September 2027 the deadline was extended to 31 October 2026, 10:59 am Bangladesh time [XU0118, XU0122, XU0119]. More than 200 Bangladeshis have taken part over 52 years [XU0121].

## Visit visa and the visa bond

The B1/B2 visa is for business and tourism [XU0064]. Since 21 January 2026 a Bangladeshi found eligible for it must post a bond before the visa is issued, wherever they apply; the rule began as a pilot and was made permanent from 3 August 2026 [OU0022, OU0001, OU0003]. In US fiscal 2024, 5.73% of Bangladeshi visitors overstayed, more than twice the 2.33% for all visitors who need a visa [OU0098, OU0099].

### Step 1: Apply and attend the interview

1. Fill in the DS-160 online and book the interview at www.ustraveldocs.com/bd/en more than 7 days ahead, as described for students (see Step 2: Apply for the visa) [OU0047, OU0044].
2. Pay the USD 185 visa fee at Eastern Bank or online [OU0061, OU0046].
3. Attend the interview in person at the US Embassy with your passport, fee receipt, DS-160 confirmation page and appointment letter [XU0062, OU0046].

### Step 2: Post the bond

1. If the officer finds you eligible, the officer tells you the amount and how to pay: USD 15,000 as a rule, USD 10,000 if you cannot pay that but can fund the trip, or USD 20,000 if your contacts in the US suggest 15,000 would not ensure you leave [OU0002, OU0005, OU0006, OU0007].
2. Pay only through the link on Pay.gov (the US Treasury's payment site) that the officer gives you, and only after being told to; never through a third-party website. Money paid outside US government systems, or without an officer's direction, is not returned [OU0002, OU0010, OU0024, OU0025].
3. Go back to the Consular Section to complete the visa. If the officer then refuses the visa, the bond is cancelled [OU0010, OU0011].
4. The visa is valid for one or several entries within three to 12 months of issue [OU0008].

### Step 3: Travel only by commercial airport

Enter and leave the US only through commercial airports (including US preclearance airports abroad), never by land or sea, and leave by the date you are allowed to stay until [OU0009, OU0017].

### Getting the bond back

The bond is cancelled and the money returned in US dollars, without interest, once DHS records show you left on time, never travelled before the visa expired, or were refused entry at the airport; any debt you owe the US government can be deducted [OU0127, OU0013, OU0012, OU0014]. You get an Immigration Bond Cancelation Notice [OU0015]. If you decide not to travel, book an appointment at a US consulate outside the US before the visa expires; the officer cancels the visa and the bond [OU0016]. A holder who applies in time to extend the stay or change status, and gets it, is not in breach [OU0021].

> **Need to know**
> - How many weeks after departure does the bond money arrive, and is it paid back to the card or account it came from?

### Losing the bond

The bond is breached and the whole amount forfeited if the holder overstays, takes unauthorised work or otherwise does not keep its conditions; applying to change out of visitor status, including claiming asylum, is sent to USCIS as a possible breach [OU0017, OU0018, OU0023]. A breach finding can be appealed following DHS instructions [OU0020].

### Business travellers

Bangladesh's National Security Adviser asked the US Trade Representative on 8 January 2026 to ease travel for Bangladeshi businesspeople, and Bangladesh planned to ask in March 2026 for the B-1 business category to be exempted [XU0076, XU0100]. There is no procedure for an applicant to apply for a waiver, and in November 2026 the US Embassy still required the bond of every Bangladeshi found eligible for a B1/B2 visa [OU0019, OU0001].

### Scams

- **Tried by travellers:** Facebook posts showing how to come on a visitor visa and switch to asylum, which puts the bond at risk (see Losing the bond) [XU0025, OU0023].
- **Done to travellers:** fake "visa fee" demands by phone or email (see Scams and mistakes), and third-party websites for posting the bond, which the State Department says not to use (see "A visit or tourist visa sold as a job" in the Dalal scams doc) [OU0048, OU0024].

## Family immigrant visa: the green card

An immigrant visa gives a green card on entry and permanent residence; it covers family-based green cards (spouse, parents, children, siblings) and employment-based permanent residence [XU0086].

### Step 1: The petition and the queue

1. The US citizen or permanent resident relative files Form I-130 with USCIS; it costs USD 675 when collected by a US embassy [OU0039].
2. After USCIS approves it, the National Visa Center (NVC) collects the fees, the DS-260 application form, the Affidavit of Support and documents, and schedules the interview in Dhaka when the case is ready [OU0124].
3. Except for immediate relatives, visas are limited in number: the interview is scheduled when the applicant's priority date (the date the petition was filed) becomes current [OU0037]. In November 2026 the cut-off dates were 22 October 2012 for brothers and sisters of US citizens, 22 October 2015 for their married sons and daughters, 22 January 2021 for their unmarried adult sons and daughters, 22 August 2020 for unmarried adult sons and daughters of permanent residents, and 22 October 2026 for permanent residents' spouses and children under 21 [OU0032, OU0033, OU0034, OU0036, OU0035].

### Step 2: Two visits to the Embassy

Since 1 June 2026 the US Embassy in Dhaka handles every immigrant visa in two visits [OU0029].

1. Upload all required documents to your CEAC (Consular Electronic Application Center) account before the appointment [OU0030].
2. First visit, at the appointment NVC makes: bring all original documents and copies; the Embassy reviews them and takes fingerprints and photos [OU0030]. Bring two visa photos from the last six months, original government-registered birth, marriage, divorce and spouses' death certificates, a valid police certificate, and evidence of the relationship such as photos, call or chat logs and remittance receipts [OU0041].
3. If nothing is missing, you get an interview appointment, usually the same week; the Embassy keeps the originals except your passport, which you bring to the interview [OU0031].
4. If something is missing, you get a letter listing it; upload the documents to CEAC and reschedule the interview through your profile at usvisascheduling.com. Missing documents can mean refusal under section 221(g) until they are provided [OU0123, OU0042].
5. Pay USD 325 per person for the visa application [OU0038]. If USCIS asks for a DNA test, the USCIS office in Dhaka collects the sample, not the Embassy [OU0043].

### The 2026 pause

- **What it did:** from 21 January to 21 August 2026 no immigrant visa was issued to citizens of 75 countries including Bangladesh, although applications and interviews continued [OU0026, XU0079].
- **Why:** on 27 November 2025 President Trump wrote that he would permanently pause immigration from all third-world countries [XU0189]. The State Department said the pause follows a November 2025 order to tighten screening of would-be immigrants who might become a financial burden [XU0078]. On a list of 120 countries ranked by immigrant welfare use that President Trump posted on social media in January 2026, Bangladesh was 19th, with 54.8% of Bangladeshi immigrant households in the US taking US government benefits [XU0070].
- **Who was hit:** relatives of US citizens with petitions already filed, including people whose interviews were already scheduled, and US citizens who newly married; three US immigration lawyers said lakhs of Bangladeshis would be harmed [XU0087, XU0089, XU0088, XU0091].
- **How it ended:** on 21 August 2026 a federal court in CLINIC v. Rubio struck the pause down and the State Department said it was no longer in effect; the plaintiffs then reported that the State Department had ordered a new global pause on immigrant visa appointments, citing a training programme [OU0026, OU0027, OU0028].
- **Screening:** USCIS updated its policy in August 2025 to extend social media vetting of applications to searches for "anti-American activity" [XU0071].

### Children who turn 21

The Child Status Protection Act (CSPA) protects some children from "ageing out" [OU0119]. A child of a US citizen who was under 21 when the petition was filed does not age out [OU0121]. In family preference cases (for example a sibling's children), the child's "CSPA age" is their age when a visa becomes available minus the days the petition was pending; if that is under 21, they stay a child, provided they apply for permanent residence within one year of the visa becoming available [OU0119, OU0120].

### Health and means checks

In November 2025 the State Department told visa officers to consider an applicant's health conditions that need costly care, such as heart disease, respiratory disease, cancer, diabetes, metabolic, neurological and mental conditions, and obesity, and whether the applicant can pay for treatment without government help, as grounds to find them ineligible [XU0184, XU0185]. Charles Wheeler of the Catholic Legal Immigration Network said it applies to almost all visa applicants but will most likely be used only for those applying to live permanently in the US [XU0186].

### Applying from inside the US

Since May 2026 people in the US on temporary visas (student, visit and so on) must go back to their own country to apply for a green card: USCIS guidance says changing to permanent status from inside the US ("adjustment of status") is not a right but a matter of discretion, judged case by case [XU0199, XU0200, XU0201]. Officers must weigh visa violations, overstaying, working without permission, fraud and whether the applicant kept the conditions of entry [XU0202]. Some "dual intent" visa categories still allow the holder to seek a green card while in the US, with no guarantee of one [XU0203].

### Filling in the forms

A US-based columnist reported parents whose immigration form had a date entered in the "date of death" field; at the interview they were told they had died in 2002 and had to prove they were alive [XU0027].

### Scams

A Narail man posing on Facebook as a US-based pilot of Bangladeshi origin promised to marry a divorced woman and take her to the US, and took Tk 1 crore 9 lakh from her, which she said he asked for in instalments for a passport, visa and other pretexts [XU0002, XU0003]. She sent the money to 13 different mobile numbers, and said she sold 37 bhori of gold and took a bank loan [XU0004, XU0003]. When she found his Khulna address was false he threatened to post private videos of her [XU0005]. She filed a case at Wari police station and police arrested him, but under pressure to repay her debts she settled for Tk 60 lakh [XU0127, XU0006]. (see "Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes" in the Dalal scams doc).

## Work visa: H-1B

US employers use the H-1B visa to hire skilled foreign workers, mostly in technology but also in hospitals, banks and universities [XU0082, XU0179]. It is usually the only realistic way for a skilled foreigner to work long-term in the US and later get a green card (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0178].

**How many:** 65,000 a year, plus 20,000 for holders of a US master's or higher degree; universities and their non-profits, non-profit research organisations and government research organisations are outside the limit [OU0077, OU0078, OU0079].

**How they are chosen:** from the fiscal 2027 registration round, selection is weighted by the wage offered: a registration at the top wage level (IV) goes into the draw four times, level III three times, level II twice and level I once [OU0084, OU0085].

**The USD 100,000 payment:**
- A presidential proclamation of 19 September 2025 requires it with certain H-1B petitions filed from 21 September 2025 for workers who are outside the US and have no valid H-1B visa; the employer pays it on Pay.gov before filing [OU0075, OU0125, XU0164].
- It does not apply to valid H-1B visas or petitions filed earlier, to H-1B holders travelling in and out, or to a petition for someone inside the US who is granted a change of status or extension, such as a student moving to H-1B [OU0126, OU0076].
- On 8 June 2026 a federal court in Massachusetts vacated the guidance that put it into effect; on 24 July 2026 the appeals court refused to pause that ruling, and DHS said it would comply while considering next steps but still plans to collect the payment if the ruling is lifted [OU0072, OU0073, OU0074].
- In September 2026 the proclamation was extended for another year [XU0209].
- The normal H-1B fee is USD 2,000 to 5,000 [XU0210].

In October 2025 DHS also proposed a rule to change which employers are exempt from the limit, scrutinise employers that break the rules more strictly and watch third-party placements (workers placed at another company) more closely [XU0176, XU0177].

### Changing employer

1. The new employer files a new Form I-129 petition before your H-1B stay runs out [OU0081].
2. You can start work for the new employer once it is filed (or on its requested start date, if later), provided you have not worked without permission since your last entry [OU0080, OU0081].
3. If the employer fires you before your authorised stay ends, it must pay reasonable return transport home; it need not if you resign [OU0082, OU0083].

## Work permits

An EAD (Employment Authorization Document, Form I-766) proves permission to work in the US for a set period; green card holders do not need one, and H-1B and L-1B (intra-company transfer) holders generally do not either [XU0183]. A renewal application filed on or after 30 October 2025 no longer extends the old permit automatically; before, a timely renewal kept it valid for up to 540 days [OU0117, OU0118]. USCIS advises applying 180 days before the permit expires, warning that the later the application, the greater the risk of having to stop work [XU0182].

## Joining a husband in the US

A columnist who works with abused women wrote in February 2024 that the wives of Bangladeshis who go to the US to study or work mostly cannot legally work there, and their own studies can be very costly [XU0139]. She wrote that families should find out, before the marriage, whether the husband's visa will create obstacles for the wife, what his immigration plans are, whether he disagrees with her studying or working, and whether he has a previous marriage or a history of domestic violence; a wife is even more helpless if the husband disappears after the wedding or abandons her [XU0147, XU0146, XU0145].

### If your husband abuses you

- **What abusers use:** a new wife who does not know the immigration rules may be threatened with being sent back, divorced or separated from her children; in a 2019 Pew survey only 55% of Bangladeshis in the US were comfortable in English, against 72% of Asians, which keeps many women from getting out [XU0137, XU0138].
- **The law:** the US has domestic violence laws, and a victim can get a protection order [XU0141, XU0136].
- **Your own papers:** a wife abused by a husband who is a US citizen or green card holder can petition for her own green card under the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) without his knowledge or help, on Form I-360 with no fee, and can then apply for a work permit [OU0093, OU0094, OU0095, OU0096].
- **Help:** call the National Domestic Violence Hotline on 800-799-7233 (TTY, for deaf callers, 800-787-3224) [OU0097]. Many organisations give free services, and South Asian women's organisations in many cities can be contacted in Bangla, among them Daya Houston in Texas (https://www.dayahouston.org/) and Maitri in California (www.maitri.org) [XU0141, XU0140, XU0142, XU0144, XU0143].
- **Case:** Nowrin Nahar Tuli, 21, who had a protection order, was strangled by her husband Nasib Ahsan in Texas on 24 December 2023, the columnist reported; he is held in Collin County jail [XU0136, XU0135].

## Asylum

Asylum is claimed on Form I-589 within one year of arriving in the US [OU0087]. The applicant can apply for a work permit only 150 days after filing, and must pay an Annual Asylum Fee for every year the claim is pending [OU0088, OU0090]. A visitor who posted a bond and claims asylum is referred to USCIS as a possible bond breach (see Losing the bond) [OU0023]. On 28 November 2025, after the shooting of National Guard members in Washington DC, USCIS halted decisions on all asylum applications; a federal court in California blocked that hold on 24 August 2026 and ordered USCIS to decide applications in the normal way [XU0188, XU0187, OU0092, OU0091]. The US set its refugee ceiling for fiscal 2026 at 7,500, the lowest in its history, mainly for white South Africans [XU0085, XU0083]. Bangladeshis who post on Facebook how to switch from a visitor visa to asylum risk making visas harder for everyone, a US-based columnist wrote [XU0025, XU0026]. (see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc).

## Dual citizenship

In late 2025 Republican Senator Bernie Moreno introduced the Exclusive Citizenship Act of 2025: dual citizens would get a deadline, probably one year, to give up their other citizenship or lose US citizenship automatically [XU0191, XU0192]. As of December 2025 it was only a proposal in the Senate and must pass both houses of Congress to become law [XU0193].

## Irregular routes and undocumented stay

### Overland through South America and Mexico

In the case of Saifullah Al-Mamun, a Bangladeshi extradited from Brazil on 8 July 2026 and charged in Laredo, Texas, with conspiring to smuggle people from Bangladesh into the US, the charges describe this route [XU0110, XU0111]:
1. People paid large sums to brokers in Bangladesh [XU0114].
2. Saifullah housed them in São Paulo, Brazil, and arranged their travel [XU0112].
3. Mohammad Milon Hossain kept them in Tapachula, Mexico, and sent them to Monterrey [XU0112].
4. Moktar Hossain kept them in Monterrey and told them how to cross the Rio Grande into the US, which many found hard to cross safely [XU0113].

Milon and Moktar pleaded guilty and were each sentenced to 46 months; Saifullah faces 5 to 15 years if convicted of smuggling for financial gain [XU0115, XU0116]. The Justice Department's Joint Task Force Alpha has arrested more than 464 smugglers and associates, and more than 414 have been convicted in the US [XU0117]. (see "The United States through South America and Mexico" in the Illegal routes doc).

### Money paid to brokers who never send you

Zakir Hossain, 24, of Chatkhil, Noakhali, paid Tk 25 lakh to a Lakshmipur and Feni broker ring to go to the US, and two and a half years later had not gone; his family holds papers for Tk 19 lakh of it [XU0045]. When he asked for his money back the brokers threatened him, and in August 2025 he and a friend were found dead in a car in a Dhaka hospital car park; his family suspects the ring, and police had not yet found the cause of death [XU0046, XU0123, XU0047].

### Where undocumented Bangladeshis live

Bangladesh's Foreign Ministry has no figure, but officials said irregular Bangladeshis are likely concentrated in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut [XU0019].

### Deportation to Bangladesh

- **Who is sent back:** people who stayed after losing their immigration cases, including some convicted of crimes in the US; Bangladesh's Foreign Ministry is told about each one in advance [XU0033, XU0034].
- **Bangladesh's terms:** it takes people back once their citizenship is confirmed, and asked the US not to send them in handcuffs [XU0018, XU0126].
- **How they arrive:** 34 people from 6 March to 21 April 2025; 39 by chartered plane on 2 August 2025; 30 more (29 men and one woman) on 5 September 2025, put on the plane in handcuffs that were removed on landing in Dhaka, they told law enforcement [XU0032, XU0044, XU0048, XU0049, XU0051].
- **Totals:** 187 by September 2025 and about 500 by March 2026, with about 5,000 more in process [XU0050, XU0099].

## Health care and its cost

In the US medicine cannot be bought without a doctor's prescription [XU0160]. Health cuts under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act would harm about 2 lakh Bangladeshi immigrants, Prothom Alo reported in August 2025; people without papers lose health care, and a US-based Bangladeshi said most Bangladeshis do middle- or low-income work and rely on government health support, with home care, ride-share, restaurant, delivery and taxi workers hit [XU0159, XU0160, XU0161, XU0162]. People aged 19 to 64 who can work but do not work at least 20 hours a week will lose Medicaid (government health insurance for people on low incomes), a home-care business owner said [XU0163]. President Trump said in November 2025 that no non-citizen would get federal benefits or subsidies [XU0190].

A US-based Bangladeshi wrote in March 2025 that each of his children's two-day hospital births billed over USD 50,000, of which he paid a USD 6,000 insurance deductible, and that a vlogger in New York, being poor, got the city's insurance and paid nothing [XU0023, XU0024]. J-1 exchange visitors must carry insurance of at least USD 100,000 per illness or accident (see Exchange visitor visa: Fulbright) [OU0103].

## Sending money home

### The 1% tax on cash

Since 1 January 2026, money sent from the US in cash, money order, cashier's cheque or similar physical means carries a federal excise tax of USD 1 per USD 100; sending from a bank account, by debit card or online carries no extra tax [XU0055, XU0057]. It is part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed on 4 July 2025 [XU0056]. Experts said it hits hardest irregular migrants without bank accounts and low-income migrants [XU0058].

### Who sends it

At the April 2025 remittance fair in Jackson Heights, New York, Sonali Bank, Janata Bank and Islami Bank Bangladesh were ranked first to third for remittance from the US; Mastercard, MoneyGram and Ria first to third among non-banks; and Sonali Exchange, BA Express and Standard Express were awarded for service to the Bangladeshi community [XU0028, XU0029, XU0030, XU0031].

### The numbers

- USD 3.52 billion in fiscal 2022-23, second after Saudi Arabia [XU0001].
- USD 1.4449 billion in January-March 2025, about 18% of the total and the most from any country, ahead of the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia; USD 4.271 billion in July 2024 to April 2025 [XU0039, XU0124, XU0035].
- Fourth in 2025 with 11% of the total, and fifth by January 2026, according to Standard Express's chief executive [XU0097, XU0059].

The US became the top source after August 2024 [XU0040]. Large firms such as Western Union and MoneyGram collect remittances in an aggregated way, and some in the sector say exchange houses collect from various countries and send from the US [XU0037, XU0041]. (see "Ways to send money and what each costs" in the Sending money home doc).

## If a student or worker dies

In April 2026 two Bangladeshi PhD students at the University of South Florida went missing; a family friend reported them the next day, police found one body and arrested his roommate, who was charged with two counts of first-degree murder, and the other student's body had not been found two days later [XU0101, XU0103, XU0102, XU0198, XU0125].
1. The Bangladesh Embassy in Washington, the Foreign Ministry and the local police kept in touch with the family [XU0108].
2. The district commissioner said the family must submit an application, which the district sends to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare to bring the body home [XU0107].
3. The upazila officer said the embassy does the main work and the local administration gives the embassy any local information it needs [XU0106].

**Sending the body home:** the Bangladesh Embassy issues the certificate needed to carry a body to Bangladesh. Send it, by post to 3510 International Drive NW, Washington DC 20008 or as PDF scans to consular.bdusa@gmail.com: a copy of the deceased's Bangladesh passport (or US passport with the NVR (No Visa Required) page, Bangladeshi NID (national identity card) or Bangladeshi birth certificate), the death certificate, the local health department's disposition or transit permit, and the funeral home's embalmer's, non-infectious, non-contraband and sanitary affidavits. Include a prepaid, self-addressed USPS (US Postal Service) certified, priority or express envelope with tracking to get the original back [OU0109, OU0110, OU0111].

**Other cases:**
- **Sheikh Abir Hossain, 38,** a research assistant at Lamar University, Texas, on a scholarship, who also worked part-time in a food shop in Beaumont, was shot dead at a shop in Beaumont on 29 December 2023 by people stealing cigarettes, police said; four days later his body had still not reached his family because of US holiday-season staffing, a local journalist said, and Bangladeshi students at Lamar opened a GoFundMe fundraiser to which about 14,000 people gave USD 55,000 [XU0134, XU0129, XU0128, XU0130, XU0132, XU0133].
- **Mohammad Mahfuzul Haque,** a DoorDash food-delivery worker described by Bangladeshi community media in the US as of Bangladeshi origin, was shot dead in Kingsessing, Philadelphia, in July 2026 while making a delivery to an address where, police said, nobody had placed an order; investigators believe it was a planned killing by three masked people [XU0205, XU0206, XU0207, XU0208].
