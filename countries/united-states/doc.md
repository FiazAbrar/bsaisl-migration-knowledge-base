# United States: migration guide

How Bangladeshis go to the United States, visa by visa, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. This first version is built only from Prothom Alo reporting (2024 to November 2026), so every fact in it is cited as N and still needs an official source. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

USD is the US dollar; Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh). Prothom Alo converted at USD 1 = Tk 122.31 in January 2026 [XU0060].

## Background

### In one minute

Bangladeshis go to the United States as students, exchange scholars, visitors, family immigrants, or irregularly overland from South America [XU0013, XU0007, XU0093, XU0112]. More than 17,000 Bangladeshi students were in US higher education in 2023-24, a record [XU0013]. Since 21 January 2026 a Bangladeshi found eligible for a business or tourist visa must post a bond of USD 5,000 to 15,000 and fly in and out through Boston, New York JFK or Washington Dulles, and from the same date the US issues no immigrant (green card) visas to Bangladeshis, so family members of US citizens cannot join them for now [XU0064, XU0061, XU0066, XU0077, XU0087]. Brokers in Bangladesh sell the irregular route through Brazil and Mexico, and in one case a young man paid a broker ring Tk 25 lakh and never left [XU0114, XU0112, XU0045]. People without valid papers are being sent back: about 500 by March 2026, with about 5,000 more in process [XU0099]. USCIS halted all asylum decisions in November 2025, and the US had revoked more than 300,000 visas of all kinds by October 2026 [XU0187, XU0213]. For skilled work, one Bangladeshi engineer's route was a funded US master's and then a US job; staying on to work needs an employer to sponsor an H-1B visa, whose fee on a new application has been USD 100,000 since September 2025, though not for someone who was in the US on a student visa [XU0148, XU0150, XU0152, XU0164, XU0212].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open for students, exchange visitors and visitors who pass an in-person interview, with a bond for visitors; closed for now for immigrant visas, as of the latest reporting (January 2026) [XU0062, XU0064, XU0079].

#### Status

- **January 2025:** within three days of President Trump's inauguration the US began arresting migrants without papers [XU0158].
- **February 2025:** the US Embassy in Dhaka told the Bangladesh government by diplomatic note that the US would send back Bangladeshis who had become irregular (see Deportation to Bangladesh) [XU0017].
- **June 2025:** the US imposed a full travel ban on 12 countries; Bangladesh is not one of them [XU0084].
- **September 2025:** a USD 100,000 fee on each new H-1B work visa application took effect on 21 September (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0164, XU0166].
- **30 October 2025:** work permits (EADs, Employment Authorization Documents) stopped being extended automatically while a renewal is pending (see Work permits) [XU0180].
- **November 2025:** the State Department told visa officers to weigh applicants' health and ability to pay for their own care (see Health and means checks); on 27 November President Trump wrote that he would permanently pause immigration from all third-world countries; on 28 November USCIS (US Citizenship and Immigration Services, which decides immigration applications) halted all asylum decisions (see Asylum) [XU0184, XU0189, XU0187].
- **6 January 2026:** Bangladesh was added to the visa bond list, effective 21 January 2026 (see Visit visa and the visa bond) [XU0060, XU0061].
- **21 January 2026:** the US suspended immigrant visas for citizens of 75 countries including Bangladesh; applications can be submitted but none are issued, and no end date has been given (see Family immigrant visa: the green card) [XU0077, XU0079].
- **March 2026:** Bangladesh planned to ask for B-1 business travellers to be exempted from the bond [XU0100].
- **May 2026:** people in the US on temporary visas were told they must go home to apply for a green card (see Applying from inside the US) [XU0199].
- **September 2026:** the H-1B fee order was extended for another year [XU0209].
- **October 2026:** the State Department said more than 300,000 visas had been revoked since January 2025; a Prothom Alo headline said the US was set to make the visa bond permanent for 50 countries including Bangladesh, with a maximum bond of USD 20,000 [XU0213, XU0219].

#### The channels

- **Student visa:** the US Embassy urges students to apply early, and the US Ambassador says the US encourages Bangladeshi students (see Student visa) [XU0042, XU0109].
- **Exchange visitor (J-1) through Fulbright:** for secondary school teachers and for experienced academics and professionals (see Exchange visitor visa: Fulbright) [XU0008, XU0052].
- **Visit visa (B1/B2):** business and tourism, now with a bond (see Visit visa and the visa bond) [XU0064].
- **Family immigrant visa: the green card:** for relatives of US citizens and residents; paused since 21 January 2026 (see Family immigrant visa: the green card) [XU0086, XU0077].
- **Work visa: H-1B:** used by US companies to hire skilled foreign workers; the employer pays USD 100,000 on a new application (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0082, XU0164, XU0212].
- **Not a legal route:** the overland route through South America and Mexico (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0112].

#### Size

- **US visas issued to Bangladeshis, US fiscal 2023 (October 2022 to September 2023):** about 59,254, of them 44,674 non-immigrant and 14,580 immigrant [XU0093].
- **Bangladeshis granted US permanent residence:** 14,890 in 2019, 9,010 in 2020, 6,180 in 2021, 10,140 in 2022 and 18,240 in 2023 [XU0094, XU0095].
- **Bangladeshis living in the US:** 40,000 in 2000 and 270,000 in 2023, according to a 2025 Pew Research Center report; a US-based Bangladeshi put Bangladeshi immigrants at more than 4 lakh, and a December 2025 estimate put people of Bangladeshi origin at about 5 lakh or more [XU0195, XU0161, XU0194].
- **Income:** in 2023 a Bangladeshi household in the US earned USD 78,400 a year on average, against USD 105,600 for Asian households; 14% of Bangladeshis were below the poverty line, against 10% of Asians [XU0196, XU0197].
- **Students:** 4,802 in 2013-14 and 17,099 in 2023-24, up 26% in a year, making Bangladesh the 8th-largest sender of students to the US (13th a year before) [XU0015, XU0013, XU0014].
- **Waiting family:** some Bangladeshi families have 15 to 16 immigration applications pending, and US immigration lawyers put those waiting at several lakh [XU0096].
- **Remittance:** see Sending money home [XU0039].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

The reported ways in are a university's admission, a Fulbright grant or a relative's immigrant petition, and brokers in Bangladesh sell the irregular route (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0013, XU0052, XU0086, XU0114]. In a US Justice Department case, migrants paid large sums to brokers in Bangladesh, and a Bangladeshi ring then housed them in São Paulo, Brazil, and in Tapachula and Monterrey, Mexico, before telling them how to cross the Rio Grande river into the US [XU0114, XU0112, XU0113]. A young man from Chatkhil, Noakhali, paid Tk 25 lakh to a broker ring from Lakshmipur and Feni to go to the US and had not gone two and a half years later [XU0045]. For a skilled job, one reported route is a US degree, then a US employer that agrees to sponsor an H-1B visa, which is usually the only realistic way for a skilled foreigner to work long-term in the US and later get a green card (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0152, XU0178].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **The visa bond:** USD 5,000, 10,000 or 15,000 for a visit visa, set at the interview and returned if the visa is refused or its conditions are met (see Visit visa and the visa bond) [XU0064, XU0063].
- **The H-1B fee:** USD 100,000, paid once by the employer on a new application, on top of the normal fee of USD 2,000 to 5,000; not charged when a former US student applies (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0164, XU0167, XU0210, XU0212].
- **A first graduate job:** one Bangladeshi engineer put starting pay for a master's graduate at USD 80,000-100,000 a year in big cities and 70,000-90,000 elsewhere, with 15-20% tax in the first five years and one-bedroom rent of USD 2,500 a month in a big city and up to 1,500 in a small one (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0153, XU0154, XU0155].
- **The irregular route:** Tk 25 lakh paid to a broker ring in one 2025 case [XU0045].
- **Health care:** a US-based Bangladeshi wrote that a two-day hospital stay for a birth billed over USD 50,000, of which he paid a USD 6,000 insurance deductible (see Health care and its cost) [XU0023].
- **Sending money home in cash:** 1% tax since 1 January 2026 (see Sending money home) [XU0055].

> **Need to know**
> - What do Bangladeshi students and their families typically pay in total (tuition, living costs, agency fees) for a US degree, and what share have full scholarships?

### What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** a US-based Bangladeshi columnist wrote that a student is not legally allowed to work off campus, with an opportunity at some institutions (see Student visa) [XU0021].
- **Visitors:** the bond is returned only if all visa conditions are met [XU0063].
- **H-1B:** the employer applies and pays the fee, and must pay at least a minimum salary set for the area and occupation [XU0164, XU0172].
- **Work permits (EAD):** since 30 October 2025 a worker whose permit expires before renewal is decided may have to stop work (see Work permits) [XU0180, XU0182].

> **Need to know**
> - Can an H-1B worker change employer, and what happens to the visa when the job ends?

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are removal of people who lost their immigration cases, visas cancelled over the holder's conduct or posts, money lost to brokers, family petitions frozen by the January 2026 pause, and violent deaths of students and delivery workers [XU0033, XU0020, XU0022, XU0216, XU0045, XU0087, XU0198, XU0205].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Come on a visitor visa and switch to asylum."** Bangladeshis post this on Facebook; a US-based columnist warned that if US immigration sees such posts, visas will get harder for ordinary Bangladeshis, and US immigration officials have suspended asylum applications (see Asylum) [XU0025, XU0026, XU0085].
- **"A student can openly find restaurant or gas-station work."** A student's visa was cancelled after he asked on Facebook for help finding such work [XU0020].
- **"Paying the bond gets me the visa."** It does not; the money is returned if the visa is refused [XU0063].
- **"The immigrant visa pause stops all US visas."** It does not apply to non-immigrant, temporary, tourist or business visas, and one US immigration lawyer said student, travel and business visas may now be issued faster [XU0081, XU0098].
- **"Once in the US on a student or visit visa, I can apply for a green card there."** Since May 2026 people on temporary visas must go home to apply, except some "dual intent" visa categories (see Applying from inside the US) [XU0199, XU0203].
- **"Only criminals lose their visas."** The US has revoked visas en masse of foreigners who joined pro-Palestine protests, and over social media comments on the killing of the commentator Charlie Kirk [XU0216, XU0217].
- **"Blank boxes on a form must be filled with something."** A US-based columnist reported parents whose immigration form had a date entered under "date of death", who then had to prove they were alive [XU0027].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **Overland through Brazil and Mexico: for the smugglers it has ended in US prison.** Two Bangladeshi smugglers were each sentenced to 46 months, and a third Bangladeshi accused, extradited from Brazil, faces 5 to 15 years if convicted (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XU0115, XU0110, XU0116].
- **Staying without papers: it ends in removal to Dhaka**, sometimes in handcuffs on the plane (see Deportation to Bangladesh) [XU0051].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Immigrant visas (green cards) for now:** since 21 January 2026 no immigrant visa is approved or issued to a Bangladeshi citizen, although applications can still be submitted; a dual national applying on a valid passport of a country not on the list is exempt [XU0079, XU0080].
- **Visa-free or bond-free visit visas:** a Bangladeshi passport holder found eligible for a B1/B2 visa must post the bond [XU0064].

## Student visa

In July 2026 the US Ambassador, Brent Christensen, said the US encourages Bangladeshi students in US higher education [XU0109]. Some go on full scholarships for a PhD, as a Bangladeshi student did to the University of South Florida [XU0105].

### Step 1: Get admission and information

1. Write to EducationUSA, the US Embassy's education advising service, at EducationUSA-Bangla@state.gov for information on applying, programmes and funding [XU0016].
2. Use only the Embassy's official website and the education advising service for information, as the Embassy advises [XU0043].

> **Need to know**

### Step 2: Apply for the visa early

1. Apply for the student visa as soon as you have admission from an approved institution: the Embassy said on 31 July 2025 that qualified students should apply quickly so there is enough time for processing [XU0042].
2. Attend the interview in person: every US visa applicant must [XU0062].
3. Your social media will be checked: the US checks visa applicants' social media activity more strictly, has revoked visas over protest participation and posts, and since August 2025 USCIS vetting of immigration applications includes searches for "anti-American activity" [XU0218, XU0216, XU0217, XU0071].

A US-based Bangladeshi columnist wrote that a student is not legally supposed to work off campus, with an opportunity at some institutions [XU0021].

### After graduation: getting a US job

Syed Tahmid Hossain, a CUET (Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology) graduate, went to Georgia Southern University in 2022 with full funding for a master's in civil engineering, did a three-month paid internship during it, and got an engineering job in April 2024 right after finishing [XU0148, XU0149, XU0150]. His account, from a May 2024 interview:
1. From the start of the master's, go to the university's career fairs and ask company engineers whether they take international students and would sponsor a visa later; leaving a CV at a career fair got him the most responses [XU0152, XU0220].
2. Keep LinkedIn, Indeed and Glassdoor profiles updated and apply through them too, starting at the beginning of the final year [XU0220, XU0221].
3. He applied to 28 companies, was called by six, and got two offers after several interview rounds [XU0151].

**What the job pays, by his account:** USD 80,000-100,000 a year to start for a master's graduate in New York, Chicago or Los Angeles, and 70,000-90,000 in smaller cities; 25-35% of pay is withheld as tax, but international students pay no Medicare or Social Security for their first five years, so it comes to 15-20%; a one-bedroom flat costs USD 2,500 a month in a big city and up to 1,500 in a small one; hours over 40 a week are paid at 1.5 to 2 times the rate; paid leave is 10-15 days in the first year [XU0153, XU0154, XU0155, XU0156, XU0157].

**The visa:** staying on to work needs an employer to sponsor an H-1B visa, and the USD 100,000 fee does not apply to someone who was in the US on a student visa (see Work visa: H-1B) [XU0152, XU0212]. In September 2025 the US Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said H-1B visas would be given by salary instead of lottery, and the employer must pay a former student at least a minimum set by area and occupation: USD 136,000 for a new computer science graduate in San Francisco County, and USD 164,000 for a software developer in Las Vegas, where a landscape architect earning USD 97,000 would have a better chance [XU0171, XU0172, XU0173].

#### Done by students

- A student's visa was cancelled after he posted on Facebook, on getting it, asking for help finding restaurant or gas-station work in the US [XU0020].
- A student was sent home with his visa cancelled after allegedly trying to forcibly kiss a woman at the gas station where he worked, a columnist reported [XU0022].
- The US has revoked visas en masse of foreigners who joined pro-Palestine protests, and over social media comments on the killing of Charlie Kirk; the State Department spokesperson said in October 2026 there would be no tolerance for a visa holder who breaks the law, commits fraud or undermines national security [XU0216, XU0217, XU0215].

## Exchange visitor visa: Fulbright

Fulbright grants take Bangladeshi teachers, academics and professionals to the US on exchange; the TEA programme gives J-1 visa support [XU0007, XU0052, XU0008].

### Fulbright Teaching Excellence and Achievement (TEA)

A six-week exchange in the US for secondary school teachers, run through the US Embassy's American Center in Dhaka [XU0007].

**Who qualifies:** full-time secondary teachers of English, EFL (English as a foreign language), maths, science, foreign languages, social studies, journalism or special education, with a bachelor's degree and at least five years of full-time teaching [XU0009]. Ministry officials, education administrators, full-time teacher trainers, university teachers and private English tutors cannot apply [XU0010].

**What it covers:** J-1 visa support, a pre-departure orientation in Dhaka, return and domestic US airfare, programme fees, housing (usually shared) and meals, and accident and health insurance [XU0008].

**How to apply:**
1. Apply online with an institutional support and reference form (ISRF) filled in by your supervisor and a leave approval form (LAF) from https://fulbright.irex.org/ [XU0012].
2. If shortlisted, take a paper-based IELTS (International English Language Testing System) test (score 6.0 or more, or equivalent), which the American Center arranges, and an interview in English [XU0011].

> **Need to know**
> - When does the TEA call open in 2027, and does it run every year?

### Fulbright Visiting Scholar

A fully funded grant for one academic year of research or teaching at a US institution [XU0052].

**Who qualifies:** Bangladeshi public and private professionals, university teachers and researchers with 10 or more years of professional experience, holding a PhD or equivalent professional degree and proposing research and teaching in their field, with English good enough to work professionally at a US university [XU0052, XU0120, XU0119].

**What it covers:** return airfare, academic fees, a monthly stipend for room and living costs, a book allowance, health and accident insurance, and travel and excess-baggage allowances [XU0053].

**How to apply:** online, after creating a login account; questions go to dhakausexchanges@state.gov [XU0054]. For the round starting in September 2027 the deadline was extended to 31 October 2026, 10:59 am Bangladesh time [XU0118, XU0122, XU0119]. More than 200 Bangladeshis have taken part over 52 years [XU0121].

## Visit visa and the visa bond

The B1/B2 visa is for business and tourism [XU0064]. Since 21 January 2026 Bangladesh is on the US visa bond list of 38 countries, under a 12-month pilot that began on 20 August 2025 [XU0060, XU0061, XU0068]. Diplomatic sources said the US had signalled to Bangladesh that it was taking a hard line on overstaying, and the US Department of Homeland Security told Congress that of about 39 million visa holders due to leave in fiscal 2023, about 400,000 stayed past expiry [XU0069, XU0072]. The embassy's own payment instructions are covered elsewhere (see "The visitor visa bond" in the Illegal routes doc).

### Step 1: The interview

Attend the visa interview in person; every applicant must [XU0062].

### Step 2: Post the bond

1. If the officer finds you eligible, the officer sets the bond at USD 5,000, 10,000 or 15,000 [XU0064].
2. Submit DHS (Department of Homeland Security) form I-352 and agree to the bond terms on Pay.gov, the US Treasury's online payment platform [XU0065].
3. Paying the bond does not guarantee a visa; the money comes back if the visa is refused [XU0063].

### Step 3: Travel through the named airports

Enter and leave the US only through Boston Logan (BOS), New York JFK or Washington Dulles (IAD) airports; otherwise you may be refused entry, or your departure may not be recorded [XU0066, XU0067].

### Getting the bond back

The money is returned if the visa holder complies with all the visa's conditions [XU0063].

> **Need to know**

The Foreign Adviser said Bangladesh would seek an exemption through the normal process [XU0075].

## Family immigrant visa: the green card

An immigrant visa gives a green card on entry and permanent residence; it covers family-based green cards (spouse, parents, children, siblings) and employment-based permanent residence [XU0086].

### The pause from 21 January 2026

- **What it does:** citizens of 75 countries including Bangladesh can still submit immigrant visa applications, but none will be approved or issued, and no end date has been given [XU0077, XU0079].
- **Why:** on 27 November 2025 President Trump wrote that he would permanently pause immigration from all third-world countries [XU0189]. The State Department said the pause follows a November 2025 order to tighten screening of would-be immigrants who might become a financial burden [XU0078]. On a list of 120 countries ranked by immigrant welfare use that President Trump posted on social media in January 2026, Bangladesh was 19th, with 54.8% of Bangladeshi immigrant households in the US taking US government benefits [XU0070].
- **Who is hit:** relatives of US citizens with petitions already filed cannot go for now; new petitions can be filed but get no visa; a US citizen who newly marries cannot bring the spouse for now; three US immigration lawyers said lakhs of Bangladeshis would be harmed [XU0087, XU0089, XU0088]. Lawyer Moin Chowdhury said a child who passes 21 meanwhile cannot come [XU0090]. Lawyer Dilli Raj Bhatta said it includes people whose interviews were already scheduled, and that legal challenges were expected [XU0091, XU0092].
- **Screening:** USCIS updated its policy in August 2025 to extend social media vetting of applications to searches for "anti-American activity" [XU0071].
- **Who is not hit:** non-immigrant, temporary, tourist and business visa applicants, and dual nationals applying on a passport of a country not on the list [XU0081, XU0080].

> **Need to know**
> - Does a child who turns 21 during the pause lose the place in the queue, or does the Child Status Protection Act protect them?

### Health and means checks

In November 2025 the State Department told visa officers to consider an applicant's health conditions that need costly care, such as heart disease, respiratory disease, cancer, diabetes, metabolic, neurological and mental conditions, and obesity, and whether the applicant can pay for treatment without government help, as grounds to find them ineligible [XU0184, XU0185]. Charles Wheeler of the Catholic Legal Immigration Network said it applies to almost all visa applicants but will most likely be used only for those applying to live permanently in the US [XU0186].

### Applying from inside the US

Since May 2026 people in the US on temporary visas (student, visit and so on) must go back to their own country to apply for a green card: USCIS guidance says changing to permanent status from inside the US ("adjustment of status") is not a right but a matter of discretion, judged case by case [XU0199, XU0200, XU0201]. Officers must weigh visa violations, overstaying, working without permission, fraud and whether the applicant kept the conditions of entry [XU0202]. Some "dual intent" visa categories still allow the holder to seek a green card while in the US, with no guarantee of one [XU0203].

### Filling in the forms

A US-based columnist reported parents whose immigration form had a date entered in the "date of death" field; at the interview they were told they had died in 2002 and had to prove they were alive [XU0027].

### Scams

A Narail man posing on Facebook as a US-based pilot of Bangladeshi origin promised to marry a divorced woman and take her to the US, and took Tk 1 crore 9 lakh from her, which she said he asked for in instalments for a passport, visa and other pretexts [XU0002, XU0003]. She sent the money to 13 different mobile numbers, and said she sold 37 bhori of gold and took a bank loan [XU0004, XU0003]. When she found his Khulna address was false he threatened to post private videos of her [XU0005]. She filed a case at Wari police station and police arrested him, but under pressure to repay her debts she settled for Tk 60 lakh [XU0127, XU0006]. (see "Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes" in the Dalal scams doc).

## Work visa: H-1B

US employers use the H-1B visa to hire skilled foreign workers, mostly in technology but also in hospitals, banks and universities [XU0082, XU0179]. It is usually the only realistic way for a skilled foreigner to work long-term in the US and later get a green card (see After graduation: getting a US job) [XU0178].

**How many:** 65,000 a year, plus 20,000 for holders of a US master's or higher degree, given by lottery; universities, non-profits and some other employers are outside the limit [XU0175]. In September 2025 DHS said selection would move from lottery to salary, favouring the highest paid [XU0171].

**The USD 100,000 fee:**
- President Trump's executive order of 19 September 2025 requires US companies to pay it to bring a skilled worker on an H-1B; it took effect on 21 September 2025 with each new application [XU0164, XU0166].
- It is paid once, on the application, not every year, the White House said on 20 September 2025; the Commerce Secretary had said the day before that it would be paid every year [XU0167, XU0165].
- It does not apply to people already holding a valid H-1B, to renewals, to H-1B holders re-entering from abroad, or to people who were in the US on a student visa and then applied for an H-1B; doctors may be outside it, and some applications are accepted without it in the national interest [XU0166, XU0168, XU0169, XU0212, XU0174, XU0170].
- The normal fee is USD 2,000 to 5,000 [XU0210].
- In September 2026 the order was extended for another year [XU0209]. A federal judge ruled the fee illegal and ordered the government to stop collecting it; an appeals court in Boston has been reviewing that ruling since June 2026 [XU0211].

In October 2025 DHS also proposed a rule to change which employers are exempt from the limit, scrutinise employers that break the rules more strictly and watch third-party placements (workers placed at another company) more closely [XU0176, XU0177].

## Work permits

An EAD (Employment Authorization Document, Form I-766) proves permission to work in the US for a set period; green card holders do not need one, and H-1B and L-1B holders generally do not either [XU0183]. Since 30 October 2025 a renewal application no longer extends the old permit automatically; before, a worker who applied on time could keep working for up to 540 days after expiry [XU0180, XU0181]. USCIS advises applying 180 days before the permit expires, warning that the later the application, the greater the risk of having to stop work [XU0182].

## Joining a husband in the US

A columnist who works with abused women wrote in February 2024 that the wives of Bangladeshis who go to the US to study or work mostly cannot legally work there, and their own studies can be very costly [XU0139]. She wrote that families should find out, before the marriage, whether the husband's visa will create obstacles for the wife, what his immigration plans are, whether he disagrees with her studying or working, and whether he has a previous marriage or a history of domestic violence; a wife is even more helpless if the husband disappears after the wedding or abandons her [XU0147, XU0146, XU0145].

### If your husband abuses you

- **What abusers use:** a new wife who does not know the immigration rules may be threatened with being sent back, divorced or separated from her children; in a 2019 Pew survey only 55% of Bangladeshis in the US were comfortable in English, against 72% of Asians, which keeps many women from getting out [XU0137, XU0138].
- **The law:** the US has domestic violence laws, and a victim can get a protection order [XU0141, XU0136].
- **Help:** many organisations give free services, and South Asian women's organisations in many cities can be contacted in Bangla, among them Daya Houston in Texas (https://www.dayahouston.org/) and Maitri in California (www.maitri.org) [XU0141, XU0140, XU0142, XU0144, XU0143].
- **Case:** Nowrin Nahar Tuli, 21, who had a protection order, was strangled by her husband Nasib Ahsan in Texas on 24 December 2023, the columnist reported; he is held in Collin County jail [XU0136, XU0135].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a wife whose status depends on an abusive husband apply for her own immigration status, and what is the national domestic violence hotline number?

## Asylum

On 28 November 2025, after the shooting of National Guard members in Washington DC, USCIS halted decisions on all asylum applications until every applicant can be vetted to the maximum degree [XU0188, XU0187]. The US set its refugee ceiling for fiscal 2026 at 7,500, the lowest in its history, mainly for white South Africans [XU0085, XU0083]. Bangladeshis who post on Facebook how to switch from a visitor visa to asylum risk making visas harder for everyone, a US-based columnist wrote [XU0025, XU0026]. (see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc).

> **Need to know**
> - Has USCIS resumed asylum decisions since the November 2025 halt?

## Dual citizenship

In late 2025 Republican Senator Bernie Moreno introduced the Exclusive Citizenship Act of 2025: dual citizens would get a deadline, probably one year, to give up their other citizenship or lose US citizenship automatically [XU0191, XU0192]. As of December 2025 it was only a proposal in the Senate and must pass both houses of Congress to become law [XU0193].

## Irregular routes and undocumented stay

### Overland through South America and Mexico

In the case of Saifullah Al-Mamun, a Bangladeshi extradited from Brazil on 8 July 2026 and charged in Laredo, Texas, with conspiring to smuggle people from Bangladesh into the US, the charges describe this route [XU0110, XU0111]:
1. People paid large sums to brokers in Bangladesh [XU0114].
2. Saifullah housed them in São Paulo, Brazil, and arranged their travel [XU0112].
3. Mohammad Milon Hossain kept them in Tapachula, Mexico, and sent them to Monterrey [XU0112].
4. Moktar Hossain kept them in Monterrey and told them how to cross the Rio Grande into the US, which many found hard to cross safely [XU0113].

Milon and Moktar pleaded guilty and were each sentenced to 46 months; Saifullah faces 5 to 15 years if convicted of smuggling for financial gain [XU0115, XU0116]. The Justice Department's Joint Task Force Alpha has arrested more than 464 smugglers and associates, and more than 414 have been convicted in the US [XU0117]. (see "The United States through South America and Mexico" in the Illegal routes doc).

### Money paid to brokers who never send you

Zakir Hossain, 24, of Chatkhil, Noakhali, paid Tk 25 lakh to a Lakshmipur and Feni broker ring to go to the US, and two and a half years later had not gone; his family holds papers for Tk 19 lakh of it [XU0045]. When he asked for his money back the brokers threatened him, and in August 2025 he and a friend were found dead in a car in a Dhaka hospital car park; his family suspects the ring, and police had not yet found the cause of death [XU0046, XU0123, XU0047].

### Where undocumented Bangladeshis live

Bangladesh's Foreign Ministry has no figure, but officials said irregular Bangladeshis are likely concentrated in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut [XU0019].

### Deportation to Bangladesh

- **Who is sent back:** people who stayed after losing their immigration cases, including some convicted of crimes in the US; Bangladesh's Foreign Ministry is told about each one in advance [XU0033, XU0034].
- **Bangladesh's terms:** it takes people back once their citizenship is confirmed, and asked the US not to send them in handcuffs [XU0018, XU0126].
- **How they arrive:** 34 people from 6 March to 21 April 2025; 39 by chartered plane on 2 August 2025; 30 more (29 men and one woman) on 5 September 2025, put on the plane in handcuffs that were removed on landing in Dhaka, they told law enforcement [XU0032, XU0044, XU0048, XU0049, XU0051].
- **Totals:** 187 by September 2025 and about 500 by March 2026, with about 5,000 more in process [XU0050, XU0099].

> **Need to know**
> - What help do returnees get at Dhaka airport, and does removal bring a ban on returning to the US, for how long?

## Health care and its cost

In the US medicine cannot be bought without a doctor's prescription [XU0160]. Health cuts under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act would harm about 2 lakh Bangladeshi immigrants, Prothom Alo reported in August 2025; people without papers lose health care, and a US-based Bangladeshi said most Bangladeshis do middle- or low-income work and rely on government health support, with home care, ride-share, restaurant, delivery and taxi workers hit [XU0159, XU0160, XU0161, XU0162]. People aged 19 to 64 who can work but do not work at least 20 hours a week will lose Medicaid (government health insurance for people on low incomes), a home-care business owner said [XU0163]. President Trump said in November 2025 that no non-citizen would get federal benefits or subsidies [XU0190].

A US-based Bangladeshi wrote in March 2025 that each of his children's two-day hospital births billed over USD 50,000, of which he paid a USD 6,000 insurance deductible, and that a vlogger in New York, being poor, got the city's insurance and paid nothing [XU0023, XU0024].

> **Need to know**
> - What health insurance must students and visitors carry, and what does a student plan cost?

## Sending money home

### The 1% tax on cash

Since 1 January 2026, money sent from the US in cash, money order, cashier's cheque or similar physical means carries a federal excise tax of USD 1 per USD 100; sending from a bank account, by debit card or online carries no extra tax [XU0055, XU0057]. It is part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed on 4 July 2025 [XU0056]. Experts said it hits hardest irregular migrants without bank accounts and low-income migrants [XU0058].

### Who sends it

At the April 2025 remittance fair in Jackson Heights, New York, Sonali Bank, Janata Bank and Islami Bank Bangladesh were ranked first to third for remittance from the US; Mastercard, MoneyGram and Ria first to third among non-banks; and Sonali Exchange, BA Express and Standard Express were awarded for service to the Bangladeshi community [XU0028, XU0029, XU0030, XU0031].

### The numbers

- USD 3.52 billion in fiscal 2022-23, second after Saudi Arabia [XU0001].
- USD 1.4449 billion in January-March 2025, about 18% of the total and the most from any country, ahead of the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia; USD 4.271 billion in July 2024 to April 2025 [XU0039, XU0124, XU0035].
- Fourth in 2025 with 11% of the total, and fifth by January 2026, according to Standard Express's chief executive [XU0097, XU0059].

The US became the top source after August 2024 [XU0040]. Large firms such as Western Union and MoneyGram collect remittances in an aggregated way, and some in the sector say exchange houses collect from various countries and send from the US [XU0037, XU0041]. (see "Ways to send money and what each costs" in the Sending money home doc).

## If a student or worker dies

In April 2026 two Bangladeshi PhD students at the University of South Florida went missing; a family friend reported them the next day, police found one body and arrested his roommate, who was charged with two counts of first-degree murder, and the other student's body had not been found two days later [XU0101, XU0103, XU0102, XU0198, XU0125].
1. The Bangladesh Embassy in Washington, the Foreign Ministry and the local police kept in touch with the family [XU0108].
2. The district commissioner said the family must submit an application, which the district sends to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare to bring the body home [XU0107].
3. The upazila officer said the embassy does the main work and the local administration gives the embassy any local information it needs [XU0106].

**Other cases:**
- **Sheikh Abir Hossain, 38,** a research assistant at Lamar University, Texas, on a scholarship, who also worked part-time in a food shop in Beaumont, was shot dead at a shop in Beaumont on 29 December 2023 by people stealing cigarettes, police said; four days later his body had still not reached his family because of US holiday-season staffing, a local journalist said, and Bangladeshi students at Lamar opened a GoFundMe fundraiser to which about 14,000 people gave USD 55,000 [XU0134, XU0129, XU0128, XU0130, XU0132, XU0133].
- **Mohammad Mahfuzul Haque,** a DoorDash food-delivery worker described by Bangladeshi community media in the US as of Bangladeshi origin, was shot dead in Kingsessing, Philadelphia, in July 2026 while making a delivery to an address where, police said, nobody had placed an order; investigators believe it was a planned killing by three masked people [XU0205, XU0206, XU0207, XU0208].

> **Need to know**
> - Who pays to bring a body home from the US for a student not registered with BMET, and is the Wage Earners' Welfare Board grant available?
