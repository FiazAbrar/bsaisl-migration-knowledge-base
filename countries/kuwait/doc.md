# Kuwait: migration guide

How to go to Kuwait on a Bangladeshi passport, visa by visa, step by step. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO, research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000); KD is the Kuwaiti dinar (1,000 fils), converted throughout at KD 1 = about Tk 403, the Central Bank of Kuwait's rate of 2.483 fils per taka on 9 November 2026 [G0002]. The same day's dollar rates (USD 1 = KD 0.308 in Kuwait, Tk 124.35 in Bangladesh) give the same figure [G0001, G0003]. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is the Bangladesh government agency that registers job-seekers on its OEP (Overseas Employment Platform) website and gives every worker emigration clearance before departure [B0038, B0084]. In Kuwait, PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) issues the work permit, holds each company's file and takes labour complaints [W0002, B0114, B0136, N0083]. The Ministry of Interior (MOI) issues entry visas on the basis of the PAM permit, grants residence ("iqama", আকামা), approves sponsor changes and runs Kuwait Visa (kuwaitvisa.moi.gov.kw), its online portal for visit visas [W0002, W0020, W0009, L0385, V0082, K0072]. Sahel is the Kuwaiti government's services app, in English and Arabic only, where workers request exit permits, pay fines and bills and check travel bans [L0309, L0318, G0047, G0048, G0055]. PACI (Public Authority for Civil Information) issues the Civil ID, the card that records your residence [W0013, K0027].

## Background

### In one minute

A Kuwait job starts as a visa that costs the employer little, a KD 150 first work permit fee and a KD 10 entry visa, but is sold to Bangladeshis through brokers for KD 2,000 to 2,200 (about Tk 8-9 lakh) [W0033, W0020, N0016]. The government's cap for what an agency may take is Tk 1,06,780 [B0184]. Company (Article 18) visas reopened to Bangladeshis in May 2025 after years of bans, and BMET cleared 27,964 workers for Kuwait from 1 January to 8 November 2026 [N0014, B0004]. The 2026 Iran war shut Kuwait's airspace from 28 February to 23 April and stopped new entry visas, and visas issued before then and not used were not extended [S0001, S0013, S0012]. Women cannot go as domestic workers, and new domestic-worker visas are closed to Bangladeshis [D0012, D0016, D0001, D0002]. Since 1 August 2025 the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait attests a visa only if it pays at least KD 90 a month for a government-contract cleaner or labourer and KD 150 for most skilled trades [M0014, M0018, M0022]. The visa ties you to one employer: you need its consent to change jobs and its exit permit to leave, and working for anyone else can mean prison and deportation [L0333, L0305, L0411, U0106, L0099]. What goes wrong most is a visa with no real job behind it and pay that doesn't come: in October 2026 about 10,000 companies had not paid their foreign workers for months [U0098, N0080].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

On 9 November 2026 Kuwait is issuing company work visas (Article 18) to Bangladeshis again after the 2026 war, and new domestic-worker visas (Article 20) are closed to Bangladeshis [S0001, S0013, B0016, B0020, D0001, D0002]. A man of any trade can go if a Kuwaiti employer gets him a PAM work permit and an MOI entry visa, and the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait attests the visa at or above its minimum salary (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 3) [W0002, W0020, M0014].

#### Status

- **Private-sector company job (Article 18):** open; BMET clearances recovered from 1,376 a month in April and May 2026 to 3,749 in July, 4,538 in September and 3,914 in October, and were 1,305 in the first eight days of November [B0024, B0019, B0021, B0016, B0020, S0034]. The 2021 rule that Bangladeshis needed special Interior Ministry approval no longer applies: since May 2025 Bangladeshis get visas without it [N0041, N0014, V0096].
- **Domestic worker, house driver, cook (Article 20, the "khadem visa", খাদেম ভিসা):** closed to new Bangladeshi workers: a June 2026 MOI circular allows new domestic workers from 10 countries only, and Bangladesh is not one of them (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [D0001, D0002, N0026]. The Embassy does not attest women's domestic-worker (খাদ্দামা) visas and asks women not to go until the two governments sign an agreement [D0012, D0011, D0016].
- **An entry visa issued before the war and never used:** it lapsed; work, domestic and family entry visas held by people outside Kuwait were not extended, and the employer must apply for a new one [S0012, S0013, B0203].
- **Visit visas:** a Bangladeshi can get a family visit visa online, but not a tourist e-visa without a US, UK, Schengen or GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) visa or residence permit (see Visit visas (tourist, family, business)) [M0003, M0001, M0002].
- **Residents stuck abroad:** the extra absence permits Kuwait gave from 28 February 2026 ended on 1 September 2026, except for people who left on or before 31 August 2026; the residence status certificate in Sahel shows the date by which you must be back [S0009, S0026, S0027, S0028].
- **Getting there:** flights from Kuwait International Airport restarted on 26 April 2026, with Kuwait Airways planning Dhaka flights four times a week, but they have stopped again at short notice: a drone hit Terminal 1 on 3 June, the airspace closed again on 18 July, and the Bangladesh Embassy issued emergency hotlines after new attacks in early September (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 6) [S0002, S0003, S0021, S0025, B0202].

#### The channels

- **A visa bought through an agency or broker:** most workers go this way; the worker pays one sum to an agency or broker (দালাল), who arranges everything, including processing through the agencies the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorises (see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut) [N0013, N0002].
- **A licensed agency with a BMET permission letter:** the letter names the employer, the job, the pay and the most the agency may take (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 1) [B0169].
- **A single visa:** a relative or contact in Kuwait gets a visa from a sponsor and has it attested at the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait [B0101].
- **Government-contract or private-company visa:** until May 2025 most Bangladeshis went on "akhud" (আখুদ, contract) visas with companies, mostly cleaning firms, servicing government contracts, while the "ahli" (আহলি, private company) visa was closed; the Embassy's minimum salaries still split government contracts (aqud hukumi) from private ones (aqud ahli) [N0067, N0068, N0088, M0018, M0020].
- **Nurses:** a government-to-government route hired 670 Bangladeshi nurses under a 2024 MOU (memorandum of understanding) (see Work visa: professional and skilled) [N0060].

#### Who qualifies

- **Salary:** the Embassy attests a visa only if the pay in the work permit meets its minimum for the job (see What the job pays) [M0014].
- **Women:** few go, 230 of the 27,964 workers cleared in 2026 to 8 November, and only on company visas, mostly as cleaners; domestic work is closed to them [B0008, B0004, D0017, D0011]. BMET cleared 1,183 women for Kuwait in 2023, 414 in 2024 and 488 in 2025 [B0005, B0006, B0007].
- **Drivers and company representatives who lived in Kuwait before:** cannot get a new work visa in the same job until two years after they left [W0014].

#### Size

- **Bangladeshis in Kuwait:** 333,100 at the end of June 2026 by PACI's count, the third-largest foreign community after Indians (1.06 million) and Egyptians (about 670,000) [N0076].
- **At work:** Bangladeshis are 9.9% of Kuwait's workforce and 11.2% of private-sector employees, and 106,942 of them are domestic workers [N0078, N0079, N0028]. India has the most domestic workers in Kuwait (344,532), then Sri Lanka (157,901) and the Philippines (151,369) [N0030].
- **Departures:** BMET cleared 36,445 workers for Kuwait in 2023, 33,015 in 2024, 42,746 in 2025 and 27,964 from 1 January to 8 November 2026 [B0001, B0002, B0003, B0004]. Kuwait was Bangladesh's 7th destination in 2023, 6th in 2024, 4th in 2025 and 5th in 2026 [B0009, B0010, B0011, B0012]. Clearances reached 12,192 in August 2025, the month the Embassy's minimum salaries took effect [B0015, B0187].

#### What closed it before

- **2007:** Kuwait stopped recruiting Bangladeshis after detecting recruitment irregularities [N0035].
- **2014-2016:** the ban was lifted in 2014; in May 2016 Kuwait reopened household visas for Bangladeshi men only, one per citizen [N0036, D0019].
- **March 2018:** the Interior Minister halted work visas for Bangladeshis again, after security reports that they had become a target of iqama traders and that their numbers had grown through the illegal trade in work and residence permits [N0037, N0040].
- **2020-2021, the Papul case:** see Who controls it: the Papul case. In November 2021 Bangladesh was one of seven nationalities refused business, work and family visas except with special Interior Ministry approval [N0041]. That approval ("lamana", লামানা) was sought from the minister through brokers and could take years [N0071].
- **May 2025:** all categories reopened to Bangladeshis [N0014].
- **28 February 2026, the Iran war:** Kuwait closed its airspace to 23 April 2026, and its airlines flew from Al-Qaisumah and Dammam in Saudi Arabia, with passengers bussed four to five hours through the Nuwaiseeb border crossing [S0001, S0004, S0006]. On 22 April 2026 a Foreign Ministry circular said unused entry visas would not be extended and employers must reapply once issuing resumed [S0012, S0013]. BMET clearances fell from 2,610 in January to 1,376 in April and in May [S0031, B0024, B0019].
- **June 2026:** new domestic-worker visas closed to Bangladeshis (see Status) [D0001, D0002].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A Kuwait work visa costs the employer little: PAM charges KD 150 extra for a first work permit and MOI KD 10 for the entry visa, and there is no government price for a worker visa [W0033, W0020, N0020]. Brokers sell the same visa for KD 2,000 to 2,200, about Tk 8-9 lakh (July 2025), and the difference is the chain's cut [N0016]. The choke points are two: the processing agencies the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorises, and the Bangladesh Embassy's attestation in Kuwait [N0002, M0014].

#### The visa trade in Kuwait

- **Where the visa comes from:** brokers get visas by tempting Kuwaiti citizens with money and sell them to Bangladeshis [N0019].
- **Paper companies:** in October 2026 the Interior Minister said about 10,000 companies, each with about five workers, had not paid their foreign workers for months, and PAM had suspended 8,849 company files by June 2026 [N0080, N0083]. In July 2025 a Kuwaiti who signed for 29 companies confessed to selling residence permits to 56 workers for KD 350 to 1,200 each without giving them work [U0092, U0093].
- **Inside PAM:** in 2025 prosecutors charged a company owner and PAM officials over 382 workers recruited under 28 non-existent companies at KD 800 to 1,000 each, with PAM staff allegedly taking KD 200 to 250 a worker in bribes [U0090, U0091].
- **Who runs it:** a Bangladeshi press club leader in Kuwait says an organised broker ring controls the trade, which is why Bangladeshis pay more than other South Asians [N0021]. Bangladesh's ambassador held meetings to hold visa prices to Tk 4-5 lakh at most, but brokers set their own price [N0022].
- **Prosecutions:** in November 2026 Kuwait's Criminal Court jailed a Bangladeshi sanitation worker for four years with hard labour, and two Egyptians for five, for trading residence permits through a goods transport company, which was fined KD 10,000 per worker and lost its licence [N0056, N0057].

#### The authorised processing agencies in Dhaka

A worker may get a demand letter alone or through any agency, but the visa must be processed by one of the agencies the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorises [N0002]. The Embassy has authorised at least 10; the owner of one, Modern Overseas, says 14 or 15 [N0003, N0004]. In early 2025 these agencies were accused of charging USD 150-300 (Tk 17,000-35,000) per visa against an official processing fee of Tk 6,270, which they deny [N0001, N0005]. Complaints to the Chief Adviser's Office estimate that USD 30-37 million was taken this way from about 134,000 workers who went to Kuwait from 2018 to early 2025 [N0011].

> **Need to know**
> - Which agencies does the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorise in 2026, where is the list published, and what do they charge per visa?

#### The Embassy's check in Kuwait

The Bangladesh Embassy's Labour Wing checks the salary in the work permit against its minimum salaries before attesting a visa or demand letter (how a worker checks this is in Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [M0014, M0015]. The employer, or his registered representative, must come to the Embassy in person and show the sponsor's original Civil ID [N0025, B0103].

#### Who controls it: the Papul case

- **The arrest:** Kuwait's Criminal Investigation Department arrested Mohammad Shahid Islam "Kazi" Papul, then a Bangladeshi MP, on 6 June 2020 on charges of human trafficking, money laundering and bribing officials, for bringing workers to Kuwait through a cleaning company he managed [N0100, N0048, N0044, N0050].
- **The sentences:** the top appeals court gave him seven years, a fine reported as KD 2.7 million, and deportation in November 2021; an Interior Ministry undersecretary, a manpower director and the head of PAM were jailed in the same case [N0042, N0046, N0043, N0047].
- **The Bangladesh side:** court sources said the agency Job Bank International (RL1183) sent people to Kuwait on Kazi Badrul's orders, to be received by Papul's company Marafie Kuwaitia, and the ring recruited through brokers across the country at Tk 5-7 lakh each; the agency is still listed Active on BMET's licence list, valid to 31 December 2031 [N0051, N0052, N0093].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

People pay Tk 7-10 lakh for a Kuwait visa against a government cap of Tk 1,06,780, while Indians and Nepalis pay Tk 50,000 to 1.5 lakh [N0069, B0184]. A government-contract cleaner's minimum is KD 90 (about Tk 36,300) a month [M0018].

#### The cap

The Embassy says the government has set the migration cost for Kuwait at Tk 1,06,780, that taking more is a punishable offence, and that buying or selling a visa is a crime for both sides [B0184]. Each BMET permission letter sets its own, often lower, maximum: Tk 44,370 to Tk 1,05,000 in the 2024-2025 letters [B0169, B0179, B0178]. The employer bears all Kuwait-side costs, including the levy and airfare [B0171].

- **2016-2020:** an average of Tk 5,60,000 among 292 men surveyed, 525% above the official fee [N0007].
- **Processing alone:** USD 150-300 to an authorised processing agency (see The authorised processing agencies in Dhaka) [N0001].

#### What the job pays

The Embassy's minimum monthly salaries for new visas from 1 August 2025, for an 8-hour day [M0014, M0015, M0018, M0019, M0020, M0021, M0022]:

- government-contract (aqud hukumi) cleaner, general labourer or agricultural worker: KD 90 (about Tk 36,300) [M0018];
- government-contract driver: KD 120 (about Tk 48,400) [M0019];
- private-contract (aqud ahli) cleaner, general labourer or guard: KD 90; agricultural worker KD 120 [M0020];
- private-contract skilled worker (mason, electrician, plumber, welder, mechanic, driver and others): KD 150 (about Tk 60,500); chef KD 250 [M0021, M0022];

Workers already in Kuwait are not covered, and employers may pay more [B0187, M0014]. Before this, most Bangladeshis worked for cleaning companies at KD 75 a month [N0068]. On that pay about 90% of Bangladeshis on such visas took illegal part-time work after their shift, which gets some sent home (see What can get you jailed or deported) [N0072, N0074].

#### What goes unpaid

- **Paper employers:** about 10,000 small companies had not paid foreign workers for months in October 2026, and the Interior Minister set the 20th of each month as the payment deadline (see If you aren't paid) [N0080, N0081].
- **Large contractors too:** in July 2019 about 4,000 mostly Asian workers of a Kuwait Oil Company contractor protested at four months' unpaid pay, and some had become illegal because the company had not renewed their residence [N0084].
- **No work at all:** a cleaner who went in 2018 sat eight months in the company barracks without work, borrowing for food [N0073].

#### Money sent home

Workers in Kuwait sent Bangladesh USD 1,763.7 million through banks and exchange houses in July 2025 to June 2026, about 5% of all remittances [G0015, G0016]. In July-September 2026 Kuwait was Bangladesh's seventh-largest source, at USD 441.8 million [G0004]. Sending from Kuwait cost 1.51% of the amount on average in April-June 2026, and 3.91% for sums up to USD 200 (how to send is in Sending money home) [G0007, G0006].

#### How long the debt takes

Across all destinations, the average migration cost of Tk 4.17 lakh in a 2020 government survey took 17.6 months to recover [N0054]. A Kuwait cleaner who went in 2018 was still repaying after four and a half years [N0075].

### What ties you to the employer

An Article 18 visa ties you to one employer: your work permit and residence rest on it, and working for anyone else is a crime [W0009, L0411].

- **To enter:** the work entry visa is issued only on the employer's PAM work permit (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 3) [W0002].
- **To renew:** your residence is granted under the employer's work permit and recorded on your Civil ID (see Your residence permit) [W0009, W0013].
- **To quit:** on an open-ended contract either side gives three months' written notice, and many contracts make an early leaver repay recruitment, visa, travel and medical costs (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0017, L0090].
- **To change employer:** only after one year and with the employer's consent, except where PAM allows an earlier transfer since May 2026 for unpaid wages, abuse, a false report and similar cases (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0333, L0328, L0329].
- **To leave:** since 1 July 2025 you need your employer's exit permit, requested on Sahel, even for a holiday (see Going home on leave) [L0305, L0309].
- **Which law:** the Private Sector Labour Law (Law 6 of 2010) covers Article 18 workers; domestic workers come under their own law, Law 68 of 2015 [L0002, L0001, D0045].
- **Your passport:** keeping a worker's passport is illegal but widespread [L0320].
- **If you run away:** after 7 days' absence in a row the employer can file an absence report, people with absence cases are deported, and once deported you can't come back (see If your employer reports you absent) [U0108, L0015, L0321, U0032, L0099].
- **If your residence lapses:** when your service ends without renewal or a move to another employer, you must leave within the time MOI sets, at most six months from the end of your service [L0422].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The harms reported most are a visa bought at a high price with no real job behind it, and pay that doesn't come: workers arrive to find no work, a visa called fake, or a sponsor with no job [N0073, N0008, N0091, U0098]. In October 2026 about 10,000 companies had not paid their workers for months [N0080].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A free visa (ফ্রি ভিসা / আজাদ ভিসা) lets me work anywhere."** No such visa exists: every visa is tied to a sponsor, and working outside it is punished by fine, jail or deportation [N0089, N0090]. Workers sold one find on arrival a sponsor and no guaranteed job [N0091].
- **"I paid in full, so my visa is good."** A worker who reached his company in October 2024 was told his visa was fake and that he still owed money for it, or he would be sent home (how to check a visa is in Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [N0008].
- **"Part-time work on the side is normal, so it's safe."** It is common but illegal, and workers caught are sent home [N0072, N0074].
- **"I paid for my visa, so I'm only a victim."** A worker who got a residence permit for money faces up to one year in prison, a fine of up to KD 1,000, or both, unless he reports the crime [L0415].
- **"Women can go to Kuwait as domestic workers."** Not now: the Embassy does not attest their visas and asks women not to go [D0012, D0016].

#### Irregular routes and how they end

- **Women sent as domestic workers outside the attested route:** rings are sending women illegally, and the Embassy brought home seven who went that way in three to four months to October 2026 [N0101, N0032].
- **A visa with no working employer behind it:** it ends in unpaid wages and, when the company does not renew the residence, illegal status (see Undocumented workers and amnesties) [N0084, U0098].
- **Leaving your sponsor to work elsewhere:** workers who change jobs without transferring sponsorship fall out of legal status, and those caught are jailed and deported [U0099, U0107, U0106].
- **How it ends:** the last amnesty ran from 14 March to 30 June 2024, and a violator who missed it can only leave, with no re-entry (see Going home without valid papers) [U0013, U0023, U0024].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Tourist e-visa:** a Bangladeshi is told "not eligible" unless he holds a valid US, UK or Schengen visa or residence permit, or a GCC residence permit; Bangladesh is not on the list of countries whose nationals get a tourist e-visa or visa on arrival [M0001, M0002, V0006].
- **Visa-free entry:** only for nationals of countries named by decree, on a reciprocal basis; Bangladesh is not one [V0049].
- **New domestic-worker visa (Article 20):** closed to Bangladeshis since the June 2026 MOI circular, and to women until an agreement is signed (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [D0001, D0002, D0011].
- **Turning a family or tourist visit visa into a work visa inside Kuwait:** not allowed; only a government visit visa can become a work residence (see Changing visa type) [V0055, V0094].


## Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)

Article 18 is the work visa for company jobs in Kuwait's private sector ("Visa No. 18"): cleaning companies, construction, shops, farms, drivers and trades [W0074, M0018, M0022]. Your residence rests on your employer's work permit from PAM (Public Authority for Manpower, which issues work permits), so the employer applies for it, renews it and must agree to most changes [W0002, W0009]. There are two ways in: an agency job order with a BMET permission letter, or a single visa a relative or contact in Kuwait gets from a sponsor (Step 1) [B0169, B0101]. Both pass the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait, which since 1 August 2025 checks the salary on the work permit against its minimum salaries before attesting a visa or demand letter [B0112, M0014].

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform),** the job-seekers' website of BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, Bangladesh's government office for overseas jobs) [B0038]. An agency must choose workers from BMET's database, and BMET gives no clearance to a worker outside it [B0170].

   You'll need [B0037, B0038]:
   - your NID (national ID card);
   - a mobile number;
   - Tk 200 for the government fee.

   1. Open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) [B0038].
   2. Type your name in English, mobile and NID numbers, date of birth, division and district exactly as on the NID, the captcha, tick the terms and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [B0037].
   3. Print 2 copies of the registration slip and give your fingerprints at your DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office); DEMO addresses are on BMET's website [B0038, B0039].

   **If it fails:** call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [B0046].

2. **Learn a trade if you can.** BMET lists every TTC (Technical Training Centre) and IMT (Institute of Marine Technology), with contacts, on its website [B0040]. A skilled trade carries a higher Embassy minimum salary: KD 150 (about Tk 60,500) for a mason, electrician, plumber, painter, welder or mechanic, against KD 90 (about Tk 36,300) for a cleaner or general labourer (KD is the Kuwaiti dinar; KD 1 = about Tk 403) [M0022, M0021, M0020, G0002].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does Kuwait or BMET require a skill test or NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) certificate from Bangladeshi Article 18 workers (drivers, electricians, cooks) before the visa or clearance?

3. **Through a recruiting agency: find an agency with a Kuwait job order, then check its permission letter.** A Kuwait job order reaches Bangladesh as a permission letter from BMET (until December 2024 from the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare) to one licensed agency, naming the employer, each job, the pay, the contract, the conditions and the most the agency may take from you [B0169, B0177, B0179]. The Kuwait letters read for this guide are for cleaners and general labour at KD 75 to 90 a month on 2- or 3-year contracts [B0169, B0177, B0178, B0179].

   Examples:
   - **Rowshon Habib Overseas (RL-2011), 24 December 2024:** 300 cleaners for Al Faisalya Company, Sabhan; KD 75 a month; 3-year contract; employer pays housing, transport, medical care and insurance; 8 hours a day, 6 days a week; the agency may take at most Tk 44,370 [B0169].
   - **Times Enterprise (RL-0385), 21 July 2025:** 350 men as general labour for JM General Contracting Co.; KD 80 a month; 2-year contract; 100-day probation; employer pays the visa fee, housing, basic furniture, transport, medical care and air ticket; at most Tk 1,05,000 [B0178].
   - **Delight Overseas (RL-2287), 7 September 2025:** 266 men as office cleaners for Prosh International, Farwaniya; KD 90 a month; 3-year contract; 8 hours a day, 1 day off a week; employer pays housing, transport, medical care and air ticket; Embassy attestation no. 174 of 2 September 2025; at most Tk 81,730 [B0179].

   Every Kuwait letter carries the same conditions [B0170, B0171, B0172, B0173, B0174, B0175]:
   - the employer bears all Kuwait-side costs (levy, airfare);
   - the agency may take its fee only after your visa and flight are confirmed, and only by cheque, bank draft or pay order;
   - you sign the contract before clearance and get a Bangla translation attested by BMET;
   - an agency representative flies with your group or meets you at Kuwait airport;

### Step 2: The job offer and contract

1. **Check the visa type is "18".** Private-sector jobs need an Article 18 work visa ("Visa No. 18") [W0074]. A commercial visit visa ("Visa No. 14") is short-term, does not guarantee a transfer to Visa 18 and is not covered by the Labour Law (see Changing visa type) [W0074, W0083]. Nurses, home nurses and caretakers must come on Article 18, never on Article 20 (domestic worker), and an Article 20 visa cannot be changed to Article 18 after arrival [W0077, W0078].
2. **Check the job title matches what you will do and what you can prove.** PAM checks a worker's qualification (level, field, and whether Kuwait's Ministry of Higher Education recognises it) when it issues, renews or amends a work permit, and checks that the field of study matches the profession on the application (see Work visa: professional and skilled) [W0050, W0051]. An employer who makes a worker do work other than what the work permit says faces up to 3 years in jail and fines of KD 2,000 to 10,000 per worker [W0035].
3. **Check the pay and contract length** against the permission letter (Step 1, item 3) and the Embassy's minimum salary (Step 1, item 5) [B0169, M0014, B0193].
4. **Get the contract at least 2 days before you go** and check the job, employer, workplace, length, pay, leave and airfare [B0043]. You must sign it before BMET clearance and be given a Bangla translation attested by BMET [B0172]. Keep a copy: the Embassy asks workers to know the pay and type of work before signing [B0186].
5. **Never sign a second contract or a blank paper in Kuwait** (your rights under the contract are in Your rights at work) [W0076].

### Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and visa

This happens in Kuwait, between the employer, PAM and MOI (the Ministry of Interior, which issues visas and residence) [W0002, W0009].

1. **The employer gets the work permit from PAM.** The private-sector work entry visa is issued only on a PAM work permit [W0002]. Since 1 June 2024 an employer may recruit from abroad as many workers as its licence is estimated for, instead of the old limit of 25% from abroad, and a first-time work permit costs an extra KD 150 (about Tk 60,500) [W0034, W0033, W0036, G0002]. If the job needs a degree, the employer applies through Ashal or the Sahel Business app, and an engineering application without the required approval is rejected automatically [W0050, W0052].
2. **The employer gets the entry visa from MOI.** The entry visa for private-sector work costs KD 10 [W0020]. Since 23 December 2025 a worker who arrives on an entry visa to take up residence also pays a KD 5 monthly health insurance fee [W0029, W0019].
3. **The visa must be used in time.** In April 2026, during the Iran conflict that closed Kuwait's airspace from 28 February 2026, Kuwait's Foreign Ministry said in a circular that work, domestic and family entry visas held by people outside Kuwait who could not enter would not be extended, and that issuing of new entry visas was suspended [S0001, S0029, S0012, S0013]. The employer must apply for a new entry visa once issuing resumes [S0013, B0203]. BMET clearances for Kuwait fell to 1,376 in April and in May 2026 and rose to 3,914 in October 2026 [B0024, B0019, B0020].
4. **Drivers and mandoubs (company representatives) who lived in Kuwait before** cannot get a new work visa in either job until two years after they left [W0014].

### Step 7: After arrival

You have two months from entry to finish the residence procedures below; otherwise you are in Kuwait illegally [W0003].

1. **Keep your passport valid.** For residence your passport must be valid for at least six months; the residence period no longer depends on the passport's expiry [W0004].
2. **Take the arrival medical at an MOH centre.** A first residence needs an MOH health certificate showing you have no communicable disease [W0011, K0008]. Everyone except domestic workers needs an appointment booked in advance [K0006].
   1. Go on the appointment: MOH's Expatriate Labor Screening Unit runs five main centres, Shuwaikh (the main one), Fahaheel, Rumaithiya, Jahra and Sabhan, open Sunday to Thursday, 7:30 am to 6 pm; Ali Sabah Al-Salem (Umm Al-Hayman) has also been listed [K0003, K0007].
   2. You are tested for HIV, hepatitis B and C and tuberculosis, and for malaria and filariasis depending on your country, and may be given vaccinations, including meningitis [K0009, K0001].
   3. The result goes straight into MOI's system for your residence; ask the company for the reference number and check MOI's online "medical result readiness" enquiry [K0002, W0063].

   **If it fails:** a suspected infection is re-tested to confirm it; cases that pose a significant public-health threat are deported, and treatable cases are referred to specialised centres [K0004]. MOH has said that a worker found with a listed disease is deported and put on the banned list [K0013]. Since May 2025 an "indeterminate" HIV or hepatitis C result makes a new arrival medically unfit [K0011, K0012].

   > **Need to know**

3. **Give your fingerprints.** A first residence needs fingerprints taken by the General Department of Criminal Evidence [W0011]. Expatriates give them at any Criminal Evidence centre in their governorate, 8:00 to 14:30, not at the airport; centres for company workers include Ali Sabah Al-Salem (Umm Al-Hayman) and the old Jahra Traffic building [W0066, W0067]. 4. **The employer gets your residence (iqama).** Article 18 residence is granted on your PAM work permit [W0009]. It costs KD 20 a year and needs government health insurance at KD 100 a year (KD 10 for farm, fishing, shepherd and dairy workers), and the sponsor must pay the health insurance [W0021, W0025, W0027, W0030].

   > **Need to know**

5. **Get your Civil ID.** The Civil ID, issued by PACI (Public Authority for Civil Information), is your residence permit: since 10 March 2019 no residence sticker goes in the passport, and the card carries your passport number and profession and is needed to leave and re-enter Kuwait [W0013, K0016, K0017]. 6. **Open a bank account and get a SIM.**

**If you had to leave Kuwait before the residence was done:** a worker who entered on a work entry visa, had started the residence formalities, had to leave and stayed out no more than one month can have his visa converted to residence [W0008].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

- **The "free visa" (ফ্রি ভিসা, আজাদ ভিসা):** no such visa exists in Kuwait law; a ring in Bangladesh uses the word to sell visas for lakhs of taka, and workers find on arrival a visa tied to a sponsor with no guaranteed job [N0089, N0091]. Bangladesh's ambassador met community groups in May 2025 to warn against it [N0092].
- **The bought single visa:** an activist told bdnews24 (July 2025) that agents sell a single visa for up to KD 2,000 (about Tk 8 lakh), and workers who went in May and June 2025 paid Tk 6.5 to 7 lakh [B0200, B0199]. The Embassy says buying or selling a visa is a crime for both giver and taker [B0184].
- **The fake or unpaid visa:** the company says on arrival that the visa is fake or not fully paid, and demands money from the broker or sends the worker home (how to check is in Step 5: Medical check and checking your visa is real) [K0050, N0008].
- **The short contract:** an employer blacklisted by the Embassy was accused of bringing workers on 3- to 6-month contracts after taking Tk 3 to 4 lakh from each (Step 1, item 3) [B0195].
- **The processing overcharge:** the Kuwait-Embassy-authorised processing agencies in Dhaka were accused in early 2025 of taking USD 150-300 (Tk 17,000-35,000) per visa against an official fee of Tk 6,270 [N0001].
- **The visit visa sold as a job:** brokers send people on tourist or visit visas with false job promises [B0065]. A visit visa holder may not work and must leave when the visit ends (see Visit visas (tourist, family, business)) [M0009].
- **Wafid extras:** booking fees and extra charges for vaccinations and tests (Step 5, item 1) [W0073].
- **Women's domestic jobs:** rings are sending women domestic workers illegally, though the Embassy is not attesting these visas (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [N0101, N0031].

#### Tried by workers

- **Working outside your sponsor:** working for anyone but your own employer can get you deported even with a valid residence (see What can get you jailed or deported) [W0017, W0075, N0090].
- **Coming on a visit visa to switch to a job:** a commercial visit visa does not guarantee a transfer to Visa 18 and is not covered by the Labour Law [W0074, W0083].
- **Buying a residence or trading permits:** in November 2026 Kuwait's Criminal Court jailed a Bangladeshi sanitation worker for four years with hard labour for trading residence permits through a company, forgery and bribery; the company was fined KD 10,000 per worker and lost its licence [N0056, N0057].

#### If an agency or broker takes your money

1. **Call the Probashi call centre,** 16135 (toll free, run by the Wage Earners' Welfare Board) [B0047].
2. **Complain in writing, with evidence, to BMET's Director General:** it is free and decided within 30 working days; Director (Employment) 01711111544, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [B0096]. The Ministry's monitoring wing asks for clear details of who took the money, when and how much [B0201].
3. **From Kuwait,** give a written complaint to the Embassy's Labour Wing, which forwards it to the authorities in Bangladesh [B0135].
4. **Get free legal advice** on 16699 [B0067].

Taking money on a false job promise or holding a migrant's passport is a crime punishable by up to 5 years' jail and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [B0070].

Sample complaint (in Bangla or English, with copies of receipts): "To the Director General, BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka. I, [name], NID [number], OEP registration [number], paid Tk [amount] to [agency name, RL number / broker name, phone] on [date] at [place] for a Kuwait job as [job] with [employer]. [What happened.] I ask BMET to recover my money and act against the agency. Attached: [receipts, visa copy, messages]." [B0096, B0201]


### Your rights at work

#### Which law covers you

The Private Sector Labour Law (Law 6 of 2010) covers private-sector workers, who hold an Article 18 work residence [L0002, W0009]. Domestic workers on Article 20 are not covered; they have their own law (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [L0001]. The law sets only the minimum: better terms in your contract or the company's rules apply over it [L0003]. A worker on a visit visa has no rights under the Labour Law, even if a company brought him to work [W0083]. PAM (the Public Authority for Manpower) issues work permits and takes labour complaints at the labour unit that holds your employer's file [W0050, L0216].

> **Need to know**
> - Which articles of Law 6/2010 have changed since 2010 (amendments of 2013, 2017 and 2018), and is there an official consolidated English text on PAM's site?

#### Contract

- **Written, three copies:** the contract states the date signed, the start date, the wage, the length (if fixed) and the kind of work, with one copy each for you, the employer and PAM [L0004].
- **No paper is not the end:** without a written contract the job still counts as a contract, and you can prove your rights with any evidence, such as bank statements and messages [L0113, L0235].
- **Arabic wins:** the contract is in Arabic; a translation may be attached, but the Arabic text counts if they differ, so ask for it in Arabic and English [L0007, L0104].
- **Length:** a fixed-term contract runs one to five years and can be extended only if both sides agree; the employer may not extend it without your consent [L0008, L0091].
- **Probation:** at most 100 working days, once per employer; either side may end the contract without notice, but if the employer ends it, it still owes end-of-service pay for the time worked [L0009, L0010].
- **Same job, same pay:** the wage can't be cut once signed, and any agreement to pay less is void [L0005]. The employer may not give you work that doesn't match the contract; if it does, complain to PAM with a copy of your work permit [L0006, L0205].
- **Never sign a blank paper or a second contract in Kuwait** [L0111]. A common trick is a new, lower contract on arrival: one worker promised KD 200 a month was paid KD 90 after the company changed the contracts on arrival [L0224].

#### Pay and deductions

- **Minimum wage:** KD 75 a month in the private sector (PAM Decision 14 of 2017), for contracts and work permits made or renewed since then [L0424, L0425]. Since 1 August 2025 the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait also refuses to attest a new visa paying less than its own minimums for an 8-hour day: KD 90 for unskilled cleaners and general labourers, KD 120 for a driver on a government contract, and KD 150 for most skilled trades on a private contract (see What the job pays) [M0014, M0018, M0019, M0020, M0021, M0022].
- **When:** at least once a month, and no more than seven days late [L0036, L0035]. PAM's Director General said in October 2026 that salaries must be paid before the 7th of the following month [L0229].
- **How:** into your own account at a bank in Kuwait; cash is allowed only in a new worker's first two months [L0037, L0351]. An employer that doesn't pay through the bank can be fined up to the total it owes and must still pay [L0075]. Since February 2026 PAM's monitoring system detects a short or missing salary deposit, suspends the company's file and sends you the reason and amount through the Sahel app (the government's app for residence and labour services) [L0355, L0352].
- **Which salary counts:** regular allowances count as wage, and your dues are worked out on your last salary [L0034, L0042].
- **Deductions:** at most 10% of the wage to repay a loan from the employer, with no interest, and all debt deductions together at most 25% [L0038, L0039]. Penalty deductions may not exceed five days' wage a month, and you may be punished only after being told in writing and heard [L0012, L0122].
- **Never charged:** safety equipment [L0063]. You can't be forced to buy food or goods from the employer or a particular shop [L0040].
- **If the company shuts for reasons that aren't your fault,** it must keep paying you [L0041].

#### Hours, rest and leave

**Hours and overtime**
At most 8 hours a day and 48 a week; in Ramadan at most 36 hours a week [L0043]. After at most five hours in a row you get a break of at least one hour, which doesn't count as working time [L0044]. The employer must post the daily hours, breaks, weekly day off and holidays at the workplace [L0064]. Overtime needs a written order and is capped at 2 hours a day, 3 days a week, 90 days and 180 hours a year [L0047, L0045]. It is paid at least 25% above your normal rate; the employer must keep an overtime record, and you may prove overtime by any means [L0046, L0048]. Unpaid overtime and food allowance are among the commonest complaints workers report [L0110]. Women may not be made to work at night between 10 pm and 7 am, except in health institutions, and must get the same pay as men for the same job [L0083, L0084].

**Rest day and holidays**
One fully paid day off (24 hours) after every six working days; work on it earns at least 50% extra plus another day off [L0050, L0049]. Paid public holidays are Hijri New Year, Isra and Miraj, Arafat Day, the Prophet's Birthday, National Day (25 February), Liberation Day (26 February) and Gregorian New Year, one day each, plus three days each for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha [L0162, L0302]. Work on a holiday is paid double plus a day off in lieu [L0051].

**Annual leave**
At least 30 working days a year on full pay, earned in the first year after six months; weekly days off, public holidays and sick days inside it don't count as leave [L0408, L0409]. Leave pay is paid before you go [L0056]. The employer sets the dates, and leave can be carried over up to two years' worth [L0057, L0058]. Untaken leave is paid in cash when the contract ends [L0059]. Working for someone else during leave lets the employer take the leave pay back [L0060]. Going home on leave needs an exit permit (see Going home on leave) [L0305].

**Sick and other leave**
Sick leave each year is 15 days on full pay, 10 at three-quarters, 10 at half, 10 at a quarter and 30 unpaid, with a certificate from the employer's doctor or a government health centre [L0053, L0054]. After two years with the same employer, a worker who hasn't done Hajj gets 21 days' paid Hajj leave [L0061]. You get three days' paid leave when a close relative dies [L0062]. A woman gets 70 days' paid maternity leave and can't be dismissed during it [L0174, L0303]. The employer may not dismiss you while you are on leave [L0019].

#### Heat

From 1 June to 31 August every year, work in open areas is banned from 11 am to 4 pm under Ministerial Resolution 3 of 2026 [L0403, L0404]. PAM can extend the ban to dust storms, heavy rain or extreme heat and humidity, and urgent work in those hours needs prior approval from its Labour Inspection Department [L0347]. The employer must give cold drinking water, shaded rest areas, first aid and rest breaks [L0346]. Report a site working in the banned hours to PAM's inspection team on 24936192; the employer's file can be suspended until it complies [L0348, L0349].

#### Housing and health care

**Housing**
An employer working on a government contract or in a remote area must give free suitable housing and transport, or a housing allowance [L0011]. Make sure the contract charges nothing for housing or transport to work and says whether food is given or paid as an allowance [L0103].

**Health care**
No residence is issued or renewed without health insurance, and the sponsor must pay it (the fees are in How long you can stay) [W0030, W0031]. Dhaman (the Health Assurance Hospitals Company, a public-private partnership) runs hospitals and clinics meant for insured non-Kuwaitis [L0345]. A work injury is treated at the employer's cost (see If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job) [L0068].

#### Your passport

Keeping a worker's passport is illegal but still widespread in Kuwait [L0320]. If your company won't give it back, complain to the PAM office where your residence was stamped (see If you aren't paid, item 2) [L0094]. You must show your passport when the authorities ask and report a lost or damaged one to the Ministry of Interior (MOI) within two weeks [L0383, L0423].

#### End-of-service pay

**How much**
The indemnity (end-of-service pay) for a monthly-paid worker is 15 days' wage for each of the first five years and one month's wage for each year after, up to one and a half years' wage [L0026]. Daily, weekly, hourly and piece-rate workers get 10 days a year for the first five years and 15 days a year after, up to one year's wage [L0027]. Part years count pro rata, and any loan you owe the employer is deducted [L0028].

> **Need to know**
> - Is a Bangladeshi worker in Kuwait covered by any pension (PIFSS covers only Kuwaitis), so that the indemnity is his only retirement money, and can he join Bangladesh's universal pension scheme for migrants (Probash) from Kuwait?

**Full, part or none**
- **Full:** when the employer ends the contract, when a fixed-term contract ends without renewal, and when the contract ends through the employer's fault, your death or incapacity, or the firm's bankruptcy or closure [L0029, L0030, L0405].
- **Part, if you resign from an open-ended contract:** half after 3 to 5 years, two-thirds after 5 to 10 years, all of it after 10 years [L0031].
- **None:** if you are dismissed for causing gross loss, getting the job by fraud or disclosing company secrets [L0197]. Other dismissals for misconduct keep the indemnity, and an arbitrary dismissal can be challenged at the Labour Court for indemnity plus compensation [L0013, L0014].

### How long you can stay

You can stay as long as your employer keeps your work permit and residence valid; Article 18 residence rests on the PAM work permit and ends when the job ends, even before its expiry date [W0009, W0005].

- **Length of each residence:** up to five years, and never longer than your health insurance [W0006, W0026, W0007].
- **Renewal fees:** KD 20 a year for the residence (doubled on 23 December 2025) and KD 100 a year for health insurance (up from KD 50) [W0021, W0025, W0027, W0028]. Renewing a work permit for the same job needs no new degree papers (see Work visa: professional and skilled) [W0053].
- **Over 60 without a degree:** from 2020-21 Kuwait refused renewal to workers over 60 without a university degree, and many had to leave [W0040]. The later extra fees, which could total up to KD 900, were cancelled in November 2024, and these workers now renew and transfer on the same fees as everyone else [W0037, W0038, W0039].
- **Six months abroad:** your residence lapses if you stay outside Kuwait more than six months in a row, unless MOI gives permission before the six months end, at KD 5 a month [W0042, L0421, W0016, W0023].
- **If you were stuck abroad by the 2026 war:** the automatic extra absence permits given from 28 February 2026 ended on 1 September 2026, except for people who left Kuwait on or before 31 August 2026 [S0014, S0026, S0027]. The residence status certificate in Sahel shows the date by which you must be back [S0028]. The airspace closures came from the Iran conflict of February to July 2026 [S0029].
- **When the job ends:** if your residence ends or is not renewed, Residency Affairs may give you a departure notice of up to three months, renewable once, up to six months from the end of your service, at KD 10 a month [W0012, W0022]. How to leave properly is in Leaving at the end of the job [W0012].

### Your residence permit

Your residence (iqama) is recorded on your Civil ID card, issued by PACI (the Public Authority for Civil Information) [W0013]. It is granted under your work permit, and you can't move it to another employer without PAM's permission (see Changing employer or leaving) [W0009].

1. **Check your Civil ID shows your residence and its expiry date** [W0013].
2. **Check your fingerprints are enrolled** in Sahel (see Step 7: After arrival, item 3) [W0065].
3. **If your residence expires or is cancelled,** call Residency Affairs on 25582960, 25582961, 97288200 or 97288211, or go to your governorate's Residency Affairs office, called Jawazat (see Step 7: After arrival, item 7) [W0061, W0082].
4. **If you are trafficked or held against your will,** call MOI's Public Moral Protection and Anti-Human Trafficking department on 25589648, 25589655 or 25589696 [W0062].

### What can get you jailed or deported

The Interior Minister can deport a foreigner even with a valid residence, for example one with no legitimate income or one working for someone else, and the order can extend to dependants [L0369, L0417, L0377]. The 2025 executive regulations add deportation after a felony sentence or a crime of honour or trust, or after four criminal judgments of any kind within five years [L0419]. A person ordered deported can be held up to 30 days, renewable [L0370]. Once deported, you can't come back unless the Interior Minister personally approves [L0099, L0382]. The employer pays the deportation costs, and anyone who employed or housed you illegally pays them in full [L0418].

#### Working for someone else and visa trading

You may not work for anyone except your own employer without permission [L0411, L0106]. Under the residence law (Decree-Law 114 of 2024, in force since 5 January 2025), this carries up to two years in prison and/or a KD 5,000 to 10,000 fine, which can be settled by paying KD 3,000 [L0372, L0413, L0414]. The same law bars the employer from using you outside the job you were recruited for, hiring you out to others, or withholding your dues [L0412]. Selling or buying a visa or residence for money, for a real or fake job, is residency trading: 3 to 5 years and/or KD 5,000 to 10,000 per worker for the seller, and up to one year and/or up to KD 1,000 for the worker who paid [L0373, L0416, L0380]. A worker who paid is exempt if he reports the crime to the authorities, even after getting the permit [L0415]. Sheltering, employing or housing someone without valid residence is also a crime [L0376].

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi worker who reported his visa seller been spared punishment under the informant exemption [L0415]?

#### Absence and late papers

Being reported absent can lead to cancellation of your residence, deportation and a ban on returning (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0293, L0099]. Late residency is fined KD 2 a day for the first month and KD 4 a day after, up to KD 1,200, and an overstayed visa KD 10 a day [L0389, L0388]. The Interior Minister may waive these fines for a person who is deported and leaves [L0387].

#### Strikes and protests

Strikes and protests are illegal; workers who strike can be arrested, jailed and deported [L0105]. In January 2025, 80 Bangladeshi cleaners who stopped work over overtime, residence papers, Civil IDs and late pay were told their tickets home were ready and said they were pressed to sign resignations [L0400, L0401].

#### Drugs and alcohol

Alcohol is banned and drinking it is a serious crime [L0108]. Under the anti-drug law (Decree-Law 159 of 2025, in force since 15 December 2025), smuggling, producing or growing drugs for trade carries death or life imprisonment and fines of KD 100,000 to 2 million [L0298, L0398]. Never carry an unchecked parcel for someone else, and carry medicines only with a doctor's prescription [L0109, L0107]. Planting drugs on someone to accuse them falsely is also a crime [L0301]. An addict who asks for treatment before being charged is immune from prosecution [L0399].

> - What penalties apply to a foreign worker for drinking, making or selling alcohol, and is deportation automatic?

#### Religion and social media

Don't post or say anything against Islam or Kuwait's customs, including on social media [L0102].

#### Traffic

Most low-wage workers can't get a private driving licence: it needs two years' residence, a KD 600 salary and a university degree, except for 12 exempt categories such as professional drivers [L0396]. Under the traffic law in force since April 2025, driving without a valid licence costs KD 75, or up to 3 months in jail and KD 150 to 300 in court [L0390, L0391]. Running a red light is a felony with up to 3 years and up to KD 1,000, and using a phone while driving costs KD 75 [L0390]. Police arrest drivers under drink or drugs, drivers who injure or kill someone, and drivers who flee an accident [L0392]. Carrying passengers without a permit is a serious offence [L0394, L0289]. A foreigner can be deported for a single serious traffic offence after an investigation; 15 were deported this way in May to July 2025 [L0395, L0290]. In October 2026 the Interior Minister said 300 delivery workers had been deported for violations in a few days [L0354].

#### Debts and travel bans

Private debts lead to court travel bans that stop you leaving Kuwait: the Ministry of Justice lists unpaid bank loans, overdue instalments, unpaid mobile phone bills, bounced cheques, rent disputes, electricity bills and unpaid alimony as the commonest causes [G0051]. In June 2026 alone it recorded 4,357 travel ban requests against debtors and requests to arrest and hold 2,645 debtors [G0053]. Since 2025, debtors who can pay but don't can be jailed again, and enforcement can take 25% to 50% of a debtor's salary [G0057, G0058]. Since June 2024 the Public Prosecution also bans from travel expatriates sentenced to fines in financial cases, including in absentia and while an appeal is pending [G0060].

1. **Check for a ban** in Sahel (Ministry of Justice service), which shows civil court travel bans and the amounts owed [G0055].
2. **Pay or settle the debt;** the ban is lifted after payment or settlement [G0054].
3. **If you must travel before it is settled,** ask the Ministry of Justice's enforcement department through Sahel for a single-trip permission and follow its status there [G0056].

### If you aren't paid

PAM handles claims for unpaid wages, end-of-service pay, leave pay, overtime, unfair dismissal, work injury, and a release or cancellation of residence [L0357, L0362]. You must go to PAM first; only PAM sends the case to court [L0188]. A worker's court case is free of court fees [L0077]. Claims can't be heard more than one year after the contract ends [L0076]. Your wage may not be paid more than seven days after the due date [L0035]. If the employer doesn't pay, you may quit without notice and keep your full end-of-service pay [L0022, L0023]. Staying away for 7 days in a row without an acceptable reason lets the employer treat you as resigned and report you absent (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0015, L0323].

1. **Keep your proof.** Keep your contract, bank statements showing what was and wasn't paid, and messages with the company about pay [L0360].
2. **File a labour complaint at PAM.** Go in person to the PAM labour relations department that holds your employer's file (usually the governorate office where your residence was stamped) and ask to file a labour complaint (shakwa amaliya, শাকওয়া আমালিয়া) [L0363, L0092, L0357]. The PAM office in Hawally is in Jabriya, Block 6, Street 104, Building 7 [L0356].

   You'll need: a copy of your work permit, the contract and your proof from item 1 [L0093, L0360].

   1. Say your agreed salary, what you received and for how many months [L0357].
   2. You get a complaint number by SMS [L0364].
   3. PAM sets a hearing and tells both sides the date, time and the investigator's name [L0365].

   **What to say:** "I want to file a labour complaint (shakwa amaliya) against [company name], file number [number]. My contract salary is KD [amount]. I have not been paid for [months] / I am paid only KD [amount]. I also claim overtime and leave pay." [L0357, L0362]

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there now an online or Sahel route for an Article 18 worker's labour complaint, and what is it called on screen (the April 2026 Sahel service is for Kuwaitis' employment-support grievances) [L0355]?
   > - What are the addresses and phone numbers of PAM's labour relations departments in Capital, Farwaniya, Ahmadi, Jahra and Mubarak Al-Kabeer?

3. **Go to the hearing.** The investigator tries to settle the dispute; if there is no settlement, PAM must send the case to court within one month [L0365, L0080]. Some employers don't answer PAM's calls, to drag the case out [L0258].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does PAM take in practice from complaint to hearing, and to referral to court?

4. **Follow the case in the Labour Court.** The court sets a hearing within 3 days of receiving the case, and you follow it yourself or with a lawyer [L0081, L0220, L0407]. Workers' dues rank ahead of the employer's other debts, except its private home [L0079]. In May 2026 the Labour Court ordered a company that had not paid a worker for six months to pay his wages, full end-of-service pay, leave and overtime, let him transfer without its consent, and threw out its absconding report and its claim for recruitment costs [L0359].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does a Labour Court case take in practice, and how does a worker enforce a judgment against a company that still won't pay?

5. **Ask to transfer.** PAM can let you move to another employer before one year if wages aren't paid through the official procedure or the employer breaks the law (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0335, L0330].
6. **If you must leave Kuwait first,** authorise a lawyer or a friend in Kuwait to file the complaint at PAM for you, within one year of the contract ending [L0366, L0088, L0358].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which Bangladesh Embassy lawyer panel or NGO files PAM cases for workers who have gone home, and what does it cost?

7. **Get help.** Report the problem to the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing too, which gives any Bangladeshi worker advice and help (see Contacts) [D0044, B0134]. The Kuwait Society for Human Rights gives legal aid to expatriates [L0226].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing go with workers to PAM hearings or pay for a lawyer?

**If you are beaten or abused:** complain to the police and insist on a medical examination that records the injury [L0097]. Assault by the employer lets you quit without notice and keep full indemnity [L0131].

PAM's enforcement has tightened: in October 2026 it said it had suspended the files of 10,000 companies that had not paid workers, some for a year or more [L0350].

### If your employer reports you absent

If you stay away from work without an acceptable reason for 7 days in a row or 20 days in a year, the employer may treat you as having resigned [L0015]. It can then file an absence report (ghiyab, which workers call "absconding" or a "case"), which must reach MOI within 15 days of your first day absent [L0323]. Since March 2026 the employer files it online on PAM's Ashal portal (PAM's online system for employers), and you get an alert in the Ashal app [L0321]. The employer must take the printed report to MOI's Residency Affairs Directorate within 3 working days [L0322]. Some employers file false reports after a worker asks for his pay, and visa traders file them to free the visa for resale [L0361, L0324]. The case often appears in the system 15 to 20 days after filing [L0098].

1. **Watch for the alert** in the Ashal app [L0321].
2. **Object within 60 days.** Go to the PAM labour relations department that holds your employer's file and object that the absence was justified or wrongly reported [L0321, L0325]. Bring proof: medical certificates, travel documents, a resignation you sent, messages with the employer, and proof you were unpaid (see If you aren't paid, item 1) [L0272, L0360].
3. **Ask the employer to withdraw it.** The employer can cancel the report on PAM's portal [L0327]. A joint PAM and MOI panel may drop a report the employer withdraws or that is malicious, fraudulent or filed in error [L0326].
4. **File your own complaint for unpaid wages at the same time** (see If you aren't paid, item 2), because the court can throw out a false absconding report filed after you asked for your pay [L0234].
5. **Ask to transfer.** A false or malicious report is a ground to move to another employer before one year (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0329].
6. **If it can't be cleared,** a worker with an absence case who has his passport can surrender to the deportation authorities with a ticket; once deported he can't return (see Going home without valid papers) [L0098, L0099].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### Changing employer

The basic rule: you can transfer only after one year with your employer and with its consent [L0333]. On a fixed-term contract still running, you can't transfer or get a release without the employer's approval [L0095]. On an open-ended contract after 3 years with the same sponsor, you can move without its approval [L0096].

Since May 2026 (Administrative Decision 680 of 2026), PAM can allow a transfer before one year without consent if it finds that [L0328, L0334]:

- the employer failed to issue or complete your work permit or residence through its own negligence, or its PAM file is suspended [L0332, L0329];
- it filed a false or malicious report against you [L0329];
- it broke the Labour Law so that you may quit without notice: unpaid wages, abuse, unsafe work, fraud in the contract terms, false criminal charges [L0330];
- wages weren't paid through the official procedure, or you won a final court ruling against it [L0335];
- the firm went bankrupt or closed for good [L0331].

Apply through a complaint at PAM's labour relations department (see If you aren't paid, item 2); PAM investigates first, and can then stop the employer's new permits and recruitment [L0334, L0263]. PAM's director general said in October 2026 that transfers without sufficient reason won't be allowed [L0353].

#### Resigning or being dismissed

On an open-ended contract either side gives at least three months' written notice for a monthly-paid worker; if you skip it, you owe the wage for the notice period [L0017, L0125]. During the employer's notice you may take one paid day or 8 hours a week to look for a new job [L0018]. Whoever ends a fixed-term contract without the right to do so owes the other side compensation of up to the wage for the time left [L0021]. Many contracts make a worker who resigns early repay recruitment, visa, travel and medical costs; PAM can be asked to waive this [L0090, L0089, L0319]. You can't be dismissed for claiming your rights, or for your sex, origin or religion [L0020]. A dismissed worker can challenge the dismissal at the Labour Court and, if it was arbitrary, gets end-of-service pay plus compensation [L0014].

#### Going home on leave

1. **Get an exit permit.** Since 1 July 2025 every Article 18 worker needs his employer's exit permit to leave Kuwait, for a holiday or for good; domestic workers don't [L0305, L0316].

   You'll need: a smartphone with Sahel (in English or Arabic only), your Civil ID, and your departure and return dates [L0309, L0318].

   1. Open Sahel, go to Services, choose Public Authority of Manpower, then Expatriate Workers Services, then Exit Permit [L0311, L0309].
   2. Enter your departure and return dates and submit, no earlier than 7 days and no later than 24 hours before you fly [L0309, L0310].
   3. Your employer approves it on Sahel Business or Ashal [L0306].
   4. Once approved, the permit is valid for 7 days; print it or show it in Sahel at the airport [L0313, L0314].

   **If you have no smartphone or it's an emergency:** the employer can apply for you on Sahel Business or Ashal; how fast you get it depends on how fast the employer approves [L0312, L0315].

   **If it fails:** if the employer refuses or delays, complain to the PAM labour relations unit that holds the company's file [L0307]. There is no limit on how many permits you can get in a year [L0308]. Sponsors of "free visa" workers have been reported charging about KD 50 for an exit permit [L0317].

   > **Need to know**

   > - Who pays the ticket for annual leave? Law 6/2010 is silent; is it in the standard contract?

2. **Take your leave pay before you go** (see Hours, rest and leave) [L0056].
3. **Come back on time.** Seven days in a row away without acceptable reason lets the employer treat you as resigned and report you absent [L0015, L0323]. Your residence lapses after six months abroad (see How long you can stay) [L0421].
4. **Don't work for anyone else while on leave** [L0060].

#### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Collect what you're owed.** Your end-of-service pay, untaken leave in cash and your last wages (see End-of-service pay) [L0026, L0059]. Check the contract for who pays the ticket home [L0406].
2. **Get your service certificate and documents back.** The employer must give you a certificate with your period of service, job and last salary, and return your documents [L0033, L0032].
3. **Pay every government bill in your name.** Expatriates are stopped at the border until they pay traffic fines, electricity and water bills, Ministry of Communications (phone) bills and Ministry of Justice bills; most can be paid on Sahel, the ministries' websites or at the airport [G0046, G0049]. Check for a court travel ban too (see Debts and travel bans) [G0055].
4. **Get the exit permit** (see Going home on leave, item 1) [L0305].
5. **If anything is unpaid, complain before you go,** or within one year through a representative (see If you aren't paid, item 6) [L0076, L0366].

### Bringing your family

A private-sector worker can bring a wife and children only if the monthly salary on his work permit is at least KD 800 (see Family visa (dependants)) [F0002, F0065]. The Bangladesh Embassy's minimum salaries for company visas are KD 90 to 150 (see What the job pays) [M0018, M0020, M0022]. Combining husband's and wife's salaries doesn't count: only the sponsoring parent's salary is looked at [F0084]. For a worker below KD 800, the routes left are a family visit visa (see Visit visas (tourist, family, business)) or a baby born in Kuwait, for whom no minimum salary applies (see Having a child in Kuwait) [F0003, F0141]. The family's residence ends when your own job-based residence ends [F0018].


## Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)

Article 20 is Kuwait's household visa: Bangladeshis call it the "khadem visa", and the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait calls it the "Domestic Aid visa" or Visa-20 [N0034, D0022, D0024]. It covers housemaids, house drivers, cooks and other household staff who work for a private person (the sponsor, or kafeel), not a company [D0021, D0024]. These workers come under the Domestic Workers Law (Law 68 of 2015), not the private-sector Labour Law [D0045, D0050]. The Public Authority for Manpower (PAM), Kuwait's labour authority, handles their contracts and complaints through its Domestic Labour Office (DLO), also called the Domestic Workers Employment Department [D0060, D0084, D0101]. About 106,942 Bangladeshis work in Kuwait's homes, 12.1% of all domestic workers there [N0028].

### Who can go now

New Article 20 visas are closed to Bangladeshis, men and women [N0026, N0027, D0001, D0002].

- **Everyone: Bangladesh is not on the approved list.** In June 2026 Kuwait's Ministry of Interior (MOI) limited new domestic-worker recruitment to 10 countries (South Africa, Benin, Eritrea, Ethiopia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Nepal, and Senegal for men only), and Bangladesh is not one of them [D0001, D0002, N0027].
- **Women: the Embassy will not attest.** Bangladesh and Kuwait have signed no agreement on women domestic workers, so the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait cannot attest any women's domestic-worker ("khaddama") visa, and it asks women not to go as domestic workers until one is signed [D0011, D0012, N0031, N0097]. Bangladeshi women who work legally in Kuwait go on company (Article 18) visas, mostly as cleaners (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)) [D0017].
- **Entry visas that could not be used in the 2026 war were not extended.** In April 2026, during the Iran conflict, Kuwait stopped issuing entry visas, and a domestic-work entry visa that could not be used could not be extended; the sponsor must apply for a new one once issuing resumes [S0029, S0012, S0013].
- **Workers already in Kuwait are not affected.** A domestic worker in Kuwait legally can still renew the job and residence (akama) [N0033].

Household visas have been shut to Bangladeshis before: Kuwait reopened them in May 2016 for men only, one per citizen, and suspended them in March 2018 because of visa trading [D0019, D0020].

### What it costs and who pays

In July 2024 Al-Durra, the government's central recruitment company, charged KD 180 (about Tk 72,500), ticket included, for a driver or cook [D0066, G0002].

### Your rights as a domestic worker

**Pay:**
- at least KD 60 a month under Kuwaiti law, and the Embassy minimum in your contract (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Step 2) [D0052, M0016, M0017]
- paid at the end of each month, with no deductions [D0046, D0057]
- food, clothes, medical treatment and housing paid by the employer [D0047]

**Hours, rest and leave:**
- at most 12 hours a day, with rest [D0049]
- a weekly day off [D0049]
- 30 days' paid annual leave [D0050, D0034]

**Documents, phone and safety:**
- your passport and Civil ID stay with you unless you agree otherwise [D0048]
- you may own and use a mobile phone outside working hours [D0061]
- no dangerous or degrading work [D0058]
- no work outside Kuwait without your consent [D0062]

**End of contract:**
- end-of-service pay of one month's salary per year of service [D0059]
- your ticket home, paid by the employer [D0062, D0038]

In practice many domestic workers work over 12 hours without overtime, have wages withheld, lose rest days and end-of-service pay, and have their contracts switched [D0089]. Employers often keep passports [D0083]. PAM may not inspect private homes [D0085].

### If you aren't paid or are abused

2. **Go to PAM's Domestic Labour Office** in Rumaithiya, Block 7, Street 78, behind the main co-operative society, with your Civil ID, passport number and contact details [D0101]. PAM takes visitors from 10:30 am to 1:30 pm, and its hotline is 103 [D0100]. A release, cancellation or visa-change complaint is filed in person there, and you get a hearing date [D0108].
3. **Or complain on PAM's online form.** It takes complaints against the employer about late salary, dues, documents, leaving the country or transfer, and lets you apply to extend your residence while the complaint is open [D0098, D0099]. It is in Arabic only and asks for the employer's details [D0084].
4. **Tell the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing** in Messila (Block 7, Street 16, Villas 91 and 93; phone +965 23900913), which advises workers and forwards written complaints to Bangladesh [D0044, M0024].
5. **Appeal a PAM refusal** at the Central Administration Office in Abu Fatira within 15 days [D0110].

#### Done to workers

- **"Khaddama" visas for women:** syndicates send Bangladeshi women to Kuwait as domestic workers illegally, and many end up abused or confined [D0013, N0101]. ## Work visa: professional and skilled

Professionals come on the same Article 18 work permit and residence as any company worker (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)) [W0009]. The difference is that the job title on the permit must match a recognised degree [W0051, W0047].

1. **Attest the degree in Bangladesh:** by the university, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and finally the Kuwait Embassy [W0045].

When a work permit is issued, renewed or changed through PAM's online systems for employers (Ashal or Sahel Business), PAM records the degree as unrecognised, preliminary recognition, administrative recognition or accredited [W0050].
4. **PAM checks that your field of study matches the profession** on the work permit [W0051]. Renewing a permit for the same profession needs no new qualification papers [W0053].

### Nurses and doctors

Kuwait is the main overseas employer of Bangladeshi nurses, and the route runs government to government through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government's own recruiting agency) [K0052, K0053, K0060]. In May 2023 the Bangladesh Embassy signed an agreement with Kuwait's Ministry of Health (MOH) on recruiting medical staff, and by the end of 2024, 677 Bangladeshi nurses had been placed in Kuwait's public hospitals since 2022 [K0060, K0059].

- **The first round:** in 2022 BOESL nurses went to MOH hospitals without migration cost, on Tk 80,000 to 90,000 a month with overtime and a yearly increment [K0063, K0064].
- **The 2025 round:** in October 2025 Kuwait asked BOESL for 100 nurses with English, at least 3 years' experience and basic life support certification, offering up to Tk 1.27 lakh for BSc and Tk 1.11 lakh for diploma nurses [K0053, K0054, K0055]. Of 100 who sat the exam, 15 were selected, and by May 2026 only five had finished visa processing, because Kuwait had not given clearance during the Middle East tensions [K0056, K0057, K0058].

1. **Come only on Article 18.** Nurses, home nurses and caretakers may be recruited only on Article 18, never Article 20 [W0077].
2. **Get the MOH licence before you work.** A Kuwait MOH licence is required for any nursing job, including assistant nurse, and working without one can lead to deportation and jail [W0080]. Dataflow, a credential-checking company, verifies your certificates; the employer files the licence application with the Dataflow report on MOH's portal and pays online; then you sit a computer-based Prometric exam [K0066, K0067, K0068]. Nurses need 3 years' hospital inpatient experience, a good-standing certificate dated within 6 months, a criminal record check and vaccination records [K0069, K0070].
3. **Keep your original certificates.** Hospitals and agencies in Kuwait have confiscated nurses' certificates and refused release or transfer [W0079].
4. **If hired inside Kuwait, get an MOH-attested contract** and check that the hospital has a valid MOH licence and an MOH/PAM quota [W0081].

## Family visa (dependants)

A resident whose own salary is at least KD 800 a month can sponsor his wife and children on a family residence, called "Article 22" or "visa 22" (التحاق بعائل, "joining a provider") [F0001, F0002]. The sponsor (the "provider") applies at an MOI residency affairs department in Kuwait, and must send the family home at his own cost when their residence ends [F0001, F0015, F0085]. The Ministry of Interior (MOI) says the family visa is open to every nationality that meets the salary rule, and the residence regulations in force since 23 December 2025 name no nationality [F0083, F0002, F0105]. Since May 2025 Bangladeshis get Kuwaiti visas without the special Interior Ministry approval they needed in 2021 [V0096, N0014, V0098].

### Who can apply

#### Who qualifies

- **Salary:** at least KD 800 a month, counted only from the job the sponsor's residence was granted for (the salary on the work permit) [F0002, F0065].
- **Only one salary counts:** a husband's and wife's salaries cannot be added together; only the sponsoring parent's salary is looked at [F0084].
- **No degree needed:** an earlier university-degree requirement was dropped in July 2024 [F0080].
- **Exempt from the salary rule, if the job in Kuwait matches the person's qualification** [F0004]:
  - engineers, in government or private companies [F0005];
  - Ministry of Health and military pharmacists, nurses, paramedics and medical technicians [F0006];
  - mosque imams, preachers, muezzins and Quran teachers [F0007];
  - people who wash and bury the dead [F0008];
  - government legal researchers; university and college teachers; government school supervisors, teachers, social workers and psychologists [F0009, F0010, F0011];
  - journalists of international news stations; coaches and players of government sports federations and clubs [F0012, F0013].
- **Children who can be exempted:** the Director General of Residency Affairs may waive the salary rule for a child already in Kuwait or born in Kuwait, or born abroad and aged 5 or under to parents both resident in Kuwait; it is his decision case by case, not a right [F0003].
- **Who cannot sponsor:** domestic workers (Article 20) and students (Article 23) cannot sponsor children on a family visa [F0096].

The Bangladesh Embassy's minimum salaries for company workers are mostly KD 90 to 150 a month (KD 250 for a chef), far below KD 800 [M0018, M0020, M0021, M0022, F0002]. ## Visit visas (tourist, family, business)

On 9 November 2026 the Kuwait Visa portal lets a relative in Kuwait apply online for a Bangladeshi's family visit visa, but tells a Bangladeshi with no valid US, UK or Schengen visa or residence permit and no Gulf residence permit that they are not eligible for a tourist visa [M0003, M0001]. A business visit needs a Kuwaiti company to apply [M0010].

Tourist, family, government and business visit visas are issued online on Kuwait Visa, MOI's platform at kuwaitvisa.moi.gov.kw, which is also a mobile app [V0082, V0020]. The steps on the portal are: create an account, complete the profile, submit the application, and track updates [M0013]. Since 23 December 2025 the rules come from Ministerial Decision No. 2249/2025, the executive regulations of the foreigners' residence law, which recognise twelve types of visit visa [V0057, V0061]. A visit visa holder may not work and must leave when the visit ends [M0009]. A visitor caught working is deported together with the sponsor in Kuwait, and both go on the ban list [V0086].

Visits were disrupted in 2026: MOI extended visit visas by one month from 28 February 2026, these automatic extensions ended on 1 September 2026, and commercial visit visas were suspended for all nationalities from 10 to 19 June 2026 [S0008, S0026, S0023, S0024].

### Cost and time

- **Visa fee:** KD 10 for each month of the visa, for family, tourist, business, government, private, medical and multiple-entry visits [V0051, V0057, V0059].
- **Extension:** KD 10 a month [V0053].
- **Health insurance:** compulsory since 23 December 2025, bought from an approved private insurer; no visit visa is issued or renewed without it [V0065, V0112]. The sponsor in Kuwait or the visitor pays [V0066].
- **Refunds:** none; fees are collected only once the application is accepted [V0015, V0016].
- **Time:** a complete application can be issued in about five minutes; the portal says tourist visas usually take 1 to 2 working days [V0070, V0018].
- **Payment:** online, by credit card or another online payment option [V0022].

### Family visit visa

A relative who lives in Kuwait with a valid residence applies; the visitor does not [M0004, V0069]. There is no age limit for parents [V0088]. The visit is single entry, the visa must be used within 30 days of issue, and the stay is up to 90 days from entry [M0005].

In practice officials look hard at sponsors on very low salaries, around KD 250 a month, and at visits by elderly relatives, judged on the sponsor's job and salary [V0085, V0120].

These residences are not tied to an employer; each needs proof of income, an approved investment, or property in Kuwait [F0028, F0034, F0031].

Dependant visa applications from self-sponsored residents are decided only by him or his assistant [F0066]. He may allow the holder to work, on a letter from a government body [F0050].

## Changing visa type

A residence can be changed from one type or purpose to another under the General Administration of Residence Affairs' rules [V0047]. Since 2 August 2026, converting a visit visa into a residence costs KD 150 (Interior Ministry Decision No. 1091 of 2026); domestic workers are exempt [V0089, F0049].

A visit visa can become a residence in five cases only [V0055]:

1. **Government visit visa to work residence:** for university graduates and technical specialists invited by a ministry or public body, with the Director General's approval [V0106].
2. **Domestic workers** [V0107].
3. **Family or tourist visit visa to dependant residence (joining a family sponsor):** for a wife, sons under 18 and daughters under 21, the sponsor must earn at least KD 800 a month and pay the conversion, health insurance and residence fees [V0108, V0093]. A family visit visa can never become an Article 18 work permit [V0094].
4. **A worker who entered on a work entry visa, started the residence process, had to leave and came back within one month** [V0109].
5. **Other cases** the Director General of Residence Affairs accepts [V0110].

Each case is judged individually and needs the Director General's approval [V0125]. In August 2026 hundreds of applications were rejected because applicants did not meet the conditions [V0092]. A dependant on a family visa can be allowed to move to another residence without the sponsor's consent [F0014]. ## Asylum

Kuwait gives no asylum: it is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol, and its law has no asylum or refugee status [F0124, F0129]. A person asking for protection is treated under the ordinary residence law, with no special status [F0125]. Only UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) decides refugee claims, under its own mandate; the government generally cooperates with it [F0126, F0130]. UNHCR counts 386 refugees and 611 asylum seekers in Kuwait, mainly from Iraq, Syria and Yemen [F0128, F0127]. ## Sending money home

**Send through a bank or an exchange company licensed by the Central Bank of Kuwait (CBK), never hundi** [G0009]. Hundi (hawala: money passed outside the banking system through private agents) is a crime in Kuwait under anti-money-laundering Law 106 of 2013, and is illegal in Bangladesh too [G0070, G0074]. Hundi brokers in Bangladesh pay out through bKash and Nagad (mobile-money wallets) [G0072]. Bangladesh's Financial Intelligence Unit (BFIU) had frozen or suspended about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling by June 2026, and decided in August 2026 to freeze about 14,000 more [G0071]. Money sent legally earns the family Bangladesh's 2.5% cash incentive, kept in the FY2026-27 budget, and sometimes a further 2.5% from the bank; hundi gets neither [G0017, G0013].

### Your salary and bank account

Under Article 57 of the Private Sector Labour Law (Law 6/2010), an employer with five or more workers must pay wages into each worker's bank account and send proof to PAM (the Public Authority for Manpower, Kuwait's labour authority) [G0029]. PAM checks salary transfers every month; a company that pays late can have its PAM file suspended, which stops it hiring until it pays or enters the legal reason for any unpaid salary in PAM's Ashal system (its online portal for employers), and the suspension lifts automatically once it pays [G0029, G0030, G0032]. A suspended file does not stop existing workers renewing their permits or transferring to another employer [G0031]. Since 28 September 2026 the CBK's Kuwait Wage Payment System (KWPS) carries private-sector wage transfers through local banks, and PAM uses it to watch employers in real time and catch unpaid wages early [G0027, G0028].

Since January 2025 the CBK has told banks to open accounts for every customer, including low-paid, craft and domestic workers, and not to refuse anyone for a low salary; banks must open these "worker accounts" on request but do not advertise them (Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 7: After arrival) [G0077, G0078].

### Step by step: sending through an exchange company

You'll need:
- to send online, the Kuwait Mobile ID app [G0066];
- for a bKash wallet, the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered [G0011].

1. **Check the company is licensed.** Open cbk.gov.kw, go to Supervision, Regulated Entities, Exchange Companies, and press "Show All": the CBK lists 31 licensed exchange companies with their addresses and phone numbers, and a shop or person not on the list is not licensed [G0009, G0069]. In 2025 the CBK cleared unlicensed "exchange brokers" out of the market, and on 25 October 2026 it announced penalties on several licensed exchange companies [G0069, G0040].
2. **Choose the company.** bKash's partners in Kuwait are Al Mulla, Al Muzaini Exchange, Aman Exchange, ARY Exchange, Joyalukkas, Kuwait Bahrain International Exchange, Trans-Fast, Western Union and MoneyGram [G0010]. In the World Bank's July-August 2025 survey, sending about KD 65 (about Tk 26,200) cost a fee of KD 0.90 at Lulu Money and KD 1.25 (about Tk 500) at Al Mulla, Aman, Al Muzaini, Bahrain Exchange Company, Western Union and MoneyGram, plus the gap between the market rate and the company's rate; most transfers arrived in 2 days, Western Union's in under an hour [G0064, G0062, G0002]. The cheapest was Al Mulla online to a bank account, 5.96% in all [G0065].
3. **Send larger amounts less often.** Sending up to USD 200 from Kuwait cost 3.91% on average in April-June 2026, against 1.51% across all amounts [G0006, G0007].
4. **Send.** At the branch, give the bKash number and registered name (or the bank details) and hand over the cash; bKash accepts up to Tk 2.5 lakh (about KD 620) per transfer, any number of times [G0011, G0012, G0002]. Most customers now send through the licensed companies' own apps, which since April 2026 check identity through Kuwait Mobile ID [G0068, G0066]. Branches are most crowded at the start of each month, when salaries are paid [G0068].
5. **The family collects.** bKash remittance can be cashed out for Tk 7 per thousand at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs, and the 2.5% government incentive is added on receipt [G0012, G0013].

1. The exchange company or bank: ask for its complaint form at the branch; a bank must answer in writing within 5 working days, other CBK-regulated companies within 15 [G0038].
2. The CBK: appeal with the company's reply and your papers on the Consumers Protection Portal, services.cbk.gov.kw/apps/r/ebsprod/cbkcpp/home; enquiries 1864444 [G0038, G0039].
3. bKash, for a wallet transfer: 16247 in Bangladesh, +88-02-55663001 from abroad, support@bkash.com [G0014].

### Saving at home: the Wage Earner Development Bond

The Wage Earner Development Bond is a 5-year government bond for workers abroad, bought in taka or foreign currency, in the worker's own name or a named person's, at authorised-dealer bank branches in Bangladesh and at Bangladeshi banks' branches and exchange houses abroad [G0075]. It comes in sizes from Tk 25,000 to Tk 50 lakh, with no limit on how much you buy [G0075]. It pays 12% simple profit up to Tk 15 lakh (11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh, 9% above), every six months; the profit is income-tax free, you can borrow against it and name a nominee, and the principal can be taken in US dollars [G0076]. In Kuwait, the Sonali Bank representative office handles bank accounts, these bonds and remittance complaints (see Contacts in Kuwait) [B0161].

## Undocumented workers and amnesties

A worker becomes undocumented ("illegal", অবৈধ) when he falls out of legal residence (iqama, আকামা) status, for example when his iqama is cancelled after an absconding case or he leaves his sponsor to work elsewhere without a transfer [U0108, U0099]. No amnesty is open: the last ran from mid-March to 30 June 2024, and since 5 January 2025 a violator who missed it can only go home, with no re-entry [U0013, U0023, U0024]. Workers caught in raids go to the Deportation Department, are fingerprinted and blacklisted for life (see Going home without valid papers) [U0034, U0041].

> **Need to know**
> - Has MOI announced or hinted at any new grace period (مهلة) for residence violators in 2026?

### How workers become undocumented

- **The employer reports you absent ("absconding").** Seven days' absence in a row lets the company file an absconding case, which can cancel your iqama, and lets the employer treat you as having resigned (see If your employer reports you absent) [U0108, L0015].
- **You leave your sponsor and work elsewhere.** Workers who change jobs without transferring sponsorship fall out of legal status, and anyone caught working anywhere other than the workplace on his iqama is jailed and deported [U0099, U0107, U0106].
- **The company was fake or has closed.** In "visa trading" (ভিসা বাণিজ্য, the sale of work visas by companies with no real jobs), workers pay for a visa, find no job and are left without status [U0098]. In July 2025 a Kuwaiti who signed for 29 companies confessed to selling residence permits to 56 workers for KD 350 to 1,200 each without giving them work [U0092, U0093]. In 2025 prosecutors charged a company owner and PAM officials over 382 workers recruited under 28 non-existent companies at KD 800 to 1,000 each, with PAM staff allegedly taking KD 200 to 250 a worker in bribes [U0090, U0091].
- **The employer won't renew or transfer you without money.** Former employers illegally charge workers to transfer their residence, and paying anyone for iqama renewal, leave or transfer is illegal [U0100, U0109].
- **You protest instead of complaining.** Strikes, staying off work over unpaid wages and mass protests by foreigners are banned; the Bangladesh Embassy tells workers to complain to PAM or its Domestic Labour Department instead (see If you aren't paid) [U0113, U0059, U0110].
- **You missed biometric fingerprinting** (see The biometric fingerprint deadline) [U0067].

### Is there an amnesty now?

No: the last amnesty (সাধারণ ক্ষমা, "grace period") ran from 14 March to 30 June 2024 [U0013]. Kuwait's residence law, Decree-Law 114/2024, has applied to violators since 5 January 2025, and its settlements do not cover people who stayed illegal through the 2024 amnesty: they can only leave, with no re-entry [U0024, U0023, L0411]. Fines under the new law are under What can get you jailed or deported [U0024].

#### The 2024 amnesty

It was announced on 14 March 2024 for 17 March to 17 June, then extended to 30 June, with two choices [U0001, U0013, U0003]:
- **Leave free.** Anyone could leave from any exit without paying fines and come back later on a new visa; with a valid passport you went straight to the airport, with no fine and no need to lift an absconding report [U0003, U0012, U0008].
- **Become legal.** Pay the fine, KD 2 a day up to KD 600, and get a new residence, renewed with the sponsor present at residency affairs; an Article 18 (private-sector company) worker went first to PAM's labour department, then to residency affairs [U0002, U0011, U0010].

**Without a passport:** you got an emergency travel document from your embassy, registered it at the Farwaniya or Mubarak Al-Kabeer residency affairs department between 3 pm and 8 pm, then left through any exit [U0009].

**What happened:** more than 65,000 violators left or legalised [U0014]. About 4,650 caught after 30 June were deported at once and barred from returning, most of them arrested in Mahboula and Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh [U0016, U0015]. When a violator is arrested, MOI blocks his sponsor and tells the sponsor through Sahel (the government services app), and sponsors of violators cannot recruit from abroad [U0017, U0020].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis used the 2024 amnesty (left or legalised), and did those who left free actually get new Kuwaiti visas afterwards?

#### Earlier amnesties

Kuwait held amnesties in 2018 (about 8,000 Bangladeshis used it), April 2020 and early 2021 [U0028, U0022, U0006]. In April 2020, of an estimated 25,000 undocumented Bangladeshis nearly 4,500 registered to go home; they waited more than two weeks in deportation camps, over 2,000 of them in Abdali, and said no one from the Embassy contacted them [U0029, U0026, U0025, U0114, U0027].

### If you are undocumented now

1. **If you were reported absent,** contest the report with PAM's Labour Relations Department within 60 days of the Ashal app alert (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0321].
2. **If your company was fake or closed,** complain to PAM: PAM has said a worker brought in by a closed or non-existent company may transfer his residence once a complaint and legal action are filed against the company (see Changing employer or leaving) [U0095]. In 2019 Kuwait let 10,000 workers of fake companies owned by six Kuwaitis pay fines and transfer [U0115, U0094].
3. **If you are a victim of abuse or trafficking,** go to the state shelter for men, opened in March 2025 for up to 300 workers: it takes workers whatever their status, including those with absconding charges; once you are admitted the employer cannot file an absconding report, and you can change employer or go home without the sponsor's approval [U0101, U0102, U0103]. In August 2026, though, PAM sent 110 people from the expatriate workers' shelter to the Deportation Department [U0079].
4. **Otherwise, leave** (see Going home without valid papers) [U0023].

### Raids and crackdowns

MOI runs raids, often with PAM, where workers live and work [U0088, U0078]:
- **Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh, July-August 2026:** authorities emptied dangerous buildings in Hasawiya and Abbasiya and moved residents to a government school used as a temporary shelter; people with valid papers were released after checks, and those without went to the deportation centre [U0082, U0083, U0084]. - **Shuwaikh Industrial Area, August 2026:** 48 arrested, 36 of them Article 18 workers [U0088, U0089]. In all, Kuwait deported more than 26,000 foreign workers in 2024, most for residence violations such as absconding, and 39,487 foreigners in 2025 [D0090, U0030].

**What to carry:** the Embassy asks every Bangladeshi to keep residence papers up to date and carry the Civil ID (the residence ID card issued by PACI, the Public Authority for Civil Information) whenever outside [U0085, W0013].

### The biometric fingerprint deadline

Every resident aged 18 or over had to give biometric fingerprints, foreigners by 31 December 2024 [U0072, U0064]. Fingerprints are taken at the airport, border posts and the General Department of Criminal Evidence's eight centres, open all week, by appointment booked in advance on the app [U0065, U0071, U0070]. ### Residency trafficking: if you bought your visa

Under Decree-Law 114/2024 a worker who gets a residence permit for money faces up to one year in prison, a fine of up to KD 1,000 (about Tk 4 lakh), or both, but anyone who reports the crime is exempt, even after getting the permit [L0381, L0415, L0411, G0002]. Visa trading leaves workers who paid for a visa without a real job or legal status, and undocumented workers are deported without screening for trafficking or any check of why they lost their status [U0098, U0097].

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Bangladesh's Wage Earners' Welfare Board pays Tk 1 lakh to bring home and treat an injured or sick worker and Tk 3 lakh to the family of a worker who dies abroad [B0130, B0131]. A worker who came without a BMET card can get this cover with a Welfare Board card from the Embassy (see Help from the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait) [B0120].

### If you are injured at work

1. **Report it.** The employer must report a work injury, including one on the way to or from work, at once to the area police station, PAM's labour department and the insurer; you or someone for you can report it too [L0067, L0066, L0178]. If your employer beat or injured you, complain to the police and insist on a medical examination to record the injury [L0097].
2. **Get treatment at the employer's cost.** The employer pays treatment, medicine and transport, and must insure you against work injuries and occupational diseases [L0068, L0065, L0177].
3. **Get your wage while off work:** full wage for the whole treatment period, then half wage after six months until you recover, are declared disabled or die [L0070].
4. **Claim compensation.** The treating doctor's report sets the treatment period and the disability percentage, and compensation follows a schedule set by ministerial resolution [L0180, L0071]. You lose it if you hurt yourself on purpose, were drunk or on drugs, or broke posted safety rules, unless the injury kills you or leaves over 25% permanent disability [L0072]. An occupational disease that appears up to one year after you leave is covered [L0073].
5. **If you disagree with the disability rating,** object within one month to the Ministry of Health's Medical Arbitration Committee [L0069].
6. **If you can't go on working,** the contract ends and you are owed full end-of-service pay [L0134, L0143].
7. **If the employer won't pay,** claim at PAM (see If you aren't paid) [L0362].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the current ministerial compensation schedule pay for death and for each degree of disability?
> - Which insurer covers work injuries for Article 18 workers in practice, and how does a worker claim after going home disabled?

### If a worker dies

The contract ends on the worker's death, and his heirs are owed full end-of-service pay, and, for a death from a work injury, compensation under the schedule [L0024, L0405, L0071].

#### Steps in Kuwait after a death

1. **The NOC for the body.** The Embassy issues a free NOC (no-objection certificate) for sending the body home, the same day once the death certificate is available [B0159].
2. **Who pays for the body.** The employer pays; if there is no employer, or the sponsor (kafeel) cannot be found, or the worker had no iqama, the Embassy pays the full cost [B0159, B0208]. The visa attestation contract the sponsor signs at the Embassy makes him send the body home at his own cost [D0043].
3. **Compensation for an accidental death.** The heirs give the Embassy a power of attorney; the Embassy's lawyer pursues the case and the money goes through the Embassy to the Welfare Board, which pays the heirs [B0160].

#### Steps in Bangladesh after a death

1. **Claim from the employer through the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare:** the next of kin applies to aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd with proof of being next of kin, the employer's details and the amounts owed, and the Ministry writes to the Embassy [B0097].
2. **Receive the body.** A free ambulance takes the body from Dhaka or Chattogram airport on request (see Coming home) [B0071].
3. **Claim the Welfare Board's Tk 3 lakh** [B0131].

### If you lose the job

- **Closure or bankruptcy:** the contract ends and full end-of-service pay is due; if the firm is sold or merged, your contract carries on with the new owner [L0135, L0136, L0143]. If the firm is shut for reasons that aren't your fault while the employer wants you to keep working, you are paid for the closure [L0041].
- **Notice:** if the employer ends an open-ended contract, it owes three months' notice or pay in lieu, and during notice you may take one paid day (or eight hours) a week to look for work [L0125, L0126]. You can't be dismissed for your sex, origin or religion [L0128].
- **Transfer:** closure or bankruptcy lets PAM move you to another employer before one year (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0266].
- **The fare home:** an employer that recruited you without having real work must pay your way home [L0193].
- **The deadline to leave:** if your residence ends without a transfer, you must leave within the period MOI sets, at most six months from the end of service [L0422].

## Coming home

- **Before you fly, pay every government bill in your name.** Expatriates are stopped at the border until they pay traffic fines, electricity and water, phone and Ministry of Justice bills; most can be paid on Sahel, the ministries' websites or at the airport, and court travel bans for private debts are under What can get you jailed or deported [G0046, G0049, G0050].
- **Get the exit permit:** an Article 18 worker needs one for every trip (see Going home on leave) [G0061].
- **Flights can stop at short notice.** Kuwait's airspace was closed from 28 February to 23 April 2026 and again from 18 July 2026 after Iranian attacks; save the Embassy's emergency hotlines (see Contacts in Kuwait) [S0001, S0025, B0202].
- **Leaving Kuwait with cash and gold.** Declare cash of KD 3,000 (about Tk 12.1 lakh) or more, in any currency, and all gold in any form to customs before leaving [G0041, G0042, G0002]. Since February 2026 cash over KD 3,000 can be declared in advance on Sahel, which issues a notice that customs checks at the airport [G0045]. Not declaring gold can lead to arrest or confiscation [G0044]. Carry watches, jewellery and costly electronics in hand luggage with their receipts [G0043].
- **Household goods: get a TR certificate.** A Bangladeshi leaving Kuwait for good applies to the Embassy's Labour Wing for a TR (transfer of residence) certificate under NBR's SRO 165 of 2000 to bring household goods home; the form is free at the Embassy reception and on its website [B0166].
- **Customs in Bangladesh** (passenger baggage rules, SRO 261 of 2 June 2025) [G0018]:
  - Duty-free: one item from the duty-free list, such as a laptop, a television up to 29 inches, a microwave, a sewing machine or 100 g of gold and 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [G0018].
  - Phones: 2 used phones, plus 1 new phone once a year [G0019].
  - Gold bar: once a year, one declared bar of up to 117 g (10 tola), at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; more is confiscated, and undeclared gold means arrest and a criminal case [G0020, G0021].
  - No alcohol on a Bangladeshi passport, and goods brought to sell are seized [G0022, G0024].
  - Cash: declare more than USD 10,000 (about Tk 12.4 lakh) on the FMJ form [G0023, G0003].
  - Baggage sent separately: declare it on the Schedule-1 form handed out on the plane, or at the customs house next to the airport within 7 days [G0025].
  - Help at Dhaka airport customs: +88 01319-056591; airport hotline 13600 [G0026].
