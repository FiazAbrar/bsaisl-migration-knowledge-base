Sheikh Fahad: ‘Law will apply to all’ as firms face crackdown over unpaid wages

HH the Amir directs PAM to set aside weekly day to receive citizens

KUWAIT: 
First Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Interior Sheikh Fahad Yousef Saud Al-Sabah said His Highness the Amir Sheikh Meshal Al-Ahmad Al-Jaber Al-Sabah has directed that doors be opened to citizens, asking the Public Authority for Manpower (PAM) to set aside one day a week to receive them.
Speaking at an open meeting with company owners and representatives on Wednesday, Sheikh Fahad said HH the Amir had stressed that the law must be applied to everyone, even to his own sons. “I challenge anyone to prove that I have broken the law or passed transactions through favoritism. Kuwait is what matters most and what endures,” he said. Addressing business owners, he added: “The law will be applied to everyone. Work properly and no one will hold you to account.”
At the meeting, the second with private sector companies on the wage protection system, Sheikh Fahad said the files of 10,000 companies with five or more registered workers were under review after failing to pay salaries for between one year and a year and a half. “The injustice of unpaid salaries has reached a dangerous level, extending even to subcontracted workers at cooperative societies,” he said.
He said a strict automated system now suspends company files as soon as salary payments become irregular, leading to a marked drop in labor strikes over unpaid wages. Human trafficking, money laundering and unpaid salaries are among the main issues hurting Kuwait’s standing with international organizations, he said.
Sheikh Fahad warned again of the dangers of “tadmeen”, in which companies are registered in citizens’ names, saying some citizens had become involved in money laundering and that such cases were growing at State Security. Some citizens do not even know companies are registered in their names, he said, citing one citizen listed as earning a KD 3,000 salary who received only KD 180 a month. Such practices hurt Kuwait’s reputation with the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) and other international indicators, he said. He also cited a restaurant license registered to a single person, the owner’s son, through which KD 70 million had circulated in cooperation with two other companies.
He said violators would have all their company files and licenses closed, not only the file in violation. He directed PAM officials to open labor needs assessments to all without inspection, warning that anyone who breaks the law will be held strictly accountable. Sheikh Fahad also said authorities were studying transferring workers on government contracts to the labor market, and moving domestic workers from Article 20 visas to Article 18 private sector visas.
He said 300 delivery workers had been deported in recent days for violations, urging companies to hire organized, experienced delivery workers and drivers trained in their home countries. “Forget the old chaos,” he said. Delivery workers must not be made to work 18 hours a day, he added, calling on business owners to show compassion towards expatriates and consider their living conditions.
PAM Director General Rabab Al-Osaimi said the files of 10,000 companies that failed to pay workers had been suspended since September. Salaries must be paid before the seventh day of the following month, she said, and suspending a company’s file also halts labor support payments to citizens registered on it. Cash salary payments are allowed only for new workers’ first two months in the country, she said. “We will not allow workers to transfer to another employer without sufficient reasons,” she added.
