# Kuwait Work Permit 2026: Application Process, Documents, and Latest Updates

Last updated: May 20, 2026

Planning to work in Kuwait? You’ll need a valid work permit before you arrive, and recent rule changes have updated the fees and transfer policies.

This guide breaks down the permit types, documents, costs, and application steps.

## What Is a Kuwait Work Permit?

A Kuwait work permit is an official authorisation that lets a foreign national legally take up paid employment in Kuwait. It is issued by the [Public Authority for Manpower (PAM)](https://www.manpower.gov.kw/) on behalf of a Kuwait-based employer.

If you are planning to [fly to Kuwait](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights-to/kwi) for work, your employer must secure this permit before you travel. You can compare [flight options](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights) and [short-stay accommodation](https://kw.wego.com/en/hotels) on Wego while your paperwork is processed.

Kuwait government services distinguish between the PAM-issued work permit and the Ministry of Interior work entry visa. Travellers often use “work visa” informally for the wider employer-sponsored process.

## Latest Kuwait Work Permit Updates

Kuwait’s [Public Authority for Manpower](https://www.manpower.gov.kw/) has rolled out several rule changes affecting expat workers, employers, and renewals. Travellers searching for [cheap flights to Kuwait](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights) should factor these into their plans.

- The standard work permit processing fee rose from KWD 175 to KWD 325.
- An early-transfer fee of KWD 300 applies if a worker changes employers within three years of permit issuance.
- Transfers between employers are now permitted from day one, subject to the early-transfer fee.
- Work permits no longer distinguish between local and foreign worker categories under the unified framework.
- The previous annual KWD 250 renewal fee for expats aged 60+ with a high-school diploma or less has been revoked.

## Types of Kuwait Work Permits

Kuwait issues several work permit categories, each tied to the sector and nature of employment. Knowing which one applies to you helps avoid delays. Most expats heading to Kuwait, including those booking [flights to Kuwait](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights) for the first time, fall under the private-sector category.

| Permit Type | Who It’s For |
|----|----|
| Article 18: Private Sector | Foreign workers employed by private companies |
| Article 17: Government Sector | Foreign workers hired by government ministries or contractors |
| Article 20: Domestic Worker | Domestic helpers sponsored by Kuwaiti households |
| Temporary Work Permit | Contract assignments of less than one year |
| Part-Time Work Permit | Existing residents authorised to take an additional part-time role |

## Required Documents

The employer prepares most of the paperwork, but the employee must provide personal records that meet PAM’s requirements. Have your [travel documents](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights) ready alongside the items below.

**Employer-side documents:**

- Original signature authorisation and a copy
- Authenticated wage transfer certificate from the Work Inspection Department
- Copy of the sponsor’s passport and civil ID
- Declaration and undertaking letter (mandatory for Egyptian applicants)

**Employee-side documents:**

- Valid passport with the required remaining validity; Kuwait Government Online states that private-sector work entry visa applicants need a passport valid for no less than two years
- Educational certificate attested by the relevant authorities
- Two recent passport-size photographs
- Police clearance certificate from the home country
- Medical fitness certificate from a GAMCA/Wafid-approved clinic
- Health insurance certificate (required for the first-time residence permit)
- Disease-free certificate from a Kuwait Ministry of Health centre (issued after arrival)

Applicants must complete a pre-departure medical at a [GAMCA-approved centre](https://blog.wego.com/gamca-medical/). The official Kuwait and Wafid medical-fitness lists cover HIV/AIDS, hepatitis B and C, microfilaria/filariasis, malaria, leprosy, and tuberculosis-related chest X-ray findings.

## How to Apply for a Kuwait Work Permit

The application is employer-led. The employee’s role is limited to providing documents and completing the medical and biometric steps. Begin sorting [flights to Kuwait](https://kw.wego.com/en/flights-to/kwi) and [initial accommodation](https://kw.wego.com/en/hotels) only after the permit has been approved.

- **Employer files the application:** The Kuwait-based employer submits the application through the [PAM Ashal e-portal](https://www.manpower.gov.kw/). The submission includes the employee’s passport copy, qualifications, salary offer, and a no-objection certificate.
- **Approval and dispatch:** Once PAM approves the permit, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs forwards a copy to the Kuwait embassy in the employee’s home country. The employer also sends the employee a copy directly.
- **Medical and visa stamping:** The employee completes the GAMCA medical, then visits the Kuwait embassy or consulate to have the entry visa stamped on the passport.
- **Arrival in Kuwait:** On arrival, the employee must complete fingerprinting, a confirmatory medical at a Ministry of Health centre, and start the residence permit (Iqama) procedures within two months of entry.
- **Civil ID issuance:** After the residence permit is issued, the employee applies for a Kuwait Civil ID at the Public Authority for Civil Information.

## Kuwait Work Permit Fees

Fees are paid in Kuwaiti dinar (KWD) and vary by permit type. The figures below reflect the updated rates enforced by PAM.

| Service                                     | Fee (KWD) |
|---------------------------------------------|-----------|
| Standard work permit processing             | 325       |
| Work permit (base fee)                      | 10        |
| Entrance visa (government and civil sector) | 3         |
| First-time residence permit (civil sector)  | 10        |
| Early employer transfer (within 3 years)    | 300       |

## Employer Transfer Rules

Work permit holders can now move between employers from day one of their permit, subject to the rules below. For the full breakdown of paperwork, fees, and timelines, see our dedicated guide to [Kuwait work permit transfer](https://blog.wego.com/kuwait-work-permit-transfer/).

- Transfers within three years of permit issuance attract a KWD 300 early-transfer fee.
- Transfers after three years are exempt from the fee.
- The current employer’s no-objection consent is generally required.
- Restricted-sector workers may benefit from temporary windows announced by ministerial decree.

## Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Kuwait work permit cost?

The standard PAM processing fee is KWD 325, plus smaller statutory charges of around KWD 10 each for the work permit base fee and first-time residence permit, and KWD 3 for the entrance visa. Medical tests and document attestation are billed separately.

How long does a Kuwait work permit take to process?

End-to-end processing typically takes four to eight weeks. The timeline depends on PAM workload, GAMCA medical scheduling, and embassy stamping in your home country.

Can I apply for a Kuwait work permit on my own?

No. Only a Kuwait-registered employer with a valid commercial registration and an available sector quota can file the application with PAM. Direct self-sponsorship is not permitted under the current framework.

Is the Kuwait work permit renewable?

Yes. The sponsoring employer must submit a renewal request before the existing permit expires, and the renewal fee is payable each cycle. Failure to renew on time can lead to fines or permit cancellation.

Can I bring my family on a Kuwait work permit?

Family residency depends on your job category and salary. Workers earning above the minimum income threshold set by Kuwait’s immigration rules can sponsor spouses and children on a family residence visa, subject to additional documentation.

What happens if my Kuwait work permit is cancelled?

You must either secure a transfer to a new sponsor or leave Kuwait within the grace period set by immigration authorities, which is typically up to 60 days. Overstaying triggers daily fines and a possible re-entry ban.
