# Kuwait issues new anti-drug law

Unified decree-law tackles licensing, penalties including death, rehabilitation

**By B Izzak & KUNA**

**KUWAIT:** Kuwait has issued Amiri decree-law no. 59 of 2025 on combating drugs and psychotropic substances, creating a single comprehensive legal framework that merges and replaces the country’s previous drug-control laws. The decree-law, comprising 84 articles across 13 chapters, will take effect two weeks after its publication in the official gazette.

Under Article 83, the legislation repeals law no. 74 of 1983 on drug control and law no. 48 of 1987 on psychotropic substances, along with any conflicting provisions. Article 82 mandates the competent minister to issue all necessary executive decisions for implementation, while article 84 obliges all ministers to enforce the law within their respective jurisdictions.

The anti-drug decree-law consolidates the former drug and psychotropic substances laws into a single unified framework designed to enhance legal clarity, administrative efficiency and consistency across enforcement authorities.

Chapter One defines all key terms used throughout the law, including the ministry (health), minister (health), licensing procedures, narcotic and psychotropic substances, chemical precursors, preparations, production, import, export, cultivation, transport, smuggling, promotion, possession and related medical and administrative terminology. It also establishes definitions for addicts, users, treatment centers, rehabilitation facilities, records and prescriptions.

Chapter Two establishes the Supreme Council for Combating Drugs and Psychotropic Substances, responsible for forming a national strategy, improving inter-ministerial coordination, enhancing prevention and awareness, strengthening treatment

and rehabilitation services and ensuring compliance with international drug-control conventions.

The health ministry is required to establish dedicated rehabilitation and addiction treatment centers, including separate facilities for those under 21. Private medical units may also provide treatment services subject to ministerial licensing and oversight. The interior ministry will set up specialized correction and rehabilitation centers for inmates convicted under the decree-law, providing medical, social and reintegration programs separate from general prison populations.

Chapters Three through Seven regulate all licensing related to the import, export, transport, possession, dispensing, production, manufacture and cultivation of narcotics, psychotropic substances, and preparations. Only licensed entities may trade, dispense or manufacture controlled substances, and prohibited plants may be cultivated exclusively by authorized government bodies, research institutions, universities or licensed centers.

Chapter Nine outlines strict penalties for trafficking, production or cultivation intended for trafficking. Offenses may incur the death penalty or life imprisonment, in addition to fines ranging from KD 100,000 to KD 2 million, or an equivalent to the value of seized substances. Lesser offenses carry life imprisonment and fines between KD 50,000 and KD 500,000. Organizing an international drug-trafficking operation is punishable by death, while participation in such groups carries life imprisonment and fines from KD 20,000 to KD 50,000.

Chapter Ten encourages voluntary treatment by granting immunity from prosecution to addicts who seek rehabilitation before charges are filed. Rehabilitation centers must maintain confidentiality of all patient information and surrender any substances handed over by patients to the ministry for proper disposal.

Chapter Eleven governs verdicts and their execution, allowing courts to suspend certain sentences but prohibiting appeals of detention orders issued for rehabilitation purposes. It also permits exemptions for offenders who voluntarily report crimes or assist investigations.

Public prosecution is empowered to investigate and prosecute all offenses, and criminal courts will hold jurisdiction over cases involving narcotics and psychotropic substances. The law applies to offenses committed within Kuwait and to acts committed abroad that contribute to crimes occurring domestically. Kuwaiti nationals may also be prosecuted for drug offenses committed overseas, except when acquitted by foreign courts or after serving a foreign sentence.

Chapters Twelve and Thirteen specify arrest procedures, investigative powers and administrative oversight. Authorized judicial officers may access government, private and licensed facilities to inspect compliance and seize prohibited substances. The decree-law emphasizes inter-ministerial cooperation, public-private partnership in treatment and rehabilitation, and ongoing evaluation of enforcement measures to ensure alignment with international standards.

Amiri decree-law no. 59 of 2025 marks Kuwait’s most comprehensive reform of drug-control legislation in decades, integrating prevention, treatment, enforcement and administrative oversight to curb trafficking, reduce substance abuse and protect public health and safety.
