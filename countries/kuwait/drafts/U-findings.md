## Undocumented workers and amnesties

A worker becomes undocumented ("illegal", অবৈধ) in Kuwait when his residence (iqama, আকামা) expires or is cancelled and he stays on [U0099, U0023]. There is no amnesty open on 9 November 2026: the last ran from mid-March to 30 June 2024, and since 5 January 2025 MOI says a violator who missed it has no choice but to go home with no re-entry [U0013, U0023, U0024]. Workers caught in raids go to the Deportation Department, are fingerprinted and blacklisted for life (see Going home without valid papers) [U0034, U0041].

> **Need to know**
> - Has MOI announced or hinted at any new grace period (مهلة) for residence violators in 2026?

### How workers become undocumented

- **The employer reports you absent ("absconding").** Seven days' absence in a row lets the company file an absconding case, which can end your job and cancel your iqama (see If your employer reports you absent) [U0108].
- **You leave your sponsor and work elsewhere.** Workers routinely change jobs without transferring sponsorship and fall out of legal status, risking fines, deportation or blacklisting [U0099]. Working anywhere other than the workplace on your iqama is illegal, and workers caught are jailed and deported [U0107, U0106].
- **The company was fake or has closed.** In "visa trading" (ভিসা বাণিজ্য, the sale of work visas by companies with no real jobs), workers pay for a visa, find no job and are left without status or legal protection [U0098]. In July 2025 a Kuwaiti who signed for 29 companies confessed to selling residence permits to 56 workers for KD 350 to 1,200 each without giving them work [U0092, U0093]. In 2025 prosecutors charged a company owner and officials of the Public Authority for Manpower (PAM, Kuwait's labour authority) over 382 workers recruited under 28 non-existent companies at KD 800 to 1,000 each, with PAM staff allegedly taking KD 200 to 250 a worker in bribes [U0090, U0091].
- **The employer won't renew or transfer you without money.** Former employers illegally charge workers to transfer their residence [U0100]. Paying anyone for iqama renewal, leave or transfer is illegal [U0109].
- **You protest instead of complaining.** Strikes and staying off work over unpaid wages are banned, and mass protests by foreigners are prohibited; the Embassy tells workers to complain to PAM or the Domestic Labour Department instead (see If you aren't paid) [U0113, U0059, U0110].
- **You miss the biometric fingerprinting.** See The biometric fingerprint deadline [U0067].

### Is there an amnesty now?

No. The last amnesty (সাধারণ ক্ষমা, "grace period", مهلة) ran from 14 March to 30 June 2024 [U0013]. Kuwait's residence law, Decree-Law 114/2024, has applied to violators since 5 January 2025, and MOI says its paid settlements do not cover people who stayed illegal through the 2024 amnesty: they can only leave, with no re-entry [U0024, U0023]. Fines under the new law are covered separately (see What can get you jailed or deported) [U0024].

#### The 2024 amnesty

It was announced on 14 March 2024 for 17 March to 17 June, then extended to 30 June [U0001, U0013]. It offered two choices [U0001, U0003]:
- **Leave free.** Anyone could leave from any exit without paying fines and come back later on a new visa [U0003, U0012]. With a valid passport you went straight to the airport: no visit to residency affairs, no fine, no need to lift an absconding report [U0008].
- **Become legal.** Pay the fine, KD 2 a day up to KD 600, and get a new residence [U0002]. An expired residence was renewed with the sponsor present at residency affairs [U0011]. An Article 18 (private-sector company) worker went first to PAM's labour department, then to residency affairs [U0010]. People with court cases or "administrative obstacles" applied to residency affairs to have their case assessed [U0005].

**Without a passport:** you got an emergency travel document from your embassy, registered it at the Farwaniya or Mubarak Al-Kabeer residency affairs department between 3 pm and 8 pm, then left through any exit [U0009].

**What happened:** more than 65,000 violators left or legalised [U0014]. About 4,650 caught after 30 June were deported at once and barred from returning, and most arrests were in Mahboula and Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh [U0016, U0015]. When a violator is arrested, MOI puts a block on his sponsor and notifies the sponsor through Sahel, the government services app [U0017]; sponsors of violators are blocked from recruiting from abroad [U0020].

> - How many Bangladeshis used the 2024 amnesty (left vs legalised), and how many travel permits did the Embassy issue for it?

#### Earlier amnesties

Kuwait held amnesties in 2018 (about 8,000 Bangladeshis used it), April 2020 and early 2021 [U0028, U0022, U0006]. In the April 2020 amnesty, of an estimated 25,000 undocumented Bangladeshis nearly 4,500 registered to go home [U0029, U0026]. They waited more than two weeks in deportation camps, over 2,000 of them in Abdali, and said no one from the Embassy contacted them [U0025, U0114, U0027].

### If you are undocumented now

With no amnesty open, the routes are narrow [U0023]:
1. **If you were reported absent,** object at PAM before the case reaches MOI (see If your employer reports you absent) [U0108].
2. **If your company was fake or closed,** complain to PAM: PAM has said a worker brought in by a closed or non-existent company has the right to transfer his residence once a complaint and legal action are filed against the company [U0095]. Kuwait has let such workers pay fines and transfer before: in 2019, 10,000 workers of fake companies owned by six Kuwaitis [U0115, U0094]. How to transfer is covered separately (see Changing employer or leaving) [U0095].
3. **If you are a victim of abuse or trafficking,** the state shelter for men, opened in March 2025 for up to 300 workers, accepts workers whatever their status, including those with absconding charges; once a worker is admitted the employer cannot file an absconding report, and he can change employer or go home without the sponsor's approval [U0101, U0102, U0103]. In August 2026, though, PAM sent 110 people from the expatriate workers' shelter to the Deportation Department [U0079].
4. **Otherwise, leave** (see Going home without valid papers) [U0023].

### Raids and crackdowns

MOI runs raids, often with PAM, in the areas where workers live and work [U0088, U0078]. Examples:
- **Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh, July-August 2026:** authorities emptied dangerous buildings in Hasawiya and Abbasiya and moved residents to a temporary shelter in a government school in Hasawiya [U0082, U0083]. People with valid papers were released after checks; those without went to the deportation centre [U0084]. By 1 August the campaign had arrested 1,253 people for security, residency and labour violations, and power was cut to 1,200 illegally occupied homes [U0078, U0080]. MOI says it will move to other areas [U0081, U0086].
- **Shuwaikh Industrial Area, August 2026:** 48 arrested in 120 businesses, 36 of them Article 18 workers, plus one man wanted over a non-existent Article 18 company [U0088, U0089].

**What to carry:** the Embassy asks every Bangladeshi to keep residence papers up to date and carry the Civil ID (the residence ID card) whenever outside [U0085].

### The biometric fingerprint deadline

Every resident aged 18 or over had to give biometric fingerprints; the deadline for foreigners was 31 December 2024 [U0072, U0064]. Fingerprints are taken at the airport, border posts and the General Department of Criminal Evidence's centres: eight centres, open all week, with an appointment booked in advance on the app [U0065, U0071, U0070]. Expatriates who missed it had their government and bank transactions suspended and cannot leave Kuwait until they do it [U0067, U0068]. About 181,000 to 244,000 expatriates had not done it in early January 2025 [U0069, U0073]. Authorities said there would be no fine [U0074].

> **Need to know**

> - Can an undocumented worker who never gave fingerprints leave through the airport or only through the Deportation Department?

### Residency trafficking: if you bought your visa

Under Decree-Law 114/2024 a worker who gets a residence permit for money faces up to one year in prison, a fine of up to KD 1,000, or both, but anyone who reports the crime is exempt (see What can get you jailed or deported) [L0381]. In practice Kuwait prosecutes the sellers as "visa trading" and deports the workers they leave without status, mostly without asking how they lost it [U0098, U0097]. Workers arrested for immigration offences are deported quickly [U0105].

> **Need to know**
> - Has any worker who reported his visa seller under the law's informant exemption kept his residence?

## Going home without valid papers

Outside an amnesty, the route the sources describe is arrest and deportation through MOI's Deportation Department [U0018, U0023]. You need a passport or a travel document from the Bangladesh Embassy (see Embassy services in Kuwait) [U0009, U0018]. Deportees are fingerprinted and banned for life, and the bans are covered separately (see Can you come back) [U0034].

### Step 1: Get a passport or travel permit

1. **If your passport is valid,** go to Step 2 [U0008].
2. **If it is expired, lost or held by the employer,** apply at the Embassy for a Travel Permit (an "outpass", a one-way document home) (see Embassy services in Kuwait) [U0009, U0018]. If you are already detained, the prisons administration asks the Embassy through Kuwait's Foreign Ministry, and slow embassies are a main cause of delay [U0040, U0039].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Embassy issue a Travel Permit to a worker with an absconding case or an expired residence without a letter from MOI?

### Step 2: Leave or surrender

1. **Check for cases first.** A court or Public Prosecution travel ban keeps you in Kuwait until the case is over; travel bans for debts are covered separately (see Sending money home) [U0039].
2. **Know what happens if you are caught.** MOI lifts the security restrictions, requests a travel document if you have none, and sends you to the Deportation Department [U0018].

> **Need to know**
> - Outside an amnesty, can an undocumented worker leave through the airport on his own after paying the overstay settlement, or must every case go through the Deportation Department?
> - Outside an amnesty, can an undocumented worker with no court case walk into the Deportation Department or a residency affairs office to surrender, and does he pay the overstay fine first?

### Inside the Deportation Department

The old deportation prison at Talha in Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh closed in 2024 because it was dilapidated [U0046, U0050]. Detainees now go to a new building in the Sulaibiya prison area, the former juvenile prison, opened on 19 August 2024 for about 1,000 men and 400 women [U0045, U0047, U0043].

- **On arrival:** your identity and personal data are recorded and your fingerprints taken [U0041].
- **How long:** about three days with a passport or travel document, most people within a week, longer if the Embassy is slow or a court case is pending [U0038, U0033, U0039].
- **Calls and visits:** detainees can phone their families from landlines and meet embassy staff in designated offices [U0051, U0054].
- **The ticket:** the sponsor or the deportee buys it; if neither does, MOI buys it through travel agencies with offices in the building and registers the cost as a claim against the sponsor, with a travel ban on him until he pays [U0036, U0037]. You board through a separate airport gate [U0053].
- **Someone to act for you:** a detained person can sign an authorisation letting a relative deal with any ministry on his behalf [U0049].
- **Family:** dependants whose residence you sponsor must leave with you [U0031].

> **Need to know**

> - How much money and how many bags can a deportee take, and who collects his belongings from his room?

### Before you are deported: wages and belongings

Deportation can come before a wage case is settled: in July 2025, 127 Bangladeshis who gathered at Fintas police station to complain about five months' unpaid wages were deported without being allowed to collect their belongings, and three were kept in custody over money disputes [U0055, U0056, U0057, U0058]. Workers often go home without wages or end-of-service pay owed in a pending case [U0104].

**If you might be deported, in this order:**
1. **File the wage complaint at PAM, not the police** (see If you aren't paid) [U0112, U0059].
2. **Tell the Embassy's labour wing** at once (see Contacts); in the Fintas case it appealed to MOI, PAM and the Ministry of Social Affairs to halt the deportations, and the workers were deported anyway [U0111, U0055].
3. **Sign a power of attorney** for the Embassy or a trusted person in Kuwait to claim your wages and belongings; the labour wing told the Fintas deportees to do this as soon as they reached Bangladesh [U0060].

> **Need to know**
> - Where in Bangladesh is a power of attorney for a Kuwait wage claim made (notary, Ministry of Foreign Affairs attestation), and did any of the 127 Fintas deportees get paid?

### Can you come back

- **Deported:** you are fingerprinted at the airport and blacklisted for life [U0034]. Under the 2024 law a deported foreigner may return only with the Interior Minister's explicit approval, and the Embassy tells workers that once deported there is no chance to come back [L0382, U0106]. Media also report a five-year ban from the other Gulf (GCC) countries [U0019].
- **Left during an amnesty:** you can return on a new visa under the normal rules [U0012].
- **Coming back on a new passport doesn't work.** Biometric fingerprinting has caught hundreds of deportees who returned years later with forged passports and new names, some after burning or surgically altering their fingertips; each is deported again, even with a valid residence renewed for years [U0075, U0077, U0076].
- **Checking a ban:** the Embassy said in 2025 it would check whether deported workers were blacklisted or banned from the Gulf [U0061].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a Bangladeshi in Bangladesh check whether he is on Kuwait's blacklist (through the Embassy, a Kuwaiti lawyer, or a visa application), and is the GCC five-year ban real for Kuwait deportees in 2026?
> - Does a worker who left Kuwait on his own with an expired residence (not through the Deportation Department) face a ban?
