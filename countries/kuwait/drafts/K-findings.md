### Step 5: Medical check and checking your visa is real

(Replaces W's item 2. W's item 1, the Wafid medical, stays as it is.)

2. **Check the visa is real before you pay the rest.** BMET does not check Kuwaiti visas online, and MOI's online visa enquiry is for visit visas, so check the visa through the offices that must have handled it [B0087, W0060, K0071, K0072].
   You'll need: a copy of the visa, the demand letter and the BMET permission letter if there is one (Step 1, item 3) [B0087, B0180].
   1. Read the visa: it must be an Article 18 work visa ("Visa No. 18"), not a visit visa (No. 14) or a domestic visa (No. 20) [W0074, W0077].
   2. Take the visa and demand letter to your DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office) or to BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka: BMET checks them free within 3 working days, but only checks Saudi, Qatari, Bahraini, Singapore and UAE visas online, not Kuwaiti ones (Step 1, item 5) [B0087, B0093].
   3. Ask the agency which Kuwait-Embassy-authorised processing agency in Dhaka is handling your visa: every Kuwait work visa must pass through one of them, and the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka has authorised at least 10 [N0002, N0003]. Your Wafid result is attested by the Kuwait Embassy as part of the same visa process [K0008, K0015].
   4. Ask for the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait's attestation of the demand letter (company visas) or of the single visa (Step 1, item 4): a company recruiting Bangladeshis must have it, and the Embassy checks the company's papers first [B0112, B0101].
   5. Online, MOI's residency e-services page lists "Enquire Visa Application Status", but its links go to kuwaitvisa.moi.gov.kw and rnt.moi.gov.kw; kuwaitvisa.moi.gov.kw handles tourist, family, business and government visit visas, and work or residence status is checked separately [W0060, K0072, K0071].

   **Scams:** in 2025 a worker who came through a Bangladeshi agency to Kuwait Oil Al Naseef Company was told on arrival that his visa was fake, that he still owed money for it, and that he would be sent home unless the broker paid [K0050]. The Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka gave journalists only an email address for questions and did not answer them [K0051].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there any official way (MOI page, Sahel, Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka, Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait by phone or email) for a worker in Bangladesh to confirm an Article 18 entry visa by its number?
   > - What does a genuine Article 18 entry visa printout look like in 2026 (title, visa number, QR code, sponsor's name, profession), and what do the fakes sold in Bangladesh get wrong?
   > - Does the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka still stamp or endorse the entry visa, and what does the stamp or endorsement show?
   > - Is the list of Kuwait-Embassy-authorised processing agencies published anywhere?

### Step 7: After arrival

(Replaces W's items 2, 5 and 6. W's items 1, 3, 4 and 7 stay as they are.)

2. **Take the arrival medical at a Ministry of Health centre.** You are tested again on arrival to get the health certificate needed for your residence [K0008, W0011]. Everyone except domestic workers (who come with their sponsor) needs an appointment booked in advance [K0006].
   1. Go to the centre on the appointment: MOH's Expatriate Labor Screening Unit runs five main centres, Shuwaikh (the main centre), Fahaheel, Rumaithiya, Jahra and Sabhan, open Sunday to Thursday, 7:30 am to 6 pm [K0003]. Ali Sabah Al-Salem (Umm Al-Hayman) has also been listed as an expat examination centre [K0007].
   2. You are tested for HIV, hepatitis B and C and tuberculosis, and for malaria and filariasis depending on your country [K0009, K0001]. You may be given vaccinations, including meningitis [K0001].
   3. The result goes straight into MOI's system for your residence [K0002]. Ask the company for the reference number and check on MOI's "medical result readiness" enquiry [K0002, W0063].

   **If it fails:** a suspected infection is re-tested to confirm it [K0004]. Cases that pose a significant public-health threat are deported; treatable cases are referred to specialised centres [K0004]. MOH has said that a worker found with a listed disease is deported and put on the banned list [K0013]. Since May 2025 an "indeterminate" HIV antibody result makes a new arrival medically unfit, and the same applies to an indeterminate hepatitis C result [K0011, K0012]. Tuberculosis, hepatitis B and C and HIV are the diseases found most often [K0005].
   **Later:** food handlers, barbers, health-centre staff and similar jobs are re-examined periodically [K0010].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What is the 2026 fee for the arrival medical, and does the employer or the worker pay it in practice?
   > - Which centres take new male Article 18 workers in 2026, and is the booking still on eservices.moh.gov.kw or now on Sahel?
   > - Who pays the ticket of a worker deported as medically unfit, and does the agency refund him?
   > - How long after the test is the result ready?

5. **Get your Civil ID.** The Civil ID is your residence permit: since 10 March 2019 no residence sticker goes in the passport, and the card carries your passport number and profession and is needed to leave and re-enter Kuwait [K0016, K0017]. After the medical, fingerprints and residence, your employer requests the card through PACI's online system [K0018].
   You'll need: your Civil ID number, which the company's HR department can give you [K0020].
   1. Check the card: open services.paci.gov.kw/card/inquiry, choose "Card Status", type your Civil ID number and submit; when the card is ready the page says so and names the place to collect it [K0077, K0021]. Or call PACI on 188988 [K0022].
   2. Collect it: cards are issued by PACI's vending machines, between 9 am and 8 pm [K0030]. Collect it within 3 months [K0031]. In 2020 more than 200,000 cards lay uncollected, over 90% of them belonging to Article 18 workers [K0032, K0033].
   3. The fee is KD 5, paid online on PACI's website; delivery to your home costs KD 2 more [K0027, K0028, K0029]. A lost or damaged card costs KD 20 to replace [K0028].
   4. Check that your name and passport details on the card match your passport exactly, or you may be stopped when leaving or re-entering [K0019].
   5. Set up the digital Civil ID in the Kuwait Mobile ID app (Hawyti) once you have the number, even before the card arrives [K0023]:
      1. Install "Kuwait Mobile ID" from Google Play, the App Store or Huawei AppGallery, then tap "Get started", then "registration" [K0073, K0074].
      2. Choose "enter data manually" and type your Civil ID number and passport number; then type your email and mobile number and tap "next" [K0075, K0024, K0076].
      3. Take a face photo with the front camera; after it is verified you get an OTP (one-time password) by SMS; enter it [K0026].
      4. Set a PIN of 4 to 12 digits, not in sequence or repeated, and confirm it [K0025].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is the first Civil ID still KD 5 in 2026, and does the employer pay it or deduct it?
   > - Where do new Article 18 workers collect their cards in 2026 (the South Surra machines, other PACI branches), and does the company often collect them for workers?
   > - Is the Civil ID still issued for the residence period, and how long does a first card take after the residence is approved?

6. **Open a bank account and get a SIM.**
   - **Bank account.** Since January 2025 the Central Bank of Kuwait requires banks to open accounts for everyone, including low-paid service and craft workers, and forbids refusing an account because of salary [K0034, K0035]. Banks must open these "worker accounts" when asked but do not advertise them, so you may have to ask [K0036]. An employer with five or more workers must pay wages into each worker's bank account (see Sending money home) [G0029].
     - **ABK's Al Amel account** (Al Ahli Bank of Kuwait) is for company workers earning less than KD 300, expatriates only, with dedicated branches in Sabhan and Salmiya [K0037, K0038, K0039]. You'll need: a letter from your company asking ABK to open the account, the company's commercial licence and signature authorisation, copies of your Civil ID, passport (with the address page) and work permit, and the account form [K0040, K0041, K0042]. It gives a free debit card for 5 years; salaries from other banks can't be paid into it [K0043, K0044].
     - Ordinary salary accounts often need a higher salary: Burgan Bank's Al Rateb account needs at least KD 400 for expatriates [K0045]. Bank loans for expatriates start at a salary of KD 600 [K0046].
   - **SIM card.** Residents register a SIM with their Civil ID; before you have it, a visitor's SIM is registered on the original passport [K0047, K0048]. Zain, Ooredoo and stc must verify every new customer's identity [K0049].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Can a new worker open any bank account before his Civil ID is issued?
   > - Which banks other than ABK actually open accounts for KD 75-150 workers in 2026, and what do they charge?
   > - Do employers keep workers' ATM cards or PINs, and where does a worker complain?
   > - Does a new SIM in 2026 need a fingerprint or Kuwait Mobile ID check, and can a worker have SIMs in his name used by others (a known police risk)?

### Nurses and doctors

(Extends W's "Nurses and doctors" under "Work visa: professional and skilled".)

Kuwait is the main overseas employer of Bangladeshi nurses [K0052]. The route runs government to government through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government's own recruiting agency) [K0053, K0060].

- **How it started:** in December 2021 two Kuwaiti companies with MOH approval, City Group General Trading and Advanced Technology Company, asked for 998 nurses through BOESL; a panel of Kuwait's MOH and the companies selected 654 nurses in Bangladesh in January 2022 [K0061, K0062]. The first 50 arrived in June 2022 for MOH hospitals, officially free of cost [K0063]. They went without migration cost, at Tk 80,000 to 90,000 a month with overtime and a yearly increment [K0065, K0064]. In May 2023 the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait signed an MoU with Kuwait's health ministry on recruiting medical professionals [K0060]. By the end of 2024, 677 Bangladeshi nurses were working in Kuwait's public hospitals [K0059].
- **The 2025 round:** in October 2025 Kuwait asked BOESL for 100 nurses, needing English, at least 3 years' experience, technical skills and basic life support certification; pay offered was up to Tk 1.27 lakh for BSc and Tk 1.11 lakh for diploma nurses [K0053, K0054, K0055]. Of the 100 who sat the exam, 15 were selected, and by May 2026 only five had finished visa processing [K0056, K0057]. BOESL said Kuwait had not given clearance because of the Middle East tensions [K0058].
- **The licence:** a Kuwait MOH licence is required for any nursing job [W0080]. Consultancies describe the route as: your credentials are checked by Dataflow (primary source verification), which cannot be waived; the employer files the licence application with the Dataflow report on MOH's portal and pays the fees online; then you sit a computer-based Prometric exam [K0066, K0067, K0068]. Nurses need 3 years' experience in hospital inpatient care [K0069]. Consultancies also list a good standing certificate dated within 6 months, a criminal background check and vaccination records [K0070].

> **Need to know**
> - Is BOESL recruiting nurses or doctors for Kuwait now, and where does it post the circular (boesl.gov.bd showed no Kuwait notice on 9 November 2026)?
> - Do BOESL nurses take the Kuwait MOH Prometric exam, or does MOH's selection panel replace it?
> - What do the Dataflow check and the MOH licence cost a Bangladeshi nurse, and who pays?
> - Do private agencies sell "nurse" Article 18 visas to unlicensed Bangladeshis, and what happens to them?
