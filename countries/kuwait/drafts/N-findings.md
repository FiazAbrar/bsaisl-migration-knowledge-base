## a. Draft doc text

### Is it open now, and who can go?

**Company (Article 18) work visas are open to Bangladeshis again since May 2025, after 18 years in which they were issued only by special approval; new domestic-worker (Article 20) visas are closed to Bangladeshis, men and women, as of October 2026** [N0014, N0015, N0026, N0027]. The Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait is not attesting visas for women domestic workers (খাদ্দামা) until the two governments sign an agreement, and it asks women not to go as domestic workers until then [N0031, N0097].

#### Status

- **Private-sector company job (Article 18):** open; since May 2025 every category is issued to Bangladeshis by the normal process, and the embassy was receiving about 450 visa attestation requests a day in mid-2025 [N0014, N0024]. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) cleared 4,538 workers for Kuwait in September 2026 [N0062].
- **Domestic worker, house driver, cook (Article 20, the "khadem visa", খাদেম ভিসা):** closed to new Bangladeshi workers: a new Interior Ministry rule allows new domestic workers, men and women, from 10 listed countries only, and Bangladesh is not one of them [N0034, N0027, N0026]. Domestic workers already in Kuwait legally can still renew their job and residence (আকামা) [N0033].
- **Women domestic workers:** the embassy cannot attest a khaddama visa until a memorandum of understanding (MOU) is signed, and in the three to four months before October 2026 it brought home seven women who had gone illegally [N0031, N0032].
- **Family, visit and tourist visas:** see Visit visas (tourist, family, business) and Family visa (dependants).

#### The channels

Almost everyone goes on a visa bought through a chain: the worker pays the whole cost to an agency or a broker, who arranges everything, including processing through the agencies the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorises (see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut) [N0013, N0002]. Until May 2025 most Bangladeshis went on "akhud" (আখুদ, from the Arabic for contracts) visas for government projects, mostly with cleaning companies, while the "ahli" (আহলি, private company) visa was closed outright [N0067, N0068, N0088]. Nurses had a government-to-government route: 670 Bangladeshi nurses were hired under a 2024 MOU (see Work visa: professional and skilled) [N0060].

#### Who qualifies

The embassy attests a visa only if the salary meets the minimum it set from 1 August 2025 (see What the job pays); age, medical and skill rules are in the steps (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [N0085, N0086].

#### Size

- **Bangladeshis in Kuwait:** 333,100 at the end of June 2026 by the count of PACI (Public Authority for Civil Information, which issues the Civil ID), the third-largest foreign community after Indians (1.06 million) and Egyptians (about 670,000), and ahead of Filipinos (226,500) and Pakistanis (105,700) [N0076, N0077].
- **At work:** Bangladeshis are 9.9% of Kuwait's whole workforce (Indians 28.8%, Egyptians 14.6%, Filipinos 6.6%, Sri Lankans 5.8%, Nepalese 5.7%) and 11.2% of private-sector employees [N0078, N0079].
- **Domestic workers:** 106,942 Bangladeshis, 12.1% of 883,308 domestic workers, against 344,532 Indians, 157,901 Sri Lankans and 151,369 Filipinos [N0028, N0029, N0030].
- **Departures:** BMET counted 49,604 workers for Kuwait in 2017 alone, then only 52,135 in the four and a half years from January 2018 to August 2022 [N0063]. For 2025, the press gives 72,717 or 42,716 (the official figure is in Step 1) [N0064, N0065].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the OEP (Overseas Employment Platform) clearance total for Kuwait for 2025 and January-October 2026?

#### What closed it before

- **2007:** Kuwait stopped recruiting Bangladeshis after detecting recruitment irregularities [N0035].
- **2014-2016:** the ban was lifted in 2014; in May 2016 Kuwait restricted the hiring of male domestic workers [N0036].
- **March 2018:** the Interior Minister halted work visas for Bangladeshis again, after security reports that Bangladeshis had become a target of iqama (residence) traders and that their numbers had grown through the illegal trade in work and residence permits [N0037, N0040]. Bangladesh's labour attaché said then that many Bangladeshis were not working for their original sponsors [N0039].
- **2020-2021, the Papul case:** see Who controls it: the Papul case. In November 2021 Bangladesh was one of seven nationalities (with Iraq, Yemen, Iran, Syria, Pakistan and Sudan) refused business, work and family visas except with special Interior Ministry approval [N0041]. Under that rule a Bangladeshi visa needed the "lamana" (লামানা, no-objection) procedure: an application to the minister, made through brokers, which could take years [N0071].
- **May 2025:** all categories reopened to Bangladeshis [N0014].
- **October 2026:** new domestic-worker visas closed (see Status) [N0026].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A Kuwait visa costs the employer little: the visa processing fee is 3 KWD and the embassy attestation fee another 3 KWD, and the employer spends about 250 KWD more on the work permit and other costs [N0017, N0018]. A company hiring for its own need pays the visa, ticket and everything else, and there is no government price for a worker visa [N0020]. In practice that 6-dinar visa is sold through brokers for 2,000 to 2,200 KWD, about Tk 8-9 lakh (July 2025), and the difference goes to brokers [N0016, N0070]. Kuwait blamed its 2018 ban on exactly this trade [N0037, N0040].

#### The visa trade in Kuwait

- **Where the visa comes from:** brokers get visas by tempting Kuwaiti citizens with money and sell them to Bangladeshis [N0019].
- **Paper companies:** in October 2026 the Interior Minister said about 10,000 companies, each with about five workers, had not paid their foreign workers for months, and PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) had suspended 8,849 company files by June 2026 [N0080, N0083]. PAM's Director General says some companies register fictitious salaries, and that PAM will close the files of companies that don't pay [N0082].
- **Who runs it:** a Bangladeshi press club leader in Kuwait says an organised broker ring controls the visa trade, which is why Bangladeshis pay more than other South Asians [N0021]. Bangladesh's ambassador, Maj Gen Syed Tareq Hossain, held meetings to hold visa prices to Tk 4-5 lakh at most, but brokers set their own price [N0022].
- **Prosecutions:** in November 2026 Kuwait's Criminal Court jailed a Bangladeshi sanitation worker for four years with hard labour, and two Egyptians for five, for trading residence permits through a company, forgery and bribery; the company, a goods transport firm, was fined KD 10,000 per worker and lost its licence [N0056, N0057].

> **Need to know**

> - How much of the 2,000-2,200 KWD does the Kuwaiti sponsor keep?

#### The authorised processing agencies: the toll gate

A worker may get a demand letter alone or through any agency, but the visa must be processed by one of the agencies the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorises [N0002]. The embassy has authorised at least 10; the owner of one, Modern Overseas, says 14 or 15 [N0003, N0004]. In early 2025 these agencies were accused of charging USD 150-300 (Tk 17,000-35,000) per visa against an official processing fee of Tk 6,270 [N0001]. A Kuwait-bound worker's relative complained to the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) on 28 January 2025, saying his nephew paid about Tk 33,000 for processing [N0098, N0099]. The agencies deny charging extra, and SB International's owner says Kuwait decides which agencies process visas [N0005]. An unlicensed agency owner says some syndicates survived August 2024 and still take an extra USD 300 per worker [N0012]. Complaints to the Chief Adviser's Office estimate that USD 30-37 million was extracted from about 134,000 workers who went to Kuwait from 2018 to early 2025 [N0011].

> **Need to know**
> - Which agencies does the Kuwait Embassy in Dhaka authorise to process work visas in 2026, and where is the list published?
> - What does a processing agency charge per visa in 2026?

#### The embassy's checks in Kuwait

Since mid-2025 the employer, or his registered representative, must come to the Bangladesh Embassy in person to have a visa attested, and the embassy refuses attestation below its minimum salaries (see What the job pays; how a worker checks the attestation is in Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [N0025, N0086].

#### Who controls it: the Papul case

- **The arrest:** Kuwait's Criminal Investigation Department arrested Mohammad Shahid Islam "Kazi" Papul, then a Bangladeshi MP, on 6 June 2020 and charged him with human trafficking, money laundering and bribing officials; the Interior Minister called it one of the largest trafficking and residence cases [N0100, N0048]. He was charged with taking money from dozens of workers to bring them to Kuwait through a company he managed, a cleaning company [N0044, N0050]. Kuwaiti media reported that he said he paid one Interior Ministry official 1.1 million dinars by cheque and another one million in cash [N0049].
- **The sentences:** a criminal court gave him four years and a KD 1.9 million fine in February 2021; in November 2021 the top appeals court made it seven years, a fine reported as KD 2.7 million, and deportation after the sentence [N0045, N0042, N0046]. The Interior Ministry undersecretary Maj Gen Mazen Al Jarrah, a manpower director and the head of PAM were jailed in the same case [N0043, N0047].
- **The Bangladesh side:** court sources said the accountant of Job Bank International (RL1183), an agency owned by Papul's brother Kazi Badrul, confessed that he sent people to Kuwait through it on Badrul's orders, to be received by Papul's company Marafie Kuwaitia; the ring found people through brokers across the country and charged each Tk 5-7 lakh (Daily Star, August 2020) [N0051, N0052]. Bangladesh's CID (Criminal Investigation Department) found that the family and accomplices laundered Tk 38.22 crore [N0053]. Job Bank International is still listed Active on BMET's licence list, valid to 31 December 2031 [N0093].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

People pay Tk 7-10 lakh for a Kuwait visa against a cap of about Tk 1.1 lakh, while Indians and Nepalis pay Tk 50,000 to 1.5 lakh [N0069, N0006, N0023]. The commonest job, cleaning, paid 75 KWD a month in 2023; the embassy's minimum for new visas since August 2025 is 90 to 150 KWD [N0068, N0087].

#### The cap

A Ministry official said in July 2025 that the most an agency may take for Kuwait and five other Middle East countries is Tk 1.15 to 1.20 lakh, covering the agency fee, visa processing, medical and airfare [N0023]. Insiders quoted earlier in 2025 gave the government rate as Tk 1.06 lakh (the current figure and what it covers are in Step 1) [N0006].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the Ministry's current cap for Kuwait, and the order number and date that set it?

#### What people really pay

- **2025:** Tk 8-9 lakh (2,000-2,200 KWD) for a worker visa (July 2025) [N0016].
- **2023:** Tk 7-10 lakh, with the visa alone at Tk 7 lakh [N0069, N0070].
- **2016-2020:** an average of Tk 560,000 among 292 men surveyed, 525% above the official fee of Tk 106,780 [N0007].
- **In the Papul ring (2016-2020):** Tk 5-7 lakh each [N0052].

#### How it is paid

The worker pays the agency or broker one sum for everything, including processing [N0013].

#### What the job pays

The Bangladesh Embassy's minimum monthly salaries for new visas, from 1 August 2025, for an 8-hour day [N0085, N0087]:

- unskilled worker on a government contract ("akud hukumi"): 90 KWD;
- unskilled worker on a private-company contract ("akud ahli"): 120 KWD;
- domestic worker: 120 KWD;
- house driver or cook: 150 KWD;
- skilled driver: 120 KWD;
- skilled worker: 150 KWD.

Before this, most Bangladeshis worked for cleaning companies at 75 KWD [N0068]. Because that pay is low, about 90% of Bangladeshis on such visas took illegal part-time work after their 8-hour shift (car washing, selling vegetables, shops, offices), which is forbidden and gets some sent home (see What can get you jailed or deported) [N0072, N0074, N0090].

#### What goes unpaid

- **Paper employers:** about 10,000 small companies had not paid foreign workers for months (October 2026), and the Interior Minister set the 20th of each month as the payment deadline (see If you aren't paid) [N0080, N0081].
- **Large contractors too:** in July 2019 about 4,000 mostly Asian workers of a Kuwait Oil Company contractor sat in protest at four months' unpaid pay, and some had become illegal because the company had not renewed their residence [N0084].
- **No work at all:** a cleaner who went in 2018 sat eight months in the company barracks without work, borrowing for food [N0073].

#### Money sent home

Workers in Kuwait sent USD 1.5 billion home in 2024 (see Sending money home) [N0010].

#### How long the debt takes

Across all destinations the average migration cost (Tk 4.17 lakh in a 2020 government survey) took 17.6 months to recover [N0054]. A Kuwait cleaner who went in 2018 was still repaying after four and a half years [N0075].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The harms reported most are a visa bought at a high price with no real job or sponsor behind it, and pay that doesn't come: workers arrive to find no work, a visa called fake, or a sponsor with no job [N0073, N0008, N0091]. In October 2026 about 10,000 companies had not paid their workers for months [N0080].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A free visa (ফ্রি ভিসা / আজাদ ভিসা) lets me work anywhere."** No such visa exists in Kuwait: every visa is tied to a sponsor, and working outside that sponsor or company is an offence punished by fine, jail or deportation [N0089, N0090]. Workers sold one find on arrival a sponsor and no guaranteed job [N0091].
- **"I paid in full, so my visa is good."** A worker who reached Kuwait Oil Al Naseef Company in October 2024 was told his visa was fake and that he still owed money for it, or he would be sent home (how to check a visa is in Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [N0008].
- **"Part-time work on the side is normal, so it's safe."** It is common but illegal, and workers caught are sent home [N0072, N0074].
- **"Women can go to Kuwait as domestic workers."** Not now: the embassy is not attesting khaddama visas and asks women not to go until an MOU is signed [N0031, N0097].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **Women sent as domestic workers outside the attested route:** the embassy says rings are sending women illegally, and it brought home seven who went that way in three to four months (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [N0101, N0032].
- **A visa with no working employer behind it:** it ends in unpaid wages and, when the company does not renew the residence, illegal status (see Undocumented workers and amnesties) [N0084].
- **Buying or trading residence permits:** it ends in prison: a Bangladeshi was jailed four years with hard labour in November 2026 [N0056].

### Scams

(For "Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)".)

#### Done to workers

- **The "free visa":** a ring in Bangladesh sells visas as "free" for lakhs of taka; on arrival the visa is tied to a sponsor with no job [N0089, N0091]. Bangladesh's ambassador met community leaders in May 2025 to warn against it [N0092].
- **The fake or unpaid visa:** the company says on arrival that the visa is fake or not fully paid, and demands money from the broker or sends the worker home [N0008].
- **The processing overcharge:** USD 150-300 above the Tk 6,270 fee (see The authorised processing agencies: the toll gate) [N0001].
- **Women's domestic jobs:** offered by rings despite the closure; the embassy cannot attest them [N0101, N0031].

#### Tried by workers

- **Working outside your sponsor:** fine, jail or deportation [N0090].
- **Buying a residence renewal or a forged extension:** prison with hard labour (four years in the November 2026 case), and the company's licence cancelled [N0056, N0057].
