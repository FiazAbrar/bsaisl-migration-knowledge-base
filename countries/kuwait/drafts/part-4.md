## Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)

Article 20 is Kuwait's household visa: Bangladeshis call it the "khadem visa", and the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait calls it the "Domestic Aid visa" or Visa-20 [N0034, D0022, D0024]. It covers housemaids, house drivers, cooks and other household staff who work for a private person (the sponsor, or kafeel), not a company [D0021, D0024]. These workers come under the Domestic Workers Law (Law 68 of 2015), not the private-sector Labour Law [D0045, D0050]. The Public Authority for Manpower (PAM), Kuwait's labour authority, handles their contracts and complaints through its Domestic Labour Office (DLO), also called the Domestic Workers Employment Department [D0060, D0084, D0101]. About 106,942 Bangladeshis work in Kuwait's homes, 12.1% of all domestic workers there [N0028].

### Who can go now

New Article 20 visas are closed to Bangladeshis, men and women [N0026, N0027, D0001, D0002].

- **Everyone: Bangladesh is not on the approved list.** In June 2026 Kuwait's Ministry of Interior (MOI) limited new domestic-worker recruitment to 10 countries (South Africa, Benin, Eritrea, Ethiopia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Nepal, and Senegal for men only), and Bangladesh is not one of them [D0001, D0002, N0027].
- **Women: the Embassy will not attest.** Bangladesh and Kuwait have signed no agreement on women domestic workers, so the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait cannot attest any women's domestic-worker ("khaddama") visa, and it asks women not to go as domestic workers until one is signed [D0011, D0012, N0031, N0097]. Bangladeshi women who work legally in Kuwait go on company (Article 18) visas, mostly as cleaners (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)) [D0017].
- **Entry visas held before April 2026 are dead.** In April 2026, during the Iran war, Kuwait stopped issuing entry visas, and a domestic-work entry visa that could not be used could not be extended; the sponsor must apply for a new one once issuing resumes [S0012, S0013].
- **Workers already in Kuwait are not affected.** A domestic worker in Kuwait legally can still renew the job and residence (akama) [N0033].

Household visas have been shut to Bangladeshis before: Kuwait reopened them in May 2016 for men only, one per citizen, and suspended them in March 2018 because of visa trading [D0019, D0020].

### What it costs and who pays

- **You pay nothing to the recruiter.** The law forbids licensed recruitment offices and their partners, in Kuwait or abroad, from charging a domestic worker any fee [D0045]. An agency approved by Bangladesh to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from them [D0116].
- **The sponsor pays for the visa and papers.** The Embassy's contract says the sponsor gives the visa free and pays the residence, Civil ID (Kuwait's resident identity card) and driving licence [D0032, D0035].
- **The sponsor pays the recruitment office.** In January 2024 Kuwait capped what an office may charge a household, travel included, at KD 750 (about Tk 3 lakh) for a worker from Asia [D0064, G0002]. In July 2024 Al-Durra, the state-backed recruitment company, charged KD 180 (about Tk 72,500), ticket included, for a driver or cook [D0066, G0002].

### How a household in Kuwait recruits

1. **The sponsor checks how many workers he may sponsor.** Since June 2026 a single man may sponsor one driver only, a single woman one domestic worker, a married, divorced or widowed woman with children one worker and one driver, and a man with a family up to three workers and one driver [D0006, D0007, D0008].
2. **The sponsor applies on Sahel,** the government's app: since May 2026 citizens can issue entry visas for new house drivers there, and since June 2026 citizens and residents can manage domestic-worker and driver services there [D0009, D0005].
3. **Or the sponsor uses a licensed recruitment office.** PAM had 488 registered offices in May 2025 and suspended 25 that month [D0067]. An office guarantees the worker for 6 months, and must refund the sponsor and send the worker home in set cases, including the worker refusing to continue [D0056, D0097].
4. **The sponsor gets the visa attested at the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait** (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Step 3) [D0024].

### Step 1: Check you qualify

1. **Women: you cannot go to Kuwait as a domestic worker now** (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Who can go now) [D0012, N0031]. For other countries, Bangladesh requires women domestic workers to take a one-month (24 working days) housekeeping course at a Technical Training Centre (TTC) run by BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) and to be 25 to 45 at enrolment (returnees up to 50) [D0113, D0114]. You cannot enrol if pregnant, if your youngest child is under 6, or with a contagious or incurable disease [D0115].
2. **Men: check your age.** Recruitment offices may not place domestic workers under 21 or over 60 without a ministerial exception [D0055].

### Step 2: Check the visa, the sponsor and the contract

1. **Check it is a single Visa-20 from one named person.** The Embassy attests a Visa-20 only for a personal sponsor, and a single man may sponsor only a driver [D0024, D0007].
2. **Check the wage is at least the Embassy's minimum.** Since 1 August 2025 the Embassy checks the salary in the work permit before attesting a visa: Visa-20 house boys and general workers at least KD 120 a month (about Tk 48,400), and cooks and drivers KD 150 (about Tk 60,500), all-inclusive [M0014, M0016, M0017, G0002]. Kuwait's own legal minimum for domestic workers is lower, KD 60 a month [D0052, D0054].
3. **Read the contract before you pay or travel.** The Embassy's attestation contract (form updated 5 March 2025) says [D0032]:
   - the visa is free [D0032]
   - a KD 10 pay rise each year [D0033]
   - 30 days' paid annual leave [D0034]
   - a free air ticket to Kuwait, and home at the end [D0038]
   - food or at least KD 20 a month food allowance, and free housing [D0040]
   - the sponsor will not take your passport [D0039]
   - the sponsor will not file any case against you without telling the Embassy [D0041]
   - the sponsor helps you register with the Embassy within 15 days of arrival [D0042]
   - either side can end the contract early with 3 months' written notice [D0037]

### Step 3: The sponsor gets the visa attested at the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait

The worker cannot do this step: the Embassy attests a Visa-20 only with the sponsor, or his attorney, present [D0024].

1. **The sponsor comes in person,** or sends the holder of his power of attorney (towkil khas) or his authorisation letter [D0024].
2. **The sponsor brings:**
   - the attestation contract form, signed by the sponsor (free from Room 3) [D0026]
   - 2 copies of the visa [D0027]
   - 2 photos of the worker [D0027]
   - a copy of the sponsor's passport with the signature page [D0025]
   - the sponsor's original Civil ID and a copy [D0028]
   - the power of attorney or authorisation letter, if the sponsor does not come [D0024]
3. **The sponsor pays KD 3 per worker** (non-refundable), in cash or by card at Room 3, Sunday to Thursday, 8 am to 2 pm; the attested visa is ready at 4 pm the same day [D0029, D0030, D0031].

### Step 4: BMET clearance, medical and the flight

1. **Get BMET clearance yourself.** A worker on a single visa applies at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district office with the visa copy and contract (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 6) [B0086]. Source tax is Tk 250 on an attested single visa and Tk 400 on an unattested one [B0085].
2. **Do the Wafid medical check and the 3-day pre-departure orientation** as a company worker does (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5) [W0070, B0089].

### Step 5: After you arrive

1. **Register with the Bangladesh Embassy within 15 days;** the sponsor must help (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Step 2) [D0042].
2. **Get paid every month with proof.** PAM requires each month's salary to be documented by payment voucher or bank transfer [D0063, D0046].
3. **Keep your passport.** The employer may not keep your passport or Civil ID unless you agree [D0048, D0039].

### Your rights as a domestic worker

**Pay:**
- at least KD 60 a month under Kuwaiti law, and the Embassy minimum in your contract (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Step 2) [D0052, M0016, M0017]
- paid at the end of each month, with no deductions [D0046, D0057]
- food, clothes, medical treatment and housing paid by the employer [D0047]

**Hours, rest and leave:**
- at most 12 hours a day, with rest [D0049]
- a weekly day off [D0049]
- 30 days' paid annual leave [D0050, D0034]

**Documents, phone and safety:**
- your passport and Civil ID stay with you unless you agree otherwise [D0048]
- you may own and use a mobile phone outside working hours [D0061]
- no dangerous or degrading work [D0058]
- no work outside Kuwait without your consent [D0062]

**End of contract:**
- end-of-service pay of one month's salary per year of service [D0059]
- your ticket home, paid by the employer [D0062, D0038]

In practice many domestic workers work over 12 hours without overtime, have wages withheld, lose rest days and end-of-service pay, and have their contracts switched [D0089]. Employers often keep passports [D0083]. PAM may not inspect private homes [D0085].

### If you aren't paid or are abused

Complain in this order [D0107, D0101, D0098, D0044]:

1. **Go to the police if you are hurt.** File a complaint and insist on a medical examination, so your injuries are recorded [D0107].
2. **Go to PAM's Domestic Labour Office** in Rumaithiya, Block 7, Street 78, behind the main co-operative society, with your Civil ID, passport number and contact details [D0101]. PAM takes visitors from 10:30 am to 1:30 pm, and its hotline is 103 [D0100]. A release, cancellation or visa-change complaint is filed in person there, and you get a hearing date [D0108].
3. **Or complain on PAM's online form.** It takes complaints against the employer about late salary, dues, documents, leaving the country or transfer, and lets you apply to extend your residence while the complaint is open [D0098, D0099]. It is in Arabic only and asks for the employer's details [D0084].
4. **Tell the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing** in Messila (Block 7, Street 16, Villas 91 and 93; phone +965 23900913), which advises workers and forwards written complaints to Bangladesh [D0044, M0024].
5. **Appeal a PAM refusal** at the Central Administration Office in Abu Fatira within 15 days [D0110].

#### Shelters

- **Women:** the government shelter in Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh housed 1,252 women in the first half of 2025 [D0077]. Women come referred by PAM or an embassy, or on their own; residents keep their phones and can go out [D0078, D0079].
- **Men:** PAM's shelter in Hawally (opened 23 February 2025, 200 to 300 places) takes domestic and company workers, walk-in or referred, even if they have broken the residence or labour law [D0073, D0074, D0075].
- **What a shelter does for you:** once you are admitted, the employer cannot file an absconding report, and you can change employer or go home without the old sponsor's approval [D0080]. If no final absconding report was filed, you stay until the dispute is settled and then choose to go home or pursue the case [D0076].
- **No Bangladesh safe house in Kuwait:** the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB) runs safe houses only in Saudi Arabia, Oman, Jordan, Libya and Lebanon [D0112].

### If your sponsor reports you absent

A household sponsor must report a domestic worker who leaves to MOI within two weeks, at an MOI service centre and then at PAM, which also frees him from paying your ticket [D0095, D0096]. The report often shows on the system 15 to 20 days after it is filed [D0105]. A worker who leaves without the sponsor's consent can be detained, fined or jailed, and once deported cannot re-enter Kuwait [D0051, D0106].

1. **If you are escaping abuse, go to a shelter:** once you are admitted, the employer cannot file an absconding report (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), Shelters) [D0080].
2. **Register your case at the DLO and with the Embassy's labour wing** (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), If you aren't paid or are abused) [D0101, D0044].

### Changing household or leaving

#### Another household

You cannot move to another household without your sponsor's release, and PAM cannot override him [D0102, D0082]. If the release is approved, finish the transfer to the new sponsor within one month; otherwise you must cancel your residence and leave, because the old sponsor must then report you absent [D0109, D0111].

#### A company job (Article 18)

No move from Article 20 to a company visa is open, and you cannot switch after arrival [D0071, W0078]. The last window, from 14 July to 12 September 2024, needed the sponsor's approval, at least one year with him, and KD 50 plus KD 10 per year of service; about 55,000 domestic workers moved [D0071, D0069, D0070, D0072].

> **Need to know**
> - Is PAM planning another Article 20 to Article 18 window? [D0071]

#### Going home

- **No exit permit:** domestic workers do not need the exit permit that company workers get from their employer on Sahel [D0094, S0020].
- **If the sponsor holds your passport:** complain at the DLO; with no case against you, cancelling your residence takes one to two months, then you fly home [D0104].
- **Abuse, unpaid salary, illness or family problems:** the DLO helps send you home [D0103].
- **Claim your dues before you leave** (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), If you aren't paid or are abused): some workers have gone home without owed wages during long disputes [D0098, D0093].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

- **"Khaddama" visas for women:** syndicates send Bangladeshi women to Kuwait as domestic workers illegally, and many end up abused or confined [D0013, N0101]. The Embassy rescued and sent home 7 such women in the three months to July 2026, and asks anyone who knows of a syndicate to tell the Embassy or Bangladesh police [D0014, D0015].
- **"Free visa":** there is no free visa in Kuwait; an Article 20 visa ties you to your sponsor's home, and working for anyone else is illegal [D0022, D0023].
- **Being sold on:** employers have "sold" domestic workers to other employers on social media, while the Embassy contract forbids moving you to another sponsor without your and the Embassy's agreement [D0087, D0036].

#### Tried by workers

- **Taking outside work on a khadem visa:** it is illegal, and leaving the sponsor's home gets you reported absent (Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20), If your sponsor reports you absent) [D0023, D0095].

## Work visa: professional and skilled

Professionals come on the same Article 18 work permit and residence as any company worker (see Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18)) [W0009]. The difference is that the job title on the permit must match a recognised degree [W0051, W0047].

### Degree attestation

1. **Attest the degree in Bangladesh:** by the university, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and finally the Kuwait Embassy [W0045].
2. **Get it stamped in Kuwait** by Kuwait's Ministry of Foreign Affairs [W0046].
3. **The degree goes for equivalency** to Kuwait's Ministry of Higher Education [W0044]. When a work permit is issued, renewed or changed through PAM's online systems for employers (Ashal or Sahel Business), PAM records the degree as unrecognised, preliminary recognition, administrative recognition or accredited [W0050].
4. **PAM checks that your field of study matches the profession** on the work permit [W0051]. Renewing a permit for the same profession needs no new qualification papers [W0053].

### Engineers

An engineering job title from PAM is needed both for the work permit and for the profession on the Civil ID [W0047]. The Kuwait Society of Engineers (KSE) no longer runs an exam; PAM and the Ministry of Higher Education check the degree from documents [W0044, W0048]. Some employers still ask for KSE membership, but it does not replace government recognition [W0049]. An engineering permit application without the required approval is rejected automatically [W0052].

### Nurses and doctors

Kuwait is the main overseas employer of Bangladeshi nurses, and the route runs government to government through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government's own recruiting agency) [K0052, K0053, K0060]. In May 2023 the Bangladesh Embassy signed an agreement with Kuwait's Ministry of Health (MOH) on recruiting medical staff, and by the end of 2024, 677 Bangladeshi nurses worked in Kuwait's public hospitals [K0060, K0059].

- **The first round:** in 2022 BOESL nurses went to MOH hospitals without migration cost, on Tk 80,000 to 90,000 a month with overtime and a yearly increment [K0063, K0064].
- **The 2025 round:** in October 2025 Kuwait asked BOESL for 100 nurses with English, at least 3 years' experience and basic life support certification, offering up to Tk 1.27 lakh for BSc and Tk 1.11 lakh for diploma nurses [K0053, K0054, K0055]. Of 100 who sat the exam, 15 were selected, and by May 2026 only five had finished visa processing, because Kuwait had not given clearance during the Middle East tensions [K0056, K0057, K0058].

1. **Come only on Article 18.** Nurses, home nurses and caretakers may be recruited only on Article 18, never Article 20 [W0077].
2. **Get the MOH licence before you work.** A Kuwait MOH licence is required for any nursing job, including assistant nurse, and working without one can lead to deportation and jail [W0080]. Dataflow, a credential-checking company, verifies your certificates; the employer files the licence application with the Dataflow report on MOH's portal and pays online; then you sit a computer-based Prometric exam [K0066, K0067, K0068]. Nurses need 3 years' hospital inpatient experience, a good-standing certificate dated within 6 months, a criminal record check and vaccination records [K0069, K0070].
3. **Keep your original certificates.** Hospitals and agencies in Kuwait have confiscated nurses' certificates and refused release or transfer [W0079].
4. **If hired inside Kuwait, get an MOH-attested contract** and check that the hospital has a valid MOH licence and an MOH/PAM quota [W0081].

### Driving licences

A foreigner needs more than two years' legal residence, a university degree and a salary of at least KD 600 a month (about Tk 2.4 lakh) for a private driving licence [W0054, G0002].

**Exempt from all three:**
- government drivers and agents, and general delegates (mandoubs), with a valid licence from home [W0085]
- domestic workers who worked two years for the same sponsor and changed their residence profession to driver [W0055]
- oil-sector technicians and specialists, and medical technicians [W0086, W0056]

Engineers and doctors with a degree are exempt from the residence and salary conditions, and managers and accountants with a degree earning over KD 600 from the two-year condition only [W0057, W0058]. The licence is cancelled if you move to a job not on the list or your residence is cancelled [W0059].
