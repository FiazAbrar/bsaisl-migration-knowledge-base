### For Background, "What you pay, and what you earn and keep" → #### Money sent home

Workers in Kuwait sent Bangladesh USD 1,763.7 million through banks and exchange houses in FY2025-26 (July-June), up from USD 1,496.7 million in FY2023-24 and down from USD 1,886.5 million in FY2020-21 [G0015]. That is about 5% of the USD 35,589 million Bangladesh received from all countries in FY2025-26 [G0015, G0016]. In July-September 2026 Kuwait was Bangladesh's seventh-largest source, USD 441.8 million [G0004]. On Bangladesh Bank's measure, sending from Kuwait cost 1.51% of the amount on average in April-June 2026, and 3.91% for sums up to USD 200 [G0007, G0006]. How to send, and what hundi costs a family, is in Sending money home.

### ## Sending money home

**Send through a bank or an exchange company licensed by the Central Bank of Kuwait (CBK), never hundi.** Hundi (hawala: money passed outside the banking system through private agents) is a crime in Kuwait under anti-money-laundering Law 106 of 2013, and is illegal in Bangladesh too [G0070, G0074]. Hundi brokers in Bangladesh pay out through bKash and Nagad accounts (mobile-money wallets) [G0072]. Bangladesh's Financial Intelligence Unit (BFIU) had frozen or suspended about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling by June 2026, and decided in August 2026 to freeze about 14,000 more [G0071]. Money sent legally earns the family Bangladesh's 2.5% cash incentive, kept in the FY2026-27 budget, and sometimes a further 2.5% from the bank; hundi gets neither [G0017, G0013].

#### Your salary and bank account

Under Article 57 of the Private Sector Labour Law (Law 6/2010), an employer with five or more workers must pay wages into each worker's bank account and send proof to PAM (the Public Authority for Manpower) [G0029]. PAM checks salary transfers every month; a company that pays late can have its PAM file suspended, which stops it hiring until it pays or enters in PAM's Ashal system the legal reason for any unpaid salary, and lifts automatically once it pays [G0029, G0030, G0032]. A suspended file does not stop existing workers renewing their permits or transferring to another employer [G0031]. Employers send payroll to the banks through the "Sahel for Companies" and "Sahel for Banks" platforms [G0033]. Since 28 September 2026 the CBK's Kuwait Wage Payment System (KWPS) processes private-sector wage transfers through local banks; the CBK runs it and PAM uses it to watch employers' compliance in real time and catch unpaid wages early [G0027, G0028]. Fishing, farm and livestock employers had until the end of January 2026 to start paying through banks [G0034].

Since January 2025 the CBK has told banks to open accounts for every customer, including low-paid, craft and domestic workers, and not to refuse anyone for a low salary; banks must open such "worker accounts" on request but do not advertise them (opening one: see Step 7: After arrival) [G0077, G0078]. A month earlier, Al-Rai reported that take-up for domestic workers was still slow [G0036].

#### Step by step: sending through an exchange company

You'll need:
- to send online, the Kuwait Mobile ID app [G0066];
- for a bKash wallet, the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered [G0011].

1. **Check the company is licensed.** Open cbk.gov.kw, Supervision, Regulated Entities, Exchange Companies, and press "Show All": the CBK lists 31 licensed exchange companies, each with its address and phone number [G0009]. A shop or person not on the list is not licensed [G0009, G0069]. In 2025 the CBK cleared unlicensed "exchange brokers" out of the market, and on 25 October 2026 it announced penalties on several licensed exchange companies [G0069, G0040].
2. **Choose the company.** bKash lists these partners in Kuwait: Al Mulla, Al Muzaini Exchange, Aman Exchange, ARY Exchange, Joyalukkas, Kuwait Bahrain International Exchange, Trans-Fast, Western Union and MoneyGram [G0010]. In the World Bank's July-August 2025 survey, sending about KD 65 (about Tk 26,200) cost a fee of KD 0.90 at Lulu Money and KD 1.25 at Al Mulla, Aman, Al Muzaini, Bahrain Exchange Company, Western Union and MoneyGram, plus the gap between the market rate and the rate the company gave; most transfers arrived in 2 days, Western Union's in under an hour [G0064, G0062]. The cheapest was Al Mulla online to a bank account, 5.96% in all [G0065].
3. **Send larger amounts less often.** On Bangladesh Bank's figures, sending up to USD 200 from Kuwait cost 3.91% on average in April-June 2026 against 1.51% across all amounts [G0006, G0007]; across all countries, 2.94% up to USD 200, 0.94% for USD 201-500 and 0.50% above USD 500 [G0008].
4. **Send.** At the branch, give the bKash number and registered name (or the bank details) and hand over the cash; bKash accepts up to Tk 2.5 lakh (about KD 620) per transfer, any number of times [G0011, G0012]. Most customers now send through the licensed companies' own apps; online transfers need identity checks through the Kuwait Mobile ID app (since April 2026) [G0068, G0066]. Branches are most crowded at the start of each month, when salaries are paid [G0068].
5. **The family collects.** bKash remittance can be cashed out for Tk 7 per thousand at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs [G0012]. The 2.5% government incentive is added on receipt [G0013].

**If something goes wrong, in this order:**
1. The exchange company or bank: ask for its complaint form at the branch; a bank must answer in writing within 5 working days, other CBK-regulated companies within 15 [G0038].
2. The CBK: appeal with the company's reply and your papers on the Consumers Protection Portal, services.cbk.gov.kw/apps/r/ebsprod/cbkcpp/home; enquiries 1864444 [G0038, G0039].
3. bKash, for a wallet transfer: helpline 16247 (in Bangladesh), +88-02-55663001 from abroad, support@bkash.com [G0014].

#### Saving at home: the Wage Earner Development Bond

The Wage Earner Development Bond is a 5-year government bond for workers abroad, bought in taka or foreign currency, in the worker's own name or a named person's in Bangladesh, at authorised-dealer bank branches in Bangladesh and at Bangladeshi banks' branches and exchange houses abroad [G0075]. It comes in sizes from Tk 25,000 to Tk 50 lakh, with no limit on how much you buy [G0075]. It pays 12% simple profit on holdings up to Tk 15 lakh (11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh, 9% above), paid every six months; the profit is income-tax free, you can borrow against it and name a nominee, and the principal can be taken in US dollars [G0076].

### For "What can get you jailed or deported" or "Leaving at the end of the job" (with L): #### Debts, unpaid bills and travel bans

**Before you fly out, pay every government bill in your name.** Since 2023 expatriates are stopped at the border until they pay traffic fines, electricity and water bills, Ministry of Communications (phone) bills and Ministry of Justice bills [G0046, G0049, G0050]. Most can be paid on the Sahel app, the ministries' websites or at the airport [G0049]:
- Electricity and water: MEW-PAY or Sahel apps, or the customer service office at Terminal 4, Kuwait International Airport [G0048].
- Traffic fines: Sahel, traffic departments or airport and port collection points; speeding and disabled-parking fines only in person at a traffic department office [G0047].

**Private debts lead to court travel bans.** The Ministry of Justice lists the most common causes: unpaid bank loans, overdue instalments, unpaid mobile phone bills, bounced cheques, rent disputes, electricity bills and unpaid alimony [G0051]. In June 2026 alone the Ministry of Justice recorded 4,357 travel ban requests against debtors and requests to arrest and hold 2,645 debtors [G0053]. Decree-Law 68 of 2026 let more judges issue travel bans so that requests are decided as soon as they are filed [G0059]. Since 2025, debtors who can pay but don't can be jailed again, with instalments allowed according to ability to pay and exemptions such as pregnant women and the medically unfit [G0057, G0058]. Enforcement can take 25% to 50% of a debtor's salary [G0058]. Since June 2024 the Public Prosecution also bans from travel expatriates sentenced to fines, including in absentia and while an appeal is pending [G0060].

1. **Check for a ban** on the Sahel app (Ministry of Justice service), which shows civil court travel bans and the amounts owed [G0055].
2. **Pay or settle** the debt; a ban is lifted after payment or settlement [G0054].
3. **If you must travel before it is settled,** ask the Ministry of Justice's enforcement department for a single-trip permission through Sahel and follow its status there [G0056].

Separately, from July 2025 a private-sector worker needs an exit permit from the employer, requested with the Civil ID on Sahel or PAM's website, before every trip (see Going home on leave) [G0061].

### For "Coming home": customs both sides

**Leaving Kuwait.** Declare cash of KD 3,000 (about Tk 12.1 lakh) or more, in any currency, and all gold in any form (bars, coins, jewellery) to customs; departing passengers fill in the customs declaration before leaving [G0041, G0042]. Since February 2026 cash over KD 3,000 can be declared in advance on the Sahel app, which issues a notice that customs checks at the airport [G0045]. Not declaring gold can lead to arrest or confiscation of the money or gold [G0044]. Carry watches, jewellery and costly electronics in hand luggage with their receipts [G0043].

**Arriving in Bangladesh** (Passenger baggage rules, SRO 261 of 2 June 2025) [G0018]:
- Duty-free: one item from the duty-free list, for example a laptop, a television up to 29 inches, a microwave or rice cooker, a sewing machine, a fan, a carton of 200 cigarettes, or 100 g of gold and 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [G0018].
- Phones: 2 used phones, plus 1 new phone once a year, free of duty [G0019].
- Gold bar: once a year, one bar of up to 117 g (10 tola), declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g (about Tk 50,000 for 117 g) [G0020]. More than 117 g is confiscated; anyone carrying undeclared gold is arrested and handed to the police with a criminal case [G0021].
- Alcohol: none on a Bangladeshi passport [G0022].
- Cash: any amount, but declare more than USD 10,000 (about Tk 12.4 lakh) on the FMJ form [G0023].
- Goods brought to sell are seized [G0024].
- Baggage sent separately: declare it on the Schedule-1 form handed out on the plane; if you miss it, declare at the customs house next to the airport within 7 days [G0025].
- Help at Dhaka airport customs: +88 01319-056591; airport hotline 13600 or 09614-013600 [G0026].
