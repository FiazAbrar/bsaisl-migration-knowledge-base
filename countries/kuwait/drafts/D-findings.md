## Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)

Article 20 is Kuwait's household visa: Bangladeshis call it the "khadem visa", and the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait calls it the "Domestic Aid visa" [D0022, D0024]. It covers housemaids, house drivers, cooks and other household staff working for a private sponsor (kafeel), not a company [D0021, D0024]. These workers come under the Domestic Workers Law (Law 68 of 2015), not the private-sector Labour Law [D0045, D0050]. The Public Authority for Manpower (PAM), Kuwait's labour authority, handles their contracts and complaints through its domestic labour department, which other sources call the Domestic Workers Employment Department (DWED) or Domestic Labour Office (DLO) [D0060, D0084, D0101].

**Is the route open from Bangladesh?**

- **Women: closed.** On 1 July 2026 the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait said Bangladesh and Kuwait have signed no agreement or MoU on women domestic workers, so the Embassy cannot and does not attest any women's domestic worker ("khaddama") visa [D0011, D0012, D0016]. It asked women not to go to Kuwait as domestic workers until an agreement is signed [D0016]. Bangladeshi women who work in Kuwait legally go on company (Article 18) visas, mostly as cleaners (see Work visa: private-sector worker, Step 1) [D0017].
- **Men (house drivers, cooks, guards): unclear.** The Embassy still lists attestation of "single" Visa-20 visas for personal sponsors, last updated 18 May 2026 (Step 3) [D0024]. But on 9 June 2026 Kuwait's Ministry of Interior (MOI) limited domestic-worker recruitment to 10 approved countries (South Africa, Benin, Eritrea, Ethiopia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, India, Vietnam, Nepal, and Senegal for men only), and Bangladesh is not among them [D0001, D0002]. Bangladesh is also not on the 27-country ban list as reported [D0003].

> **Need to know**
> - Does MOI's June 2026 circular stop new Article 20 visas for Bangladeshi men (house drivers, cooks), or does it cover only women? Bangladesh is on neither list as reported [D0002, D0003, D0004].
> - Is the Embassy still attesting Visa-20 visas for Bangladeshi men after June 2026, and how many a month? [D0024, D0001]
> - Is a Bangladesh-Kuwait MoU on domestic workers being negotiated, and when is it expected? [D0011, D0018]

**What closed it before.** Kuwait reopened household visas for Bangladeshi men only in May 2016, one per citizen, with conditions such as owning a house [D0019]. In March 2018 Kuwait suspended visas for Bangladeshis; the Embassy's labour attaché said it hit only Article 20, because of visa trading [D0020]. At that time most of the roughly 200,000 Bangladeshis in Kuwait held Article 20 visas, as domestic helpers, family drivers and "houseboys" [D0021].

> **Need to know**
> - When was the 2018 Article 20 suspension for Bangladeshis lifted, and by which MOI decision? [D0020]

### Who pays what

**You pay nothing to the recruiter.** Law 68/2015 forbids licensed recruitment offices and their partners, in Kuwait or abroad, from charging a domestic worker any fee [D0045]. The Embassy's contract says the sponsor gives the visa free of cost and pays the residency, Civil ID and driving licence [D0032, D0035]. For women, an agency approved by Bangladesh to send domestic workers may take no migration cost from them [D0116].

**The sponsor pays the recruitment office.** In January 2024 the Commerce Minister capped what an office may charge a Kuwaiti household, travel included: KD 750 for a worker from an Asian country, KD 575 from Africa, and KD 350 if the sponsor provides a special passport [D0064]. In July 2024 Al-Durra, the state-backed recruitment company, set KD 180, ticket included, for drivers or cooks [D0066].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the January 2024 KD 750 cap still in force in November 2026, and does it apply to drivers? [D0064, D0066]
> - What does a Bangladeshi man pay a broker in Dhaka for a Kuwait house-driver (khadem) visa in 2026?

### How a household in Kuwait recruits

1. **The sponsor checks they may sponsor.** Since June 2026 MOI caps sponsorship by family status: a single man may sponsor one driver only, a single woman one domestic worker, a married, divorced or widowed woman with children one worker and one driver, and a man with a family up to three workers and one driver [D0006, D0007, D0008].
2. **The sponsor applies on Sahel,** MOI's government app: since May 2026 citizens can issue entry visas for new house drivers there, and since June 2026 citizens and residents can manage domestic worker and driver services there [D0009, D0005].
3. **Or the sponsor uses a licensed recruitment office.** PAM had 488 registered offices in May 2025, and suspended 25 that month [D0067]. An office guarantees the worker for 6 months, and must refund the sponsor and send the worker home in set cases, including the worker refusing to continue [D0056, D0097].
4. **The sponsor gets the visa attested at the Bangladesh Embassy** (Step 3) [D0024].

> **Need to know**

### Step 1: Check you qualify

1. **Women: you cannot go as a domestic worker to Kuwait now** (Is the route open from Bangladesh?) [D0012]. Bangladesh's general rules for women domestic workers elsewhere are a one-month (24 working days) housekeeping course at a BMET Technical Training Centre (TTC) and an age of 25 to 45 at enrolment (returnees up to 50) [D0113, D0114]. You cannot enrol if pregnant, if your youngest child is under 6, or with a contagious or incurable disease [D0115].
2. **Men: check Kuwait's age limit.** Recruitment offices may not place domestic workers under 21 or over 60 without a ministerial exception [D0055].

   > **Need to know**

### Step 2: Check the visa, the sponsor and the contract

1. **Check it is a single Visa-20 from one named sponsor.** The Embassy attests Visa-20 only for a personal sponsor, in the sponsor's presence or that of the sponsor's attorney (Step 3) [D0024]. A single man may sponsor only a driver (How a household in Kuwait recruits, item 1) [D0007].
2. **Read the contract before you pay or travel.** The Embassy's attestation contract (form updated 5 March 2025) says [D0032, D0033, D0034, D0037, D0038, D0039, D0040, D0041, D0042]:
   - the visa is free
   - salary paid at the end of each month, with a KD 10 yearly increment
   - 30 days' paid annual leave
   - free air ticket to Kuwait and home at the end
   - food or at least KD 20 a month food allowance, and free housing
   - the sponsor will not take your passport
   - the sponsor will not file any case against you without informing the Embassy
   - the sponsor helps you register with the Embassy within 15 days of arrival
   - either side can end it early with 3 months' written notice
3. **Check the wage:** at least KD 60 a month basic, the legal minimum since 2016 [D0052, D0054].

> **Need to know**

### Step 3: The sponsor gets the visa attested at the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait

The Embassy attests a single Visa-20 only with the sponsor, or the sponsor's attorney, present [D0024]. Ask to see the attested visa before you pay (BMET clearance needs it: see Work visa: private-sector worker, Step 6) [D0024].

1. **The sponsor (kafeel), or the holder of the sponsor's power of attorney (towkil khas) or authorisation letter, comes in person** to the Embassy [D0024].
2. **The sponsor brings** [D0026, D0027, D0028, D0025, D0024]:
   - the attestation contract form signed by the sponsor (free from Room 3)
   - 2 copies of the visa
   - 2 photos of the worker
   - a copy of the sponsor's passport with the signature page
   - the sponsor's original Civil ID and a copy
   - the power of attorney or authorisation letter, if the sponsor cannot come
3. **Pay KD 3 per person** (non-refundable) in cash or KNET at Room 3, Sunday to Thursday, 8 am to 2 pm; the attested visa is ready at 4 pm the same day [D0029, D0030, D0031].

### Step 4: Medical, BMET clearance and the flight

The medical, BMET clearance and flight work as for company workers (see Work visa: private-sector worker, Step 5; Work visa: private-sector worker, Step 6). [lead: cite W/B ids there]

### Step 5: After you arrive

1. **Register with the Bangladesh Embassy within 15 days;** the sponsor must help (Step 2, item 2) [D0042].
2. **Get paid monthly with proof.** PAM says each month's salary must be documented by payment voucher or bank transfer [D0063, D0046].
3. **Keep your passport.** The employer may not keep your passport or Civil ID unless you agree [D0048, D0083].

> **Need to know**

### Scams

#### Done to workers

**"Khaddama" visas for women.** One or more syndicates send Bangladeshi women to Kuwait as domestic workers illegally; many end up abused or confined [D0013]. In the three months to July 2026 the Embassy rescued and sent home 7 such women [D0014]. Report a syndicate to the Embassy or to Bangladesh police [D0015].

**"Free visa."** There is no free visa in Kuwait: Article 20 binds you to your sponsor's house, and working for anyone else is illegal [D0022, D0023].

**Being sold on.** Employers reportedly "sell" domestic workers to other employers on social media [D0087]. The Embassy contract forbids moving you to another sponsor without your and the Embassy's agreement (Step 2, item 2) [D0036].

#### Tried by workers

Taking another job while on Article 20: it is illegal, and leaving your sponsor gets you reported absent (If your sponsor reports you absent) [D0023, D0095].

### Your rights at work

#### Pay

- At least KD 60 a month basic [D0052].
- Paid at the end of each month, with no deductions [D0046, D0057].
- Food, clothes, medical treatment and housing paid by the employer [D0047].

#### Hours, rest and leave

- At most 12 hours a day, with rest [D0049].
- A weekly day off [D0049].
- 30 days' paid annual leave [D0050, D0034].

#### Documents, phone and safety

- Your passport and Civil ID stay with you unless you agree otherwise [D0048].
- You may own and use a mobile phone outside working hours [D0061].
- No dangerous or degrading work [D0058].
- No work outside Kuwait without your consent [D0062].

#### End of contract

- End-of-service pay of one month's salary per year of service (Article 23) [D0059].
- The employer pays your ticket home [D0062, D0038].

In practice many domestic workers work over 12 hours without overtime, have wages withheld, lose rest days and end-of-service pay, and have their contracts switched [D0089]. Passport confiscation remains widespread [D0083]. PAM cannot inspect private homes [D0085].

### If something goes wrong

#### If you aren't paid or are abused

**If you are hurt,** in this order [D0107, D0101, D0044]:

1. **The police.** File a complaint and insist on a medical examination, so the injuries are recorded [D0107].
2. **PAM's domestic labour department (DLO)**, Rumaithiya, Block 7, Street 78, behind the main co-operative society [D0101]. Bring your Civil ID, passport number and contact details [D0101]. PAM takes visitors 10:30 am to 1:30 pm, hotline 103 [D0100].
3. **PAM's online form.** It takes a domestic worker's complaint against the employer for late salary, dues, documents, leaving the country or transfer, and you can apply for a residence extension while it is open [D0098, D0099]. It is in Arabic only and asks for the employer's details [D0084].
4. **The Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing** gives advice and can forward your written complaint to Bangladesh [D0044].

**If PAM refuses,** you can challenge the decision at the Central Administration Office in Abu Fatira within 15 days [D0110].

#### Shelters

- **Women:** the government shelter in Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh housed 1,252 women in the first half of 2025 [D0077]. Workers come referred by PAM or their embassy, or on their own [D0078]. Residents keep their phones and can move freely [D0079].
- **Men:** PAM's shelter in Hawally (opened 23 February 2025, 200 to 300 places) takes domestic and private-sector workers, by walk-in or referral [D0073, D0074]. It accepts men even if they break the residency or labour law [D0075].
- **What a shelter can do:** once a worker is admitted, the employer cannot file an absconding report [D0080]. Residents can change employer or go home without the old sponsor's approval [D0080]. If no final absconding report was filed, you stay until the dispute is settled and choose to go home or pursue the case [D0076].
- **No Bangladesh safe house in Kuwait:** the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB) runs safe houses only in Saudi Arabia, Oman, Jordan, Libya and Lebanon [D0112].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the women's shelter's phone number, and does it take Bangladeshi women who came illegally on khaddama visas? [D0077, D0013]
> - Where does the Bangladesh Embassy house rescued women while their exit is processed? [D0010]

#### If your sponsor reports you absent ("absconding")

A household sponsor must report a domestic worker who leaves to MOI within two weeks [D0095]. The sponsor files it at an MOI service centre and then at PAM, so as not to pay your ticket [D0096]. Reports often appear on the system 15 to 20 days after filing [D0105]. Leaving without the sponsor's consent can lead to detention, fines or jail [D0051]. MOI deported more than 26,000 foreign workers in 2024, most for immigration violations including absconding [D0090].

1. **If you are leaving abuse, go to a shelter:** once you are admitted, the employer cannot file an absconding report (Shelters) [D0080].
2. **Register at the DLO** and the Embassy labour wing (If you aren't paid or are abused) [D0101, D0044].

If you are deported you cannot re-enter Kuwait (see Going home without valid papers) [D0106].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### Another household

You cannot change employer without your sponsor's release, and PAM cannot override it [D0102, D0082]. If PAM approves a release, complete the transfer to the new sponsor within one month [D0109]. Otherwise you must cancel your residency and leave, because the old sponsor must then file an absconding case [D0111].

#### Moving to a company job (Article 18)

No transfer from Article 20 to a company visa is open in November 2026 [D0071]. The last window ran from 14 July to 12 September 2024 (Ministerial Resolution 6/2024) [D0071]. It needed the sponsor's approval, at least one year with that sponsor, and KD 50 plus KD 10 per year of service [D0069, D0070]. About 55,000 domestic workers moved [D0072].

> **Need to know**
> - Is PAM planning another Article 20 to Article 18 window for 2026-27? [D0071]

#### Going home

- **No exit permit:** PAM confirmed in July 2025 that domestic workers are excluded from the exit permit required of private-sector workers [D0094].
- **Without your passport:** complain at the DLO to get it back. With no case against you, cancelling residency takes about one to two months, then you fly home [D0104].
- **Abuse or illness:** PAM/DLO helps repatriate workers leaving because of abuse, unpaid salary, health or family problems [D0103].
- **Unpaid dues:** some workers leave without owed wages during long disputes [D0093]. Claim before you leave (If you aren't paid or are abused) [D0098].
