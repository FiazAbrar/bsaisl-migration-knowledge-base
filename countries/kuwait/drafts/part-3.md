### Your rights at work

#### Which law covers you

The Private Sector Labour Law (Law 6 of 2010) covers workers on an Article 18 work residence [L0002, L0087]. Domestic workers on Article 20 are not covered; they have their own law (see Work visa: domestic worker (Article 20)) [L0001]. The law sets only the minimum: better terms in your contract or the company's rules apply over it [L0003]. A worker on a visit visa has no rights under the Labour Law, even if a company brought him to work [L0110]. The law is enforced by PAM (the Public Authority for Manpower), which issues work permits and takes labour complaints at the labour unit that holds your employer's file [L0004, L0216].

#### Contract

- **Written, three copies:** the contract states the date signed, the start date, the wage, the length (if fixed) and the kind of work, with one copy each for you, the employer and PAM [L0004].
- **No paper is not the end:** without a written contract the job still counts as a contract, and you can prove your rights with any evidence, such as bank statements and messages [L0113, L0235].
- **Arabic wins:** the contract is in Arabic; a translation may be attached, but the Arabic text counts if they differ, so ask for it in Arabic and English [L0007, L0104].
- **Length:** a fixed-term contract runs one to five years and can be extended only if both sides agree; the employer may not extend it without your consent [L0008, L0091].
- **Probation:** at most 100 working days, once per employer; either side may end the contract without notice, but if the employer ends it, it still owes end-of-service pay for the time worked [L0009, L0010].
- **Same job, same pay:** the wage can't be cut once signed, and any agreement to pay less is void [L0005]. The employer may not give you work that doesn't match the contract; if it does, complain to PAM with a copy of your work permit [L0006, L0205].
- **Never sign a blank paper or a second contract in Kuwait** [L0111]. A common trick is a new, lower contract on arrival: one worker promised KD 200 a month was paid KD 90 after the company changed the contracts without consent [L0356].

#### Pay and deductions

- **Minimum wage:** KD 75 a month in the private sector (PAM Decision 14 of 2017), for contracts and work permits made or renewed since then [L0424, L0425]. Since 1 August 2025 the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait also refuses to attest a new visa paying less than its own minimums for an 8-hour day: KD 90 for unskilled cleaners and general labourers, KD 120 for a driver on a government contract, and KD 150 for most skilled trades on a private contract (see What the job pays) [M0014, M0018, M0019, M0020, M0021, M0022].
- **When:** at least once a month, and no more than seven days late [L0036, L0035]. PAM's Director General said in October 2026 that salaries must be paid before the 7th of the following month [L0229].
- **How:** into your own account at a bank in Kuwait; cash is allowed only in a new worker's first two months [L0037, L0351]. An employer that doesn't pay through the bank can be fined up to the total it owes and must still pay [L0075]. Since February 2026 PAM's monitoring system detects a short or missing salary deposit, suspends the company's file and sends you the reason and amount through the Sahel app (the government's app for residence and labour services) [L0355, L0352].
- **Which salary counts:** regular allowances count as wage, and your dues are worked out on your last salary [L0034, L0042].
- **Deductions:** at most 10% of the wage to repay a loan from the employer, with no interest, and all debt deductions together at most 25% [L0038, L0039]. Penalty deductions may not exceed five days' wage a month, and you may be punished only after being told in writing and heard [L0012, L0122].
- **Never charged:** safety equipment [L0063]. You can't be forced to buy food or goods from the employer or a particular shop [L0040].
- **If the company shuts for reasons that aren't your fault,** it must keep paying you [L0041].

#### Hours, rest and leave

**Hours and overtime**
At most 8 hours a day and 48 a week; in Ramadan at most 36 hours a week [L0043]. After at most five hours in a row you get a break of at least one hour, which doesn't count as working time [L0044]. The employer must post the daily hours, breaks, weekly day off and holidays at the workplace [L0064]. Overtime needs a written order and is capped at 2 hours a day, 3 days a week, 90 days and 180 hours a year [L0047, L0045]. It is paid at least 25% above your normal rate; the employer must keep an overtime record, and you may prove overtime by any means, so keep your own log and photos of timesheets [L0046, L0048]. Unpaid overtime and food allowance are among the commonest complaints workers report [L0110].

**Rest day and holidays**
One fully paid day off (24 hours) after every six working days; work on it earns at least 50% extra plus another day off [L0050, L0049]. Paid public holidays are Hijri New Year, Isra and Miraj, Arafat Day, the Prophet's Birthday, National Day (25 February), Liberation Day (26 February) and Gregorian New Year, one day each, plus three days each for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha [L0162, L0302]. Work on a holiday is paid double plus a day off in lieu [L0051].

**Annual leave**
At least 30 working days a year on full pay, earned in the first year after six months; weekly days off, public holidays and sick days inside it don't count as leave [L0408, L0409]. Leave pay is paid before you go [L0056]. The employer sets the dates, and leave can be carried over up to two years' worth [L0057, L0058]. Untaken leave is paid in cash when the contract ends [L0059]. Working for someone else during leave lets the employer take the leave pay back [L0060]. Going home on leave needs an exit permit (see Going home on leave) [L0305].

**Sick and other leave**
Sick leave each year is 15 days on full pay, 10 at three-quarters, 10 at half, 10 at a quarter and 30 unpaid, with a certificate from the employer's doctor or a government health centre [L0053, L0054]. After two years with the same employer, a worker who hasn't done Hajj gets 21 days' paid Hajj leave [L0061]. You get three days' paid leave when a close relative dies [L0062]. A woman gets 70 days' paid maternity leave and can't be dismissed during it [L0174, L0303]. The employer may not dismiss you while you are on leave [L0019].

#### Heat

From 1 June to 31 August every year, work in open areas is banned from 11 am to 4 pm under Ministerial Resolution 3 of 2026 [L0403, L0404]. PAM can extend the ban to dust storms, heavy rain or extreme heat and humidity, and urgent work in those hours needs prior approval from its Labour Inspection Department [L0347]. The employer must give cold drinking water, shaded rest areas, first aid and rest breaks [L0346]. Report a site working in the banned hours to PAM's inspection team on 24936192; the employer's file can be suspended until it complies [L0348, L0349].

#### Housing and health care

**Housing**
An employer working on a government contract or in a remote area must give free suitable housing and transport, or a housing allowance [L0011]. Make sure the contract charges nothing for housing or transport to work and says whether food is given or paid as an allowance [L0103].

#### Your passport

Keeping a worker's passport is illegal but still widespread in Kuwait [L0320]. If your company won't give it back, complain to the PAM office where your residence was stamped (see If you aren't paid, item 2) [L0094]. You must show your passport when the authorities ask and report a lost or damaged one to the Ministry of Interior (MOI) within two weeks [L0383, L0423].

#### End-of-service pay

**How much**
The indemnity (end-of-service pay) for a monthly-paid worker is 15 days' wage for each of the first five years and one month's wage for each year after, up to one and a half years' wage [L0026]. Daily, weekly, hourly and piece-rate workers get 10 days a year for the first five years and 15 days a year after, up to one year's wage [L0027]. Part years count pro rata, and any loan you owe the employer is deducted [L0028]. For example, a monthly-paid worker on a KD 100 basic salary whose contract ends after 3 years gets 45 days' wage, about KD 150 [L0137, L0142].

**Full, part or none**
- **Full:** when the employer ends the contract, when a fixed-term contract ends without renewal, and when the contract ends through the employer's fault, your death or incapacity, or the firm's bankruptcy or closure [L0029, L0030, L0405].
- **Part, if you resign from an open-ended contract:** half after 3 to 5 years, two-thirds after 5 to 10 years, all of it after 10 years [L0031].
- **None:** if you are dismissed for causing gross loss, getting the job by fraud or disclosing company secrets [L0197]. Other dismissals for misconduct keep the indemnity, and an arbitrary dismissal can be challenged at the Labour Court for indemnity plus compensation [L0013, L0014].

### How long you can stay

You can stay as long as your employer keeps your work permit and residence valid; Article 18 residence rests on the PAM work permit and ends when the job ends, even before its expiry date [W0009, W0005].

- **Length of each residence:** up to five years, and never longer than your health insurance [W0006, W0026, W0007].
- **Renewal fees:** KD 20 a year for the residence (doubled on 23 December 2025) and KD 100 a year for health insurance (up from KD 50) [W0021, W0025, W0027, W0028]. Renewing a work permit for the same job needs no new degree papers (see Work visa: professional and skilled) [W0053].
- **Over 60 without a degree:** from 2020-21 Kuwait refused renewal to workers over 60 without a university degree, and many had to leave [W0040]. The later extra fees of up to KD 900 a year were cancelled in November 2024, and these workers now renew and transfer on the same fees as everyone else [W0037, W0038, W0039].
- **Six months abroad:** your residence lapses if you stay outside Kuwait more than six months in a row, unless MOI gives permission before the six months end, at KD 5 a month [W0042, L0421, W0016, W0023].
- **If you were stuck abroad by the 2026 war:** the automatic extra absence permits given from 28 February 2026 ended on 1 September 2026, except for people who left Kuwait on or before 31 August 2026 [S0014, S0026, S0027]. The residence status certificate in Sahel shows the date by which you must be back [S0028].
- **When the job ends:** if your residence ends or is not renewed, Residency Affairs may give you a departure notice of up to three months, renewable once, up to six months from the end of your service, at KD 10 a month [W0012, W0022]. How to leave properly is in Leaving at the end of the job [W0012].

### Your residence permit

Your residence (iqama) is recorded on your Civil ID card, issued by PACI (the Public Authority for Civil Information) [W0013]. The employer applies for it and renews it, and you can't move it to another employer without PAM's permission (see Changing employer or leaving) [W0009].

1. **Check your Civil ID shows your residence and its expiry date** [W0013].
2. **Check your fingerprints are enrolled** in Sahel (see Step 7: After arrival, item 3) [W0065].
3. **If your residence expires or is cancelled,** call Residency Affairs on 25582960, 25582961, 97288200 or 97288211, or go to your governorate's Residency Affairs office, called Jawazat (see Step 7: After arrival, item 7) [W0061, W0082].
4. **If you are trafficked or held against your will,** call MOI's Public Moral Protection and Anti-Human Trafficking department on 25589648, 25589655 or 25589696 [W0062].

### What can get you jailed or deported

The Interior Minister can deport a foreigner even with a valid residence, for example one with no legitimate income or one working for someone else, and the order can extend to dependants [L0369, L0417, L0377]. The 2025 executive regulations add deportation after a felony sentence or a crime of honour or trust, or after four criminal judgments of any kind within five years [L0419]. A person ordered deported can be held up to 30 days, renewable [L0370]. Once deported, you can't come back unless the Interior Minister personally approves [L0099, L0382]. The employer pays the deportation costs, and anyone who employed or housed you illegally pays them in full [L0418].

#### Working for someone else and visa trading

You may not work for anyone except your own employer without permission [L0411, L0106]. Under the residence law (Decree-Law 114 of 2024, in force since 5 January 2025), this carries up to two years in prison and/or a KD 5,000 to 10,000 fine, which can be settled by paying KD 3,000 [L0372, L0413, L0414]. The same law bars the employer from using you outside the job you were recruited for, hiring you out to others, or withholding your dues [L0412]. Selling or buying a visa or residence for money, for a real or fake job, is residency trading: 3 to 5 years and/or KD 5,000 to 10,000 per worker for the seller, and up to one year and/or up to KD 1,000 for the worker who paid [L0416, L0380]. A worker who paid is exempt if he reports the crime to the authorities, even after getting the permit [L0415]. Sheltering, employing or housing someone without valid residence is also a crime [L0376].

> **Need to know**

#### Absence and late papers

Being reported absent leads to arrest and deportation (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0099]. Late residency is fined KD 2 a day for the first month and KD 4 a day after, up to KD 1,200, and an overstayed visa KD 10 a day [L0389, L0388]. The Interior Minister may waive these fines for a person who is deported and leaves [L0387].

#### Strikes and protests

Strikes and protests are illegal; workers who strike can be arrested, jailed and deported [L0105]. In January 2025, 80 Bangladeshi cleaners who stopped work over overtime, residence papers, Civil IDs and late pay were told their tickets home were ready and said they were pressed to sign resignations [L0400, L0401].

#### Drugs and alcohol

Alcohol is banned and drinking it is a serious crime [L0108]. Under the anti-drug law (Decree-Law 159 of 2025, in force since 15 December 2025), smuggling, producing or growing drugs for trade carries death or life imprisonment and fines of KD 100,000 to 2 million [L0298, L0398]. Never carry a parcel for someone else, and carry medicines only with a doctor's prescription [L0109, L0107]. Planting drugs on someone to accuse them falsely is also a crime [L0301]. An addict who asks for treatment before being charged is immune from prosecution [L0399].

#### Religion and social media

Don't post or say anything against Islam or Kuwait's customs, including on social media [L0102].

#### Traffic

Most low-wage workers can't get a private driving licence: it needs two years' residence, a KD 600 salary and a university degree, except for 12 exempt categories such as professional drivers [L0396]. Under the traffic law in force since April 2025, driving without a valid licence costs KD 75, or up to 3 months in jail and KD 150 to 300 in court [L0391]. Running a red light is a felony with up to 3 years and up to KD 1,000, and using a phone while driving costs KD 75 [L0390]. Police arrest drivers under drink or drugs, drivers who injure or kill someone, and drivers who flee an accident [L0392]. Carrying passengers for money without a permit, the side "taxi" many drivers run, is a serious offence [L0394, L0289]. A foreigner can be deported for a single serious traffic offence after an investigation; 15 were deported this way in May to July 2025 [L0395, L0290]. In October 2026 the Interior Minister said 300 delivery workers had been deported for violations in a few days [L0354].

#### Debts and travel bans

Private debts lead to court travel bans that stop you leaving Kuwait: the Ministry of Justice lists unpaid bank loans, overdue instalments, unpaid mobile phone bills, bounced cheques, rent disputes, electricity bills and unpaid alimony as the commonest causes [G0051]. In June 2026 alone it recorded 4,357 travel ban requests against debtors and requests to arrest and hold 2,645 debtors [G0053]. Since 2025, debtors who can pay but don't can be jailed again, and enforcement can take 25% to 50% of a debtor's salary [G0057, G0058]. Since June 2024 the Public Prosecution also bans from travel expatriates sentenced to fines in financial cases, including in absentia and while an appeal is pending [G0060].

1. **Check for a ban** in Sahel (Ministry of Justice service), which shows civil court travel bans and the amounts owed [G0055].
2. **Pay or settle the debt;** the ban is lifted after payment or settlement [G0054].
3. **If you must travel before it is settled,** ask the Ministry of Justice's enforcement department through Sahel for a single-trip permission and follow its status there [G0056].

### If you aren't paid

PAM handles claims for unpaid wages, end-of-service pay, leave pay, overtime, unfair dismissal, work injury, and a release or cancellation of residence [L0357, L0362]. You must go to PAM first; only PAM sends the case to court [L0188]. Complaining is free, and so is the court [L0077]. Claims can't be heard more than one year after the contract ends, so don't wait [L0076]. Your wage is late if it isn't in your bank account seven days after the due date [L0035]. If the employer doesn't pay, you may quit without notice and keep your full end-of-service pay [L0022, L0023]. Walking away without complaining first gets you reported absent (see If your employer reports you absent) [L0361].

1. **Keep your proof.** Keep your contract, bank statements showing what was and wasn't paid, payslips, your overtime log and messages with the company about pay [L0360].
2. **File a labour complaint at PAM.** Go in person to the PAM labour relations department that holds your employer's file (usually the governorate office where your residence was stamped) and ask to file a labour complaint (shakwa amaliya, শাকওয়া আমালিয়া) [L0363, L0092, L0357]. The PAM office in Hawally is in Jabriya, Block 6, Street 104, Building 7 [L0356].

   You'll need: your Civil ID, a copy of your passport and work permit, the contract and your proof from item 1 [L0093, L0360].

   1. Say your agreed salary, what you received and for how many months [L0357].
   2. You get a complaint number by SMS [L0364].
   3. PAM sets a hearing and tells both sides the date, time and the investigator's name [L0365].

   **What to say:** "I want to file a labour complaint (shakwa amaliya) against [company name], file number [number]. My contract salary is KD [amount]. I have not been paid for [months] / I am paid only KD [amount]. I also claim overtime and leave pay." [L0357, L0362]

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there now an online or Sahel route for an Article 18 worker's labour complaint, and what is it called on screen (the April 2026 Sahel service is for Kuwaitis' employment-support grievances) [L0355]?
   > - What are the addresses and phone numbers of PAM's labour relations departments in Capital, Farwaniya, Ahmadi, Jahra and Mubarak Al-Kabeer?

3. **Go to the hearing.** The investigator tries to settle the dispute; if there is no settlement, PAM must send the case to court within one month [L0365, L0080]. Some employers don't answer PAM's calls, to drag the case out [L0258].

   > **Need to know**

4. **Follow the case in the Labour Court.** The court sets a hearing within 3 days of receiving the case, and you follow it yourself or with a lawyer [L0081, L0220, L0407]. Workers' dues rank ahead of the employer's other debts, except its private home [L0079]. In May 2026 the Labour Court ordered a company that had not paid a worker for six months to pay his wages, full end-of-service pay, leave and overtime, let him transfer without its consent, and threw out its absconding report and its claim for recruitment costs [L0359].

5. **Ask to transfer.** PAM can let you move to another employer before one year if wages aren't paid through the official procedure or the employer breaks the law (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0335, L0330].
6. **If you must leave Kuwait first,** authorise a lawyer or a friend in Kuwait to file the complaint at PAM for you, within one year of the contract ending [L0366, L0088, L0358].

   > **Need to know**

7. **Get help.** Report the problem to the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing too (see Contacts) [L0400]. The Kuwait Society for Human Rights gives legal aid to expatriates [L0226].

   > **Need to know**

**If you are beaten or abused:** complain to the police and insist on a medical examination that records the injury [L0097]. Assault by the employer lets you quit without notice and keep full indemnity [L0131].

PAM's enforcement has tightened: in October 2026 it said it had suspended the files of 10,000 companies that had not paid workers, some for a year or more [L0350].

### If your employer reports you absent

If you stay away from work without an acceptable reason for 7 days in a row or 20 days in a year, the employer may treat you as having resigned [L0015]. It can then file an absence report (ghiyab, which workers call "absconding" or a "case"), which must reach MOI within 15 days of your first day absent [L0323]. Since March 2026 the employer files it online on PAM's Ashal portal (PAM's online system for employers), and you get an alert in the Ashal app [L0321]. The employer must take the printed report to MOI's Residency Affairs Directorate within 3 working days [L0322]. Some employers file false reports after a worker asks for his pay, and visa traders file them to free the visa for resale [L0361, L0324]. The case often appears in the system 15 to 20 days after filing [L0098].

1. **Watch for the alert** in the Ashal and Sahel apps [L0321].
2. **Object within 60 days.** Go to the PAM labour relations department that holds your employer's file and object that the absence was justified or wrongly reported [L0321, L0325]. Bring proof: medical certificates, travel documents, a resignation you sent, messages with the employer, and proof you were unpaid (see If you aren't paid, item 1) [L0272, L0360].
3. **Ask the employer to withdraw it.** The employer can cancel the report on PAM's portal [L0327]. A joint PAM and MOI panel may drop a report the employer withdraws or that is malicious, fraudulent or filed in error [L0326].
4. **File your own complaint for unpaid wages at the same time** (see If you aren't paid, item 2), because the court can throw out a false absconding report filed after you asked for your pay [L0234].
5. **Ask to transfer.** A false or malicious report is a ground to move to another employer before one year (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0329].
6. **If it can't be cleared,** a worker with an absence case who has his passport can surrender to the deportation authorities with a ticket; once deported he can't return (see Going home without valid papers) [L0098, L0099].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### Changing employer

The basic rule: you can transfer only after one year with your employer and with its consent [L0333]. On a fixed-term contract still running, you can't transfer or get a release without the employer's approval [L0095]. On an open-ended contract after 3 years with the same sponsor, you can move without its approval [L0096].

Since May 2026 (Administrative Decision 680 of 2026), PAM can allow a transfer before one year without consent if it finds that [L0328, L0334]:

- the employer failed to issue or renew your work permit or residence through its own negligence, or its PAM file is suspended [L0332];
- it filed a false or malicious report against you [L0329];
- it broke the Labour Law so that you may quit without notice: unpaid wages, abuse, unsafe work, fraud in the contract terms, false criminal charges [L0330];
- wages weren't paid through the official procedure, or you won a final court ruling against it [L0335];
- the firm went bankrupt or closed for good [L0331].

Apply through a complaint at PAM's labour relations department (see If you aren't paid, item 2); PAM investigates first, and can then stop the employer's new permits and recruitment [L0334, L0263]. PAM's director general said in October 2026 that transfers without sufficient reason won't be allowed [L0353].

#### Resigning or being dismissed

On an open-ended contract either side gives at least three months' written notice for a monthly-paid worker; if you skip it, you owe the wage for the notice period [L0017, L0125]. During the employer's notice you may take one paid day or 8 hours a week to look for a new job [L0018]. Whoever ends a fixed-term contract without the right to do so owes the other side compensation of up to the wage for the time left [L0021]. Many contracts make a worker who resigns early repay recruitment, visa, travel and medical costs; PAM can be asked to waive this [L0090, L0089, L0319]. You can't be dismissed for claiming your rights, or for your sex, origin or religion [L0020]. A dismissed worker can challenge the dismissal at the Labour Court and, if it was arbitrary, gets end-of-service pay plus compensation [L0014].

#### Going home on leave

1. **Get an exit permit.** Since 1 July 2025 every Article 18 worker needs his employer's exit permit to leave Kuwait, for a holiday or for good; domestic workers don't [L0305, L0316].

   You'll need: a smartphone with Sahel (in English or Arabic only), your Civil ID, and your departure and return dates [L0309, L0318].

   1. Open Sahel, go to Services, choose Public Authority of Manpower, then Expatriate Workers Services, then Exit Permit [L0311, L0309].
   2. Enter your departure and return dates and submit, no earlier than 7 days and no later than 24 hours before you fly [L0309, L0310].
   3. Your employer approves it on Sahel Business or Ashal [L0306].
   4. Once approved, the permit is valid for 7 days; print it or show it in Sahel at the airport [L0313, L0314].

   **If you have no smartphone or it's an emergency:** the employer can apply for you on Sahel Business or Ashal; how fast you get it depends on how fast the employer approves [L0312, L0315].

   **If it fails:** if the employer refuses or delays, complain to the PAM labour relations unit that holds the company's file [L0307]. There is no limit on how many permits you can get in a year [L0308]. Sponsors of "free visa" workers have been reported charging about KD 50 for an exit permit [L0317].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does PAM take to act on a complaint about a refused exit permit, and can it issue the permit itself?
   > - Who pays the ticket for annual leave? Law 6/2010 is silent; is it in the standard contract?

2. **Take your leave pay before you go** (see Hours, rest and leave) [L0056].
3. **Come back on time.** Seven days in a row away without acceptable reason lets the employer treat you as resigned and report you absent [L0015, L0323]. Your residence lapses after six months abroad (see How long you can stay) [L0421].
4. **Don't work for anyone else while on leave** [L0060].

#### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Collect what you're owed.** Your end-of-service pay, untaken leave in cash and your last wages (see End-of-service pay) [L0026, L0059]. Check the contract for who pays the ticket home [L0406].
2. **Get your service certificate and documents back.** The employer must give you a certificate with your period of service, job and last salary, and return your documents [L0033, L0032].
3. **Pay every government bill in your name.** Expatriates are stopped at the border until they pay traffic fines, electricity and water bills, Ministry of Communications (phone) bills and Ministry of Justice bills; most can be paid on Sahel, the ministries' websites or at the airport [G0046, G0049]. Check for a court travel ban too (see Debts and travel bans) [G0055].
4. **Get the exit permit** (see Going home on leave, item 1) [L0305].
5. **If anything is unpaid, complain before you go,** or within one year through a representative (see If you aren't paid, item 6) [L0076, L0366].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the employer have to cancel the residence and pay the ticket home at the end of a contract under any Kuwaiti rule, or only if the contract says so? Law 6/2010 makes it pay only when it recruited workers it didn't need [L0193].
> - Are the "pay before you leave" checks still enforced at the airport in 2026, and has any bill been added or dropped [G0046]?

### Bringing your family

A private-sector worker can bring a wife and children only if the monthly salary on his work permit is at least KD 800 (see Family visa (dependants)) [F0002, F0065]. The Bangladesh Embassy's minimum salaries for company visas are KD 90 to 150 (see What the job pays) [M0018, M0020, M0022]. Combining husband's and wife's salaries doesn't count: only the sponsoring parent's salary is looked at [F0084]. For a worker below KD 800, the routes left are a family visit visa (see Visit visas (tourist, family, business)) or a baby born in Kuwait, for whom no minimum salary applies (see Having a child in Kuwait) [F0003, F0141]. The family's residence ends when your own job-based residence ends [F0018].
