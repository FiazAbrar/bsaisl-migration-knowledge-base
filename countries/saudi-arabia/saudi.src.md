# Saudi Arabia: migration guide

How to go to Saudi Arabia from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step. Citations like \[3\] are official sources; \[N3\] are news or other third-party sources, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year in a citation, like \[N3 (2022)\], means the source is older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a fact not yet confirmed. "You'll need" lists what to have ready before you start a procedure.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000), SR the Saudi riyal and USD the US dollar; SR 800 is a little over Tk 26,000 [A0003]. BMET, the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, is the Bangladesh government agency whose job is to increase overseas employment and make migration safe for workers [A0111]. In Saudi Arabia a foreign worker's visa, residence and right to work are tied to a sponsor, normally the employer; this is called the kafala system [A0002].

## Work visa: company worker

For a job with a Saudi company. **Need to know:** how long the whole process usually takes, from job offer to departure.

**Who pays what.** The Saudi employer pays the recruitment fees, the iqama (residence card), the work permit and its renewals, exit and re-entry fees and your ticket home when the job ends [A0005]. You pay your own ticket home only if you are unfit for the work or leave without a legitimate reason [A0006]. You pay the OEP registration (Tk 200) [A0028]; the TTC course admission fee if you take a course (Tk 748) [A0030]; a passport (Tk 4,025 for a 48-page, 5-year e-passport with regular delivery) [A0124]; the skill test (USD 85) [A0007, A0008]; the Wafid medical booking (USD 10) and the medical tests (step 4) [B0004]; police clearance (step 6) [B0052]; the Wage Earners' Welfare Board welfare fee (Tk 5,500) [A0011]; the migrant worker insurance (Tk 1,000, covering death or permanent disability for Tk 10 lakh over 5 years) [A0012]; and source income tax at BMET clearance, Tk 500 for a Saudi single visa [B0096]. **Need to know:** the fee for the medical tests at the centre; the fee for the 3-day pre-departure orientation (PDO); whether BMET still charges Tk 250 for clearance and the smart card; whether the DEMO charges for fingerprints. For some job orders BMET sets a limit on what the agency may take from you, such as Tk 50,000 or Tk 58,610 (Before paying, below) [A0016, A0019]. **Need to know:** the government's current maximum cost for going to Saudi Arabia; the last reported figure is Tk 1.65 lakh, set in 2016 [A0013].

**What workers actually pay.** Workers pay about Tk 5 lakh on average and some pay up to Tk 9 lakh, mostly because visas are bought and sold several times before they reach the worker [A0014, A0083]. Many travel without knowing the company, job or wage, or find on arrival that the contract names a different employer, job or wage [A0084]. Step 1 covers how to check the company, the job and the agency.

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP).** OEP is BMET's website for people going abroad; you apply through it for TTC training, the compulsory 3-day pre-departure orientation and BMET clearance [A0109, A0027].
   You'll need: your NID (national ID card), a mobile phone with that number, a bKash, Nagad or other mobile banking account with Tk 202, and a recent clear photo that matches your face today [A0026, A0029, A0027].
   1. Open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) in Google Chrome, or scan the QR code on a BMET or TTC poster [A0026].
   2. Click through to registration, then type every detail exactly as it is on your NID; leave out any dots in your name and put the spaces where the NID has them [A0026].
   3. Press the verify button, type the OTP (one-time code) sent to your phone and press Submit, then "Save and continue" [A0026].
   4. Fill in the rest of the form correctly, go to payment, choose Mobile Banking, choose bKash, Nagad or another service, and enter your account number, the OTP and your PIN [A0026]. The government fee is Tk 200; Tk 202 is taken with the payment charge [A0028, A0029]. The fee is paid only online, inside OEP itself [A0026, A0029].
   5. Download the Registration Card; your BMET registration is done [A0026]. Print 2 copies of the registration slip and take them to your district employment and manpower office (DEMO, BMET's office in each district) to give your fingerprints [A0028]. BMET's [list of DEMOs](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) gives each office's address, phone and email [A0112, A0113]. **Need to know:** what the DEMO gives you after the fingerprints, and how long it takes.
   6. If you registered before, don't register again; log in with your mobile number and OTP [A0029, A0027].
   For problems with registration, login or payment, call the OEP hotline 09614445271 [A0128]. **Need to know:** how to correct a wrong name or date of birth after registration; what scams target workers at this step (for example fake registration sites or shops charging extra).

2. **Learn a trade and get a certificate.** Workers in the trades Saudi Arabia lists must pass the Takamol skill test (step 3), and the test expects real skill and work experience in the trade [A0058, A0070]. BMET's Technical Training Centres (TTCs) run 3-month courses [A0030], and BMET's [trades page](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dc3c933eb65569e0f2b7) lists the trades and seats at every TTC [A0129]. TTC Mymensingh, for example, teaches computer operation, electrical installation and maintenance, refrigeration and air-conditioning, welding and fabrication, plumbing and pipe fitting, civil construction, general mechanics, consumer electronics and automechanics with driving [A0114]. Each course ends with an assessment under the BNQF (Bangladesh National Qualifications Framework), the national skills standard [A0034, A0030].
   You'll need: your OEP registration, an SSC pass or a JSC/class 8 pass depending on the course, age 18 to 40 (21 to 40 for driving), and Tk 748 for the admission fee [A0030].
   1. Watch for your TTC's admission notice on its website, for example [TTC Gaibandha's notice board](https://ttc.gaibandha.gov.bd/pages/notices/6a1171662de6aeac14f7e3b4) [A0115]. Courses run in three-month sessions: TTC Gaibandha posted its July-September 2026 notice on 23 May 2026 [A0115], and TTC Magura took applications for its October-December 2026 session from 18 August to 15 September [A0030].
   2. Log in to OEP and go from your profile to the Training portal, then Training, Trade/Skills, your division, district and institution, and press Search [A0027, A0032].
   3. Select the course, press Apply; if your photo is right press Continue (the photo must match your face today), then Process to Enroll and Confirm [A0027]. Applications are capped at three times the number of seats [A0030].
   4. Sit the written admission test at the TTC on the date in the notice: Bangla, English, maths, general science and general knowledge about overseas jobs, visas and labour law [A0030].
   5. If you pass you get an SMS; log in again and pay the admission fee under Training Portal, My Course, Admission Fee [A0030]. The fee is Tk 748; the driving course is free and includes the licence fee [A0030, A0031]. **Need to know:** what happens if you fail the admission test or miss the fee deadline, for example whether you can apply for the next session.
   6. Attend at least 80% of classes and sit the final BNQF assessment; if found competent you get Tk 150 a day for travel and food (Tk 100 on the driving course), when the budget allows [A0031]. The TTC helps with job placement after the course [A0033].

3. **Ask relatives or friends in Saudi Arabia whether their own company is hiring.** Saudi sponsors sell visas for jobs they don't need, known as "free visas" (below) [A0079, A0083]. A Saudi company can get work visas only through Qiwa, the labour ministry's platform, and only if its commercial registration and work permits are valid, it pays wages through the Wage Protection System and enough of its staff are Saudi [A0074, A0075].
   You'll need from your relative: the company's name and its unified national number, to check it in item 5 [A0098, A0127], plus the job title and the monthly wage it is offering [A0107].
   1. **Your relative asks the company to hire you.** **Need to know:** what a Saudi company's HR office asks for (documents, trade proof) to request a visa for a named worker from abroad.
   2. **No one pays the company or sponsor for the visa.** The employer pays the visa and recruitment costs [A0005]. Being asked for money for the visa means it is being sold, and sponsors sell visas for jobs that don't exist [A0079, A0083].
   3. **On the Saudi side, the visa is handled by an authorised office.** The employer authorises a recruitment office on the Saudi visa platform to deal with the Saudi embassy for the work visa [A0076]. A DEMO official said in 2024 that even a visa got directly from a relative's employer has to go through a registered Bangladeshi agency [A0077]. If you use an agency, check it first (item 4). **Need to know:** whether a worker can get the visa stamped without a Bangladeshi agency, and what the employer enters on the Saudi visa platform to authorise a Bangladeshi agency.
   4. **The company gets the demand letter attested by the Bangladesh Embassy in Riyadh** (step 2) [A0102, A0103]. Single visas need this attestation as well as group visas [A0087].
   5. **On the Bangladesh side, BMET gives clearance directly to a worker who got the visa on their own,** in 3 working days (step 8) [A0057]. Then continue from step 3 (skill test).
   **Need to know:** the most an agency may charge for processing a visa the worker brings.

4. **Ask licensed agencies what Saudi jobs they have, and check each one.** Under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013, no one may recruit workers without a licence issued under the Act [A0125].
   You'll need: the agency's name or RL (recruiting licence) number.
   1. Open [OEP's agency list](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies) and type the name or RL number into Search. The row shows the licence status, licence validity date, office address and office phone [A0089].
   2. Go no further unless the status is "Active" and the validity date has not passed; other statuses are Expired, Suspended and Cancelled [A0091]. Licences are cancelled for visa fraud [A0092].
   3. Contact the agency at the office address and phone in the list, and ask which Saudi jobs they have for your trade [A0089, A0108].
   4. Deal only with the licensed recruiting agency itself: sending workers is not the job of travel agencies [A0125, A0093].
   5. Ask to see the employer's demand letter, which names the company, jobs and pay, with the Bangladesh Embassy's attestation (step 2) [A0107].
   6. Read other workers' reviews of the agency on [Recruitment Advisor](https://www.recruitmentadvisor.org/search-an-agency), a review site run by trade unions; each review says what the worker paid, for which country, and whether they got the promised job and pay [A0094, A0095].
   **One agency with outside evidence:** Bonanza Overseas (RL-0579, Active, office phone +88028881118) was the Bangladeshi agency in an ILO-backed fair recruitment pilot for construction jobs in Qatar in 2018-2021 [A0090, A0096, A0097]. BMET has approved it for Saudi job orders, with a cap of Tk 58,610 per worker in one letter [A0019].
   **Need to know:** which other agencies place workers in the promised Saudi job at a fair cost; there is no public rating of agencies.

5. **Check the company exists.** Saudi companies are identified by a unified national number starting with 7, which replaced the old commercial registration (CR) number [A0123, A0127].
   You'll need: the company's name or unified national number.
   1. Open the Ministry of Commerce service at [mc.gov.sa/en/eservices/Pages/Commercial-data.aspx](https://mc.gov.sa/en/eservices/Pages/Commercial-data.aspx) [A0098].
   2. Search by the company's name or unified national number, type the verification code shown, and press Search; the company's registration data appear at once [A0098]. The service is free but in Arabic [A0099]. The ministry's help line is 1900, a Saudi number; from Bangladesh, email cs@mc.gov.sa [A0100, A0126].
   3. Compare the name with the employer on your demand letter (step 2) [A0098, A0107]. If the company is not found or the name doesn't match, don't pay, and complain about the agency to BMET (below) [A0056]. **Need to know:** which fields the result shows (status, activity, city), since the page is in Arabic.

**Avoid a "free visa".** There is no such thing as a free visa [A0024]. It is a visa bought from a Saudi sponsor who has no job for you: you enter as the sponsor's employee, must find your own work and housing, often pay the sponsor a monthly cut, work illegally and can be deported [A0083, A0082, A0081, A0079]. Signs: the visa is from a household or person who doesn't need a worker; you get only a 3-month iqama; the contract you sign in Bangladesh is not the one used in Saudi Arabia [A0081, A0080]. If you are offered one, have it and your other papers checked at BMET or your DEMO (see "Have your papers checked", below) [A0108]. To report the broker or agency selling it, complain to BMET or the ministry (below) [A0056, A0048], or call the ministry hotline 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [A0025]; in an emergency call 999, the police emergency number [B0171].

**Before paying:**

- **Get the employer's name, the job, the salary and the contract in writing,** and check that the contract is attested by the embassy [A0107]. Know the agency's name, licence number, office address and phone [A0108].

- **Look for BMET's permission letter for your job.** For a group job order BMET issues a permission letter naming the agency and its RL number, the Saudi company and its sponsor ID, the block visa and demand numbers, the job, the number of workers, the salary and benefits, and the most the agency may take from each worker [A0017].
  You'll need: the agency's name or RL number.
  1. Open [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) (Miscellaneous information), type the agency's name, its RL number or "Saudi" into the search box and press search [A0020, A0021].
  2. Open the PDF in the row with that agency's name and Saudi Arabia, and compare the company, job, salary and maximum cost with what you have been told [A0017].
  3. In one letter for kitchen workers in Riyadh, the agency could take at most Tk 50,000, only by cheque, bank draft or pay order and only once the visa, BMET clearance and flight were confirmed; the employer paid all Saudi-side costs including the levy and airfare; and each worker had to sign the contract and get a Bangla translation [A0016, A0018].
  4. If there is no letter for your job on the site, have the agency's papers checked at your DEMO (next point) [A0022].
  **Need to know:** whether BMET still issues permission letters for Saudi group visas and, if so, where they are published now.

- **Have your papers checked before paying.** Take copies of the demand letter, contract and visa to your DEMO or to BMET (89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000) and ask them to check that the papers are real [A0022, A0110]. DEMO addresses and phone numbers are in BMET's [list of DEMOs](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [A0112, A0113]. **Need to know:** whether the check costs anything, how long it takes, and whether you get the answer in writing.

- **Pay the agency through its bank account, by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and keep the receipt** [A0023, A0016]. Workers who complain without written receipts often fail to get proper compensation [A0043].

- **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid in monthly instalments over up to 3 years including a grace period [A0052, A0053]. You can apply only once you have the visa and BMET card, since both are required [A0050].
  You'll need: age 18 to 60 and no unpaid loan anywhere; 3 recent passport-size photos; copies of your NID, passport, visa and BMET smart card (step 8), and the contract if you have one; 3 signed MICR cheque leaves from a bank account in your name, with its statement; Tk 500 to open a savings account at the branch; and 2 guarantors who, like you, live permanently in the branch's area [A0049, A0050, A0051].
  1. Choose guarantors: one well-off person who brings 3 signed MICR cheque leaves, a bank statement and proof of income, and one family member or close relative; each brings a photo, an NID copy and proof of address such as a utility bill [A0051].
  2. At your nearest PKB branch, fill in the free application form and open the savings account [A0050]. Branch addresses are on PKB's [branch list](https://pkb.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e0de933eb65569e28f0c) [A0120].
  3. Pay the fees: 0.5% processing plus VAT, 0.5% documentation plus VAT, and Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover; bring 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10, and 3 adhesive stamps of Tk 100 for each guarantor [A0055, A0052].
  4. The bank must decide within 7 working days of complete papers [A0052]. For help, call 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [A0054]. **Need to know:** how and when the loan is paid out, and into which account.
  **Scam at this step:** PKB staff have been accused of asking for Tk 10,000-20,000 in bribes for a Tk 3 lakh loan; the loan has no such fee [A0040, A0055]. To complain about a bribe demand, write to PKB's grievance officer, the General Manager (Administration and Accounts), at gm2@pkb.gov.bd or +88-02-48321195, who has 30 working days [A0121]; if that fails, to the Deputy Managing Director at dmd@pkb.gov.bd or +88-02-48319468, who has 20 working days [A0122]. You can also call the ministry hotline 16135 [A0025] or file at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), the government's complaint system [A0036].

- **If the agency takes your money and doesn't send you, sends you to a different job, or charges more than allowed, complain to BMET.** The service is free and is meant to take 30 working days: an inquiry committee holds a hearing with both sides, BMET sends its recommendation to the ministry, and the ministry decides [A0056].
  You'll need: your name, NID number, passport number, BMET smart card number (if any), age, father's or husband's name, mother's name, permanent address and phone; if you are abroad, your address there and a relative's name, relation and mobile number to attend for you; the agency's name, RL number and address, or the broker's name, age, parents' names and address; whether there were earlier complaints against them [A0037]; and your proof, such as receipts and the contract [A0056].
  1. **File online.** Since July 2026 you or your family can file on OEP's online complaint system, upload documents, follow progress and get notifications; BMET's "online complaint" link goes to employee.oep.gov.bd [A0039, A0038]. **Need to know:** the menu path to the complaint form inside the OEP account.
  2. **Or file on paper.** Fill in BMET's [complaint form (Form Ka)](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-bmet/2024/12/fe647f3d68c14792a15f496990376a8a.PDF), giving the reason and a short description of what happened, and hand it with copies of your proof to BMET (89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000), your DEMO, or the labour wing of the Bangladesh Embassy if you are abroad [A0037, A0110]. Sign or put your thumbprint at the end [A0037]. **Need to know:** whether you get a receipt or complaint number for a paper complaint, and how to follow it up.
  3. **If BMET doesn't act in 30 working days,** write to its complaint officer, the Director (Employment), at diremi@bmet.gov.bd or 01711111544; he has 3 months [A0036, A0056].
  4. **If that fails,** appeal to the Secretary of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare at secretary@probashi.gov.bd (phone 02-41030444, or +880 2 41030444 from abroad), who has 1 month; then file at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), the government's complaint system, which has 3 months [A0036].
  5. **You can also complain to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare directly,** to its Enforcement-2 branch at sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd or 02-41030255, with an application, your proof, your full address and the agency's name, RL number and address; it's free, action starts within 3-5 working days and the complaint is meant to be settled within 30 working days [A0048]. The ministry hotline is 16135, or +880 9610102030 from abroad [A0025].
  **Free legal help:** OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) gives free legal advice and help filing a case on 01842773300 [A0046, A0047]. **Need to know:** whether ILO or IOM in Dhaka take up an individual worker's complaint.

### Step 2: Get the attested demand letter

The employer registers on the Bangladesh Embassy Riyadh's e-demand system at [beriyadh.org](https://beriyadh.org/employers/create) with its company registration number, and uploads the demand letter (its request for workers), a power of attorney naming the agency, the employment contract, a letter of undertaking, a letter of guarantee, its project or contract papers and its wage protection certificate, with a list of at least 10 of its current workers [A0102, A0103, A0085]. The embassy's labour wing checks the papers and attests them, now online [A0086, A0088]. Single visas need this attestation as well as group visas [A0087]. **Need to know:** the embassy's attestation fee and processing time; whether forged attestations on Saudi demand letters have been reported.

1. **Ask the agency for a copy of the attested demand letter and contract** [A0107]. If the agency won't show them, don't pay, and complain to BMET (step 1, Before paying) [A0056].
   You'll need: nothing from you; the agency or employer holds the papers.
2. **Have them checked** at your DEMO or BMET, as in step 1 (Before paying) [A0022]. **Need to know:** whether workers can check an attestation themselves on beriyadh.org.

### Step 3: Pass the skill test

Workers going to Saudi Arabia in the listed trades must pass the Takamol skill test, run under Saudi Arabia's Skills Verification Program (SVP) [A0058]. You need to have skill and work experience in the trade; you can't pay your way through [A0070]. The test runs at 28 centres in Bangladesh: 27 TTCs and BRTC Gazipur, the training institute of the Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation [A0061, A0130]. A retake costs the full fee again [A0009]. **Need to know:** the pass mark and how long the certificate stays valid.

You'll need: your passport (valid, with the number typed exactly), your NID, your own email address that you can open, a Visa or Mastercard endorsed for US dollars with USD 85 on it, and the occupation on your visa [A0062, A0063, A0007]. **Need to know:** how a worker without a dollar-endorsed card can pay (which banks endorse or issue one, the cost and the time), and whether a card in someone else's name is accepted.

1. **Check where your trade is tested.** BMET's [list of occupations by test centre](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-bmet/2026/2/dc7e2b1f-5458-4a46-9197-958e2cd8941e.docx) ("TTC wise Occupation under SVP", on bmet.gov.bd under Training, Takamol) shows that some centres test only loading and unloading, while Bangladesh-Korea TTC Dhaka tests 32 occupations [A0105, A0106].
2. **Register yourself on the SVP site** [A0117, A0118]. On [svp-international.pacc.sa](https://svp-international.pacc.sa/) press Sign-up and enter your first name, last name, NID number, passport number, country, nationality, your own email, date of birth, passport expiry date and a password of at least 8 characters with a capital letter, a small letter, a number and a symbol; tick "Agree to acknowledge that all the data are correct" and type the code sent to your email [A0062]. Copy everything exactly from your passport [A0062]. If you have only one name, write it as the first name and your father's name as the last name; the same name in both counts as an error [A0064]. With a wrong passport number you can't sit the test, so fix it first through Support at the bottom right of the site [A0067].
3. **Book.** Sign in with the email and password and the code emailed to you; in Account Dashboard press Start Verification, choose your occupation (it can't be changed later), Direct Assessment (In-person), your division or city (the centre is assigned automatically), the test language and a date shown in green; tick "I declare that the information entered is correct" and press Confirm [A0063, A0064, A0068].
4. **Pay USD 85** with the card's number, CVV, expiry date and holder name, then press Pay; the test ticket appears in your dashboard under Reservation and is emailed to you [A0007, A0008, A0063, A0066]. Pay no one more than the fee: there is no way to pass for extra money, and a worker caught in an illegal payment has the account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [A0070, A0071]. **If someone offers to fix the test for money,** report it to the police (call 999, or your local police station) [A0070, B0171], tell BMET's Takamol and Japan Support Centre, BMET Bhaban (4th floor), Kakrail, Dhaka, 01901165981 or 01901165982 [A0072], and complain to BMET (step 1, Before paying) [A0056].
5. **Prepare if you need to; it's optional** [A0069]. A 3-day loading and unloading course costs Tk 2,000 at 24 TTCs [A0069]. Free courses run for gas station attendants (Kishoreganj TTC) and sellers (Bangladesh-Korea TTC Chattogram), 5 days each, and for food and beverage counter staff (Dhaka Mohila TTC), barbers (Sylhet TTC), concrete finishers (Bangladesh-Korea TTC Dhaka) and building and construction workers (Khulna, Jhalokathi, Moulvibazar, Nilphamari and Chapainawabganj TTCs), 10 days each [A0116]. The course certificate is only proof of attendance; it doesn't count as an SVP certificate or towards passing [A0069].
6. **Take the test** with your original passport, a photocopy of it, the test ticket and one photo, and arrive before the 30-minute briefing [A0066, A0059]. The computer test takes 30 minutes: 15 questions answered by clicking, in the language you chose [A0059, A0060]. The practical test takes 1 hour 30 minutes and is in Bangla for every trade [A0059, A0060]. Phones and bags go in a locker, and leaning to look at another candidate counts as cheating [A0066].
7. **To change the date,** cancel and rebook once, up to 96 hours before the test [A0068]. If you miss the test you are marked absent, and you can apply and pay again 96 hours after the slot [A0068].
8. **Get the result and certificate.** Results appear on the SVP portal [A0073]; Pakistan's official SVP partner says a pass is uploaded to the candidate's SVP account within 24 hours, and a candidate who fails books a new test [A0119]. Anyone can check on the SVP portal that a Takamol certificate is genuine [A0073]. To correct a wrong detail on the certificate, press Support at the bottom right and raise a ticket with your name, email, a subject and description, Request Type "Suggestion", Client "Test Taker", your country and passport number, and attach your passport, ticket and certificate [A0068]. **Need to know:** the exact page on the SVP portal for checking a certificate; how to dispute a fail result; whether fake Takamol certificates are being sold.

### Step 4: Pass the Wafid medical check

Wafid is the Gulf Health Council's programme for the health check that everyone going to work or live in a Gulf country must pass; you book it online and a medical centre in your city does the tests [B0001]. A result is valid for visa issuing for only 2 months, so book it when the agency or employer is ready to apply for your visa, after the skill test (step 3) [B0017].

**You'll need:** your passport (number, issue date, issue place, expiry date), your NID number, an email address, a mobile phone that can receive a code, and a payment card that works online for USD 10 or USD 25 [B0005, B0006, B0004].

1. **Book the slip yourself.** Open [wafid.com](https://wafid.com/en/book-appointment/) and press Book an Appointment. Choose Bangladesh, your city and Saudi Arabia as the country you are travelling to [B0001]. Choose Standard (USD 10; the centre is assigned for your city) or Premium (USD 25; you choose the centre and date) [B0004]. Enter your details exactly as on your passport, type the passport number twice, choose Work Visa, enter the code sent to you and tick the box confirming the details are correct [B0005, B0006]. Pay by card, entering the card's expiry as year then month [B0002]. Print the slip; if you lose it, open Print GCC slip and enter your passport number and nationality [B0007].
2. **Go to the centre straight away** with the printed slip [B0003]. The centre takes your photo and fingerprints at reception and again during the tests, so the person tested is the person travelling [B0037]. The tests themselves are paid at the centre, and the Gulf Health Council doesn't set that fee [B0038]. Pay no more than about Tk 8,500-9,000 in total [B0030]. **Need to know:** the official fee for the Wafid medical tests in Bangladesh.
3. **Check the result** on [Wafid status search](https://wafid.com/en/medical-status-search/) with your passport number and nationality, or the slip number [B0008]. It can take up to 26 days to appear [B0009].
4. **If a detail on the record is wrong** (name, passport number, date of birth and so on), ask the centre to submit a change request, and write down the request number and date. It is free; pay nothing for it [B0010, B0011].
5. **If you paid but got no slip,** email Wafid at support@wafid.com with your nationality, the date and time of payment, passport number, full name, card number, cardholder's name, email address, a copy of the card and a bank statement showing the payment [B0012, B0013].

**Don't buy a "fit" result.** Agencies and brokers sell "packages" that guarantee a fit report for about Tk 20,000-30,000 [B0032, B0030]. Saudi Arabia tests you again after you arrive [B0018]; a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for a fit report was found unfit there and deported [B0031]. Some centres declare people unfit first and then ask for a bribe to change it [B0035].

**If you are declared unfit,** you can't take the test again for 3 months [B0027]. Unfit means, among others, HIV, hepatitis B or C, malaria, leprosy, tuberculosis or other chest X-ray findings, kidney, liver or heart failure, uncontrolled blood pressure or diabetes, and cancer [B0016].

**If the result is wrong, the test wasn't done properly or the centre demanded extra money:**

1. **Complain in writing to the Gulf Health Council within 2 weeks** of the test; later complaints aren't considered, and anonymous ones are refused [B0020, B0021]. Use the Complaint Form at the end of the [Wafid regulations](https://wafid.com/static/pdf/Regulations%20of%20Medical%20Examination%20of%20Expatriates%20Coming%20to%20GCC%20States%20for%20Work%20or%20Residence.pdf) [B0150]. Give your name, phone and email, the centre's name and city, what happened (for example, "the X-ray was not done", "error in the result", "the centre asked me for Tk 10,000 to issue a fit report") and any proof such as receipts, messages or another lab's report [B0023, B0024]. Send it by email to info@ghc.sa, by post to P.O. Box 7431, Riyadh 11462, Saudi Arabia, or hand it in at the Council's office on Samha Street, Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh; its phone is 920020885 [B0022, B0021, B0148, B0149]. Fax is also allowed [B0021]. **Need to know:** the Council's current fax number. The Council tells you within 3 working days whether it accepts the complaint and gives its decision within 30 days [B0151, B0152]. Its decision is final; if the centre is at fault it can be warned, re-checked, suspended or closed [B0019, B0025]. The Council also has a Grievance Committee that takes appeals from migrants [B0026]. **Need to know:** how to file an appeal with the Grievance Committee and by when.
2. **In Bangladesh, complain about overcharging** to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, whose monitoring and enforcement wing takes formal complaints about extra charges for health certificates [B0033]. Send an application with your receipts, your full address and the centre's name and address to its Enforcement-2 branch at sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd or call 02-41030255; it's free and the complaint is meant to be settled within 30 working days [A0048]. The ministry hotline is 16135 [A0025].

Saudi Arabia accepts apostilles, so no embassy attestation is needed, and anyone can check one by scanning its QR code [B0068, B0067].

**Pay no "embassy fee" or "visa fee" to anyone.** There is no fee for applying for the visa at the embassy [B0069]; the employer pays the recruitment and visa costs [B0116], and at Tasheer you pay only its service fee (step 5) [B0155].

### Step 9: After arrival

**If you came without BMET clearance,** join the Welfare Board from Saudi Arabia to get its insurance and help: apply online at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/) with your passport and proof that you work legally; the embassy labour wing processes it and you pay in riyals [E0020]. The welfare fee for workers abroad is Tk 5,500 [B0100, B0099]. **Need to know:** the amount in riyals now charged by the labour wing.

**Iqama (residence card).** The employer must get your iqama within 90 days of arrival, after you pass a medical test and it buys your health insurance [B0108]. The employer pays for it and for renewals [B0113, B0116]. Your part is the medical test and fingerprints; it usually takes 1-3 weeks once the papers are complete [B0144, B0143]. When you get it, check your name is exactly as in your passport; the employer corrects it at the Passports Directorate (Jawazat) [B0110]. Carry it always and show it to police when asked [B0111]. Without it you can't open a bank account, use an ATM, send money home or get a SIM card [B0112].

You enter on a 3-month temporary permit, and the employer must get your iqama and work permit within that time, paying about SR 11,000 a year for them [B0131]. If it doesn't, you become undocumented and can be detained and deported [B0132, B0135].

*If you have no iqama after 90 days:*

1. Call the labour ministry on 19911 (Sunday to Thursday, 8 am to 5 pm) and register a complaint [B0109, E0052]. **Need to know:** whether 19911 has a Bangla option.
2. If nothing happens, follow the steps under If you aren't paid, and call the embassy labour wing on 8001000125 [E0024].

**Set up Absher,** the Interior Ministry's portal for your iqama, visas and appointments [B0124]. *You'll need:* your iqama number and a Saudi mobile number [B0125, B0128]. Open [absher.sa](https://www.absher.sa/), press New User, enter your iqama number, set a password and enter the SMS code [B0125, B0128]. Then activate the account with the Nafath app, the Saudi digital ID [B0127], or at an Absher kiosk in a mall or Jawazat office: enter your iqama number, scan both index fingers, then enter your mobile number and the code sent to it [B0126]. If that fails, book an appointment on Absher and activate it at a Jawazat office, which charges no fee [B0176]. Absher support: 920020405 [B0175].

**Check your contract on Qiwa,** the labour ministry's platform; every contract must be written and documented there [B0117]. *You'll need:* your iqama number, your date of birth as on the iqama and the Nafath app [B0130]. Register at [sso.qiwa.sa](https://sso.qiwa.sa/en/registration) and confirm your identity with Nafath [B0142]. The employer sends the contract to your account, and what you approve becomes the record of your pay and terms [B0129], so read it and make sure you understand every term before you approve it [B0123]. Keep a copy [B0118]. Qiwa support: 920000105 or support@qiwa.sa [B0174].

**Keep your passport.** No employer may keep your passport or iqama, even "for safekeeping" [B0119, B0114]. If it does, complain to the labour ministry: call 19911 (Sunday to Thursday, 8 am to 5 pm) [E0052], or file a complaint on its Beneficiary Page (Client page) at [selfservice.hrsd.gov.sa](https://selfservice.hrsd.gov.sa/node/), logging in with your Absher account; it is free and the ministry replies within 5 business days [B0120, B0168, B0169, B0170]. You can also use the steps under If you aren't paid.

**Pay.** The employer must pay your wages on time through the Wage Protection Program, which records every payment [B0121], and give housing or a housing allowance [B0122]. **Need to know:** how a worker can see the wage payments recorded for them under the Wage Protection Program. The employer bears all recruitment, visa and iqama costs, so nothing may be deducted from your pay for them [B0116]. Typical monthly pay: helpers SR 1,200-1,800, cleaners SR 1,000-1,700, construction workers and masons SR 1,600-3,000, drivers SR 1,500-2,500, electricians SR 1,800-3,500 [B0137, B0136, B0138, B0139, B0140].

### Your rights at work

**Contract.** Your contract must be recorded on Qiwa, the labour ministry's online platform for job contracts and job changes, and you and the employer each keep a copy [C0016]. Qiwa is opened with Nafath, the Saudi digital ID app [C0151]. A foreign worker's contract is for a fixed term, usually 2 years; if no length is written, it counts as one year, and if both sides carry on after it ends it renews for the same length [C0095, C0026]. You may work only for the employer and in the job title written on your work permit [C0021]. The contract should say how much leave you get, whether it is paid and who pays the airfare [C0152].

**Hours.** Normal hours are 8 a day and 48 a week, and 6 a day and 36 a week for Muslims in Ramadan [C0001, C0002]. Total hours may never pass 12 a day [C0096]. After 5 hours in a row you get a break of at least 30 minutes [C0004]. From 15 June to 15 September, no one in a private company may work in direct sun between 12 noon and 3 pm [C0108].

**Overtime.** Each extra hour pays your full hourly wage plus half of your basic hourly wage, and work on a public holiday counts as overtime [C0005, C0010].

**Pay.** There is no minimum wage for foreign workers, so the wage in your contract is what you can claim [C0092]. Pay goes into your bank account on the date set in the contract [C0006]. Never sign a paper saying you were paid before the money is in your account; signing can stop you claiming it later [C0025]. The employer pays your recruitment, iqama (residence permit) and work permit fees and their renewal, any change of job title, exit and re-entry visas, the fee for transferring you from another employer, and your ticket home when the contract ends; none of these may be taken from your pay [C0020].

**Rest and leave.** You get one day off a week, normally Friday, and it can't be swapped for money [C0003]. Paid annual leave is at least 21 days a year, and 30 days after 5 years with the same employer; the employer sets the dates but must tell you 30 days ahead, and any days you haven't taken are paid when you leave [C0007, C0009, C0008]. Paid public holidays are 4 days at each Eid, Founding Day (22 February) and National Day (23 September) [C0010]. Sick leave is up to 120 days a year: the first 30 at full pay, the next 60 at three-quarters, the last 30 unpaid [C0011]. Other paid leave: 3 days when your child is born, 5 days for your wedding, 5 days when a spouse, parent or child dies, and 10 to 15 days once for Hajj after 2 years' service; women get 12 weeks' maternity leave [C0012, C0013].

**Housing, food and transport.** The employer gives you housing or a housing allowance, and transport to work or a transport allowance [C0014]. Food or a food allowance is given only if your contract says so, so check it before signing [C0099].

**If any of these rights is broken.** Report it in the labour ministry's app (Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development, on Google Play and the App Store) or in Tawakkalna, the Saudi government app, using the service Reporting Violations of Labor Regulations: you sign in with Absher, the Interior Ministry's portal, choose the type of report, and give the workplace's location, the company's details and proof of the violation [C0157, C0158]. It is free, the ministry acts within 3 days, and the report's status shows in the app [C0157, C0158]. Money you are owed, such as unpaid overtime or leave pay, is claimed through friendly settlement (If you aren't paid) [C0024]. The ministry's help line is 19911, Sunday to Thursday 8 am to 5 pm, in Arabic and English [C0050].

**Health insurance.** The employer must insure you [C0015]. You can check your cover on the Council of Health Insurance's Insurance Information Inquiry service at [chi.gov.sa](https://www.chi.gov.sa/en/ServicesDirectory/Pages/Eservices-CheckInsurance.aspx) [C0110]. **Need to know:** which number the check asks for (iqama or passport) and what the result shows. If you aren't insured, or the employer takes the premium from your pay, complain at [care.chi.gov.sa](https://care.chi.gov.sa/), as a guest or with an account: fill in your details, the employer's contact details and what happened, upload scans of your proof and submit [C0159]. The council handles it within 15 working days and contacts the employer without saying who complained [C0159]. The insurance is basic and doesn't pay for treatment of serious illness, so go only if you are fit [C0101].

**Your passport.** No employer may keep your passport or iqama for any reason [C0022]. If yours is kept, complain on the labour ministry's Beneficiary Page, its complaints portal [C0023]. On [hrsd.gov.sa](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/ministry-services/services/client-page), go to Contact Us, then Beneficiary Page, press Start the Service and log in with Nafath; then press Complaints, then Add a Request, choose the sector, main category and subcategory, fill in the fields and press Send [C0154, C0155]. You need an account with the ministry or with Absher; the service is free and answered within 5 days, and is also in the ministry's app [C0156].

**Probation.** Your contract can set a trial period of up to 180 days [C0017]. In that time either side can end the contract without compensation or end-of-service pay, and if it ends you pay your own ticket home [C0017, C0018, C0098].

**Leaving without notice.** You may stop work without notice and keep all your rights if the employer doesn't meet its duties (for example, doesn't pay), puts you in a different job without your consent, assaults you, treats you cruelly, leaves a known serious danger at work unfixed, or lied about the job when you signed [C0029, C0094]. If you stop coming to work, the employer can report you absent (see If your employer reports you absent (huroob)) [C0062], and can dismiss you without end-of-service pay after more than 30 days' absence in a contract year or 15 days in a row, once it has warned you in writing [C0028].

### How long you can stay

**As long as you have a contract and a valid iqama.** The employer renews the iqama and pays for it [C0020]. Work visas are for ages 18 to 60 [C0100].

**No pension.** Foreign workers are covered by GOSI, the Saudi social insurance agency, only for work injuries, not for a pension [C0109]. What you get instead is end-of-service pay when you leave (Leaving at the end of the job) [C0031].

### What can get you jailed

**Don't bring** narcotic drugs, alcohol, anything containing pork, poppy seeds (khas khas), khat, gutkha, pan masala, religious literature of religions other than Islam, obscene material, amulets or anything linked to black magic; check your phone too, since obscene or banned material on it counts [C0181, C0102, C0160, C0103].

**Never carry a parcel you haven't packed and checked yourself,** even for a relative or friend; refuse parcels from people you don't know [C0104]. Saudi Arabia executes foreigners for drug smuggling [C0107].

**Bring medicines** only for your own use, at most 30 days' supply, in their original packs, with a medical report and a prescription less than 6 months old, both translated into Arabic [C0105, C0106].

**Don't work for anyone other than the employer on your permit, or in a different job,** even for a few hours: both you and the employer break the law [C0021].

### If you aren't paid

You can claim unpaid wages, overtime, leave pay and end-of-service pay. Act in this order.

1. **Collect proof first.** Keep your Qiwa contract showing the wage, your bank statements, payslips and every message about pay, and write down the months and amounts missing [C0143, C0016]. Don't sign anything saying you were paid [C0025].

2. **File a complaint with the labour ministry (friendly settlement).** This is the first step for every wage claim; the ministry tries to settle it, then sends it to the labour court [C0024, C0039].
   - **You'll need:** your iqama number and a phone number to get the SMS codes (a Bangladeshi number can be added), the employer's name, your job title and start date, the city where you last worked, each thing you are claiming with the months and amounts, and your proof as PDF, JPG, JPEG, TIF or PNG files of up to 5 MB each [C0039, C0048, C0034, C0049, C0043].
   - **Where:** open the ministry's [Friendly Settlement service page](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/ministry-services/services/269970), press Start Service and log in to the Individual Portal with your user name and password [C0034, C0039, C0161]. **Need to know:** how a foreign worker first creates that login (Nafath or Absher).
   - **What to click:** choose Amicable Settlement Lawsuit Filing (file a claim); check your details; choose your employer from the list, or Another Establishment to type it in; enter the job details and choose the settlement office for the city where you last worked; add each claim subject (for example, unpaid wages) and fill in its amount; attach your proof; tick the declaration; review and press Submit Claim [C0039, C0034]. You get a claim number at once and the review result within 48 hours by SMS [C0044].
   - **What to write in the claim:** keep it short, with dates and amounts, and list what you attach [C0143]. Template: "I worked for [company] as [job] from [date]. My contract wage is SR [amount] a month. I have not been paid for [months], a total of SR [amount]. I ask for these wages to be paid. Attached: contract, bank statements, messages."
   - **Deadline and time:** file within 12 months; the ministry tries to settle within 21 working days [C0035, C0042].
   - **The session:** all sessions are now online by Webex video call; you show your iqama to the mediator, and there is no interpreter for people who don't speak Arabic [C0046, C0045]. **Need to know:** whether an interpreter or the embassy labour wing may join the session with you. If you miss the session, the claim is dismissed, but you can reopen it within 21 working days from the My Claims page [C0041, C0162]. If the employer doesn't come, the ministry suspends the company's services and sets a new date; if it keeps not coming, you can be moved to a new employer without its consent and the claim goes to the labour court [C0040].
   - **If it is settled:** a settlement report is made, and you can print it from My Lawsuits in the ministry's e-services [C0163]. **Need to know:** how to enforce the settlement if the employer still doesn't pay.
   - **If it isn't settled:** the claim goes on to the labour court on Najiz, the Justice Ministry's online court system [C0024, C0042].
   - **Help line:** 19911, Sunday to Thursday 8 am to 5 pm, in Arabic and English [C0050]; the settlement service itself also works in Urdu [C0038].
   - **Need to know:** whether filing costs anything (SR 0 or SR 100) [C0036, C0037].

3. **Ask the Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing to help, at the same time.** It advises workers in disputes with employers and goes to the labour court with them [C0115, C0116]. Call free on 8001000125 inside Saudi Arabia, or 0114195300 extensions 401, 402 or 403, or email counselorriyadh@probashi.gov.bd or labourwing.riyadh@yahoo.com [C0145, C0175]. Or use the wing's [online service request form](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/): give your name, mobile, city, iqama and passport numbers, choose the problem type (for unpaid wages, the one for housing, food and wage problems), describe it in Bangla or English, and attach copies of your iqama and passport [C0118, C0119]. Template: "I am [name], iqama [number], working for [company] in [city]. I have not been paid since [month]. I have filed a friendly-settlement claim, number [number]. Please help me with the hearing." The embassy is at 8039 Dareen Street, Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh [C0117]. **Need to know:** the labour wing's office hours, what to bring to a walk-in visit, and how soon it answers the online form. **Need to know:** whether the labour wing will file and attend a claim for a worker as attorney, and how a worker gives it a power of attorney.

4. **From Bangladesh, a relative can ask the government to act.** Send the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (Welfare-1 branch, saswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030251) a short application describing what happened, with copies of your passport, work permit (iqama), BMET smart card and Saudi ID number, the employer's name and full address, and the wages, allowances or insurance owed. It is free, and within 7 working days the ministry writes to the embassy labour wing and the Wage Earners' Welfare Board to act [C0122]. Template: "My [brother], [name], passport [number], works for [company], [address], Saudi Arabia. He has not been paid since [month]; SR [amount] is owed. Copies attached. Please ask the labour wing in Riyadh to recover his wages." If nothing happens, the officer to report to is the head of the ministry's Mission and Welfare wing: 02-41030219, 01711427769, adsmission@probashi.gov.bd [C0180]. OKUP, a migrant workers' organisation in Bangladesh, gives free legal advice and helps families take cases (01842773300) [C0140, C0144].

5. **If the whole company has stopped paying, claim wage insurance.** It pays only when the employer has not paid 80% or more of its workers for over 6 months, so file friendly settlement (step 2) first [C0073]. It covers up to 6 months' unpaid wages in your Qiwa contract and up to SR 1,000 for a ticket home, at most SR 17,500 in all, and not end-of-service pay [C0074, C0075, C0076]. You can claim while still in Saudi Arabia, and even after moving to a new employer [C0077]. The insurer is Al-Etihad Cooperative Insurance (free phone 800 3040 110) [C0178]. Check whether you are covered by entering your iqama number at [labor.aletihad.sa](https://labor.aletihad.sa/) [C0078]. To claim, use the Worker Portal on the same site (Submit Claim): enter your iqama or border number and mobile number and say whether you have left Saudi Arabia [C0177]. You claim yourself or through a legal representative, with the claim form and its declaration, proof of the last wage you received, a copy of your contract, the official document showing the wages owed, and your iqama copy [C0079]. The employer has 10 working days to object [C0080]. For the ticket you must also show your final exit visa [C0081]. Domestic, seasonal, temporary and farm workers are not covered [C0082]. **Need to know:** how long the insurer takes to pay, and what to do if the claim is refused.

6. **After 3 months without pay you may move to another employer without your employer's consent** (If you want to change employer) [C0055].

### If you want to change employer

**When you can move without your employer's consent.** The new employer must send you the job offer through Qiwa [C0051]. For the 12-month route below, your current contract must also be valid and recorded on Qiwa [C0179]. You can move:

- when your Qiwa contract has ended [C0052];
- during the contract, once you have worked 12 months since you entered Saudi Arabia, after giving 90 days' notice (less if the employer agrees) [C0053, C0054];
- at any time if you haven't been paid for 3 months in a row, your work permit or iqama has expired, the employer has disappeared (travel, prison, death), or you report that the business is secretly run by a foreigner in a Saudi's name and you weren't part of it [C0055, C0056];
- if the employer keeps missing your friendly-settlement sessions [C0040].

Your employer is only notified and can't refuse [C0057].

1. **Find a new employer yourself.** **Need to know:** whether any official service matches foreign workers already in Saudi Arabia with employers. Check the new company exists the same way as in Step 1 (its CR number on the Ministry of Commerce's verification page). Never pay anyone for a job offer or a transfer: the new employer pays the transfer fees [C0020].
2. **The new employer sends you a job offer on Qiwa.** You'll need your Qiwa account, opened with Nafath [C0151]. Log in, open the offer, check the job title and wage, and accept or reject it [C0051].
3. **Your employer is notified; your notice period runs** unless the employer waives it [C0051, C0153].
4. **The new employer completes the move and your new work permit is issued;** once the system approves it, the update is immediate, and it costs you nothing [C0051, C0172, C0020, C0153].

Keep working for your current employer until Qiwa shows the move is done: workers who leave first and work elsewhere often never get a permit and stay illegal [C0150]. Don't share your Absher or Qiwa password or SMS codes with anyone, including your employer [C0087]. **Need to know:** what to do if the transfer is rejected or never completed.

**What happens in practice.** Some employers try to block a move by cancelling the worker's iqama with a final exit visa or by accusing the worker of theft or unpaid debts, and some new employers ask for the old employer's approval even though the law doesn't require it [C0086, C0060, C0061]. If you are blocked, file a friendly-settlement claim (If you aren't paid) [C0072].

### If your employer reports you absent (huroob)

Huroob ("running away") is an employer's report on Qiwa that a worker has stopped coming to work. Your status becomes "Absent from Work", the report can't be withdrawn, and you are removed from the company's records [C0063, C0064]. An employer can file it only if you have no contract recorded on Qiwa, your work permit or iqama is valid for at least 60 more days, and you have been absent more than 30 days in a contract year or more than 15 days in a row [C0062, C0147]. So check that your contract is recorded on Qiwa. When a Qiwa contract ends, you get 60 days to move, leave or sign again before you are marked absent [C0147, C0148, C0069].

1. **Check your status often.** Log in to Absher and look on your dashboard for "Absent from Work" or blocked services [C0112]. You can also search your iqama number, or passport number and nationality, on the labour ministry's [expatriate employee inquiry](https://es.hrsd.gov.sa/services/inquiry/nonsaudiempinquiry.aspx) [C0111]. You should get an SMS, but it may not arrive if your phone number isn't linked to the government system [C0065, C0088].
2. **Within 60 days of the report, move or leave.** Transfer to a new employer on Qiwa (as under If you want to change employer) if you have been in Saudi Arabia over 12 months, or issue yourself a final exit visa from your own Absher account and go home [C0063, C0066]. For a final exit visa you must be in Saudi Arabia, with a valid iqama, a passport valid for at least 60 days, all traffic fines paid, your fingerprints registered and no vehicle in your name [C0165, C0167, C0168]. **Need to know:** the menu path in a worker's own Absher account, the fee, and how long the final exit visa stays valid. If you do neither, you stay "absent" and bear the penalties [C0067].
3. **After 60 days,** if you have been in Saudi Arabia over 12 months, a new employer can still move you on Qiwa, paying any overdue permit fees [C0070, C0071]. Otherwise apply to the embassy's Special Exit Programme (see Going home without valid papers) [C0121]. It doesn't cover people with a police or court case against them [C0120].
4. **If the report is false,** tell the embassy labour wing through its online form, choosing the huroob problem type; if the employer has also accused you of a crime, choose the court or jail case type [C0118]. **Need to know:** whether friendly settlement or the labour court can cancel a false absence report, and how a worker answers a false theft charge.

Anyone who offers to remove a huroob for a fee is a scam; a real change shows only on Absher, Qiwa or the labour ministry's site [C0113].

### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Give notice:** 30 days before the end of a fixed-term contract, 60 days for an open-ended one [C0097]. If you resign, the resignation counts as accepted after 30 days if the employer doesn't answer; the employer can delay it by up to 60 days, and you can withdraw it within 7 days [C0027].
2. **Get your final exit visa yourself** on Absher or Qiwa; you don't need the employer's consent, the employer is notified, and you bear all the consequences, financial or otherwise, of any breach of your contract [C0128, C0127]. The conditions are as in If your employer reports you absent, step 2 [C0165, C0167, C0168]. **Need to know:** the steps in a worker's own Absher or Qiwa account, the fee, and how long the visa stays valid.
3. **Collect what you are owed:** your last wages and entitlements within 1 week of the contract ending, or 2 weeks if you ended it [C0093]. End-of-service pay is half a month's last wage for each of the first 5 years and a full month's for each later year, with part-years counted [C0031]. If you resign, you get a third of it after 2 to 5 years, two-thirds after 5 to 10 years, and all of it after 10 years [C0032]. You also get pay for leave not taken [C0008].
4. **Ask for your service certificate,** free, showing your dates, job and last wage, and get back any papers you gave the employer [C0030]. You can also request one on Qiwa yourself: log in to qiwa.sa, choose Individual Account, then Employment Certificates under Suggested Actions, press Apply Now, choose Service Certificate, select the job, press Next Step and submit; it is approved at once and you download it [C0149, C0176].
5. **The employer pays your ticket home** at the end of the contract [C0020].
6. **If anything is unpaid,** file a friendly-settlement claim within 12 months (If you aren't paid) [C0035].

### Going home on leave

You get at least 21 days' paid leave a year, 30 after 5 years; the employer sets the dates, with 30 days' notice [C0007, C0009]. Apply for an exit and re-entry visa yourself on Absher; the visa doesn't need the employer's approval, the employer is notified, and the employer pays the fee [C0146, C0020]. The fee is SR 200 for one trip of up to 2 months; longer trips and multiple-trip visas cost more (SR 100 for each extra month; SR 500 for multiple trips over 3 months) [C0164, C0169]. You must be in Saudi Arabia, with a passport valid for at least 90 days, your fingerprints registered, all traffic fines paid, and no unpaid fines; your iqama and Qiwa contract must be valid, and for a trip set in months the iqama must be valid for at least 90 days plus the trip [C0165, C0167, C0129]. The visa's "Return Before" date is the last day you can come back [C0166]. On Absher you declare that you understand the measures taken if you don't return before the end of the leave your employer approved [C0171]. **Need to know:** what those measures are; the old three-year entry ban for not returning has been lifted [C0170]. Who pays the ticket depends on your contract [C0152]. At the airport in Bangladesh, the Probashi Kallyan (expatriate welfare) desk checks the re-entry visa of workers going back to Saudi Arabia [C0125].

### Bringing your family

Skilled workers may now bring a spouse, sons under 25 and unmarried daughters; see Family residence [C0130]. Every work permit is graded High-skilled, Skilled or Basic, and the grade shows on your employer's Qiwa account [C0173, C0174]. If the grade is wrong, a request can be made to correct it, which depends on the job title in your Qiwa contract being right [C0173, C0174]. **Need to know:** whether a worker can see their own grade on their Qiwa account, and whether High-skilled workers count as skilled for this rule.

### Scams

Done to workers:

- **Free visa:** see Step 1, Avoid a "free visa".
- **Shell-company visas:** visas issued in the names of companies that don't exist; the broker disappears once the worker lands [C0133]. Check the company's CR number as in Step 1.
- **Tourist visa sold as a job:** brokers promise a 15-month iqama, send the worker on a 3-month tourist visa, and the worker is illegal after 3 months [C0132]. Check the visa type yourself (Step 7).
- **A different job and a short iqama:** workers promised one job have been given another, with an iqama of only 3 months [C0134]. Before paying, check that the job title on the visa (Step 7) is the job promised.
- **Visas bought through "embassy contacts":** officials of the Saudi embassy in Dhaka have been arrested for selling work visas [C0135]. Check any visa yourself (Step 7).
- **"We can remove your huroob":** anyone offering to remove a huroob for a fee is a scam; a real change shows only on Absher, Qiwa or the labour ministry's site [C0113].
- **Asking for your Absher or Qiwa codes:** whoever has them can act in your name; never share them [C0087].

## Work visa: domestic worker

For work in a Saudi household. Saudi Arabia lists 14 domestic jobs, among them domestic worker (housemaid), private (house) driver, nanny, house cook, housekeeper and house farmer (gardener) [D0023]. The Saudi Labour Law exempts domestic workers and their equivalents, and leaves their rights to separate regulations issued by the labour minister [D0171, D0172]; the regulation that sets them is the Domestic Workers Regulation [D0173]. Musaned, the labour ministry's domestic-worker platform, handles their recruitment contracts and pay [D0157, D0045]. The Saudi employer pays the recruitment costs, the iqama (residence card), the work permit and any transfer fees [D0008]. A woman going as a domestic worker pays nothing: an agency allowed to send women domestic workers may not take any migration cost from her [D0059], and Saudi recruitment offices may not take any commission from any domestic worker, before or after she starts work [D0024]. No skill test is needed for domestic work [D0161]. **Need to know:** what an agency may lawfully charge a man going as a house driver or other male domestic worker.

### Step 1: Check you qualify

1. **Age.** For BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) clearance, a woman going as a domestic worker must be 25 to 45 [D0039]. The housekeeping course (step 3) takes women aged 25 to 45, and returnees up to 50 [D0050]. Saudi law bans employing any domestic worker under 21 [D0001], and the contract normally ends at 60 [D0022].
2. **Children and health.** A woman whose youngest child is under 6, who is pregnant, or who has a contagious or incurable disease cannot join the housekeeping course (step 3) [D0051].
3. **Education.** The course asks for at least class 5 pass, though this can be relaxed; the interview checks that she can read and write [D0050, D0054].
4. **Guardian's consent.** She needs a no-objection letter from her legal guardian, both for the course and for BMET clearance [D0052, D0039].
5. **Never let anyone change your age.** Agents have forged a second birth certificate with a false date of birth to get a passport for an under-age girl [D0070, D0071]. Check the birth certificate the passport will use at [everify.bdris.gov.bd](https://everify.bdris.gov.bd/), the birth registration office's check page: enter the 17-digit birth registration number and the real date of birth to verify the record [D0196]. If an agent offers to change the age, call the Probashi call centre on 16135 for advice [E0021]. **Need to know:** which office takes a report of a forged birth certificate used for a passport.

### Step 2: Find the job through an approved agency

A Saudi family chooses a worker through a Saudi recruitment office on Musaned, which exchanges the paperwork electronically with the recruiting agency in Bangladesh; the visa is then sent to the Saudi embassy through the Foreign Ministry's visa platform [D0157, D0159].

1. **Use only an agency the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved to send women domestic workers.** The ministry approves agencies by name; each must deposit a Tk 15 lakh security with BMET, employ at least one woman in its office, and sign an undertaking that its licence can be cancelled if a worker it sent is abused [D0060, D0059]. Ask the agency for the ministry's approval letter (its memo number and date) and check that the agency's name and RL (recruiting licence) number appear in it [D0060]. Check the licence itself is active on OEP, the Overseas Employment Platform, as in Work visa: company worker, step 1. **Need to know:** whether the ministry publishes one current list of all agencies approved to send women domestic workers, where it posts the approval letters, and how to confirm a letter is genuine. Until then, the ministry's enforcement branch takes complaints about agencies (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255) [A0048].
2. **Pay nothing.** If anyone asks a woman for money for the visa, ticket, training or "processing", that is against the approval conditions [D0059]. Complain as in Work visa: company worker, step 1, Before paying.
3. **Get the job in writing before leaving.** The law requires that you know the contract terms, the type of work, the workplace, the wage and the rest periods before you are recruited and leave Bangladesh [D0160]. The wage is the one in the unified contract signed by you and the employer [D0158]. The Arabic text of the contract is the official one, and it must be translated into a language you understand [D0002]. Ask the agency for the Bangla translation before you sign [D0002]. If the agency won't show the contract or its translation, or the terms differ from what you were told, don't sign, and complain as in Work visa: company worker, step 1, Before paying [A0048]. **Need to know:** how a worker in Bangladesh can see her Musaned contract herself before departure.

### Step 3: Do the one-month housekeeping course (women)

The one-month housekeeping course is compulsory for women going to Saudi Arabia as domestic workers; the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's policy sets how BMET's Technical Training Centres (TTCs) run it [D0050, D0055].

1. **You'll need:** your class 5 certificate (if you have one), your legal guardian's no-objection letter, and a photocopy of your birth certificate, NID (national ID card) or passport [D0052]. Bring the originals to the interview [D0054].
2. **Apply online on OEP** ([employee.oep.gov.bd](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp)): log in with your mobile number and the code sent to it, open Training, then Trade/Skills, choose your division, district and centre, press Search, pick the housekeeping course, press Apply, then Process to Enroll and Confirm; "My Course" shows your applications [A0027]. You pay the course fee and choose the centre and the start date online; BMET publishes the admission calendar [D0053]. **Need to know:** the course fee, and the exact name of the housekeeping course in the OEP list.
3. **Interview.** A selection committee at the TTC (the principal as chair, the local women's affairs officer and an assistant director of the district employment and manpower office (or their nominees), and the course coordinator) interviews you, checks your original papers and checks that you can read and write; you must come in person [D0054].
4. **Live at the centre for one month** (24 working days); the course is residential, and you must attend 90% of sessions [D0055]. You learn Saudi food and cooking, household appliances and their safety, the language, the culture, personal safety, how to reach the nearest police station and the Bangladesh embassy, and first aid [D0056].
5. **Pass the final test:** 100 marks, pass mark 70%, with 30 marks for language and 30 for practical work [D0057].
6. **Download the certificate** from the online platform [D0058]. It is valid for 2 years. If you haven't left within 2 to 3 years, do a 3-day refresher; after 3 years, do the full course again. A returnee who worked abroad for at least a year does the 3-day refresher before going again [D0058].
7. **Don't buy a certificate.** Brokers have sold housekeeping certificates for money [D0072].

### Step 4: Medical check, visa and BMET clearance

1. **Medical check, Tasheer fingerprints and checking the visa:** as in Work visa: company worker, steps 4, 5 and 7.
2. **BMET clearance** as in Work visa: company worker, step 8; a woman domestic worker also brings proof of age and her guardian's no-objection letter [D0039].
3. **Before you fly,** save these numbers in your phone and on paper: the embassy labour wing's toll-free line 8001000125 [E0086, E0024], the Probashi call centre 16135 (+880 8000102030 from abroad) [E0021].

### After arrival: your rights

- **Who arranges what:** after you arrive, the employer arranges and pays for your medical test, your iqama and its renewal, and your health insurance or treatment [D0029]. Saudi recruitment offices must tell you the work and the pay, and must house and support you if you leave or refuse to work for the employer [D0025, D0026].
- **Probation:** up to 90 days, during which either side may end the contract [D0003].
- **Hours and rest:** at most 10 hours' work a day, a break of at least half an hour after every 5 hours, at least 8 hours' continuous rest, and one full paid day off a week [D0004, D0005].
- **Pay:** the wage in your contract, paid monthly [D0158]. All employers must pay domestic workers electronically through a bank account or digital wallet in the worker's name linked to Musaned; your mobile number is linked to your iqama number for this [D0045, D0046]. At most a quarter of your wage can be deducted, and only for damage you caused, an advance, or an official order [D0013]. The 2015 recruitment agreement between Saudi Arabia and Bangladesh set a monthly wage of SR 800 for Bangladeshi domestic workers [D0193]. **Need to know:** whether SR 800 is still the agreed wage; some reports give SR 1,000 for women.
- **Housing, food and contact:** suitable housing, and food or a food allowance [D0011]. The employer must let you contact your family and the embassy [D0010] and may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [D0009].
- **Leave:** sick leave of up to 30 days a year with a medical report, the first 15 days on full pay and the rest on half pay [D0007]. After two years, if you renew, 30 days' paid leave and a return ticket home [D0006].
- **Only your own job:** the employer may not make you work for other people or in another job, and you may not work for anyone else [D0012]. Working for yourself or others brings a fine of up to SR 2,000, a ban from working in Saudi Arabia, or both [D0020]; a worker found working illegally on her own account can be fined SR 10,000 and deported [D0030].
- **At the end:** wages and dues within a week of the contract ending, or two weeks if you end it; end-of-service pay of one month's wage for every four years with the employer [D0015, D0014]. If either side ends the contract without a valid reason, the other gets two months' wages unless the contract says otherwise [D0016]. If you leave without a valid reason, you pay your own ticket home [D0019].

### If something goes wrong

**You can leave the employer and keep your rights** if the employer breaks the contract, lied to you about the work, assaults you or behaves immorally towards you, gives you dangerous work, or rents you out to others [D0017]. The labour ministry can move you to a new employer without your employer's consent if your wages are unpaid for three months (in a row or not), nobody collects you from the recruitment office or shelter within 15 days, no iqama is issued or renewed within 30 days, you are rented out, given dangerous work or ill-treated, the employer misses two dispute sessions, or the employer ends the contract during probation [D0031, D0032, D0033, D0034, D0035, D0163, D0164].

**Complain in this order:**

1. **The embassy labour wing.** Call the toll-free line 8001000125 from inside Saudi Arabia [E0086, E0024], or fill in the online form at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/) [D0048]. *You'll need:* your name, a mobile number, your city, your iqama or border number, your passport number, the type of problem (the list includes "domestic worker problems" and "food, housing and pay problems"), a description in Bangla or English, and photos of your iqama and passport if you have them [D0048, D0049]. Follow up on the embassy's "Your answer (SRS)" page ([bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/index.php/main/viewrecord/](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/index.php/main/viewrecord/)): search by your reference number, iqama number or mobile number [D0174, D0175]. The description box asks for the problem in detail [D0048], for example: "I am a housemaid in [city]. My employer [name, phone] has not paid me for [N] months and keeps my passport. Please help me get my wages and go home." In Jeddah, the consulate's labour wing is on +966 12 689 6276 ext 131 [E0036]. A family's complaint from Bangladesh (item 4) can also lead the labour wing to trace the employer and recover wages [D0066].
2. **If you must leave the house, go to the embassy or consulate,** not to a stranger. The embassy is at 8039 Dareen Street, Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh [E0025]. The labour wings run safe homes for women workers in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah, and the Welfare Board (Wage Earners' Welfare Board) pays for staff, food and treatment [E0038, E0019]. Women sheltering there have waited over a month to fly home, sometimes 3 to 6 months [D0065]. Leaving without telling anyone can lead to an absence (huroob) report, which makes you illegal (see If your employer reports you absent (huroob)) [D0063, D0037].
3. **The Saudi labour ministry.** Domestic-worker disputes are filed online on the ministry's site through the "File a domestic labour lawsuit" service; the ordinary settlement service for company workers does not take domestic cases [D0043, D0044]. The ministry tries to settle within 5 working days; if that fails, a committee or the labour court decides [D0027, D0043]. File within 12 months of the contract ending [D0018]. Musaned's line is 920002866 [D0061]. If you can't use a phone freely in the house, a relative in Bangladesh can complain for you (item 4) [D0062]. The embassy labour wing helps workers settle disputes with employers and attends labour courts with them [E0034]. **Need to know:** the exact link and steps of the "File a domestic labour lawsuit" service, and whether a worker without Absher can use it.
4. **From Bangladesh, a relative can act.** For a woman worker, send an application to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) describing what happened, with copies of the worker's passport, BMET smart card and iqama, the agency's details, and the accused person's mobile number and email; it's free, and within 7 working days the ministry writes to the labour wing or BMET to act [D0040]. To bring her home, apply the same way with proof that she is a migrant worker, her workplace and home address in Saudi Arabia, the agency's name and address and the employer's mobile number [D0041]. These two services are for women workers [D0040, D0041]. For any worker, man or woman, a relative can apply about unpaid wages to the ministry's Welfare-1 branch (saswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030251) with details of what happened, proof the worker is a migrant (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Saudi ID number), the employer's name and full address and the dues owed; it's free, and within 7 working days the ministry writes to the labour wing and the Welfare Board [E0001]. If nothing happens, write to the Joint Secretary (Mission and Welfare), 02-41030219, adsmission@probashi.gov.bd [E0002]. A relative can also ask for a safe-home place through the district probashi kallyan (expatriates' welfare) branch at the Deputy Commissioner's office, the district employment and manpower office (DEMO), or the Welfare Board directly; it's free [E0019]. The Probashi call centre (16135) gives advice [E0021].
5. **Legal help.** OKUP, a migrants' organisation in Bangladesh, gives free legal advice and help filing cases, on 01842773300 [A0046].

### Scams

- **Age changed on the passport:** agents raise a girl's age with a second birth certificate [D0070, D0071].
- **Bought training certificate:** brokers sell the housekeeping certificate without the course [D0072].
- **Households with no job:** brokers sell house-driver and domestic-worker visas from households that don't need anyone; the worker arrives to no job, and working elsewhere is illegal [D0074, D0012].
- **Money taken from a woman:** any fee taken from a woman domestic worker breaks the agency's approval conditions [D0059].

### House drivers

1. **Cost and pay.** Agencies have asked Tk 4-6 lakh for a house-driver visa [D0076], although the Saudi employer is meant to pay the recruitment costs [D0008] and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker [D0024]. Get any payment agreed in writing and pay only into the agency's bank account, as in Work visa: company worker, step 1, Before paying; the lawful charge is still a Need to know (above). New private drivers earn about SR 1,500-1,800 a month [D0077].
2. **Check the visa.** Brokers promise a company job and send the worker as a private driver, or never arrange the licence [D0078]. Check the visa online as in Work visa: company worker, step 7: the visa shows the occupation and the sponsor's name [B0073, B0075]. The occupation must be private driver, one of the domestic jobs [D0023], and the sponsor must be the household employer you were told about [B0075]. **Need to know:** how "private driver" is written in Arabic on the visa.
3. **Licence.** The employer must arrange and pay for your Saudi driving licence [D0028]. You can drive on your Bangladeshi licence for at most 3 months if it is translated by an accredited agency [D0079]. For the Saudi licence, register at a driving school on Absher (Traffic Services, Book Driving School), do the medical at an approved clinic and open a file at the school; a full beginner course costs about SR 2,500-3,000 [D0081, D0080]. **Need to know:** whether the official theory test is offered in Bangla; ask the driving school when you open the file.

## Umrah visa

For Muslims of any nationality, all year except the Hajj season (15 Dhul Qa'dah to 15 Dhul Hijjah) [D0082]. The visa costs USD 80, plus USD 10.50 visa service fee, USD 10.50 insurance service fee and the insurance itself; a single-entry visa is valid for 90 days and allows up to 90 days' stay [D0084, D0085]. Packages from Bangladesh cost about Tk 1.2 lakh for an economy group to Tk 3 lakh or more [D0155].

1. **Choose a licensed agency.** Bangladeshis can't apply on Nusuk, the Saudi Umrah booking platform, by themselves; a licensed operator applies for them [D0087, D0083]. The Ministry of Religious Affairs publishes each year's list of licensed Hajj and Umrah agencies on its website and on the Hajj portal, [hajj.gov.bd](https://hajj.gov.bd/); pay no agency that isn't on it [D0092]. Fake agencies advertise on Facebook and YouTube [D0093].
2. **You'll need:** a passport valid for 6 months with 2 blank pages, a white-background photo, and a meningitis (ACWY) vaccination certificate from at least 10 days before arrival [D0088, D0089]. Enrol your face and fingerprints in Saudi Visa Bio, the Saudi Foreign Ministry's app on Google Play and the App Store, free to install: after the visa application, it takes your email address, a photo of your passport, your face and your fingers; anyone 6 or older can use it, under-18s with an adult [D0168, D0090]. The agent books a Nusuk-approved hotel and transport [D0088].
3. **Travel within 30 days** of the visa being issued, or it is cancelled automatically [D0091].
4. **Don't work, and don't stay for Hajj.** The Umrah visa doesn't allow Hajj [D0086], and the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah says Umrah visas can't be used for work [D0094]. Working illegally brings a fine of SR 10,000 and deportation the first time [D0030]. Brokers also sell tourist visas as jobs [D0075].
5. **If the agency cheats you,** write an application to the Secretary, Ministry of Religious Affairs, saying what you paid, when and how, and what wasn't delivered, with the agency's name, licence number and address; the ministry can order the agency to refund within 7 working days under the Hajj and Umrah Management Act 2021, or take action against it [D0176]. The ministry's Hajj and Umrah section: +880 2 55100899, morahajsection@gmail.com [D0177]. **Need to know:** whether a refused Umrah visa can be appealed, and who helps a pilgrim stranded in Saudi Arabia when the agency fails.

## Hajj visa

Only within Bangladesh's yearly quota, through the Ministry of Religious Affairs [D0106]. For Hajj 2027 the quota was 78,500, it is full, and the registration deadline of 24 September was not extended [D0106]. Children under 15, people with serious chronic or infectious illness, and women late in or at high risk in pregnancy can't go [D0105]. **Need to know:** when pre-registration for the next Hajj opens and how it is announced; for questions, call the Hajj helpline 16136 [D0099].

1. **Pre-register** during the window the ministry announces: pay Tk 30,000, with your NID or passport (birth registration if under 18) [D0095, D0096]. For the government package, register yourself on [hajj.gov.bd](https://hajj.gov.bd/) or at a DC office, Islamic Foundation office, Union Digital Centre or the Dhaka Hajj Office [D0097]; for a private package, go through a licensed agency on the ministry's list [D0092].
2. **Primary registration:** deposit Tk 3.5 lakh; for the government package pay at any Sonali Bank branch with a voucher, for a private package into the agency's account shown on the Hajj portal [D0102, D0104].
3. **Pay the rest and complete final registration** by the deadline (10 December 2026 for Hajj 2027) [D0103]. 2027 government packages cost Tk 5,05,648 to Tk 6,15,263, and the private standard package Tk 5,13,648 [D0107, D0108].
4. **Give the agency your passport and registration certificate, and do your biometrics** in the Saudi Visa Bio app [D0098]. Collect your passport, ticket, visa, ID card and luggage tags from the agency before the flight [D0101]. Helpline 16136, or +880 9602666707 from abroad in office hours [D0099, D0100].
5. **Don't do Hajj on any other visa.** Hajj without a permit brings a fine of up to SR 20,000, deportation and a ban of up to 10 years [D0109, D0110].
6. **Complaints.** Call the Hajj helpline 16136 (+880 9602666707 from abroad in office hours) [D0099, D0100]. If a private agency takes your money and doesn't deliver, apply to the Ministry of Religious Affairs as in Umrah visa, item 5 [D0176, D0177]. **Need to know:** the Hajj office's complaint route for services missing during Hajj.

## Family residence

For a worker's spouse, sons under 25 and unmarried daughters, if the worker's work permit is graded skilled [D0111]. Every work permit is graded high-skilled, skilled or basic: skilled covers occupational groups 4 to 8 of the Saudi occupation classification, with pay above a ministry threshold and the professional accreditation for the job, while elementary jobs (group 9), or pay below the threshold, count as basic [D0112, D0113, D0114]. Your grade shows in your employer's Qiwa account, and you can ask for a wrong grade to be corrected [D0115]. A skilled worker's family can get entry visas at the same time as the worker's own work visa, finishing residence after arrival [D0111]. **Need to know:** whether the new rule replaced the older salary and profession conditions below, and whether family visas are given to basic-level workers at all.

1. **Check you qualify.** The salary on your GOSI (General Organization for Social Insurance) record should be at least SR 3,500 a month [D0116].
2. **Prepare the papers in Bangladesh.** Get the marriage certificate, the children's birth certificates and your degree certificate attested and translated into Arabic [D0122]. Bangladesh and Saudi Arabia are both parties to the Apostille Convention, under which an apostille certificate replaces embassy legalisation, and Saudi Arabia did not object to Bangladesh joining [D0169, D0170]. Apply for an e-apostille on [mygov.bd](https://www.mygov.bd/); it carries a QR code anyone can check [B0066, B0067]. **Need to know:** whether Absher and the Saudi embassy accept an apostille in place of attestation for family visa papers.
3. **Apply on Absher** (the Interior Ministry's portal): My Services, Expatriates Services, New Family Visa Application; if Absher shows an error, apply at the recruitment (Istiqdam) office instead [D0119]. Print and sign the form and give it to your company's HR, which attests it on the Chamber of Commerce site within 30 days (SR 30) [D0120].
4. **Pay the SR 2,000 visa fee** through SADAD, the Saudi bill-payment system in your bank's app or internet banking, with your iqama and application numbers [D0195, D0117]; after 2-3 working days Absher shows the visa number; send it to your family [D0121]. **Need to know:** whether the SR 2,000 is charged once per family or for each family member.
5. **The family applies through Tasheer,** the company that runs the Saudi visa service centres, in Dhaka or Chattogram: it checks the papers, takes biometrics and fees and sends the passports to the Saudi embassy [D0178, D0179]. Book at [vc.tasheer.com/appointment](https://vc.tasheer.com/appointment) as in Work visa: company worker, step 5 [B0044]. Tasheer asks for a medical report for resident visas [B0147]. Track the file with Track Status on vc.tasheer.com [B0157]. **Need to know:** Tasheer Bangladesh's document list, fee and processing time for a family residence visa, and whether a refusal can be appealed.
6. **After arrival,** each dependant pays SR 400 a month after the first 90 days [D0118]. A dependant can work in health or education through Ajeer, the labour ministry's platform (see Changing visa type) [E0060]. Register a baby on Absher (My Services, Services, Civil Affairs, Newborn Registration) within 30 days; late registration is fined [D0123, D0124]. A child of a Bangladeshi father or mother is a Bangladeshi citizen [D0125]. For the baby's Bangladeshi passport, first get the Saudi birth registration certificate (Shahada Milad), then a Bangladeshi birth registration certificate: apply at the consulate in Jeddah, or register in Bangladesh at a parent's permanent address with Saudi Arabia as the place of birth [D0167]. Then apply online at [passport.gov.bd](http://www.passport.gov.bd/) and bring the Saudi certificate, copies of both parents' passports and iqamas, the Bangladeshi birth certificate, an A4 colour photo of the baby on a grey or white background, and passport photos of the baby and a parent [D0166]. **Need to know:** the fee and processing time, and whether the Riyadh embassy follows the same steps.

## Family visit visa

For relatives of someone living and working in Saudi Arabia; the visa is valid for 90 days, allows up to 90 days' stay, and costs USD 80 plus USD 21 in service fees, plus insurance [D0128]. Bangladeshis without a US, UK or Schengen visit visa can't get the e-visa and go through a visa service centre [D0126, D0127]. Umrah is allowed on it, Hajj is not [D0129]. Saudi Arabia can suspend new visit visas for Bangladeshis around the Hajj season and tell holders already inside to leave or face fines and a ban [D0137, D0138]. **Need to know:** whether the suspension will recur before Hajj 2027.

1. **Your relative applies** on the Foreign Ministry's visa platform ([visa.mofa.gov.sa](https://visa.mofa.gov.sa/)): log in through Nafath, the Saudi digital ID app, choose Family visit visa, press Apply, fill in the details, press Add and enter your details exactly as in your passport, approve the agreement, Save and print [D0131, D0130]. The relative needs a work iqama with at least 3-4 months left [D0133].
2. **The employer approves it** online through "Ratify individuals applications"; a domestic worker's employer can do the same [D0132, D0134]. Then a visa document number is issued; your relative sends it to you [D0135].
3. **You apply through Tasheer** (Work visa: company worker, step 5) with the visa document number and proof of the relationship, such as a marriage or birth certificate [D0135, D0136, D0178]. Track it with Track Status on vc.tasheer.com [B0157]. **Need to know:** Tasheer Bangladesh's full document list, fee and processing time for this visa, and whether a refusal can be appealed.
4. **Don't work.** A visit visa can't become a job (see Changing visa type) [D0094, D0030].

## Tourist visa

Bangladeshis can't get the online e-visa unless they hold a US, UK or Schengen visit visa [D0127, D0139].

- **Through the Tasheer visa centre:** USD 80 plus USD 21 in service fees, plus insurance; 90 days [D0126, D0128, D0139].
- **Package visa:** an instant tourist e-visa sold with a package of confirmed flights and hotel by a travel agent approved by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Tourism; it is open to Bangladeshi citizens [D0140, D0181]. Buy it only from a partner listed on the Saudi tourism site [Visit Saudi](https://www.visitsaudi.com/en/campaigns/package-visa): Reserval ([reserval.com/packagevisa](https://www.reserval.com/packagevisa)), Almosafer, webook.com ([webook.com/en/e-visa](https://webook.com/en/e-visa)) and Almatar ([almatar.com](https://almatar.com/en/?visaType=tourist-visa)) [D0182]. Choose the package, upload your passport details and a photo, pay, and the visa follows an automatic check [D0184]. The package must include a round-trip flight and a licensed hotel of 4 stars or more, and cost at least SR 4,000 per adult for the first two days plus SR 1,000 for each further day [D0183]. It is issued within 48 hours, valid for 3 months, single entry, and can't be changed after issue [D0140, D0141].
- **If refused:** **Need to know:** whether a refused tourist visa can be appealed, and who to complain to about a package seller.
- **Don't work.** Brokers sell 3-month tourist visas as jobs with a "15-month iqama"; the worker becomes illegal [D0075, D0030].

## Business visit visa

For meetings, conferences and partnerships, not a job: the visit must not be linked to a permanent work contract [D0144]. The Saudi business sends an invitation letter; after the letter and the application it takes 1-2 working days [D0145]. Applications made outside Saudi Arabia go through Tasheer [D0178]. It costs USD 80 plus a USD 10.50 visa service fee and a USD 10.50 insurance service fee, plus the insurance; a single-entry visa is valid 90 days, a multiple-entry one a year, each with stays of up to 90 days [D0185, D0186]. A "business visa" for a factory job is illegal work [D0144, D0030]. **Need to know:** the documents a Bangladeshi applicant needs (for example, how the invitation letter must be attested), and whether a refusal can be appealed.

## Student visa

Applying on [studyinsaudi.sa](https://studyinsaudi.sa/), the Ministry of Education's platform, is free, and agents who charge to apply for you are not authorised [D0149]. Official Saudi sites end in .sa or gov.sa [D0192]. Full scholarships cover tuition and study costs; for Bangladeshis they have also covered airfare, housing, meals, books and a monthly stipend [D0148, D0154].

1. **Check you qualify.** Age limits are 17 to 25 for a bachelor's, 30 for a master's and 35 for a PhD, and IELTS 5.5 or English-medium schooling is needed for courses taught in English [D0151, D0152]. **Need to know:** the next application window; the last one closed on 6 October 2026 [D0153].
2. **Apply on the platform.** Choose up to 5 universities and 3 programmes at each [D0150]. Upload a valid passport, a medical report showing you are free of infectious diseases, attested certificates and the other documents the university asks for [D0188]. Only contact details can be changed after you submit [D0189].
3. **Wait for the decision.** The university reviews the application; if it approves, the university and the Ministry of Education complete the final admission [D0187]. Follow the status on the platform until it shows accepted or rejected; a rejection means an admission requirement wasn't met [D0190]. Ask for help through a support ticket on the platform [D0191].
4. **Get the study visa.** The long-term study visa needs admission from an accredited institution, proof of funds or a scholarship, a medical report, medical insurance and proof of the language of study; it is renewed yearly [D0146, D0147]. **Need to know:** where a Bangladeshi student gets the study visa issued (Tasheer or the embassy), its fee and processing time, and whether a refusal can be appealed.
5. **Check the offer is real.** Admission decisions come through the platform, and an agent charging a fee to apply for you is not authorised [D0187, D0149]. **Need to know:** how to confirm an admission letter directly with the university.

## Other visas

**Temporary work visa.** A Saudi employer requests it on Qiwa (the labour ministry's work platform) for project or contract work; the worker may stay 90 days, with one 90-day extension, and the visa is not linked to an iqama (the Saudi residence permit) [E0065, E0066]. The employer pays the SR 1,000 fee [E0066]. **Need to know:** how a Bangladeshi gets this visa (who finds the job, whether a recruiting agency is involved and whether BMET gives clearance for it), what the worker pays, and the scams on this route.

**Hajj seasonal work visa.** Issued only for work in the Hajj season: entry from the first day of Shawwal, a 4-month stay, not renewable and not convertible to a work visa; a SR 1,000 entry fee is required [E0067]. **Need to know:** whether it is issued to Bangladeshis, and through whom.

**Stopover (transit) visa.** For a passenger flying Saudia through Saudi Arabia to another country, and open to Bangladeshis [E0141, E0068]. Apply on saudia.com while booking the flight (on the Seats & Extras or Additional Services page) with a passport valid at least 6 months, a confirmed onward ticket, a visa for the final destination and a photo of 200 x 200 pixels, at most 20 KB; you pay for the flight and visa together and get the visa and insurance by email [E0141, E0142]. It allows one stay of up to 96 hours, can't be extended, and passport control decides entry on arrival [E0141]. **Need to know:** the fee, and whether any work is allowed on it.

**Premium Residency.** Residence not tied to an employer, sold by the Premium Residency Center at pr.gov.sa [E0071]. **Need to know:** its cost and who qualifies.

**Medical treatment visa.** Bangladeshis who don't hold a US, UK or Schengen visa, or residence in the US, UK, EU or a Gulf country, can't get it online; they apply on the KSA Visa platform (ksavisa.sa) and then through a visa service centre or the Saudi embassy [E0069, E0070]. In Dhaka the Saudi visa centre is run by Tasheer, at Jamuna Future Park (ground floor), KA-244, Kuril, Progoti Shoroni; book an appointment at vc.tasheer.com [E0178, B0040]. It costs a USD 80 visa fee, a USD 10.50 visa digital-service fee and a USD 10.50 insurance digital-service fee, plus the insurance itself, priced by the insurer you choose [E0069]. A single-entry visa lasts 90 days and a multiple-entry one a year, with stays of up to 90 days; processing takes from a minute to 3 working days; Hajj is not allowed on it [E0069]. **Need to know:** the documents needed, and whether Tasheer's Dhaka centre takes medical visa applications.

## Changing visa type

**Visit, tourist, Umrah or business visit visa to a work iqama: not allowed.** Jawazat (the Passports Directorate) says a visit visa can't be converted into an iqama, and family visitors may not work [E0054]. The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah says Umrah visas may not be used for work [E0056]. People on social media offer to "convert" visit visas for a fee; Jawazat has denied these claims [E0054, E0055]. The Interior Ministry asks people to report breaches of residence rules to the police on 911 in Makkah, Madinah, Riyadh and the Eastern region, or 999 elsewhere [E0146]. Working on a tourist or Umrah visa brings fines, jail, deportation and a ban of 3 to 10 years [E0059].

**Seasonal work visa to an iqama: not allowed** [E0067]. **Need to know:** whether a temporary work visa can be converted to an iqama.

**Family dependant to worker in health or education.** A spouse or mahram (a close male relative who is the guardian) living in Saudi Arabia on a dependant iqama can work for a health or education employer without moving to its sponsorship, through Ajeer ([ajeer.qiwa.sa](https://ajeer.qiwa.sa)), the labour ministry's platform for lending and placing workers [E0148, E0152, E0147].

1. **You'll need:** a valid dependant iqama (the main worker's iqama must be valid too), to be 18 or over, any professional certificate the job requires (for example a nurse's or teacher's licence), and an offer from a health or education employer in the low green band or higher of Nitaqat [E0150, E0151]. Nitaqat grades employers by the share of Saudis they employ [E0060], under Saudisation, the policy of reserving jobs for Saudi nationals [E0124].
2. **The employer issues the contract on Ajeer** (Contracts, Issue Contract, Contracting Service Between Workforce, Ajeer Accompanying Service); once the contract is approved, Ajeer issues the dependant's work permit [E0149, E0060].
3. **It is free** [E0150].
4. **If it doesn't go through,** call Ajeer on 920011040 or the labour ministry on 19911 [E0147, E0051]. **Need to know:** how long it takes.

**Need to know:** whether and how a dependant can work outside health and education.

**Domestic worker to another household.** Done on Musaned ([musaned.gov.sa](https://musaned.gov.sa)), the labour ministry's platform for domestic workers [E0153, E0180].

1. **You'll need:** the agreement of three people: your current employer, you and the new employer [E0063, E0153]; and your own account on Absher, the Interior Ministry's portal [E0154].
2. **Your current employer starts the transfer on Musaned** and fills in the request; the new employer accepts or rejects it on Musaned; a new contract is drawn up and the request completed through Absher [E0153].
3. **You pay nothing:** the service is free and the employer bears transfer fees [E0154, E0180]. Payments between the employers go through Musaned, within maximums the ministry sets, SR 16,188 for a Bangladeshi worker [E0062].
4. **You get** a new work contract for a fixed period [E0144]. **Need to know:** how long the transfer takes, and how you can see the new contract's terms before you agree.
5. **If your employer refuses, or there is a dispute over money,** file a claim with the domestic workers' dispute committees: on the ministry's e-services Individuals Portal or the unified app, choose My Services, then Settlement Claims for Domestic Workers; it is free, and you need your original iqama or passport [E0155]. Musaned's line is 920002866 [E0140]; the embassy labour wing's free line is 8001000125 [E0024]. **Need to know:** whether a domestic worker can move without the current employer's consent.

**Domestic worker to a company job.** Possible only in your first year in Saudi Arabia: once your iqama has been renewed after a year, the request is refused [E0133].

1. **You'll need:** a company that has signed a work contract with you for the new job, and your mobile number, to which Qiwa sends the request [E0133, E0132].
2. **The company requests the transfer on Qiwa,** naming your new job [E0132].
3. **Qiwa sends you an SMS;** approve or reject it within 10 days [E0132].
4. **Your household employer is then asked to agree** [E0134]. Qiwa support: 920000105 [E0181]. **Need to know:** what happens if the household employer refuses, what the transfer costs and how long it takes.

**Company worker to another company:** see If you want to change employer.

**Job title on the iqama.** Only the employer can apply, on Qiwa, through the Change expatriate profession service [E0048]. It is free, the worker pays nothing, and the change takes effect immediately; the employer must be in the platinum or green Nitaqat band, and a job reserved for Saudis can't be given to a foreign worker [E0048, E0049, E0050, E0137]. Since January 2026 some titles, including sales representative, marketing specialist and procurement manager, can't be given to foreign workers this way [E0064]. **Need to know:** what a worker can do if the employer refuses to change the title.

## Asylum

Saudi Arabia has no asylum system and has not signed the 1951 Refugee Convention [E0072]. Its law says the state may grant political asylum "if public interest so dictates", but in practice it generally does not grant asylum [E0072, E0074]. People in the country illegally, including those who overstayed a pilgrimage visa, are refused refugee status and are pressed to leave or deported [E0074]. The only protection is through UNHCR, the UN refugee agency: people it recognises may stay temporarily as visitors while it arranges resettlement elsewhere or return home, and only if they keep valid identity papers; people without travel documents are sometimes detained indefinitely [E0073, E0075]. UNHCR's Riyadh office takes applications by email to saprstaf@unhcr.org, giving your full name, your family members' names, date of birth, sex, nationality, address, phone, any earlier UNHCR registration and the reason [E0164]. Its toll-free line, 009668001000281, answers on Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday [E0163]. The office is in Riyadh's Diplomatic Quarter, which you can enter only with an appointment [E0165].

## Sending money home

**Use a legal channel.** BMET's advice is to send money only through legal channels, never hundi (the illegal informal channel) or unlicensed agents [E0046]. Saudi police have arrested expatriates for moving money abroad outside legal channels, and Bangladesh's Financial Intelligence Unit has frozen thousands of bKash, Nagad and Rocket accounts linked to hundi [E0079, E0078]. Without a valid iqama you can't use a bank account or send money through formal channels [B0112].

**Compare the cost** on the World Bank's price comparison for Saudi Arabia to Bangladesh, [remittanceprices.worldbank.org/corridor/SA/BD](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/corridor/SA/BD), which lists each provider's fee, exchange-rate margin, total cost and speed [E0080]. In its 2025 survey, sending SR 750 cost about 7.2% in total with the STC Pay mobile-wallet app, 7.5% with TeleMoney, 8.3% with Western Union and 8.7% with MoneyGram, but 14.8-15.9% at bank branches (Al Rajhi, Albilad and Saudi British Bank, now Saudi Awwal Bank) because of a SR 75 fee [E0080]. STC Pay delivers in under an hour to cash pickup, a bank account or a mobile wallet [E0080]. **Need to know:** what STC Pay, TeleMoney and the other apps need to open an account.

**To send to a bKash account:**

1. **You'll need:** the family member's bKash number and full name exactly as registered when the account was opened, and the cash or money in your app [E0083].
2. **Use a bKash partner in Saudi Arabia:** Saudi Awwal Bank, STC Pay, URpay, Mobily Pay, tiqmo, barq, Western Union or MoneyGram [E0082]. At an agent or branch, give the number and name and hand over the cash; in an app, enter the same details [E0083].
3. **Limit:** up to Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer, as many times as you like [E0084].
4. **Your family gets** the money in bKash, plus the 2.5% government incentive on legal remittances, added automatically with no papers to submit [E0076, E0081, E0085]. They can cash it out at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs for Tk 7 per thousand [E0084].
5. **If the money doesn't arrive or went to the wrong number,** complain first to the company you sent it with (its branch, agent or app) and to bKash on 16247, then to that company's complaint cell [E0083, E0173]. If it is still not solved, complain to Bangladesh Bank's Customer Interest Protection Centre: 16236 during office hours on working days, bb.cipc@bb.org.bd, or its online form, with a description, your name, address and phone, and the provider's final reply letter [E0172, E0174]. A family member complaining for the account holder needs the holder's written authorisation [E0174].

## Going home without valid papers

Overstaying any visa can bring a fine of up to SR 50,000, up to 6 months in prison and deportation [E0053]. The ban on returning usually runs 3 to 5 years for overstaying or a huroob report (the employer reporting you absent) and 3 to 10 years for working on a tourist or Umrah visa, counted from the day you leave [E0057, E0058, E0059]. If you leave without a legitimate reason, you pay your own ticket [E0136].

**Special Exit Programme (SEP), the Bangladesh Embassy's route to an exit visa for workers with an expired iqama, no iqama or a huroob report.**

1. **Check you qualify.** It covers workers whose sponsor's company is under the labour offices of Riyadh, Al Qassim, Hail, Al Jouf, Northern Borders or the Eastern Province and whose iqama has expired, who never got an iqama after arriving, or who have a huroob report; house drivers and male domestic workers with an expired iqama also qualify [E0027]. It doesn't cover women domestic workers, anyone with a police or court case or wanted by the police (matlub), or anyone who didn't leave on an earlier exit visa; a person with a case must go to the authority that wants them and settle it first [E0028].
2. **You'll need:** your iqama number, your original iqama and a copy of your passport showing its validity [E0029, E0030].
3. **Apply online,** without visiting or phoning: open [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html), read the rules, and press "Special Exit programme(SEP) Application Form" [E0026, E0031].
4. **Check progress** by entering your iqama number in SEP Search at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub/). If an appointment appears, print it and go to the embassy on that day with the documents [E0031].
5. **What happens next.** For a Riyadh company, the embassy submits your case to the labour office, which sends the exit clearance to the embassy; you then hand in your original iqama and passport copy, and the embassy sends them to Jawazat for the exit visa [E0029]. For a company under another labour office, or a huroob case with a sponsor outside Riyadh, the embassy gives you a letter; you take it to that labour office or Jawazat and collect the exit visa there in person [E0029, E0030].
6. **How long:** the embassy gives no time limit. In practice, workers have waited several months for clearance, most of them without work in the meantime [E0096].

**Need to know:** whether SEP costs anything, whether Saudi fines must be paid before the exit visa is issued, and what to do if the application is refused.

**If your sponsor is under any other labour office** (for example Makkah, Madinah, Tabuk, Asir, Jazan, Najran or Al Baha), SEP doesn't cover you [E0027]. **Need to know:** the procedure for these workers; contact the Jeddah consulate's labour wing (Contacts) [E0166].

**For any other problem with papers, use the embassy's online request form** at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/): choose the problem (for example huroob, no iqama, in jail, salary, domestic worker), describe it in Bangla or English, and attach copies of your iqama and passport [E0089]. If there is no reply, call the labour wing free on 8001000125 [E0024], then have a relative write to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare in Bangladesh, Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), as below [E0004, E0005]. **Need to know:** how long the embassy takes to reply to the form.

**Without a passport:** a Bangladeshi without a passport needs a travel permit (outpass) from the embassy's passport wing to go home [E0088]. Call the passport wing free on 8001000126, or book a "Consular & Welfare Service" appointment at [passportriyadh.com/appointment](https://passportriyadh.com/appointment/), which asks for your full name, phone number, passport number and OID number [E0023, E0131]. If you are held in a deportation centre, embassy officials visit and arrange the travel permit there [E0088]. **Need to know:** the documents and fee for a travel permit in Saudi Arabia, what the OID number is, and how to book without a passport number.

**Grace periods.** Saudi Arabia sometimes sets a window in which people with expired visas can leave without a fine; the Bangladesh Embassy announces these in notices, and posts its notices on the Notice page of its website, [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/notice.html](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/notice.html) [E0176, E0175]. In a past window, people whose visit, Umrah or final exit visa expired on 25 February 2026 could leave until 18 April 2026 by going to the airport with a ticket, with no fine [E0087].

**From Bangladesh, a relative can ask the government to bring a worker home.**

1. **You'll need:** proof the person is a migrant worker (copies of passport, BMET smart card, iqama or work permit), the workplace and home addresses in Saudi Arabia, the recruiting agency's name and address, and the employer's mobile number [E0005].
2. **Send an application** describing what happened, with these papers, to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) [E0004, E0005].
3. **It is free.** Within 7 working days of the letter, the ministry asks the embassy, the Welfare Board or BMET to bring the worker home [E0005].
4. **If nothing happens,** write to the head of the ministry's Mission and Welfare wing (adsmission@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030219, 01711427769) [E0002].

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

**If a worker dies in Saudi Arabia:**

1. **Call the embassy.** Death hotline +966570212180; labour wing 8001000125, free inside Saudi Arabia [E0086, E0024]. The labour wing registers the death, follows up with the employer and the authorities, and issues the no-objection certificate (NOC) needed to bury the body in Saudi Arabia or send it home [E0033, E0034].
2. **The employer pays** to prepare the body and fly it home, unless the family agrees to burial in Saudi Arabia [E0135].
3. **The family in Bangladesh applies to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board** (the government fund for migrant workers, Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Dhaka) [E0145], saying whether it wants the body brought home or buried in Saudi Arabia; the Board sends the family's wish to the embassy and follows up until the body arrives, free [E0017]. **Need to know:** how the family applies to the Board (in person, by post or online), which papers it needs, and how long it takes. Or apply to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) with a description of what happened, the cause of death, copies of the worker's passport, work permit or iqama, BMET smart card and Saudi ID number, and the death certificate; it is free and the ministry acts within 7 working days [E0004]. If the worker left with BMET clearance or joined the Welfare Board through the embassy, the Board pays to bring the body home; if not, the family can still apply and the Board asks the ministry to pay [E0091]. BRAC, a Bangladeshi development organisation, runs a Migration Programme that helps families make these applications [E0111, E0143]; its Information Center opposite Dhaka airport is on 01700797182 and 01700797183 [E0170].
4. **The cause of death** is recorded in Saudi Arabia, usually as "natural", and Bangladesh does not check it [E0093]. **Need to know:** how a family can ask for a post-mortem or challenge the recorded cause of death.
5. **At the airport,** the Welfare Board desk phones the family about the papers, hands over the body at the cargo section and gives a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, free and on the spot [E0042, E0007]. **You'll need:** a copy of the worker's passport, the death certificate, the NOC, the original air waybill, a heirship or family certificate from the chairman of the Union Parishad (the elected local council) or the ward councillor, the NID of the wife, father or mother, the BMET clearance card if any, and a power of attorney if someone else collects [E0007, E0042]. The Board's ambulance takes the body from Dhaka, Chattogram or Sylhet airport, free [E0008].
6. **Claim the Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant.** It is for families of workers who went with BMET clearance or joined the Board through the embassy [E0037]. After the body arrives, the Board writes to the family listing the papers [E0015]. **You'll need:** a family-members certificate from the Union Parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor, an undertaking on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper (samples of both are on [wewb.gov.bd](https://www.wewb.gov.bd/)), a statement of the receiving person's account at any scheduled bank showing the account and routing numbers, and 2 passport-size colour photos of each heir [E0015]. Send them to the Board; it is free, and the money goes straight to the account, with an SMS, within 60 working days of complete papers [E0015]. In practice, families report going back to the Board again and again before they are paid [E0092].
7. **Claim the Tk 10 lakh life insurance.** Every worker who left with BMET clearance from 12 March 2023 has paid a one-time Tk 1,000 premium covering 5 years from the card's issue [E0044]. If the body comes home, no application is needed; if the worker was buried in Saudi Arabia or died after coming home, apply online at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/) or at the Board with the BMET clearance card and a passport copy; it is free and paid within 30 working days [E0013]. Claims are often rejected when the death is recorded as suicide, and middlemen offer to speed up payouts for a large cut; the claim is free and needs no middleman [E0097, E0098].
8. **Claim compensation and unpaid wages from Saudi Arabia.** The embassy collects compensation and dues for heirs who give it a power of attorney, attends the Saudi courts, and sends the money through BMET [E0032]. When compensation is possible, the Board writes to the family; the family prepares a power of attorney (the Board's legal cell helps) and the same four papers as for the grant, and the Board sends them to the embassy and follows the case until it ends; it pays the heirs within 60 working days of receiving the money, free [E0016]. A relative can also apply to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) with proof of migrant status, the employer's name and full address and the wages, allowances and insurance owed; the ministry instructs the embassy and the Board within 7 working days, free [E0003].
9. **Work-death compensation from GOSI.** GOSI (the General Organization for Social Insurance, the Saudi social insurance agency) insures every worker against work injury whatever their nationality, paid for by the employer [E0100, E0101]; domestic workers are covered only if GOSI's board decides so [E0160]. If a non-Saudi worker dies from a work injury, GOSI pays the family a lump sum of 84 times the worker's average monthly wage registered with GOSI, and pays to prepare the body and send it home [E0157, E0158, E0159]. The claim is handled at a GOSI office (toll-free 199044) [E0161, E0162]; a family in Bangladesh claims through the embassy with a power of attorney, as in step 8 [E0032, E0016]. **Need to know:** whether domestic workers are now covered, which papers GOSI needs from the family, and how long payment takes.

**If a worker is injured or seriously ill in Saudi Arabia,** the worker or the family applies to the Welfare Board with the BMET clearance or membership and a passport copy; the Board contacts the embassy to bring the worker home and, in some cases, admits the worker to hospital, free [E0014]. At the airport, the desk takes sick workers to hospital by ambulance [E0040].

**Treatment money after coming home:** injured or seriously ill returnees can get up to Tk 2 lakh. **You'll need:** the BMET clearance card or membership, a passport copy, the medical papers and a 6R colour photo of the worker. Apply on the Board's web portal, at a district welfare centre or at the Board; the Board checks by video call with the worker and pays within 60 working days [E0014].

**Disability insurance:** permanent disability within the 5 years pays 10% to 100% of the Tk 10 lakh, depending on the damage [E0045, E0012]. **You'll need:** the BMET clearance card, a passport copy, the medical papers and a 6R colour photo showing the disability; apply at the Board, free, paid within 30 working days (contact: Deputy Director Welfare-2, +88 02 48317085, 01321-158354, dd.welfare2@wewb.gov.bd) [E0012].

**If you lose the job within 6 months of going and come home,** the insurance pays Tk 50,000 [E0045, E0011]. **You'll need:** the BMET clearance card, a passport copy and copies of the passport pages with the departure and arrival stamps; apply at the Board, free, within 30 working days (contact 01712-107301, jahanwewb@gmail.com) [E0011]. Illness you had before leaving is not covered, and returnees who come home because of serious illness are often refused [E0097].

**If the Board doesn't deliver,** complain first to the officer named for that service, then to the Board's grievance officer (+88 02 55138267, 01684-501891, azizwelfare@gmail.com), who has 30 working days; then to the appeal officer, the Joint Secretary (Administration) of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare (+88 0241030261, jsadmin@probashi.gov.bd), who has 20 working days; then at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), the government's complaint system, which has 60 working days [E0022].

## Coming home

**Goods.** Duty-free, once a year: 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one; 100 grams of gold jewellery and 200 grams of silver jewellery, no more than 12 pieces of one kind; one carton of 200 cigarettes; and household items such as a TV up to 29 inches, a rice cooker or a fan [E0103, E0102]. You may bring up to 117 grams (10 tola) of gold, or one 10-tola bar, once a year if you declare it and pay Tk 5,000 duty per tola; more is confiscated, and undeclared bars lead to arrest and a criminal case [E0104, E0105]. Bangladeshi passport holders may bring no alcohol, and goods brought to sell are seized [E0107, E0109]. Cash over USD 10,000 or its equivalent in foreign currency must be declared at customs on the FMJ form, the foreign-currency declaration form [E0108]. Declare goods on the form handed out on the plane; if you miss it, declare at the custom house beside the airport within 7 days [E0106]. Customs hotline: 13600 [E0110].

**Help at the airport.** The Probashi Kallyan (expatriate welfare) desk at Dhaka airport helps returning workers with immigration, customs and the airline, free (+88 02 8901040, 01321-158352) [E0008]. Women who come home within a year after abuse, torture or fraud get Tk 5,000 cash at the desk; bring your passport, BMET clearance, visa, ticket and air waybill [E0039]. Sick returnees are handed to their family or taken to hospital by ambulance [E0040]. BRAC's Migration Programme also helps returnees at the airport and with longer-term reintegration, counselling and job support [E0111]. Its Information Center is opposite Dhaka airport: Rahman Villa (2nd floor), 402 Ashkona, Dakshinkhan, Dhaka; 01700797182, 01700797183 [E0170]. **Need to know:** whether this centre serves workers returning from Saudi Arabia.

**A place to stay in Dhaka.** The Welfare Board's Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport puts up departing and returning workers for a short stay. Book online at [wec.wewb.gov.bd](http://wec.wewb.gov.bd/wewb-centre/booking/search) or in person with your BMET clearance or Board membership, passport copy and air ticket; it costs Tk 100 to register and Tk 200 a night, with free transport to the airport (01321-158301, bwec@wewb.gov.bd) [E0010]. The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives workers with BMET clearance food at up to 30% off and free wifi, phone and printing [E0009].

**Legal help at home.** If you or your family need legal help in Bangladesh, for example if your family is threatened while you are away, apply to your district probashi kallyan centre (the Welfare Board's district office) or directly to the Welfare Board; the Board writes to the local administration to act, free [E0018]. The Board lists every district centre with its address, phone, mobile and email under "District Probashi Kallyan Centre list" on [wewb.gov.bd](https://wewb.gov.bd/) [E0177]. **Need to know:** which papers to bring for help inside Bangladesh, and how long it takes.

**Business loan (PKB reintegration loan).** Probashi Kallyan Bank lends returnees who start a business up to Tk 50 lakh over up to 10 years at 9% simple interest: up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral, Tk 3-5 lakh with a co-guarantee, and above Tk 5 lakh against a registered mortgage of land [E0113, E0114].

1. **You'll need:** the bank's free application form; 3 recent passport-size photos; copies of your passport and NID; a certificate of your present and permanent address from the Union Parishad or municipality; a guarantor (a parent, sibling, spouse, close relative or well-off person) with 2 photos, an NID or passport copy, an address certificate and a clean CIB report (the Credit Information Bureau report, Bangladesh Bank's record of a person's loans); an up-to-date trade licence (compulsory); a description of the business with its address and 2 years' income and spending; the rent agreement or proof of ownership of the premises; a declaration of your own investment and of any other loans; copies of your papers from abroad; any training certificate; and 3 signed cheque leaves from the business's bank account and 3 from a guarantor's account, with statements [E0115]. **Need to know:** how a guarantor gets a CIB report.
2. **Apply at the PKB branch in the area of the business** [E0117].
3. **Pay** 0.5% processing and 0.5% documentation fees, each at most Tk 15,000 [E0116].
4. **You get** a decision within 15 working days of a complete application; help line 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [E0116].
5. **If the branch doesn't resolve a problem,** complain to PKB's grievance officer, the General Manager (Administration and Accounts) (+88-02-48321195, gm2@pkb.gov.bd), who has 30 working days; then to the appeal officer, the Deputy Managing Director (+88-02-48319468, dmd@pkb.gov.bd), who has 20 working days [A0121, A0122]. **Need to know:** whether a refused applicant can appeal the decision or reapply.

## Saudi Arabia as a destination

**Numbers.** In 2025 over 750,000 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work, more than two-thirds of the 1.1 million who went abroad, up 16% from about 628,000 in 2024 [E0118, E0119]. Bangladeshis are the largest foreign group in Saudi Arabia, about 2.59 million, ahead of Indians (2.31 million), Pakistanis (2.23 million), Yemenis (2.21 million), Egyptians (1.8 million) and Sudanese (1 million) [E0121]. In December 2024, 89% of Bangladeshi workers going abroad went to Saudi Arabia, as Malaysia had stopped normal recruitment and Oman and the UAE were shrinking [E0122].

**2026 slowdown.** Early in 2026 the Saudi labour ministry cancelled thousands of visa applications after employers filed fake demand and workers arrived to no job or no iqama; departures to Saudi Arabia fell 33% from January to February [E0127]. From 1 January to 17 October 2026, 315,935 Bangladeshis went, against 577,010 a year earlier, a 45% drop [E0129]. The Iran war that began in February 2026 and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia disrupted Saudi airports, and over 1,000 flights were cancelled [E0130]. Nine in ten complaints BMET received from migrant workers in 2025 concerned Saudi Arabia: no iqama, no work and unpaid wages [E0128].

**Past bans.** Saudi Arabia restricted recruitment from Bangladesh from 2008, reopened to women domestic workers in early 2015 and to men in mid-2016, and from November 2016 to January 2017 refused male visas unless a quarter of each agency's workers were women [E0126]. From 13 April 2025, ahead of Hajj, it stopped issuing business visit, tourist and family visit visas to Bangladeshis and citizens of 13 other countries, after people used them to do Hajj [E0123].

**Growth.** BMET says over a third of the workers going to Saudi Arabia now go with a skill certificate from Takamol's skill test, and it has started training for mining jobs; construction and projects under Vision 2030, the Saudi government's plan to build up the economy beyond oil, may create up to 300,000 jobs for Bangladeshis in 2026 [E0120].

**Jobs closing to foreigners.** Under Saudisation, more jobs are reserved for Saudis each year. In 2026 housing supervisor became a Saudi-only job, and firms must fill 60% of sales and marketing jobs and 30% of engineering jobs with Saudis [E0124, E0125].
