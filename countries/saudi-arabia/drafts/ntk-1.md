## Background

**In one minute.** Saudi Arabia takes two of every three Bangladeshis who go abroad for work, though 2026 departures are 45% down on 2025 [E0129]. Every work visa belongs to a Saudi sponsor (kafil, কফিল), who can sell it, and it reaches the village through agents and brokers at several times its price [SB0007]. A man pays Tk 4-5 lakh or more, mostly borrowed, against a legal maximum of Tk 1.65 lakh [SB0024]. A cleaner or labourer earns SR 500-1,200 a month (Tk 16,500-40,000) [SJ0007, SL0033]. The sponsor holds the iqama (আকামা, the residence card), and a visa sold for a job that doesn't exist (a "free visa") leaves the worker without an iqama, work or pay [E0128]. A woman going as a domestic worker should pay nothing [D0059].

**Is it open now, and who can go?** Open as of 7 November 2026, and still the largest destination [E0129]. A man aged 21 to 60 can go on a visa for a named job, and usually needs a Saudi skill certificate; a woman going as a domestic worker must be 25 to 45 and needs none [SH0001, SM0036, D0039, D0161].

*Current situation (7 November 2026; re-check by 7 December 2026).* In February 2026 the Saudi labour ministry cancelled thousands of visa applications filed for jobs that didn't exist, and visas that had appeared on the Saudi system disappeared [E0127, B0079]. The Iran war and Houthi attacks on Saudi airports have cancelled over 1,000 flights since February; Biman flies Dhaka-Dammam daily from 7 November, Dhaka-Riyadh stays suspended, and Riyadh-bound workers go via Jeddah or Madinah [E0130, SH0007, SH0008, SH0009].

The market can shut suddenly: Saudi Arabia stopped hiring Bangladeshi men from 2008 to 2016, and in 2025 it stalled three times over paperwork rules, the skill test and the Hajj season [SB0031, E0126, SH0021, SH0016, SB0043].

*Three rules that hold every year.*
- Pay the bulk only after the visa is issued and you hold BMET clearance; BMET's permission letters allow the agency's fee only after visa, clearance and flight are confirmed [A0016].
- Fly before the entry visa expires: an expired Saudi work visa is very hard to renew [SH0010].
- Expect delays around Hajj: in 2025 block work visas on Qiwa paused for Bangladeshis through the Hajj season until about early July, and visit and tourist visas from 13 April [SB0043, E0123].

*Channels.* Visas come as single visas (সিঙ্গেল ভিসা) or group visas (গ্রুপ ভিসা), a group visa being 25 or more workers for one company [SH0006]. Nearly all go on single visas: 21.36 lakh workers against 90,000 on group visas in the five years to 2024, by BMET figures [RD0015]. A single visa is not the same as a visa sent by a relative. The channels:
- Resold visas: small Saudi firms sell demand letters to Bangladeshi brokers, Bangladeshis working in Saudi Arabia resell them, and Bangladeshi agencies, which often can't get job orders from employers directly, buy visas from Saudi agents and resell them down the chain (2020, 2024) [RD0005, RD0004, SB0012, SI0028].
- Relatives abroad, a major source: in IOM's (the International Organization for Migration's) 2019-2020 survey, 33.2% went on a visa relatives procured, against 42.3% through agencies [RD0021].
- Women domestic workers: through ministry-approved agencies and Musaned, the Saudi domestic-worker platform (Work visa: domestic worker) [D0060, D0157].
- Not BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the state agency): under the 2025 agreement recruitment goes through private agencies [SB0003].

Whoever the visa comes from, a Bangladeshi agency the Saudi embassy has approved must carry the papers (Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 2) [SI0007].

*Who the sponsor is.* The Bangladesh embassy in Riyadh sorts sponsors three ways:
- Company (শারিকা, sharika) [SM0044]: on a company visa the company often provides food and lodging [SH0026].
- Establishment (মুয়াসসাসা, muassasa), such as a shop, workshop or small contracting firm [SM0044].
- Household, as private driver (সায়েক খাস, sayeq khas) or household worker (আমেল মানজিলি, amel manzil) [SM0043]. Each Saudi household can recruit eight workers as drivers, gardeners or cooks, and in 2020 these household visas were being used for free visas [SH0024]. A worker on a household visa is a domestic worker in law, outside the Labour Law, even if promised a company job, and may work only for that household [C0131, SK0001].

A visa also names a profession (মিহনা, the job title on the visa): a man whose visa says farm worker but who works in a coffee shop or on a building site is fined if caught, and the kafil can deny knowing [SM0015]. Changing the profession is the employer's cost [C0020].

*Who qualifies.* The Saudi embassy in Dhaka's 2019 rules set higher minimum ages for some trades (industrial tailor 30, barber 35, garment shop worker 40), and drivers need a valid licence [SH0001, SH0003, SH0005]. The skill test, the Skills Verification Program (SVP, run by Takamol), is compulsory only for trades Takamol has listed, 108 by late 2026; the list includes street, hospital, office and roof cleaners, load-and-unload workers and construction workers, so most men going for labouring jobs need it [SM0036, SM0037, SM0038]. How to take it: Work visa: company worker, Step 3 [A0058].

*Size.* In 2025, 755,194 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work; Qatar came next with 107,598 [SB0017]. Saudisation (reserving jobs for Saudis) closes some trades: housing supervisor became Saudi-only in 2026, and firms must give Saudis 60% of sales and marketing jobs [E0124, E0125].

**How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut.** The choke point is the Saudi embassy in Dhaka: of 2,807 licensed agencies, only those it approves can file Saudi work visas, and in September 2025 BMET wrote that it did not know which agencies those are [SI0007, SI0009, SI0008].

*The root: a visa the sponsor owns.* An employer issues work visas itself, instantly, on Qiwa, the Saudi labour ministry's platform, if its Nitaqat rating (the Saudisation rating) is at least "medium green" and its government account has enough credit [SI0001, SI0002]. The visa costs about SR 2,000 (Tk 66,000) [SB0006]. A Bangladeshi labour supplier in Saudi Arabia said in October 2026 that sponsors take out two or three permits in their own name for workers they don't need and charge Tk 1-1.5 lakh each, so a permit is worth about Tk 2 lakh before it leaves Saudi Arabia [SI0036, SI0037, SB0007].

*The Saudi side: okala and MOFA.* On the Saudi visa platform the employer delegates the visa to a recruitment office [A0076]; agencies call this the okala (ওকালা, from the Arabic wakala, authorisation), and an okala visa sits in the agency's name, not a worker's [SI0004]. It becomes a named worker's visa when the agency files that worker against it; agents call this application the MOFA (মোফা, after the Saudi foreign ministry), and it shows the sponsor's name and an application number [SI0006]. Tasheer, the visa centre in Dhaka, takes the fingerprints and fills in the form on Enjaz, the Saudi foreign ministry's visa platform [SI0019, SG0043]. Since May 2023 Bangladeshis get Saudi work visas as e-visas, with no sticker in the passport [SM0001]. How to check the MOFA: Work visa: company worker, Step 7.

*The embassy's approved list.* In March 2023 Nazaha, Saudi Arabia's anti-corruption authority, arrested the Saudi embassy's own head of consular affairs and his deputy for taking money to issue work visas, so an agent's "embassy contact" is no guarantee [SB0036, SB0044]. The agencies with the most Saudi job orders, and the allegations against some, are under Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4 [SC0001].

*The Bangladesh side.* The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in Riyadh attests the employer's demand letter, for single visas too since 2025 (Work visa: company worker, Step 2) [A0087]; TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) found some officials attest without checks or for bribes [SB0009]. In October 2025 BMET's additional director general said employment contracts are found for only 3-4% of workers going to Saudi Arabia [SI0032], and workers often first see a contract at the airport [SB0013].

*The village end.* Most agencies deal with workers only through sub-agents beyond regulatory oversight [SB0013]. A village broker in Cumilla got Tk 20,000 from the agent for each man in 2024 [SJ0042]. Since 2023 an agency may use only registered sub-agents (Work visa: company worker, Step 1, "Brokers") [SG0005].

*Women domestic workers: the money should run the other way.* The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi domestic worker (July 2026), and in 2020 the office paid the Bangladeshi agency about USD 2,000 per woman through Musaned, so she should pay nothing [SM0035, SI0029]; yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [SI0030]. How it works: Work visa: domestic worker.

*The medical.* Brokers sell guaranteed "fit" results from the Wafid medical check (the Gulf health check) at centres allegedly controlled by a few influential owners who issue fake certificates; a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for a fit certificate was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [SB0040, B0031]. The check itself: Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

**What you pay, and what you earn and keep.** The legal maximum for a Saudi job is Tk 1.65 lakh, and the Saudi employer is meant to pay nearly every cost; in practice a man pays Tk 3.5-5 lakh or more, mostly borrowed [A0013, C0020, SJ0004, A0014].

*The rule.* The expatriates' ministry set the Tk 1.65 lakh maximum in 2016-2017 and has not changed it; in October 2026 the state minister said new country-by-country maximums are coming [SJ0019, SJ0001, SB0016]. BMET's permission letters for group visas put every Saudi-side cost, levy and airfare included, on the employer, and let the agency take only what the letter allows, Tk 50,000 or Tk 58,610 in two 2025 letters, by cheque or pay order [A0016, A0019]. The fees a worker legitimately pays in Bangladesh: Work visa: company worker, "Who pays what".

*What people really pay.*
- The whole cost: in 2026 agencies put it at Tk 3.5-5 lakh and a press investigation at about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [SJ0004, A0014].
- A free visa (also called an azad visa, আযাদ ভিসা): Tk 3-5 lakh through an agency and more through a broker, up to Tk 5-7 lakh [SM0045, SM0026, SM0011].
- The medical: the set fee is about Tk 10,000, but most workers pay double or more; a worker declared unfit pays Tk 30,000-80,000 for a "fit" report, and many make a "contract" (কন্ট্রাক্ট) with a broker in advance to be passed [SJ0013, SJ0014].
- Tasheer's fingerprints: about Tk 694, or about Tk 2,100 for the premium service [SJ0010].

*How they pay.* 81% of men borrow to go [SJ0025]. The lenders:
- Family: loans mostly interest-free [SJ0034].
- NGOs: 10-15% interest [SJ0034].
- Moneylenders (মহাজন): far higher interest [SJ0034].
- Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers: up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% without collateral once the worker has the visa (Work visa: company worker, Step 1, "Before paying") [A0052].

Workers pay intermediaries part in advance, often before the visa is confirmed, and mostly get no receipt [SB0013, SJ0040].

*The iqama: the employer's bill.* Each year a company worker's iqama costs the employer SR 9,150-10,350, plus compulsory health insurance [SM0022, SM0023]:
- iqama fee: SR 650;
- work permit fee: SR 100;
- expat levy: SR 800 a month (SR 700 for each foreign worker up to the number of Saudis in the firm);
- health insurance.

Every item is the employer's cost, and nothing may be deducted from wages for it [C0020, C0014]. If you are asked to pay any of it, complain to the labour ministry on 19911 (Work visa: company worker, "If you aren't paid") [B0109]. In practice some small kafils and supply companies deduct the iqama and health-insurance cost from wages [SM0033]. The work permit and levy can be renewed for 3, 6, 9 or 12 months at a time [SM0024]: that is how a kafil hands a worker a 3-month iqama and comes back for money every quarter [A0080]. Check the expiry date yourself on Absher, the interior ministry's app [SM0040].

*On a free visa you pay it yourself.* The worker bears the whole iqama renewal, SR 8,000-12,000 a year (Tk 2.6-4 lakh), plus a monthly or yearly cut to the kafil called kamishon (কামিশন) [SM0013, SM0012]. Workers on bought visas pay the kafil SR 5,000-6,000 (Tk 1.65-2 lakh) at each renewal [SG0002]. In 2026 workers told of paying a kafil Tk 4-8 lakh for a renewal that never came [SL0028].

*What the job pays.* Saudi Arabia sets no minimum wage for foreign workers; the contract wage is what counts [C0092]. Monthly wages (the first four from BMET's 2025 permission letters [SJ0012, SJ0003, SJ0002]):
- kitchen workers: SR 800 plus SR 200 for food;
- construction labourers: SR 1,100 with food and housing;
- carpenters, masons, steel fixers, electricians and painters: SR 1,200 plus SR 300 for food;
- heavy equipment operators: SR 1,500;
- cleaners, loaders and other low-skilled workers, nearly 80% of those going: SR 800-1,000 by recruiters' account [SJ0008, SJ0007]; cleaners promised SR 1,000-1,200 often get no more than SR 500 [SL0033];
- new house drivers: about SR 1,500-1,800 [D0077].

*What it costs to live.* A worker living outside a camp pays a month (November 2026) [SM0008]:
- shared room: SR 500-900;
- food he cooks himself: SR 500-800;
- transport: SR 150-300;
- phone and internet: SR 50-100.

*What reaches home, and how long the debt takes.* Many low-skilled Gulf workers can send only Tk 15,000-20,000 a month [SB0022]. A man on SR 800 (Tk 26,400) who buys his own food and phone, SR 550-900 a month, keeps at most SR 250 (Tk 8,250) [SM0008]. At Tk 15,000-20,000 a month a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes 23-30 months before interest, against a usual 2-year contract [SB0022, C0095]; in 2020 men took 19 months or more to recover the cost [SJ0021]. Sending costs and channels: Sending money home.

**What ties you to the employer.** Your visa and iqama belong to your kafil, normally your employer [A0002]. A company worker can resign, leave, and after a year change employer without consent, but only with a valid iqama and a contract recorded on Qiwa; domestic and farm workers don't have these rights [C0053, C0146, SK0006].

- *To enter:* tied. The work visa names the sponsor and your occupation [A0002, B0075]. On arrival you get a border number (বর্ডার নম্বর), the 10-digit number Jawazat (the passports directorate) gives you until the iqama is issued [SM0030].
- *To renew:* tied. Only the employer gets and renews your iqama and work permit, and pays for them [C0020].
- *The first six months:* since 19 February 2025 a trial period of up to 180 days can be written into the contract, and either side may end the contract during it [SM0002, SM0003]. If the employer ends it, the employer pays the ticket home: the worker pays only if unfit for work or if he quits without a valid reason [SM0005].
- *To quit:* free, with notice, and without notice if the employer breaks its duties (Your rights at work) [C0027, C0029]. Just stopping work lets the employer dismiss you without end-of-service pay and report you absent [C0028].
- *To change employer (kafil bodol, কফিল বদল, or kafala, কাফালা):* tied in the first 12 months; after that you can move mid-contract with 90 days' notice, and at any time if you go unpaid 3 months, your iqama or permit expires or the employer disappears; the employer is only notified [C0053, C0055, C0057]. How: If you want to change employer.
- *To leave:* a company worker issues his own exit and re-entry visa (khuruj, খুরুজ) or final exit (khuruj nihai) on Absher or Qiwa [C0146, SM0047]; a domestic worker can't, because the employer issues it [SK0021]. A worker the sponsor reports for a crime or damage, such as theft, is declared matloob (মাতলুব, wanted) and must settle the case before he can leave [SM0046, SM0039].
- *Working for someone else:* only through Ajeer, the labour ministry's platform for temporary work for a company that isn't your sponsor; the other company applies for an Ajeer permit, the proof you are lawfully at its site [SM0006, SM0007]. Any other outside work is illegal [C0021].

*Changing employer: what it costs.* Since 2025 the law bars an employer's compensation claim against a worker who resigns [SM0004]. The new employer pays a government transfer fee: SR 2,000 for a worker's first transfer, SR 4,000 for the second, SR 6,000 from the third, plus any iqama fees the old employer left unpaid [SM0016]; anything a worker is charged beyond that is a broker's or kafil's cut, and agencies openly advertise kafils for hire [SM0034]. A firm in the red Nitaqat band cannot take you [SM0017].

*Which law covers you.* The Labour Law covers expatriate workers in private companies [SK0005]. It excludes domestic workers and those counted as such, and agricultural workers [SK0001, SK0002]. Domestic workers, including house drivers, gardeners and cooks, come under the Domestic Workers Regulation (Work visa: domestic worker) [D0023], and can move without consent only in listed cases, such as 3 months' unpaid wages or no iqama in time [D0031].

*Your passport.* Your employer may not keep it: Your rights at work [C0022].

*Huroob (হুরুব), or "absent from work".* The employer's report on Qiwa can't be withdrawn [C0064]. You then have 60 days to take a final exit or move to a new employer, and you can move only if you have been in Saudi Arabia more than 12 months; a newcomer reported absent can only leave [C0063, SM0027]. After 60 days you are illegal, with a 3 to 5 year re-entry ban [C0067, E0057]. Employers file false reports, and some demand bribes to withdraw them [C0114]; kafils answer workers who protest about money or the iqama with huroob [SL0036]. What to do: If your employer reports you absent (huroob). An employer can file it only if your contract isn't on Qiwa [C0062].

*An expired iqama.* You are undocumented: no bank, money transfer or SIM, and detention and deportation if stopped [B0132]. The passports directorate fined a failure to renew SR 500, then SR 1,000, then deported (2019) [SK0018]. An expired iqama also lets you change employer without consent, if a new employer takes you [C0055]. Getting home: Going home without valid papers.

**What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe.** Nearly every Saudi complaint is one of three things: no iqama, no work, or pay that is late, short or missing, and behind them, again and again, is a visa sold for a job that didn't exist or an employer who won't pay for the permit [E0128, SL0014].

*Complaints.* Of 114 complaints to BMET in 2023-2024 that OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) studied, 47% got no work permit on arrival [SL0004]. Men without a permit were often kept until their entry visa ran out, then counted as absconders [SL0011]. Only 39% of complaints were resolved, usually for Tk 20,000 to just over Tk 1 lakh [SL0007]. How to complain: Work visa: company worker, Step 1, "Before paying", and "If you aren't paid" [A0056, C0039].

*What people believe, and the facts.*
- **"A free visa lets you work for anyone."** Brokers sell it as "go and work wherever you like" [SM0011]. In fact a worker may work only for the employer and job on the permit, or for another company through an Ajeer permit, which that company must apply for [C0021, SM0007]. Many jobs also need a baladiya (বালাদিয়া, municipal) health card, which free-visa workers don't easily get [SM0014]. Of 10 men one Cumilla broker sent, three got a proper job with a contract (2024) [SL0020]. How to spot one: Work visa: company worker, Step 1, "Avoid a free visa" [A0079].
- **"The iqama is the worker's cost: pay the kafil and he renews it."** A Bangladesh embassy official confirmed the employer pays (The iqama: the employer's bill) [SL0031].
- **"Saudi Arabia abolished the kafala system in 2025."** No Saudi announcement was made: the story began in an 18 October 2025 article by ImpACT International, a UK think tank, citing no Saudi source [SK0008].
- **"A visit, tourist or Umrah visa can be turned into an iqama once you're there."** A visit visa can't become an iqama, and working on a tourist or Umrah visa brings a ban of 3 to 10 years (Changing visa type) [E0054, E0057].
- **"Pay extra and the medical is guaranteed fit."** See The medical, above [B0032, B0031].

*Irregular routes and how they end.* Workers fall out of status in four ways:
- a free visa with no real job [A0081];
- an employer who never turns the three-month entry permit into an iqama, often to avoid the fee [B0131, SL0014];
- a kafil who keeps the renewal money or files huroob [SL0028, SL0036];
- a visit or Umrah visa overstayed [E0057].

Many free-visa men end up running or staffing a shop or workshop registered in a Saudi's name (tasattur, commercial concealment): that carries up to 5 years in prison, a fine of up to SR 5 million, and deportation with a ban on returning to work [SM0020, SM0018, SM0019, SM0021]. The end comes in a raid: detainees usually spend one to two weeks in a deportation centre and land in Dhaka with almost nothing [SG0032, SG0033], then face a re-entry ban [E0057]. Irregular returnees get no Welfare Board benefits [E0092]. Anyone who shelters, transports or helps a violator faces up to 15 years in prison and a fine of up to SR 1 million [SL0040]. The ways out without a raid (the embassy's Special Exit Programme, grace periods, and an employer regularising an expired permit by the end of 2026) are under Going home without valid papers [E0026, E0087, G0014].
