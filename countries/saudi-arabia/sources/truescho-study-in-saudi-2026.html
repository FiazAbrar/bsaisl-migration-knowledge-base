The study in saudi arabia application date 2026 is one of the most searched scholarship questions across the Middle East and Africa this year — and for good reason. The Saudi Ministry of Education's official portal, studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa, is the single gateway to 50+ Saudi public and private universities (the figure shown on the official homepage on 5 September 2026), all offering fully funded scholarships to international students. Whether you are a high-school graduate aiming for a bachelor's degree at King Saud University, a master's candidate eyeing King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah, or a PhD researcher targeting King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), the platform is where it all starts. This guide breaks down the official 2026 opening date, the 7-step application process, every required document, and the post-acceptance roadmap so you can apply confidently and avoid the most common rejection causes.

As of a live check on 5 September 2026, the official homepage publishes a Semester 2 2026 calendar: applications for international students and residents open on 16 September 2026 and close on 6 October 2026. The earlier May 2026 bachelor's window is over. Visa issuance and class start dates follow each university's decision after that close — do not treat last year's August–September visa window as automatic for this intake.

What is the Study in Saudi Arabia Platform?

The Study in Saudi Arabia platform (studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa) is the centralized digital gateway operated by the Saudi Ministry of Education for non-Saudi students who want to enroll in any of the Kingdom's public and licensed private universities under a fully funded government scholarship. Launched as part of Vision 2030, the portal consolidates what used to be 27 separate university applications into a single account where you create a profile once, upload your documents once, and rank up to several universities and programs by preference.

The platform serves two main applicant categories: external applicants (international students residing outside the Kingdom) and internal applicants (foreign residents already inside Saudi Arabia with a valid Iqama). Each category has its own document checklist, but the portal interface, deadlines, and decision flow are identical.

In its current iteration, the platform supports bachelor's, master's, PhD, and Arabic-language preparatory programs across more than 160 source countries. It integrates directly with the Ministry of Interior's visa system, which means once a university issues an admission letter, the student visa pre-approval is triggered automatically — a workflow no other Gulf country offers at this scale.

The platform is bilingual (Arabic and English), free to apply, and the only legitimate channel for the Saudi government scholarship. Any third-party "agent" charging a fee to submit on your behalf is unauthorized; the Ministry of Education has repeatedly warned applicants against such intermediaries.

Why is the Saudi Scholarship Platform Unique?

The Saudi scholarship is one of the most generous government-funded programs in the world, and the platform that distributes it is unique for several measurable reasons.

First, the scale: 50+ universities listed on the official platform in a single application — including five universities ranked in the QS World Top 500 (KAUST, KFUPM, KSU, KAU, and IMSIU). No other Arab government portal aggregates this many institutions.

Second, the funding package. Successful applicants receive a 100% tuition waiver, a monthly stipend of 850–1,000 SAR (~$225–270), free on-campus housing or a housing allowance, comprehensive health insurance, one annual round-trip flight ticket, plus a two-month settlement allowance on arrival and a similar departure allowance at graduation. Saudi Arabia's annual budget for international student scholarships exceeds $200 million, according to Ministry of Education disclosures.

Third, the language flexibility: programs in Sharia, Arabic literature, and humanities are taught in Arabic and do not require IELTS, while engineering, medicine, and computer science tracks are increasingly delivered in English with IELTS waivers available for graduates of English-medium high schools.

Finally, the geographic reach: the platform actively recruits from 160+ countries, with dedicated quotas for African, Southeast Asian, and Central Asian applicants — making it one of the most demographically diverse government scholarships in the Gulf.

Eligibility & Required Documents

Eligibility on the Study in Saudi Arabia platform is split by degree level. Each level has a specific age cap, GPA threshold, and document set, so it is critical to match your profile to the right track before you start the application.

Bachelor's eligibility:

  • Age 17 to 25 at the time of application.
  • High school certificate (or equivalent) issued within the last 5 years.
  • Minimum grade: Good (typically 70%+, though competitive programs require 85%+).
  • Valid passport with at least 6 months validity.
  • Medical fitness certificate (free of communicable diseases such as HIV, TB, hepatitis B/C).
  • Police clearance / good conduct certificate.
  • Two passport-size photos.
  • For Arabic-medium programs: an Arabic proficiency declaration.
  • For English-medium programs: IELTS 5.5+ or proof of English-medium schooling.

Master's eligibility:

  • Age up to 30.
  • Bachelor's degree with Very Good or above (~75%+ / GPA 3.0+).
  • Two academic recommendation letters.
  • Statement of purpose (1–2 pages).
  • CV and academic transcripts (translated and notarized).
  • Language proof matching the program's medium.

PhD eligibility:

  • Age up to 35.
  • Master's degree with strong academic standing.
  • Detailed research proposal aligned with a Saudi faculty member's research area.
  • Three academic references.
  • Publications (preferred but not always mandatory).
  • Language proficiency evidence.

How to Apply Step-by-Step (2026) — 7 Steps

The application is fully online via studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa. Follow these 7 official steps in order; skipping any of them is the most common cause of rejection.

Step 1 — Create your account (from 16 September 2026). Visit studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa, click "New Applicant," and register with a valid email and passport number. The system sends a verification link; click it within 24 hours or the account locks.

Step 2 — Complete your personal profile. Enter biographical data exactly as it appears on your passport. Mismatches between the platform and your passport are the #1 reason applications fail at the visa stage. Double-check name spelling, date of birth, and nationality.

Step 3 — Upload your academic record. Scan certificates and transcripts in PDF, max 2 MB per file. Use the "Education" tab to enter each institution, dates, GPA, and grading scale. If your scale is different (e.g. 20-point French system), the platform auto-converts to the Saudi scale.

Step 4 — Choose your universities and programs. You can rank up to 5 universities and 3 programs per university. Use the filter to match your major, language of instruction, and city. Top picks for international students typically include King Saud University (Riyadh), King Abdulaziz University (Jeddah), Umm Al-Qura (Makkah), the Islamic University of Madinah, and KFUPM (Dhahran). Female applicants often rank Princess Nourah University (PNU) — read our dedicated Princess Nourah scholarship guide for program-specific details.

Step 5 — Upload supporting documents. Medical report, police clearance, recommendations, motivation letter, and language proof if applicable. Each file must be PDF, English or Arabic only. For other languages, attach the certified translation merged into the same PDF.

Step 6 — Submit and track. Once you click "Submit," the application becomes read-only. You can track status under "My Applications" — possible statuses are Under Review, Forwarded to University, Accepted, Conditional, or Rejected. Response times follow each institution after the 6 October close — do not assume the old June–August calendar for this semester-2 window.

Step 7 — Accept the offer and start the visa. When you receive an admission letter, accept within the deadline (usually 7 days). The Ministry of Foreign Affairs then issues a visa pre-approval; you complete the visa at the nearest Saudi embassy. Travel dates follow the admission letter for this intake; do not book on the old mid-August to mid-September assumption until the university issues dates.

Real Student Experience

Yusuf, a Tunisian student now in his second year of Computer Science at King Saud University, applied through the platform in May 2025. "I had heard the portal was complicated, but it took me four evenings to complete," he says. "The trickiest part was getting my high school transcript notarized in Tunis and then certified at the Saudi embassy — that took two weeks." Yusuf ranked KSU first, KAU second, and IMSIU third; he received a conditional offer from KSU on July 18 and a final acceptance on August 6. "The 850 SAR stipend covers everything because food and dorms are free. I send 400 SAR home every month." His advice to 2026 applicants: "Start the medical certificate early. Mine expired and I had to redo it because I waited too long between applying and getting the visa." Stories like Yusuf's are common in the platform's success threads on Reddit and the Saudi MoE's official Telegram channel.

Common Application Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

Saudi Ministry of Education advisors publish a yearly bulletin highlighting the most frequent rejection causes. Here are the seven most common ones for 2026:

  1. Name mismatch between platform, passport, and certificate. Even one missing middle name triggers a hold. Use the passport spelling everywhere.
  2. Expired medical certificate. Saudi medical reports must be issued within 6 months of the application date. Do not get it too early.
  3. Uncertified translations. Translations must come from a government-accredited translator stamped by the Foreign Ministry of your country and the Saudi embassy. Freelance translators are rejected.
  4. Wrong document format. Files must be PDF, under 2 MB, single language. JPG screenshots and Word docs are auto-rejected.
  5. Choosing programs you do not qualify for. A 65% high-school average will not get into KFUPM Engineering — review each program's minimum on the program detail page before ranking.
  6. Missing the conditional acceptance window. When you get a conditional offer, you have 7 days. Many applicants miss it because they do not check the platform daily.
  7. Applying through unauthorized agents. The platform is free. If anyone asks for $200–$500 to apply on your behalf, it is a scam — and using their account voids your eligibility.

What's New in the 2026 Platform Update

The 2026 redesign of studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa introduces three significant changes applicants should know about. First, an AI-assisted document verifier now scans uploaded transcripts in real time and flags missing pages or low-resolution scans before submission, reducing back-and-forth with universities by an estimated 40%. Second, the platform is now integrated with the Absher national services portal, allowing applicants who pass the conditional acceptance stage to begin their student visa application without leaving the scholarship dashboard. Third, the mobile experience has been rebuilt — the new responsive interface allows full application from a phone, which matters because Ministry data shows 62% of applicants from Africa apply on mobile only.

After Graduation: Work and Residency in Saudi Arabia

Many applicants overlook what happens after the degree. Saudi Arabia's labor reforms under Vision 2030 now allow international graduates to convert their student visa into a work residency within the same year, provided they secure a job offer from a Saudi-registered employer. STEM graduates from KFUPM, KSU, and KAUST are particularly sought after by Aramco, NEOM, and the Public Investment Fund's portfolio companies. The new Premium Residency for Distinguished Talent, launched in 2024, offers a fast-track to long-term residency for international graduates who score in the top 10% of their cohort and have a job offer above 30,000 SAR per month. This pathway makes Saudi Arabia one of the few Gulf countries where a free bachelor's degree can lead directly to a long-term career — a strong selling point versus European scholarships that typically require visa renewal every year.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does the Study in Saudi Arabia platform open in 2026?

The live homepage timeline verified on 5 September 2026 says international and resident applications for Semester 2 2026 open on 16 September and close on 6 October. Always re-check studyinsaudi.sa before you submit, because individual universities can add extra windows.

Is the Study in Saudi Arabia platform free?

Yes, application through studyinsaudi.moe.gov.sa is completely free. The Ministry of Education has explicitly warned against unauthorized agents who charge fees — using such intermediaries can void your application.

Do I need IELTS to apply for a Saudi scholarship?

Not for Arabic-medium programs. Sharia, Arabic literature, and most humanities tracks require no IELTS. English-medium programs (engineering, medicine, computer science) typically require IELTS 5.5–6.0, but you can be exempted with a high-school transcript from an English-medium school.

What is the monthly stipend for international students in Saudi Arabia?

The stipend ranges from 850 to 1,000 SAR per month (~$225–270), depending on your major. Science and engineering students receive the higher end. On top of that, you get free housing, health insurance, and an annual return flight.

Can I apply to multiple Saudi universities at once?

Yes. The platform allows you to rank up to 5 universities and 3 programs per university in a single application. Universities review applications in order of your ranking, so put your top choice first.

Can family members come with me on a Saudi student scholarship?

Generally no — the standard scholarship covers the student only. Married postgraduate students (PhD level) may apply for a family visa separately, but housing and stipend remain calculated for the student alone.

What happens if my application is rejected?

If your application is rejected, you can reapply in a later cycle (watch the official calendar rather than assuming May 2027) with an improved profile. Rejection is most often due to grade thresholds, missing documents, or program oversubscription, not character — fix the gap and try again. You can also explore alternative regional options like the HBKU Qatar scholarship or the Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship.

How long does it take to hear back from Saudi universities?

Most universities respond between June and August 2026 for bachelor's applicants. Postgraduate decisions can extend into October. You can track each application's status live in the "My Applications" section of the platform.

Conclusion

The current Study in Saudi Arabia application window, verified on 5 September 2026, runs 16 September to 6 October 2026 for Semester 2. The process is free and online. Prepare your documents before 16 September (notarized transcripts, medical report, motivation letter) and rank your universities strategically. With monthly stipends of 850–1,000 SAR, free housing, annual flights, and a clear post-graduation pathway to work residency under Vision 2030, the Saudi scholarship is arguably the most competitive package in the Gulf.
