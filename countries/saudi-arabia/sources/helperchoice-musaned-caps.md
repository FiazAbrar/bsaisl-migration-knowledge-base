## **Understanding Musaned & Employer Legal Requirements**

In Saudi Arabia, all domestic worker recruitment, contracts, and visa issuances are legally regulated through the **Musaned** platform. Musaned ensures standardized electronic employment contracts and protects the rights of both employers and workers.

Currently, domestic workers can be legally recruited through Musaned from 17 authorized countries, including the **Philippines, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Kenya, Uganda, Bangladesh, India, Pakistan, Ethiopia, Nepal, and Thailand.\
\**
Domestic workers from the Philippines remain as the top choice by most families in Saudi Arabia and the Middle East. Recruitment costs for Filipinos ranges between 16,000 to 25,000 riyals.**\**

### **Key Legal Requirements for Employers:**

- **Sponsorship Eligibility:** Male employers must generally be married to issue a domestic worker visa.

- **Income Requirements:** Employers must meet baseline income thresholds set by HRSD (e.g., a minimum monthly salary of SAR 10,000 for a first housemaid visa, and SAR 18,000 for secondary visas).

- **Official Contracts:** Employers must issue an official household worker visa and sign a bilingual HRSD contract detailing duties, working hours, accommodation, and salary.

## **Domestic Worker Salaries & Minimum Wage Rules**

Monthly salaries for domestic workers in Saudi Arabia depend on nationality, prior experience, specialized skills (such as cooking or childcare), and the job scope.

### **Minimum Monthly Salaries by Country:**

Several sending countries mandate official baseline salaries for their citizens working in Saudi Arabia:

- **Philippines:** USD 500 per month (approx. **SAR 1,875**).

- **Nepal:** **SAR 1,300** per month (includes a SAR 300 monthly food allowance).

- **Uganda:** **SAR 900** per month.

### **Average Salaries for Other Nationalities:**

For countries without a mandatory embassy minimum, average monthly salaries range between **SAR 950 and SAR 2,500** based on experience and qualifications.

## **Updated Musaned Recruitment Cost Ceilings**

The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development periodically sets upper limits for standard agency recruitment costs (excluding VAT). Current official maximum ceilings include:

- **Philippines:** SAR 14,700

- **Sri Lanka:** SAR 13,800

- **Bangladesh:** SAR 11,750

- **Kenya:** SAR 9,000

- **Uganda:** SAR 8,300

- **Ethiopia:** SAR 5,900

- **Thailand:** SAR 10,000

## ⚠️ **Mandatory Rule: Electronic Salary Transfer**

Paying domestic workers in cash is **strictly prohibited** in Saudi Arabia. Under Ministry regulations, employers must transfer monthly wages electronically via Musaned-approved channels (such as official digital wallets or WPS-linked bank accounts).

This guarantees transparent payment records, generates automatic digital receipts, and legal protection for both parties.

## **Essential Rights & Best Practices for Employers**

Building a positive, long-term relationship with your helper requires clear communication and respecting mandatory labor protections:

- **Working Hours & Rest:** Maximum 8 to 10 working hours daily, at least 9 consecutive hours of daily rest, and **1 full day off per week**.

- **Living Conditions & Health:** Employers must provide suitable private accommodation, meals, and mandatory health insurance coverage.

- **Paid Leave & Flights:** 30 days of paid annual leave with round-trip airfare to their home country upon completing a 2-year contract.

- **End-of-Service Benefit:** 1 month’s salary benefit given after 4 consecutive years of service.
