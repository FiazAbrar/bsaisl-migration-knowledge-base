Saudi Huroob (Absconding) 2026: Check, Remove or Transfer

Saudi huroob and absconding guide for 2026. Check absent-from-work status, understand the 60-day Qiwa period, transfer or final exit options, false reports and domestic worker rules.

GulfVisaHub Editorial Team

August 10, 2026 · 31 min read

Share:XFacebookEmail
Editorial policy

In everyday expat language, huroob or an absconding case usually means that a worker has an absence-related status in Saudi labour or residency records. Saudi official systems now more commonly use terms such as work discontinuation and absent from work.

For many private-sector Qiwa cases, the important period is 60 days. During that period, an eligible worker may be able to transfer legally to a new employer or complete final exit. If no permitted action is completed, the status can become absent from work, and the normal transfer or final-exit route can become harder.

There is no single button that removes every huroob case. The correct solution depends on the exact Qiwa status, report or action date, contract history, whether the worker is inside Saudi Arabia, whether the case is false, and whether a current status-correction route is available.

What Does Huroob Mean in Saudi Arabia?

South Asian workers often use several words for this problem:

  • Huroob
  • Absconding
  • Runaway report
  • Escape report
  • Absence report
  • Absent case
  • "بلاغ هروب"
  • "بلاغ تغيب"

These words are not always the exact status shown by the Saudi government system.

For a private-sector worker, you may instead see wording such as:

What Does "Escape Report of Resident ID Number Has Been Recorded" Mean?

Some users see English wording such as:

"Escape report of resident ID number has been recorded."

This can be a translated or system-generated description of an absence-related report linked to the resident's Iqama or employment record.

Do not make a major decision from that sentence alone.

Check:

  1. The exact status in Qiwa
  2. The employer name
  3. The contract status
  4. The official action date
  5. Whether a 60-day option is still available
  6. Whether Absher shows final-exit or other residency information
  7. Whether you are a private-sector employee or a domestic worker

If the wording is unclear, contact MHRSD through an official support channel and give them your Iqama details and the exact message shown in the system.

Is Huroob the Same as a Criminal Case?

No, not automatically.

An absent-from-work labour status is mainly connected to:

  • Employment relationship
  • Work permit
  • Employer record
  • Iqama and residency status
  • Transfer eligibility
  • Final exit
  • Government labour systems

A separate police or criminal case can exist at the same time, but it is a different matter.

Examples of separate cases could include:

  • Theft allegation
  • Fraud
  • Assault
  • Forgery
  • Court judgment
  • Another police complaint

Clearing an employment-status problem does not automatically clear a separate police or court case.

Likewise, do not assume you have a criminal case simply because Qiwa shows work discontinuation or absent from work.

When Can an Employer Rely on Absence Under Saudi Labour Law?

Article 80 of the Saudi Labour Law allows an employer to terminate a worker without award, notice or compensation in specified serious cases, provided the worker has an opportunity to state objections.

One of those cases concerns unjustified absence.

The current official law states that this can apply when the worker is absent without a legitimate reason:

  • For more than 30 days during one contractual year, or
  • For more than 15 consecutive days

The law also requires written warning before termination for this reason:

  • After 20 days of absence in the first situation
  • After 10 consecutive days of absence in the second situation

This does not mean every absence automatically becomes huroob.

A worker may have a legitimate reason, for example:

  • Approved annual leave
  • Official sick leave
  • Medical emergency
  • Employer instruction not to attend
  • Employer preventing the worker from entering the workplace
  • Documented workplace safety problem
  • Official detention
  • Another reason that can be supported with evidence

Article 62 of the Labour Law also says that if a worker presents themselves for work or shows readiness to work but cannot work because of a reason attributable to the employer, the worker is entitled to wages for that period.

The practical lesson is simple: keep evidence.

How the Private-Sector Qiwa Process Works

The MHRSD procedural guide explains the standard work-discontinuation process for relevant private-sector cases.

Step 1: The Employer Submits the Work-Discontinuation Action

The employer or authorised representative uses Qiwa to submit the action when the official conditions are met.

The published guide connects the process to the same main absence thresholds described above.

Step 2: The Worker and Employer Are Notified

The worker and employer are notified electronically.

The worker's record is removed from the establishment, and the worker is no longer counted under that employer for Nitaqat.

Step 3: The 60-Day Period Starts

The worker is given 60 days from the official action date.

During that period, an eligible worker may have two main options:

  1. Transfer to another employer
  2. Complete final exit

For the standard transfer route in the published guide, transfer eligibility includes the worker having spent more than 12 months in Saudi Arabia.

The live Qiwa result is still what matters for your own case.

Step 4: If No Action Is Completed

If the worker does not complete an eligible transfer or final exit during the allowed period, the status can become absent from work in the connected ministry systems.

The standard transfer and final-exit requests may then stop being accepted through the normal 60-day route.

This is why waiting is dangerous.

What the 60-Day Period Really Means

The 60 days are not a punishment period that you should simply wait out.

They are a limited time to correct the situation.

Option 1: Transfer to a New Employer

If your goal is to stay in Saudi Arabia and keep working, an eligible new employer normally starts the transfer request in Qiwa.

The process may include:

  1. New employer checks your eligibility
  2. New employer creates the transfer request
  3. Employment contract is added
  4. Worker reviews and accepts the request
  5. MHRSD processes the transfer
  6. Connected government records are updated
  7. New employer completes the remaining work-permit and residency steps

Do not accept the sentence "we applied for transfer" as proof that the transfer is finished.

Check the live Qiwa status until the employment relationship is actually moved.

Option 2: Final Exit

If you want to leave Saudi Arabia permanently, an eligible final-exit route may be available during the allowed period.

Before leaving, check:

  • Passport
  • Iqama
  • Traffic violations
  • Family members
  • Unpaid salary
  • Leave balance
  • EOSB position
  • Bank account
  • Loans
  • Vehicle
  • Housing
  • Mobile and utility accounts
  • Final flight

A final exit may solve the residency side, but it does not automatically settle every employment claim.

What Happens After the 60 Days Expire?

Under the standard private-sector procedure, if the worker does not complete a permitted transfer or final exit within the 60-day period, the status can become or remain absent from work.

This can create problems such as:

  • Normal transfer route no longer accepting the request
  • Normal final-exit request no longer accepting the request
  • Work permit complications
  • Iqama and residency complications
  • Inability to work lawfully for another employer
  • Need for a special correction route
  • Greater risk of enforcement under labour and residency rules
  • Difficulty proving a clean end to the old employment relationship

Do not wait until day 55 to start looking for a new employer.

Employer onboarding, Qiwa eligibility, official fees, contract creation and system updates can take time.

Can an Old Huroob or Absent-From-Work Status Be Cleared in 2026?

Possibly, depending on the record.

MHRSD launched a Qiwa initiative starting from 18 September 2025 to regularise the status of specified professional workers recorded as absent from work.

The ministry said the initiative includes several groups, such as:

  • Workers whose status became absent from work after the normal 60-day period
  • Workers whose status changed after expiry or termination of a documented employment contract
  • Expatriate workers with older absence reports from before the work-discontinuation initiative

MHRSD also listed important conditions, including:

  • The worker must have completed at least 12 continuous months in Saudi Arabia before the work discontinuation or contract expiry
  • The new employer must agree to pay outstanding work-permit financial charges connected with the worker

The initiative can allow an eligible worker to transfer legally to a new employer.

Important: Do Not Assume You Automatically Qualify

Eligibility may depend on:

  • Worker category
  • Contract history
  • Report or status date
  • Time spent in Saudi Arabia
  • New employer
  • Establishment eligibility
  • Work permit
  • Outstanding fees
  • Connected government records
  • Whether the correction option is currently available for your record

Check your live Qiwa account and ask the new employer to check the transfer route before paying anyone.

How to Remove Huroob in Saudi Arabia

There is no universal "remove huroob" button.

The route depends on what your official record actually says.

Situation 1: You Are Still Inside the 60-Day Period

Your practical options may be:

  • Complete an eligible transfer to a new employer, or
  • Complete final exit

Act before the deadline.

Situation 2: The 60 Days Already Passed

Check whether the current Qiwa status-correction initiative is available for your record.

A real new employer should check Qiwa eligibility and confirm:

  • Whether transfer is allowed
  • What outstanding fees appear
  • What contract is required
  • What government steps remain

Do not pay a private person to create a fake company transfer.

Situation 3: The Report Is False or Malicious

Do not simply search for a paid "huroob removal" service.

Save your evidence and challenge the employer's action through official channels.

Situation 4: You Are a Domestic Worker

Use Musaned and the domestic-worker rules, not the standard private-sector Qiwa process.

Situation 5: You Are Outside Saudi Arabia

Check whether the problem is actually an exit-and-re-entry or "exited and did not return" status rather than an inside-Kingdom work-discontinuation case.

Can the Employer Cancel the Absence Report?

For the standard private-sector process described in the MHRSD procedural guide, the employer's submitted work-discontinuation action is described as non-retractable after the worker is removed from the establishment's records.

This is important.

Do not rely on a promise such as:

"Pay me and I will cancel your huroob tonight."

A false, malicious or incorrect case can still be challenged, but the solution may require:

  • MHRSD labour-violation report
  • Qiwa support
  • Friendly Settlement
  • Labour court where applicable
  • Inspection or official review
  • Correction by the relevant authority

A system correction is different from the old employer simply pressing an undo button.

What If the Huroob or Absence Report Is False?

MHRSD's employer-compliance guidance treats an incorrect absence report as a labour violation where an employer reports a worker as absent while the worker is actually on the job in order to blackmail, harm or pressure the worker.

If you believe the report is false, act quickly.

Evidence for a False Huroob or Absence Report

  • Qiwa contract and current contract-status screenshots
  • Iqama and passport copies
  • Attendance or fingerprint records
  • Worksite access records
  • Duty roster or timesheets
  • Payslips and bank salary transfers
  • Work emails, reports or completed assignments
  • Messages with supervisor or HR
  • Approved annual-leave record
  • Official sick-leave record
  • Medical or emergency documents
  • Messages showing the employer told you not to attend
  • Messages showing you asked to return to work
  • Evidence that the workplace was closed or you were prevented from entering
  • GOSI employment record where relevant
  • SMS or screenshot showing the work-discontinuation action
  • Threats linking the report to resignation, money, transfer or withdrawal of a complaint
  • Names of witnesses who can confirm attendance or employer instructions

Do not create fake attendance records, edit screenshots, or manufacture messages. False evidence can damage your case.

Step 1: Save the Exact Status and Date

Take screenshots from Qiwa and save any official SMS.

Record:

  • Employer
  • Contract
  • Status wording
  • Official action date
  • Last day actually worked
  • Date you discovered the status
  • Whether you were on approved leave
  • Whether you remained ready to work

The official action date matters because it can control the 60-day period.

Step 2: Contact the Employer in Writing

Keep the message factual.

State that the status is incorrect, give the dates, and ask the employer to explain the official basis for the action.

Avoid threats, insults and emotional arguments.

Step 3: Report the Possible Labour Violation

MHRSD provides a Reporting Violations of Labor Regulations service for private-sector labour violations.

The official service is free.

Explain:

  • What the system shows
  • Why you believe it is incorrect
  • When you worked
  • What evidence you have
  • Whether the employer used the report to pressure you

Save the report reference number.

Step 4: File Friendly Settlement for Your Personal Claims

A labour-violation report tells MHRSD about possible non-compliance.

It may not by itself recover your personal money claims.

Use Friendly Settlement where you also have disputes involving:

  • Unpaid wages
  • Leave pay
  • Deductions
  • Contract compensation
  • Unlawful termination
  • Forced resignation
  • Document withholding
  • EOSB where legally due
  • Other employment entitlements

MHRSD states that Friendly Settlement is the first stage for labour disputes and that unresolved cases can be referred to labour court.

Our Saudi labour complaint guide explains the filing process in simple steps.

Step 5: Keep Checking the 60-Day Options

Do not assume that filing a complaint automatically pauses every Qiwa deadline.

Continue checking whether an eligible transfer or final-exit option remains available while the complaint is being reviewed.

Ask MHRSD what applies to your exact record.

What If You Left Because the Employer Broke the Contract?

Article 81 of the Saudi Labour Law allows a worker to leave without notice while retaining statutory rights in specified serious situations.

These include certain cases where:

  • Employer fails to meet essential contractual or legal obligations
  • Employer deceived the worker about employment conditions
  • Worker is assigned fundamentally different work without consent contrary to the law
  • Employer or responsible person commits assault or serious misconduct
  • Employer treatment is harsh, unjust or humiliating
  • Workplace presents a serious safety or health danger that the employer knew about and did not correct
  • Employer conduct forces the worker to appear responsible for ending the contract

Article 81 is not an automatic online protection against an absence status.

If you leave because of a serious employer breach, keep evidence and use the official complaint process.

Possible evidence includes:

  • Unpaid salary records
  • Safety complaints
  • Medical reports
  • Police reports where relevant
  • Threats
  • Contract mismatch
  • Written requests asking the employer to correct the problem
  • MHRSD reports
  • Witness details

Do not simply disappear and try to explain the reason months later.

What If the Employer Has Your Passport?

Passport withholding is a separate employment problem.

If your employer refuses to return your passport, ask for it in writing and keep proof of the request.

A missing passport can make final exit, transfer, travel and case handling more difficult.

What If You Are Outside Saudi Arabia?

A worker who left Saudi Arabia on an exit-and-re-entry visa and did not return can have a different immigration status from a worker who stopped attending work inside Saudi Arabia.

Check:

  • Exit and re-entry visa status
  • Return-before date
  • Iqama
  • Passport
  • Employer record
  • Absher
  • Muqeem
  • Qiwa

Do not tell an agent only that you have huroob. Show the exact official status.

Domestic Worker Huroob and Absence Rules

Domestic workers do not normally use the standard private-sector Qiwa process.

They use Musaned and separate domestic-worker rules.

This can include:

  • Housemaids
  • Domestic drivers
  • Household nurses
  • Private guards
  • Gardeners
  • Other professions classified under domestic or support labour

Employer Withdrawal Period

The published domestic-worker controls state that, where the conditions apply, the employer may withdraw an absence report within 15 days from submission. After that, the report becomes final under those controls.

The same published controls state that the employer cannot withdraw a report submitted during the worker's first 90 days after entering Saudi Arabia.

If the Contract Is Ended for Work Discontinuation During the First Two Years

MHRSD's current Musaned guidance says that if the domestic worker's contract is terminated because of work discontinuation during the first two years after entry to Saudi Arabia, the worker must complete final exit within 60 days.

If the worker does not leave within that period, the worker can become non-compliant with residency and labour rules.

If the Worker Has Been in Saudi Arabia for More Than Two Years

If the domestic worker's contract is ended because of work discontinuation after two years from entry, the worker must normally either:

  • Complete final exit, or
  • Transfer to a new employer

within 60 days from the work-discontinuation date.

If the worker does neither, the worker can become absent from work and non-compliant with the applicable rules.

Old Domestic-Worker Correction Initiative

MHRSD announced a separate correction window for older absent domestic workers and extended it from November 2025 for six months.

Do not assume that temporary correction window is still open in September 2026.

Check the current Musaned screen and MHRSD announcement before relying on any old article, video or social-media post.

Can an Agent Clear Huroob?

No private agent can lawfully guarantee the outcome of a government status.

Be careful if someone promises:

  • "Huroob removal in one hour"
  • Transfer without a genuine employer
  • Fake Qiwa contract
  • Backdated attendance
  • Guaranteed MHRSD decision
  • Guaranteed final exit
  • Government cancellation for cash paid to a personal account
  • "Inside contact" who needs your Absher or Nafath verification code

Use only:

  • Qiwa
  • MHRSD
  • Absher
  • Musaned for domestic workers
  • Genuine employer representatives
  • Licensed lawyers where legal representation is actually needed

Never share:

  • Absher password
  • Nafath approval
  • Qiwa login
  • Bank OTP
  • Email verification code

Common Problems

I Did Not Receive an SMS

Do not assume there is no case.

Check Qiwa directly. An old mobile number, network issue or missed message can prevent you seeing the SMS.

I Do Not Know the Official Report Date

Ask MHRSD or Qiwa support to confirm the official action date.

Do not calculate the 60-day period from rumours or memory.

A New Employer Says Transfer Is Impossible

Ask the employer to show you the actual Qiwa eligibility result.

Possible blockers include:

  • Worker category
  • Less than the required time in Saudi Arabia for the relevant route
  • Old report date
  • Work permit
  • Outstanding fees
  • New establishment eligibility
  • Pending request
  • Contract issue
  • Another government status

The Old Employer Wants Money

Do not pay a manager or private person merely because they say they can change the government status.

An employer may have a genuine legal or contractual claim in some cases, but that is different from demanding unofficial cash for a government correction.

Use documented official payment routes and written legal claims.

I Have Unpaid Salary

File the appropriate employment claim.

Status correction and wage recovery are separate problems.

My Iqama Is Expired

An expired Iqama can make the case more complicated.

Check:

  • Qiwa transfer eligibility
  • Current professional-worker correction initiative
  • Work-permit status
  • New employer requirements
  • MHRSD guidance

Do not assume Iqama expiry automatically creates huroob. Check the actual official status.

The 60 Days Already Passed

Check whether the September 2025 professional-worker status-correction initiative is available in your Qiwa record.

Ask a genuine new employer to check the transfer route.

The Employer Says a False Report Cannot Be Challenged

The standard private-sector action may be described as non-retractable by the employer, but an incorrect or malicious report can still be reported and reviewed through official channels.

These are two different things.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistakes That Can Make a Huroob Case Worse

  • Ignoring the first Qiwa or SMS notice
  • Waiting until the last days of the 60-day period
  • Using the word huroob without checking the exact official status
  • Paying an unofficial agent for guaranteed removal
  • Accepting a fake employer transfer
  • Working for a new employer before the legal transfer is complete
  • Deleting attendance, salary or message evidence
  • Stopping work without keeping evidence of the reason
  • Assuming a complaint automatically pauses the 60-day period
  • Assuming the old employer can always cancel a private-sector report
  • Confusing absent from work with exited and did not return
  • Using Qiwa private-sector rules for a domestic worker
  • Signing a false resignation or settlement
  • Giving up genuine salary claims because someone promises a status change
  • Assuming EOSB is always payable or always lost without checking how the contract legally ended
  • Sharing Absher, Nafath, Qiwa, bank or email verification codes
  • Relying on an expired temporary correction initiative without checking the current portal

Official Support Channels

Use the channel that matches your problem.

  • Qiwa: employment contract, private-sector work status, transfer and professional-worker correction
  • MHRSD: labour guidance, violation reports and employment complaints
  • MHRSD phone: 19911
  • Friendly Settlement: salary, termination, compensation and other personal labour disputes
  • Absher: personal residency, visa and travel services
  • Musaned: domestic-worker contracts, mobility and absence matters
  • Police or emergency services: violence, confinement, threats or a separate criminal matter

Keep every reference number.

Bottom Line

A Saudi huroob or absconding case is not one simple status with one simple solution.

Start by checking the exact official status and date.

For many standard private-sector work-discontinuation cases, the worker has 60 days to complete an eligible transfer to a new employer or final exit. If no action is completed, the status can become absent from work and the normal route can become much harder.

If the report is false, collect evidence and use the official MHRSD violation and labour-dispute channels immediately.

If the 60-day period has already passed, check whether the September 2025 professional-worker Qiwa correction initiative is available for your record.

If you are a domestic worker, use Musaned and the domestic-worker rules.

Do not pay an unofficial person for guaranteed huroob removal. The safest path is the one shown by Qiwa, MHRSD, Absher or Musaned for your exact case.
