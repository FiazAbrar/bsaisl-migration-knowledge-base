Saudi Arabia remains the single largest destination for Bangladeshi workers, year after year, more of our people fly to Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam than anywhere else on earth. The process is well-worn but genuinely bureaucratic: two governments, one medical network, a digital contract platform and an embassy queue all have to agree about you before wheels leave the runway.

This guide walks the entire journey in order, with the real names of each system, Wafid, Musaned, MOFA, so that when your agency says "your enumber is done", you'll know exactly what that means and what comes next. It's the same sequence our own office tracks for every Saudi file.

## How Saudi recruitment actually works

Everything starts in Saudi Arabia, not Bangladesh. A Saudi employer (company or, for domestic workers, a household) obtains permission to hire foreign workers, **block visas** allocated by trade and nationality. Against that allocation, the employer issues a **demand letter and power of attorney** to a licensed Bangladeshi recruiting agency, attested through the proper chain (chamber of commerce, Saudi embassy).

Two Saudi digital platforms matter to you:

- **Musaned**, the Ministry of Human Resources' contract platform. For applicable categories, your employment contract is created and verified here; Bangladesh's systems increasingly cross-check it.
- **MOFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) enumber**, the visa authorization number issued against your name/passport that the embassy in Dhaka stamps against.

If an agency can show you a real demand letter, a visa number/ID, and (where applicable) a Musaned contract, the job exists. If they can't, it doesn't. It's that binary.

## Step-by-step: from passport to fly-out

- **Step 1, File opening.** Passport (12+ months validity recommended), photos, NID, and your trade history. A serious agency also briefs you on the actual job: salary in riyals, hours, housing, the Saudi summer.
- **Step 2, Wafid (GAMCA) medical.** Gulf-bound workers must pass the standardized GCC medical at a centre registered under the Gulf Health Council's **Wafid** system (the network many still call GAMCA). Blood panel, chest X-ray, urine/stool, physical exam. The result, FIT or UNFIT, is recorded in the system itself. *Critical detail: the medical has a limited validity window, so a competent agency sequences it against your visa readiness. Our office tracks both dates in software precisely because expired medicals quietly kill files.*
- **Step 3, Visa allocation.** Your name is placed against the employer's visa: you get a **visa number and ID**. For applicable categories the **Musaned contract** is generated and you should see and understand it before anything is stamped.
- **Step 4, MOFA enumber.** The employer's side authorizes your specific visa application with MOFA, the enumber your embassy submission will reference.
- **Step 5, Biometrics.** Fingerprint/biometric enrolment at the designated centre in Bangladesh, a short visit, but files stall when it's forgotten. (Ours get scheduled together with police clearance to save you trips.)
- **Step 6, Police Clearance Certificate.** Standard requirement; takes days, costs little, forgeries cost everything. Do it properly.
- **Step 7, Embassy submission & stamping.** Passport + dossier (visa number, enumber, medical FIT, PCC, photos, contract) goes to the Saudi embassy/authorized centre in Dhaka. Queue times vary week to week; your agency should give you dated updates, not "processing cholche".
- **Step 8, BMET clearance, PDOT & smart card.** Registration in BMET's database, Wage Earners' Welfare Fund enrolment, the mandatory pre-departure orientation, and your emigration clearance smart card, the legal green light. Full details in our [BMET guide](blog-bmet-smart-card-guide.html).
- **Step 9, Ticket & fly-out.** Book against a verified PNR (check it yourself on the airline's site), attend the final document briefing, fly. On arrival: iqama (residence permit) processing is the employer's obligation, know that before you land.

## Costs: what's legitimate and what's inflation

Real cost categories for a Saudi worker file from Bangladesh:

- **Government-side fees**, BMET/welfare fund (~BDT 2,500–3,500), police clearance, attestations: modest, fixed, receipted.
- **Wafid medical**, a few thousand taka at the approved centre (plus retest costs if required).
- **Agency service charge**, regulated ceilings exist by destination; demand the current official figure in writing and how your quote maps to it.
- **Air ticket**, market fare; insist on seeing it separately (our [ticketing guide](blog-best-ticketing-agencies-bangladesh.html) explains the labour-fare trap).

Anything beyond these categories should have a name and a receipt. The historical pattern of Saudi files ballooning to four or five lakh through "visa purchase" chains is exactly what the demand-letter verification step protects you from: **if the visa is real and the demand is real, you are not paying anyone to "find" you a visa.**

> Ask one question of any quote: "এই টাকাটা কার কাছে যাচ্ছে, আর রিসিট কোথায়?", where is this money going, and where's the receipt? Honest desks answer instantly.

## Timeline: the honest version

With a verified demand letter and a FIT medical, a typical Saudi file completes in **2–4 months**. The variable stretches are embassy queues and employer-side MOFA processing. Files that drag past that usually have a fixable cause, an expired medical, a name mismatch between passport and NID, a stuck attestation, which is why we track every file stage-by-stage in our internal system and tell candidates precisely where theirs stands.

## Know before you go: rights and realities

- **Your contract is your shield.** Keep your signed copy (photo it to family). Salary disputes in Saudi Arabia are winnable with paper and nearly hopeless without.
- **Iqama within 90 days.** Your employer must process your residence permit; without it you can't bank, and you're exposed. If it's delayed, contact your agency and the Bangladesh mission early.
- **Never surrender your passport permanently.** Employers holding passports remains common practice but is contrary to Saudi labour rules, at minimum, photograph everything and keep copies in the cloud and with family.
- **The climate is not a joke.** Outdoor trades in the Saudi summer are brutal; midday work bans exist seasonally. Hydration discipline is a survival skill, our pre-departure briefing covers it because clinics fill with new arrivals every June.
- **Help exists.** The Bangladesh embassy (Riyadh) and consulate (Jeddah) run labour wings; save their numbers before you fly, alongside your agency's. Through us, that's two phone lines answered six days a week, [it's in our written commitments](commitments.html).

## What you'll earn, and surviving the first three months

Typical base salaries on the labour orders we process, loading/unloading, cleaning, facility and packaging work, run **SAR 1,000–1,400 per month**, with overtime lifting real totals meaningfully for those who want the hours. Construction helpers and skilled trades (masons, steel fixers, electricians, AC technicians) command more, and certificates plus a passed trade test move you up the scale before you ever fly. At roughly BDT 29–32 per riyal, a normal month grosses BDT 30,000–45,000, with experienced skilled workers going well beyond.

Budget the other side honestly: most labour contracts include employer-provided accommodation, and many include food or a food allowance, confirm which, in the contract, because it's the difference between saving and surviving. Phone/data (SAR 30–60), personal expenses, and remittance fees take their bite. A steady worker on a standard order typically remits **BDT 25,000–40,000 monthly** once settled.

The first three months deserve their own plan. Salaries commonly start flowing at the end of month one; iqama processing can eat weeks; homesickness is real; and the heat, if you land in summer, is a physical opponent. Carry modest pocket money (SAR 200–400 equivalent), keep your documents photographed and backed up, learn the twenty Arabic words of your trade fast, and save your first months' contact list: supervisor, agency (us), and the Bangladesh mission's labour wing. Workers who stumble usually stumble in month one or two, workers who plan those months calmly tend to complete contracts and renew on better terms.

## FAQ: Saudi work visas from Bangladesh

### What's the difference between a "free visa" and a company visa?

Legally, there is no such thing as a "free visa." Every work visa binds you to a specific employer (kafeel). What brokers sell as "free visa" is usually a visa detached from a real job, meaning you land with no salary, no duties, and no protection, then hunt informal work illegally. It is the single most destructive product in this market. Refuse it.

### My medical came back UNFIT. Is my file dead?

Often no. Many UNFIT causes are treatable (some infections, manageable conditions); after treatment and the appropriate waiting period you can retest. What kills files is hiding the condition or buying a fake FIT, both end in deportation from the destination's own screening.

### Who pays for the visa, ticket and iqama?

It varies by job order and contract, which is exactly why you demand the itemized cost sheet showing what the employer covers versus what you pay in Bangladesh. Iqama issuance after arrival is the employer's legal obligation.

### Can I switch employers after I arrive?

Saudi labour reforms have opened conditional transfer paths (contract expiry, employer violations, notice periods), but the rules are specific and change, don't fly planning to switch. Fly on a contract you'd be content to complete.

### How do I check my visa is real before stamping?

With the visa number and ID your agency provides, enquiry services (including MOFA's own portal) can confirm the visa's existence and employer linkage. Any licensed agency will sit with you and verify it on screen, we do it as a standard step.
