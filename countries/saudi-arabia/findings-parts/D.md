## Domestic worker

**What changed from the draft, and why**
- Draft said women must be 25-45 (or 50 with experience abroad). Verified: BMET citizen charter (p.1, service 1, item 6) needs proof of age 25-45 and a legal guardian's NOC for BMET clearance [D0039]. The housekeeping policy takes 25-45, returnees up to 50 [D0050]. Saudi side: Domestic Workers Regulation art. 6 bans employing anyone under 21 [D0001]. The "up to 50" applies to the course; the BMET charter says 25-45 for clearance. Possible conflict for a returnee aged 46-50: the course admits her, the clearance line says 25-45. Not put in the doc as a Need to know because the charter is the clearance rule; flag for corridor experts.
- Draft said "41 TTCs" for the housekeeping course (old source). Removed: the 2025-issued policy gives no number; it says upazila TTCs get priority.
- Draft step "Accept the contract on Musaned: SMS link, 5 days". Re-checked: that HRSD service (D0047) is "Electronic documentation" for a worker already in Saudi Arabia (she enters her residence number) and is optional for employers. It is not the recruitment step for someone in Bangladesh. Replaced with the recruitment flow (Saudi office and foreign office exchange documents on Musaned, D0157; visa delegation sent to the embassy, D0159) and the law that terms must be known before departure (D0160).
- The 2025 Domestic Workers Regulation on musaned.gov.sa is newer than the 2024 HRSD guide and differs: sick leave 30 days (15 full, 15 half) vs guide's "30 days per year"; employer penalties up to SR 20,000 vs guide's SR 2,000-5,000; domestic worker penalties: regulation "fine up to SR 2,000 or ban on working in the Kingdom" vs guide "or banning him permanently". Doc uses the regulation (current text). The regulation page is undated; the HRSD guide is March 2024.
- Daily rest: regulation says total rest at least 8 continuous hours; the HRSD guide's employer list says "not less than nine hours per day". Doc uses 8 (regulation). Conflict logged here only.
- Draft said domestic workers "can't change employer freely" and gave grounds from Migrant-Rights 2023 [old N153]. Replaced with the official HRSD "Controls to Improve the Contractual Relationship" (May 2024) list of transfer-without-consent grounds (D0031-D0035, D0163, D0164).
- Absence reports for domestic workers (D0036-D0038): employer can withdraw within 15 days; worker has 60 days to transfer or take final exit; otherwise a residency violator. The "after two years from first entry" condition in article 3 is ambiguous in the English text; not put in the doc. The huroob section (other agent) should check it.
- Complaint channel changed in Oct 2024: domestic labour disputes start online through "رفع دعوى عمالة منزلية" (File a domestic labour lawsuit) on the HRSD site, amicable settlement, then labour courts under the Ministry of Justice (D0043). The general Friendly Settlement service (Wudy) explicitly excludes domestic labour (D0044). Could not find the service page URL or its steps; Sayidaty, Ajel and Al Yaum articles only repeat the announcement. Kept as Need to know.
- E-salary: final phase 1 Jan 2026 covers all employers (D0045, D0046). Old draft cited Gulf News Dec 2025; now official.
- Pay: no official minimum. BBC Bangla (22 Oct 2026) restates the 2015 agreement: 2-year contracts, SR 800, free migration (D0068). Prothom Alo, Jugantor and Bangla Tribune hits say SR 800 (contract) and one returnee got 800 against a contracted 1,000. Banglatelegraph headline "doubling" not opened. No current official figure found in Bangla or English searches. The old draft's SR 800-1,300 (GCC Domestic, a recruitment site) and SR 1,000-1,900 for drivers/cooks were dropped as not verified.
- Women's zero cost: the old citation was a 1 Sep 2025 ministry letter approving 21 agencies (D0059, D0060). Conditions: Tk 15 lakh FDR security to BMET within 30 working days, no migration cost from the woman, Tk 300 stamp undertaking that the licence may be cancelled if she is abused, at least one woman employee. The 21 agencies (RL numbers): 2480, 2702, 1664, 1523, 2482, 2159, 2047, 2098, 1863, 2035, 1582, 2542, 1603, 1980, 0497, 2160, 992, 1546, 1639, 1930, 1876. This is one order; other approval letters exist. No single public list found: Need to know.
- Safe homes: Abrar's open item (draft said three from a 2018 source; Netra News said Bangladesh funds two). Current sources: WEWB citizen charter (Sept 2026, official, p.6 item 21, page image checked) lists safe houses in Saudi Arabia at Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [E0019]; WEWB annual report 2023-24 p.39 says one each in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [E0038]. BSS (6 Sep 2026) quotes the state minister in parliament: three safe homes in total, Riyadh and Jeddah in Saudi Arabia plus Muscat [D0064]. Official documents say three in Saudi; the minister's statement says two. Doc follows the official charter. Unresolved; a corridor expert or the embassy should confirm whether Madinah's is still open.
- Housekeeping course fee: not in the policy (it says BMET sets it "from time to time"). Facebook posts from TTCs show Tk 1,000 and Tk 3,000; not reliable enough. Need to know.
- Male domestic workers' fee: no BMET permission letter or cap found for individual (household) visas; Probash Guide gives Tk 4-6 lakh (D0076). Need to know.
- House driver theory test in Bangla: old draft cited KSA Expats; the 2026 KSA Expats page fetched does not say so. Need to know.

**Reality check (detail kept out of the doc)**
- MRRORS (Oct 2024): complaints by domestic workers are low; no effective inspection; most employers restrict phones; leaving to complain risks an absconding report (D0062, D0063).
- BBC Bangla (Oct 2026): NGO research, 90% of returnees surveyed came back because of physical or mental abuse; about 1,000 of 1,500 women returnees up to Sept 2026 came from Saudi Arabia; embassy sources blame untrained recruits; former ambassador Golam Moshi says agents sent village women with no training (D0067, D0069).
- Dhaka Tribune (Mar 2026): 70,000 women returned in 7 years; embassy letter on at least 55 domestic workers sheltering at the embassy; cases of rape, burning, death (D0073).
- Chandpur Somoy (Dec 2020, government letter): women wait 1 to 6 months in the Saudi female deportation centre and embassy safe home before flying (D0065). Old; no newer figure found.
- Probashir Diganta (Sep 2022) and C0139: embassy recovered six years' wages (SR 68,017, about Tk 19 lakh) for Nur Nahar after the family complained (D0066). Shows the labour wing can act.
- C0141 (another part, MFA): BMET arbitration settled a domestic worker's claim for less than half after agency threats. Relevant to the Bangladesh-side complaint route.
- Sonali News (2018): most women sent without the compulsory training; brokers sold certificates; ages and religion changed in passports (D0072, D0165).
- BBC Bangla (2020): age doctoring via second birth certificate (D0070, D0071).
- Outside bodies: ILO takes no individual complaints (C0123). OKUP gives free legal help (A0046). BRAC gives tele-counselling (E0111). IOM: no evidence found that it takes individual Saudi cases; not mentioned in the doc.

## Family visit
- ksavisa.sa "Family and Friends" page: e-visa only for eligible countries or US/UK/Schengen visa holders and GCC residents; otherwise service centres or embassy; 90/90 days; fees; 1 minute to 3 business days (D0126-D0129). This page describes the visitor-led visit visa; the resident-led family visit (invitation) is in the MOFA platform user manual: resident, Nafath login, "Family visit visa", Add visitor, Save, print; "Ratify individuals applications" (D0130-D0132).
- Old draft said "USD 101 plus insurance, single entry, up to 30 days" (N11 Bangladesh Monitor Feb 2025). Fragomen (Jul 2025) says single-entry only for the 14 nationalities after the 2025 suspension, max stay 90 days (D0137). Doc now says 90 days. Feb 2026 Facebook post (Saudi Times) says online renewals of family visit visas suspended from 10 Feb 2026; not verified, not used.
- KSA Expats (Jan 2026): sponsor needs a work iqama with 3-4 months; document number; 3-5 business days (D0133-D0136). Old draft said "at least 3 months" citing N301 (2022 blog); updated.
