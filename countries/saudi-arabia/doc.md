# Saudi Arabia: migration guide

How to go to Saudi Arabia from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step. A raised number such as 3 cites an official source; N3 a news or other third-party source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, as in N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a fact not yet confirmed; "You'll need" lists what to have ready.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000), SR the Saudi riyal and USD the US dollar. Riyal amounts are converted at SR 1 = Tk 33, the remittance rate on 7 November 2026 [SM0010]. BMET, the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, is the Bangladesh government agency for overseas employment and safe migration [A0111].

## Background

### In one minute

Saudi Arabia takes two of every three Bangladeshis who go abroad for work, though 2026 departures are 45% down on 2025 [E0129]. Every work visa belongs to a Saudi sponsor (kafil, কফিল), who can sell it, and it reaches the village through agents and brokers at several times its price [SB0007]. A man pays Tk 3.5-5 lakh or more, most often Tk 4-5 lakh, mostly borrowed, against a legal maximum of Tk 1.65 lakh [SJ0004, SB0024, SJ0025, A0013]. A cleaner or labourer earns SR 500-1,200 a month (Tk 16,500-40,000) [SJ0007, SL0033]. The sponsor holds the iqama (আকামা, the residence card), and a visa sold for a job that doesn't exist (a "free visa") leaves the worker without an iqama, work or pay [E0128]. A woman going as a domestic worker should pay nothing, yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [D0059, SI0030].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open as of 7 November 2026, but disrupted (see Current situation), and still the largest destination [E0129]. A man can go on a visa for a named job, and usually needs a Saudi skill certificate; a woman can go as a domestic worker, and needs none [SM0036, D0161]. Ages: see Who qualifies.

#### Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 (re-check by 7 December 2026): open, but two shocks hit this year. In February 2026 the Saudi labour ministry cancelled thousands of visa applications filed for jobs that didn't exist, and visas that had appeared on the Saudi system disappeared [E0127, B0079]. The Iran war and Houthi attacks on Saudi airports have cancelled over 1,000 flights since February; Biman flies Dhaka-Dammam daily from 7 November, Dhaka-Riyadh stays suspended, and Riyadh-bound workers go via Jeddah or Madinah [E0130, SH0007, SH0008, SH0009]. Workers holding visas have been stranded in Bangladesh by the flight cuts, and agencies have rushed to fly them before the visas expire (see When to pay, when to fly, and Hajj-season pauses) [E0130, SH0009]. In October 2026 Bangladesh was discussing with Saudi Arabia extending or renewing the stranded workers' visas [SG0036].

#### When to pay, when to fly, and Hajj-season pauses
- When to pay: pay the bulk only after the visa is issued and you hold BMET clearance; BMET's permission letters allow the agency's fee only after visa, clearance and flight are confirmed [A0016].
- When to fly: fly before the entry visa expires; an expired Saudi work visa is very hard to renew [SH0010].
- Hajj-season pauses: in 2025 block work visas on Qiwa paused for Bangladeshis through the Hajj season until about early July, and visit and tourist visas from 13 April [SB0043, E0123].

#### Channels

A visa reaches a worker through a recruiting agency, through a relative or friend working in Saudi Arabia, or as a free visa; across all destinations, IOM's (the International Organization for Migration's) 2019-2020 survey of returnees found 42.3% went through recruiting agencies, 33.2% on a visa relatives procured and 19.5% on a free visa [RD0021]. From Cumilla and other districts about 80% go on independent visas from relatives and friends, the rest on company visas (2024) [A0078].

> **Need to know**
> - Of workers going to Saudi Arabia in 2025-2026, what share got their visa through an agency, through a relative or friend, or as a free visa?

The channels:
- Recruiting agencies and resold visas: small Saudi firms sell demand letters to Bangladeshi brokers, Bangladeshis working in Saudi Arabia resell them, and Bangladeshi agencies, which often can't get job orders from employers directly, buy visas from Saudi agents and resell them down the chain (2020, 2024) [RD0005, RD0004, SB0012, SI0028].
- Relatives and friends working in Saudi Arabia [A0078].
- Women domestic workers: through ministry-approved agencies and Musaned, the Saudi domestic-worker platform (see Work visa: domestic worker) [D0060, D0157].

Whichever channel the visa comes through, a Bangladeshi agency the Saudi embassy has approved must carry the papers [SI0007]. Under the agreement on recruiting general workers that Bangladesh and Saudi Arabia signed on 6 October 2025, that is a private agency, not BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the state agency) [SB0001, SB0003]. There is no public way to check embassy approval (see The embassy's approved list); how to check the agency and get proof it holds the visa: see If a relative or friend offers you a visa, item 2.

BMET counts visas by form, not channel: a single visa (সিঙ্গেল ভিসা) is one sponsor's visa for one or a few workers, and may come from a relative or from an agency; a group visa (গ্রুপ ভিসা) is 25 or more workers for one company [SH0006]. By BMET figures, 21.36 lakh workers went on single visas against 90,000 on group visas in the five years to 2024 [RD0015].

#### Who the sponsor is

The sponsor type on your visa decides your rights: on a household visa you are a domestic worker in law, outside the Labour Law, even if you were promised a company job, and you may work only for that household [C0131, SK0001]. The Bangladesh embassy in Riyadh sorts sponsors three ways:
- Company (শারিকা, sharika) [SM0044]: on a company visa the company often provides food and lodging [SH0026].
- Establishment (মুয়াসসাসা, muassasa), such as a shop, workshop or small contracting firm [SM0044].
- Household, as private driver (সায়েক খাস, sayeq khas) or household worker (আমেল মানজিলি, amel manzil) [SM0043]. Each Saudi household can recruit eight workers as drivers, gardeners or cooks, and in 2020 these household visas were being used for free visas [SH0024].

A visa also names a profession (মিহনা, the job title on the visa): a man whose visa says farm worker but who works in a coffee shop or on a building site is fined if caught, and the kafil can deny knowing [SM0015]. Changing the profession is the employer's cost [C0020].

#### Who qualifies

Men must be 21 to 60, women going as domestic workers 25 to 45 [SH0001, D0039]. Most men going for labouring jobs need the skill test, the Skills Verification Program (SVP, run by Takamol): it is compulsory only for trades Takamol has listed, 108 by late 2026, but the list includes street, hospital, office and roof cleaners, load-and-unload workers and construction workers; domestic workers don't need it [SM0036, SM0037, SM0038, D0161]. How to take it: Work visa: company worker, Step 3 [A0058]. The Saudi embassy in Dhaka's 2019 rules also set higher minimum ages for some trades (industrial tailor 30, barber 35, garment shop worker 40), and drivers need a valid licence [SH0001, SH0003, SH0005]. Saudisation (reserving jobs for Saudis) closes some trades: housing supervisor became Saudi-only in 2026, and firms must give Saudis 60% of sales and marketing jobs [E0124, E0125].

#### Size

In 2025, 755,194 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work; Qatar came next with 107,598 [SB0017]. From January to 17 October 2026, 315,935 went, 45% fewer than a year before [E0129].

For trades closed to foreigners by Saudisation, see Who qualifies.

#### What has closed it before

The market can shut suddenly: Saudi Arabia stopped hiring Bangladeshi men from 2008 to 2016, and in 2025 recruitment stalled three times: over the Bangladesh embassy's single-visa attestation rule from late January, the skill test from mid-2025, and the Hajj season until about early July (see When to pay, when to fly, and Hajj-season pauses) [SB0031, E0126, SH0021, SH0016, SB0043].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A Saudi sponsor's work visa is sold on, and Bangladeshi agencies, sub-agents and brokers each add a commission until the worker pays three or four times the original price (see The root: a visa the sponsor owns) [SB0006, SB0007]. The choke point is the Saudi embassy in Dhaka, which decides which agencies may file Saudi work visas and doesn't publish the list (see The embassy's approved list) [SI0007, SI0008].

#### The root: a visa the sponsor owns

An employer issues work visas itself, instantly, on Qiwa, the Saudi labour ministry's platform, if its Nitaqat rating (the Saudisation rating) is at least "medium green" and its government account has enough credit [SI0001, SI0002]. Each visa costs it about SR 2,000 (Tk 66,000) [SB0006]. Sponsors take out two or three permits in their own name for workers they don't need and sell each for Tk 1-1.5 lakh; by the time a permit leaves Saudi Arabia it is worth about Tk 2 lakh, a Bangladeshi labour supplier in Saudi Arabia said in October 2026 [SI0036, SI0037, SB0007].

#### From okala to e-visa: the Saudi side, then Tasheer in Dhaka

On the Saudi visa platform the employer delegates the visa to a recruitment office [A0076]; agencies call this the okala (ওকালা, from the Arabic wakala, authorisation), and an okala visa sits in the agency's name, not a worker's [SI0004]. It becomes a named worker's visa when the agency files that worker against it; agents call this application the MOFA (মোফা, after the Saudi foreign ministry), and it shows the sponsor's name and an application number [SI0006]. So a visa is not yours until the agency files a MOFA in your name, and you can check which it is (see Work visa: company worker, Step 7) [SI0004, SI0006].

Once the MOFA is filed, the Bangladesh end starts: Tasheer, the visa centre in Dhaka, takes the fingerprints and fills in the form on Enjaz, the Saudi foreign ministry's visa platform [SI0019, SG0043]. Since May 2023 Bangladeshis get Saudi work visas as e-visas, with no sticker in the passport [SM0001].

#### The embassy's approved list

The Saudi embassy in Dhaka decides which of the 2,807 licensed agencies may file Saudi work visas, and doesn't publish the list: in September 2025 BMET wrote that it did not know which agencies those are [SI0009, SI0007, SI0008]. An agent's "embassy contact" proves nothing: in March 2023 Nazaha, Saudi Arabia's anti-corruption authority, arrested the Saudi embassy's own head of consular affairs and his deputy for taking money to issue work visas [SB0036, SB0044]. The agencies with the most Saudi job orders, and the allegations against some, are under Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4 [SC0001].

#### The Bangladesh embassy's attestation, and the missing contract

The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in Riyadh attests the employer's demand letter, for single visas too since 2025 (see Work visa: company worker, Step 2) [A0087]; TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) found some officials attest without checks or for bribes [SB0009]. In October 2025 BMET's additional director general said employment contracts are found for only 3-4% of workers going to Saudi Arabia [SI0032], and workers often first see a contract at the airport [SB0013].

#### The village end

Since 2023 an agency may use only registered sub-agents, but most agencies still deal with workers only through sub-agents beyond regulatory oversight [SG0005, SB0013]. A village broker in Cumilla got Tk 20,000 from the agent for each man in 2024 [SJ0042]. How to deal with a broker: see Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4.

#### Women domestic workers: the money should run the other way

The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi domestic worker (July 2026), and in 2020 the office paid the Bangladeshi agency about USD 2,000 per woman through Musaned, so she should pay nothing [SM0035, SI0029]; yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [SI0030]. How it works: see Work visa: domestic worker.

#### The medical

Brokers sell guaranteed "fit" results from the Wafid medical check (the Gulf health check) at centres allegedly controlled by a few influential owners who issue fake certificates; a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for a fit certificate was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [SB0040, B0031]. The check itself: Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The Saudi employer is meant to pay nearly every cost, and the legal maximum a worker may pay is Tk 1.65 lakh in all; yet a man pays two to three times that, about Tk 5 lakh on average and up to Tk 9 lakh, mostly borrowed [C0020, A0013, A0014, SJ0025]. Most of the jobs pay SR 800-1,000 a month [SJ0007]; a man on SR 800 who buys his own food and phone keeps at most SR 250 (Tk 8,250) (see What reaches home, and how long the debt takes) [SM0008]. The rule and the whole cost: see The legal maximum and what people really pay.

#### What the visa, medical and fingerprints really cost
- The visa: it costs the Saudi sponsor about SR 2,000 to issue, the sponsor sells it for Tk 1-1.5 lakh, and with each commission on the way the worker ends up paying three or four times the original price [SB0006, SI0037, SB0007]. On a genuine company visa you should pay only the Bangladesh-side fees, about Tk 19,800 in all, before the skill test, passport and ticket (see If a relative or friend offers you a visa, What it costs, official against real) [A0028, RB0021, B0052, RB0024, B0166, A0011, A0012, B0096]. A free visa (also called an azad visa, আযাদ ভিসা) is the scam visa with no real job [SM0045]; what it costs: see the same section.
- The medical: the set fee is about Tk 10,000, but most workers pay double or more; a worker declared unfit pays Tk 30,000-80,000 for a "fit" report, and many make a "contract" (কন্ট্রাক্ট) with a broker in advance to be passed [SJ0013, SJ0014]. A bought "fit" report can get you deported (see The medical) [B0031].
- Tasheer's fingerprints: the service fee is about Tk 660-694, or about Tk 2,100-2,200 in its premium Lounge; the appointment slip is free, so an agency asking money for it is overcharging (see Who pays what) [RB0024, SJ0010, RB0026].

#### How they pay

81% of men borrow to go [SJ0025]. The lenders:
- Family: loans mostly interest-free [SJ0034].
- NGOs: 10-15% interest [SJ0034].
- Moneylenders (মহাজন): far higher interest [SJ0034].
- Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers: up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% without collateral once the worker has the visa (see If you need to borrow) [A0052].

Workers pay intermediaries part in advance, often before the visa is confirmed, and mostly get no receipt [SB0013, SJ0040].

#### The iqama: the employer's bill

Each year a company worker's iqama costs the employer SR 9,150-10,350, plus compulsory health insurance [SM0022, SM0023]:
- iqama fee: SR 650;
- work permit fee: SR 100;
- expat levy: SR 800 a month (SR 700 for each foreign worker up to the number of Saudis in the firm);
- health insurance.

Every item is the employer's cost, and nothing may be deducted from wages for it [C0020, C0014]. If you are asked to pay any of it, complain to the labour ministry on 19911 (see If you aren't paid) [B0109]. In practice some small kafils and supply companies deduct the iqama and health-insurance cost from wages [SM0033]. The work permit and levy can be renewed for 3, 6, 9 or 12 months at a time [SM0024]: that is how a kafil hands a worker a 3-month iqama and comes back for money every quarter [A0080]. Check the expiry date yourself on Absher, the interior ministry's app [SM0040].

#### On a free visa you pay it yourself

On a free visa the worker pays the whole iqama renewal himself, SR 8,000-12,000 a year (Tk 2.6-4 lakh) [SM0013]. On top of that he pays the kafil: a monthly or yearly cut called kamishon (কামিশন), and an extra SR 5,000-6,000 (Tk 1.65-2 lakh) when the iqama comes up for renewal [SM0012, SG0002]. In 2026 workers told of paying a kafil Tk 4-8 lakh for a renewal that never came [SL0028].

#### What the job pays

Nearly 80% of those going are cleaners, loaders and other low-skilled workers on SR 800-1,000 by recruiters' account, and cleaners promised SR 1,000-1,200 often get no more than SR 500 [SJ0008, SJ0007, SL0033]. Saudi Arabia sets no minimum wage for foreign workers; the contract wage is what counts [C0092]. Monthly wages in BMET's 2025 permission letters [SJ0012, SJ0003, SJ0002]:
- kitchen workers: SR 800 plus SR 200 for food;
- construction labourers: SR 1,100 with food and housing;
- carpenters, masons, steel fixers, electricians and painters: SR 1,200 plus SR 300 for food;
- heavy equipment operators: SR 1,500.

New house drivers get about SR 1,500-1,800 [D0077].

#### What it costs to live

A worker living outside a camp needs about SR 1,450-2,650 a month (November 2026), more than the SR 800-1,000 most men earn [SM0008, SJ0007]. What the employer must provide: see Your rights at work, Housing, food and health care. The main items for a worker living outside [SM0008]:
- shared room: SR 500-900;
- food he cooks himself: SR 500-800;
- transport: SR 150-300;
- phone and internet: SR 50-100.

#### What reaches home, and how long the debt takes

A man on SR 800 (Tk 26,400) who buys his own food and phone, SR 550-900 a month, keeps at most SR 250 (Tk 8,250) [SM0008]. At that rate, and assuming free housing, a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes about 55 months, over four years, to repay before interest, against a usual 2-year contract [C0095]. Many low-skilled Gulf workers struggle to send even Tk 15,000-20,000 a month (2024) [SB0022]; a worker who does manage that still needs 23-30 months for the same debt [SB0022]. In 2020 men took 19 months or more to recover the cost [SJ0021]. Sending costs and channels: see Sending money home.

### What ties you to the employer

You are tied to your kafil, normally your employer: your visa and iqama belong to him [A0002]. After 12 months a company worker can change employer without consent if his contract is recorded on Qiwa, and he can resign and leave on his own exit visa, which needs a valid iqama; domestic and farm workers don't have these rights [C0053, C0179, C0146, C0165, SK0006].

- *To enter:* only your sponsor can bring you in, because the work visa names the sponsor and your occupation [A0002, B0075]. On arrival you get a border number (বর্ডার নম্বর), the 10-digit number Jawazat (the passports directorate) gives you until the iqama is issued [SM0030].
- *To renew:* only the employer gets and renews your iqama and work permit, and pays for them [C0020].
- *The first six months:* since 19 February 2025 a trial period of up to 180 days can be written into the contract, and either side may end the contract during it [SM0002, SM0003]. Who pays the ticket home: see If you resign or are dismissed.
- *To quit:* you are free to quit with notice, and without notice if the employer breaks its duties (see If you resign or are dismissed) [C0027, C0029]. Just stopping work lets the employer dismiss you without end-of-service pay and report you absent [C0028].
- *To change employer (kafil bodol, কফিল বদল, or kafala, কাফালা):* for the first 12 months you are tied to your employer; after that you can move mid-contract with 90 days' notice, and at any time, even without a valid iqama or a Qiwa contract, if you go unpaid 3 months, your iqama or permit expires or the employer disappears; the employer is only notified [C0053, C0055, C0057]. How: see If you want to change employer.
- *To leave:* a company worker issues his own exit and re-entry visa (khuruj, খুরুজ) or final exit (khuruj nihai) on Absher or Qiwa [C0146, SM0047]; a domestic worker can't, because the employer issues it [SK0021]. A worker the sponsor reports for a crime or damage, such as theft, is declared matloob (মাতলুব, wanted) and must settle the case before he can leave [SM0046, SM0039].
- *Working for someone else:* only through Ajeer, the labour ministry's platform for temporary work for a company that isn't your sponsor; the other company applies for an Ajeer permit, the proof you are lawfully at its site [SM0006, SM0007]. Any other outside work is illegal [C0021].

#### Changing employer: what it costs

Officially you pay nothing: the new employer pays the government transfer fee, SR 2,000 for a worker's first transfer, SR 4,000 for the second, SR 6,000 from the third, plus any iqama fees the old employer left unpaid [SM0016, C0020]. Anything a worker is charged beyond that is a broker's or kafil's cut, and agencies openly advertise kafils for hire [SM0034]. A firm in the red Nitaqat band cannot take you [SM0017]. What resigning costs you: see If you resign or are dismissed.

#### Which law covers you

The Labour Law covers expatriate workers in private companies [SK0005]. It excludes domestic workers and those counted as such, and agricultural workers [SK0001, SK0002]. Domestic workers, including house drivers, gardeners and cooks, come under the Domestic Workers Regulation (Work visa: domestic worker) [D0023], and can move without consent only in listed cases, such as 3 months' unpaid wages or no iqama in time [D0031]. Moves with everyone's agreement (to a company job only in the first year): see Domestic worker to another household, and Domestic worker to a company job.

#### Your passport

Your employer may not keep it (see Your rights at work, Your passport) [C0022].

#### Huroob (হুরুব), or "absent from work"

Huroob is the employer's report on Qiwa that you stopped coming to work. An employer can file it only if your contract isn't recorded on Qiwa [C0062], and for a company worker the report can't be withdrawn [C0064]. Kafils answer workers who protest about money or the iqama with huroob [SL0036], and in 2022 workers reported employers filing false reports and demanding bribes to withdraw them [C0114]. Left unresolved, it makes you illegal and brings a 3 to 5 year re-entry ban [C0067, E0057]. The deadline and what to do: see "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)".

#### An expired iqama

You are undocumented: no bank, money transfer or SIM, and detention and deportation if stopped [B0132]. The passports directorate fined a failure to renew SR 500, then SR 1,000, then deported (2019) [SK0018]. An expired iqama also lets you change employer without consent, if a new employer takes you [C0055]. Getting home: see Going home without valid papers.

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

Nearly every Saudi complaint is one of three things: no iqama, no work, or pay that is late, short or missing, and behind them, again and again, is a visa sold for a job that didn't exist or an employer who won't pay for the permit [E0128, SL0014]. The false beliefs that go with them are about the same visa and permit: that a "free visa" lets you work for anyone, as brokers sell it [SM0011], and that the iqama is the worker's cost, when the employer should pay [SL0031].

#### Complaints

Of 114 complaints to BMET in 2023-2024 that OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) studied, 47% got no work permit on arrival [SL0004]. Men without a permit were often kept until their entry visa ran out, then counted as absconders [SL0011]. Only 39% of complaints were resolved, usually for Tk 20,000 to just over Tk 1 lakh [SL0007]. To complain, see "If an agency, broker or relative takes your money" and If you aren't paid [A0056, C0039].

#### What people believe, and the facts
- **"A free visa lets you work for anyone."** Brokers sell it as "go and work wherever you like" [SM0011]. In fact a worker may work only for the employer and job on the permit, or for another company through an Ajeer permit, which that company must apply for [C0021, SM0007]. Many jobs also need a baladiya (বালাদিয়া, municipal) health card, which free-visa workers don't easily get [SM0014]. Of 10 men one Cumilla broker sent, three got a proper job with a contract (2024) [SL0020]. To spot one, see Avoid a "free visa" [A0079].
- **"The iqama is the worker's cost: pay the kafil and he renews it."** A Bangladesh embassy official confirmed the employer pays (see "What you pay, and what you earn and keep", The iqama: the employer's bill) [SL0031].
- **"Saudi Arabia abolished the kafala system in 2025."** No Saudi announcement was made: the story began in an 18 October 2025 article by ImpACT International, a UK think tank, citing no Saudi source [SK0008].
- **"A visit, tourist or Umrah visa can be turned into an iqama once you're there."** A visit visa can't become an iqama, and working on a tourist or Umrah visa brings a ban of 3 to 10 years (Changing visa type) [E0054, E0057].
- **"Pay extra and the medical is guaranteed fit."** This is false (see "How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut", The medical) [B0032, B0031].

#### Irregular routes and how they end

Irregular status can end in a raid: those detained usually spend one to two weeks in a deportation centre and land in Dhaka with almost nothing [SG0032, SG0033], then face a re-entry ban [E0057], and irregular returnees get no Welfare Board benefits [E0092]. There are ways out without a raid: the embassy's Special Exit Programme, grace periods, and, until the end of 2026, an employer regularising an expired permit (see Going home without valid papers) [E0026, E0087, G0014].

Workers fall out of status in four ways:
- a free visa with no real job [A0081];
- an employer who never turns the three-month entry permit into an iqama, often to avoid the fee [B0131, SL0014];
- a kafil who keeps the renewal money or files huroob [SL0028, SL0036];
- a visit or Umrah visa overstayed [E0057].

Many free-visa men end up running or staffing a shop or workshop registered in a Saudi's name (tasattur, commercial concealment): that carries up to 5 years in prison, a fine of up to SR 5 million, and deportation with a ban on returning to work [SM0020, SM0018, SM0019, SM0021]. Anyone who shelters, transports or helps a violator faces up to 15 years in prison and a fine of up to SR 1 million [SL0040].

## Work visa: company worker

This visa is for a job with a Saudi company. The whole process usually takes about 1-3 months [A0004].

### Who pays what

**The employer pays** the recruitment fees, the iqama (residence card), the work permit and its renewals, exit and re-entry fees and your ticket home when the job ends [A0005]; when you pay the ticket home yourself: see If you resign or are dismissed.

**The flight out:** in two BMET permission letters from September 2025 (for RL-1059 and for Bonanza Overseas, RL-0579), the employer pays the airfare [A0016, ZZ0017]. Find the letter for your own job on BMET's website and check that it says the same (how: see Before you pay anyone). On a single visa there is no such letter, so nothing official says who pays the flight out [RB0034] (see Need to know below).

**You pay** about Tk 19,800 in Bangladesh-side fees, plus the skill test, a passport and any course (list below), and the agency may take only a capped amount (see The legal maximum and what people really pay) [A0028, RB0021, B0052, RB0024, B0166, A0011, A0012, B0096]. The list:

- OEP registration: Tk 200 [A0028].
- A TTC course admission fee, if you take a course: Tk 748 [A0030].
- A passport: Tk 4,025 for a 48-page, 5-year e-passport, regular delivery [A0124].
- The skill test, if your trade is listed: USD 85 per attempt (see Step 3) [A0007, A0008].
- The Wafid medical: agencies put it at about Tk 10,000 (2025), the USD 10 booking (about Tk 1,500 through a shop) plus about Tk 8,500 at the centre; the centre's official fee is not published (see Step 4) [B0004, RB0021].
- Police clearance: Tk 1,500 (see Step 6) [B0052].
- Tasheer's service fee: about Tk 660-694, or about Tk 2,100-2,200 in its premium Lounge (see Step 5) [RB0024, SJ0010].
- The pre-departure orientation (PDO): Tk 100 plus a Tk 100 platform charge (see Step 8) [B0166].
- The Wage Earners' Welfare Board fee: Tk 5,500 [A0011].
- Migrant worker insurance: Tk 1,000, covering death or permanent disability for Tk 10 lakh over 5 years [A0012].
- Source income tax at BMET clearance: Tk 500 for a Saudi single visa [B0096].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the medical centre's official fee for the tests?
> - Does BMET still charge Tk 250 for clearance and the smart card?
> - Does the DEMO charge for fingerprints?
> - On a single visa, who pays the outbound ticket: the employer or the worker?

#### The legal maximum and what people really pay

**The rule**
The expatriates' ministry set a maximum of Tk 1.65 lakh in all in 2016-2017 and has not changed it; in October 2026 the state minister said new country-by-country maximums are coming [A0013, SJ0019, SJ0001, SB0016]. Only group job orders need the ministry's recruitment permission (2017) [RB0034]. Two BMET permission letters from September 2025 (RL-1059 and RL-0579) put every Saudi-side cost, levy and airfare included, on the employer, and let the agency take only what the letter allows, Tk 50,000 and Tk 58,610 respectively, by cheque or pay order [A0016, ZZ0017, A0019]. A worker on a single visa has no such letter, so only the Tk 1.65 lakh cap applies (how many go on single visas: see "Is it open now, and who can go?", Channels) [RB0034]. The fees a worker legitimately pays in Bangladesh are listed at the start of this section (see Who pays what).

**What people really pay**
In 2026 agencies put the whole cost at Tk 3.5-5 lakh and a press investigation at about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [SJ0004, A0014].

> **Need to know**
> - Do new country maximums replace Tk 1.65 lakh?

#### If you need to borrow

Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period [A0052, A0053]. You can apply only once you hold the visa and the BMET smart card, which come near the end (see Step 8) [A0050].

You'll need [A0049, A0050, A0051]:

- age 18 to 60 and no unpaid loan anywhere;
- 3 passport-size photos;
- copies of NID, passport, visa, BMET smart card (see Step 8) and contract if any;
- 3 signed MICR cheque leaves and a statement from your own bank account;
- Tk 500 to open a savings account;
- 2 guarantors who, like you, live permanently in the branch's area, each with a photo, NID copy and proof of address such as a utility bill:
  - one well-off person with 3 signed MICR cheque leaves, a bank statement and proof of income;
  - one family member or close relative.

1. At your nearest branch ([branch list](https://pkb.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e0de933eb65569e28f0c)), fill in the free form and open the savings account [A0050, A0120].
2. Pay 0.5% processing and 0.5% documentation (each plus VAT) and Tk 900 per lakh risk cover; bring 16 revenue stamps of Tk 10, plus 3 adhesive stamps of Tk 100 for each guarantor [A0055, A0052].
3. The bank must decide within 7 working days of complete papers [A0052]. Help: 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [A0054].

> **Need to know**
> - How, when and into which account is the loan paid?

**Scam at this step:** PKB staff have been accused of asking Tk 10,000-20,000 in bribes for a Tk 3 lakh loan; there is no such fee [A0040, A0055]. Complain to PKB's grievance officer, the General Manager (Administration and Accounts), gm2@pkb.gov.bd, +88-02-48321195, who has 30 working days [A0121]; then the Deputy Managing Director, dmd@pkb.gov.bd, +88-02-48319468, who has 20 working days [A0122]. Or call the ministry hotline 16135 [A0025] or file at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), the government's complaint system [A0036].

### Step 1: Find a real job

You find a job in one of two ways: ask licensed agencies what Saudi jobs they have (item 1), or take a visa a relative or friend in Saudi Arabia offers (item 2). Single visas are often resold through brokers (2024) (how jobs reach workers: see "Is it open now, and who can go?", Channels) [RD0005]. Before paying anything, check the agency, the company and the visa (see Before you pay anyone) [A0107, A0108]. Register on OEP first (see While you look, item 1) [A0109].

1. **Ask licensed agencies what Saudi jobs they have, and check each one.** Recruiting without a licence under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 is not allowed, and travel agencies may not send workers [A0125, A0093].

   You'll need: the agency's name or RL (recruiting licence) number.

   1. Search the name or RL number on [OEP's agency list](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies); the row shows licence status, validity date, office address and phone [A0089]. Go no further unless it says "Active" and the date hasn't passed (others are Expired, Suspended, Cancelled; licences are cancelled for visa fraud) [A0091, A0092].
   2. Ask the agency, at that address and phone, which Saudi jobs they have for your trade, and ask to see the employer's demand letter (company, jobs, pay) with the embassy's attestation (see Step 2) [A0089, A0108, A0107].
   3. Read workers' reviews on [Recruitment Advisor](https://www.recruitmentadvisor.org/search-an-agency), a trade-union site, giving what each paid, for which country, and whether they got the promised job and pay [A0094, A0095].

   **Which agencies.** Only Bonanza Overseas (RL0579) has outside evidence of fair recruitment (its entry is below) [A0096, A0097]; the others are listed by how many Saudi workers BMET approved for them, not by quality, and some carry red flags. No official grading of agencies exists, and the state minister for expatriates' welfare has said he could not confidently recommend any agency [SC0020, SC0021]. These had the most Saudi workers in the 1,148 permission letters BMET posted from June 2023 to December 2025 (70,145 workers from 370 agencies, counted by the team; workers means posts approved, not people who went); a high count shows activity, not fair treatment [SC0001]. Red flags are allegations or investigations, not court verdicts; the full list of agencies and flags is in the knowledge base's [agencies folder](https://github.com/FiazAbrar/bsaisl-migration-knowledge-base/tree/main/agencies).

   - **Greenland Overseas (RL0040, Active):** 44 letters, 7,421 workers [SC0001, SC0002]. Red flag: the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) filed a case in September 2025 against its owners, one of 13 agencies accused of forming a syndicate that charged Malaysia-bound workers nearly five times the government rate [SC0012, SC0013].
   - **Career Overseas Consultants (RL0403, Active):** 24 letters, 3,757 workers [SC0001, SC0003]. Red flag: Bloomberg (2026) reports agents' allegations that, during Malaysia's 2016-2018 hiring round, it and the other nine agencies Malaysia routed hiring through charged workers a syndicate fee of about USD 1,350; it denies collecting unlawful fees, and a 2018 lawsuit against the 10 agencies was withdrawn with no findings of wrongdoing [SC0016, SC0023, SC0022, SC0015].
   - **A.J. International (RL1304, Active):** 26 letters, 2,755 workers [SC0001, SC0004].
   - **Bashundhara Employment Services (RL1064, Active):** 12 letters, 2,090 workers [SC0001, SC0005].
   - **Amco Enterprise (RL1537, Active):** 28 letters, 1,933 workers [SC0001, SC0006].
   - **United Gulf Services (RL1036, Active):** 23 letters, 1,850 workers [SC0001, SC0007]. Red flag: Prothom Alo (September 2026) reports it is on Malaysia's 2026 list of 25 agencies and is owned by the wife of Abdul Hai, whose Greenland Overseas was in the earlier Malaysia syndicate; no allegation is made against United Gulf itself [SC0014].
   - **Bonanza Overseas (RL0579, Active, +88028881118):** 21 letters, 1,642 workers [SC0001, A0090]. The only one with outside evidence of fair recruitment: it was the Bangladeshi agency in an ILO-backed fair recruitment pilot for construction jobs in Qatar (2018-2021), not Saudi Arabia [A0096, A0097]. BMET capped its cost at Tk 58,610 per worker in one Saudi letter [A0019].
   - **Azam Overseas (RL0535, Active):** 10 letters, 1,221 workers [SC0001, SC0008].

   In July 2026 the ministry revoked 49 licences in the Malaysia syndicate case, among them Catharsis International (RL0549), Rabbi International (RL0258) and Sultan Overseas (RL0906), which held Saudi letters for 971, 936 and 887 workers; OEP shows them Cancelled [SC0019, SC0017, SC0018, SC0001, SC0009, SC0010, SC0011].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which other agencies place workers in the promised Saudi job at a fair cost?

   **Brokers.** Since 2023 an agency may appoint only a person registered under the rules as its sub-agent [SG0005]. Pay the agency itself through its bank account (see Before you pay anyone), not the broker [A0023], and write down the broker's name, age, parents' names and address, which a BMET complaint asks for [A0037].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there a public list of registered sub-agents to check a broker against?
   > - Must the agency repay money its broker took?

2. **If a relative or friend in Saudi Arabia offers you a visa.** A visa for one or a few workers is a single visa (সিঙ্গেল ভিসা); one that comes through someone abroad is also called a personal or FNF (friends and family) visa (single and group visas, and how many go this way: see "Is it open now, and who can go?", Channels) [SH0006, SI0023, RA0041]. Even a visa that came free from family must then be carried by a Bangladeshi agency the Saudi embassy has approved (what to do: see If a relative or friend offers you a visa) [SI0007].

#### Before you pay anyone

Pay nothing until the agency (item 1), the company (below) and the visa (Step 7) check out, and pay the bulk only after the visa is issued, BMET clearance is done and the flight is confirmed [A0016]. Pay the agency through its bank account, by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and keep the receipt [A0023, A0016]; complaints without proof of payment fail at BMET and at legal aid, or win little compensation [RC0048, RC0017, A0043].

- **Get the employer's name, job, salary and embassy-attested contract in writing,** and the agency's name, licence number, address and phone [A0107, A0108].

- **Check the company exists.** Saudi companies have a unified national number starting with 7, which replaced the commercial registration (CR) number [A0123, A0127].

  You'll need: the company's name or unified national number.

  1. On [mc.gov.sa/en/eservices/Pages/Commercial-data.aspx](https://mc.gov.sa/en/eservices/Pages/Commercial-data.aspx) (Ministry of Commerce, free, in Arabic), search the name or number, type the verification code and press Search; the registration data appear at once [A0098, A0099]. Help: 1900 in Saudi Arabia, cs@mc.gov.sa from Bangladesh [A0100, A0126].
  2. If the company isn't found or doesn't match the employer on your demand letter, don't pay, and complain about the agency to BMET (see "If an agency, broker or relative takes your money") [A0098, A0107, A0056].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Which fields does the result show?

- **Look for BMET's permission letter for your job.** A worker on a single visa won't find a letter (see The legal maximum and what people really pay), so for them the DEMO check (next point) is the one that counts [RB0034]. A letter names the agency and RL number, the Saudi company and sponsor ID, the visa and demand numbers, the job, the number of workers, salary and benefits, and the most the agency may take from each worker [A0017].

  You'll need: the agency's name or RL number.

  1. On [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos), search the agency's name, RL number or "Saudi" [A0020, A0021].
  2. Open that agency's Saudi PDF and compare company, job, salary and maximum cost with what you were told [A0017]. One letter, for example, allowed at most Tk 50,000, by cheque, bank draft or pay order only, after visa, clearance and flight were confirmed, with the employer paying all Saudi-side costs and each worker signing the contract and getting a Bangla translation [A0016, A0018].
  3. No letter for your job? Have the papers checked at your DEMO (next point) [A0022].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Does BMET still issue these letters for Saudi group visas, and where?

- **Have your papers checked.** Take copies of the demand letter, contract and visa to your DEMO or BMET (89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000) and ask them to check they are real [A0022, A0110]; DEMO addresses and phones are in BMET's list of DEMOs (see Step 1, While you look, item 1) [A0112, A0113]. The visa check is free and takes 3 working days (see Step 7, item 2) [B0077].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Is the check of a demand letter and contract also free, and how long does it take?
  > - Is the answer in writing?

- **If you need to borrow:** see If you need to borrow.

- **If the agency, a broker or a relative takes your money:** see "If an agency, broker or relative takes your money".

#### If a relative or friend offers you a visa

**Check the visa and the sponsor yourself before any money moves (item 1 below).** The paper doesn't show where the visa came from. A relative's visa is one of three things, and all three arrive as the same work visa [A0077, SI0007]:

- *A real job with the relative's own employer.* The employer pays the visa, recruitment fees, the iqama and their renewals, so the visa itself should cost you nothing [RA0010, A0005]. Relatives still charge: one migrant paid a close relative Tk 4 lakh for a visa the relative had got free [RA0003].
- *A visa the relative bought from a kafil (the sponsor) or a visa trader.* It has already cost about Tk 2 lakh before it leaves Saudi Arabia (see "How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut", The root) [SI0037, SB0007], and the sponsor will pass his yearly levy and iqama fee to you (see If it turns out to be a free visa, below). These are the free (azad) visas, meaning you find your own work [SM0045], and new ones are issued as amel adi (ordinary labourer) or load-unload (লোড আনলোড) visas, because load-unload needs no skill test [RA0028, RB0045, RB0027]. The job may not be the one promised: a cousin paid Tk 4 lakh for a driver visa and the employer put the worker to herding goats, then camels [RA0001].
- *A visa the relative sells as a business.* A returnee broker in Cumilla charged Tk 3 lakh a head and passed most of it to the friends in the Gulf who supplied the visas [SL0021]. Many men present a relative as the source when they really bought from a broker [RB0006].

Some are free visas or household visas with no real job (2020, 2025) [SH0024, RA0028]. Item 1 tells you which one you have.

**You'll need from the relative, before any money moves** [B0076, B0073, SM0044, A0127, RB0013]:

- the 10-digit visa number;
- the sponsor's name and his Saudi ID, iqama number or company registration (CR) number;
- the occupation on the visa;
- whether the sponsor is a company, an establishment or a household;
- the wage;
- the name and RL (recruiting licence) number of the Bangladeshi agency that will hold the visa.

1. **Check the visa and the sponsor before paying anything.** On [ksavisa.sa/track-application](https://ksavisa.sa/track-application), the Saudi Foreign Ministry's visa platform, enter the visa number with the sponsor's Saudi ID, iqama or CR number (the inviter's number) and the captcha [B0073]. Check a company on the Ministry of Commerce site by its name or unified national number (see Step 1, Before you pay anyone) [A0098, A0127]. For a household visa, the relative can have the sponsor check Musaned's Visa Status Inquiry with the sponsor's ID and the request number while you are on a call [RC0012]. Your own passport number shows nothing useful until the agency files the MOFA (see item 5.2), which attaches your passport to the visa [RB0023, SI0006].

   *The trap:* a PDF or photo on WhatsApp proves nothing. In Bishwanath, Sylhet, a family with a brother in Saudi Arabia took Tk 5 lakh for a driving visa and handed over a PDF of a different, three-month visa [RC0019, RC0020].

   *What it tells you:* if the sponsor is a person or household rather than a company, it is a household visa: you'll be a domestic worker, outside the Labour Law, working only for that household, not at the relative's company [SK0001, A0081]. These are the classic free visas [SH0024]: a Saudi household pays no levy on its first four domestic workers, so the visa costs it nothing a year to keep in levy [RH0001]. A load-unload or amel adi occupation carries the same warning [RA0028].

   *If it doesn't match* what you were promised (another sponsor, another job, no record), don't pay. Visas can also vanish when the labour ministry cancels applications filed on fake demand letters (see "Is it open now, and who can go?", Current situation) [B0079, B0080].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Before the MOFA, does ksavisa.sa track-application show a work visa's sponsor and occupation from the visa number and the sponsor's ID or CR number, or nothing?

2. **Get the agency's name from the relative, check it, and get proof it holds the visa.** The worker rarely chooses: the okala goes to whatever agency the visa seller already deals with, sometimes one whose licence he rents [RD0006]. The sponsor files the okala (Arabic tafweed or wakala) on ksavisa.sa (still called Enjaz) under Authorisation on a visa, entering the visa number, his unified number, the agency's name, your city of arrival and the number of visas [RB0011, RB0012, RB0013, SI0004]. It takes one working day and the government fee starts at SR 75 (Tk 2,500); Saudi service firms charge about SR 200 to file it [RB0011, RB0015, RB0014]. This is the sponsor's cost, not yours [B0116].

   Check the agency's RL number on OEP: "Active", with a validity date not passed (see Step 1, item 1) [A0089]. Then, before paying the agency anything, get it to confirm in writing, or by a screenshot from its own login on the Saudi visa platform, that it holds the okala for your visa number [SI0003, A0076]. Later, check that the same agency appears on your MOFA printout (see item 5.2).

   *The trap:* once the okala is filed you are stuck with that agency. Moving means the first agency cancels both the okala and the MOFA, which costs money and time, doesn't always work, and often leaves the passport unusable for a new visa [RB0041, RB0042].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does it cost in 2026 to cancel an okala and MOFA and move to another agency?
   > - Is the office that "processes" a single visa often a sub-agent's, not the agency named on the okala?

3. **Make sure the demand letter will be attested.** Since 2025 single visas, like group visas, need the employer's demand letter attested by the Bangladesh Embassy in Riyadh, which does it online on beriyadh.org for registered companies (see Step 2) [A0087, A0102]. A sponsor who won't attest is a warning: companies that bring workers only to let them loose without an iqama are slow to give a demand letter [RB0033].

   > **Need to know**
   > - In November 2026, does BMET clear a one-person Saudi visa without the attested demand letter?
   > - How does a household sponsor, who can't register on beriyadh.org as a company, get one attested?

4. **Pay in stages you can check, and so you can prove it.** The relative will usually want money first, because he has already paid the kafil in riyals; your family pays taka at home to his family, outside the banks, and part leaves by hundi (হুন্ডি, informal money transfer) [RA0018, SB0010]. Tie each payment to something you have checked yourself: nothing until the visa and sponsor check out (item 1) and the agency confirms it holds the okala (item 2); a part once the MOFA printout shows the promised sponsor and agency (item 5.2); the rest only once the e-visa is issued and checked (see Step 7). Pay by bank or bKash to a named account, never cash: in a Banshkhali case a Saudi-based man took Tk 3.58 lakh through his wife for a visa he never sent, and police confirmed the fraud from the bank and bKash records [RA0031, RA0032].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Do families take a stamp-paper deed or a security cheque from a relative selling a visa?
   > - Has either ever won a case?

5. **Let the agency carry the file, in this order.** From medical to flight usually takes 1.5-2.5 months, a month at the fastest [RB0031].

   1. *Medical:* book the Wafid slip yourself (see Step 4). Agencies say you can't, and take it for about Tk 1,500 (USD 10 plus about Tk 200 for the shop) [RB0044, RB0021].
   2. *MOFA (মোফা):* after a fit result the agency files your visa application on Enjaz with your passport and photo; from then a search shows the sponsor's name and the application number (see Step 7) [RB0023, SI0006]. Check the sponsor and agency on it before paying the next part. Drop out after MOFA and the agency charges a "MOFA fine", usually Tk 3,000 [RB0023]. Some agencies won't file until you bring police clearance, so apply early (see Step 6) [RB0025].
   3. *Skill test,* if the occupation is a listed trade (see Step 3) [A0058]. It can also come before the medical, since the certificate lasts five years [A0104].
   4. *Tasheer fingerprints:* the agency books the slip (what it costs and what the agency may charge: see Step 5). Once all papers reach the Saudi mission the e-visa can be issued in 3-4 days; agencies quote a week to two months [RB0046, RB0029]. Check the e-visa yourself (see Step 7) [B0075, B0077].
   5. *Orientation and BMET's own fingerprint:* the 3-day orientation (see Step 8), then a second fingerprint, BMET's bio-enrolment ("bio finger"), for the clearance card [RB0028].
   6. *BMET clearance (ম্যানপাওয়ার):* the agency applies, in 3 working days (see Step 8) [B0095]. BMET's charter lets a worker who got a visa on his own apply alone [A0057, B0098], but after the okala the visa sits in the approved agency's name, so the agency handles the okala, MOFA and Tasheer and in practice the clearance too [SI0007, SI0004].
   7. *Payment and ticket:* you settle the agency's bill before it takes the clearance and buys the ticket [RB0030].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Since September 2025, has BMET's emigration branch cleared a Saudi single visa for a worker who applied without an agency?

   *The risk of delay:* a visa that sits too long during attestation, the medical or Tasheer can lapse, and the seller then asks for money for a new one.

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does a Saudi work visa stay usable after issue on Qiwa?
   > - What do sellers charge when it lapses before the worker flies?

6. **Know who answers if it fails.** The visa seller signs the agency a written undertaking (মুচলেকা, muchleka) taking all liability for the workers, so on a single visa the agency is liable for nothing beyond the clearance papers [RD0007, RD0008]. The agency got perhaps Tk 20,000 of the Tk 4-5 lakh, so a BMET case against it recovers little, and single-visa workers who come back without work get back not even Tk 1 lakh [RD0010, RD0011]. If you named a relative as the source but bought from a broker, there is no one in Bangladesh to pursue [RB0006]. What to do if the relative cheats you: see "If an agency, broker or relative takes your money", If the relative cheats you.

7. **After arrival.** In the first week set up Absher and check your Qiwa contract against what you were promised (see Set up Absher and check your Qiwa contract). Every month check your iqama expiry and any "Absent from Work" flag on Absher (see "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)") [SM0040, C0112]. Keep your passport; the sponsor may not hold it (see Your passport) [B0114]. The iqama, permit, levy, renewals and any transfer fee (SR 2,000 for a first transfer) are the employer's [A0005, C0020, SM0016]. If no iqama comes or the job isn't there, see If it turns out to be a free visa, below.

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does a free-visa worker pay a broker or kafil in 2026 to transfer his sponsorship to a real employer?

**What it costs, official against real.** A relative's visa is not cheaper than an agency's: workers paid relatives Tk 286,642 on average, brokers Tk 303,351 and agencies Tk 243,531 [RD0023].

- *Genuine company visa:* you pay only the Bangladesh-side fees, about Tk 19,800 in all (the list under Who pays what), before the skill test, passport and ticket [A0028, RB0021, B0052, RB0024, B0166, A0011, A0012, B0096]. **Women.** A woman going as a domestic worker should pay nothing and goes through a ministry-approved agency and Musaned (Work visa: domestic worker) [D0059, D0060]. A relative arranging it is a warning: in October 2026 a Muktagachha woman's cousin met her at the Saudi airport, had her sign an Arabic paper and, she says, had "sold" her to a sponsor for two years on a khadema (খাদেমা, housemaid) visa at SR 1,000 a month; he was arrested under the human trafficking law [RC0061, RC0044, RC0045].

**If it turns out to be a free visa**

*The iqama traps.* By law the employer gets and pays for your iqama within 90 days of arrival, and a late one costs the employer a SR 500 fine, not you [B0108, RC0033, C0020]. On a bought visa it rarely works that way. A three-month iqama is legal, but it is the classic sign of a free visa: if no job turns up before it runs out, you are illegal [RC0059, A0080]. Nine men from Gurudaspur, Natore, each paid a villager settled in Saudi Arabia Tk 4-5 lakh; two years later none had an iqama, and his wife said it was because they hadn't paid extra for it [RC0051, RC0052]. On a free visa the kafil passes the levy and iqama fee to you (see "What you pay, and what you earn and keep", On a free visa you pay it yourself) [SM0022, SG0054]. In 2026 kafils took SR 16,000-18,000 a year for making and renewing the iqama [RA0023]; one 2025 account puts the yearly cut, called faida (ফায়দা), at SR 10,000-12,000 on average, rising as the years go on [RA0027]. Anything above the levy and fees, or any levy demanded from a company worker, is the sponsor's cut. Brokers have sold a "one-year" iqama for SR 5,000 and handed over a three-month one [RA0024]. One worker flown home from a Saudi jail in November 2026 had paid a middleman Tk 3.7 lakh for an iqama that was never made [RC0056].

*The sponsor's weapons:* working for anyone but the sponsor breaks Article 39 of the Labour Law, a SR 10,000 fine and deportation for the worker the first time [RA0009, D0030]; since February 2026 a business that lets its workers work for others faces up to SR 100,000 [RC0034]. So a sponsor whose worker is caught outside reports him absent (huroob), and one worker-writer described kafils filing theft cases, sometimes for over a crore taka, which stop the worker going home [RC0035]. Don't sign or thumbprint blank or unexplained papers for the kafil [RC0036].

*If it fails, on a company visa:* no iqama after 90 days, complain on 19911 (After you arrive) [B0109]. You can move employer without consent once your work permit has expired or was never issued [C0055], issue your own exit and re-entry or final exit visa on Absher or Qiwa [C0146], and if reported absent you have 60 days to transfer or take a final exit [C0063].

*If it fails, on a household visa:* only the domestic worker rules apply. The ministry can move you to another employer if your iqama isn't issued or renewed within 30 days [D0031], but you cannot issue your own final exit visa; the employer does it on Absher, which is the kafil's real hold over free-visa men [SK0021].

*Either way:* the embassy's online form has a "no iqama after arrival" option, and the Special Exit Programme covers workers whose iqama was never issued (Going home without valid papers) [E0089, E0027]. Until the end of 2026 an employer can still regularise a permit never issued within 6 months of joining [G0015].

3. **Book:** sign in with your email, password and the emailed code; in Account Dashboard press Start Verification and choose your occupation (fixed after this), Direct Assessment (In-person), your division or city (the centre is assigned automatically), the test language and a green date; tick "I declare that the information entered is correct" and press Confirm [A0063, A0064, A0068].

#### Avoid a "free visa"

A "free" (azad) visa is a real visa bought from a sponsor who has no job for you: you must find your own work and housing, work illegally, often pay the sponsor a monthly cut and renewal money (see "What you pay, and what you earn and keep", On a free visa you pay it yourself), and can be deported [A0083, A0082, A0081, A0079]. BMET warns that no visa is free [A0024].

**Signs of a free visa:**

- a household sponsor, not a company (see Who the sponsor is) [SH0024];
- an amel adi (ordinary labourer) or load-unload occupation [RA0028];
- a 3-month iqama [RC0059, A0080];
- a contract in Saudi Arabia that isn't the one signed in Bangladesh [A0081].

Have the papers checked (see Before you pay anyone) [A0108]; report the seller to BMET or the ministry (see "If an agency, broker or relative takes your money") or on the ministry hotline 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [A0056, A0048, A0025]; in an emergency call 999, the police emergency number [B0171].

### After you arrive

#### Residence card (iqama) and medical

You enter on a 3-month permit; within it the employer must get your iqama and work permit, and it pays for them and their renewals (what that costs the employer: see The iqama: the employer's bill; a 2024 report put it at about SR 11,000 a year) [B0108, B0113, B0116, B0131]. The iqama is issued after your medical test and once the employer has insured you [B0108]; you do the medical test and fingerprints, and it takes 1-3 weeks once papers are complete [B0144, B0143]. Without an iqama you become undocumented and can be detained and deported [B0132, B0135]. Check the name matches your passport; the employer corrects it at the Passports Directorate (Jawazat) [B0110]. Carry it always and show it to police on request [B0111]. Without it you can't open a bank account, use an ATM, send money or get a SIM [B0112].

*No iqama after 90 days:* complain to the labour ministry on 19911 (Sunday to Thursday, 8 am to 5 pm) [B0109, E0052]; then take the steps for unpaid wages (see If you aren't paid) and call the embassy labour wing on 8001000125 [E0024].

### Your rights at work

If a right is broken: see Where to complain.

#### Contract

Your contract must be recorded on Qiwa (After you arrive), with a copy for you and the employer [C0016]. It runs for a fixed term, usually 2 years; with no length written it counts as one year, and it renews for the same length if both sides carry on [C0095, C0026]. It should state your leave, whether it is paid and who pays the airfare [C0152].

**Probation**
Probation can last up to 180 days if the contract sets it; either side can end the contract during it without compensation or end-of-service pay [C0017, C0018]. Who pays the ticket home: see If you resign or are dismissed.

#### Pay and deductions

**How you are paid**
There is no minimum wage for foreign workers; the contract wage is what you can claim [C0092]. The employer must pay it on time, into your bank account on the contract date, through the Wage Protection Program, which records each payment [C0006, B0121]. Never sign that you were paid before the money arrives; it can block a later claim [C0025].

> **Need to know**
> - How can a worker see their recorded wage payments?

**What can't be taken from your pay**
Recruitment, iqama and work permit fees and renewals, job-title changes, exit and re-entry visas, transfer fees and your ticket home at the contract's end are the employer's and can't be taken from your pay [C0020, B0116]. Some brokers, small kafils (sponsors) and labour-supply companies still cut the iqama and health-insurance cost from wages [SM0033]. If the health-insurance premium is taken: see Housing, food and health care.

**What can be taken**
These rules come from the Labour Law (Royal Decree M/51 of 27 September 2005), in the English text on the labour ministry's website [ZX0007]. Amendments to the law took effect on 19 February 2025 [SM0002]. Without your written consent the employer may deduct only what the law lists, such as repayment of its loan (at most 10% of the wage), fines for your violations and damage you cause [ZZ0002, ZZ0004]. A fine for one violation is at most five days' wage, and fines taken in one month are at most five days' wage [ZZ0001]. For loss or damage you cause, by your own fault, to the employer's machinery or products, the employer may take what repair costs but at most five days' wage a month [ZY0005]. You can dispute the claim or the amount at the labour court within 15 work days of the employer telling you; if the court finds for you, the employer must return what it took wrongly within seven days [ZY0006]. All deductions together may not exceed half your wage, unless the labour court allows more [ZZ0003, ZY0007].

> **Need to know**
> - Did the February 2025 amendments change the deduction and fine limits (Articles 70, 91, 92 and 93)?

**Market pay**
What a new Bangladeshi recruit is really paid: see What the job pays. General market pay in Saudi Arabia [B0137, B0136, B0138, B0139, B0140]:

- helpers: SR 1,200-1,800;
- cleaners: SR 1,000-1,700;
- construction workers and masons: SR 1,600-3,000;
- drivers: SR 1,500-2,500;
- electricians: SR 1,800-3,500.

#### Hours, rest and leave

You work at most 8 hours a day and 48 a week (6 and 36 for Muslims in Ramadan), never over 12 a day in total [C0001, C0002, C0096], with a break of at least 30 minutes after 5 hours in a row [C0004]. From 15 June to 15 September, no private-sector work in direct sun from 12 noon to 3 pm; report a breach on 19911 or in the labour ministry's app (see Where to complain) [C0108, SG0026, C0157]. Heat stroke (confusion, slurred speech, fainting, seizures, very high temperature, hot dry or drenched skin) kills if treatment is delayed: get emergency care, move the person into shade, take off outer clothing and cool them with water [SG0027, SG0028]. Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness and heavy sweating are heat exhaustion: get out of the heat, drink and get checked at a clinic [SG0029, SG0044].

**Overtime**
Overtime pays your full hourly wage plus half the basic hourly wage per extra hour; public-holiday work counts as overtime [C0005, C0010].

**Rest and leave**
- *Weekly rest:* one day off a week, normally Friday, not swappable for money [C0003].
- *Annual leave:* paid, at least 21 days, 30 after 5 years with the same employer, on dates the employer sets with 30 days' notice; untaken days are paid when you leave [C0007, C0009, C0008].
- *Paid holidays:* 4 days at each Eid, Founding Day (22 February), National Day (23 September) [C0010].
- *Sick leave:* up to 120 days a year, 30 at full pay, 60 at three-quarters, 30 unpaid [C0011].
- *Other paid leave:* 3 days for a child's birth, 5 for your wedding, 5 for the death of a spouse, parent or child, 10 to 15 days once for Hajj after 2 years; 12 weeks' maternity leave [C0012, C0013].

#### Housing, food and health care

The employer gives housing and transport or allowances for them [C0014, B0122]; food only if the contract says so [C0099].

**Health insurance**
The employer must insure you, but the cover doesn't pay for serious illness, so go only if you are fit [C0015, C0101]. Check your cover with the Council of Health Insurance's Insurance Information Inquiry at [chi.gov.sa](https://www.chi.gov.sa/en/ServicesDirectory/Pages/Eservices-CheckInsurance.aspx) [C0110].

> **Need to know**

If you aren't insured or the premium is taken from your pay, complain at [care.chi.gov.sa](https://care.chi.gov.sa/) (guest or account) with your details, the employer's contact details, what happened and scanned proof; it is handled within 15 working days without naming you to the employer [C0159].

#### Your passport

No employer may keep your passport or iqama, not even "for safekeeping"; the fine is SR 5,000, though sponsors still commonly do it [C0022, C0091, B0119, B0114]. To complain, call 19911 [E0052] or use the labour ministry's Beneficiary Page ([selfservice.hrsd.gov.sa](https://selfservice.hrsd.gov.sa/node/)) [C0023, B0120]: [hrsd.gov.sa](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/ministry-services/services/client-page) > Contact Us > Beneficiary Page > Start the Service > log in with Nafath > Complaints > Add a Request; choose the sector and categories, fill in the fields, press Send [C0154, C0155]. ### Staying long-term

#### How long you can stay

You can stay as long as you have a contract and a valid iqama, which the employer renews and pays for [C0020]. Work visas are issued for ages 18 to 60 [C0100].

#### Pension

There is no Saudi pension for foreign workers: GOSI, the Saudi social insurance agency, covers them only for work injuries [C0109]; you get end-of-service pay instead (Leaving at the end of the job) [C0031].

A worker's 48-page, 5-year e-passport costs SR 125 (SR 190 urgent), a 10-year one SR 210 [SG0016].

#### Bringing your family

Most new Bangladeshi labourers can't bring family to live: family residence is open only to a worker whose permit is graded skilled, and elementary jobs such as cleaners and labourers, the jobs most new Bangladeshi labourers do, are graded basic (the full rules, including the salary needed: see Family residence) [C0130, D0114, SJ0007]. ### What can get you jailed or deported

- **Don't bring** narcotic drugs, alcohol, anything with pork, poppy seeds (khas khas), khat, gutkha, pan masala, non-Islamic religious literature, obscene material, amulets or anything linked to black magic, including on your phone [C0181, C0102, C0160, C0103].
- **Never carry a parcel you haven't packed and checked yourself,** even for family [C0104]; Saudi Arabia executes foreigners for drug smuggling [C0107].
- **Medicines:** your own only, at most 30 days' supply, in original packs, with a medical report and a prescription under 6 months old, both translated into Arabic [C0105, C0106].
- **Don't work for anyone but the employer on your permit, or in another job,** even for a few hours [C0021].
- **An expired iqama, or none:** see Residence card (iqama) and medical.
- **Being reported absent (huroob):** see "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)".
- **Adultery, or an "indecent act" in a mosque,** can bring death: in October 2026 a Bangladeshi man and woman were executed for an "indecent act" inside a mosque; the man had been held under the cybercrime law and the woman on an adultery allegation [G0007, G0001].
- **Cybercrime:** breaking the cybercrime law has brought Bangladeshis 6 months to 5 years in prison [G0003].

#### Driving

If you cause a crash that kills someone, the family can claim blood money (diya): a Riyadh court ordered an uninsured foreign driver to pay SR 300,000 per person killed and kept him in prison until he paid [SG0023]. Compulsory motor insurance pays victims up to SR 10 million, but the insurer can take that money back from a driver without the right class of licence or with an expired one [SG0020, SG0022, SG0021].

#### If your employer reports you absent (huroob)

Huroob ("running away") is a Qiwa report that you stopped coming to work: your status becomes "Absent from Work", the report can't be withdrawn, and you are removed from the company's records [C0063, C0064]. You can be reported absent in two ways:

- **The employer files it.** It can't while you have an active contract recorded on Qiwa, so make sure yours is recorded. It can file only once there is no active Qiwa contract (none was recorded, or it has ended), your permit or iqama has at least 60 days left, and you have been absent over 30 days in a contract year or 15 in a row [C0062, C0147].
- **Qiwa files it automatically.** When your Qiwa contract ends you get 60 days to move, leave or sign again; after that Qiwa marks you absent without any report from the employer [C0147, C0148, C0069].

These are the rules for company workers on Qiwa; for a domestic worker, see Work visa: domestic worker, If something goes wrong.

1. **Check your status often** on your Absher dashboard ("Absent from Work" or blocked services) [C0112] or, with your iqama number or passport number and nationality, on the ministry's [expatriate employee inquiry](https://es.hrsd.gov.sa/services/inquiry/nonsaudiempinquiry.aspx) [C0111]. The SMS alert may not arrive if your number isn't linked to the government system [C0065, C0088].
2. **Within 60 days, move or leave:** transfer on Qiwa if you have been in Saudi Arabia over 12 months, or issue yourself a final exit visa on Absher and go home [C0063, C0066, SM0027]. Do nothing and you stay "absent" and illegal, and leaving that way brings a 3-5 year re-entry ban [C0067, E0057]. A final exit visa needs [C0165, C0167, C0168]:

   - you in Saudi Arabia;
   - a valid iqama;
   - a passport valid at least 60 days;
   - traffic fines paid;
   - fingerprints registered;
   - no vehicle in your name.

#### If an agency, broker or relative takes your money

**If the agency takes your money and doesn't send you, sends you to a different job or overcharges, complain to BMET.** It is free and meant to take 30 working days: an inquiry committee hears both sides, BMET recommends, the ministry decides [A0056]. It recovers at most the official cost of the job, Tk 1.65 lakh, not what you paid [A0013, RC0047]. In practice many complaints stay unresolved for years [A0044]; in one study of BMET complaints only 39% were resolved, typically with Tk 20,000 to just over Tk 1 lakh [SL0007].

- proof such as receipts and the contract [A0056].

4. **Then** appeal to the Secretary, Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, secretary@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030444 (+880 2 41030444 from abroad), who has 1 month; then [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), 3 months [A0036].

**If the relative cheats you, get free legal advice on 16430 before going to the thana (police station)** [RC0027]. The law reaches the relative, but it can reach you too. Section 31 of the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 (OEMA) makes it a crime for any person, not only an agency, to take money with a false promise of a job abroad or to keep a migrant's passport or visa: up to 5 years and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [RC0009, RC0002, RC0001]. These offences are cognisable, non-bailable and can't be settled privately [RC0010]. But section 33 makes buying or selling a visa inside Bangladesh an offence for any person, 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and at least Tk 3 lakh, so a worker who paid for a visa can be accused too, and the seller will say so [RA0011, RA0008].

All of these routes can run at once:

- **Free legal help first.** OEMA gives people cheated in the name of migration a right to legal aid [RC0008]. Call 16430, free in office hours (from abroad +880 9123 16430), or apply at the District Legal Aid Office in the district judge court building, which also mediates fraud and money disputes free for people earning up to Tk 1 lakh a year [RC0027, RC0028, RC0029, RC0030]. OKUP, 01842773300, helps families file cases [A0046].
- **Name the people in Bangladesh who took the money.** When the relative lives in Saudi Arabia, his family at home usually received the payment, as with the Banshkhali man's wife and the Bishwanath family [RA0031, RC0019]. Complain against each person named on the bank and bKash records, not only the man abroad [RA0032].
- **Village salish (সালিস) or the Union Parishad chairman.** Most families go here first, and it rarely returns the money: in Natore a salish ordered the man to repay Tk 1 lakh to each family, a fraction of what they paid, and he ignored it; in Bishwanath the accused ignored three notices, and the chairman certified the victims to go to court [RC0053, RC0020]. BRAC's migration programme runs social arbitration instead, where volunteers and local leaders press the broker to repay [RC0022, RC0023].
- **Village court (গ্রাম আদালত)** at the Union Parishad, for money owed under an agreement, receipt or other document, up to Tk 3 lakh since 2024: apply on Form 1 with a Tk 20 fee [RC0024, RC0025, RC0026].
- **Complain to BMET or the ministry against the relative by name.** Section 41 lets anyone harmed complain against any person, not only an agency; the inquiry is meant to take 30 working days, and section 43 lets the government recover the money and pay it to you [RC0005, RC0006, RC0007]. Use the agency complaint route ("If an agency, broker or relative takes your money", items 1-5), giving the person's name, age, parents' names and address [A0037]. BMET settled 2,054 complaints and recovered nearly Tk 9.92 crore in 2025 [SL0002], but it pays on the official cost of the job, the legal maximum of Tk 1.65 lakh (see The legal maximum and what people really pay), rather than what you paid, and can't enforce its decision [A0013, RC0047, RC0049].
- **Police case (FIR) at the thana** under OEMA section 31, or under the human trafficking law if the person was sold, confined or forced to work [RC0045]. Expect resistance: in Natore the officer in charge refused nine complaints, saying the men hadn't gone abroad legally so the money wasn't the police's job [RC0054].
- **If the police won't register it, file a complaint case (সি.আর. মামলা, CR case) at the magistrate's court,** as the Bishwanath victims did at the Senior Judicial Magistrate's court in Sylhet [RC0021]. A first-class judicial magistrate tries OEMA offences, and the trial is meant to end within 4 months of charges, extendable by 2 [RC0003, RC0004]. You can sue for compensation in a civil court alongside [RC0011].

Relatives, friends and neighbours with connections are the people who most often cheat migrants, which is why victims avoid the courts: in a survey of 5,400 households in Kalihati, Tangail, 40% of migrant households had been defrauded and 19% had paid and never gone [RC0014, RC0015].

**If the relative lives in Saudi Arabia,** a case against him alone stalls: a Cumilla broker's case against the friend in Saudi Arabia who supplied his visas went nowhere, and in Natore the salish happened only after the man came home and the families went to his house [RA0037, RC0062].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### If you want to change employer

**You can move without your employer's consent:**

- when your Qiwa contract has ended [C0052];
- after 12 months' work since entering Saudi Arabia, with 90 days' notice (less if the employer agrees) and a valid contract on Qiwa [C0053, C0054, C0179];
- at any time if you haven't been paid for 3 months in a row, your work permit or iqama has expired, the employer has disappeared (travel, prison, death), or you report that the business is secretly run by a foreigner in a Saudi's name and you weren't part of it [C0055, C0056];
- if the employer keeps missing your friendly-settlement sessions [C0040].

Your employer is only notified and can't refuse [C0057]. These are the company-worker rules; a domestic worker's routes are different (see Work visa: domestic worker; see Domestic worker to another household; see Domestic worker to a company job) [D0031, E0153, E0133].

1. **Find a new employer yourself;** workers go to industrial areas and apply at every factory [C0059]. Check the company (see Step 1, Before you pay anyone). Pay no one for a job offer or transfer; the new employer pays the fees [C0020].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does any official service match foreign workers already in Saudi Arabia with employers?

2. **Accept its offer on Qiwa:** log in with Nafath, open the offer, check the job title and wage, accept or reject [C0151, C0051].
3. **Your employer is notified and your notice runs,** unless waived [C0051, C0153].
4. **The new employer completes the move;** once approved, the new work permit is issued at once, at no cost to you [C0051, C0172, C0020, C0153].

Keep working for your current employer until Qiwa shows the move is done, or you may never get a permit and stay illegal [C0150]. Never share your Absher or Qiwa password or SMS codes, even with your employer [C0087]. Some employers block moves by cancelling the iqama with a final exit visa or accusing the worker of theft or debt, and some new employers wrongly ask for the old employer's approval [C0086, C0060, C0061]; if blocked, file friendly settlement [C0072].

#### If you resign or are dismissed

You can resign before a fixed-term contract ends, and since 2025 the employer can't claim compensation from you [SM0004].

**Notice if you resign.** A resignation counts as accepted 30 days after you hand it in if the employer doesn't answer; the employer can delay it up to 60 days, and you can withdraw it within 7 days [C0027].

**Notice at the end of a contract.** To leave when the contract ends, give notice 30 days before a fixed-term contract ends, or 60 days on an open-ended one [C0097].

**If the employer dismisses you.** If the employer ends the contract without a valid reason, you are owed 15 days' wages per year of service on an open-ended contract or the wages for the rest of a fixed-term one, and never less than 2 months' wages [C0033].

**What you pay if you leave early.** The employer pays your ticket home, also when it ends the contract in a trial period; you pay it only if you are unfit for the work or quit without a valid reason [A0006, SM0005]. For what you are still owed, see Leaving at the end of the job, item 4.

**Leaving without notice**
You may stop work without notice and keep all your rights if the employer doesn't meet its duties (for example, doesn't pay), moves you to another job without consent, assaults or mistreats you, leaves a known serious danger unfixed, or lied about the job when you signed [C0029, C0094]. If you just stop coming to work, the employer can report you absent (see "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)") [C0062] and, after a written warning, dismiss you without end-of-service pay for over 30 days' absence in a contract year or 15 in a row [C0028].

#### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Check for a travel ban before you book.** An unpaid loan, credit card, rent or a cheque given as security can bring a court travel ban of up to 3 years per order [SG0039, SG0040]. A ban shows in Absher's travel ban enquiry, and the debt case on Najiz, the Justice Ministry's portal (log in with Nafath) [SG0037, SG0038].
2. **Give notice** (how much: see If you resign or are dismissed).
3. **Get your final exit visa yourself** on Absher or Qiwa, without the employer's consent; the employer is notified, and you bear the consequences of any breach of contract [C0128, C0127]. Conditions: see "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)", item 2 [C0165, C0167, C0168].
4. **Collect what you are owed** within 1 week of the contract ending, or 2 if you ended it [C0093]. End-of-service pay is half a month's last wage per year for the first 5 years and a full month per later year, part-years counted [C0031]; if you resign, you get a third after 2 to 5 years, two-thirds after 5 to 10, all after 10 [C0032]. Untaken leave is paid [C0008].
5. **Get your service certificate,** free, with your dates, job and last wage, and your papers back [C0030]. Or issue it yourself: qiwa.sa > Individual Account > Employment Certificates (Suggested Actions) > Apply Now > Service Certificate > select the job > Next Step > submit; it is approved at once for download [C0149, C0176].
6. **The employer pays your ticket home** [C0020]. If anything is unpaid, file friendly settlement within 12 months [C0035].

## Work visa: domestic worker

This visa is for work in a Saudi household, in one of 14 listed domestic jobs such as housemaid, private driver, nanny, cook or gardener [D0023]. Domestic workers fall outside the Saudi Labour Law; the Domestic Workers Regulation sets their rights [D0171, D0172, D0173]. Musaned, the labour ministry's domestic-worker platform, handles contracts and pay [D0157, D0045]. The employer pays recruitment (up to SR 11,750 to the recruitment office for a Bangladeshi worker), the iqama, work permit and transfer fees [SG0030, D0008]. A woman domestic worker pays nothing: her agency may take no migration cost from her [D0059], and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker, before or after she starts work [D0024]. No skill test is needed [D0161]. You can change employer without consent only in listed cases (see Work visa: domestic worker, If something goes wrong) [D0031]. Men going as house drivers: see House drivers.

### Your rights at work

- **Who arranges what:** the employer pays your medical test, iqama and renewal, and health insurance or treatment [D0029]. The Saudi recruitment office must tell you the work and pay, and house and support you if you leave or refuse the employer [D0025, D0026].
- **Contract on Musaned:** you get an SMS link, enter your residence number and the code sent to you, and accept within 5 days; it is free (Musaned 920002866) [D0047].
- **Insurance:** a contract made through a recruitment office on Musaned is insured for its first two years [G0024]. It covers:
  - permanent disability from an accident [G0025]
  - your unpaid wages if the employer dies or is disabled [G0025]
  - sending your body and belongings home if you die [G0026]
- **Probation:** probation lasts up to 90 days; either side may end the contract [D0003].
- **Hours:** you work at most 10 hours a day, with a half-hour break after every 5 hours, at least 8 hours' continuous rest, and one paid day off a week [D0004, D0005].
- **Pay:** you are paid the contract wage monthly, into a bank account or digital wallet in your name linked to Musaned through your mobile and iqama numbers [D0158, D0045, D0046]. At most a quarter can be deducted, only for damage you caused, an advance or an official order [D0013]. The Saudi-Bangladesh recruitment agreement set SR 800 a month [D0193].

  > **Need to know**

- **Housing, food and contact:** the employer must give you suitable housing and food or a food allowance [D0011]; the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy, and may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [D0010, D0009].
- **Leave:** you get up to 30 days' sick leave a year with a medical report, 15 on full pay, the rest on half [D0007]; after two years, if you renew, 30 days' paid leave and a return ticket [D0006].
- **Only your own job:** the employer may not make you work for others or in another job [D0012]. If you work for anyone else yourself: up to SR 2,000 fine, a work ban or both [D0020]; working on your own account, SR 10,000 and deportation [D0030].
- **At the end:** you get your wages and dues within a week, or two if you end the contract, and end-of-service pay of one month's wage per four years [D0015, D0014]. Ending the contract without a valid reason costs two months' wages to the other side unless the contract says otherwise [D0016]; if you leave without one, you pay your ticket home [D0019].

### If something goes wrong

**You can end the contract and keep your rights** if the employer breaks the contract, lied about the work, assaults you or behaves immorally towards you, gives you dangerous work or rents you out [D0017]. But don't just walk out (see If you are reported absent). Only your employer can issue your final exit visa [SK0021].

**If you are reported absent (huroob).** Leaving without telling anyone can bring an absence (huroob) report, which makes you illegal [D0063, D0037]. The employer can withdraw it within 15 days of filing, after which it is final [D0036]. The company-worker rules in "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)" (60 days to transfer, issuing your own final exit) are for company workers on Qiwa, not for you [SK0021]. If you must leave, go to the embassy or consulate (Complain in this order, item 2). Going home once the report is final: see Going home without valid papers.

**The labour ministry can move you to a new employer without your employer's consent** if [D0031, D0032, D0033, D0034, D0035, D0163, D0164]:

- wages are unpaid for three months (in a row or not)
- nobody collects you from the recruitment office or shelter within 15 days
- no iqama is issued or renewed within 30 days
- you are rented out, given dangerous work or ill-treated
- the employer misses two dispute sessions
- the employer ends the contract during probation

Any other move: see Domestic worker to another household and Domestic worker to a company job.

## Asylum

Saudi Arabia has no asylum system, hasn't signed the 1951 Refugee Convention and generally doesn't grant asylum [E0072, E0074]. People there illegally, including pilgrimage overstayers, are refused refugee status and deported [E0074]. The only protection is UNHCR, the UN refugee agency: people it recognises may stay temporarily, with valid identity papers, until resettled or returned; those without travel documents are sometimes detained indefinitely [E0073, E0075].

Apply by email to saprstaf@unhcr.org with [E0164]:

- your full name;
- family members' names;
- date of birth, sex and nationality;
- address and phone;
- any earlier UNHCR registration;
- the reason.

Toll-free line 009668001000281, Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday [E0163]. The Riyadh office, in the Diplomatic Quarter, is by appointment only [E0165].

## Going home without valid papers

Without a valid iqama, the way out is the Bangladesh Embassy's Special Exit Programme (SEP), if your sponsor's labour office is covered (see item 1 below) [E0027]. There is no time limit; workers have waited months, mostly without work [E0096].

Overstaying any visa can bring a fine up to SR 50,000, up to 6 months in prison and deportation [E0053]. The re-entry ban, from departure, is usually 3 to 5 years for overstaying or huroob (the employer's report that a worker is absent from work) and 3 to 10 years for working on a tourist or Umrah visa [E0057, E0058, E0059]. Leaving without a legitimate reason means paying your own ticket [E0136].

**To leave through SEP:**

1. **Check you qualify:**
   - You qualify if your sponsor's company is under the labour office of Riyadh, Al Qassim, Hail, Al Jouf, Northern Borders or the Eastern Province, and your iqama has expired, was never issued or carries a huroob report; house drivers and male domestic workers with an expired iqama also qualify [E0027].
   - Excluded: women domestic workers; anyone who didn't leave on an earlier exit visa; anyone with a police or court case or wanted (matlub), who must settle it with that authority first [E0028].
2. **You'll need** [E0029, E0030]:
   - your iqama number;
   - your original iqama;
   - a passport copy showing its validity.
3. **Apply online** at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html) ("Special Exit programme(SEP) Application Form"); no visit or call needed [E0026, E0031].
4. **Check progress** with your iqama number at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub/); when an appointment appears, print it and go to the embassy on that day with the documents [E0031].
5. **Get the exit visa:**
   - For a Riyadh company, the embassy gets labour office clearance; you then hand in your original iqama and passport copy, and the embassy sends them to Jawazat for the exit visa [E0029].
   - For a company under another labour office, or a huroob case with a sponsor outside Riyadh, the embassy gives you a letter to take to that labour office or Jawazat, where you collect the exit visa [E0029, E0030].

### Injured or ill

**To get home:** if you are injured or seriously ill in Saudi Arabia, you or your family apply to the Welfare Board with BMET clearance or membership and a passport copy; free, the Board gets the embassy to bring the worker home, sometimes into hospital, and the airport desk takes them to hospital by ambulance [E0014, E0040].

**Treatment in Saudi Arabia:** the employer must insure you, but the cover doesn't pay for serious illness (see Housing, food and health care) [C0015, C0101]. A company worker gets up to 120 days' sick leave a year, 30 at full pay, 60 at three-quarters, 30 unpaid [C0011]; a domestic worker up to 30 days with a medical report, 15 on full pay, the rest on half [D0007].

**Treatment of a work injury:** the rules in this paragraph come from the Social Insurance Law of 2024 (Royal Decree M/273 of 2 July 2024), in force from the day after it was issued [ZX0001, ZX0002]. That law does not apply to a worker who already had GOSI contribution periods from before it came into force and has not been paid compensation for them; the earlier Social Insurance Law (Royal Decree M/33 of 3/9/1421 Hijri, 2000) still applies to such a worker [ZX0003, ZX0004]. Under the 2024 law, GOSI covers a work injury only if it happened after your GOSI registration was complete, or within the time the rules give the employer to register you (or you, if the employer refuses or delays) [ZY0001]. If covered, GOSI gives medical care for as long as your condition needs it, at the hospitals and clinics it specifies [ZZ0005, ZZ0006, ZZ0007]. If the injury was reported in the way and within the time the rules set, then for each day you can't work, holidays included, from the day after the injury, GOSI pays a daily allowance of 100% of your daily registered wage in the month before the month of the injury, 75% while it pays for your treatment in any hospital, medical centre or the like [ZY0002, ZZ0008, ZZ0009, ZZ0010]. The allowance stops when you can work again, recover or die, or when permanent disability is established [ZY0003].

> **Need to know**
> - What are the time limits and method for registering a worker and for reporting a work injury to GOSI?
> - What medical care, daily allowance and lump sums does the earlier Social Insurance Law (2000) give a worker who was registered with GOSI before July 2024?

**GOSI work-injury compensation:** GOSI insures workers against work injury at the employer's cost; domestic workers only if its board decides so [E0100, E0101, E0160]. If a work injury leaves 50-99% or total permanent disability, GOSI pays a non-Saudi worker a lump sum; claim at a GOSI office (toll-free 199044) [E0161, E0162]. Under the 2024 law (for who it covers, see Treatment of a work injury), the monthly benefit behind the lump sum is 100% of your average registered wage over the three months before the month of the injury for total disability, or that times your disability percentage for 50% or more [E0158, ZZ0015, ZZ0012]. The lump sum is 84 times that monthly benefit for total disability and 60 times for partial disability [ZZ0011]. Below 50% disability you get a lump sum of 60 times the partial-disability monthly benefit if you were 45 or under on the day of the injury [ZZ0013, ZY0008]. If you were older, take off one month's benefit for each year over 45, counting part of a year of six months or more as a full year [ZZ0016, ZY0004]. Whatever the age, the under-50% lump sum is at least 36 times the assumed monthly benefit [ZZ0014].
