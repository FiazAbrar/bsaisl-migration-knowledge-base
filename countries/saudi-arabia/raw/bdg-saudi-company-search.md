## What is the official Saudi Arabia business registry?

Saudi Arabia’s primary business registry is the **Commercial Registration (CR)** system maintained by the **Ministry of Commerce (MoCI)**. Every company operating in the Kingdom must obtain a CR, which is the foundational proof of legal existence for Saudi companies.

The Ministry of Commerce has been named at mc.gov.sa and has operated the **Wathq** platform (<https://wathq.sa>) as its public-facing business verification service. <https://wathq.sa> currently rejects this hostname and does not serve a public search page. The www variant does not resolve. <https://developer.wathq.sa/en> is a developer portal that lists commercial-registration APIs behind login (title Homepage). No replacement official public CR search URL was confirmed.

<https://mc.gov.sa> did not present a valid security certificate when last checked, so MoCI portal screens are not described here.

Under the Commercial Register Law effective 3 April 2026, the Kingdom consolidated its previously fragmented regional CR system into a single national register. Every commercial register is now identified by a unified national number starting with the digit “7”, replacing the old regional-prefix CR number. The register no longer expires; it requires only an annual data confirmation instead of periodic renewal. Businesses still holding a pre-reform CR number have a five-year transition window to migrate to the unified format. That older 700-series identifier issued by the National Information Center is now folded into the same CR number. Confirm those statutory notes at source.

VAT registration is administered separately by the **Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA)** at [zatca.gov.sa](https://zatca.gov.sa), not by the Ministry of Commerce. ZATCA issues the VAT registration number and operates a Taxpayer Lookup, described below. Wathq and ZATCA are different systems: a Wathq search will not return VAT data, and a ZATCA search will not return full CR details.

The Commercial Registration system covers:

- Limited Liability Companies (Sharikat Dhat Mas’uliyya Mahduda, equivalent to LLC)
- Joint Stock Companies (Sharikat Musahama, equivalent to SA/PLC)
- General Partnerships and Limited Partnerships
- Professional Companies
- Sole Proprietorships
- Branches of Foreign Companies
- Special Purpose Entities

The **Saudi Exchange** at <https://www.saudiexchange.sa> is the listed-company homepage, titled Home - Saudi Exchange. Listed-company disclosure pages beyond that homepage were not walked; confirm at source. The address without the www prefix did not resolve.

The **Ministry of Investment (MISA)**, formerly SAGIA, at misa.gov.sa handles foreign investment licenses. A company with a foreign investment license appears in both the MISA register and the MoCI commercial register. MISA’s public homepage is not restated as a live page here; confirm at source.

## What you legally cannot do

The Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Royal Decree M/19 of 2021, effective September 2023) governs the processing of personal data of natural persons in Saudi Arabia. Director names, partner identity information, and beneficial ownership data extracted from Saudi company records constitute personal data under the PDPL when processed by a private entity. A lawful basis for processing is required, and certain transfers of personal data outside the Kingdom are subject to prior authorization from the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA).

Automated bulk extraction of data from Wathq or the MoCI portal is prohibited under the terms of service of both platforms when those platforms are reachable.

Saudi Arabia’s AML and CFT framework is governed by the Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree M/31 of 2003 and subsequent amendments) and the Counter-Terrorism Financing Law. The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) is the named supervisor. Saudi Arabia is a FATF member. This guide did not read Saudi Arabia’s entry on the published black and grey lists; confirm at source. This page does not prescribe enhanced due diligence.

Commercial data obtained for counterparty due diligence, KYC, or AML screening purposes falls within permitted commercial use under Saudi law, provided the processing entity has a documented lawful purpose and complies with PDPL obligations.

## Practical tips for foreign users

- **Use the CR number for lookups.** The Commercial Registration number is the stable unique identifier across Wathq, MoCI, MISA, ZATCA, and financial institution systems. Company names can vary between transliterations. Since the 3 April 2026 reform, new and migrated registrations carry a unified number starting with “7”; older registrations may still show a pre-reform CR number until they migrate within the five-year transition window. Whether both formats are accepted in Wathq could not be re-checked while <https://wathq.sa> does not serve a public search. ZATCA’s Taxpayer Lookup does accept CR National Number as one of its type options.
- **VAT number is a separate lookup from CR.** Wathq, when reachable, confirms company identity and status but does not return VAT data. VAT registration is administered by ZATCA. The Taxpayer Lookup form is described above. Cost and account rules for that lookup were not published as a fee table on the page; confirm at source.
- **Check MISA for foreign-invested companies.** Companies with foreign shareholders or foreign investment licenses appear in both the MoCI commercial register and the Ministry of Investment (MISA) database. MISA’s public homepage is not restated as a live page here; confirm at source.
- **Vision 2030 has changed the foreign ownership rules.** The Investment Law of 2024 (replacing the Foreign Investment Law of 2000) expanded sectors open to full foreign ownership and streamlined the investment licensing process. Older company records may show restricted foreign ownership structures that are no longer legally required for new formations. Read the current shareholding structure, not only the historic founding structure.
- **Annual accounts are not publicly searchable.** Unlike EU or common-law jurisdictions, Saudi Arabia does not provide public access to filed financial statements for private companies via the commercial register. Audited financials for private companies are available only with the company’s consent or through licensed credit bureaus. For listed companies, the Saudi Exchange homepage is titled Home - Saudi Exchange; disclosure pages beyond that homepage were not walked. Confirm at source.
- **Wathq is currently unreachable as a public search.** <https://wathq.sa> rejects this hostname. A saved Wathq result cannot be re-run there until the host serves a search page again.
- **Saudi fiscal year.** Many Saudi companies follow the Hijri calendar for their fiscal year, though the Gregorian calendar is also used. Accounting dates and CR filing dates may be expressed in Hijri (H) format.

## FAQ

### What is the Wathq platform and how does it differ from the MoCI portal?

Wathq (<https://wathq.sa>) is the Ministry of Commerce’s public-facing company verification service, designed for quick online status checks of Saudi companies. The host currently rejects this hostname, so those checks cannot be run there. The developer site at <https://developer.wathq.sa/en> is an API portal, not a substitute search form. The MoCI portal (mc.gov.sa) is the broader ministry administrative system; it did not present a valid security certificate when last checked.

### What does the CR number mean, and why does it start with “7” now?

Before 3 April 2026, the Commercial Registration (CR) number was a 10-digit identifier whose first three digits indicated the issuing MoCI regional office. Under the Commercial Register Law effective that date, Saudi Arabia consolidated its regional registers into one national system, and every commercial register is now identified by a unified national number starting with the digit “7”. Existing businesses have a five-year transition window to migrate from the old format. The full number, in either format, is the reliable unique identifier.

### How do I look up a Saudi company’s VAT number?

VAT registration is administered by ZATCA (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority), not by the Ministry of Commerce, so it does not show up in a Wathq search. ZATCA’s Taxpayer Lookup ([Taxpayer Lookup](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/eServices/Pages/TaxpayerLookup.aspx)) is an English form with type options VAT Account Number, VAT Certificate Number, and CR National Number. ZATCA’s on-page terms restrict this data away from marketing, promotion, data analysis, and selling. For volume use, a licensed commercial data supplier is the route used by institutions.

### Does Saudi Arabia have a beneficial ownership register?

Saudi Arabia’s AML regulations require financial institutions and Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs) to verify beneficial ownership as part of CDD obligations under SAMA and MoCI regulatory frameworks. There is no publicly searchable national UBO register equivalent to those in EU member states. Beneficial ownership data is held by companies, regulatory supervisors, and licensed bureaus.

### Is Saudi Arabia on the FATF grey list?

This guide did not read Saudi Arabia’s entry on the published lists. Confirm at [fatf-gafi.org](https://www.fatf-gafi.org). This page does not prescribe enhanced due diligence.

### Are financial statements publicly available for Saudi companies?

For companies listed on the Saudi Exchange, full financial statements have historically been disclosed there. The homepage at <https://www.saudiexchange.sa> is titled Home - Saudi Exchange; disclosure pages beyond that homepage were not walked. Confirm at source. For private companies, there is no public financial statement filing requirement comparable to EU member states. Private company financials are accessible only with company consent or through licensed credit bureaus under formal data-sharing agreements.

### What changed under the Saudi Investment Law 2024?

The Investment Law of 2024 replaced the Foreign Investment Law of 2000 and removed or streamlined licensing requirements for foreign investors in many sectors. Foreign companies can now establish wholly foreign-owned entities in a wider range of sectors without the historic requirement for a Saudi partner holding a majority stake. MISA at misa.gov.sa is the authority for foreign investment licensing under the new law. The changes are part of the Kingdom’s Vision 2030 economic diversification agenda.
