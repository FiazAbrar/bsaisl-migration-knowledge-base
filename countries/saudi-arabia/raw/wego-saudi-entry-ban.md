# Saudi Arabia Entry Ban 2026: What Can Trigger a Travel Ban to the Country

Last updated: Jul 1, 2026

Most bans hit travellers who broke specific rules, by [overstaying a visa](https://spa.gov.sa/en/N2572721), attempting Hajj without a permit, working on a tourist or Umrah visa, or absconding from a sponsor.

The good news is simple: if you understand what triggers a ban, you can avoid one, and if you already have one there are clear steps to check, fight or wait it out.

## Who gets a Saudi entry ban – main categories

Common categories of people who pick up an entry ban:

- Expatriates who overstayed an entry, visit or Umrah visa
- Workers reported as runaway (huroob) by a Saudi sponsor
- Pilgrims caught performing Hajj without an official permit
- Visitors caught working illegally on a tourist or Umrah visa
- Anyone deported after a criminal, labour or immigration case

The ban shows up at every Saudi port of entry, including the airline check-in counter, where agents are blocked from issuing a boarding pass to a banned traveller flying with [Saudia](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/airline/SV) or any other carrier.

The single biggest cause of new entry bans in 2026 is performing or attempting Hajj without a permit, after Saudi Arabia introduced harsher penalties this season.

### Hajj without a permit

The Saudi Ministry of Interior has confirmed [fines of up to SAR 20,000](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2316587) (around USD 5,330), plus deportation and a 10-year re-entry ban for any foreign national caught entering Makkah or the holy sites without an official Hajj permit.

The rules apply from the first day of Dhu Al Qi’dah to the 14th of Dhu Al Hijjah — so anyone visiting [hotels in Makkah](https://www.wego.com/hotels/in/makkah) during this window without a Hajj permit is at risk.

### Helping someone else perform illegal Hajj

Anyone who issues a visit visa, drives, hosts or accommodates an unauthorised pilgrim faces [fines of up to SAR 100,000](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-sets-sr100000-fines-for-illegal-hajj-pilgrims-1.500507495) (around USD 26,660).

Penalties double for repeat offenders, and vehicles used to transport unauthorised pilgrims can be confiscated.

### Overstaying any Saudi visa

Visitors who remain in the Kingdom after their visa expires face [fines of up to SAR 50,000](https://spa.gov.sa/en/N2572721), up to six months in prison, and deportation followed by a multi-year entry ban.

Hosts and sponsors who fail to report an overstayer can be fined the same SAR 50,000 amount and face up to six months in jail, even if the overstayer has already left.

### Working on a tourist, visit or Umrah visa

None of these visas permit any form of paid work — not freelance, not gig work, not short-term services. Enforcement actions include fines, jail, deportation and a multi-year entry ban, with the sponsor or host typically penalised too.

### Runaway (huroob) status

When a Saudi employer files a huroob (absconding) report, the worker is treated as having abandoned their job and faces a three to five-year entry ban on top of deportation.

Lifting a huroob ban is one of the hardest cases — usually only the original sponsor can withdraw the report, and they have no legal obligation to do so.

## How long the ban lasts and what fines come with it

Ban duration depends on the violation, and 10-year bans are now the standard penalty for unauthorised Hajj and serious immigration offences in 2026.

Here is what each major violation carries:

| Violation | Fine | Entry ban |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Hajj without a permit | Up to SAR 20,000 | 10 years |
| Facilitating illegal Hajj | Up to SAR 100,000 | 10 years (often + asset seizure) |
| Overstaying a visit or Umrah visa | SAR 15,000–50,000 | 3–5 years |
| Working on a tourist or Umrah visa | Up to SAR 50,000 + up to 6 months jail | 3–10 years |
| Runaway (huroob) | Varies (often labour court ruling) | 3–5 years |
| Serious or repeat criminal offences | Case-by-case | Up to permanent |

Almost every ban also comes with full deportation at the violator’s own expense, and the deported person’s biometrics are flagged across GCC immigration systems.

The clock on your ban starts the day you leave Saudi Arabia after deportation — not the day you committed the violation.

## How to check if you have a Saudi entry ban

The fastest way to check your status is through [Absher](https://www.absher.sa), the Saudi government’s official identity and services portal.

Steps to check your ban status on Absher:

- Visit absher.sa or download the Absher app on iOS or Android
- Log in with your National ID (citizens) or Iqama number (residents)
- Open *My Services*, then *Queries*
- Select *Travel Ban Inquiry* or *General Status Query*
- Submit and view the result

If the response reads *“no generalization report registered against you,”* you have no active ban. If a ban exists, the system shows the issuing authority — usually the Ministry of Interior, Jawazat, a court, or a labour office.

People who no longer have an Iqama cannot log into Absher directly. The workaround is to ask a Saudi-based contact (former employer, immigration lawyer, or licensed visa agent) to run the check on the Muqeem platform, which is the employer/establishment portal linked to Absher.

You can also contact the nearest Saudi embassy or consulate in your home country and request a ban verification before booking [flights to Jeddah](https://www.wego.com/flights-to/jed) or any other Saudi city.

## How to deal with a Saudi entry ban

The right path depends on why the ban was issued — some bans can be lifted, others simply have to run their term.

#### 1. Settle any outstanding fines or debts

Many overstay and labour-related bans can be lifted only after the underlying fine, salary claim or service fee is paid in full. Settlement is usually done through Absher, SADAD, or directly with the relevant ministry.

#### 2. Ask your former sponsor to file a lift request

For huroob and labour bans, the original Saudi employer is usually the only party who can submit a lift request to Jawazat or the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD).

Without their cooperation the ban runs its full duration, so it is worth approaching them early — ideally through a lawyer if the relationship ended badly.

#### 3. Submit an appeal through the Saudi embassy

If you believe the ban was issued in error, file a formal appeal at the nearest Saudi embassy or consulate. Be ready to provide your passport, old Iqama details, exit stamp, and any supporting evidence such as court documents, end-of-service papers or settlement receipts.

#### 4. Use the grievance system inside the Kingdom

Licensed lawyers in Saudi Arabia can lodge a grievance with the Board of Grievances (Diwan Al Mazalim) on your behalf. This is the formal route for wrongful or disproportionate administrative bans, particularly those tied to labour disputes.

#### 5. Wait it out

For 10-year Hajj bans and most criminal bans, there is no appeal route — the term has to expire before re-entry is possible. The clock starts on the date of your last exit from the Kingdom, not the date of the offence.

One useful policy change to know about: effective 16 January 2024, [Jawazat scrapped the automatic 3-year ban](https://www.saudigazette.com.sa/article/639701) for expatriates whose exit and re-entry visas expired while they were abroad.

If you missed your return because of a flight cancellation or family emergency, you may not need to wait at all to come back to [Riyadh](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/RUH), [Jeddah](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/JED), [Madinah](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/MED) or [Dammam](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/DMM).

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can I check a Saudi entry ban without an Iqama?

Not directly through Absher. You will need to ask a Saudi-based contact to run the check through the Muqeem platform, or contact the Saudi embassy in your home country for a verification.

Does the 10-year Hajj ban apply if I only visited Makkah, not performed Hajj?

Yes. Entering Makkah or the holy sites without a valid Hajj permit during the restricted period (1 Dhu Al Qi’dah to 14 Dhu Al Hijjah) is enough to trigger the ban — even if you never reached the rituals.

Will paying the fine automatically lift my entry ban?

Usually not. Paying the fine is often a prerequisite to lifting the ban, but the ban itself must be formally removed by Jawazat or the issuing authority before you can return.

Are first-time overstayers treated more leniently?

Slightly. A short first-time overstay typically incurs a SAR 15,000 fine and deportation, while repeat or aggravated overstays escalate to SAR 25,000–50,000 and jail time of up to six months — but all of them come with an entry ban.

Can I transit through Saudi airports if I have an entry ban?

Usually yes. An entry ban applies to entering Saudi territory, so sterile international transit at airports like [Jeddah](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/JED) and [Riyadh](https://www.wego.com/flight-tracking/RUH) usually remains possible — but always confirm with your airline before booking.
