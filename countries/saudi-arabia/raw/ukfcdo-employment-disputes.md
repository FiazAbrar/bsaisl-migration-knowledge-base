## Saudi labour law 

The Saudi Labour Law (Royal Decree No. M/51 of 2005, as amended) and its Executive Regulations govern employment in Saudi Arabia. This law covers:

- employment contract requirements
- working hours and overtime
- annual and sick leave entitlements
- end-of-service benefits
- termination procedures and notice periods
- wage protection
- dispute resolution

## Getting help with an employment dispute

If you’re experiencing problems with your employer in Saudi Arabia, you can get advice from the [Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD)](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/labor-culture). MHRSD handles private-sector labour disputes.

## Submitting an employment complaint

You can file an employment complaint:

- online through the [Qiwa labour market platform](https://www.qiwa.sa/) (website only available in Saudi Arabia)
- through the [MHRSD website](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en) 
- by calling the unified number: 19911

After you submit a complaint, MHRSD will try to find a solution to the dispute (mediation).

If mediation fails, MHRSD will refer the case electronically to the Labour Court. The court conducts hearings online through the [Najiz online legal services platform (in Arabic)](https://www.najiz.sa/) and handles issues such as:

- payment of unpaid wages
- end-of-service benefits
- compensation for unfair dismissal
- contract enforcement
- damages for breach of contract

You do not have to have legal representation, but you should consider getting a lawyer for a complex case. [Find a lawyer in Saudi Arabia](https://find-a-professional-service-abroad.service.csd.fcdo.gov.uk/find/lawyers?country=saudi%20arabia).

You must raise a labour dispute with the MHRSD first before referral to the Labour Court.

### If you’re a domestic worker 

If you’re a domestic worker, you should submit an employment complaint through the [Musaned domestic workers employment platform (in Arabic)](https://www.musaned.com.sa/). Domestic workers include housemaids, drivers and nannies.

There are separate laws for domestic workers. Domestic workers are not covered by all the provisions of the general Labour Law.

### Visas and job changes during disputes

The Saudi authorities may prevent you from travelling or moving to a new job until a labour case is resolved.

Once your case is resolved, if you’re an eligible foreign private sector worker, you can normally request visas and job transfers online through the Qiwa and Absher platforms. This includes:

- final exit visas
- exit/re-entry visas
- transfer employment (job mobility)

Eligibility for these services varies, but you must generally:

- hold a valid resident ID (Iqama)
- comply with the terms of your employment contract, including any notice period

The British Embassy in Riyadh cannot issue exit visas or override Saudi immigration systems.

### Employers holding workers’ passports

Employers are not allowed to hold an employee’s passport without the passport holder’s written consent. 

If your passport has been withheld, you should report this to the MHRSD and the local police.

## Enforcing Labour Court judgments 

If the Labour Court rules in your favour, it will issue the judgment electronically. All parties have 30 days from the date shown on the court judgment to appeal.

If your employer does not comply with the court’s judgement, you can apply to have it enforced through the [Najiz online legal services platform (in Arabic)](https://www.najiz.sa/).

Enforcement courts can freeze employer bank accounts or take other legal measures.

## Complaining about human rights violations 

If you think your human rights have been violated, you can complain to the [National Society for Human Rights (NSHR)](https://nshr.org.sa/en/homev3/). This can include labour exploitation. 

## Getting help from the British embassy

The [British Embassy in Riyadh](https://www.gov.uk/world/organisations/british-embassy-riyadh) can:

- give you general information about Saudi procedures
- raise concerns about your welfare where appropriate
- give you consular assistance if you’re arrested or detained

The embassy cannot: 

- give you legal advice
- represent you in court
- pay your legal fees or compensation
- issue you an exit visa or residency permit
- override Saudi judicial decisions

Read about [UK help and services in Saudi Arabia](https://www.gov.uk/world/saudi-arabia).
