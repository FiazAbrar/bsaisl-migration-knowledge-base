                  NCP leader Hasnat Abdullah accuses BMET of harassing migrants                

Staff Correspondent

                      17 September, 2026, 19:41                    

                        Print

National Citizen Party leader Hasnat Abdullah has accused the manpower bureau of harassing aspirant migrant workers by suspending emigration clearance for a week.

‘We have been receiving complaints that the Bureau of Manpower Employment and Training card allocation has been suspended for the past week,’ he said in a verified Facebook post today.

He said people are not getting clearance cards, leading to announcements of protests. However, BMET cards are secretly being issued through back channels for higher sums of money.

‘Even under the new government, the competition to take bribes continues,’ the first-time Member of Parliament said.

There are over 10 million Bangladesh migrants working abroad and sending home about $30 billion a year, making it the lifeline of Bangladesh’s economy.

Before migrating abroad with a work visa, they need to have BMET emigration clearance.

July Uprising leader Hasnat Abdullah said for migrant workers, one essential task is creating a registration-based database.

He suggested that the database should be formed by integrating data from both NID and passport servers.

‘We call for the BMET to stop the continuous harassment of migrants. The BMET must be transformed from a gatekeeper into a digital registrar. The migrant registration must shift away from physical plastic cards to a database-driven system,’ Hasnat Abdullah said.

According to World Bank estimates, Bangladesh has among the highest migration costs in South Asia.

According to a survey by the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, across key corridors like Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Qatar, UAE and Kuwait, the average recruitment fee of each Bangladeshi migrant worker is T4.17 lakh, and it takes 17.6 months for them to recoup the money.

‘In reality, after accounting for bribes, broker syndicates, and airport commissions, costs exceed five lakh taka,’ Hasnat Abdullah said.

Consequently, the plight of Bangladeshi migrant workers remains dire, he added.

‘To provide harassment-free services to migrants, the harassing registration system driven by faulty software must be radically reformed. The migrants must be freed from all forms of harassment,’ he added.
