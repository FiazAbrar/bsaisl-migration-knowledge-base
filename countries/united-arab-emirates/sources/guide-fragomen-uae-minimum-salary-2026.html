The following countries have announced changes in salary requirements:

Albania

Updated December 24, 2025

Effective January 2026, the national minimum wage increased to ALL 50,000 gross per month – up from ALL 40,000.

Other relevant considerations:

  • Market salary. Foreign workers must receive the market salary rate for the proposed minimum wage level.
  • Compliance. The new requirements will apply to current permit holders, as well as all new, pending or renewal applications.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances may only be included in the minimum salary calculation if they are specified in the employment contract, guaranteed and fixed, and are not paid in kind. 
  • Currency. Employers must ensure that the salary threshold in ALL is met, regardless of payroll location and/or exchange rate fluctuations. 
  • Cost of living. The foreign national’s salary must be sufficient to cover the cost of living of the principal and any accompanying dependents. Applicants must submit bank statements with their residence permit application to demonstrate access to sufficient funds. 
     

Austria

Updated January 5, 2026 

Work permit minimum salary levels. Effective January 1, 2026:

  • Red-White-Red Card (key employee): EUR 3,465 gross per month, up from 3,225 gross per month, unless the minimum salary level under any applicable collective bargaining agreement requires a higher amount.    
  • EU Blue Card: 55,678 gross per year, unless the minimum salary level under any applicable collective bargaining agreement requires a higher amount. 
  • Super key employee: EUR 8,316 gross per month, up from EUR 7,740 
  • LSD-BG (Austrian Wage and Social Dumping Combating Act) exemption: EUR 8,316 gross per month, up from EUR 7,740 

Work authorization-exempt status minimum salary levels. As of January 2026, the minimum salary necessary for either international company executives or internationally renowned researchers to be granted work authorization-exempt status is EUR 8,316 gross per month – up from EUR 7,740. Salaries are counted over 14 payments (12 monthly payments plus two annual special payments). These candidates require a residence permit, but no separate work permit.     

Collective bargaining agreements. In the following situations, minimum salaries are not determined by the above figures, but rather by the applicable collective bargaining agreement for the role in question:

  • Short-Term Work Permit;
  • Short-Term Secondment Permit; EU Intracompany Transferee (ICT) Permit;
  • Red-White-Red Card for professionals in shortage occupations; and
  • Red-White-Red Card holders/applicants who are graduates from Austrian universities.

Benefits and allowances​ typically cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation under collective bargaining agreements.

Other relevant considerations: 

  • Existing employees. Employers of foreign nationals currently under a Red-White-Red Card do not need to increase a foreign national’s salary to comply with any new salary level for the card. However, they do have to account for any salary increases under any relevant collective bargaining arrangement.
  • 14 payments per year. Salaries must be paid in 14 installments per year.   
  • Financial capacity:  
      • Single individuals. Must have at least net EUR 1,308.39 per month, after all deductions.  
      • Dependents. If dependents are accompanying the third-country national, the income must be sufficient to provide for the family. Rent payments are to be deducted when considering whether a candidate meets such thresholds. For married partners it is EUR 2.064,12. For each child, an additional net amount of EUR 201.88 is required. 
  • Posted worker notification requirements. The following employees, posted by an employer based in the EU/EEA/Switzerland, are exempt from posted worker notification requirements (and certain document keeping requirements):  
      • Employees with a gross monthly salary of at least EUR 8,316 (prior and during the posting), up from EUR 7,740.   
  • Currency. Employers must guarantee the salary in EUR regardless of payroll location and/or exchange rate fluctuations. 

Australia

Updated July 9, 2026   

The Department of Home Affairs has confirmed that the following income thresholds apply to nomination applications lodged on or after July 1, 2026: 

  • Core Skills Income Threshold: AUD 79,423 (up from AUD 76,515)  
  • Specialist Skills Income Threshold: AUD 146,576 (up from AUD 141,210)  
  • The Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT), applicable to regional sponsored visa programs (subclass 494 and subclass 187): AUD 79,423 (up from AUD 76,515) 

Employers must continue to ensure sponsored employees are paid at least the applicable income threshold and the annual market salary rate for the nominated occupation. Where guaranteed annual earnings are below AUD 250,000, employers must also demonstrate that sponsored workers receive terms and conditions no less favorable than those provided to an equivalent Australian worker performing the same role in the same location. 

Additionally, effective July 1, 2026: 

  • The Fair Work High Income Threshold has increased from to AUD 190,100 (up from AUD 183,100). 
  • The National Minimum Wage has increased to AUD 1,004.90 per week or AUD 26.44 per hour (up from AUD 948.10 per week or AUD 24.95), from the first full pay period commencing on or after July 1, 2026. 
  • Minimum award wages have increased by 4.75%.  

 The Fair Work High Income Threshold remains relevant for certain employer-sponsored visa exemptions, including specific age-related exemptions under the Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) and Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) (subclass 494) programs.

Bangladesh

Bangladesh’s minimum salary level requirements vary and are based on nationality and job designation. They range from USD 1200 or BDT equivalent (up from USD 650) for skilled workers to USD 3,000 or BDT equivalent (up from USD 2,000) for investors and executives. The E Visa and Work Permit category is subject to these minimum salary requirements. 

Belarus

Updated December 3, 2025

The minimum salary levels for Special Work Permit and Temporary Residence Permit applications are:  

    • Highly qualified employees: Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum salary level for ‘highly qualified employees’ is BYN 4,290 per month (five times the minimum wage of BYN 858 per month) – up from BYN 3,630.  
    • Other applicants: Effective January 1, 2026, the migration authorities recommend that the minimum salary level for other foreign nationals be at least BYN 2,250 per month – up from BYN 2,100. 
    • Dependents: Sponsors of dependents (including dependent spouses and children) must also earn a minimum subsistence amount for each dependent. These amounts – which vary depending on the type of dependent, among other factors – are revised quarterly. Affected individuals should contact their immigration professional for further details.

Other relevant considerations:

  • Compliance. The new amounts apply to pending and new applications.
  • Local payroll and currency. Foreign workers must be on Belarussian payroll and employers pay the salary in BYN. The amount of the salary may be set in a foreign currency at the rate of the service bank on the day of payment.
  • Social security. The employer contributes an additional 34% of the monthly salary as mandatory social security insurance payments. 1% of the monthly salary is withheld from the foreign national as a mandatory social security insurance payment. The employer also pays a mandatory accident insurance contribution of approximately 6% of the salary.
  • Income tax. 13% of the salary is withheld from the foreign national as personal income tax (in some cases, the payer is eligible for tax deductions).
  • General provisions. The above is based on general provisions within the legislation. In some cases, different features may be established by specific legislation.

Other relevant considerations include:

  • New, renewal and pending applications. This includes applications pending from before this date. Employers must also notify authorities about salary increases to such pending applications. Applications that do not meet the minimum salary will be rejected.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and cost allowances generally cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation. In exceptional cases, allowances can be included provided they are: (1) remuneration in return for work performed within the framework of the employment agreement; (2) guaranteed and fixed; (3) taxable; and (4) indicated in the employee’s pay slip.

Bulgaria

Canada

Updated July 21, 2026 

Effective July 17, 2026, Canada increased the Temporary Foreign Worker Program wage thresholds by an average of just over 4%. The updated thresholds determine whether a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) application must be submitted under the High-Wage or Low-Wage stream, based on the offered wage relative to the applicable provincial or territorial threshold. Applications that no longer meet the revised High-Wage threshold will be assessed under the Low-Wage stream, which is subject to additional program requirements. Employers should ensure that offered wages reflect market rates for comparable Canadian workers, as increasing wages solely to qualify under the High-Wage stream may result in a negative LMIA decision. 

Colombia

Updated January 2, 2026

Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum wage in Colombia increased to COP 1,750,905 per month (approximately USD 460), a 23% percent increase from the 2025 minimum wage. The increase does not directly affect the hiring of foreign nationals from an immigration perspective; however, employers sponsoring work visas should ensure that even with the increase in the minimum wage, their financial statements for the past three, four or six months (depending on the visa type) still show a monthly average balance of at least 100 times the minimum wage. For labor purposes, a foreign national’s salary may not be less than the monthly minimum wage.

Denmark

Updated October 30, 2026

Pay limit applications. The gross minimum salary level for the following categories is: 

  • General Pay Limit Scheme applications (including those filed under the Fast-Track Scheme): Effective January 1, 2026, DKK 552,000 per year, up from DKK 514,000.
  • Supplementary Pay Limit Scheme applications: Effective January 1, 2026, DKK 446,000 per year, up from DKK 415,000.  
  • Country-Limited, Registered Employer Pay Limit Scheme: Effective January 1, 2027, companies which have obtained the relevant certificate from SIRI will be able to employ full-time foreign nationals from eligible countries for a minimum salary of DKK 322,000. The eligible countries are Albania, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, India, Japan, Malaysia, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Singapore, Ukraine, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

Other relevant considerations:

  • Currency. All amounts must be reflected in DKK in the employment contract, and if the salary is stated in a foreign currency, an addendum stating the salary in DKK must be submitted.
  • Initial applications. Employers of foreign nationals seeking to obtain a Work Permit after these minimum salary level changes must increase the foreign national’s salary to comply with the new rule. Immigration applications that do not meet the minimum salary will be refused.
  • Pending applications. These minimum salary level changes do not affect pending applications submitted prior to the change.
  • Renewal applications. The minimum salary level for renewal applications remains the same as the minimum salary for the year the original application was submitted.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation. Only basic salary, paid holiday allowance and payments to the labor market pension scheme can be included in the minimum salary calculation.
  • DISCO code: Initial and renewal applications must have a salary and Danish International Standard Classification of Occupations (DISCO) code that corresponds to the applicable Danish Income Statistic.
  • Payment in a Danish bank account. The foreign national’s full salary must be paid into a Danish bank account when working in Denmark for more than six months. For Fast-Track cases, the salary does not need to be paid out to a Danish bank account.

National minimum wage. Effective January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage will increase to EUR 14.15 per hour, up from EUR 13.50.     

Other minimum salary levels. The Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment (DETE) has published a Roadmap for Minimum Annual Remuneration (MAR) Salary Thresholds and confirmed increases to Minimum Annual Remuneration (MAR) Thresholds for Employment Permits, effective March 2026. Further phased increases are envisaged through 2030. 

    • General Employment Permit. Effective March 1, 2026, minimum salary levels for this permit are as follows: 
      • General. EUR 36,605, up from the current EUR 34,000.  
      • Healthcare assistants and home carers special category. EUR 32,691, up from the current EUR 30,000.  
      • Horticultural workers and meat processors special category. EUR 32,691, up from the current EUR 30,000. 
      • Recent graduates. EUR 34,009.  
    • Atypical Working Scheme Permission. This will increase to EUR 36,605 from March 1, 2026, to match the General Employment permit threshold.
    • Critical Skills Employment Permit. Effective March 1, 2026, minimum salary levels for this permit are as follows: 
      • Candidates with a relevant degree and a role in the critical skills list. EUR 40,904, up from the current EUR 38,000. 
      • Individuals without a relevant degree and/or with a role not in the critical skills list. EUR 68,911, up from the current EUR 64,000. 
      • Recent graduates. EUR 36,848.  
    • Intra-Company Transfer Employment Permit. Effective March 1, 2026, minimum salary levels for this permit are as follows: 
      • Trainee transferees. EUR 36,605, up from the current EUR 34,000; and  
      • All other transferee categories. EUR 49,523, up from the current EUR 46,000. 
    • Exemptions to new MAR thresholds: Certain roles, subject to the public service pay agreement (i.e. individuals employed by certain State bodies) are to be exempt from the March 1, 2026 increases noted above and will be subject instead to the public service pay agreement pay scales. This will also apply to community and voluntary organizations whose pay scales are linked to the public sector pay deal.
    • Students. As of June 30, 2025:  
      • Non-visa-nationals need to demonstrate access to EUR 10,000 if studying a course that is one year in duration (up from EUR 7,000); or EUR 833 per month, if studying for six to eight months (up from EUR 585).  
      • Visa nationals need to demonstrate access to EUR 10,000 if studying for one year, and a further EUR 10,000 for each subsequent year of study; or EUR 833 per month, if studying for six to eight months.  

Other relevant considerations:  

  • Currency. Employers must guarantee the salary in EUR regardless of payroll location and/or exchange rate fluctuations.  
  • Working week. The above Employment permit MAR thresholds are based on a 39 hour week. For longer weeks, the figures will need to increase proportionally. For shorter working weeks, the minimum MAR that applies to 39-hour weeks will still need to be met.  
  • Compliance. New applications, as well as renewals, must meet the new minimum salary level threshold, otherwise they will be refused. 
  • Benefits and allowances. For ICT permits, the basic salary must meet the Irish national minimum wage before benefits and allowances may be considered.   
  • Minimum wage exemptions. Workers under the age of 18 are subject to lower minimum wage levels, according to a progressive downward scale. Workers who are employed by a close relative or in a statutory apprenticeship are, in certain situations, not entitled to receive the minimum wage.

Local Employees

Effective July 1, 2026, sponsoring companies will only be able to include local employees earning at least SGD 1,800 (up from SGD 1,600) in calculating their foreign worker quota allocation, per a rule called a ‘local qualifying salary’ that requires employers to properly compensate local workers in order to qualify to hire foreign workers. 

Slovakia

Updated December 30, 2025

National minimum wage:

  • Effective January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage will increase to EUR 915 gross per month, up from the current EUR 816.
  • The increase generally affects all local-hired employees, including EU, non-EU and Slovakian nationals, but does not apply to EU ICT Permit or EU Blue Cards.

EU ICT Permit. Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum salary for this permit will increase to EUR 915 gross per month, up from the current EUR 816.  

EU Blue Card. Effective July 15, 2024, applicants must receive a minimum salary that is at least 1.2 times the average gross annual salary for the year prior to the application year.

National Visa in the Interest of the Slovak Republic permit. Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum salary for this permit will increase to EUR 3,048 gross per month, up from EUR 2,860. 

Other relevant considerations:

  • Compliance. In case of threshold changes, the new amounts apply to all new, renewal, and pending applications. Except for EU Blue Card holders, the changes also apply to current permit holders.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation.
  • Sufficient means. All foreign nationals must have sufficient financial means of support, i.e. must have funds available at the level of the minimum living allowance per month (amounts indexed annually in July; threshold varies based on the number of dependents). ​

Slovenia

Updated March 27, 2026 

  • National Minimum Wage. Effective January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage increased to EUR 1,482 gross per month, up from the previous EUR 1,277.72. 
  • Single Permit applications. A Single Permit applicant must earn either the national minimum wage or a salary comparable to a local worker in the same position (per the applicable collective bargaining agreement), whichever is higher. 
      • Subsistence income. From April 1, 2026, Single Permit applicants will need to prove a subsistence income for themselves of at least EUR 507.43 per month for the duration of their stay in Slovenia (up from EUR 494.09).  
      • Dependents. From April 1, 2026, Single Permit applicants with accompanying dependents will need to have access to a further monthly income of EUR 507.43 per dependent (up from EUR 494.09). The exact amount may vary depending on the number of dependents, whether the person is a single parent, or whether the spouse is employed, among other factors.  
  • EU Blue Card. On July 21, 2025, the minimum salary threshold for EU Blue Cards was lowered to the average gross yearly salary; down from the previous requirement of 1.5 times the average gross yearly salary.  

Other relevant considerations: 

  • Compliance. If the threshold changes, the new thresholds apply to current permit holders, pending and initial applications.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation.
  • Currency. Employers must guarantee compliance with the salary level in EUR regardless of payroll location. For local hires, employers must pay the salary in EUR.

Spain

Updated July 8, 2026 

  • Minimum wage. Effective January 1, 2026, EUR 17,094 per year, up from EUR 16,576.  
  • General minimum salary. The annual minimum salary for each industry is set out in the applicable Collective Bargaining Agreement but, in any event, cannot be lower than the annual minimum wage. Recent foreign graduates and low-skilled foreign workers will often be subject to this general minimum salary.
  • EU Intra-corporate Transfer Permit/National Intra-corporate Transfer Permit. The annual minimum salary for each industry is set out in the applicable Collective Bargaining Agreement but, in any event, cannot be lower than the annual minimum wage. 
  • Local Hire Permit/Highly Qualified Professionals. As of June 22, 2026, the minimum salary level for this permit category is EUR 41,356.36 gross per year. The previous minimum salary distinction between managers/directors (EUR 54,142) and specialists (EUR 41,356.36) has been removed, with all applicant types now subject to the lower EUR 41,356.36. 
  • EU Blue Card Permit. Effective June 1, 2026, the minimum salary level for this permit category is:  
    • General: EUR 41,356.36 gross per year, up from EUR 39,269.92. 
    • Foreign nationals with a university degree earned less than three years prior to filling an application: EUR 33,085.09 gross per year, up from EUR 31,415.90. 
    • Occupations experiencing labor shortages have variable minimum salary levels as determined by the government from time to time. 

Other relevant considerations: 

    • Recommended amounts. To reduce the risk of an application being rejected on minimum salary grounds, paying marginally above the minimum salary is recommended. 

Sweden

Updated July 15, 2026  

National Work Permit: Effective June 16, 2026, SEK 34,470 gross per month (90 percent of the median salary) – up from SEK 33,390.  

    • Exceptions. Certain work permit applicants are subject to  a lower minimum salary of SEK 28,725 gross per month (75% of the national median salary) – up from SEK 27,825. This includes:  
      • 27 shortage occupation classifications (representing a broad occupation category rather than a single job title); and
      • specific groups, including employees at startup companies (including in tech or life sciences), individuals with qualifications in certain licensed professions (e.g. doctors, nurses, dentists, or pharmacists), recent graduates, and temporary protection beneficiaries (Ukraine).
    • Transitional measures. National Work Permit holders who received their permit before June 1, 2026 and who apply for an extension between June 1 and December 1, 2026, are subject to the previous salary level of 80% of the median salary (which as of June 16, 2026 is SEK 30,640 gross per month).
    • Other permits. This minimum salary level does not affect EU Blue Card and EU Intra-Company Transferee Permit holders or seasonal workers, or individuals holding temporary residence status in any other EU Member State. They are subject to different thresholds.  

EU Blue Card: Effective July 15, 2026, the minimum salary level for an EU Blue Card is SEK 53,625 per month (up from SEK 52,000). 

Residence Permit for Visiting Researcher: Effective August 28, 2023 (and still current as of June 23, 2025), the minimum salary level for a Visiting Researcher is SEK 9,700 per month.

Intra-Company Transferee (ICT) Permit: The minimum salary level for EU ICT Permit must be the equivalent of the applicable collective bargaining agreement or the level customary for the profession or industry.

Other relevant considerations:

  • Existing employees and renewals. Employers of foreign nationals currently under a work permit do not need to increase foreign nationals’ salaries before filing an extension application if their permits expire between June 1 and December 1.
  • Initial applications. Minimum salaries must be met according to the new threshold if the start date is after June 1.
  • Pending applications. Applications that are pending as of the effective date stated above and that are below any new minimum salary level will not be processed.
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation.
  • Maintenance/dependents. Foreign workers must have an income sufficient to cover the cost of housing and living expenses for themselves and their dependents.  
  • Currency. Salary thresholds must be met in SEK regardless of payroll location.

Thailand

Updated June 27, 2025

Starting October 1, 2025, for companies with BOI-promoted certificates issued on or after June 5, 2025; and January 1, 2026 for companies with certificates issued before June 5, 2025, for companies promoted by the Board of Investment under the new regulations, the following minimum salary thresholds will apply: 

  • Executives. THB 150,000 gross per month (newly enforced minimum). 
  • Management staff (without relevant educational qualifications). THB 75,000 gross per month. 
  • Management staff (with relevant educational qualifications). THB 50,000 gross per month. 
  • Operational staff. THB 50,000 gross per month. 

Turkiye

Updated December 26, 2025
 
Minimum wage. Effective January 1, 2026, Türkiye’s minimum wage increased to TRY 33,030 gross per month, up from TRY 26,005.50. 
 
Other minimum salary levels. Effective January 1, 2026: 
    • High-level managers and pilots. TRY 165,150 gross per month (five times the minimum wage), up from TRY 130,027.50.  
    • Engineers and architects. TRY 132,120 gross per month (four times the minimum wage), up from TRY 104,022.  
    • Managers. TRY 99,090 gross per month (three times the minimum wage), up from TRY 78,016.50.  
    • Employees with expertise and teachers. TRY 66,060 gross per month (two times the minimum wage), up from TRY 52,011.  
    • Household workers and others (i.e. sales officers, low-level marketing officers). TRY 33,030 gross per month (minimum wage), up from TRY 26,005.50.

Other relevant considerations include:

  • Initial and renewal applications. Employers of foreign nationals who submitted an initial or renewal work permit application on or after the official date of the minimum wage increase must increase foreign nationals’ salaries to comply with the new rule. Aside from household or tourism workers, work permit applications stating a salary less than three times the minimum wage will face increased scrutiny.
  • Pending applications. Employers of foreign nationals with pending work permit applications as the official date of the minimum wage increase may need to increase foreign nationals’ salaries upon Ministry of Labor’s request.
  • Benefits and allowances. As before, benefits and allowances may not be included in the minimum salary calculation.
  • Safe margin. As before, Fragomen recommends slightly rounding the salary up, to maintain a safe margin above the threshold.
  • Permit duration. As before, employers are required to guarantee the salary throughout the validity of the work permit.

Ukraine

Updated December 17, 2025

Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum salary level for foreign nationals will increase to UAH 8,647 gross per month (up from the current UAH 8,000). This minimum salary only applies to local hire work permits and not to work permits based on a service agreement between a foreign employer and a Ukrainian entity.

  • Compliance. The new salary levels apply to current permit holders, pending, and new applications.  
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances cannot be included in the minimum salary calculation. 
  • Currency. Employers are required to guarantee the salary in UAH regardless of payroll location and/or exchange rate fluctuations. 

United Arab Emirates

Updated January 5, 2026

‘Scientists and Specialists’ UAE Golden Visa:

  • Applicants for Dubai who do not have a government nomination:  
    • Current income. Effective November 2025, AED 30,000 per month as a basic salary i.e., excluding all allowances such as housing and transport allowances, among others. Between August 2024 and November 2025, allowances could be included in this amount; however, authorities have since reverted to the stricter pre-August 2024 approach. 
    • Prior income. As of December 2025, applicants must also prove that in the two years preceding the application they were earning a monthly basic salary of at least AED 30,000. Benefits and allowances are not included in the calculation.  
  • Applicants for Dubai who have a government nomination. Effective May 2024, AED 30,000 per month, with at least 50 percent of the income not being made up of allowances (for instance, housing and transport, among others). Previously, there was no percentage restriction on the amount of the salary that could be made up of allowances.
  • Applicants for all other emirates. AED 30,000 per month including allowances (for instance, housing and transport, among others).

Emirati nationals in the private sector. Effective January 1, 2026, the monthly minimum wage for Emirati nationals working in the private sector increased to AED 6,000, up from AED 5,000. Emirati nationals are required to obtain a work permit to work in the private sector. This increased minimum salary threshold applies to all new, renewed, or amended work permits for Emirati employees. 

United Kingdom

Updated March 5, 2026

  • National minimum wage (for foreign nationals and local employees). Beginning April 1, 2025, the national minimum wage will increase to GBP 12.21 per hour (for those 21 or over).  
  • Salary thresholds (for foreign nationals only). Beginning April 9, 2025, the minimum salary threshold will increase to GBP 25,000, in cases where it was previously GBP 23,200. In many cases, however, a higher threshold will apply (see below).  
  • Healthcare and education occupations have been increased based on national pay scales to reflect current salary structures. 

Visa-specific minimum salary levels:

Effective July 22, 2025, minimum salary thresholds are as follows for new applicants:

 

  • Skilled Worker Visa
    • GBP 41,700, up from GBP 38,700 (minimum rate for the visa category or the occupation if higher, based on market-rate salaries and Standard Occupation Classifications (SOC) codes);
    • GBP 37,500, up from GBP 34,830, if the applicant has a PhD that is relevant to the role; or
    • GBP 33,400, up from GBP 30,960, if the applicant (1) has a PhD relevant to the role in a STEM subject, (2) the role is on the shortage occupation list, (3) the applicant is a new entrant as defined by the authorities, or (4) the role is in a listed health or education occupation.
    •  Update March 5, 2026: Effective April 8, 2026, the salary of the Skilled Worker Visa holder will need to meet the required level in each pay period.    
  • Senior or Specialist Worker Visa​​: GBP 52,500, up from GBP 48,500 (minimum rate for the visa category or the occupation if higher, based on market-rate salaries and SOC codes)
  • Graduate Trainee Visa: GBP 27,300, up from GBP 25,410 (minimum rate for the visa category or the occupation if higher, based on market-rate salaries and SOC codes)
  • Update March 5, 2026 - Seasonal Worker: Effective April 8, 2026, GBP 12.71 per hour, up from GBP 12.21. An additional related change is that the time spent outside the United Kingdom is now calculated by the Certificate of Sponsorship start date. 

Update September 10, 2024: The maintenance requirement for student visa applicants to demonstrate they have funds to support themselves for each month of their course (up to nine months) must now match the maintenance loans available for UK students for the 2024-25 academic year. UKVI will ensure the amount aligns with UK students’ maintenance loans in the future. 

Other salary-related considerations:

  • Allowable work hours. SOC code salary thresholds for affected workers are now calculated based on a 37.5-hour work week, rather than the standard 39-hour work week.
  • Thresholds for employees with fluctuating work hours. Additional guidance has been released regarding salary thresholds for workers with fluctuating work hours week-to-week. Changes include the following:
    • work in excess of 48 hours in some weeks can be considered toward the salary threshold, provided the average over a regular cycle (which can be less than, but not more than, 17 weeks) is not more than 48 hours a week;
    • any unpaid rest weeks will count toward the average when considering whether the salary thresholds are met; and
    • any unpaid rest weeks will not count as absences from employment for the purpose of the rules which allow the cancellation of permission where there are absences from work for more than four weeks.
  • Tightened authority discretion on threshold compliance. It is now mandatory for authorities to deny applications where there are reasonable grounds to believe that the salary threshold does not comply with the National Minimum Wage Regulations or Working Time Regulations, whereas previously compliance guidance for thresholds was unclear.
  • Stricter salary deduction rules. New rules, effective April 9, 2025, ensure that paid allowances are treated consistently, prevent sponsorship costs from being passed to foreign nationals, and close a loophole where foreign nationals could invest in their sponsor’s business to artificially inflate salaries.
  • New entrant salary reduction rule. Foreign nationals claiming the new entrant salary reduction for professional training must be working towards a UK-recognized qualification. Previously, this was not required.

Uzbekistan

Updated October 14, 2026

Accreditation Card: Effective September 1, 2026, the minimum salary level for the accreditation card increased to UZS 1,360,000 per month, up from UZS 1,271,000. 

Vietnam

Updated January 22, 2026 

Effective January 1, 2026, the Vietnam government increased the minimum regional wage by 7.2%. All employees who work under labor contracts under the labor code which includes businesses, organizations, cooperatives, households and individuals who hire employees will benefit from the increase. 

  • Regional variation. The government will increase the minimum monthly wage to the following amounts depending on the region where the employee is located: VND 5,310,000 in region 1; VND 4,730,000 in region 2; VND 4,140,000 in region 3; and VND 3,700,000 in region 4. 
  • Hourly pay. The government will also increase the minimum hourly wage rates to the following amounts: VND 25,500 (up from VND 23,800) per hour in region 1; VND 22,700 (up from VND 21,200) per hour in region 2; VND 20,000 (up from VND 18,600) per hour in region 3; and VND 17,800 (up from VND 16,600) per hour in region 4. 
  • Benefits and allowances. Benefits and allowances may not be included in the minimum salary calculation for local hires. 
  • Currency. Employers are required to guarantee the salary in VND regardless of payroll location and/or exchange rate fluctuations.  
  • Compliance. The new salary levels apply to current permit holders, pending and new applications. 
  • Penalties. If an employer pays less than the legal minimum wage, whether to a foreigner or a Vietnamese national, the company is subject to administrative penalties. 

Monetary fines. For a company, the fine is generally double the individual amount, meaning roughly: 

  • VND 40–60 million VND if 1-10 employees affected;
  • VND 60–100 million VND if 11-50 employees affected; and
  • VND 100–150 million VND if 51+ employees affected

Required corrective actions. In addition to fines, the employer will be required to: 

  • Pay the difference between what was paid and the correct minimum wage 
  • Pay interest on the underpaid amounts (calculated at the highest non-term deposit rate announced by state banks at the time of the violation)
