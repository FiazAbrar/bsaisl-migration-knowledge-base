Existing Government-to-Government Agreement on Protection of Migrant Workers

Following paragraph provides an overview of the existing bilateral labour agreements and Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) the Government of Bangladesh has signed with several main countries of destinations. 

Bangladesh- United Arab Emirate (UAE) MoU in 2018

A memorandum of understanding was signed in 2018 between the UAE and Bangladesh for Bangladeshi workers. The signing of this MoU ensures the rights and benefits of the workers. This Memorandum of Understanding mentions the responsibilities and duties of the Government of both countries and recruitment agencies, and procedures for the recruitment of staff from Bangladesh provisions in nineteen categories. At the same time, it promises the responsibilities and duties of the employers, the provisions of the employment contract and a separate dispute resolution system. The Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) describes the responsibilities of workers, employers and the governments of both countries in accordance with a law that came into force in 2017 to protect the interests of these workers in the UAE. This agreement was signed with a view of achieving the goal of safe, tidy, and responsible labor migration. To implement the MoU, provision was made to form a joint committee by the comprising officials of both countries.

Bangladesh-Malaysia MoU in 2012 & 2015

In November 2012, the Government of Bangladesh had signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Government of Malaysia to provide an opportunity to the potential migrant workers from Bangladesh to migrate to Malaysia. The MoU provided the opportunity to the Bangladeshi workers in low cost, corresponding to that a huge number of workers applied for the jobs in Malaysia. The Government applied a lottery system to make the selection process fair, though it became very challenging and only very few could go to Malaysia through this process. Again in 2015, Malaysia and Bangladesh government made another MoU to receive and protect the rights of the Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia.

Bangladesh–Jordan MOU, 2012 or 2008

Bangladesh and Jordan have signed the MoU in 2012 and both governments have agreed to protect the rights of workers and employers by the legislation and the law in coherence with international standards and treaties in this regard. It is agreed that recruiting and employing of workers will be after the completion of all necessary legal procedures according to legislation in force in both countries. All would be governed by transparency, justice and mutual benefits. Study 2 (ilo.org)

Bangladesh–Saudi Arabia domestic worker agreement (2015)

Both countries have come into agreement in the year 2015. It refers to both costs and regulation of private employment agencies: (a) “Endeavour to control recruitment costs in both countries” (article 3.2); and (b) “Take legal measures against recruitment offices, companies or agencies in violation of the laws of either country” (article 3.6). Given the observed tendency for women migrants to be widely exposed to recruitment malpractices and resultant trafficking, it is important to take into account their specific vulnerability in designing these processes.

A good practice can be found in this agreement: “Ensure that Bangladeshi recruitment agencies, offices, or companies shall not charge or deduct from the salary of the [domestic service workers] any cost attendant to his/her recruitment and deploy mentor impose any kind of unauthorized salary deductions.”

The standard contract in the Bangladesh–Saudi Arabia domestic worker agreement states in article 7: “In case of dispute between the employer and the [domestic worker] the two contracting parties may refer the dispute to the appropriate Saudi authorities for conciliation and/or resolution.” This clause is vague because the appropriate authorities are not mentioned.

The Saudi Arabia domestic worker agreements provide for paid leave for return to the home country at the end of two years’ service. The Saudi Arabia domestic worker agreements provide that the COD facilitates the issuance of exit visas for the repatriation of domestic workers upon contract completion, emergency situations, or as the need arises. A provision can be made for compensation and repatriation as needed in case of serious illness, work accidents, or disability of the worker. In the event of the death of a worker, the funeral or the repatriation of the remains should be arranged at the expense of the employer in line with COD laws. Any back pays and all remaining dues should be transferred to the next of kin. Study 2 (ilo.org)

Bangladesh-South Korea MoU, 2012

Bangladesh and South Korea government has concluded MoU in 2012. According to the agreement, the sending agency has to explain the content of the labor contract to each worker so that he/she can fully understand it and decide whether or not to sign the labor contract on his/her own free will. The MoU states that the Korean Ministry of Employment and Labour will conduct post-arrival education and medical check-ups for workers before they start work.

Bangladesh–Libya agreement, 2008

Agreement has been signed between the countries in 2008. Only a few agreements in Asia specifically include a separate article on equality of treatment. In many cases, the employment contract section mentions that the same labour laws apply to migrant workers. A good provision can be found in this agreement: “The employee shall enjoy all rights and privileges enjoyed by the employees of the host country in accordance with the labour laws in force in the host country.”
