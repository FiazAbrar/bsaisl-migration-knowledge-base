    UNITED ARAB EMIRATES (UAE) POPULATION STATISTICS 2026

    Posted by GMI Research Team Posted in infographics, Statistics

    Oct 19 2026 at 3:40am

    The United Arab Emirates population in 2026 continues to grow steadily as the country strengthens its position as a global centre for business, trade and innovation.

    Professionals, entrepreneurs and investors from around the world choose the UAE for its economic opportunities, advanced infrastructure and high quality of life, contributing to a diverse and rapidly expanding population.

    Understanding UAE population statistics offers more than simple demographic insight.

    These numbers reveal how the country is evolving, which nationalities form the largest communities, where residents are concentrated, and how the workforce is changing over time.

    In the sections ahead, we break down the latest UAE population statistics for 2026, including population growth trends, expatriate demographics and key insights into the country’s changing demographic landscape.

    Explore the data to understand how these shifts continue to shape the UAE’s development and future growth.

    UAE Population 2026 (Key Statistics)

    • As of Oct 2026, the population of the United Arab Emirates stands at 11.57 million, based on data collected by the GMI research team. This figure includes both UAE nationals and expatriates living across the seven emirates.

    • According to the research conducted by the GMI Team, Dubai’s population reached 4.47 Million in 2026. 

    • The latest available data from the statistics centre – Abu Dhabi (SCAD) shows that Abu Dhabi’s population reached 4.14 Million in 2024.

    • The total male population in the UAE in 2026 is approximately 7.36 million

    • The total female population in the UAE in 2026 is 4.21 million

    • According to research by the GMI Team, the total expat population in the UAE in 2026 is 10.24 million, while the number of Emirati citizens is 1.33 million. This mix of people is important for the country’s growth and culture.

    • In 2026, the Indian community in the UAE is estimated to number nearly 4.39 million, accounting for approximately 37.96% of the country’s total population. They represent the largest expatriate group and contribute significantly to the UAE’s economy and daily life.

    • The largest age bracket in the UAE’s population falls between 25 and 54 years, making up 7.45 million people.

    • In 2026, the UAE’s urban population reached 10.22 million, with 1.35 million people living in rural areas. This highlights the country’s strong focus on city life and development.

    • According to Digital 2026 report by DataReportal, the median age of the population in the UAE is 31.6. This figure offers a glimpse into the country’s demographic profile, suggesting a relatively young and dynamic populace.

    • As of 2026, the UAE has an estimated population density of around 138 people per square kilometre (359 people per square mile).

    UAE Demographics in 2026

    Our 2026 data analysis shows a clear gender distribution across the UAE’s population.

    In 2026, males account for around 63.6% of the UAE’s population, while females represent about 36.4%.

    This difference reflects the country’s long-standing demographic pattern shaped largely by labour migration, where many male workers move to the UAE for employment opportunities.

    The gender distribution is generally more balanced among children and younger age groups.

    However, the working-age population shows a higher proportion of males due to the continued inflow of migrant workers.

    Despite the imbalance, women play an increasingly important role in the UAE’s economy and society. Government initiatives promoting female leadership, equal pay and board representation have helped strengthen women’s participation across business, government and education.

    Overall, these figures highlight how migration and workforce demand continue to influence the UAE’s demographic structure.

    Age Distribution of the UAE Population in 2026

    The age distribution of the UAE’s population in 2026 shows a country largely made up of people in their prime working years.

    This demographic structure reflects the UAE’s role as a major destination for global talent and employment.

    People aged 25–54 years form the largest segment, accounting for 64.39% of the population, or around 7.45 million residents.

    This group represents the core of the UAE’s workforce, supporting key industries such as construction, technology, healthcare, trade and services.

    The large share of this age group is closely linked to the inflow of expatriate professionals and workers.

    Children aged 0–14 years make up about 15.91% of the population, or roughly 1.84 million people.

    This indicates a stable presence of families living and working in the UAE.

    The 15–24 years age group accounts for around 12.53%, or about 1.45 million individuals.

    Many people in this category are students or early-career professionals entering the labour market.

    Older age groups remain relatively small.

    Residents aged 55–64 years represent about 5.34% (0.62 million), while those aged 65 and above account for around 1.83% (0.21 million).

    This reflects the UAE’s demographic pattern, where most residents move to the country primarily for employment rather than retirement.

    Overall, the UAE’s population structure is strongly centred around the working-age population, which supports economic activity, labour demand and continued development across multiple sectors.

    UAE Population by Gender in 2026

    The gender distribution across age groups in the UAE highlights how migration patterns influence the country’s demographic structure.

    In the 0–14 years category, boys and girls are almost equal, with 0.92 million each. This balanced ratio reflects typical birth patterns and the presence of family households across the country.

    The difference becomes visible in the 15–24 years group. There are about 0.87 million young men compared to 0.58 million young women.

    This shift partly reflects migration, as many young men move to the UAE for education, training and early career opportunities.

    The gap is most pronounced in the 25–54 years age group. Around 5.07 million men compared with 2.38 million women fall within this bracket.

    This segment forms the core of the UAE’s workforce, with many expatriate professionals and workers contributing to sectors such as construction, logistics, engineering, hospitality and technology.

    In the 55–64 years category, the population decreases to 0.39 million men and 0.23 million women.

    Many expatriates leave the country after completing their careers, which keeps older age groups relatively small.

    Among residents aged 65 and above, there are around 0.12 million men and 0.09 million women.

    The limited size of this group reflects the UAE’s migration-driven population growth rather than a naturally ageing population.

    UAE Population by Emirates in 2026

    Note: 1 – 2022, 2 – 2017, 3 – 2015, 4 – 2014, 5 – 2005 (latest available data from reliable sources)

    Dubai remains the most populous emirate, with more than 4.47 million residents in 2026. Its position as a global business and tourism hub continues to attract expatriates, entrepreneurs and skilled professionals from across the world.

    Abu Dhabi follows with approximately 4.13 million residents, according to the most recent population figures released by the Statistics Centre – Abu Dhabi in 2024.

    Sharjah ranks third with about 1.8 million residents, according to the Sharjah Census 2022, which recorded significant population growth in the emirate.

    Ajman has an estimated 504,846 residents, based on population statistics reported in 2017. Despite its smaller size, the emirate has experienced steady residential growth due to its proximity to Dubai and Sharjah.

    Ras Al Khaimah is estimated to have around 345,000 residents, with the figure derived from official population estimates published in 2015.

    Fujairah has a population of roughly 202,667 people, with the most widely referenced population data dating back to 2014.

    Umm Al Quwain remains the least populated emirate, with about 49,159 residents, based on the latest recorded population statistics from 2005.

    Overall, the distribution of residents across the UAE highlights a strong concentration in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, which together account for the majority of the country’s population due to greater employment opportunities, infrastructure and international connectivity.

    Emirati vs Expat Population in UAE (2026)

    The UAE remains one of the few countries where expatriates make up the overwhelming majority of the population. In 2026, foreign residents continue to play a central role in the country’s economy, workforce, and urban development, while the Emirati population represents a smaller but steadily growing share of the total population.

    The UAE’s population structure is closely linked to its rapid economic growth and international workforce demand.

    Over the past few decades, the country has attracted professionals, entrepreneurs, skilled workers, and labor communities from around the world, helping transform cities such as Dubai and Abu Dhabi into major global business and tourism centers.

    According to the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, residents from more than 200 nationalities currently live and work across the country.

    Indian nationals form the UAE’s largest expatriate community, followed by sizeable populations from Pakistan, Bangladesh, the Philippines, Egypt, Nepal, and several Western and Arab nations.

    This diverse population contributes to key sectors including construction, healthcare, hospitality, finance, logistics, retail, technology, and transportation.

    Dubai has the highest concentration of expatriates in the UAE due to its strong private-sector economy, international business environment, and tourism-driven job market.

    Abu Dhabi also hosts a large foreign workforce, particularly in industries such as energy, engineering, infrastructure, and financial services.

    While expatriates continue to dominate the workforce, the Emirati population has shown stable long-term growth supported by improved healthcare, higher life expectancy, and national development initiatives.

    Emirati citizens play an important role in public administration, policymaking, strategic industries, and cultural preservation across the country.

    The UAE’s high expatriate population ratio makes it one of the most internationally diverse nations in the world.

    Long-term residency programs, expanding business opportunities, and continued infrastructure development are expected to support further demographic growth in the coming years.

    Population estimates are based on official UAE demographic publications, government reports, and publicly available statistical data for 2026.

    UAE Population by Nationality in 2026

    The UAE’s population is shaped by a large international workforce that contributes significantly to the country’s economy, infrastructure, tourism, healthcare, finance, logistics, and technology sectors.

    Over the years, the country has become a major destination for professionals, entrepreneurs, and workers from across Asia, the Middle East, Europe, and Africa.

    Indian nationals form the largest population group in the UAE, accounting for an estimated 37.96% of the country’s total population in 2026, or nearly 4.39 million residents.

    Pakistanis represent the second-largest community with around 1.94 million people, followed by Bangladeshi and Filipino populations.

    UAE nationals make up approximately 11.5% of the country’s total population, reflecting the UAE’s unique demographic structure where expatriates form the majority of residents.

    Dubai has the highest concentration of expatriates due to its strong private-sector economy, tourism industry, and global business environment, while Abu Dhabi hosts a large foreign workforce in sectors such as energy, engineering, infrastructure, and financial services.

    The UAE’s nationality distribution reflects its position as a global hub for trade, aviation, tourism, technology, and international investment.

    Continued business expansion, infrastructure development, and long-term residency initiatives are expected to support further population growth in the coming years.

    Note: The figures are approximate and based on research conducted by the GMI team.

    Indian Population in UAE in 2026

    As of 2026, the Indian community remains the largest expatriate group in the UAE, with approximately 4.39 million residents, representing nearly 38% of the country’s total population.

    The size of this community reflects the strong economic and social ties between India and the United Arab Emirates.

    Over the years, Indian residents have contributed significantly to the UAE’s workforce and business environment.

    Indian entrepreneurs are active across sectors such as retail, real estate, logistics, technology, healthcare and professional services.

    In recent years, more than 18,000 new Indian companies joined the Dubai Chamber of Commerce, the highest number of registrations among expatriate groups.

    Economic cooperation between the two countries has also expanded. Since the implementation of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), bilateral non-oil trade between India and the UAE has reached around USD 100 billion.

    Indian students also form a large share of international university enrolments in Dubai, accounting for over 40% of the international student population in recent academic years.

    Overall, the Indian community continues to play an important role in the UAE’s economic activity, business ecosystem and demographic structure.

    UAE Population 2026: Urban vs Rural

    The UAE is a highly urbanised country, with around 10.22 million people (88.3%) living in urban areas, while approximately 1.35 million residents (11.7%) live in rural regions.

    This concentration reflects the country’s economic structure, where major cities such as Dubai and Abu Dhabi serve as the primary centres for employment, business and infrastructure development.

    These cities continue to attract both expatriates and Emirati residents seeking career opportunities and modern living environments.

    Urban areas also offer extensive transport networks, residential developments and commercial districts that support a growing population.

    As a result, the majority of residents prefer living in cities rather than rural communities.

    Overall, the UAE’s population distribution shows a strong urban concentration, which continues to influence national planning in areas such as housing, transport and infrastructure development.

    UAE Population Growth From 1985 to 2026

    The UAE’s population has grown rapidly over the past four decades, reflecting the country’s transformation into a global economic hub.

    During the 1980s and 1990s, steady population growth accompanied early infrastructure development and expanding oil revenues.

    As the country invested in modern cities and industries, migration increased gradually.

    Growth accelerated during the 2000s, driven by large-scale construction projects, expanding free zones and rising international business activity.

    This period marked a major shift as the UAE attracted a growing expatriate workforce.

    In the 2010s, continued urban expansion, tourism development and new economic sectors supported further population growth.

    Despite global disruptions around 2020, the UAE maintained a stable demographic trend.

    By 2026, the population is estimated to reach 11.57 million, highlighting the country’s sustained economic growth and its continued appeal as a destination for professionals, entrepreneurs and investors.

    Overall, the UAE’s population growth closely reflects its economic expansion, labour demand and international connectivity.

    UAE Population by Religion in 2026

    The United Arab Emirates is home to a highly diverse multicultural population, shaped by decades of global migration and economic growth. While Islam remains the dominant religion in the country, the UAE also has significant communities practicing Christianity, Hinduism, Buddhism, and other faiths.

    The country’s religious diversity reflects its large expatriate population, with residents from Asia, Europe, Africa, and other parts of the Middle East contributing to its multicultural social structure.

    Islam in the UAE

    Islam is the official religion of the UAE and represents nearly three-fourths of the country’s population. Mosques are located throughout the country, and Islamic traditions play an important role in daily life, governance, culture, and national celebrations.

    The majority of Emirati nationals are Muslims, while the Muslim expatriate population includes residents from countries such as India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Egypt, and other Arab nations.

    Christianity in the UAE

    Christianity is the second-largest religion in the UAE, accounting for approximately 12.9% of the population. The Christian community mainly consists of expatriates from the Philippines, India, Europe, Africa, and other Western countries.

    The UAE is home to several churches and worship centers across emirates such as Dubai and Abu Dhabi, reflecting the country’s support for religious tolerance and coexistence.
