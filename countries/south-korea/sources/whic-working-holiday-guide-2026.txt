   Korea has a Working Holiday Program with thirty countries/region
   worldwide. The main purpose of this visa is to allow you to have an extended
   holiday while supplementing your funds with short-term work in Korea.
   This one-time only visa allows you tremendous flexibility to immerse yourself
   in Korea culture.
   Working Holiday Permit holders are also permitted to study or train at a
   private educational institution during their stay.

[Long-term Stay]
Homestay

Living as part of a Korean-speaking household, you will have your own
bedroom and have meals with your hosts. It is a great way to meet Koreans and
share their way of life. You will also be able to improve your fluency in Korean.

One Room
The One Room is a widespread and extremely popular housing option among
students and single adults. The One Room is a large single room equipped with
a bathroom, kitchenette and living space.

Goshiwon (Goshitel or Livingtel)
Goshiwon was initially for people who were preparing for national exams
to become public servants. However, now that regular One Room or studio
apartments are demanding high rent, Goshiwon is becoming a popular
accommodation choice. It is fully furnished and prices can go higher if there is a
private bathroom and window within the room itself.

Officetel/Studio Apartment
The "Officetel" is a combination of Office and Hotel such that people can
live and work in the same building. Many of them come fully furnished. The
Officetel is also more expensive and offers larger living spaces/facilities than
the One Room. Tenants are also responsible for monthly maintenance fees and
additional utility fees.

Q&A  01 Am I restricted in the type of work I can do in Korea
             on my Working Holiday Visa?

              Working Holiday (H-1) visa holders may generally undertake temporary
              employment to support their stay. However, certain occupations are
              restricted, including some entertainment-related work and professional
              services such as medical practitioners, lawyers, professors, pilots, and
              language instructors. Other positions, including language- and cooking-
              related jobs, may require additional qualifications or a different visa.
              Eligibility depends on the specific duties and immigration regulations.
              Before accepting employment, please confirm with the Ministry of Justice's
              Immigration Contact Center (Domestic: 1345 / International: +82-2-6908-
              1345 / 1346).

     02 How much money will I earn?

              Salaries vary depending on your skill, experience, education, certification,
              and type of employer (industry).
              The minimum wage for 2026 has been set at 10,320 KRW per hour.
              Foreigners will enjoy the same level of minimum wage as Korean workers.
              Part-time workers often find it difficult to cover their living expenses with
              the low wage. Careers using languages(translator, editor, etc.) usually fall
              into the high paying job category in Korea.

     03 Is there any restriction with my working hours as a
             Working Holiday Visa holder?

              A ccording to the Ministry of Justice, H-1 (Working Holiday) Visa holders cannot
              work more than *25 hours per week.
              The participants should engage in employment as an incidental aspect of their
              holiday, rather than primary purpose, in order to supplement their travel funds.
               * Canadian participants are exempt from the 25-hour restriction and may

                 work up to 40 hour per week (2,080 hours per year).

Foreigner     Foreigner (Alien) registration (Applying for Residence Card) is
Registration  compulsory for all Working Holiday Makers arriving in Korea.
              Registration must be done within 90 days of entry at the nearest
              Immigration Office of your stay (jurisdiction).

              A foreigner registration card (Residence Card) is required for many
              activities in Korea, such as opening a bank account, purchasing a
              mobile phone, signing a lease and working legally in Korea.

              It is essential that you submit all relevant documents with your
              application. Immigration officer will take your fingerprints and give you
              a receipt with the date that you can return and pick up your Residence
              Card. It usually takes 3 weeks to process to issue Residence Card.

              * If your individual information has been changed while residing in
                the Republic of Korea (such as your name, nationality, date of birth,
                passport number, passport validity date, passport issued date, your
                address in Korea), you need to report that to the immigration office
                within 14 days. Regarding this, please contact Immigration Contact
                Center for the further reporting process, call 1345 without area code.

              * If you have lost your residence card, you need to report it to the
                immigration office.

Bank  All Working Holiday Makers are advised to open a Korean bank account. Keeping
      your money in a bank account is safer than carrying cash and it is easier for you to
      keep track of your spending. Opening an account is quite simple, just bring your
      passport (or foreigner residence card) to a branch. There is no charge for opening
      and maintenance of bank accounts.

      A check (debit) card is one of the most common payment methods in Korea. The
      amount is immediately deducted from your account as you make a payment. To
      make a check (debit) card, you need your Residence Card, and passport. Check
      cards can also be used for making cash deposits and withdrawals.

      For further information on banking please contact the appropriate bank branch.

      The Korean Currency is called Won(KRW)
      Bills: 1,000won, 5,000won, 10,000won, 50,000won
      Coins: 10won, 50won, 100won, 500won

8 FAQ

           Q. Can I come to Korea on a Working Holiday?

                           Citizens of certain countries and age groups are able to come to Korea on a
                           Working Holiday. Generally, the Working Holiday Visa may be granted for
                           people between 18 and 30 years (inclusive) from thirty countries/regions
                           worldwide. *This could be different by nationality.
                           Regarding the detailed age eligibility by nationality, please refer to this page
                           here : https://whic.mofa.go.kr/contents.do?menuNo=90&contentsNo=38

           Q. Can I apply for Korean Working Holiday visa in Korea?

                           Completed visa application form together with necessary supporting
                           documents can only be lodged at Korean embassy/consulate in your country
                           of origin. Applicants must obtain the Korean Working Holiday Visa prior to
                           arrival to Korea.

                               E xception: If you are Austrian, you can also submit your application in Japan (Tokyo),
                               China (Beijing, Shanghai), Hong Kong and Taiwan.
                               If you are a German national, please contact KVAC in Berlin ( kvacdeinfo@iom.int /
                               website : www.visaforkorea.eu ) or your jurisdictional Korean Consulate in Germany for
                               further application details.
                               If you are a French national, please contact KVAC in Paris ( kvacfrinfo@iom.int / website :
                               www.visaforkorea.eu ),
                               If you are from the U.K., please contact KVAC in London ( kvacukinfo@iom.int /website :
                               www.visaforkorea.eu ) for further application details.
                               If you are from the Netherlands, please contact the KVAC in Hague ( kvacnlinfo@iom.int /
                               www.visaforkorea.eu ) for further application details.

           Q. If I was previously granted a Korean Working Holiday Visa,
                        can I apply for a Working Holiday Visa again?

                           No. As a general rule, if you have previously been granted a Korean Working
                           Holiday Visa, you cannot apply for another one. However, exceptions apply
                           depending on nationality. Canadian and Japanese citizens may participate
                           in the program up to twice, in accordance with the relevant bilateral
                           agreements. For further information about a second application, please
                           contact the Embassy or Consulate of the Republic of Korea with jurisdiction
                           over your place of residence.

             Q. Can I extend my Working Holiday Visa in Korea?

                               Working Holiday Visa cannot be extended but you may be eligible to switch
                              to another type of visa in limited circumstances. Please contact Korea
                              Immigration Service for more details. (www.immigration.go.kr)

             Q. What documents will be required to obtain Korean
                           Working Holiday Visa?
                              Please refer to the <1. Korea Working Holiday Program/Visa> on this
                              brochure for general information. Since the application requirements may
                              vary we highly recommend you to contact directly to the respective Korean
                              Embassy or Consulates.

             Q. Do I need a job in order to get a Korean Working Holiday Visa?

                              No, you don't need an offer of employment to apply for a Korean Working
                              Holiday Visa.

             Q. Am I eligible to study in Korea on a Working Holiday Visa?

                              If you are planning to pursue an academic degree other than a Korean
                              Language Course, you will usually be required to apply for a Student Visa
                              (D-2).

             Q. What happens if my passport is lost or stolen? Can I get the
                           visa transferred to my new passport?
                              Visit the Embassy/Consulate to cancel your current passport and apply for a
                              new one. You do not need to obtain a new Working Holiday Visa if you have
                              registered with Immigration Office (Residence card). You can legally reside
                              in Korea on Working Holiday Visa status with your Residence Card. However,
                              you must report your new passport information to the immigration office
                              over your jurisdiction within 14 days. (report change of information : passport
                              number, validity and issue date etc.)

Q. I am planning on applying for the visa soon. Within how many weeks/
      months do I have to leave for Korea once the Visa is issued?
       Once the Visa is issued, you will have a valid entry date specified on your
       issued Visa. You will usually have 1 year before your entry to Korea but can
       vary among respective Embassies/Consulates.

Q. Do I need to visit the embassy in person or can I apply online/ via post?

       You have to directly consult the Korean Embassy/Consulate/KVAC in your
       country/region for this matter as each embassy/consulate has a different
       visa application policy.

Q. Is the Working Holiday program supported by Korean government?
      If yes, What kind of jobs or accommodation are offered?
       This Visa allows you to opportunities to stay in Korea but participants cannot
       expect any direct help from the Korean government in finding a job and
       accommodation. Basically you have to do it all by yourself since there is no
       particular program, accommodation or job placement coming along with the
       Visa. Working Holiday Info Center provides participants in Korea with free
       consultation on finding jobs, accommodation and life in general so please do
       not hesitate to contact us upon your arrival in Korea.

Q. Is there any online community/blogs or websites where Working Holiday
      makers in Korea can refer to?
       Usually there are many meet-up communities for foreigners/expats residing
       in Korea and you can find them by online search or Social Network Service
       (SNS). Working Holiday Info Center also runs a Facebook page with
       information that can be useful for the participants.
