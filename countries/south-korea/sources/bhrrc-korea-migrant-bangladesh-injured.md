# S. Korea: Migrant workers, incl. from Bangladesh, Vietnam and Nepal, injured & abused, unable to change jobs & access remedy under visa scheme

Business and Human Rights Centre (story page; timeline items dated 8 Jul 2025 and 17 Sep 2025). Saved via Firecrawl 2026-11-19, main content only.

Migrant workers under South Korea’s Employment Permit System (EPS) often cannot change employers even when injured or subjected to abuse and violence. Because a transfer requires the current employer’s consent, requests are frequently met with retaliation, including wage theft, reduced or irregular work, and even criminal charges.

A Nepali worker was injured while working at a metal factory and faced verbal abuse and physical violence. When he sought a transfer, his employer stopped assigning him tasks, withheld his wages, and refused permission to move. After he confronted the employer, he was charged with “obstruction of business” and received little government support.

In Pocheon, Gyeonggi Province, a Bangladeshi worker was injured in 2024 at a manufacturing plant. His boss instructed him not to report the accident or tell anyone. When he returned to work and requested a transfer because of his injury, the employer again refused.

Earlier this year in Gumi, North Gyeongsang Province, a Vietnamese construction worker died of suspected heatstroke while finishing his shift. Reports indicate that only his Korean colleagues had been allowed shorter hours during the heatwave.

In Yongin, a Vietnamese woman worker was assaulted by her manager. Because she had already reached the maximum number of workplace transfers permitted under the EPS, she was required to prove the abuse before being allowed to move. The manager was later sentenced to one year in prison for the assault.
