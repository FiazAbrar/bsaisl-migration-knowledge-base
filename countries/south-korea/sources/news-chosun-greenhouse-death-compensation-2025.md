# Court orders state to compensate migrant worker's family

20 million won awarded for death in unheated greenhouse dormitory

By Bang Geuk-ryeol. The Chosun Daily (English). Published 2025.09.19. Saved via Firecrawl 2026-11-19, article text only.

A high court ruled on the 19th that the state must compensate the family of Nuon Sokheng, a migrant worker who died while living in an unheated vinyl greenhouse dormitory, with 20 million Korean won.

The Seoul Central District Court Civil Appeals Division 3-2 (Presiding Judge Park Pyung-gyun) overturned the first trial’s dismissal of the family’s lawsuit against the government and ordered, “The government must pay each plaintiff 10 million Korean won.” According to this ruling, Sokheng’s parents will receive a total of 20 million Korean won. The court also decided that 80% of the litigation costs should be borne by the plaintiffs, with the remaining 20% covered by the government.

Sokheng, a Cambodian national, was found dead in December 2020 at a vinyl greenhouse dormitory on a farm in Pocheon, Gyeonggi Province. While the official cause of death was complications from liver cirrhosis, critics pointed to the poor living conditions of migrant workers as the root cause of the incident. At the time, foreign workers, including Sokheng, resided in makeshift structures inside the greenhouse.

In December 2021, on the first anniversary of Sokheng’s death, his family applied for industrial accident compensation from the Korea Workers’ Compensation & Welfare Service, which approved the claim in May 2022. The family then filed a separate damages lawsuit against the state in September 2022.

In August of last year, the first trial ruled, “The evidence submitted by the family alone is insufficient to prove the state’s violation of duties or wrongdoing regarding Sokheng’s death.” However, the appellate court reversed this decision and acknowledged the government’s liability. The family’s argument—that the government neglected its duty to manage and supervise the poor living conditions of migrant workers despite being aware of them—was partially accepted.
