 This content provides legal information regarding the employment of foreign workers
    holding the "Non-Professional (E-9)" or "Work and Visit (H-2)" visa status.

Employment eligibility for foreign workers

Employment Permit System (EPS)

- Foreigners who wish to be employed under the "Non-Professional (E-9)" or "Work and
    Visit (H-2)" visa status must, in principle, engage in employment activities only
    through the general EPS or the special EPS (Articles 8 and 12 of Act on the
    Employment of Foreign Workers).

    Restrictions on Change of Status of Stay
   Q. I am currently staying in Korea. Can I change my visa to E-9 and start working?
   A. The Non-Professional (E-9) or Work and Visit (H-2) status of stay must be

        obtained in the applicant's home country through the Employment Permit System
        (EPS) procedures prior to entry into the Republic of Korea. Therefore, changing
        one's current status of stay to Non-Professional (E-9) or Work and Visit (H-2)
        while already residing in Korea is, as a general rule, not permitted.

 "Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance" and "Accident Insurance"

Q. I was injured while working and had to receive hospital treatment. Can a
    foreign worker receive workers' compensation?

A. Yes, they can. Foreign workers holding the Non-Professional (E-9) or Work and
    Visit (H-2) visa status are eligible for coverage under the Industrial Accident
    Compensation Insurance and are entitled to receive the same compensation as
    Korean nationals for injuries, illnesses, disabilities, or death occurring on
    the job (Article 6 of Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act).

      However, in cases of injuries or illnesses not related to work, or death and
      permanent disability not caused by occupational injury, compensation is
      available through the Accident Insurance, which is an insurance exclusively
      for foreign workers (Article 23 (2) of Act on the Employment of Foreign
      Workers; Article 28 (2) of Enforcement Decree of the Act on the
      Employment of Foreign Workers).

Change of workplace (business or worksite)

- Foreign workers holding the Non-Professional (E-9) visa status must, in principle,
    continue working at the workplace (business or worksite) where the employment
    agreement was signed at the time of entry. They may change their workplace only
    in the event of compelling circumstances such as business suspension or closure,
    and only up to three times initially (with two additional changes allowed)
    (Article 25 (1) and (4) of Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

- Foreign workers holding the Work and Visit (H-2) visa status may change their
    workplace freely without restrictions (Article 25 (1) of Act on the
    Employment of Foreign Workers).

Re-employment of foreign workers and employment after re-entry

Period of employment and re-employment (extension of employment period)

- Foreign workers may engage in employment activities for up to three years from the
    date of entry (Article 18 of Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

- If an employer applies to the Minister of Employment and Labor for re-employment
    before the initial three-year employment period expires, the foreign worker may
    extend the employment period once for up to less than two years (up to 1 year and
    10 months), even beyond the initial three-year limit (Article 18-2 (1) of Act
    on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

 Extension of Stay

Q. My period of employment has been extended. Can I just keep working without
    doing anything else?

A. No. A foreign worker with the status of stay for Non-Professional (E-9) or Work
    and Visit (H-2) whose employment has been extended and intends to continue
    working beyond the original period of stay must obtain approval for an
    extension of stay before the current period of stay expires (Article 25 (1) of 
    Immigration Act).

        Employment after re-entry

- A foreign worker with the status of stay for Non-Professional (E-9) who has
    departed after working in Korea may not re-enter for employment under Act on
    the Employment of Foreign Workers until six months have passed since the date of
    departure (Article 18-3 of Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

- However, a foreign worker with the status of stay for Non-Professional (E-9) who
    has been re-employed and whose extended employment period has ended may re-enter
    for employment purposes one month after departure, provided the employer has
    applied for re-employment approval before the worker's departure (Article 18-4 (1)
    of Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

           Special Re-entry Program through TOPIK

          Q. I heard that a former Non-Professional (E-9) worker who passes TOPIK becomes
               eligible to be re-employed in Korea. Is that true?

          A. Yes, it is. A foreign worker with the status of stay for Non-Professional (E-9)
               who voluntarily departs Korea before their stay expires after being re-employed
               may re-enter Korea for employment purposes six months after the departure date,
               if they pass a special Korean language test [Article 18-3 of Act on the
               Employment of Foreign Workers; Comprehensive Guide to the Employment
               Permit System for Foreign Workers (Human Resources Development Service of
               Korea)].

Return of foreign workers to their home countries

Compliance with departure date

- A foreign worker may      stay in Korea only within    their permitted stay
    period and must depart  before the stay period ends  (Article 17 (1) of
    Immigration Act).

- A foreign worker who fails to depart after the expiration of the stay period may
    be forcibly removed from Korea and may be subject to imprisonment for up to three
    years or a fine of up to KRW 30,000,000 (Subparagraph 7 of Article 94 and of
    Article 46 (1) 8 of Immigration Act).

Application for foreign worker-exclusive insurance benefits

- A foreign worker who has worked continuously for 1 year or more may claim the
    Departure Guarantee Insurance upon returning to their home country after the
    employment agreement ends (Article 13 (1) of Act on the Employment of Foreign
    Workers; main clause of Article 21 (2) 2 of Article 21 (2) of Enforcement
    Decree of the Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

   · If the amount of the Departure Guarantee Insurance is less than the retirement
      benefit under Act on the Guarantee of Employees' Retirement Benefits, the
      employer must pay the difference to the foreign worker (Article 21 (3) of 
      Enforcement Decree of the Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers).

- A foreign worker may also claim the Return Cost Insurance to cover expenses needed
    for their return home (Article 15 (1) of Act on the Employment of Foreign
    Workers; Article 22 (2) of Enforcement Decree of the Act on the Employment
    of Foreign Workers).
