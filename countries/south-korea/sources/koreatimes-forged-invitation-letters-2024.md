A group of document forgers who created fake corporate invitations to illegally bring foreign nationals to South Korea have been arrested by the police.

The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency announced Thursday the arrest of four Pakistani nationals on charges of forgery and other crimes. Since June 2022, the group has been forging documents necessary for the issuance of short-term business visas, enabling illegal entry into South Korea. The main suspect, 46, has been detained.

Police have also requested Interpol to issue red notices for two brokers, who are based overseas. The two brokers, both of Pakistani origin, had previously acquired South Korean citizenship but later returned to Pakistan.

They are believed to have coordinated the illegal entries. Of the 29 foreigners who entered South Korea through this scheme, 18, including a 37-year-old individual, have been arrested for violating immigration laws, while the remaining 11 are being pursued after having been placed on a no-fly list.

They charged between \$10,000 and \$13,000 per case, forging invitation letters in the name of small and medium-sized South Korean companies to secure visas.

To avoid detection, they limited the number of invitees per company to three or four people and provided false contact numbers using burner phones to receive verification calls from South Korean embassies abroad.

The 46-year-old, who has a previous criminal record for similar offenses, ran this operation with family members, including his nephew and ex-wife.

The 29 foreign nationals involved paid the two Pakistani brokers for visa processing, with the brokers subcontracting the 46-year-old's group to forge essential documents such as certificates of sponsorship and business licenses.

These documents were submitted to four different diplomatic missions, including the South Korean Embassy in Dubai, which issued the visas. Upon arriving at Incheon International Airport, the foreigners met with the 46-year-old's group and paid an additional \$3,000 in fees.

A police official said, "They even forged notarized documents, making it nearly impossible for the embassies to catch the fraud."

Additionally, the group exploited the refugee application system. Under South Korea's refugee law and international agreements, applicants can remain in the country while their case is under review, even if refugee status is not granted. The group took advantage of this loophole to extend the stay of foreigners, allowing them to work illegally here.

A police official said, "There is no set time limit for the Refugee Review Committee’s decision, and administrative lawsuits can take years, allowing applicants to stay for as long as 10 years by reapplying with new grounds for refugee status."

The police have notified the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, recommending improvements to the visa issuance process, including the implementation of more rigorous on-site verification procedures to confirm the authenticity of invitations.
