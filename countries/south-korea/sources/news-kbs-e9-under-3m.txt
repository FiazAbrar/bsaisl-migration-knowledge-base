7 in 10 E-9 Visa Holders Earn Less Than 3 Million Won Per Month

Written: 2026-03-11 09:25:52
 / 
Updated: 2026-03-11 10:05:43

Share

Print

Photo : YONHAP News

                                Nearly seven out of ten foreign workers in South Korea who hold E-9 visas for blue collar employment earn an average monthly income of less than three million won, or roughly two-thousand-39 U.S. dollars, according to government data released Tuesday.

Supplemental information from the Ministry of Data and Statistics’s 2025 Survey on Immigrants’ Living Conditions and Labor Force showed that as of May of last year, one-point-69 million foreigners aged 15 or older resided in South Korea.

Among them, 24-point-two percent were overseas Koreans, 19 percent were blue collar workers, 14 percent were international students and nine–point-six percent had permanent resident status.

Holders of E-9 visas made up a plurality, or 28-point-nine percent, of the pool. Nearly 80 percent of those workers were employed in mining and manufacturing; about 15 percent in agriculture, forestry and fisheries; and approximately three percent in construction.

Professional workers and marriage immigrants represented a plurality of the mining and manufacturing industries while foreign students and visiting workers filled positions in wholesale and retail, lodging and the restaurant sector.

Just over 50 percent of all foreign workers earned, on average, less than two million won per month, and just under 37 percent earned more than three million won.

E-9 visa holders, at 68-point-nine percent, accounted for the highest proportion of earners on the lower end of the scale.

Fifty-seven-point-five percent of foreigners lived in the greater Seoul area, but less than one percent of blue collar workers resided in the capital.

The ministry said the proportion of foreign workers residing in Seoul had decreased due to high housing costs.
