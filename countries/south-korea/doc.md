# South Korea: migration guide

How to go to South Korea from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step, for Bangladesh passport holders, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

KRW is the Korean won; Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000). On 19 October 2026 the Bank of Korea valued Tk 1 at KRW 10.86, so KRW 10,000 was about Tk 920, and Bangladesh Bank's rate was US$1 = Tk 123.65 [KA0002, KA0003, KA0001].

## Start here

If you have never been abroad, read this first. Every point is set out in full further down.

### Can you go now?

Yes, but few are picked. Korea takes Bangladeshi workers through the EPS, a government-to-government scheme in which only BOESL, the state-owned recruiting company, may send you, and a Korean employer picks you from a roster [C0012, R0063]. BMET cleared 1,349 workers for Korea from 1 January to 18 November 2026 [C0059].

### Your first five steps

1. Check you qualify: 18 to 39, SSC passed, not colour-blind (Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment), Step 1) [R0071, R0072].
2. Get an e-passport: Tk 4,025 for 5 years, regular delivery (Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment), Step 2) [R0009].
3. Learn Korean from HRD Korea's free EPS-TOPIK Standard Textbook, the basis of every test question (Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment), Step 3) [R0087, R0092].
4. When BOESL announces a round, register on eps.boesl.gov.bd and pay Tk 4,000 plus Tk 46 by bKash (Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment), Step 4) [R0112, R0075, R0068].
5. Once rostered, track your status on www.eps.go.kr; BOESL takes nothing more until you are selected and medically fit, then only by bank pay order (Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment), Step 7) [R0101, R0110].

### What it really costs

The test costs Tk 4,000; once selected you pay BOESL's charges, itemised at Tk 38,584 in a cost table still awaiting Board approval, and your air tickets [R0068, R0188, R0189]. The real cost is the wait: about two-thirds of rostered candidates are likely to be dropped after 2 years [R0106]. Brokers arrested in Dhaka in October 2026 had taken Tk 40,000 to Tk 10 lakh a person, the Special Response Battalion said [R0222, R0223].

### Three scams to watch for

- *A "Korea visa" sold by an agency or a person:* only BOESL sends EPS workers, and it has no agent and takes no cash [R0094, R0110].
- *A coaching centre or broker that "guarantees" a pass or a job:* the employer alone picks 1 of 3 profiles, and no one can intervene [R0063, R0100].
- *A visit visa sold as a job:* working on it is a crime, with up to 3 years in prison [V0028, V0151, V0149].

### Who to call

- In Bangladesh: BOESL, 02-48319125 or 01765411653, 9 am to 4 pm [R0113]; Probash-Bondhu, 16135, free, or +8809610102030 from abroad [R0004].
- In Korea: the Foreign Workforce Counselling Centre, 1577-0071, then 12 and * for Bangla, every day 09:00 to 18:00 [R0237].
- The Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing in Seoul: +82-2-796-9010 ext. 300, hotline +82-10-9188-4056 [R0236].

## Background

### In one minute

South Korea takes Bangladeshi workers mainly through the EPS (Employment Permit System), a government-to-government scheme under which Korea, not a recruiting agency, controls which foreign worker each employer gets, mostly for small factories, construction and farms [KA0004, C0024, C0020, C0021]. On the Bangladesh side only BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the state-owned recruiting company under the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare) may send EPS workers; on the Korean side HRD Korea (Human Resources Development Service of Korea) sets the test and registers passers on the job roster [C0012, KA0005, KA0006, C0004, C0020]. A worker aged 18 to 39 with at least the SSC (Secondary School Certificate) pays Tk 4,000 (plus Tk 46 bKash charge) to sit the EPS-TOPIK, a computer-based Korean language test, then a skills test; those who pass go on a roster, and Korean employers pick one of every three profiles shown to them, a choice Korea controls [C0003, C0006, C0004, C0008, C0014, C0020]. Once selected, the worker pays BOESL's own charges, itemised at Tk 38,584 in a cost table still marked as awaiting its Board's approval, against a 2026 Korean minimum wage of KRW 2,156,880 a month [C0081, C0080]. The problem is getting picked: in 2025 Korea issued 9,648 E-9 visa issuance certificates to Nepalis but only 1,807 to Bangladeshis [C0109, C0107]. Thousands who passed the test have been deleted from the roster after two years without a job offer, and brokers arrested in October 2026 had taken up to Tk 10 lakh a person promising work visas and exam passes, the arresting battalion said [C0018, C0112, C0115]. Once in Korea the E-9 visa ties the worker to one employer for up to 4 years 10 months, with at most three moves in the first three years and only for reasons the law allows; walking out makes him undocumented, and the government counted 344,387 undocumented foreigners in September 2026 [C0063, C0069, C0070, C0047].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Korea is open to Bangladeshi EPS workers, but few are being picked: BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which clears every worker leaving Bangladesh) cleared 1,349 workers for South Korea from 1 January to 18 November 2026, 28th among destinations, out of 652,482 for all countries [C0059, C0060].

#### Status

- *Open on 19 November 2026, with a smaller Korean intake.* Korea cut its 2026 E-9 (non-professional employment visa, the EPS visa) quota to 80,000 from 130,000 in 2025, a fall of 38.5%, citing falling vacancies in manufacturing and construction [C0037, C0036]. Of the 80,000, 70,000 are split by sector (50,000 for manufacturing and 10,000 for farming and livestock, the rest for other sectors), and 10,000 are a flexible reserve any sector can draw on if demand changes unexpectedly [C0034, KA0009].
- *Bangladesh fills a fraction of its places.* Over 2021 to 2025 Korea allocated Bangladesh 39,255 places; BOESL received job orders for 17,247 and sent 14,829, or 37% [C0015].
- *A new test round is under way.* BOESL's notice of 1 July 2026 opened registration for up to 20,000 people to sit the Korean test from 1 September 2026, to roster just 2,000 manufacturing workers for the second half of 2027; the skills and competency tests are planned for January and February 2027 and the final result for April 2027 [C0001, C0008]. Ten seats are offered for every rostered place [C0001].
- *People deleted from the roster get first chance.* The first registration window (10 to 12 July 2026) was reserved for people deleted, or due to be deleted, from the manufacturing, construction, fishing or shipbuilding roster between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2026; the second (14 to 16 July 2026) was first come, first served for new applicants [C0002].

#### The channels

- *EPS, E-9, through BOESL.* The main route: Korean test, skills test, roster, employer's choice, then a visa (see "Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment)") [C0020, C0012]. Bangladeshis go almost entirely to manufacturing: of the 1,807 E-9 visa certificates Korea issued to Bangladeshis in 2025, 1,796 were for manufacturing, 9 for fishing and 2 for construction [C0107]. The factories make food, paper, plastics, electrical and electronic goods, wood, metal, machinery and textiles [C0084].
- *E-8 seasonal farm work.* Korean cities hire seasonal farm workers for 5 to 8 months under agreements with the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare: in July 2026 BOESL called for 28 workers (10 men, 18 women) for Dangjin City under the ministry's MoU (memorandum of understanding) with the city, open only to permanent residents of Cumilla and Kishoreganj aged 25 to 45 with an official certificate of more than a year's farm work from the Department of Agricultural Extension (see E-8 seasonal agricultural work) [C0125, KA0012, KA0011]. Only 59 Bangladeshis held E-8 visas in September 2026 [C0044].
- *E-7 skilled jobs, through private agencies.* Shipyards such as Hanwha Ocean have hired experienced Bangladeshi welders on the E-7 skilled visa through private recruiting agencies such as RRC Human Resources; 76 had gone this way by January 2024 [C0117]. These jobs want 2 to 5 years' experience, preferably 10 to 12 [C0119]. Shipyard E-7 pay is not fixed: in 2026 HD Hyundai Heavy Industries offered its roughly 1,600 directly hired foreign workers contracts with KRW 170,000 to 200,000 less base pay a month, and about 200 joined the metal workers' union [C0120].
- *E-7-4 after EPS.* An E-9 or E-10 worker who has worked long in Korea can switch to the E-7-4 skilled-worker visa after checks on Korean, income and work history; 52,648 people of all nationalities held it in September 2026 [C0122].
- *Students.* 9,221 Bangladeshis held D-2 student visas and 533 D-4-1 Korean-language-trainee visas in September 2026 (see "Student visas (D-2 degree, D-4 language)") [C0052].

#### Who qualifies

- *Age.* 18 to 39; for the 2026 round, born between 15 July 1986 and 14 July 2008 [C0003].
- *Education.* At least SSC or equivalent [C0006].
- *Language.* Basic Korean reading, writing and comprehension, proved by the EPS-TOPIK: 40 questions (20 reading, 20 listening) for 200 marks in 50 minutes, with passers chosen in score order from those above 120 [C0006, C0009].
- *Health.* No colour blindness, drug addiction or syphilis; a worker who tests positive for drugs or TB (tuberculosis) after arriving in Korea is sent home at his own cost [C0006, C0013].
- *Record.* No criminal conviction, no travel ban, never illegally resident in Korea, and not more than five years in Korea on E-9 or E-10 [C0006].
- *Sex.* Women may apply, but few go: 76 of the 25,214 workers BOESL had sent by 2022 were women, and 48 of the 1,807 E-9 visa certificates for Bangladeshis in 2025 [C0105, C0111].

Against its quota, by BOESL's figures, BOESL sent 5,891 of 5,941 in 2022, 4,804 of 10,200 in 2023 and 2,779 of 11,700 in 2024 [C0016]. Nepal sent 16,300 against a quota of 16,400 in 2024 and Sri Lanka 7,122 against 10,634 [C0017]. Demand to sit the test far exceeds the places: 55,403 registered in 2023 [C0138]. In March 2024 BOESL's managing director said 35,000 people with Korean had been selected, and at least 20,000 more would be chosen by lottery to sit the next test [C0027].

#### Who is in Korea

Korea counted 44,721 Bangladeshis on 30 September 2026 (36,612 men and 8,109 women), up from 21,928 at the end of 2022 [C0044, C0045]. They include 13,093 on E-9, 6,164 on E-7 skilled visas, 9,221 students, 1,459 language trainees, 6,287 family dependants (F-3) and 1,224 on G-1, the temporary status given while a refugee claim, a wage-arrears case or an industrial-accident claim is pending [C0044, C0049]. The 13,093 Bangladeshis were under 4% of the 349,660 E-9 holders of all nationalities [C0044, C0053]. 19,418 Bangladeshis held work visas, against 66,634 Nepalis and 30,399 Sri Lankans [C0046, C0145]. Over the same period the number of Nepalis in Korea rose from 47,865 to 106,426 [C0132].

#### What has closed it before

- *2020-21, COVID-19.* Korea stopped admitting EPS workers from March 2020 to November 2021 and resumed in December 2021 [C0028]. Departures fell from 1,647 in 2019 to 208 in 2020 and 108 in 2021, then jumped to 5,910 in 2022 [C0054].
- *Quotas Bangladesh cannot fill.* Since 2023 fewer than half of Bangladesh's places have been used [C0016]. The 2023 shortfall was put down to Korean employers' poor view of Bangladeshi workers for frequent job changes, weak employer demand, little promotion from Bangladesh, poor Korean and cultural differences [C0026]. A World Bank study ranked Bangladesh last of seven source countries in Korean employers' preference for 2011 to 2015 [C0023].

#### Where it may grow, and where it may tighten

- *Shipbuilding.* Korea's separate shipbuilding E-9 quota ran from April 2023 to the end of 2025; shipyards now hire through the manufacturing quota [C0035]. Skilled welders go on E-7 through private agencies, at about Tk 5 lakh in migration costs for a salary of about Tk 2.5 lakh [C0118].
- *E-7-4.* Korea set the 2026 E-7-4 quota at 33,000, down from 35,000; firms and workers say complex procedures and the Korean requirement are the main obstacles to switching [C0121, C0123].
- *Seasonal farm work.* Korea raised its E-8 seasonal intake to 109,000 for 2026 from 96,000 [KA0010]. BOESL's managing director said in April 2026 that BOESL had given Korean companies US$1,200 each to take seasonal workers, and had asked Korea to open 104 job sectors to Bangladeshis [C0140].

- *Fewer E-9 places.* The 2026 E-9 cut to 80,000 shrinks the pool all 17 sending countries compete for [C0037, C0004].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

An EPS job starts with a Korean employer's request to a Korean job centre; it reaches Bangladesh only as a profile pulled from HRD Korea's roster, and the choke point is the employer's choice, which BOESL says it does not control [C0020, C0014]. Only BOESL may send EPS workers, so brokers sell promises instead: work visas and test passes, for up to Tk 10 lakh [C0012, C0115].

#### The chain, by rule

1. Korea sets the E-9 quota each year, by sector [C0034].
2. BOESL registers candidates; HRD Korea sets the EPS-TOPIK, charging US$28 per candidate in all 17 sending countries (it passes US$8 of this to the sending agency), and BOESL adds a Tk 500 registration fee [C0004, C0005].
3. Those who pass the Korean test take a skills and competency test; BOESL sends the list of passers to HRD Korea, which registers them on the roster [C0020, C0001].
4. A Korean employer's request goes through the Ministry of Employment and Labor to its job centres, which show the employer three profiles at random for each worker wanted; the employer picks one [C0020, C0014].
5. A rostered candidate who is not picked within the roster period (two years) is deleted automatically and must pass the Korean, skills and medical tests again [C0014, C0018].
6. When submitting the job application, every candidate signs an affidavit on Tk 300 stamp paper that passing the test and being rostered does not guarantee a job in Korea [C0007].

Employers choose from the pool by nationality, age and physique as well as scores [C0043]. Bangladeshi workers go mainly to small and medium firms, not large companies, and BOESL has six representatives in Korea who keep in touch with employers [C0021, C0022].

#### The roster backlog

A candidate waiting since 2024 told Bonik Barta in September 2025 that about 1,700 people rostered after passing in 2022 had reached the end of their two years without going, and that more than 7,400 passed in 2024 [C0019]. On 14 May 2026 people who had passed but were never sent protested at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, saying about 12,000 from the 2022 and 2023 batches had been deleted, alleging irregularities and bribery by some BOESL officials, and demanding an "auto-roster" and a halt to new circulars [C0112, C0113]. They said they would pay any official cost to be sent [C0114]. In April 2026 BOESL's managing director said BOESL had asked Korea to take candidates whose certificates had expired [C0140].

#### Where dalals still get in

- *Exam centres.* Brokers wait where candidates gather: on 9 October 2026 the Special Response Battalion (SRB-4), acting on complaints from BOESL and the Bangladesh-Korea Technical Training Centre (BKTTC), arrested 12 people at Technical Mor, Dhaka, who it said took Tk 40,000 to Tk 10 lakh from candidates gathering for the Friday and Saturday tests at BKTTC, promising work visas and test passes; a mobile court jailed seven for seven days to three months and fined five [C0115, C0116, KA0008]. SRB-4 said BOESL takes no fee from candidates in its recruitment process, and that BKTTC only trains and neither sends workers nor arranges jobs [KA0006, KA0007].
- *Coaching centres.* BMET's own four-month Korean course costs Tk 1,000 to join, and Korean is taught at 40 government technical training centres [C0133, C0083]. Private language centres also teach for the test; the director of one in Meherpur said many candidates' certificates expire before they get a visa [C0144]. BOESL says it will take legal action against any recruiting agent, language school or middleman that sends workers outside BOESL [C0012].
- *"Korea visa" sellers.* Only BOESL may send EPS workers, so any agency or person offering an EPS job for money is outside the scheme (to check an offer, see Done to workers) [C0012].
- *E-7 and other skilled visas.* These go through private recruiting agencies, and the migration cost is about two months' salary, around Tk 5 lakh [C0117, C0118].

#### Cheating on the test

A Korean business paper reported in September 2025 that proxy-exam firms on Telegram offered to sit the EPS-TOPIK for candidates in countries including Bangladesh for KRW 2.2 million (2 million with a discount), with a KRW 500,000 deposit paid in gift vouchers or the Tether stablecoin, and claimed to bribe local exam supervisors [C0039, C0040]. HRD Korea does not publish how much cheating it detects [C0040]. In 2020 Korea caught 52 Nepalis who had used proxies and 6 proxy takers, and deported 51 [C0042]. BOESL's rule is that anyone caught cheating loses the test and is barred for three years [C0010]. The number of EPS-TOPIK takers across all countries rose from about 30,000 in 2021 to about 490,000 in 2023 and 430,000 in 2024 [C0041].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The legal EPS route costs Tk 4,000 for the test, the charges in BOESL's cost table (US$240 in total) once selected, and the air ticket (details in Who pays what) [C0004, C0081, C0082]. A Korean minimum-wage job pays KRW 2,156,880 a month in 2026 before overtime [C0080].

#### The official cost

BOESL's cost table, in which the South Korea row is still marked as needing Board approval, gives a total of US$240 but lists items that add up to Tk 38,584, including a Tk 3,500 welfare fee that rose to Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 (see Who pays what) [C0081, R0038]. In 2022 BOESL put the total at Tk 33,524, and the worker also pays the air ticket both ways [C0104, C0082]. BOESL also takes a refundable security deposit, at a rate the ministry approved on HRD Korea's advice, so that workers keep to their contracts and come home on time; in 2024 it was reported at Tk 1 lakh per worker [C0031, C0025].

#### What people really spend

The real cost is time and the risk of never being picked. A diploma engineer who quit his job in 2021 to learn Korean passed in 2023, waited two years without a visa, and saw his certificate expire in 2025 [C0141]. People who pay brokers have lost up to Tk 10 lakh [C0115]. Skilled E-7 workers who go through private agencies pay around Tk 5 lakh [C0118].

#### What the job pays

- *Minimum wage.* KRW 10,320 an hour in 2026, KRW 2,156,880 a month on 209 hours; KRW 10,700 an hour (KRW 2,236,300 a month) from January 2027 [C0080, C0124].
- *Typical pay.* A 2024 survey of 1,225 small manufacturers found foreign workers averaged KRW 3.02 million a month including KRW 386,000 of accommodation: base pay KRW 2.09 million and overtime KRW 425,000 [C0130]. In 2025, 68.9% of E-9 workers earned under KRW 3 million a month [C0128].
- *What workers say.* Bangladeshis in Korea say the minimum is about Tk 2 lakh a month and over Tk 3 lakh with overtime, and most firms provide food and housing [C0143].
- *Hours.* 8 a day and 40 a week, with up to 12 hours' overtime a week [C0068]. Farm work is excluded from the limits on hours, weekly rest and breaks [C0098].

#### What comes off

The employer may deduct housing and food only within caps: at most 20% of the monthly ordinary wage for housing and meals together (13% if the housing is a temporary structure), or 15% for housing alone (8% for a temporary structure) (see "Housing, and deductions for accommodation and food") [C0078]. HRD Korea's guidance allows a housing deduction even when the housing is a vinyl greenhouse or container [C0079].

#### Severance and pension

The employer must pay 8.3% of the worker's monthly ordinary wage each month into departure guarantee insurance with Samsung Fire & Marine, starting within 15 days of the contract [C0075, C0076]. After at least a year's continuous work the worker claims it on going home; if it is less than the legal severance pay, the employer must pay the difference [C0067]. The worker can check the expected payout on the EPS website or app [C0077]. E-9 workers can also claim back their National Pension contributions as a lump sum when they leave [C0136].

#### Remittances from Korea

Bangladesh Bank recorded US$135.46 million of remittances from South Korea in 2021-22, US$118.69 million in 2022-23 and US$16.76 million in July to September 2026, 14th among source countries [C0102, C0101]. (Legal channels are under Sending money home.)

### What ties you to the employer

You are tied to one employer for most of your stay: the E-9 visa lets you work only at the workplace in the contract you signed before entry, and you may move only for reasons the law lists [C0061, C0070].

- **Entering.** The employer chooses you from the roster and the contract names one workplace [C0014, C0061].
- **Renewing.** You may work three years from entry; the employer decides whether to apply for re-employment, which adds once up to 1 year 10 months, 4 years 10 months in all [C0063].
- **Quitting or changing employer.** Allowed only if the employer ends or refuses to renew the contract; or the business closes or is suspended, its permit is cancelled, the dormitory breaks the law, or the employer breaks labour conditions or treats you unfairly; or an injury makes the work impossible [C0070]. At most three changes in the first three years and two in the re-employment period, though moves for closure, violations or unfair treatment do not count [C0069, C0071]. You must apply within one month of the contract ending and get the change approved within three months, or leave Korea; the job centre recommends employers in your region of Korea, and you cannot choose a specific one (see How many changes, and for what reasons) [C0073, C0072, C0074]. You also need the immigration office's permission [C0103].
- **Leaving and coming back.** After leaving, an E-9 worker must wait six months to return under EPS; a re-employed worker whose employer applied before he left may return after one month, and one who left before his stay ended may return after six months by passing a special Korean test (see Coming back: the sincere worker re-entry and the Special EPS-TOPIK) [C0064, C0065].
- **Running away.** Leaving the employer without approval or staying past the permit makes you undocumented: you can be removed and face up to three years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million (see Overstaying and losing status) [C0066]. In January to September 2026, 4,091 E-9 workers newly became undocumented, 53.4% of all new cases among work-visa holders [C0048].

BOESL discourages job changes and holds a deposit for this reason; Bangladeshi workers say many still move for more overtime, or because the work is hard or the food unfamiliar [C0025, C0142].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The top problems are being stuck with an employer you cannot leave, injury, unpaid wages and bad housing; for people still in Bangladesh, the top problem is waiting on a roster that never calls [C0093, C0086, C0088, C0089, C0019].

#### What goes wrong

- *Stuck with the employer.* Because a move needs the employer's consent or proof of a legal reason, workers who ask to leave have faced wage theft, reduced work and even criminal charges, the Business and Human Rights Centre reported in September 2025 [C0093]. In the same report, a Bangladeshi injured at a Pocheon factory in 2024 said his boss told him not to report the accident and refused him a transfer [C0094].
- *Injury and death.* 77 foreign workers died in work accidents in Korea in 2025 and 102 in 2024, a year that included the Hwaseong (Aricell) battery-factory fire that killed 18 migrants [C0134, C0087]. A 2024 study for Korea's human rights commission found migrant workers' accident death rate consistently 1.7 times Koreans' [C0086]. Among all workers in Korea in 2025, falls were the biggest killer, and 40.6% of accident deaths were at workplaces with fewer than five staff [C0135, KA0013]. BOESL has paid Tk 20,10,500 from its own fund to 17 sick or injured EPS returnees, including Tk 1 lakh to a worker who lost a limb [C0032].
- *Unpaid wages.* Wages owed to migrant workers have averaged over KRW 100 billion a year since 2019, and foreign workers reported about 10,000 wage cases in 2024 (see If you aren't paid) [C0088, C0092].
- *Housing.* A 2021 labour ministry survey found nearly half of migrant workers, and 70% in farming, housed in non-residential accommodation [C0089]. A Cambodian farm worker was found dead in December 2020 in an unheated vinyl-greenhouse dormitory in Pocheon; in September 2025 a court ordered the state to pay the family KRW 20 million for failing to supervise such housing [C0126, C0127]. Korea told a UN committee in April 2025 that new employment permits were no longer being issued for such housing [C0090].
- *Farm work.* Amnesty International's 2014 report "Bitter Harvest" found EPS farm workers working over 10 hours a day, 28 days a month, about 50 hours a month more than their contracts, mostly unpaid, and half illegally hired out to other farms in the November to February off season [C0097, C0099].

#### Beliefs against the facts

*"Pass the test and you will go to Korea."* Passing puts you on a roster for two years; employers choose 1 in 3 profiles, and BOESL makes every candidate sign that rostering does not guarantee a job [C0014, C0018, C0007]. About 12,000 from the 2022 and 2023 batches were deleted without going [C0112].

*"A broker or agency can get you an EPS visa."* Only BOESL may send EPS workers, and the employer, not anyone in Bangladesh, picks the worker [C0012, C0020]. People promised visas and passes have paid up to Tk 10 lakh [C0115].

*"You can pay to pass the Korean test."* Proxy-exam sellers exist, but a cheater loses the result and is barred for three years in Bangladesh, and Korea has deported workers who used proxies [C0039, C0010, C0042].

*"Korea pays Tk 2 to 3 lakh a month."* That needs overtime: the 2026 minimum is KRW 2,156,880 a month, and 68.9% of E-9 workers earned under KRW 3 million in 2025 [C0143, C0080, C0128].

*"Once there, you can change jobs whenever the pay is better."* Moves are allowed only for listed reasons, at most three in three years, within your region [C0069, C0070, C0072].

#### How the irregular routes end

- *Overstaying or running away.* Removal, and up to three years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine [C0066]. Of 397,522 undocumented foreigners in 2024, 134,580 had been registered workers, including E-9, who overstayed or lost status [C0095]. Undocumented workers who go to labour offices for help are still detained and deported [C0106]. A worker who has been illegally resident in Korea cannot sit the EPS test again [C0006].
- *Refugee claims.* Bangladeshis lodged 3,526 refugee claims in Korea from 1994 to 2022, 394 in 2024 and 468 in January to September 2026; Korea has recognised 2.7% of all claims it has decided (see Asylum) [C0050, C0051].
- *Proxy tests.* Deportation for those caught in Korea, and a test ban [C0042].
- *Seasonal workers who stay on.* In January to September 2026, 1,616 E-8 seasonal workers of all nationalities newly became undocumented, 21.1% of new cases among work-visa holders [C0100].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Visa-free entry and K-ETA:** K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization, an online pre-travel approval) is only for nationals of visa-free countries, and Bangladesh is not on Korea's visa-waiver list or the K-ETA list, so a Bangladeshi passport holder needs a visa for every visit [V0105, V0101, V0102]. Without a K-ETA or a visa an airline will not board you [V0103]. A website selling a Bangladeshi a K-ETA is a scam: fake K-ETA sites charge over USD 100, against a real fee of KRW 10,000 [V0104].
- **Visa-free transit (B-2):** foreigners holding a valid US, Canadian, Australian, New Zealand or one of 32 European visas can normally enter Korea in transit for up to 30 days without a Korean visa, but Bangladesh is one of 23 nationalities excluded, so Bangladeshis need a visa even with a US or Canadian visa or permanent residence (see Transit) [V0106, V0107, V0108].
- **Jeju visa-free entry and the Incheon transit tour:** the Incheon airport transit-tour entry (up to 3 days) excludes the 23 nationalities not granted visa-free entry to Jeju [V0109].
- **Working Holiday (H-1):** Korea has working-holiday agreements with 30 countries and regions (from Andorra to the USA), and Bangladesh is not one of them [V0112]. Applicants must also be 18 to 30 and apply from their own country [V0113].
- **Seasonal farm work (C-4) sold in Dhaka:** the Korean Embassy in Dhaka said in December 2023 that it had never issued an agricultural (C-4-1-4) visa in Bangladesh, and that "visa issuance certificates" for it sold by agencies were fake (for the real route, see E-8 seasonal agricultural work) [V0072].

## Work visa: EPS (E-9 non-professional employment)

The Employment Permit System (EPS) is Korea's government-to-government scheme for hiring foreign workers into small and medium factories, construction, farming, fishing and some services when Korean employers cannot find Korean staff; the worker enters on an E-9 ("non-professional employment") visa [R0161, R0155, R0205]. Bangladesh is one of the 17 EPS sending countries and has sent workers under its 2007 MoU (memorandum of understanding) since 2008, 38,018 of them by August 2026 [R0205, R0194]. Under the MoU only BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the only government organisation under the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare that sends workers abroad) may send workers to Korea under EPS: no private recruiting agency, language school or middleman has any part in it [R0094, KB0001]. On the Korean side the process is run by HRD Korea (Human Resources Development Service of Korea), which sets the Korean test and keeps the job roster, through its EPS Center in Dhaka [R0104, R0117].

The route has five filters, and each one drops people: a Korean language test (the EPS-TOPIK), a skills and competency test, a medical, a two-year wait on the "job roster" for a Korean employer to pick you, and the visa [R0073, R0098, R0099, R0162]. Passing does not guarantee a job: over five years Korea allotted Bangladesh quotas for 39,255 workers, employers sent job orders for 17,247 and BOESL sent 14,829 [R0070, R0227]. The whole selection, from registering for the test to getting on the roster, usually takes 10 to 12 months or more [R0096].

### Who pays what

#### What you pay

- **EPS-TOPIK registration:** Tk 4,000 (HRD Korea's US$28 test fee, about Tk 3,500, which covers the Korean test and the skills test, plus BOESL's Tk 500 online registration fee) and a Tk 46 bKash charge, paid through the bKash app; it is not refunded [R0068]. HRD Korea in turn pays the sending agency US$8 per candidate for test logistics [KB0002].
- **BOESL's charges after you are selected:** BOESL's cost table for South Korea EPS lists a service charge of Tk 22,560, VAT Tk 3,384, welfare fee Tk 3,500, pre-exit processing and training (PEPT) fee Tk 2,000, insurance Tk 490, smart card Tk 250, immigration clearance Tk 800, endorsement or visa processing fee Tk 5,400 and data entry Tk 200: Tk 38,584 in all, though the table's total column says US$240 [R0188]. In 2022 a BOESL deputy general manager put the total at Tk 33,524 [R0189].
- **BMET's fees within that, from 1 October 2026:** the Wage Earners' Welfare Board welfare fee is Tk 5,500, up from Tk 3,500 [R0038]; the migrant worker insurance premium has been Tk 1,000 since 12 March 2023 [R0035]; BMET's charter sets the immigration clearance fee at Tk 250 per worker, and source income tax on group visas at Tk 800 for unskilled and Tk 1,200 for skilled workers [R0039].
- **Air tickets both ways:** paid by the worker [R0189].
- **In Korea, return cost insurance:** KRW 500,000 for Bangladeshis, paid within 3 months of arrival, and paid back to you when you leave (Step 12, item 4) [R0174, E0090, R0175].
- **In Korea, casualty insurance:** a one-off KRW 15,600 for the contract period (Step 12, item 4) [E0095].
- **Passport and police clearance:** your own cost (Step 2 and Step 7) [R0009, R0247].

#### How to pay BOESL

BOESL takes no service charge or fee until you are finally selected and have passed the medical, and then only by bank pay order; it takes no cash and has no agent, sub-agent or representative [R0110]. The only fee before that is the Tk 4,000 test registration, paid by bKash with your own Submission ID (Step 4) [R0075].

### Step 1: Check you qualify

You can register only if all of these are true [R0071, R0072, R0133]:
- you are 18 to 39 years old (new applicants in the 2026 round had to be born 15 July 1986 to 14 July 2008) [R0067];
- you have passed the SSC (Secondary School Certificate) or equivalent;
- you can read, write and understand basic Korean;
- your name, date of birth and photo match on your passport and NID (national ID card);
- you are not colour-blind;
- you are willing to do "3D" (dirty, difficult and dangerous) work;
- you have no criminal conviction, are not a drug user and do not have syphilis;
- you have never stayed in Korea illegally or been deported from Korea, and are not barred from leaving Bangladesh;
- you have not stayed in Korea more than 5 years in total on E-9 or E-10 visas.

Bangladeshis go mainly into manufacturing: food, paper, plastics, electrical and electronics, wood, metal, machinery, textiles and garments [R0191]. The 2026 test round was for manufacturing only: 20,000 candidates could sit the Korean test, to put 2,000 on the roster [R0065]. Korea also lists Bangladesh as a sending country for E-9 construction and E-9 fishing [R0206].

**How big the chance is.** Korea's whole E-9 quota for 2026 is 80,000 across all 17 countries [R0232]. Bangladesh gets about 4,000 places in an average year and got 8,000 in 2026 [R0105]. Korea shares the quota among countries each year on how each country's sending agency performs, how many of its workers stay in their jobs and how many stay illegally [R0095]. In 2024 Bangladesh sent 2,779 workers against a quota of 11,700, and in 2025 only 1,244 by September against 10,300 [R0228]. BMET cleared 1,901 workers for South Korea in 2025 [R0233].

Apply online for an e-passport exactly as on your NID; applicants over 20 must use the NID [R0005, R0006]. A 48-page e-passport for 10 years costs Tk 5,750 (regular), Tk 8,050 (express) or Tk 10,350 (super express), and for 5 years Tk 4,025, Tk 6,325 or Tk 8,625, VAT included; pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay or at a bank [R0009, R0008].    2. Pay the Tk 200 government fee by bKash, Nagad or another mobile-banking service and download your registration card [R0001, R0032].
   3. Take 2 copies of the registration slip to your DEMO (district employment and manpower office) to give your fingerprints [R0002].

   **If it fails:** OEP hotline 09614445271, or Probash-Bondhu, the 24-hour call centre of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [R0003, R0004].

You'll need [R0074, R0076, R0068]:

### Step 8: Wait for an employer, and how employers choose

**How the job reaches you.** A Korean employer must first try for 7 days to hire Koreans through its local Job Center (Korea's public employment office) [R0159]. It then applies for an employment permit and is shown 3 roster candidates for each vacancy: either the Job Center refers them, or the employer picks, interviews and hires from 3 per vacancy on the EPS website [R0160]. Candidates are put in front of employers by random selection at that 1:3 ratio, and the employer chooses 1 of the 3 [R0063].

**What the employer looks at:** your photo, age, height, weight, work experience, skills, physical fitness and the video taken at your skills test [R0100]. Those high on the merit list tend to be noticed first [R0107]. A 2025 Korean press investigation found employers also weigh preferred nationality, age and physique [R0199]. In a World Bank study of 2011 to 2015 hiring, Bangladesh ranked seventh among sending countries in employers' preferences [R0231].

**No one can get you picked.** Whether, whom, when and from which country to hire is entirely the employer's decision; no person, organisation, sending agency or third party can intervene [R0100]. In Korea, anyone other than the Job Center who intervenes in selecting or hiring EPS workers faces up to 1 year in prison or a fine of up to KRW 10 million [R0158].

**If no employer picks you.** Korea keeps up to three times the quota on the roster: for a quota of 1,000, 3,000 are rostered and 2,000 will be deleted [R0106]. So about two-thirds of rostered candidates are likely to be dropped when the 2 years run out [R0106]. A deleted candidate must pass the Korean test and the other stages again [R0099]. In April 2026 HRD Korea refused to restore deleted Bangladeshis to the roster or set a special cut-off for them, but let eligible deleted workers sit the next test without the lottery [R0115, R0116].

### Step 9: The Standard Labour Contract: read it before you agree

When the employer gets the permit, the conditions it wrote become a Standard Labour Contract (SLC), Korea's fixed-form employment contract, sent through HRD Korea to BOESL [R0161]. BOESL contacts you to confirm you agree, then returns it electronically and the contract is concluded [R0161, R0153]. Any correction must be approved by HRD Korea [R0161].

**Read these lines before you agree:**
- **Wage:** at least Korea's minimum wage, KRW 10,320 an hour in 2026, paid at least once a month on a set payday [R0242].
- **Hours:** 8 a day and 40 a week, with at most 12 hours' overtime a week [R0243].
- **Probation:** at most 3 months; during it the hourly minimum wage can be cut by up to 10%, and the pay agreed in the contract cannot be lowered afterwards [R0186].
- **Housing and food:** the employer is not obliged to pay for them and may charge you, but only up to the amount written in the contract [KB0004]. Korea's guidance caps the charge at 20% of monthly ordinary wage for housing and food in a house or flat (13% in temporary housing such as containers or vinyl greenhouses), or 15% for housing alone (8% temporary) [R0239, R0241]. Taking the money out of your wage in advance needs a separate written consent in your own language that matches the contract [R0240].
- **Start date:** the contract takes effect on the day you enter Korea [R0168].

BOESL's leaflet says an EPS job pays about Tk 2 lakh a month and allows up to nine and a half years' work over two terms [R0107].

4. **The four insurances.** The EPS law requires four insurances just for foreign workers, on top of Korea's social insurances; all are signed during training [KC0018, R0167]. Departure guarantee, return cost and casualty insurance are run by Samsung Fire & Marine, and wage guarantee insurance by SGI Seoul Guarantee [E0084].
   - **Departure guarantee insurance (출국만기보험, employer):** taken out within 15 days of the contract taking effect, 8.3% of your monthly ordinary wage every month, as your severance pay [KC0004, R0225]. An employer who does not take it out within 15 days faces a fine of up to KRW 5 million [E0098]. It is paid to you only if you worked at least 1 year at the same workplace and leave Korea (not on a temporary trip); if you leave before 1 year, it goes to the employer [R0170]. If it is less than legal severance pay, the employer must pay the difference [R0172].
   - **Wage guarantee insurance (보증보험, employer):** compulsory for employers with fewer than 300 regular workers or not covered by the Wage Claim Guarantee Act; it pays up to KRW 4,000,000 of unpaid wages (to claim, see If you aren't paid) [KC0002, E0028].
   - **Return cost insurance (귀국비용보험, you):** KRW 500,000 for Bangladeshis, who fall in the "other countries" group (KRW 400,000 for China, the Philippines, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam, KRW 600,000 for Sri Lanka), paid within 3 months of entry, in one go or up to 3 instalments, into the bank account opened during training [R0174, E0090]. Not taking it out can bring a fine of up to KRW 5 million [E0149]. It is paid back into your own account before you leave, with a little interest after a year, and to your family if you die [R0175].
   - **Casualty insurance (상해보험, you):** a one-off KRW 15,600, paid within 15 days of the contract taking effect, for the contract period [E0095, R0176]. It pays only for death or disability from causes not related to work, not for ordinary hospital treatment: up to KRW 30 million for death or permanent disability from a non-work accident and KRW 15 million for death or severe disability from illness; unused premium is refunded when you leave [E0096, R0176, R0177].
5. **To the workplace.** If the health check is clear and training is complete, the employer collects you from the training institution [R0168].

**Claiming the insurances when you leave:** follow the checklist in Coming home (see Before you leave Korea) [R0171].

### Step 13: Alien registration card

Anyone staying more than 90 days must register within 90 days of entry at the immigration office for their place of residence and get a residence (alien registration) card, the ARC (외국인등록증) [R0219, E0127]. You'll need the application form, your passport, a 3.5 x 4.5 cm colour photo, the documents for your status and KRW 35,000; book a visit on HiKorea (www.hikorea.go.kr) first [R0219, R0220]. The card comes in about four weeks, in person or by post [R0220]. Carry it at all times; it is your ID for opening a bank account, and registration also enrols you in health insurance [R0221, E0039, E0035].

Report a change of address within 15 days of moving [E0060]. Not registering is a ground for deportation and can bring up to 1 year in prison or a KRW 10 million fine [KC0008, KC0012, KC0010].

### Coming back: the sincere worker re-entry and the Special EPS-TOPIK

**Sincere worker (성실근로자) re-entry.** Korea started the "sincere worker" re-entry system on 2 July 2012 to stop workers overstaying and keep skilled ones [R0178]. An E-9 worker whose 4 years 10 months have run out can, at the employer's request, leave Korea and come back after 1 month to the same workplace [R0182].

You qualify if [R0183]:
- you stayed at one workplace until the 4 years 10 months ran out, or changed workplace within the same industry or for reasons not your fault and have at least a 1-year contract with your last employer;
- your total stay on E-9 is under 5 years (6 if extended);
- you have a contract of at least 1 year from the day you return;
- the business is in a sector where Koreans are hard to hire [E0150].

1. You and the employer sign the contract [R0185].
2. The employer applies 90 to 7 days before your re-employment period ends, with your signed consent, the standard contract, copies of your alien registration card and passport, and your departure notice [R0185].
3. The employment permit is issued; you leave Korea [R0185].
4. After 1 month you return in a group under HRD Korea's entry plan, take the health check and insurance, and go back to the employer [R0185]. You skip the Korean test, skills test and employment training [R0184].
5. Back in Bangladesh, within a week, e-mail BOESL the arrival-report form and a colour passport scan (JPEG, at most 200 KB) to committedeps@gmail.com, with the reference and registration numbers from BOESL's list in the subject [R0132].

It is allowed only once and gives another 3 years plus 1 year 10 months [R0184].

**Special EPS-TOPIK, for returnees without an employer.** Workers who left Korea voluntarily within their permitted stay (on or after 1 January 2010) can sit a separate Special EPS-TOPIK; if HRD Korea finds you did not leave voluntarily, your test is void whatever your score [R0137]. Those who worked more than a year at their last workplace and test for the same industry can be placed directly with a job ("designated job placement") [R0136]. Passers are chosen from those scoring 120 or more, by each country's quota [R0138]. In 2026 HRD Korea listed 936 Bangladeshi returnees; they registered on brms.boesl.gov.bd on 10 and 11 March for Tk 3,916 (US$28 test fee, Tk 3,416, plus Tk 500), sat the test on 8 May and got results on 8 June [R0144, R0147]. Returnees who took HRD Korea's resettlement support training paid no test fee and could get up to 25 extra points, and those who came home after seasonal work got 10 [R0145, R0146].

### E-8 seasonal agricultural work

The E-8 visa lets Korean local governments (counties and cities) hire foreign workers legally for a short period in the busy farming and fishing seasons [R0252, R0217]. Korea's Ministry of Justice shares out seasonal workers to local governments twice a year, judging each one's capacity to manage them and to prevent human-rights abuses and absconding [R0217, R0218].

**Is Bangladesh a source?** Yes, through MoUs that BOESL runs:

- In March 2026 BOESL recruited 20 workers (16 men, 4 women) for farms in Hampyeong County under an MoU between the Ministry and the county, for 3 to 8 months at KRW 10,030 an hour [R0125, R0126].

1. As for EPS, colour-blind people, drug users, people with syphilis or a criminal punishment, people who stayed illegally in Korea and people barred from leaving Bangladesh were excluded [KB0005].
2. Applications ran on brms.boesl.gov.bd from 10 am to 3:30 pm on 16 March 2026, first come, first served, and closed automatically at 300 registrations for 20 places [R0125]. Register, verify with the OTP sent to your phone, log in, complete your profile (personal details, passport, work experience with the certificate as a PDF, family) and pay the Tk 100 fee by mobile banking [R0131, R0126].

The cheques are used if the worker avoids the job or overstays, and are returned when the worker comes home [R0129]. The guarantors need not hold Tk 5 lakh in their accounts, and BOESL takes all money from selected workers by pay order [KB0006].

### E-10 seafarers on coastal fishing boats

Foreign fishers enter Korea by boat size: boats under 20 tonnes, fish farms and salt farms hire through EPS on an E-9 visa (run by the Ministry of Employment and Labor and HRD Korea), while boats of 20 tonnes or more hire through the "foreign seafarer system" on an E-10-2 visa (run by the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries and Suhyup) [R0208, R0211].

**Is Bangladesh a source country?** For E-9 fishing, yes: Korea lists Bangladesh as a specialised sending country for E-9 fishing, and the 2026 BOESL notice mentions Bangladeshis deleted from the fishing roster [R0206, R0066]. For E-10, Suhyup's 2023 report names Indonesia, Vietnam, China and Myanmar as the source countries, not Bangladesh [R0209].

**How E-10 recruits.** E-10 runs on private contracts: Suhyup with Korean management firms, and those firms with sending companies abroad, which recruit people with boat experience or fisheries schooling; EPS instead recruits through government agencies on Korean test scores [R0210].

**The risks.** Suhyup sets a cap on what E-10 sending firms may charge (US$4,900, about KRW 7 million), but Korea has in effect no law regulating these fees [R0213]. In 2026 an Indonesian E-10 fisher told Newstapa he paid about KRW 13 million, half as an "escape deposit" against running away [R0212]. He described working 3 am to 9 pm, about five hours' sleep, up to 40 days at sea with no contact home, and no life jacket [R0215]. In 2025, 9,517 foreigners worked on Korean coastal boats of 20 tonnes or more, over half the crew [R0214].

**The scams.** Suhyup recruits E-10 fishers from Indonesia, Vietnam, China and Myanmar, not Bangladesh, so an "E-10 fishing visa" sold in Bangladesh is not a listed route; an E-9 fishing job comes only through BOESL and the EPS-TOPIK [R0209, R0094].

**Where to get help:** to report a fishing-job offer made in BOESL's name, call BOESL on 02-48319125 or 01765411653, 9 am to 4 pm, or Probash Bondhu, 16135 [R0113].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

**Fake EPS jobs and "Korea visa" sales.** In June 2026 BOESL warned that people were using its name in social-media adverts to cheat people with jobs in South Korea, Russia, Jordan, Malaysia and other countries [R0111]. BOESL posts jobs only on www.boesl.gov.bd and facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd and takes applications only on brms.boesl.gov.bd (and eps.boesl.gov.bd for the test) [R0112, R0075]. Selling or buying a visa inside Bangladesh, or getting one by illegal means, is a crime under Bangladesh's 2013 Act: 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 3 lakh [R0023, R0024]. **Spot it:** BOESL has no agent or sub-agent, takes no cash, and charges nothing beyond the test fee until you are selected and medically fit [R0110].

**Paying to be "picked" by an employer.** **Spot it:** the employer alone chooses, from 3 candidates the system shows it, and no one can intervene (Step 8) [R0100, R0063]. A BOESL official said in 2025 that the whole process is controlled by Korea [R0230].

**EPS-TOPIK proxy test-takers.** In 2025 a proxy-exam company on Telegram offered to sit the EPS-TOPIK for a foreigner and forge ID for KRW 2.2 million, with a KRW 500,000 deposit in mobile gift vouchers or Tether, saying it works in all EPS countries including Bangladesh [R0196, R0195]. Such firms advertised in 2025 that they bribe local exam supervisors; HRD Korea says it appoints local examiners fairly [R0197]. **Spot it:** proxy test-taking is listed cheating, and the person who orders it is caught too: in 2020, 51 of 52 Nepalese who ordered proxy tests were deported from Korea [R0140, R0198].

**Coaching centres that "guarantee" a pass or a visa.** In October 2026 SRB-4 (Special Response Battalion-4) arrested 12 members of a ring at Technical Mor, Dhaka, after complaints from BKTTC (Bangladesh-Korea Technical Training Centre) and BOESL; SRB-4 said they took Tk 40,000 to Tk 10 lakh per person for promised cheap work visas and passes in the written test, and a mobile court jailed 7 and fined 5 [R0222, R0223]. **Spot it:** BKTTC trains in 24 trades but does not send workers or arrange jobs [R0224]; the test questions come from an unpublished question bank [R0087].

**"We can put you back on the roster."** **Spot it:** HRD Korea does not reinstate deleted names; a deleted candidate must sit the test again (Step 8) [R0115, R0099].

**Bribes for the medical.** **Spot it:** report anyone at the Civil Surgeon's office who asks for unofficial money (Step 7, item 1) [R0123].

#### Tried by workers

- **Sending someone else to sit the test, or swapping names and numbers:** this is cheating, and HRD Korea bars cheaters from applying for 4 years [R0140, R0139]. Anyone caught using unfair means at a Bangladesh test has the test cancelled and is barred from the next test [R0090, R0118].

- **Not leaving Korea when the stay ends:** people who stayed illegally in Korea can never re-register, and a Special EPS-TOPIK result is void if you did not leave voluntarily [R0072, R0137]. An E-8 worker who overstays puts his guarantors' Tk 5 lakh cheques at risk [R0129].

### Your rights at work

#### Which laws protect you

E-9 (Non-Professional Employment) workers are hired under the Employment Permit System (EPS), the government-to-government scheme run by Korea's Ministry of Employment and Labor (MOEL) [E0008]. Three laws matter most:

- **The Labor Standards Act (근로기준법).** Korea's main labour law, covering every workplace with 5 or more regular workers [E0107]. Foreign workers get the same hours, breaks and leave as Koreans [E0006].
- **The Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers (외국인고용법).** The EPS law: it sets your work period, workplace changes, the four EPS insurances and the employer's duties [E0009, E0066, E0055]. It bans unfair discrimination against you for being a foreign worker [E0145].
- **The Minimum Wage Act.** You must be paid at least the minimum wage, at least once a month on a fixed payday [E0053].

**Your contract.** The employer must use the standard employment contract, made in two copies, and give one copy to you [E0161]. Keep it for the whole job: it is the proof for every claim below. The contract takes effect on the day you enter Korea [E0162].

**What the employer can never do.** No employer may force you to work through violence, threats, confinement or other unjust restraint [E0109]. No employer may hit you, for any reason [E0110].

#### Pay: the minimum wage

The minimum wage for 2026 is KRW 10,320 an hour, KRW 290 (2.9%) more than in 2025 [E0001, E0053]. For a 209-hour month that is KRW 2,156,880 a month [E0001]. In 2027 it rises 3.7% to KRW 10,700 an hour, or KRW 2,236,300 a month on a 40-hour week and 209 hours [C0124].

**How you must be paid.** Wages must be paid in money, directly to you and in full, at least once a month on a fixed date [E0112]. Deductions are allowed only where a law or collective agreement allows them [E0112].

> **Need to know**

#### Hours, overtime, night and holiday pay

- **Normal hours:** 8 hours a day and 40 a week [E0054].
- **Overtime:** up to 12 hours a week, by agreement [E0054, E0113]. Every overtime hour is paid at least 50% extra on your ordinary wage [E0115].
- **Night work (10 pm to 6 am):** at least 50% extra [E0117].
- **Holiday work:** at least 50% extra for up to 8 hours, and 100% extra beyond 8 hours [E0116].
- **Breaks:** 30 minutes for 4 hours of work, 1 hour for 8 hours [E0002].

#### Rest days and leave

- **Weekly rest day:** at least one paid day off a week on average [E0114]; a worker with full attendance in the week is entitled to it [E0005].
- **Annual leave:** 15 paid days a year after a year with at least 80% attendance [E0003]. In your first year, or with under 80% attendance, you get 1 paid day for each full month worked [E0004]. After 3 years you gain 1 extra day every 2 years, up to 25 days [E0118].

#### The 5-employee exception and the farm and fishing exception

**Workplaces with 4 or fewer workers.** The Labor Standards Act applies in full only to workplaces with 5 or more regular workers; for 4 or fewer, only the parts listed in a presidential decree apply [E0107, E0108]. Severance (retirement pay) is owed even at workplaces with 4 or fewer workers, in full since 1 January 2013 [E0104]. Choose the right workplace size ("4 or fewer" or "5 or more") when you check your severance in the EPS app (Before you leave Korea, item 2) [E0201].

**Farms, livestock and fishing.** The Labor Standards Act's rules on working hours, breaks and holidays do not apply to farming, livestock or fisheries work [E0119, KC0001, E0120]. So the weekly overtime limit, the break rules and the weekly rest day in this section do not bind farm, livestock or fishing employers [E0119, E0120].

#### Housing, and deductions for accommodation and food

**What the law requires.** If the employer gives you a dormitory, it must meet the Labor Standards Act standards and keep you healthy and safe [KC0014, E0123]. Before you sign the contract the employer must tell you the dormitory's structure and facilities, location, living environment and floor area, and tell you again if any of this changes [E0146]. A dormitory that breaks these rules is a ground for changing workplace that does not count against your limit (see How many changes, and for what reasons) [E0068, E0070]. Even temporary housing must have a washroom and toilet in or near it, and men and women may not be housed in the same room [E0051].

**A tighter rule from 10 June 2027.** From that date the use recorded for the housing in the building register (or the register of temporary buildings) must be fit for living in, and the employer must also file the housing information with the job centre [E0147, E0148].

**Deduction caps.** The Human Resources Development Service of Korea (HRD Korea) guides employers to keep what they charge within these caps, as a share of your monthly ordinary wage [E0043]:

- **Housing and meals, in an apartment, house, multi-unit house or similar:** at most 20% [E0043].
- **Housing and meals, in other temporary housing (container, plastic greenhouse and the like):** at most 13% [E0044, E0052].
- **Housing only, apartment or similar:** at most 15% [E0045].
- **Housing only, temporary housing:** at most 8% [E0046].

**Rules on deducting.** The employer may charge only within the amount written in your standard employment contract [E0048]. To take it from your wages in advance, the employer needs a separate written consent in your own language [E0047]. If the amount deducted differs from the contract, it can count as unpaid wages under the Labor Standards Act [E0049]. Electricity, gas, heating and internet are not in the caps and are settled separately afterwards [E0050]. Rent may be charged even for a container or plastic greenhouse [E0052].

#### Health insurance and the other social insurances

- **National Health Insurance (NHI):** as an E-9 worker with alien registration, you are automatically a workplace subscriber [E0058, E0035]. The employer must report you to the National Health Insurance Service within 14 days of employment [E0034]. You and the employer each pay half of the premium, deducted from your wage [E0036].
- **Industrial accident insurance:** mandatory; you get the same compensation as a Korean for a work injury, illness, disability or death [E0007].
- **Employment insurance:** mandatory for the job-stability and training parts; the unemployment-benefit part is voluntary for E-9 workers [E0042].
- **National Pension:** applies only if your home country covers Korean nationals the same way [E0041]. Nationals of countries whose pension schemes do not cover Koreans are excluded [E0041]. If you are enrolled, as an E-9 worker you can claim your contributions back as a lump sum when you leave, whatever your nationality [C0136].

**The four EPS insurances.** On top of these, the EPS law requires departure guarantee, wage guarantee, return cost and casualty insurance just for foreign workers; what each costs, who pays and what it covers are under Step 12: Arrival, employment training and the four insurances [KC0018].

#### Your passport and alien registration card

No one, including your employer, may take your passport or alien registration card as security for an employment contract or a debt, or force or arrange that you hand it over [E0124]. Breaking this rule is a crime punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine [E0125, E0126]. A foreigner who breaks it can also be deported [KC0009].

#### Safety and the Serious Accidents Punishment Act

**Why it matters.** Migrant workers' rate of industrial accident deaths was 1.7 times that of Koreans in a 2024 study by Korea's National Human Rights Commission [E0197]. The June 2024 fire at the Aricell battery plant killed 18 migrant workers [E0198]. MOEL counted 77 foreign workers killed in work accidents in 2025, down from 102 in 2024, the year of that fire [C0134].

**The Serious Accidents Punishment Act (중대재해처벌법).** A "serious industrial accident" is one that kills at least one worker, or injures two or more in the same accident so that they need 6 months or more of treatment [E0155]. An owner or chief executive whose breach of safety duties causes a death faces at least 1 year in prison or a fine of up to KRW 1 billion [E0157]. The law has covered workplaces with fewer than 50 workers since January 2024, 3 years after it was promulgated on 26 January 2021, but not individually owned businesses with fewer than 5 workers [E0158, E0156]. Victims can claim damages of up to 5 times the loss for intentional or grossly negligent breaches [E0159].

**Employers who kill.** An employer punished under the Occupational Safety and Health Act for a foreign worker's death can be barred from hiring foreign workers for 3 years [E0205, E0206].

#### Bullying and harassment

Workplace harassment, meaning using a superior position to cause physical or mental suffering beyond the proper scope of work, is banned [E0121]. Anyone can report it to the employer, who must then investigate objectively without delay [E0122]. For the complaint route, see If you are abused or sexually harassed.

### Your stay

#### How long you can stay

- **First period:** you may work for up to 3 years from the day you enter [E0009].
- **Extension (re-employment):** if your employer applies to MOEL before the 3 years end, you can extend once by up to 1 year 10 months [E0010]. The employer files the application at the local job centre at least 7 days before your work period ends [E0153]. That makes 4 years 10 months in total [E0193].
- **Extension of stay:** after the work period is extended you must still get an extension of stay from immigration before your current stay expires [E0011]. Apply by the expiry date, by booking a visit on HiKorea (hikorea.go.kr) or online; the fee is KRW 60,000 [E0065, E0064].

#### Coming back for a second term

An E-9 worker who leaves Korea cannot be employed again under the EPS until 6 months after departure [E0061]. The two exceptions, the 1-month re-entry (재입국 특례) arranged by your employer and the special Korean test for those who leave early, are set out in full (see Coming back: the sincere worker re-entry and the Special EPS-TOPIK) [E0012, E0013].

#### The alien registration card

Registration, the card, address changes and the penalty for not registering are set out in Step 13: Alien registration card [E0127]. If your passport runs out while you are in Korea, the Bangladesh Embassy in Seoul issues a 48-page e-passport for KRW 150,000 (5 years) or KRW 187,000 (10 years), paid by bank or ATM, at the October to December 2026 rates [B0143].

#### Leaving and returning (holidays)

A registered E-9 worker who leaves Korea and comes back within 1 year (or before the stay ends, if that is sooner) does not need a re-entry permit [E0172]. A trip home does not end your departure guarantee insurance: it is paid only on a departure that is not temporary [E0163].

### What can get you jailed or deported

#### Overstaying and losing status

Staying after your permitted stay ends, without an extension, is a crime punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine, and you can be forcibly removed [E0014, E0040]. People who leave under a deportation order can be refused entry for 5 years [E0133].

**How E-9 workers become undocumented.** The most common route is the workplace-change deadline: if you do not apply for a new workplace within 1 month of your contract ending, or do not get a new workplace within 3 months of applying, you must leave, and the job centre sends the names to immigration every month [E0076, E0081]. The other route is leaving the workplace: the employer must report you to the job centre and immigration within 15 days if you are absent 5 days or more without approval, or if your whereabouts become unknown [E0160, E0130, E0154]. Korean law calls this leaving the workplace, 이탈 (i-tal) [E0167]. Of the 397,522 undocumented people in Korea in 2024, 134,580 had originally been registered workers, E-9 holders among them [E0200].

**Leaving voluntarily.** Someone who would be deported but leaves at their own expense can get a departure order (출국명령) instead of a deportation order [E0135]. How to report and leave, the fines and bans, and the special departure schemes are set out step by step (see Going home without valid papers) [E0178, E0177].

#### Working for another employer

You may work only at the workplace named for you; working anywhere else breaks the Immigration Act [E0129]. It can bring up to 1 year in prison or a KRW 10 million fine, and is a ground for deportation [KC0011, KC0010, E0131]. An employer who makes you work at a site other than the one in your contract can be barred from hiring foreign workers [E0170].

#### Drugs

Entry can be banned for drug addicts [E0134]. Drug money is also one of the funds that illegal remittance operators have been caught moving [E0184].

#### The entry ban

Entry can be banned for people who left under a deportation order less than 5 years ago, for drug addicts and for people with good reason to fear they would harm Korea's interests or public safety [E0133, E0134, KC0013].

#### If you aren't paid

Your employer must pay everything owed, including wages and compensation, within 14 days of your leaving, unless you both agree in writing to a later date [E0111]. Before you go home the employer must settle all wages and money owed; not doing so is punishable by up to 1 year in prison or a KRW 10 million fine [E0141, KC0007, E0143].

Claim in this order:

1. **File a petition (진정) with the labour office.** Do it online on the MOEL website (labor.moel.go.kr) or at the local employment and labour office covering your workplace, after counselling there [E0018]. You can ask either for payment (a petition) or for the employer to be punished (a criminal complaint) [E0017].
2. **The investigation.** A labour inspector calls you and the employer in and investigates [E0019]. If unpaid wages are confirmed, the inspector orders the employer to pay; if the employer refuses, the case goes to the prosecutor [E0019, E0063]. Ask the office for a confirmation of unpaid wages (금품체불확인원): you need it for every claim below [E0087].
3. **Claim the wage guarantee insurance.** If your employer's permit was cancelled for unpaid wages or you have changed workplace [KC0003], send SGI Seoul Guarantee the claim form, the confirmation of unpaid wages, a copy of your passport or ARC and a copy of your bank book; it pays up to KRW 4,000,000 [E0087, E0029].
4. **Claim substitute payment (대지급금) for the rest.** The state pays unpaid wages in place of the employer through the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service (KCOMWEL) [E0023]. The simplified version pays up to KRW 10 million at workplaces with industrial accident insurance [E0089]; ask the labour office's Labour Improvement Guidance Division (근로개선지도과) about it [E0087, E0088]. If you are still employed, the cap is KRW 7,000,000 for up to 3 months' wages, and you qualify only if your 3-month average ordinary wage is under 110% of the minimum wage and you have worked there at least 6 months; you must also file the petition and get the confirmation within 1 year of the latest unpaid wages, or a final court judgment within 2 years [E0026, E0024, E0025, KC0017, KC0016]. Claim with the labour office's confirmation or a court judgment [E0027].
5. **Go to court if needed.** You can attach the employer's assets, use the small-claims procedure for KRW 30,000,000 or less, or sue [E0020].

Unpaid wages are also a ground for changing workplace without it counting against your limit (see How many changes, and for what reasons) [E0068, E0070].

#### If you are injured at work

1. **Get treatment and keep records.** Industrial accident compensation covers medical costs, medicine, nursing and transport [E0031].
2. **Apply to KCOMWEL.** If you need at least 4 days of treatment for a work accident, apply for medical care benefits to KCOMWEL, with the application form and a doctor's note [E0030].

4. **Changing workplace.** An injury that makes you unfit for that workplace but able to work elsewhere is a ground for changing workplace [E0169]. The deadlines for changing workplace pause while you are in treatment [E0078].

### Changing workplace or leaving

#### How many changes, and for what reasons

You must normally stay at the workplace you signed for before entering [E0008]. You can change only for one of three reasons [E0067, E0068, E0069]:

1. **The employer ends or won't renew your contract.** The employer ends the contract during its term for a just reason, or refuses to renew it when it expires [E0067].
2. **It is not your fault.** Suspension or closure of the business, cancellation of the employer's permit, a hiring ban on the employer, a dormitory that breaks the housing rules, or the employer's breach of working conditions or unfair treatment, as listed in MOEL's notice [E0068].
3. **Injury.** You are unfit to keep working at that workplace but can work elsewhere [E0069, E0169].

You can change at most 3 times in the first 3 years and 2 times in the 1 year 10 month extension [E0066]. Changes for reason 2 do not count towards these limits [E0070].

**Region rule.** The country is split into 5 regions, and you are referred to jobs only in the region of your first workplace [E0071]:

- Seoul, Gyeonggi and Incheon (capital region);
- Busan, Gyeongnam and Ulsan;
- Daegu, Gyeongbuk and Gangwon;
- Gwangju, Jeonnam, Jeonbuk and Jeju;
- Daejeon, Chungnam, Chungbuk and Sejong [E0072].

You may choose to move from the capital region to a non-capital region, and if you get no referral within a month of applying you can be referred to another non-capital region [E0073].

#### The deadlines

- **1 month to apply:** apply to the job centre within 1 month of the day your contract ends [E0076].
- **3 months to find a new workplace:** you must get immigration's permission for the new workplace within 3 months of applying [E0076].
- **What happens if you miss either:** you must leave Korea, and the job centre reports you to immigration in its monthly list [E0076, E0081].
- **Pauses:** for a work injury, illness, pregnancy or childbirth the clock starts when the reason ends; pregnancy and childbirth extend it until 90 days after the birth [E0077, E0078].

#### The process at the Job Center

1. **Apply.** Go to the job centre (고용센터) of the employment and labour office for your workplace [E0080]. Bring the workplace change application, the confirmation of the reason for change, your passport, your ARC and any proof of the reason (for example the labour office's confirmation of unpaid wages) [E0079].
2. **Get your job-seeker certificate.** The job centre registers you and issues a job-seeker registration certificate (구직등록필증) [E0074]. It must decide within 15 days, extendable once by 15 days [E0152].
3. **Wait for referrals.** MOEL refers you to suitable workplaces; you cannot be referred to a workplace you choose yourself ("designated referral" is banned) [E0075]. The new employer must go through the normal hiring steps, including first trying to recruit Koreans [E0082].
4. **Sign and register.** The new employer gets a new employment permit and you sign a new contract [E0074]. You then need immigration's permission for the change of workplace [E0059].

#### Leaving at the end of the job

The departure notice, the severance and insurance claims, the pension refund and your final wages are set out in order (see Before you leave Korea) [E0094, E0111].

#### End-of-job tricks

- **"Sign this resignation."** Contract expiry or non-renewal by the employer is a legal reason to change workplace [E0067]; resigning is not one of the listed reasons [E0067, E0068, E0069].
- **Being reported absent.** An employer can report a worker who stops coming for 5 days as missing, and must also report it to immigration within 15 days [E0160, E0130].
- **Pledging your severance.** Your right to the departure guarantee money cannot be transferred or used as security, except up to 50% in set hardship cases [E0164].
- **Selling your bank account before you fly.** Selling or lending your account or card is a crime with up to 5 years in prison (see Never sell or lend your bank account or SIM) [E0136, E0137].
- **Holding back the last month's pay.** The employer must settle everything before you leave [E0141]; file a petition before your flight if not (see If you aren't paid) [E0018].

## Skilled work visas (E-7)

E-7 ("Special Activity") is the visa for work in fields that Korea's Ministry of Justice (MOJ, which runs immigration through the Korea Immigration Service) designates [P0262]. It has four sub-types: professional (E-7-1), semi-professional (E-7-2), general skilled (E-7-3) and the points-based skilled worker (E-7-4) [KD0002]. E-7-2 covers 10 office and service occupations, such as office workers and care workers [P0028]. One grant of stay lasts at most 3 years (5 years in a few special cases) [P0205]. Semi-professional, general skilled and skilled-worker jobs carry extra controls (qualification rules, a minimum wage and a cap per company) to protect Korean jobs [P0206]; professional (E-7-1) occupations are approved at the local immigration office's discretion, while the other three need MOJ approval [P0207].

The 2026 minimum annual pay for each type applies from 1 February to 31 December 2026 [P0198]:
- E-7-1: KRW 31,120,000 [P0196];
- E-7-2 and E-7-3: KRW 25,890,000 [P0197];
- E-7-4: KRW 26,000,000 [P0196].

A few occupations have their own wage standard set by MOJ [P0199].

**The online "Employment Visa Guide":** MOJ is piloting a tool on HiKorea (hikorea.go.kr, the government's immigration e-service portal) that lets you look up the 96 E-7 occupations with their qualification, wage and document rules, calculate E-7-4 points and print a document checklist [P0005, P0006, P0263]. Its results are for reference only and have no legal effect: the mission abroad or the immigration office decides each case [P0013].

### E-7-1: professionals

E-7-1 covers 67 manager and professional occupations, such as software developers and electrical engineers [P0027].

**Who qualifies.** You must meet one of these [P0200, P0202]:
- a master's degree or higher in a field related to the job;
- a related bachelor's degree plus at least 1 year of experience in the field;
- at least 5 years of experience in a related field.

Experience counts only from after the degree or certificate; internships before graduation count only in high-tech fields such as IT, bio and nano [P0201]. A graduate of a Korean university (bachelor's or higher) can work in any allowed occupation regardless of major, with no experience requirement, and a graduate of a Korean junior college is exempt from the experience requirement for jobs related to the major [P0203, P0204].

**The contract.** The employment contract must state the pay (hourly rate and daily hours), a contract period of at least 3 months and the duties [P0261], and the annual pay must be at least KRW 31,120,000 in 2026 [P0196].

HiKorea's checklist lists [P0012, P0261]:
- the employment contract, written by the employer and you;
- proof of qualification (degree or graduation certificate, experience certificates, valid licences); documents issued outside Korea need a Korean or English translation, and the key documents need consular confirmation by the Korean mission or an apostille.

### E-7-3: shipbuilding welders and painters

E-7-3 covers 14 trade occupations, for example shipbuilding welders and power-line electricians [P0026]. In January 2026, besides those on the metropolitan visa below, about 13,000 E-7-3 workers and about 8,000 E-9 (Employment Permit System) workers were employed in Korean shipbuilding [P0059].

#### How the job reaches you

E-7-3 shipyard workers are selected privately, not by the Korean government: the Korea Offshore & Shipbuilding Association (KOSHIPA, the shipbuilders' industry body) runs skill tests in the workers' home countries, and MOJ then reviews the status of stay [P0065]. Because selection is private, brokers have more room to operate than in the government-run E-9 system [P0065].

In January 2024 Hanwha Ocean hired Bangladeshi welders through RRC Human Resources Service Ltd, a Bangladeshi private recruiting agency [P0074]; by then Bangladesh had sent 76 workers on E-7, 36 of them through RRC [P0075]. RRC said it had sent 36 welders for Samsung Heavy Industries in 2023 [P0076]. RRC said 2 to 5 years of welding experience was required and that 10 to 12 years got preference [P0077]. The same report put migration cost at about Tk 500,000, roughly two months' pay, against a monthly salary of about Tk 250,000 [P0078].

Two provinces also recruit shipyard workers under the pilot "metropolitan visa" (gwangyeok-hyeong visa, run by 15 provinces from April 2025 to December 2026, separate from F-2-R): Ulsan (440 places) and South Gyeongsang (540 places) [KD0012, P0062]. Under it, the province runs its own skill test and Korean-language test, and only the experience requirement is relaxed for shipbuilding [P0063]. In January 2026, 206 people were in Korea on it in shipbuilding (88 in Ulsan, 118 in South Gyeongsang) [KD0013].

#### Pay, cost and what ties you to the shipyard

The E-7-3 wage standard was cut from 80% of Korea's per-capita GNI (gross national income) to 70% in 2023, and to the minimum wage in 2024 [P0068]. A 2025 survey of shipyard migrants found 66.2% of E-7-3 workers earned under KRW 2.5 million a month, against 38.1% of E-9 workers [P0068]. The official minimum for E-7-3 in 2026 is KRW 25,890,000 a year [P0197].

In the same survey, 58.1% of E-7-3 workers had paid broker fees of KRW 10 million or more and 13.1% had paid KRW 20 million or more; only 6.6% of E-9 workers had paid KRW 10 million or more [P0066]. ### E-7-4: from E-9 to skilled worker by points

E-7-4 lets a worker who came on E-9 (Employment Permit System) or E-10 (seafarer) and has worked long and well in Korea convert to a long-term skilled-worker status [P0001]. MOJ checks Korean ability, income and length of work or stay [P0002]. It covers manufacturing, root industries, construction, farming and livestock, shipbuilding, fishing and coastal shipping [P0025]. It is a change of status inside Korea only: a worker who has already gone home cannot be re-invited on it [P0010]. Holders are granted up to 2 years at a time, extendable [P0032], and family may join them (see F-3: family of E-7 holders) [P0033].

#### The quota

After the quota was raised from 2,000 to 35,000 in September 2023, 52,648 people held E-7-4 status in September 2026 [P0003]. The 2026 annual quota is 33,000, slightly down from 35,000 in 2025 [P0015]. MOJ sets it at the end of each year after consulting ministries and provinces [P0031]. Within it, the Ministry of Employment and Labor (MOEL) issues recommendations for a 2026 manufacturing quota of 2,000 conversions [P0050].

Each employer can hold only a limited number of E-7-4 workers: up to 30% of its Korean staff in general, and up to 50% in depopulation areas, root industries and (from June 2026) farming, livestock and fishing; a workplace with 4 or fewer Korean staff can hire 1 (2 under the special rule) [P0047, P0023].

#### Who qualifies

You'll need all of these [P0029, P0034, P0035, P0036, P0037, P0264]:
- at least 4 years' stay on E-9, E-10 or H-2 (working visit) within the last 10 years, as a registered foreigner currently working normally for your employer (or, as a special case, 3+ years outside the capital region of Seoul, Gyeonggi and Incheon, with a provincial recommendation);
- an E-7-4 contract with your current employer for at least 2 more years at KRW 26 million a year or more (KRW 25 million in farming, livestock, fishing and coastal shipping);
- a recommendation from the company where you have worked for at least 1 year;
- at least 200 of 300 points (bonus points included), with at least the minimum 50 points in both average income and Korean.

The mandatory minimums are an average annual income of KRW 26 million or more over the last 2 years, and Korean at TOPIK (Test of Proficiency in Korean) level 2, completion of level 2 of KIIP (the Korea Immigration and Integration Program, MOJ's social integration course), or placement at level 3 in the KIIP pre-test [P0030]. Until 31 December 2026 there is a concession: if you score 150 points or more without Korean, you can make up the Korean requirement by your first extension (2 years later) [P0038, P0056].

If you changed job for reasons that were not your fault (business closure, assault, unpaid wages), time at the previous workplace now counts toward the 1-year recommendation rule and the 3-year long-service bonus [P0049, P0024].

You are excluded if you have [P0039]:
- a fine of KRW 1 million or more;
- unpaid taxes (you can apply once paid);
- 4 or more Immigration Act violations;
- 3 months or more of illegal stay.

#### The points

The pass mark is 200 of 300, bonus included [P0029, P0037]. The rules and points may change in operation [P0046].

**Average income (last 2 years), up to 120 points** [P0040]:
- KRW 25-30 million: 50;
- KRW 30-35 million: 65;
- KRW 35-40 million: 80;
- KRW 40-45 million: 95;
- KRW 45-50 million: 110;
- KRW 50 million or more: 120.

Workers in farming, livestock, fishing and coastal shipping qualify from KRW 24 million [P0041].

**Korean, up to 120 points** [P0042]:
- TOPIK level 2, KIIP level 2, or KIIP pre-test score 41-60: 50;
- TOPIK 3, KIIP 3, or pre-test 61-80: 80;
- TOPIK 4 or higher, KIIP 4 or higher, or pre-test 81 or higher: 120.

**Age, up to 60 points** [P0043, KD0001]:
- 19-26: 40;
- 27-33: 60;
- 34-40: 30;
- 41 and over: 10.

**Bonus points** (can be combined; only one of the ministry and province recommendations counts) [P0044]:
- recommendation by a central ministry: 30, or by a province: 30;
- recommendation by your employer: 50;
- 3 or more years at your current workplace: 20;
- working in a depopulation area or a town or township (eup or myeon): 20;
- a qualification certificate or a Korean degree: 20;
- a Korean driving licence: 10.

**Deductions** [P0045]:
- a fine under KRW 1 million: 5, 10 or 20 points (once, twice, three times or more);
- having had a stay permit restricted for unpaid taxes: 5, 10 or 15;

### F-2-R: the regional visa

F-2-R ("regional talent") is a residence status for foreigners who meet MOJ's education or income rules and are recommended by a local government, on condition that they live, work or run a business in a depopulation area for at least 5 years [KD0007, P0183]. It sits inside MOJ's regional visa scheme, which runs in 107 local governments with 13,572 allocated places; 11,534 people were staying on it in March 2026 [P0189, KD0008].

You'll need (rules in force from 3 August 2026) [P0180, P0181, KD0011, P0183, P0184, P0186]:
- a recommendation letter from the head of the province or metropolitan city;
- either a Korean associate degree or higher, earned after entering a Korean institution on a D-2 student visa and staying at least 2 years in Korea, or annual income at or above the recommending province's living wage;
- the intention to live, work or run a business in the recommended area for at least 5 years;
- KIIP level 3 completed (or level 4 or higher in the KIIP pre-test), or TOPIK level 3 or higher.

The target areas are the depopulation areas and depopulation "areas of concern" in provinces that take part in the scheme [P0187, KD0010]; the 89 depopulation areas include, for example, Gapyeong and Yeoncheon in Gyeonggi [P0194].

For E-9, E-10 and H-2 workers the regional route is E-7-4R (regional skilled worker): 2 or more years on one of those statuses, a local-government recommendation, and the condition of living and working 3 or more years in a depopulation area or area of concern [P0188]. E-7-4R was created in 2025 [P0193].

#### Step 1: The local government selects you

1. The city or county selects eligible foreigners within its allocation and sends the list to the province [P0185].
2. The province may issue the recommendation letter [P0185].

#### Step 2: Contract, recommendation and change of status

1. Sign the employment contract [P0190].
2. Get the provincial recommendation letter [P0190].
3. Get permission (for example, a change of status) at the immigration office for your area [P0190].
4. Start work [P0190].

Normally an employer must have at least one Korean employee to hire an F-2-R holder [P0192]. From 18 May 2026 to 31 December 2027, a small business in manufacturing, wholesale or retail, or restaurants, or a farm corporation, in one of the 89 depopulation areas may hire one F-2-R holder even with no Korean staff; MOJ weighs criteria such as 3+ years of trading and prior-year sales of KRW 100 million or more [P0191, KD0009].

**Where to get help:** MOJ Regional Stay Support Division, 02-2110-4364 [P0195].

## Visit visa (C-3)

The C-3 (short-term general visit) visa covers short stays for tourism, transit, visiting relatives, conferences, cultural events, religious events and similar purposes, never for profit [V0028]. The common sub-types for Bangladeshis are C-3-9 (tourist), C-3-1 (events, seminars, meetings and family visits) and C-3-4 (business, see Business visit (C-3-4)) [V0052, V0059, V0057, V0061]. The Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Dhaka decides every application; since 2 September 2026 you hand in the papers at the Korea Visa Application Center (KVAC), run by the company VFS Global, not at the Embassy [V0001, V0002, V0011]. VFS Global only handles the paperwork and has no part in approving or refusing visas [KD0015].

### Cost and time

- **Visa fee:** USD 40 (Tk 5,000) for a single entry under 90 days, USD 70 (Tk 8,750) double entry, USD 90 (Tk 11,250) multiple entry, paid in taka at KVAC [V0009, V0024]. The Embassy converts at USD 1 = Tk 125 [V0076].
- **KVAC service fee:** Tk 2,150 per application, paid online in advance (after the first two weeks of opening, when it was paid in cash) [V0007, V0008].
- **Optional KVAC extras:** photocopy Tk 60 (first 4 pages), printing Tk 110 (first 3 pages), photos Tk 480 (4 copies), SMS updates Tk 301, form filling Tk 1,056, courier return of the passport Tk 1,464, premium lounge Tk 7,076 [KD0016, V0043, V0045]. None of them speeds up or influences the decision [V0044].
- **Refunds:** the visa fee is not refunded if the visa is refused, the application is cancelled, or you get a single-entry visa after asking for multiple [V0010]. The KVAC service fee can be refunded only if you cancel the appointment at least 2 business days before and file VFS Global's online refund form within 30 days; there is no refund if you miss the appointment [KD0017, KD0018].
- **Processing:** at least 10 working days in Dhaka; the time is only indicative [V0025].
- **No e-Visa for ordinary visitors:** Korea's e-Visa (issued online) is limited to listed groups such as master's and PhD students at accredited universities and patients invited by registered hospitals, so a tourist or family visitor applies on paper through KVAC [V0121, V0122, V0002].

3. **For a tourist trip,** an invitation and affidavit of support are optional: submit them, notarized, only if you have them [V0055].

### How long you can stay

A C-3 visit allows at most 90 days and cannot be extended in Korea [V0114]. A double-entry visa allows 30 days per visit within 6 months [V0026]. Staying past your permitted period is a crime under the Immigration Act, punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million [V0149, V0150].

### Can you work?

No. C-3 is for non-profit purposes [V0028], and working without a status that permits work is a crime with the same penalty of up to 3 years in prison or KRW 30 million [V0149, V0151]. Immigration raids reach workplaces, subway stations and markets (see Going home without valid papers) [V0178]. People without status cannot get health insurance and cannot safely report abuse at work [V0180].

### If the visa is refused

1. **Do not expect the fee back:** visa and KVAC fees are not refunded [V0010].
2. **Wait at least 3 months before reapplying;** KVAC says an earlier reapplication is possible but unlikely to succeed [V0020].
3. **Do not "fix" the file with bought documents:** forged bank statements, visas, immigration stamps and certificates lead to refusal, a ban on entering Korea and arrest [V0067, V0068].

Overstayers are deported as well as punished (see How long you can stay) [V0150]. Leaving on your own costs less than being caught (see Going home without valid papers) [V0153].

#### Fake invitations

In October 2024 Seoul police said they had broken a ring of Pakistani nationals that, since June 2022, had forged invitation letters from small Korean companies for short-term business visas, charging USD 10,000 to 13,000 per case plus USD 3,000 on arrival in Korea [KD0022, V0181, KD0023]. ## Business visit (C-3-4)

The C-3-4 short-term business visa is for business trips such as consulting or contracts with a Korean organisation, setting up a branch, buying, or learning to install or repair machinery without being paid for it [V0030]. It uses the same e-Form, KVAC appointment, fees and processing time as the visit visa (see "Visit visa (C-3)") [V0009, V0025]. People coming for industrial training cannot use it and need a training status instead [V0037].

### What is different

1. **Ask the Korean company for its papers:** an invitation letter and a guarantee letter (affidavit of support), both sealed and signed by the company president, and its business registration certificate issued within the last year, which it can download from nts.go.kr [V0061, V0033]. The Embassy provides a Letter of Guarantee form in English [V0066].
2. **Prepare your own business papers:** trade licence (initial and current), the Bangladeshi company's tax certificate, challan and tax return receipt, your personal bank statement with solvency certificate, the company's bank statement issued within 6 months, and your visiting card [V0062, V0063, V0064, V0065].
3. **If you are an employee,** add your employment certificate and office ID card [V0064].
4. **Add the air-ticket booking, hotel booking and tour itinerary** [V0064].
5. **If you want a multiple-entry visa, ask for it:** business applicants may apply for one, but the Embassy decides single or multiple entry, and the fee is not refunded if you get single entry [V0027, V0010].
6. **If the invitation comes from the Korean government, an international organisation or a major company such as Samsung, LG, Hyundai or Youngone,** you may submit without an appointment in consultation with the Embassy [V0021].

For seminars, events and meetings the sub-type is C-3-1, which needs the organiser's invitation and guarantee letter and proof of the event, such as its schedule [V0059, V0060].

### How long you can stay and can you work?

The stay is at most 90 days and cannot be extended [V0114]. Business meetings are allowed, but paid work in Korea needs a work status; working without one can bring up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine [V0030, V0149, V0151].

### Scams

The invitation-forging ring broken in Seoul in 2024 sold short-term business visas based on fake invitations from small Korean companies (see Fake invitations) [V0181]. Korean embassies call the inviting company to check, and the ring answered those calls on burner phones [V0182].

> - Does the Embassy phone the Korean company and the applicant's own employer in Bangladesh before deciding?

### If the visa is refused and where to get help

Refusal and reapplication work as for the visit visa (see If the visa is refused): fees are not refunded, and KVAC advises waiting at least 3 months before reapplying [V0010, V0020]. For help, contact KVAC on 09606777156 or info.korea.BD@vfshelpline.com [V0005], and report a broker or fake invitation to the Korean Embassy consular section, +880-2-5881-2088 to 90, consuldhaka@mofa.go.kr [V0070].

## Student visas (D-2 degree, D-4 language)

Students on degree courses (associate, bachelor's, master's, doctorate, research, exchange) need a D-2 visa; students on non-degree courses such as Korean language training need a D-4 (D-4-1 is Korean language) [V0124, V0125]. The university admits you, but the Embassy issues the visa, and screening checks that you intend to go home when your stay ends [V0127, V0011].

### Which university to choose

- **IEQAS-certified universities** (International Education Quality Assurance System, certified each year by the Ministries of Justice and Education): 184 institutions for degree programmes and 126 for Korean language programmes; their students get relaxed visa screening and more weekday work hours [V0148, KE0014, V0146]. The list is on studyinkorea.go.kr under "Accredited Universities" [V0148].
- **Non-accredited universities** that fail inspection are designated for stricter visa screening on language and finances [V0147].

### Costs

- **What families actually paid in one 2026 case:** KRW 7.13 million (about Tk 600,000) per student for tuition, admission and dormitory at SinGyeongju University, paid before any visa [V0163].

- **TOEFL or IELTS score** within 2 years [V0090].
- **Air-ticket booking** [KE0021].

1. **Fill in the e-Form** on visa.go.kr and print it with the bar code; it is mandatory for D-2 and D-4 [V0077].
2. **Book a KVAC appointment** (VIC holders can walk in) and submit in person [V0023, V0019].

1. **Register as a foreigner within 90 days of entry** [V0129].
2. **Report any change of address within 15 days,** or face a fine of up to KRW 1 million [V0143].

### How long you can stay

D-2 and D-4 are granted for up to 2 years at a time and can be extended [V0115, KE0022]. A bachelor's student can stay up to 6 years after admission [V0126].

### Part-time work

You must get permission before you start [V0130]. Working without it, or breaking its conditions, is a crime under the Immigration Act punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine, and illegal work in construction brings a deportation order without exception, however few the violations [V0152, KE0001, V0137].

1. **Check you qualify:** degree students can work from the start; D-4 language students only after 6 months in Korea [V0131, V0133].
2. **Check your hours:**
   - **Language students:** 10 hours a week without TOPIK 2 (Test of Proficiency in Korean, level 2); with it, 20 hours, or 25 at a certified university [V0132].
   - **Associate and 1st-2nd year bachelor's:** 10 hours without TOPIK 3; with it, 25 weekday hours (30 at a certified university) and no limit at weekends and in vacations [V0133].
   - **3rd-4th year bachelor's:** the same hours, but TOPIK 4 is needed [V0134].
   - **Master's and doctoral:** 15 hours without the language level; with it, 30 weekday hours (35 at a certified university) and no limit at weekends [V0135].
3. **Get a standard employment contract** stating the hourly wage, and the confirmation form signed by your university's international student advisor [V0130].
4. **Apply online or in person** with passport, residence card, application form, advisor's form, transcript or attendance record, Korean certificate, the employer's business registration and the contract [V0136].
5. **Get the sticker or printed permit** before you start [V0130].

Korea's government has proposed reforms, reported in November 2026, under which TOPIK 3 would be needed for part-time work and TOPIK 4 or KIIP level 4 would let most students skip proof of funds; they are proposals, not yet rules [KE0025, V0185, V0187].

### After graduation: the D-10 job-seeker visa

1. **Check you qualify:** D-10-1 is for looking for professional jobs in the E-1 to E-7 categories; manual and unskilled work is not allowed [V0138]. You need at least a bachelor's (or a Korean associate degree) and a points score; Korean graduates with TOPIK 4 within 3 years of graduating are exempt from the score [P0174, V0118, KE0023].
2. **Apply to change status** with the application, photo, passport copy, fee, job-seeking plan, degree certificate, proof of residence, and Korean or career certificates if you have them [V0140].
3. **Proof of funds:** KRW 900,000 a month for 6 months (about KRW 5.4 million), not needed the first time you change from D-2 to D-10 [V0141, V0142].
4. **Extend in 6-month steps,** up to 2 years; internships are allowed for up to a year in total and 6 months per company [V0116, V0139, KE0024].

### Bringing family

A D-2 bachelor's-or-higher student who has stayed more than 6 months can invite a spouse and children as dependants (F-3), but nationals of high-overstay countries must apply at a Korean mission abroad, and some D-2 and D-4 students who have stayed under 2 years cannot invite family without humanitarian reasons [V0119, V0120].

## Family visas

### F-6: marriage to a Korean

F-6 (marriage migrant) is for a Korean national's spouse; a parent raising a child born of a marriage (including an unregistered one) with a Korean; and someone who cannot continue the marriage because the Korean spouse died, disappeared or for another reason that was not their fault [P0080]. F-6 has no restriction on work, and after 2 years on F-6 you can apply to change to F-5 permanent residence [P0082].

If you marry abroad, you enter Korea directly on an F-6 visa; if you marry in Korea, you change your existing status to F-6 [P0081]. The F-6-1 marriage visa needs an invitation from the Korean spouse [P0083]. The compulsory international-marriage guidance programme applies only to spouses from China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Cambodia, Mongolia, Uzbekistan and Thailand, so not to Bangladeshis [P0084].

#### What MOJ checks

- how you met and whether you intend to marry, and that the marriage is valid under both countries' law [P0085];
- whether the Korean spouse invited another foreign spouse in the last 5 years [P0085];
- that you have exchanged health and criminal-record information [P0086];
- the sponsor's income (see The income threshold) [P0090];
- your basic Korean (see The language rule) [P0092];
- a normal home for the couple: a gosiwon (tiny rented room), motel or vinyl greenhouse does not count [P0087];
- a sponsor who got Korean nationality or F-5 through marriage must have held it for 3 years [P0088];
- a sponsor convicted of a false marriage registration must wait 5 years [P0089].

#### The income threshold

The Korean sponsor's pre-tax income for the year before the visa application must be at least [P0090, P0091]:
- 2-person household: KRW 25,195,752;
- 3-person: KRW 32,154,216;
- 4-person: KRW 38,968,428.

The income rule is waived if the couple already have a child together, or if they lived abroad together for at least 1 year after marrying so the sponsor had no Korean income [P0094].

#### The language rule

You must show basic Korean in one of these ways [P0092, P0093]:
- TOPIK beginner level 1 or higher (still valid);
- a Korean course of 120+ hours at a Korean Education Centre (level 2) or King Sejong Institute (beginner);
- a beginner course at an institution designated by the Korean mission (a consul tests you directly if it was completed over 2 years ago);
- KIIP level 2 or higher (same 2-year rule);
- a Korean-related university degree.

The rule is waived if the couple have a child together, if you have lived in Korea for 1+ year continuously, or if the Korean spouse has lived 1+ year continuously in a country where your native language is an official language; in the last two cases the consulate can still test you directly if your language seems too weak [P0095, P0096].

#### Step 1: Register the marriage

1. If you marry in Korea, the foreign spouse attaches proof from their own country's authorities or embassy that they meet that country's marriage requirements [P0222].
2. If you marry in Bangladesh, the Korean spouse must still register the marriage in Korea, attaching the marriage certificate and a translation [P0223], within 3 months at the Korean mission [P0224].

#### Step 2: Apply for F-6

You'll need [P0098]:
- the visa application form and passport;
- a guarantee letter and the marriage-migrant invitation from your spouse;
- your spouse's family relation certificate;
- income and housing documents;
- your Korean-language documents.

Inside Korea, you or an agent apply for the change of status at the local immigration office [P0097].

## Permanent residence and citizenship

F-5 (permanent residence) needs no extensions, frees you from re-entry permits, lets you vote in local elections after 3 years (age 18+), and has no work restriction; you can be deported only for specified serious offences [P0100, P0101].

### F-5: who can get it

Every F-5 applicant needs good conduct, the means to support themselves (own or family income or assets), and basic Korean and knowledge of Korean society [P0102]. The routes most relevant to Bangladeshis:
- **Marriage:** 2+ years on F-6 [P0082].
- **General:** an adult with 5+ years in Korea on D-7 to E-7 or F-2 [P0103, P0171]; for this route, basic knowledge means completing KIIP level 5 [P0172].
- **Degree or technical certificate:** 3+ years in Korea, employed at pay above MOJ's threshold [P0104].

From E-9 the published ladder is E-9 to E-7-4 (4+ years on E-9), then F-2 long-term residence (5+ years on E-7, good conduct, livelihood, KIIP level 4 or 81+ pre-test points), then F-5 [P0034, P0170]. MOJ said in October 2026 it will shorten the stay needed for F-2 [P0008].

You cannot apply for F-5 [P0105, P0106]:
- within 5 years of finishing a prison sentence;
- within 3 years of paying a fine;
- with 3 or more Immigration Act violations in the last 5 years (not counting administrative fines);
- within 7 years of leaving under a deportation order, or 5 years under a departure order.

### Naturalisation

Naturalisation is general, simple or special; a foreigner legally married to a Korean can use simple naturalisation [P0208].

**Simple naturalisation by marriage** needs 2+ years' continuous address in Korea while married, or 3 years since the wedding with 1+ year's address in Korea while married [P0209]. The period starts on the day you entered lawfully and registered as a foreigner [P0211]. An unregistered (de facto) spouse must use general naturalisation, which needs 5+ years' continuous residence [P0210]. If the Korean spouse died or disappeared, the marriage broke down through no fault of yours, or you are raising your child together, you can still apply after completing the remaining period [P0219].

**Every applicant needs** adulthood (19), good conduct, the means to support themselves, Korean and knowledge of customs, and no threat to national security [P0212]. Good conduct fails, for example, within 5 years of paying a fine or with unpaid taxes [P0213].

You'll need [P0214, P0215, P0216]:
- the application form and proof that you are a foreigner;
- proof of livelihood: for simple naturalisation, KRW 30 million in financial assets or property, or a certificate of employment; for general naturalisation, income of at least last year's per-capita GNI, or KRW 60 million in assets;
- for marriage, the Korean spouse's family relation certificate.

1. Submit the application at the immigration office [P0216].
2. MOJ may visit your home and check criminal and stay records; you take a comprehensive evaluation and interview (married simple-naturalisation applicants who completed KIIP may be exempt) [P0217].
3. You become Korean when you take the oath before the Minister and receive the certificate [P0218].
4. Within 1 year you must give up Bangladeshi nationality, or lose Korean nationality automatically [P0220]. A spouse naturalised through marriage may instead pledge not to use the other nationality in Korea [P0221].

Most people naturalised come from China and Vietnam: of 10,359 people naturalised in January-September 2026, 57.6% were Chinese and 21.5% Vietnamese [P0120].

## Changing visa type

To do activities that belong to a different status you must get MOJ's permission to change status first [P0158]. Working requires a status that permits it, at the designated workplace only, and no one may employ a person without it [P0159]. Breaking these rules can lead to deportation [P0260]. No one can switch to E-9 inside Korea: it is obtained only from your home country through EPS, before entry [R0238].

### E-9 to E-7-4

E-9 and E-10 holders are in principle barred from changing to any E-7 type except E-7-4; the full procedure is under E-7-4: from E-9 to skilled worker by points [P0169]. The regional alternative, E-7-4R, needs only 2 years on E-9, E-10 or H-2 plus a recommendation from the local government head, but you must live and work in a depopulation or designated area for 3 years [P0188].

### Student to E-7

A student on D-2 or D-4 moves to E-1 to E-7 by meeting each status's employment rules [P0175]. Korean university graduates are exempt from the E-7 experience requirement, and junior-college graduates are exempt for jobs related to their major [P0203, P0204]. The job must still meet the E-7 wage minimum, KRW 31.12 million a year for E-7-1 (see E-7-1: professionals) [P0196].

### D-10 job seeker to E-7

D-10 (job seeker) is for graduates (bachelor's degree, or a Korean associate degree) looking for E-1 to E-7 work [P0174]. Points are waived for Korean graduates within 3 years of the degree who hold TOPIK level 4 or the equivalent KIIP result [P0173, KE0023]. Once you have an E-7 job offer, you change status by meeting that occupation's E-7 rules [P0175].

### Visit to work: not allowed

When a Korean mission issues a C-3 short-term visit visa (90 days or less), it must tell you that a change of status after entry will not be permitted [P0163, P0164]. Short-term status is for tourism, visits and similar stays of up to 90 days [P0162]. Working without a status that permits work is punishable by up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine, and working at a place other than the designated one by up to 1 year or a KRW 10 million fine [P0159, V0151, KE0001, P0161, KE0004].

## Asylum

Korea acceded to the 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol in 1992, and its Refugee Act came into force on 1 July 2013 [P0155]. A refugee is a foreigner who cannot or will not accept the protection of their own country because of a well-founded fear of persecution for race, religion, nationality, membership of a social group or political opinion [P0124]. Debt is not a ground: in June 2024 the Supreme Court rejected a debt-based claim, ruling that pressure from creditors is not persecution under the Refugee Act [KE0036, P0149].

### How few are recognised

From 1994 to September 2026, 147,720 people applied and 66,030 cases were decided; 1,785 people were recognised as refugees and 2,740 got humanitarian stay [P0109, P0110]. The cumulative recognition rate is 2.7% [P0111]. In 2025, 5,467 cases were decided and 135 people recognised [P0113]. The largest group of claims (64,378) were on grounds outside the Refugee Convention [P0112].

Bangladeshis made 3,526 claims from 1994 to 2022, 394 in 2024 and 468 in January-September 2026 [P0121, P0122, P0123].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis has Korea recognised as refugees or given humanitarian stay since 1994?

### The procedure

1. Apply in writing at the local immigration office, with your passport or residence card and any evidence [P0126]. You get a receipt at once [P0127].
2. You are interviewed; MOJ must decide within 6 months, extendable by 6 months [P0133].
3. If refused, you can object to MOJ within 30 days through the immigration office; the Refugee Committee reviews it [P0134].
4. You can stay in Korea until the decision is final, including any court case [P0128]. The whole process (first decision, objection, courts) averages over 4 years [P0148].

Humanitarian stay is for someone who is not a refugee but faces a real risk of torture or inhumane treatment [P0125]. No recognised refugee, humanitarian sojourner or applicant may be forcibly returned [P0143].

### G-1 status and work

A worker on E-9, H-2 or a short-term visa who applies for refugee status is moved to G-1 (miscellaneous), the same status used for unpaid-wage mediation and industrial-accident claims [P0116]. G-1 is granted for 1 year and is extendable [P0177].

Applicants are barred from work for the first 6 months [P0256]. After 6 months MOJ may permit work, as permission for activities outside the status of stay [P0136, P0140]. During the first 6 months, living-cost support goes only to pregnant women, the elderly, people with infants, and the sick or disabled [P0257]; from September 2026 it is paid as points on a Woori Card debit card instead of cash, and using it for cash withdrawals or remittances is to be strictly restricted [KE0038, KE0039, P0258]. Support lasts up to 6 months from the application, extendable by up to 6 months for serious illness or disability [P0139]. Applicants' minor children get the same primary and secondary schooling as Koreans [P0138].

### Abuse, and what happens to it

MOJ may skip part of the screening for applicants who [P0129, P0130, P0131]:
- submit fabricated documents or make false statements;
- re-apply without a major change in circumstances;
- have lived in Korea 1+ year and apply just before their stay expires, or apply to delay deportation.

Missing three interviews in a row lets MOJ end the case [P0132]. Applicants who re-apply without a major change, who apply late or to delay deportation, or whose case is in court get no living-cost, housing or medical support, except for emergency or humanitarian needs [P0137, KE0040]. An applicant who deliberately destroyed a passport or used a false ID can be detained up to 10 days (extendable by 10) to confirm identity [P0135]. Getting refugee status or humanitarian stay through false documents, false statements or concealment is punishable by up to 1 year in prison or a KRW 10 million fine [P0141, P0142].

### Brokers selling refugee claims

Brokers sell refugee applications as a way to stay long-term and work [P0144, P0146]. MOJ caught 9 brokers in 2021, 14 in 2022, 24 in 2023 and 24 in 2024 [P0145]; a legal-sector source quoted in April 2025 said brokers supply fake documents for KRW 600,000 to 10 million per case [KE0049, P0146]. Cases:
- 2023: Incheon Airport Immigration caught two Malaysian brokers who arranged false claims for 184 Malaysians at KRW 800,000 each, as a way to stay and work illegally, and on 21 November 2023 referred them to prosecutors in detention on suspicion of Immigration Act violations and forging documents [P0144, KE0037].
- 2026: immigration officials referred a Korean broker and 10 alleged accomplices to prosecutors, accused of taking about KRW 400 million from 131 Vietnamese on seasonal or short-term visas [P0151]; investigators said the broker had them memorise fabricated stories and spread the filings across 21 immigration offices [P0152].

Brokering false applications is punishable under the Immigration Act by up to 3 years in prison or a KRW 30 million fine [P0147]. At end-September 2026, 29,463 first-instance cases and 12,761 objections were waiting [P0114].

**Where to get help:** MOJ Refugee Policy Division, 02-2110-4178 [P0259]; UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) has an office in Seoul [P0265].

## Having a child in Korea

Korean nationality at birth comes through a parent: a child with at least one Korean parent is Korean from birth [P0226], and that birth is reported within 1 month with the doctor's or midwife's birth certificate [P0228]. If the child also holds Bangladeshi nationality, they must choose one before age 22 [P0227].

### Korean birth registration for foreign children

Korea has no law for registering the births of foreign children: on 27 August 2026 the Constitutional Court unanimously held this gap unconstitutional [P0229, KE0010]. The case was brought for a child born in April 2019 to Vietnamese parents who had come on E-9 and become undocumented [P0230]. The Court said Korea's family registers and foreigner registration do not effectively protect a foreign child's right to be registered [P0231]. It held that the state must pass a law letting any child born in Korea be registered under Korean law, whatever the parents' nationality or status of stay, with safeguards so that parents registering a birth are not targeted for Immigration Act enforcement [KE0011, KE0012].

### If the parents are undocumented

Undocumented parents' children can get status through MOJ's scheme for long-term resident children, open from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2028 [P0232]. A child qualifies if born in Korea or arrived before age 6 and has lived in Korea 6+ years (7+ years if they arrived aged 6 or older), and attends a Korean school or has finished high school in Korea [P0233, KE0045]. ## Sending money home

Bangladesh asks workers to send money only through legal channels, never through hundi (informal transfer through unlicensed agents) [B0017]. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with a further 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps the government incentive [B0191, B0018].

### The legal channels from Korea

In Korea a foreigner sends money abroad either through a foreign exchange bank he has designated for remittances or through a registered small-sum overseas remittance company (소액해외송금업자) [B0205, B0197].

- **Banks:** through his designated foreign exchange bank a foreigner can send up to USD 50,000 a year with no supporting documents; above that he must prove where the money came from, for example with a payslip or income statement [B0205, B0207]. Any single transfer over USD 10,000 is reported to the National Tax Service and the Korea Customs Service [B0206].
- **Small-sum remittance companies:** each registered company may send up to USD 5,000 per transfer and USD 50,000 a year [B0198]. The four Korean companies that bKash lists as its partners are all on the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) register [B0188]:
  - E9pay (이나인페이), registration 2017-1, 02-877-7200 [B0204].
  - GME, registered as Global Money Express (글로벌머니익스프레스), registration 2017-2, 1588-6864 [B0203].
  - Gmoney Trans (지머니트랜스), registration 2017-6, 1670-4565 [B0201].
  - Hanpass (한패스), registration 2017-11, 1522-0767; Hanpass says sign-up needs only a passport or Alien Registration Card (ARC) [B0200, B0194].
- **Global operators:** Western Union and MoneyGram also send from Korea to bKash [B0188].

You'll need:
- To send to a bKash wallet: the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as given when the account was opened [B0190].
- To send to a bank account: your own or a relative's account in Bangladesh; banks and exchange houses with a "drawing arrangement" with a Bangladeshi bank pay straight into it [B0019].

1. **Check the company is registered.** Open the FSS consumer portal FINE (fine.fss.or.kr), go to 소액해외송금업자 > 등록현황 조회(독립형), and look for the company's name, registration number and phone; the list showed 33 entries in November 2026 [B0196, B0197]. A company marked 폐지 (closed) is no longer registered, even though it stays on the list [B0199].
2. **Send.** For bKash, give the partner the receiver's bKash number and registered name; up to Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer, as often as you like [B0189, B0190]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [B0020].
3. **The family collects.** bKash money can be cashed out at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs for Tk 7 per thousand [B0192].

### Hwanchigi: hundi in Korea

The Korea Customs Service calls hundi hwanchigi (환치기): you hand won to an operator in Korea, and his partner abroad pays the same value from his own account or through acquaintances, without going through a foreign exchange bank; the reverse, money from abroad paid out in won, counts too [B0208]. It is punishable under Article 27-2 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act by up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 300 million won [B0209]. Customs says money changers have been prosecuted for moving voice-phishing proceeds, crypto payments and drug money this way [B0210]. Anyone can report suspected hwanchigi to Customs on 125, or online at www.customs.go.kr > 국민참여 > 신고마당 > 밀수신고 > 신고하기; the reporter's identity is protected and rewards go up to 75 million won [B0211, KF0003, B0212].

### Never sell or lend your bank account or SIM

Handing over, selling, buying or lending a bank account in Korea (a daepo tongjang, an account used in someone else's name), or its access means such as the card, passbook or online banking details, has been punishable since 20 August 2020 by up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 30 million won under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act [B0213, E0136, E0137]. Brokering, advertising or recommending such deals for payment is also punished, and giving or keeping account details knowing they will be used for crime carries the same 5 years or 30 million won [E0186, B0214]. For closing your account when you go home, see Before you leave Korea, item 7 [B0213].

### Saving and pension in Bangladesh

#### NRCTA (Non-Resident Convertible Taka Account)

A taka savings, current or fixed-deposit account for Bangladeshis abroad, funded by remittances, at a scheduled bank's AD (Authorised Dealer, licensed for foreign exchange) branch or Offshore Banking Unit, under Bangladesh Bank's Circular 41 of 8 October 2026 [B0023, B0029]. A worker can open one before departure with no initial deposit, or during a visit home [B0024]. It earns market interest, and principal and interest can be sent abroad freely [B0026]. Someone in Bangladesh can operate it under a power of attorney, the money can go to a non-resident nominee if the holder dies, and the account can be kept after coming home for good [B0027]. Banks may lend in taka against it, including to buy a home for your own use [B0028]. The trade-off: money sent into an NRCTA generally gets no 2.5% incentive unless specifically stated [B0025].

#### Wage Earner Development Bond (WEDB)

A 5-year government bond [B0030].
- Sizes: Tk 25,000, 50,000, 1 lakh, 2 lakh, 5 lakh, 10 lakh and 50 lakh [B0030].
- Where sold: Bangladesh Bank, any scheduled bank's AD branch, and abroad at branches of Bangladeshi banks, exchange houses and exchange companies [B0030].
- Who buys: a legal wage earner, in taka, with remittance sent through legal channels, in his own name or a named person's, with no ceiling [B0033].
- Profit at maturity, paid every six months: 12% for holdings up to Tk 15 lakh, 11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh and 9% above [B0031].
- Cashing early pays less (up to Tk 15 lakh): 8.70% after 6-12 months, 9.45% after 12-18, 10.20% after 18-24 and 11.20% after 24-60 [B0032].
- Other terms: profit and investment are income-tax free; you can name a nominee and borrow against the bond; it has a 40-50% death-risk benefit [B0034].

#### The Probash universal pension

The National Pension Authority's Universal Pension "Probash" scheme is for Bangladeshis working or living abroad: you pay in foreign currency and the pension is paid in taka [B0035]. You join at 18 to 50 with your NID (national ID card) or passport number; over 50 only under special consideration, paying 10 years without a break [B0040]. You pay until 60, and the pension is paid for life [B0039]. The monthly contribution is Tk 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500 or 10,000; Tk 1,000 a month for 10 years gives about Tk 1,530 a month [B0037].

> **Need to know**

**The rules:**
- If you die, your nominee gets the pension until you would have turned 75; if you die before paying 10 years, the deposit comes back with profit [B0039].
- No lump-sum withdrawal and no leaving before pension age; a late contribution has 1 month's grace, then a late fee of 1% a day; never give anyone cash for contributions [B0045].
- From Korea you pay in foreign currency, by card, through an exchange house or banking channel, or from the Bangladesh account that receives your remittances, not in taka [B0042, B0044]. Back home you can pay the same amount in taka, or change scheme [B0035].
- You can also open a Surakkha account (the scheme for self-employed and informal workers) for a spouse, parent or sibling in Bangladesh and pay it in foreign currency [B0047, B0124, B0038].

You'll need [B0041]:
- Your NID number (or passport number).
- Your Bangladesh bank account name, number and routing number.
- Your nominee's ID number.

1. **Register** at www.upension.gov.bd: click Registration, choose Probash, and type your NID or passport number, date of birth, mobile number and the captcha [B0041]. Union Digital Centres help with registration [B0042].
2. **Fill in** occupation, yearly income, address, bank details and the nominee's details; submit, pay the first contribution, and keep the payment slip and the email with your pensioner ID [B0041]. If you registered with a passport, submit your NID once you have one [B0043].

**Help:** National Pension Authority, 43 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000; hotline 16131 or +88 09610 900800 (9 am-5 pm Bangladesh time); support@upension.gov.bd [B0046].

## Going home without valid papers

A foreign worker who stays after his permitted stay ends may be forcibly removed from Korea and faces up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 30 million won [B0165]. Enforcement runs all year, with regional crackdown teams at 8 immigration offices (Suwon, Busan, Gwangju, Daejeon, Daegu, Yangju, Incheon and Ulsan) and regular joint crackdowns by 5 ministries [B0259]. Almost 140,000 foreigners were deported, fined or given a departure order in 2024, up from 98,000 in 2022 [B0261]. In a 2026 crackdown on undocumented foreign delivery riders, 541 of the 628 caught were fined and 55 removed from Korea [B0154]. A Bangladeshi undocumented worker in Hwaseong told The Korea Herald in 2026 that raids come every one to two months, now also at subway stations and markets, and that people caught are taken to immigration offices to wait for deportation; a friend broke his arm falling from a second floor while running from officers [B0262, B0263, B0264].

Since November 2025, government officials are exempt from their duty to report undocumented status when the person is a foreign worker owed unpaid wages, so a worker can report unpaid wages without being handed to immigration (see If you aren't paid) [B0153].

### Leaving voluntarily: the fine and the ban

Korea runs a standing voluntary departure system: an undocumented foreigner who leaves on his own gets a lighter entry ban, and since July 2020 one who pays his fine (beomchikgeum, 범칙금, an administrative fine set by immigration) when leaving is exempted from the entry ban [B0257]. If he is caught instead, he is deported and banned [B0149].

> **Need to know**
> - What fine does immigration set for each length of overstay, and how long is the entry ban for someone who leaves voluntarily without paying, or who is deported, after 1, 3 and 5 years' overstay?

### The special voluntary departure programmes

From time to time the Ministry of Justice runs a special voluntary departure period, with more generous terms:
- **30 September 2024 to 31 January 2025:** fines and entry restrictions waived for all undocumented foreigners who left (the period first ran to 30 November 2024 and was extended by two months) [B0256].
- **1 December 2025 to 28 February 2026 (90 days):** undocumented foreigners who reported and left had their fine waived and entry restrictions deferred, even without paying the fine [B0146, B0148]. Parents (or others responsible for the child) leaving with children under 17 also had their administrative fine (과태료) waived [B0150]. Excluded: people who entered illegally, used forged or altered passports, criminal offenders, people subject to deportation such as those who ignored a departure order, and anyone who became undocumented on or after 1 December 2025 [B0147]. Crackdowns went on during the period [B0149].

With crackdowns and the 2025-26 programme, the number of undocumented foreigners fell from about 430,000 in 2023 to about 340,000 in April 2026 [B0152].

   - **From Bangladesh:** send an agent to an NPS office or post the application, passport and bank proof; documents made in Bangladesh must be attested by a consulate (private ones also notarised) and translated into Korean [B0172, B0173, B0174]. The refund must be claimed within 5 years [B0183].
6. **Collect your final wages.** All wages and money owed must be paid within 14 days of leaving, unless you both agree in writing to a later date; if the employer holds them back, file a petition before your flight (see If you aren't paid) [E0111, E0141, E0018].
7. **Empty and close your bank account.** Selling or lending it is a crime (see Never sell or lend your bank account or SIM) [B0213].

Workers who forget to collect this money lose a lot: in 2022 the EPS Center in Dhaka of HRD Korea (the Korean agency that runs EPS policy, tests and the job roster) looked for over 300 returnees who had left Tk 30,000 to Tk 3 lakh each in insurance behind, and in 2024, with BOESL, for about 400 returnees [B0274, B0246, B0269]. The lists were posted on the EPS Center's Facebook page, and a dead worker's wife or children (or parents, if unmarried) could apply with proof [B0247, B0248]. Unclaimed Departure Guarantee and Return Cost money expires 3 years after it falls due and is then moved to HRD Korea within 1 month [E0139, E0140].

### What you can bring home

Bangladesh's Non-tourist Passenger Baggage Rules 2025 (SRO 261, gazetted 3 June 2025) set what a returning worker may bring [B0082].
- **Duty-free:** up to 65 kg of baggage, plus 35 kg more of clothes, personal items and books [B0083]. One each of a laptop, printer, camera, microwave, rice cooker, blender, sewing machine, fan, a TV up to 29 inches, a music system and others; 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one a year [B0084, B0095]. Declared gold or silver jewellery once a year, no more than 12 pieces of one type [B0085].
- **With duty:** one gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) once a year, declared, at Tk 5,000 per 11.664 g; anything over is confiscated, and an undeclared bar can lead to arrest [B0086, B0094]. A TV of 30-36 inches costs Tk 10,000; a refrigerator Tk 5,000; a split air conditioner up to 18,000 BTU Tk 15,000; a washing machine Tk 3,000 [B0089].
- **Not allowed:** alcohol, for a Bangladeshi passport holder [B0087].
- **Declaring:** fill in the baggage declaration form or submit it online; with nothing dutiable, use the green channel [B0088]. Up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency comes in without declaring; above that, declare on the FMJ (Foreign Money and Jewellery) form [B0090, B0096]. Customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 [B0097].

### Support after return

1. **Korean-company jobs and the returnee community.** In 2019, 73 Bangladeshi EPS workers in Korea filled in job application forms from HRD Korea for Korean companies in Bangladesh, at an Embassy seminar [B0271]. With the Korean Embassy, returnees from Korea have formed a community with eight local groups, including Dhaka, meeting at least once a year to share experience and help each other settle, for example by starting businesses [B0270].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does HRD Korea still run a job-matching service or job fair in Dhaka for returnees with Korean companies in Bangladesh, and how does a returnee register?
2. **PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) rehabilitation loan.** Returnees starting a project can borrow up to Tk 50 lakh: up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral; up to 10 years at 9% simple interest; decided within 15 working days [B0099]. Fees: 0.50% processing and 0.50% documentation, each at most Tk 15,000 [B0101]. Apply at the branch for the project's area with 3 photos, your passport, NID, an address certificate from the municipality or union parishad, an updated trade licence (compulsory), the project details, copies of your return papers, 3 signed cheques and a solvent guarantor with a clean credit (CIB) report [B0100]. By 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees had got PKB's special reintegration loan [B0102].
3. **BRAC Migration Programme.** BRAC, a development organisation founded in Bangladesh in 1972 and the organisation behind this guide, runs a Migration Programme that gives returnees airport help, counselling, help to start earning, and legal assistance [B0106, B0107].
4. **Children's scholarships.** WEWB pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC (Secondary School Certificate) level, Tk 34,000 at HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) level) [B0109].
5. **If your family is threatened** or its property seized, apply to WEWB directly or through the Deputy Commissioner's office or the DEMO (district employment and manpower office); WEWB writes to the local administration [B0110, B0111].
6. **Savings.** An NRCTA can be kept after coming home for good, and Probash pension contributions can continue in taka (Sending money home) [B0027, B0035].
