# S findings: scam compounds and trafficking (Cambodia)

Agent S, 11 November 2026. Facts: `countries/cambodia/work/S/facts.csv` (S0001-S0238). Sources: `work/S/sources.csv` (42 registered).

## a. Draft doc text

### Background inserts

**For "In one minute" (one or two sentences the lead can fold in):**

Thousands of Bangladeshis who went to Cambodia in 2025-2026 for "computer operator", "data entry" or "customer service" jobs, many with BMET clearance and a smart card, were met at Phnom Penh airport by Bangladeshi brokers and sold to Chinese-run scam compounds for USD 1,500 to 5,000 each [S0008, S0037, S0039, S0051, S0089, S0043, S0110]. Cambodia has since deported more than 3,000 Bangladeshis in nine months and says the compounds are gone; independent groups say recruitment into them continues [S0182, S0227, S0228].

**For "Is it open now, and who can go?" (#### Status):**

- In 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has tightened visa checks for Cambodia, Vietnam and Laos [S0056]. Workers to Cambodia, Laos and Brazil may no longer go under the "embassy attestation exemption" (the rule that let up to five workers go without the Bangladesh embassy attesting the job papers); a proper work visa and full verification are required before BMET clearance [S0118, S0120].
- Cambodia passed a Law on Combating Online Scams, in force from 7 April 2026 [S0183]. On 28 August 2026 its Commission for Combating Online Scams (CCOS, the government body leading the crackdown) said Cambodia no longer hosts any scam compounds [S0227]; an anti-trafficking consultant and Amnesty International say compounds and recruitment into them continue [S0228, S0233].

**For "How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut" (#### The scam-compound chain):**

The scam-compound chain starts with an ad for an office job in Facebook, WhatsApp or Telegram groups, or with a friend or relative who says he knows a company [S0090, S0157, S0070]. A broker in Bangladesh takes Tk 2 lakh to Tk 7.7 lakh, part of it often passed to a licensed recruiting agency that processes BMET clearance [S0009, S0003, S0016]. The visa that arrives names another job (construction) or is a one-month tourist or visit visa, while the broker says it is a two-year work visa [S0017, S0038, S0074, S0087]. In Phnom Penh a Bangladeshi receiver keeps the worker in a hotel for a few days, then drives him to a compound "for an interview" and sells him for USD 1,500 to 5,000 [S0039, S0020, S0043, S0110, S0131]. Bangladeshi officials report brokers collect USD 2,000 to 5,000 per person [S0131]. The compound then treats the price as a debt the worker must work off, or that the family must pay to free him [S0152, S0023, S0111].

**For "What goes wrong most" (#### Scam compounds):**

The danger that defines this corridor is being sold into a scam compound (see Scam compounds and trafficking) [S0008, S0117]. BRAC counts more than 1,000 of the 15,921 Bangladeshis cleared by BMET for Cambodia in 18 months as still trapped in compounds [S0117]. Common beliefs set against the facts:

- **"BMET clearance and a smart card mean the job is real."** Many of those sold into compounds had BMET clearance and a smart card [S0004, S0051, S0088].
- **"The broker says it is a two-year work visa."** Many travelled on one- or two-month tourist or visit visas [S0074, S0087].
- **"Cambodia is a step to a job in Thailand, Canada, Australia, Europe or the UAE."** Some victims were told that and taken to Cambodia instead, or were told to "gain experience" in the compound before being sent on [S0124, S0024, S0096].
- **"The compound will let me go if I ask."** Those who asked were beaten or told to pay the price back, sometimes double [S0026, S0048, S0212].

### Scam compounds and trafficking

A scam compound is a guarded building or estate, usually Chinese-run, where trafficked foreigners are forced to cheat people online, mostly in the United States and other rich countries, through fake romances and fake investments [S0089, S0022, S0044, S0082, S0224]. Many are former casinos and hotels taken over by mostly Chinese gangs after Cambodia banned online gambling in 2019 [S0150]. Compounds have surveillance cameras, barbed wire and guards, often with electric-shock batons and sometimes guns [S0151]. Bangladeshis work there alongside Pakistanis, Indians, Nepalis and Bhutanese [S0047].

#### How Bangladeshis are recruited

1. **The ad.** Recruiters offer jobs as computer operator, data entry worker, typist, call-centre or customer-service agent, sometimes factory or casino work, paying Tk 50,000 to over Tk 1 lakh (USD 800) a month [S0008, S0037, S0061, S0090, S0192]. The offers circulate through fake websites, email, Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram [S0090]. Some Telegram groups contain fake "successful migrants" who belong to the ring [S0189].
2. **The person you know.** The UN Human Rights Office found nearly three in four victims were recruited through relatives, friends, neighbours or community members [S0070]. Traffickers make people inside compounds recruit others to win their own release, and have made Bangladeshi victims film recruitment videos [S0172, S0134, S0135].
3. **The test.** Some rings give a computer literacy or typing test first, which makes the job look real [S0210, S0033]. Networks pick people with computer, digital or language skills; most of those found at scam sites are men aged 18 to 40 [S0223, S0226].
4. **The payment.** Families paid Tk 2 lakh to Tk 7.7 lakh, in the cases reported often about Tk 5 to 6 lakh [S0009, S0003, S0078, S0086, S0109]. One man paid Tk 1 lakh to the broker and the rest to a recruiting agency in Fakirapool, Dhaka [S0016].
5. **The papers.** Victims were given visas and papers that did not match the promised job [S0038]. In several cases the invitation letter named a construction company, or the worker found on arrival that he held a tourist or one-month visit visa [S0017, S0042, S0087]. Many still received BMET clearance and a smart card [S0004, S0088]. Since the rules tightened, some traffickers use fake BMET cards, or send people to Thailand as tourists first and on to Cambodia from there [S0055].
6. **The route.** In the cases reported, brokers routed people through Thailand or, when there was no direct flight, via China [S0063, S0030]. At Dhaka airport one victim, told he needed a Thai transit visa, was sent by the broker to stand by a pillar and send a WhatsApp photo; a man arrived and got him a boarding pass within 10 minutes, and another handed him USD 2,000 before boarding [S0018, S0019]. Other traffickers recruit Bangladeshis already working in Dubai and the Gulf with promises of higher pay, then move them across the Thai border into Cambodia, Laos or Myanmar [S0132, S0125, S0133].
7. **The handover.** On arrival a Bangladeshi receiver meets the worker, keeps him in a hotel for one to three days, then drives him to a compound "for an interview" two to four hours away [S0039, S0020, S0031, S0049]. There the worker learns he has been bought, for USD 1,500 to 5,000 [S0023, S0032, S0043, S0110].

#### Where the compounds are

Amnesty International identified 53 compounds in 16 towns and cities in June 2025 and 86 by April 2026 [S0148, S0233]. They cluster in Sihanoukville, along the Thai and Lao borders (notably Bavet, on the Vietnam border), and increasingly in rural areas [S0170, S0147]. One Bangladeshi was taken from Phnom Penh to the Long Bay compound in Dara Sakor, a special economic zone 350 km away [S0128]. After the 2025-2026 raids, groups moved into smaller sites: gated housing estates (called borey in Khmer), apartments, condominiums and hotels [S0177, S0237]. Managers sometimes knew about raids in advance and moved workers, in one case by bus into the mountains [S0142, S0234].

#### What happens inside

- **Passport and phone taken** on entry, along with any cash; one man was allowed his phone for 10 minutes [S0021, S0009, S0035, S0049].
- **Hours:** the promised eight-hour day becomes 12 to 16 hours, often at night (one worker: 10 pm to 10 am) [S0046, S0127, S0005].
- **The work:** posing as a young woman or a romantic partner online, building trust, then getting people to "invest"; teams split the stages, one collecting phone numbers, another building relationships [S0022, S0058, S0045].
- **Pay:** little or none: one man got USD 476 for the first month and nothing after [S0027]. One was fed only dry noodles, sometimes once a day [S0036].
- **Punishment:** those who miss targets, refuse or ask to leave are taken to isolated "dark rooms", given electric shocks, beaten with hockey sticks and denied food; one man was locked up for 17 days [S0026, S0080, S0062, S0153].
- **Resale:** workers who miss quotas or cannot repay the "debt" are sold on to other compounds, sometimes at a higher price; one Bangladeshi was sold three times for USD 3,500 to 10,000 [S0173, S0034, S0129, S0194].
- **Paying to get out:** the compound or the broker demands the purchase price, sometimes double, for release; families paid USD 1,500 to 5,000 and more for return tickets [S0048, S0212, S0067, S0111, S0112]. Families are called directly and asked for up to USD 3,000 under threat of resale [S0190].
- **No legal status:** the promised one-year visa and work permit never come, so the worker overstays and becomes liable to arrest [S0007, S0121].

#### If you or a relative is trapped

Bangladesh has no embassy or consulate in Cambodia; the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok covers it [S0193, S0198]. Rescues happened more often when a foreign embassy was involved than through the Cambodian hotline alone [S0165].

Ask for help, in this order:

1. **The Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok, labour and welfare wing.** Hotline for Bangladeshis in Thailand and Cambodia: +66-818-70-84-43 [S0198]. Email: fsbangkok@probashi.gov.bd [S0199]. Main line +66 2 390 5107 to 8; mission.bangkok@mofa.gov.bd and consular.bangkok@mofa.gov.bd [S0201, S0202]. The labour first secretary in 2026 was Zahir Imam [S0100]. Give the full name, passport number, the compound's exact location and a photo, which is what Cambodian police ask for before they act [S0166]. Sample message: "My [son/husband] [name], passport [number], went to Cambodia on [date] through [broker/agency name]. He is held at [compound name / live-location link] in [town]. His phone: [number]. Please help him." In one rescue, embassy officials confirmed a woman's location by video call and, with Bangladeshis living in Phnom Penh, brought her out [S0196].
2. **Share live location.** One victim shared his live location with his wife before he was locked up; his brother then reached Bangladeshis in Cambodia, who traced him [S0065, S0066].
3. **Cambodian police.** The anti-trafficking police hotline is 1288 [S0238]; it takes Chinese, English, Khmer and Vietnamese [S0167]. Scam-related cases are referred to a separate hotline run by the police Central Security Department [S0168]. The National Police also run a 24-hour online scam-reporting site, hotline.police.gov.kh, with a complaint form (ដាក់ពាក្យបណ្ដឹង, "file a complaint") [S0205]. In many police rescues officers met a manager at the gate and were handed only the person who had called; several survivors were beaten after bosses found they had contacted police [S0154, S0155].
4. **BRAC Migration Programme in Dhaka.** Families and victims who contacted BRAC's Migration Centre online got help arranging the return, and returnees got legal advice at BRAC's Migration Service Centres [S0113, S0073].
5. **IOM (the International Organization for Migration).** IOM helped more than 3,500 victims of forced criminality in Southeast Asia from 2022 to 2025, Bangladeshis among the largest groups [S0206], but only people given victim status can get its help, including the ticket home [S0207].

#### After you get out: detention, fines and the flight home

- **Victim or offender.** Cambodian authorities rarely give victim status; people freed from compounds are usually detained and deported without screening [S0139, S0145, S0163]. None of the 73 survivors Amnesty interviewed, Bangladeshis among them, was recognised as a trafficking victim [S0139, S0141]. Some were held indefinitely, asked for bribes by police, or resold to compounds [S0164, S0140].
- **Immigration detention.** People are often held about three months, in overcrowded conditions, without phones [S0145, S0140, S0099]. A Bangladeshi in a Cambodian detention centre reported being attacked by other inmates [S0146].
- **Shelter.** As of April 2026 only one shelter in Cambodia took scam-compound victims, with room for 100 [S0143, S0144]. Many people freed in the January 2026 releases were stranded in Phnom Penh without a passport or money for a flight [S0159, S0160].
- **Overstay fines.** Overstaying costs USD 10 a day [S0028]. In June 2026 Cambodia let overstayers leave without paying fines; at the Bangladesh embassy's request it extended the window to 1 September, then from 1 September let overstayers leave voluntarily without fines, with the waiver due to end on 31 October 2026 [S0097, S0094, S0091]. One man's fine was waived 24 days after he applied [S0029]. After buying a ticket, people whose fines were waived had to send their travel details to Cambodia's immigration department [S0187]. Waivers can require departure within days [S0208]. Cambodia's new rules allow arrest, fines and jail for foreigners who do not leave within one month [S0186].
- **Registering with the embassy.** At least 1,796 Bangladeshis registered with the Bangkok embassy during the return window [S0101]. On the embassy's instructions many bought refundable tickets while waiting [S0102]. Cambodian officers interview everyone on the list and question those who entered irregularly in more detail [S0103]. On 3 September 2026, 32 Bangladeshis with tickets were turned back at Phnom Penh airport and taken to an immigration detention centre [S0098]. A Bangladeshi without a passport gets a Travel Permit from the embassy to fly home (see Going home without valid papers) [S0203, S0197].
- **Brokers in the way.** Bangladeshi traffickers in Cambodia intimidate victims trying to leave and stall returns to extract more money [S0095].

#### Coming home and taking action

- **At Dhaka airport** returnees from Cambodia got emergency help, counselling and travel money from Civil Aviation Security, the Expatriate Welfare Desk and BRAC's Migration Programme [S0085, S0081]. Some had their phones seized by the CID (the police Criminal Investigation Department) on arrival [S0105].
- **A criminal case.** Victims can file a case at a police station; in October 2026 a case at Paltan Police Station under sections 6, 7, 8, 9 and 22 of the Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Prevention and Suppression Act, 2026 led the CID to arrest two alleged traffickers [S0114, S0108]. Under section 54 of the 2026 Act, a trafficking victim is not criminally liable for illegal entry or stay, or a false travel or identity document, that resulted directly from the trafficking [S0136, S0137]. Most victims cannot produce documents, payment records or receipts to file a case, [S0191]; one survivor kept bank transaction records linked to his broker [S0071].
- **What happens in practice:** many returnees settle with the broker to recover money instead of filing a case, and the CID says cases are few compared with returnees [S0054, S0052, S0072].

#### The crackdown

From July 2025 to 20 August 2026 Cambodian police raided 624 sites, detained nearly 30,000 suspects of 39 nationalities and rescued and deported 58,266 foreigners, according to CCOS [S0230, S0231]. From January to September 2026 the immigration department deported 52,039 foreigners, including more than 3,000 Bangladeshis, and identified more than 18,000 of them as directly involved in online fraud; no figure says how many deported Bangladeshis were trafficking victims [S0182, S0217, S0218]. Under the Law on Combating Online Scams, scammers face 2 to 5 years in prison and a fine of up to 500 million riel, and bosses 15 to 30 years or life if their operations cause death [S0184, S0185]. Two alleged kingpins, Chen Zhi and Li Xiong, were arrested and extradited in 2026 [S0232]. In Bangladesh, RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion) arrested a Cambodia-based ring leader, Nazmul Islam, in 2022, saying his gang had trafficked more than 500 Bangladeshis [S0209, S0213].
