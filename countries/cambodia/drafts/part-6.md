## Asylum

Asylum in Cambodia is only for someone with a well-founded fear of persecution because of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion [U0103]. A claim based purely on economic need or the wish for a better life can be rejected at once, with no appeal or review [U0114, U0127]. The rules are set by a 2009 sub-decree (a government regulation), and the Ministry of Interior decides claims through the Immigration Department of the National Police [U0103, U0104]. UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) says Cambodia's General Department of Immigration (GDI, the Ministry of Interior's immigration department) oversees 26 refugees and 11 asylum seekers of all nationalities, that problems remain with access to asylum and case processing, and that UNHCR itself has no programmes or staff in Cambodia [U0121, U0122, U0123, W0064]. Permission to stay lasts only as long as the procedure [U0107]. People forced into scam work are screened as possible trafficking victims when they apply to go home (see Going home without valid papers, Step 2) [U0024, U0025].

### How to apply

1. **Apply to any Cambodian official,** by any appropriate means [U0105].
2. **Get your permission to stay:** a free written authorisation, either a visa in your passport or, without a passport, a letter signed and sealed by the Commissioner General of the National Police [U0106].
3. **Return the application form** completed and signed within one week [U0108].
4. **Attend the interview,** held within 15 days of the form, before two Interior Ministry officials and one Foreign Ministry official; a qualified interpreter is free at every stage [U0109, U0117]. Missing the interview twice without good reason ends the case, and not giving accurate information gets the claim rejected at once [U0116, U0115].
5. **Wait for the written decision,** due within 45 days of the interview, extendable to 90 days in all [U0110].

An asylum seeker can approach UNHCR through the Ministry of Interior at any time [U0120]. UNHCR's office covering Cambodia is in Bangkok: 3rd A Floor, United Nations Building, Rajdamnern Nok Avenue, Bangkok 10200; +66 2 288 2180; thaba@unhcr.org [U0124, U0125].

### If it is refused

1. **Appeal once,** in writing to the Immigration Department within 30 days, stating why; it acts within 15 days, and its decision is final [U0111, U0112, U0113]. There is no appeal to a court [U0126].
2. **Leave Cambodia within 15 days** of the final refusal (see Going home without valid papers) [U0113].

### If you are recognised

A refugee may work or run a business under Cambodian law [U0118]. Status is cancelled if it was won with false information or documents [U0119].

## Irregular routes and undocumented workers

Many Bangladeshis went to Cambodia with valid passports, visas and government clearance and were then forced into scam compounds; others lost their status after arrival [B0201, U0063, U0037]. The Bangladesh embassy put the number overstaying at 4,000 to 4,500 in mid-2026 [U0061]. Cambodia's Ministry of Interior said it deported 3,364 Bangladeshis between 1 September 2023 and 13 September 2026, 852 of them linked to online scams [U0035]. Overstaying costs USD 10 (about Tk 1,250) a day; entering illegally brings prison and then expulsion, and working without a work card can bring expulsion (see What can get you jailed or deported) [U0044, G0002, U0094, U0097]. Breaking the rules on registration, residence cards and exit permits is fined 50,000 to 1,000,000 riel (Cambodia's currency; about Tk 1,500 to Tk 30,500), and 1,000,001 to 2,000,000 riel (up to about Tk 61,000) for a repeat [U0101, G0003].

### How people lose their papers

- **A visit or tourist visa instead of a work visa:** one worker left with BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) emigration clearance and found on arrival he held only a one-month visit visa; another, promised an employment visa, found he had a tourist visa [B0059, U0063, U0064].
- **A work permit that never comes:** recruiters promised one returnee a one-year visa and work permit and provided neither, leaving him without legal status [U0037].
- **A job that doesn't match the papers:** the same returnee was promised data entry, but his invitation letter named construction work [U0038].
- **A visa left to expire:** one victim said his middlemen refused to extend his expired visa [U0066].
- **Tourist e-visas by design:** in 2022 RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion, Bangladesh's elite police unit) said a trafficking gang sent Bangladeshis on Cambodian tourist e-visas arranged with Bangladeshis living in Cambodia [U0068].
- **Routes round the checks:** after Bangladesh tightened checks on travel to Cambodia, some recruiters allegedly used fake BMET cards or flew workers to Thailand as tourists before moving them into Cambodia [U0062].
- **Leaving the employer:** the owner of Fahim Recruiting Agency said in July 2026 that some workers leave for better-paid jobs and their original employers then refuse to take them back [U0067].
- **Coming back after deportation:** Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, a Bangladeshi survivors' group, warned in October 2026 that deported Bangladeshis may be lured back into Cambodia illegally through another country [S0219].

To check a visa and work permit before and after you fly, see Work visa: company worker, Step 5: The visa, and checking it is real.

### Cambodia as a route to Thailand and Malaysia

Thai police said in 2024 that since early 2023 Bangladeshis have been smuggled into Thailand via Cambodia, moved in stages from the Cambodian border to Bangkok and then to southern Thailand, heading for Malaysia [U0069, U0070]. Cases on record:

- **December 2023, Sa Kaeo:** 11 Bangladeshis bound for Malaysia, who had flown to Cambodia and crossed into Thailand illegally, were caught when their pickup truck overturned in Sa Kaeo province [U0075, U0076].
- **January 2024, Narathiwat:** Thai immigration police arrested 19 Bangladeshis at Taba Market, Tak Bai district [U0071]. Bangladeshis in Cambodia had taken their passports to get them fake Thai entry stamps; each paid Tk 4 to 5 lakh for the stamps and the agents, and all 19 passports carried irregular visa stickers [U0072, U0073, U0074].
- **April 2026, southern Thailand:** 21 Bangladeshis who paid brokers Tk 7 lakh each for passage to Malaysia flew to Cambodia, crossed into Thailand on foot on 4 April, were hidden in a seaside resort in Chana whose owner took their phones, and were charged with illegal entry and stay [U0078, U0079, U0077, U0081, U0080].
- **May 2026, Sa Kaeo:** six Bangladeshis who had flown to Sihanoukville in January and worked about five months as labourers without pay crawled under the barbed-wire border fence from the Poipet side, meaning to reach the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok for help going home; they were handed to Khlong Luek police for prosecution [U0086, U0087, U0088, U0089].
- **July 2026, Sa Kaeo:** seven Bangladeshis, none with a passport or ID, were among 11 people caught crossing from Cambodia after fleeing Cambodian crackdowns; each had paid a smuggling network 8,000 baht (Thai currency), was driven from Poipet to the border and walked across a natural crossing by three Cambodian guides, who escaped before Thai officers arrived [U0082, U0083, U0084, U0085].

All land border crossings between Cambodia and Thailand, including Poipet, are closed until further notice [V0042]. Entering Cambodia secretly or by fraud is itself punished with prison and expulsion (see What can get you jailed or deported) [U0094].

### Raids, detention and deportation

Cambodia's crackdown on online-scam operations has brought deportations on a large scale [U0039, U0056]:

- **Scale:** the GDI deported more than 45,000 foreigners in January to June 2026, not counting those who left on their own, and 52,039 from 87 countries in January to September 2026, more than 3,000 of them Bangladeshis [U0039, U0033]. Cambodia's Commission for Combating Online Scams said it repatriated 23,760 foreigners and raided 972 scam operations between July 2025 and October 2026 [U0056].
- **Victims:** no figures show how many deported Bangladeshis were trafficking victims forced into scams [U0034]. Amnesty International said Cambodia treats people leaving scam compounds as irregular migrants and detains them without access to lawyers or their embassy [U0048].
- **Leaving on their own:** 241,888 foreigners, Bangladeshis among them, left Cambodia voluntarily between mid-January and 19 April 2026 [U0057].

Where people are held:

- **Immigration detention centres** in Phnom Penh and Sihanoukville [U0049]. Detainees report officers demanding bribes to return phones, for a bed, bedding and a fan, to deliver food and for release [U0050]. One former scam worker said free drinking water was available only one hour a day and otherwise cost USD 2 (about Tk 250) [U0047, G0002]. Detainees may receive visitors [U0051].
- **Bati Pre-Deportation Centre,** Sopheakmit Residence, Lumpong commune, Bati district, Takeo province, which in May 2026 took in 994 foreigners suspected of scam work, Bangladeshis among them [U0054, U0052, U0053]. How long a person stays there depends on his embassy issuing travel documents to those without passports (see Going home without valid papers, Step 3) [U0055].

The expulsion order and how to challenge it are under What can get you jailed or deported; what happens to people taken out of compounds is under Scam compounds and trafficking.

## Going home without valid papers

This is for anyone whose visa has expired, whose passport is held by a broker or employer, or who has been released from a compound or detention [U0036, U0061, U0055]. In 2026 people went home this way: they waited for a window when Cambodia waived overstay fines, registered with the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok, got a Travel Permit if they had no passport, and flew out [U0005, U0010, U0061, U0036]. Outside a waiver, overstaying costs USD 10 (about Tk 1,250) a day, which can add up to thousands of dollars [U0044, G0002].

### Help from Bangladesh

- **The Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok** handles Bangladeshis in Cambodia, because Bangladesh has no embassy there (see Step 2: Register with the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok) [B0197].
- **Probashi call centre** of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB, the government fund for migrant workers): 16135, free in Bangladesh, or +8809610102030 from abroad [B0021].
- **WEWB by application:** a family can apply for a worker jailed abroad to be brought home, and for legal help abroad, through the expatriate welfare branch of the DC (Deputy Commissioner's) office, the DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office) or directly to the Welfare Board, which writes to the embassy and tells the family the result [L0144, B0249, B0003].
- **Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment:** a family can write to its grievance officer, the Joint Secretary (Admin and Finance), 02-41030224, 01712992086, adsadmin@probashi.gov.bd, who has 30 working days to reply [B0051].
- **BRAC:** victims and families who contacted BRAC Migration Centre online got help arranging the return [S0113].

### Step 1: Check whether a fine waiver is open

1. **Find out whether Cambodia is waiving overstay fines.** In June 2026 Cambodia let overstayers leave without paying fines; at the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok's request it extended the window to 1 September, and from 1 September it again let overstayers leave voluntarily without fines [U0005, U0006, U0001]. That waiver was due to end on 31 October 2026 [L0123, U0021]. Embassies negotiate waivers country by country, and the process is slow [U0045]. One returnee learned of the waiver on Facebook, applied, bought a ticket and flew home on 14 June 2026; another's fine was waived 24 days after he applied [U0036, S0029].
2. **Leave inside the window.** Waivers are time-limited and can require departure within days [S0208]. Someone who refused to leave in a window and was then detained is barred from Cambodia for life once deported (see Bans after you leave) [U0040].

### Step 2: Register with the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok

1. **Ask the Embassy's labour wing to put you on the return list.** The 1,796 Bangladeshis stranded in September 2026 had registered with the Embassy during Cambodia's return window, and requests for help reached it by phone, email, in person and through other Bangladeshis in Cambodia [U0010, U0028]:
   - **Welfare hotline** for Bangladeshis in Thailand and Cambodia: +66-818-70-84-43 [B0175].
   - **Labour wing:** First Secretary (Labour) Jahir Emam, +66 02-3905064, fsbangkok@probashi.gov.bd [B0176].
   - **Consular email:** consular.bangkok@mofa.gov.bd [B0173].
2. **Expect a Cambodian screening interview.** Cambodian officers interview everyone on the return list to check whether they are trafficking victims or linked to scam centres and trafficking networks, and question those who entered irregularly in more detail [U0024, U0012]. Those found to be trafficking victims were allowed home [U0025]. In October 2026 the screening was paused, and the Bangladesh mission asked for it to restart [U0026]. By mid-October Cambodia had accepted a list of 1,797 Bangladeshis, with about 400 more in the same situation [U0128].

> **Need to know**

### Step 3: Get a Travel Permit if you have no passport

1. **Apply for a Travel Permit,** a one-way document the Embassy issues to a Bangladeshi without a passport who wants to go home [B0179]. In 2026 many Bangladeshis overstaying in Cambodia received travel passes to return, and the Embassy arranged one woman's return on a travel pass with the Cambodian government's cooperation [U0061, S0197].
   You'll need [B0180]:
   - copies of your old passport and its visa page;
   - your air ticket;
   - your birth certificate or NID (national identity card);
   - a GD (general diary, a police report) if the passport was lost;
   - 2 photos and the filled-in form;
   - an interview in person at the Embassy.
2. **Pay the fee:** 550 baht (ordinary) or 1,100 baht (urgent) [B0181, B0182]. The consular section takes applications 9:30 am to 12:00 noon and delivers 3:30 to 4:30 pm, Monday to Friday [B0178].

All land border crossings between Cambodia and Thailand are closed, so a worker in Cambodia cannot reach the Embassy in Bangkok overland [V0042]. For people held at the Bati centre, release waits on their embassy issuing travel documents (see Raids, detention and deportation) [U0055].

### Step 4: Buy the ticket and leave

1. **Buy a refundable ticket.** On the Embassy's instructions, many stranded Bangladeshis bought refundable tickets while they waited [U0011]. Returnees pay their own fares: the 30 released from detention in October 2026 bought their own tickets, one stranded man said he bought tickets two or three times and only lost money, and one returnee spent USD 2,266 (about Tk 2.8 lakh), including overstay penalties, to get home [U0030, U0029, U0065, G0002]. High airfares and paperwork kept many from leaving even when fines were waived [U0022].
2. **Wait for immigration clearance before going to the airport.** On 3 September 2026, 32 Bangladeshis with tickets were turned back at Phnom Penh airport while waiting for clearance and taken to an immigration detention centre (see If you are detained) [S0098, U0007].
3. **If a broker stops you leaving,** tell the Embassy's welfare hotline at once (see Step 2: Register with the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok) [B0175]. Returnees told BRAC that Bangladeshis involved in trafficking in Cambodia intimidate victims trying to go home; one said brokers delayed his return by two months, and a stranded man's wife said his broker was stopping him from leaving [U0004, U0014, U0013].

> **Need to know**

### Step 5: At Dhaka airport

1. **Expect questioning.** In October 2026 CID (the police Criminal Investigation Department) carried out forensic checks on returnees from Cambodia and examined their phones and belongings; one returnee had his phone seized [U0032, U0015].
2. **Get help at the airport.** BRAC's Migration Programme gave returnees from Cambodia emergency help, counselling and money to reach home [U0003]. The airport welfare desks and filing a case are under Coming home and Scam compounds and trafficking.

> **Need to know**

### If you are detained

The 32 Bangladeshis taken from Phnom Penh airport in September 2026 were held without phones and could not contact their families; the Embassy sent food through a local representative [U0007, U0008, U0009]. Thirty were released after more than six weeks, once WEWB's expatriate welfare branch wrote to the Embassy's First Secretary (Labour) on an application by Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, and reached Dhaka on 20 October 2026 [U0031, B0199, L0124]. A family can make the same application to WEWB (see Help from Bangladesh) [L0144, B0249]. Where people are held and what happens there is under Raids, detention and deportation.

### Bans after you leave

- **After deportation for illegal entry or work:** blacklisted from re-entering Cambodia for at least three to five years, the GDI's deputy director general said in 2025 [U0043].
- **After refusing to leave in a waiver window and then being detained:** barred from Cambodia for life once deported, the GDI's Director General, Sok Veasna, said in July 2026 [U0040, U0041].
