### Background inserts

For **What ties you to the employer** (one line, for the lead to fit in):

- **Expired visa:** once your visa runs out you owe a fine of USD 10 a day, and if you are deported you are barred from Cambodia for at least three to five years (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [U0044, U0043].

For **What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe**:

#### How the irregular routes end

Many Bangladeshis who end up undocumented in Cambodia went legally, with a passport, visa and BMET clearance, and lost their status there: the visa turned out to be a one-month visit or tourist visa, the promised work permit never came, or the middleman refused to renew [U0130, U0063, U0064, U0037, U0066]. The Bangladesh embassy put the number overstaying at 4,000 to 4,500 in mid-2026 [U0061]. Cambodia deported more than 3,000 Bangladeshis in January to September 2026 alone [U0033]. Those who try to leave overland end up arrested in Thailand: since early 2023 Thai police have caught groups of Bangladeshis crossing from Cambodia on their way to Malaysia, after paying brokers Tk 4 lakh to Tk 7 lakh each [U0069, U0073, U0078]. (See Irregular routes and undocumented workers.)

**Belief: "Cambodia is a door to Thailand and Malaysia."** The overland route is illegal smuggling, not a door: people walk across fields or crawl under the border fence with Cambodian guides and are charged with illegal entry in Thailand [U0084, U0088, U0080]. Thailand's land checkpoints with Cambodia have been closed since the 2025 border fighting [U0135, U0136].

### Irregular routes and undocumented workers

#### How people lose their papers

Most undocumented Bangladeshis in Cambodia did not sneak in; their papers were wrong or ran out after a legal-looking departure [U0130]:

- **Visit visa instead of a work visa:** one worker had BMET clearance and found on arrival he held only a one-month visit visa; another, promised an employment visa, found he had a tourist visa [U0063, U0064].
- **Work permit never issued:** recruiters promised one returnee a one-year visa and work permit and never provided either, leaving him without legal status [U0037].
- **Job that doesn't match the papers:** the same returnee was promised data entry, but his invitation letter named construction work [U0038].
- **Visa left to expire:** one victim said his middlemen refused to extend his expired visa [U0066].
- **Tourist e-visas by design:** in 2022 RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion, Bangladesh's elite police unit) said a trafficking gang sent Bangladeshis on Cambodian tourist e-visas arranged with Bangladeshis living in Cambodia [U0068].
- **Routes round the checks:** after Bangladesh tightened checks on travel to Cambodia, some recruiters allegedly used fake BMET cards or flew workers to Thailand as tourists before moving them into Cambodia [U0062].
- **Leaving the employer:** a recruiting agency owner said some workers leave for better-paid jobs and their original employers then refuse to take them back [U0067].

To check your own visa and work permit before and after you fly, see Work visa: company worker, Step 5: The visa, and checking it is real.

#### Cambodia as a route to Thailand and Malaysia

Since early 2023 Thai police have reported Bangladeshis being smuggled into Thailand via Cambodia, moved in stages from the Cambodian border to Bangkok and then to southern Thailand, heading for Malaysia [U0069, U0070]. Cases on record:

- **December 2023, Sa Kaeo:** 11 Bangladeshis bound for Malaysia were caught when their pickup truck overturned in Sa Kaeo province; they had flown from Bangladesh to Cambodia and crossed into Thailand illegally [U0075, U0076].
- **January 2024, Narathiwat:** Thai immigration police arrested 19 Bangladeshis at Taba Market, Tak Bai district, Narathiwat province [U0071]. Bangladeshis in Cambodia had taken their passports to get them fake Thai entry stamps; each paid Tk 4 to 5 lakh to the agents, and all 19 passports carried irregular visa stickers [U0072, U0073, U0074].
- **April 2026, southern Thailand:** 21 Bangladeshis who paid brokers Tk 7 lakh each for passage to Malaysia flew to Cambodia, crossed into Thailand on foot on 4 April, were hidden in a seaside resort in Chana whose owner took their phones, and were charged with illegal entry and stay [U0078, U0079, U0077, U0081, U0080].
- **May 2026, Sa Kaeo:** six Bangladeshis who had flown to Sihanoukville in January and worked about five months as labourers without pay crawled under the barbed-wire fence from the Poipet side, meaning to reach the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok; they were handed to Khlong Luek police for prosecution [U0086, U0087, U0088, U0089].
- **July 2026, Sa Kaeo:** of 11 people caught crossing from Cambodia, seven were Bangladeshis, none with a passport or ID; each had paid a smuggling network 8,000 baht, was driven from Poipet to the border and walked across a natural crossing by three Cambodian guides, who escaped before Thai officers arrived [U0082, U0084, U0085]. They had fled after Cambodian crackdowns on foreigners intensified [U0083].

Thailand's land checkpoints with Cambodia, including the main Ban Khlong Luek-Poipet crossing, stayed closed after the 2025 fighting, and Thailand's prime minister said in May 2026 they would stay closed until Cambodia accepted Thailand's conditions [U0136, U0135]. Anyone crossing by land now crosses illegally [U0094].

#### Raids, detention and deportation

Cambodia's crackdown on online-scam operations has turned into mass deportation of foreigners [U0039, U0056]:

- **Scale:** Cambodia's General Department of Immigration (GDI, under the Ministry of Interior) deported more than 45,000 foreigners in January to June 2026, not counting those who left on their own [U0039]. From January to September 2026 it deported 52,039 foreigners from 87 countries, including more than 3,000 Bangladeshis [U0033]. Cambodia's Commission for Combating Online Scams said 23,760 foreigners were repatriated and 972 scam sites raided between July 2025 and October 2026 [U0056].
- **Bangladeshis:** the Ministry of Interior said 3,364 Bangladeshis were deported between 1 September 2023 and 13 September 2026, 852 of them linked to online scams [U0035]. No figures show how many of them were trafficking victims forced into scams [U0034].
- **Voluntary departures:** 241,888 foreigners, Bangladeshis among them, left Cambodia on their own between mid-January and 19 April 2026 [U0057].

Where people are held:

- **Immigration detention centres** in Phnom Penh and Sihanoukville [U0049]. Detainees there report officers demanding bribes to return phones, for a bed, bedding and a fan, to deliver food and for release [U0050]. One former scam worker said free drinking water was available only one hour a day and otherwise cost USD 2 [U0047]. Detainees may receive visitors [U0051].
- **Bati Pre-Deportation Centre,** at Sopheakmit Residence, Lumpong commune, Bati district, Takeo province, which in May 2026 took in 994 foreigners suspected of scam work, Bangladeshis among them [U0054, U0052, U0053]. How long a person stays there depends on their embassy issuing travel documents to those without passports [U0055].

Amnesty International said Cambodia treats people leaving scam compounds as irregular migrants and detains them without access to lawyers or their embassy [U0048]. Cambodia has one shelter for trafficking victims, which in June 2026 was full with a waiting list of hundreds [U0046]. In September 2026, 32 Bangladeshis waiting at Phnom Penh airport for immigration clearance to fly home were turned back and taken to an immigration detention centre, where they had no phones and could not contact their families; the Bangladesh embassy sent food through a local representative [U0007, U0008, U0009]. Thirty of them were released only after more than six weeks, after the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB) and a survivors' group pressed the embassy [U0031, U0019]. For what happens to people taken out of scam compounds, see Scam compounds and trafficking.

#### The blacklist

- **Deported for illegal entry or work:** blacklisted from re-entering Cambodia for at least three to five years, the GDI's deputy director general said [U0043].
- **Refused to leave during an amnesty and then detained:** barred from Cambodia for life once deported, the GDI's Director General, Sok Veasna, said [U0040, U0041].

#### What the law says

Cambodia's Law on Immigration (1994) sets these penalties [U0094]:

- **Entering illegally** (secretly, by fraud or any other illegal means): three to six months in prison, then expulsion [U0094]. Anyone who helps bring in or hide such a person gets the same sentence [U0095].
- **Faking, altering or lending** a residence card, work card or employment contract, or using one in another person's name: five to fifteen years in prison [U0096].
- **Working without a work card:** the Minister of Interior may expel you [U0097]. Anyone who entered illegally is expelled [U0098].
- **Breaking the registration, residence-card and exit-permit rules:** a fine of 50,000 to 1,000,000 riel, and 1,000,001 to 2,000,000 riel for a repeat [U0101].
- **Your host must report you:** landlords and hosts must report any foreigner staying with them to the authorities within 24 hours [U0102].
- **Expulsion:** the order takes effect at once and is carried out within 7 days unless a court suspends it; you may complain to a court within two months [U0099, U0100].
- **Overstaying:** a fine of USD 10 a day [U0044].
- **Scam work:** the Anti-Cyber Scam Law of 7 April 2026 allows fines up to USD 500,000 and life imprisonment for online fraud [U0060]. (See What can get you jailed or deported.)

### Going home without valid papers

If your visa has expired, your passport is with a broker or employer, or you were released from a compound or detention, these are the steps people have used in 2026 [U0036, U0061]. Cambodia charges overstayers USD 10 a day, which can reach thousands of dollars, so the fine-waiver windows below matter [U0044].

#### Step 1: Find out whether a fine waiver is open

1. **Check whether Cambodia is waiving overstay fines.** In June 2026 Cambodia let overstayers leave without paying fines; at the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok's request it extended the window to 1 September, and from 1 September it again let overstayers leave voluntarily without fines [U0005, U0006, U0001]. That window was due to end on 31 October 2026 [U0021]. Embassies negotiate these waivers country by country, and the process is slow [U0045]. One returnee learned of the waiver on Facebook, applied, bought a ticket and flew home on 14 June 2026 [U0036].
   **If you stay on after a window closes:** Cambodia's GDI said people who refused to leave in the window and were then detained are barred from Cambodia for life once deported [U0040].

> **Need to know**
> - Was the waiver extended past 31 October 2026, and is there a waiver open on 11 November 2026?
> - Where does the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok announce waiver windows (Facebook page URL)?

#### Step 2: Register with the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok

Bangladesh has no embassy in Cambodia; the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok, Thailand, handles Bangladeshis in Cambodia [U0016, U0017].

1. **Contact the embassy's labour wing and ask to be put on the return list.** The 1,796 people stranded in September 2026 had registered with the embassy during Cambodia's return window, and help requests reached it by phone, email, in person and through other Bangladeshis in Cambodia [U0010, U0028].
   - **Welfare hotline** for Bangladeshis in Thailand and Cambodia: +66-818-70-84-43 [B0175].
   - **Labour and welfare email:** fsbangkok@probashi.gov.bd [B0173]. The First Secretary (Labour) is Zahir Imam [U0018].
   - **Consular email:** consular.bangkok@mofa.gov.bd [B0173].
   Ready wording [U0010, U0028]: "I am [name], Bangladeshi passport [number], in [city], Cambodia. My visa expired on [date] / my passport is held by [employer or broker]. I want to return to Bangladesh. Please add me to the return list. Phone/WhatsApp: [number]."
2. **Expect a Cambodian interview.** Cambodian authorities interview people on the return list to check whether they are trafficking victims or linked to scam centres and trafficking networks; if they find someone entered irregularly, the interview is detailed [U0024, U0012]. Those found to be trafficking victims were allowed home [U0025]. In October 2026 this screening was paused, and the Bangladesh mission asked for it to restart [U0026]. Cambodian authorities had accepted a list of 1,797 Bangladeshis by mid-October, with about 400 more waiting to be added [U0128].

> **Need to know**

#### Step 3: Get a Travel Permit if you have no passport

1. **Apply for a Travel Permit,** a one-way document the embassy issues to a Bangladeshi without a passport who wants to go home [B0179].
   You'll need: copies of your old passport and its visa page, your air ticket, birth certificate or NID (national ID card), a police report (general diary) if the passport was lost, 2 photos and the filled-in form; the embassy's page says the applicant must attend an interview at the embassy [B0180].
   Fee: 550 baht (regular) or 1,100 baht (urgent) [B0181, B0182].
   In 2026 the embassy issued many Bangladeshis in Cambodia travel passes to return [U0061]. For people held at the Bati centre, release waits on their embassy issuing travel documents to those without passports [U0055].

> **Need to know**
> - With the Thai land border closed, how does someone in Cambodia without a passport attend the "interview at the embassy" in Bangkok: does the labour wing take applications in Phnom Penh, by video, or through a representative?
> - Is the 550-baht Travel Permit same-day or two working days? (The embassy's two pages differ.)

#### Step 4: Buy the ticket and leave

1. **Buy a refundable ticket.** On the embassy's instructions, many stranded Bangladeshis bought refundable tickets while they waited for clearance [U0011]. Returnees pay their own fares: the 30 released from detention in October 2026 bought their own tickets, one man said he bought tickets two or three times and only lost money, and one returnee spent USD 2,266, including overstay penalties, to get home [U0030, U0029, U0065]. High airfares and paperwork kept many people from leaving even when fines were waived [U0022]. In 2020 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs arranged special flights from Cambodia that returnees paid for themselves [U0092].
2. **Wait for immigration clearance before flying.** The 32 Bangladeshis turned back at Phnom Penh airport in September 2026 while waiting for immigration clearance were taken to a detention centre [U0007].
3. **If a broker stops you leaving,** tell the embassy labour wing (Step 2) at once [B0175]. Returnees told BRAC that Bangladeshis involved in trafficking in Cambodia intimidate victims trying to go home; one returnee said brokers' interference delayed his return by two months, and a stranded man's wife said his broker was stopping him from leaving [U0004, U0014, U0013].

> **Need to know**

#### Step 5: At Dhaka airport

1. **Expect questioning.** In October 2026 CID (the police Criminal Investigation Department) carried out forensic checks on returnees from Cambodia and examined their phones and belongings; one returnee had his phone seized [U0032, U0015].
2. **Get help home.** BRAC's Migration Programme gave returnees from Cambodia emergency help, counselling and money to reach home [U0003]. For WEWB help on arrival, see Coming home.

### Asylum

Asylum in Cambodia is for people who fear persecution, not for workers who lost their job or papers: a claim based purely on economic need or wanting a better life can be rejected immediately [U0103, U0114]. UNHCR says Cambodia's GDI looks after 26 refugees and 11 asylum seekers in total, and that problems remain with access to asylum and case processing [U0121, U0122]. UNHCR has no staff in Cambodia [U0123].

**Who counts as a refugee:** someone with a well-founded fear of persecution because of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion [U0103]. The Ministry of Interior decides claims, with the Immigration Department as its authority [U0104].

**The procedure:**

1. **Apply** to any Cambodian official, by any appropriate means; the Ministry of Interior decides, through its Immigration Department [U0105, U0104].
2. **Get your stay permission:** a free written authorisation to stay, either a visa in your passport or, without a passport, a letter signed and sealed by the Commissioner General of the National Police [U0106]. It lasts only while the procedure lasts [U0107].
3. **Return the form** completed and signed within one week [U0108].
4. **Attend the interview,** within 15 days of the form, before two Interior Ministry officials and one Foreign Ministry official; a qualified interpreter is free at every stage [U0109, U0117]. Missing the interview twice without good reason ends the case [U0116].
5. **Decision** in writing within 45 days of the interview, extendable to 90 days in all [U0110].
6. **If refused,** appeal once to the Immigration Department within 30 days, stating why; it decides within 15 days, its decision is final, and a refused applicant must leave Cambodia within 15 days [U0111, U0112, U0113]. There is no right to appeal to a court [U0126]. A claim rejected immediately (economic, or not cooperating) gets no appeal or review [U0127, U0115].

**If recognised:** a refugee may work or run a business under Cambodian law [U0118]. Status is cancelled if it was won with false information or documents [U0119].

**UNHCR:** asylum seekers can reach UNHCR through the Ministry of Interior at any time [U0120]. Cambodia is covered by UNHCR's office in Bangkok: 3rd A Floor, United Nations Building, Rajdamnern Nok Avenue, Bangkok 10200; +66 2 288 2180; thaba@unhcr.org [U0124, U0125].
