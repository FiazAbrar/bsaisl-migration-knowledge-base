## a. Draft doc text

### In one minute (draft for the lead; rewrite last)

Cambodia was a small destination for Bangladeshis until 2025, when BMET cleared 12,263 workers for it, almost all men recorded as construction workers for Chinese-run and Bangladeshi-owned building firms in Phnom Penh [N0005, N0014, N0047, N0048, N0074, N0078]. The official papers set the pay at USD 420 a month and the most an agency may take at about Tk 80,000-1.55 lakh, but workers report paying Tk 4-10 lakh, often to a village broker who promised a computer, data-entry or office job [N0062, N0073, N0063, N0109, N0110, N0118, N0150]. Many found no job: a BRAC study found 98% of people who returned from Cambodia had not got the promised job and 90% were forced into online scams [N0196]. Bangladesh has no embassy in Cambodia; its embassy in Bangkok covers it [N0141]. Clearances almost stopped in 2026, and since 4 November 2026 BMET may not clear anyone for Cambodia without the Ministry's prior approval [N0035-N0041, N0097].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

#### Status

On 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) not to issue emigration clearance for Cambodia, or for 11 other countries including Russia, Laos and Vietnam, without the Ministry's own recruitment permission or approval [N0097, N0104]. Every demand letter for these countries must now be approved first [N0106]. The Ministry gave suspected trafficking, deception and workers ending up in detention as its reasons [N0101]. BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) has told its member agencies by circular [N0098]. This is not a ban: in July 2026 the State Minister said there would be no blanket ban, but that workers for Cambodia would need proper work visas and full checks before clearance [N0116, N0115].

In practice the corridor has been nearly shut since spring 2026: BMET cleared 102 workers for Cambodia in April, 52 in May, 2 in June, 17 in July, 16 in August, 7 in September and none in October or 1-10 November 2026 [N0034-N0041]. In May 2026 BMET's Director General said clearance for Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam had been stopped since he took office three months earlier, and asked why people should go to countries no richer than Bangladesh [N0120, N0121].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Ministry approved any Cambodia demand letter since 4 November 2026, and which desk at the Ministry handles them?
> - Is the Ministry's 4 November 2026 letter (memo number) published on probashi.gov.bd or bmet.gov.bd?

#### The channels

- **Agency route with BMET permission (the official one):** a licensed Bangladeshi agency gets a BMET permission letter naming the Cambodian employer, job, number of workers, pay and the most it may take, then gets each worker's emigration clearance (the smart card) [N0072, N0076, N0062, N0063]. In 2025 the letters were for male construction workers for firms in Phnom Penh [N0076, N0081, N0083, N0072].
- **Small-batch "individual" route:** a demand letter for up to five workers could go to BMET without attestation by the Bangladesh embassy, and an estimated 70-75% of workers migrated this way; the State Minister said syndicates split large orders into small batches to avoid the embassy's checks [N0112, N0114, N0113]. In July 2026 the State Minister said Cambodia would no longer be allowed this exemption [N0115]. Prothom Alo reports the Ministry's approval was not needed for demand letters of up to seven workers before 4 November 2026 [N0105].
- **Tourist or visit visa with a broker's job promise (irregular):** the Ministry warns that brokers send people to Cambodia on tourist and visit visas with promises of jobs, and tells people not to go abroad for work this way [N0092, N0093]. Rights groups link the corridor's growth since 2021 to easily obtained online tourist visas used alongside a BMET clearance [N0122] (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers).

There is no government-to-government recruitment channel: Bangladesh and Cambodia signed a labour and vocational-training memorandum of understanding (MoU) on 4 December 2017 and renewed it in May 2022, but no recruitment scheme under it was found [N0180, N0183, N0181].

> **Need to know**
> - Has BOESL (the state recruiting agency) ever sent workers to Cambodia, or is there any plan under the renewed 2022 MoU?

#### Who qualifies

The 2025 BMET permission letters were for men aged 20-45 on 2-year contracts [N0064, N0075, N0079]. BMET cleared one woman a year for Cambodia in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and two in 2026 to 10 November [N0008-N0011].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET require a TTC (Technical Training Centre) construction skill certificate for Cambodia construction clearances?

#### Size

- 2021: none [N0001]
- 2022: 368 [N0002]
- 2023: 1,378, 18th of Bangladesh's destinations [N0003, N0012]
- 2024: 929, 23rd [N0004, N0013]
- 2025: 12,263, 8th [N0005, N0014]
- 2026 to 10 November: 3,700 (12th to 7 November) [N0006, N0015]

The 2025 rise came late in the year: 1,469 clearances in September, 3,235 in October (the highest month on record), 2,469 in November and 2,550 in December 2025, then 2,223 in January 2026 before the fall [N0027-N0031]. BRAC says about 16,000 people went with BMET clearance but there is no way to know where they are or what they are doing [N0148].

Job titles on the clearances for 2025: Construction Worker 3,428, Construction Labour 2,849, A Builder 1,456, Building/Construction Helper 1,081, Cleaner 678, A Casual Worker 429, Production Manager 388, Quality Controller 300, Interpreter 30, Garment Production Manager 20 [N0047-N0056]. In 2026 to 10 November the top titles were A Casual Worker 736, Farm Worker 580, A Builder 390, Agriculture Labour 250, and 25 were recorded as "Agent" [N0057-N0061].

On the Cambodian side, the labour minister said in June 2022 that about 400 Bangladeshis held Cambodian work permits [N0179]. In 2022 the Bangladesh embassy attested requisitions for 210 workers while BMET registered 3,127 [N0163]. In July 2026 the embassy estimated 4,000-4,500 Bangladeshis were overstaying in Cambodia [N0137].

Compared with other countries: Cambodia's General Department of Immigration reported deporting 52,039 foreigners from 87 countries in January-September 2026, including about 23,000 Chinese, over 8,000 Indonesians, over 4,000 Vietnamese, over 3,000 Bangladeshis, over 2,500 Pakistanis, about 2,000 Burmese and over 1,500 Thais [N0191, N0192, N0193].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis hold a valid MLVT (Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training) work permit in 2026?
> - How many Indians and Nepalis were cleared or deported in 2025-2026, for comparison?

#### What closed it before

- **2022-2023 attestation gap:** after reports of workers sold to scam companies, the Ministry found BMET had registered 3,127 workers against 210 attested by the embassy; BMET's then Director General said stopping registration would hurt remittances [N0163, N0164].
- **Thailand-Cambodia border fighting (2025):** in July 2025 the embassy in Bangkok opened a hotline for Bangladeshis in Cambodia and its labour counsellor met displaced workers in Phnom Penh [N0189, N0190]. In December 2025 over 150 Bangladeshi construction workers fled a site near the Thai border after bombing on 9 December; immigration police detained them, though they said most had valid visas and work permits, and asked each for USD 800 within two days to send them home [N0184, N0185, N0186, N0187].
- **2026 slowdown and the 4 November 2026 order:** see Status above [N0120, N0097].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

#### The root: a demand letter that can be split

A job reaches Bangladesh as a demand letter from a Cambodian employer, which the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok is meant to attest before an agency recruits and gets BMET clearance [N0111, N0162]. Because batches of up to five workers did not need the embassy's attestation, syndicates split large orders into small ones, and most workers went this way [N0112, N0113, N0114]. In 2022 a scam victim's agent said work visas were not available, so he would go on a business visa [N0165].

#### The employers

The 2025 BMET permission letters name Phnom Penh construction firms, several of them Chinese-run: China State Construction (Cambodia) Co. Ltd., Asbic Construction, Xin Cai Fu (Cambodia), Yong Sheng Global Trading, Chip Hong Insee, Sim Sia Cambodia, Sok Hang Group, Vain SR Construction and Phanimex Group [N0083, N0074, N0078, N0082]. Others appear to be Bangladeshi-owned: Royal Bengol Engineering and Constructors, Abrar Pvt (Cambodia), Maliha Jamman M.S Co. and Golden Cresent Development [N0074, N0071]. The Bangladesh government first explored Cambodia in October 2019 as a market for construction workers on Chinese-financed projects [N0204].

> **Need to know**
> - Which of these employers really took the workers named in the permission letters, and are they still hiring?

#### The Bangladeshi agencies

Agencies with BMET permission letters for Cambodia in 2025 (agency, licence, date, workers, cost cap), with their OEP licence status on the agency list:
- **Puspita Overseas (RL-2648), Fakirapool, Dhaka:** 24 March 2025, 144 construction workers, Tk 80,770; Active [N0076, N0077, N0086]. A Sirajganj victim says he paid most of Tk 7 lakh to "Pushpita Overseas in Fakirapool" for a promised computer job and was trafficked into a scam compound [N0126, N0127].
- **Al-Grand Sultan Overseas (RL-1582), Savar:** 20 April 2025, 96 construction workers, Tk 80,520; Active [N0081, N0091].
- **Fahim Recruiting Agency (RL-2594), Naya Paltan:** 25 June 2025, 96 construction workers, Tk 1,05,000; Suspended [N0083, N0084, N0087]. A Chapainawabganj returnee paid Tk 4.8 lakh through a local middleman and went through Fahim, which sent about 200 workers in a year [N0110]. Its letter says the other terms follow "Malaysia's labour law" [N0085].
- **Mongol Global Services (RL-2527):** 31 August 2025, Tk 1,55,450; Suspended [N0063, N0088].
- **SAM International (RL-1203):** 10 September 2025, 100 construction workers, Tk 80,770 [N0072, N0073].

Other names in the press: Ronnie Tours and Travels and MR International (a Rajbari man paid Tk 10 lakh and was sent on a tourist visa) [N0118, N0119]; Alif International Agency (a Narsingdi man sent on 30 October 2025 for computer work, who found a scam centre) [N0197]; Mashallah Overseas (2022) [N0169]; and M/S Right Job, a licensed agency in Dhanmondi that advertised a USD 600 tiles-installer job on Facebook for Tk 3.5 lakh in June 2022 [N0175]. In 2023 the police counter-terrorism unit said it had found criminal rings linked to about six recruiting agencies trafficking Bangladeshis to Cambodia [N0177].

> **Need to know**
> - Why were Fahim and Mongol Global suspended, and was it for Cambodia cases?

#### The middlemen

Most cases start with a local middleman: a BRAC study found 66% of cyber-scam victims were recruited through middlemen [N0103]. A friend or relative pitches a computer-operator, data-entry, factory or customer-service job paying Tk 50,000 to over Tk 1 lakh a month, "faster than Saudi" [N0124, N0133, N0150]. Ads run in WhatsApp and Facebook groups, and middlemen run Telegram groups with fake "successful migrants" [N0156, N0157, N0176]. A Bangladeshi businessman in Cambodia said in 2022 that workers are lured with USD 1,000 salaries, charged a couple of thousand dollars, then sold to casinos for USD 2,000-3,000 [N0168]. Agents handed one group's passports to a company and took USD 3,600 a head [N0167].

#### The BMET card as a trust signal

A BMET clearance card makes a fake job look legal: BRAC says it is used as a trust signal [N0123]. Victims went with BMET smart cards on visas naming construction work while they had been promised office jobs, and the broker told one the construction title was "temporary" [N0170, N0128, N0129, N0153]. One Lakshmipur returnee with BMET clearance found he had only a one-month visit visa [N0136]. When checks tightened, recruiters used fake BMET cards or sent workers to Thailand as tourists and moved them on to Cambodia [N0138]. In one case a victim's fingerprint did not match at the airport Probashi desk, and an official called his agency and let him board [N0178].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

#### The cap

The most an agency may take for Cambodia is set in each BMET permission letter: Tk 80,520-80,770 for Puspita, Al-Grand Sultan and SAM International, Tk 1,05,000 for Fahim and Tk 1,55,450 for Mongol Global in 2025 [N0077, N0081, N0073, N0084, N0063]. The employer is to pay all costs of getting to Cambodia, such as levy and airfare [N0068]. The work permit and visa fees are paid by the employer in advance and then deducted from the worker's wage [N0066].

#### What people really pay

- Tk 4.8 lakh, Fahim Recruiting Agency via a middleman (2025) [N0110]
- Up to Tk 5.3 lakh for promised IT and computer-operator jobs [N0109]
- Tk 5 lakh, construction worker displaced in the border fighting [N0188]
- Tk 2-7 lakh, most scam victims [N0151]
- Tk 7 lakh, promised Tk 80,000 a month [N0126]
- Tk 7.70 lakh for a data-entry job [N0152]
- Tk 10 lakh, sent on a tourist visa [N0118]
- USD 2,835-4,450 paid to brokers [N0158]

A Bangla visa-guide site quotes Tk 4-6 lakh through an agency, Tk 6-8 lakh through a broker and Tk 2-3 lakh through a relative in Cambodia [N0199, N0200, N0201].

#### How it is paid

Payment is made in parts: one man paid Tk 1 lakh to the broker and the rest to the agency [N0127]. His first meeting with the broker was at a tea stall across the highway from Dhaka airport; at the airport he was told to stand by a pillar and send a WhatsApp photo, a man got his boarding pass, and a broker's contact handed him USD 2,000 cash before boarding [N0125, N0130, N0131].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the USD 2,000 handed over at the airport for (show money at Phnom Penh immigration, or money for the receiving handler)?

#### How they borrow

Families borrow: one family borrowed much of Tk 6 lakh for a promised Tk 1.5 lakh-a-month computer job, and the father then spent about Tk 13 lakh over 1.5 years keeping his son alive in Cambodia [N0145, N0146]. A woman borrowed for a Cambodia job promising Tk 80,000 a month [N0139].

#### What the job pays

The 2025 permission letters set USD 420 a month for construction workers, 8 hours a day, 6 days (48 hours) a week [N0062, N0065, N0075, N0079]. Guide sites put construction and farm pay at USD 300-400, and Cambodian pay generally at USD 250-500 a month [N0203, N0208]. The promised Tk 80,000-1.5 lakh a month office jobs do not match any of these [N0126, N0145, N0150].

#### What comes off

The work permit and visa fees are deducted from the wage [N0066]. In 2019 a Bangladesh government official estimated USD 287 a year for the visa renewal, USD 160 for the work permit and about USD 100 a month for food, against pay of USD 400-450 [N0206 (2019)].

#### How long the debt takes

The same official warned in 2019 that a worker who pays extra to go to Cambodia is unlikely to earn it back, because higher pay is not available there [N0207 (2019)]. One returnee who applied for a casino job spent USD 4,047 and earned USD 972 [N0160].

> **Need to know**
> - How many months does a construction worker on USD 420 need to repay Tk 4.8 lakh, after the deducted visa and permit fees, food and housing?
> - Do the Phnom Penh construction employers provide housing and food, or deduct them?

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

#### What goes wrong most

- **No job at all:** 98% of returnees in a BRAC study found no job, and 90% were forced into online fraud [N0196]. BRAC says over 1,000 of the 15,921 cleared in 18 months ended up in cyber-scam compounds [N0108] (see Scam compounds and trafficking).
- **A different job and visa from the one promised:** visas name construction while the promise was office work [N0128, N0153, N0134].
- **Visa and permit never provided:** recruiters promised a one-year visa and work permit but never provided them [N0154].
- **Stranded with expired visas:** the embassy in Bangkok has had requests about 1,796 Bangladeshis stranded in Cambodia; the government counts 1,300-1,400 awaiting return, and the Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation over 2,000 [N0142, N0143]. One Manikganj man has been stranded 3.5 years, jobless, on an expired visa [N0147].
- **Deportation:** Cambodia's Ministry of Interior deported 3,364 Bangladeshis between 1 September 2023 and 13 September 2026, 852 of them linked to online scams [N0149].
- **Deaths:** the bodies of three Bangladeshis were brought home from Cambodia on 8 October 2026, including Sohag Ahmed, 23, who went in April 2025 with BMET clearance [N0102].
- **Unpaid construction work:** a Pirojpur man went as a construction worker and worked almost unpaid for 1.5 years while brokers held passports and gave USD 50 a month [N0171, N0172, N0173].
- **Leaving the assigned employer:** a Fahim official says some workers leave for better-paid jobs and the employers will not take them back [N0117].

#### What people wrongly believe

- **"A BMET card means the job is real."** Victims with BMET smart cards were trafficked, and BMET has no record of where the 16,000 cleared are [N0170, N0148].
- **"Cambodia pays Tk 80,000-1.5 lakh for office work."** The official rate is USD 420 for construction, and WARBE's chair says Cambodia's appeal is manufactured by agencies selling IT or hospitality jobs [N0062, N0159].
- **"Cambodia is a door to a richer country."** BRAC says many are taken to Cambodia with promises of high-paying jobs in developed countries before being forced into scams [N0194] (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers).
- **"Cambodian garment factories are hiring Bangladeshis."** A guide site says so, but BMET cleared 20 garment production managers and no garment workers in 2025 [N0202, N0056, N0047-N0055].
- **"Go on a tourist visa and the agency will fix the work visa."** The Ministry tells people not to go for work on a tourist or visit visa [N0093].
- **"If cheated, the broker will repay."** The CID (Criminal Investigation Department) says few returnees file cases and many negotiate with brokers for money back [N0140].

### Work visa: Scams (ordinary job scams; agent S has the compounds)

Signs a Cambodia job offer is a trap:
- The job is computer operator, data entry, customer service, receptionist or casino work at Tk 50,000 to over Tk 1 lakh a month [N0133, N0150, N0174, N0160].
- The visa or invitation names construction, or it is a tourist or one-month visit visa, while the job promised is office work [N0128, N0153, N0136, N0119].
- The broker says the visa title is "temporary" or that a work visa is unavailable so you'll go on a business visa [N0129, N0165].
- The ad is in a WhatsApp, Telegram or Facebook group, with "successful migrants" vouching [N0156, N0157, N0176].
- You are told to meet near the airport, stand at a pillar, and are handed cash before boarding [N0125, N0130, N0131].
- You are routed through Thailand as a tourist, or via China [N0138, N0132].
- The price is several lakh taka, far above the cap in the BMET permission letter [N0134, N0077, N0063].

**Check it is real:** ask the agency for the BMET permission letter for Cambodia and compare the employer, job, pay (USD 420 for construction) and cost cap with your offer [N0062, N0063, N0076]. Check the agency's licence status on the OEP agency list (oep.gov.bd/agencies): Fahim and Mongol Global are suspended [N0087, N0088].

> **Need to know**
> - Where can a worker see a BMET permission letter for his own job (bmet.gov.bd miscellaneous-infos only, or does the agency have to show it)?
> - Does BMET's smart-card check (QR) show the visa type, so a worker can see a tourist visa before flying?

**If cheated:** the CID says few file cases [N0140] (see If you aren't paid, and Scam compounds and trafficking for rescue routes). The Bangkok embassy covers Cambodia; its 2025 hotline was +66 81 870 843 [N0141, N0189].
