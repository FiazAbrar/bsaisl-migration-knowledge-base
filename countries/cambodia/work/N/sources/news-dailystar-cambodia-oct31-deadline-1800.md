# Stranded in Cambodia: Oct 31 deadline looms over 1,800 Bangladeshis

Mohammad Sentu has been stranded in Cambodia for three and a half years. Jobless and with an expired visa, he has repeatedly tried to return to Bangladesh but failed.

“I have been cheated repeatedly. I bought tickets two or three times but lost the money,” said Sentu, from Singair in Manikganj.

“I have no money left to buy a ticket,” he said. He now occasionally cooks for other Bangladeshis in exchange for food.

Sentu is among hundreds of Bangladeshis stranded in Cambodia after being lured by promises of lucrative jobs. Some were allegedly forced to work in online scam centres and tortured when they tried to escape.

### [How Bangladeshi jobseekers are trafficked into Cambodia's scam compounds](/nrb/migration/manpower-fraud/news/how-bangladeshi-jobseekers-are-trafficked-cambodias-scam-compounds-4224436)

Government data show that around 1,300 to 1,400 Bangladeshis are awaiting return, while Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, a survivor-led rights organisation, estimates that more than 2,000 remain stranded. Around 1,800 face uncertainty over an October 31 deadline linked to immigration overstay penalties, the organisation said.

The plight of those trying to return came into focus on September 3, when Cambodian immigration police detained 32 Bangladeshis at Phnom Penh airport as they attempted to leave after allegedly being exploited in online scam centres.

Sources said they were detained over irregular immigration status and other complications during immigration checks.

> ## The Bangkok mission had received requests concerning 1,796 Bangladeshis stranded in Cambodia through phone calls, emails, direct applications and community contacts.

Two of them were released earlier, while the remaining 30 were held at an immigration detention centre until Friday evening following prolonged diplomatic negotiations.

The 30 people are expected to return in the next couple of days, Zahir Imam, first secretary (labour) at the Bangladesh Embassy in Thailand, told The Daily Star. Sources said they would pay for their own tickets.

However, the fate of hundreds of others remains uncertain, including those unable to afford tickets home.

Bangladesh has no embassy in Cambodia, and its Bangkok mission handles consular affairs there.

Imam said the mission had received requests concerning 1,796 Bangladeshis stranded in Cambodia through phone calls, emails, direct applications and community contacts.

Around 350 to 400 have returned since July, but the mission could not independently verify all the names or establish the current whereabouts and circumstances of everyone on its list, he said.

Cambodian authorities had interviewed Bangladeshis seeking repatriation to determine whether they were trafficking victims or linked to scam centres and trafficking networks. Those verified as victims were allowed to return, Imam said.

The process has temporarily stopped, and the Bangladesh mission has requested its resumption.

Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation estimates that more than 2,000 Bangladeshis remain stranded, with around 1,800 facing uncertainty over the October 31 deadline under an arrangement concerning immigration overstay penalties. Its president and co-founder, Al Amin Nayon, said the organisation had sought clarification on the arrangement’s conditions, eligibility criteria and provisions for waiving fines.

Nayon said the foundation would submit a memorandum to the expatriates’ welfare and overseas employment minister today, with copies to the foreign affairs and home ministries. It is demanding urgent repatriation, individual screening to identify trafficking victims, protection from punishment for crimes committed under coercion, rehabilitation and action against trafficking networks.

**FAMILIES IN DEBT**

Kabir Hossain said he had spent around Tk 13 lakh supporting his 24-year-old son Shawon since he travelled to Cambodia nearly one and a half years ago.

Shawon was promised a computer-related job paying around Tk 1.5 lakh a month. His family borrowed much of the Tk 6 lakh needed for his journey, but he could not send money home. Instead, Kabir repeatedly sent money for his son’s food, accommodation and other expenses.

“Now I don’t know how I will repay these debts,” Kabir, who works for a private company, told The Daily Star.

He said he had lost money on two attempts to arrange his son’s return and was preparing to buy another ticket, hoping Shawon could return on October 19 or 20.

**QUESTIONS OVER OVERSIGHT**

Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training (BMET) records show that 15,963 Bangladeshis received employment clearance for Cambodia between January 1, 2025, and September 30, 2026.

Shariful Hasan, associate director of BRAC’s Migration Programme and Youth Platform, questioned the lack of effective monitoring of workers’ employers, working conditions and whereabouts despite the clearances.

“We have sent around 16,000 people there with BMET clearance, yet we do not have a mechanism to find out where they are or what they are doing,” he told The Daily Star.

He cited allegations of harassment and torture against Bangladeshis trying to leave Cambodia. Survivors have described being lured by promises of legitimate jobs and then forced into cyber scam operations. Some said their passports were confiscated and they faced threats and extortion.

On October 8, the bodies of three Bangladeshis were brought home from Cambodia, including 23-year-old Sohag Ahmed of Jhenaidah, who allegedly became trapped in a scam compound after travelling with BMET clearance.

Shariful said the government must identify those stranded, establish their circumstances and ensure their safe return.
