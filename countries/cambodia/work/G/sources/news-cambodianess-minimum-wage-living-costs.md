## Cambodia’s \$2 Minimum Wage Increase Raises Concerns Over Rising Living Costs

![Cambodia’s \$2 Minimum Wage Increase Raises Concerns Over Rising Living Costs](https://img.cambodianess.com/uploads/article/10-2026/1790852795.jpg "Cambodia’s $2 Minimum Wage Increase Raises Concerns Over Rising Living Costs")

Garment workers in Phnom Penh walk out for lunch during their break time. Photo by Phoeurng Phearak

By:

[Phoeurng Phearak](https://cambodianess.com/by_author?name=phoeurng-phearak)

#####  October 1, 2026,  6:02 PM

00:00 / 00:00

**PHNOM PENH – Minimum wages for workers in the garment, footwear and travel goods sectors will rise by \$2 to \$212 a month next year. However, workers and union representatives say this will do little to ease pressure from rising living costs.**

The Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training announced the increase on Sept. 30 after the National Council for Minimum Wage voted overwhelmingly to retain the existing \$210 minimum wage for 2027.

The government says workers will also continue to get allowances and other benefits on top of their basic wage.

For Nita, a 31-year-old worker from Kampong Chhnang who has worked in Phnom Penh for five years, the increase is welcome but too small to significantly change her finances.

“In the city, food is expensive and there are water and electricity bills and rent. There are many expenses,” she said.

After paying for her room, utilities, food and other costs, Nita said little is left. “I just get by,” she said.

Nita said she would need to work overtime to bring her monthly income to about \$320, including transport, accommodation and food allowances.

The decision followed negotiations between employers and worker representatives.

Employers proposed keeping the minimum wage at \$210, while worker representatives proposed \$225. The \$210 figure received 47 of 51 votes from the National Council before Prime Minister Hun Manet added another \$2.

Trade unions had initially pushed for a much higher figure of \$250.66. In a joint statement, [](https://central-cambodia.org/publication/statements/join-statement/civil-society-organizations-unite-in-solidarity-to-support-trade-unions%E2%80%99-proposal-for--minimum-wage-increase-to-usd-250.66-for-2027) [125 civil society organizations](https://central-cambodia.org/publication/statements/join-statement/civil-society-organizations-unite-in-solidarity-to-support-trade-unions%E2%80%99-proposal-for--minimum-wage-increase-to-usd-250.66-for-2027) supported the unions’ proposal, saying it was based on research into workers’ living conditions and expenses.

The groups cited studies showing that average individual earnings covered about 60 percent of average household expenditure, while a 2025 study found average monthly take-home earnings of \$287 compared with median household spending of \$391.

Yang Sophorn, president of the Cambodian Alliance of Trade Unions, said she was disappointed with the increase, arguing that it did not adequately reflect workers’ living costs.

“A \$2 increase cannot respond to inflation in the market,” she said.

She also raised concerns about workers’ diets, saying some workers struggle to afford enough nutritious food.

Yang said unions had sought a \$250 minimum wage, with \$225 as another proposed figure, but negotiations also had to consider employers’ concerns about operating costs and the possibility that investment could move to other countries.

**Hike Acceptable Under Current Conditions**

Economist Hong Vannak said the different positions reflected the interests of both sides, with workers seeking higher wages and employers seeking to contain costs.

“The important thing is negotiation and finding a balance that both sides can accept,” he said.

Vannak said global economic uncertainty, including the effects of wars and geopolitical tensions, had also created difficulties for employers.

He said the additional \$2 would not fully offset inflation and rising prices.

However, he said the increase still represented an additional benefit for workers given the economic conditions and the government’s ability to raise wages.

The government defended the decision as a balance between workers’ livelihoods, employers’ competitiveness and employment opportunities.

Labour Minister Heng Sour said the minimum-wage process considers three main factors: workers’ living costs, the country’s competitiveness and labour-market conditions.

The government has also said workers will continue receiving other mandatory benefits and allowances in addition to the \$212 minimum wage.

According to the government, the total amount workers receive depends on allowances, seniority and other benefits.

The new minimum wage will take effect on Jan. 1. The garment, footwear and travel goods sectors employ nearly a million workers in Cambodia, the majority of whom are women.

The pace of wage increases has slowed in recent years. The minimum wage rose from \$194 in 2022 to \$200 in 2023 and \$204 in 2024, before reaching \$208 in 2025 and \$210 in 2026.
