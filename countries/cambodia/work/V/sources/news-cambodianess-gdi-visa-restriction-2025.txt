PHNOM PENH – The General Department of Immigration (GDI) has affirmed that Cambodia does not have a policy of denying visas, entry, or visa extensions to any nationality. However, immigration officers have recently implemented stricter measures aimed at preventing cross-border crimes and maintaining public order.

This clarification follows social media reports and comments from some foreigners alleging increased restrictions by immigration authorities on entry visa applications and visa extensions for certain nationalities in Cambodia.

In a statement posted on its official Facebook page on July 7, the GDI highlighted a recent rise in the number of foreigners suspected of crimes abroad attempting to enter Cambodia.

As a result, immigration officers have intensified efforts to safeguard national security, uphold public order, and combat cross-border crimes, including human trafficking.

“Under the immigration law and relevant regulations, officers may refuse entry or deny extensions of temporary stay in Cambodia to individuals of any nationality who fail to meet various requirements,” the statement said.

These requirements include having a clear travel history and purpose, demonstrating the ability to financially support oneself during the stay, providing a guarantor to resolve potential issues, and ensuring there is no reasonable suspicion that the individual is involved in activities that threaten public order, social morality, or national and international security.

The department emphasized that no new restrictive policies have been introduced; rather, these measures are part of the normal duties of immigration officers, who carry out their work with a strong sense of responsibility.

On June 30, GDI Director General Sok Veasna reported that Cambodia deported nearly 5,000 foreigners during the first half of 2025, spanning over 2,000 cases involving 88 nationalities.

Veasna noted that this figure represents an increase compared to the same period last year, with most deportations related to illegal use of explosives, unlawful immigration, unauthorized entry, illegal employment, and online fraud.

On July 3, 38 Vietnamese nationals—including 11 women—were deported through the Bavet International Border Gate Police Station in Svay Rieng province. The deportations were due to illegal stay, working without passports, unauthorized border crossing, and drug trafficking offenses.
