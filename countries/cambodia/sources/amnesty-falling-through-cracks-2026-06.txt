In July 2025, the Cambodian government launched
the largest nationwide crackdown on scamming
operations to date. It issued a 9-point order "for
the campaign to crackdown on online scams",
with Prime Minister Hun Manet acknowledging the
growing threat posed by the industry and instructing
provincial officials, law enforcement agencies, the
courts, and the national gambling commission, to
take action. Since then, the government has claimed
to have shuttered more than 250 "scam centres",
opened criminal cases against 1,089 suspects
and deported 13,039 foreign nationals involved
in online scams.

HUMAN RIGHTS ABUSES

Two survivors interviewed by Amnesty International
said they spent weeks at this compound; one of them
(Survivor F) was lied to during recruitment about the
work they would be doing; believed they could not
escape because of the presence of security guards;
and was subjected to torture or other ill-treatment.

A second survivor (Survivor M) had their passport
confiscated by managers, was deprived of their
liberty at the site, told Amnesty International they
witnessed the torture and other ill-treatment of other
people at the site (including Survivor F) and provided
photographs to corroborate some of the story.

Based on this testimony, Amnesty International
determined that Survivor F was highly likely
trafficked into the compound, they were forced to
work, they were subjected to torture or other ill-
treatment, and they were deprived of their liberty.
Additionally, Amnesty International determined that
Survivor M was likely deprived of their liberty at the
site and was a victim of human trafficking.
