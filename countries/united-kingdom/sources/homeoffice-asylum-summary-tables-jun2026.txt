1	Appeals data for April 2023 onwards was unavailable at the point of data extraction for this release due to ongoing work as immigration data transitions to a new case working system. The data in this table is the most recent available, to March 2023. (Asy_06_07) Updated data will be included in a future edition of the Immigration system statistics release.
2	Statistics on ‘Asylum claims by prior route’ are classified as 'Official Statistics in Development' in line with the Official Statistics policy. This label means that the underlying methodologies and data-matching processes, may not yet be fully developed, and the statistics have not yet undergone the full quality assurance processes applied to ‘Accredited Official Statistics’. As such, the figures presented might be subject to future revision. Further information can be found in the Office for National Statistics guide to ‘Official Statistics in Development’.
3	Summary tables Asy_03b and Asy_03c will no longer be produced after this release. This reflects declining user relevance for the breakdown presented, with information on total claims and decisions remaining available in other tables.
4	The ‘Removals’ figures in summary table Asy_02e, People considered under inadmissibility rules, were updated in the August 2026 release to include individuals returned to France under the UK-France Agreement on the Prevention of Dangerous Journeys, to improve the consistency of the measures in the table. Previously, these individuals were not included in the removals figures but were included in the other figures in the table.

26	Provisions within the Nationality and Borders Act 2022 (NABA), which came into force on 28 June 2022, set out the framework to differentiate between two groups of refugees who ultimately remain in the UK: “Group 1” and “Group 2”. Those who fail to meet one or more of these requirements will be ‘Group 2 refugees’ and may be given lesser entitlements as a result. However, the differentiation policy was paused in the July 2023 Immigration Rules changes. This means we have stopped taking grouping decisions under the differentiated asylum system and those individuals who are successful in their asylum application, including those who are granted humanitarian protection, will receive the same conditions.
