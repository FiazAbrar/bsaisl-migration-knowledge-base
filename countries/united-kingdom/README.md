## 1 Background

<a id="in-one-minute"></a>

### 1.1 In one minute

In this reporting Bangladeshis reach the UK mainly as students, and some come on student, visit or work visas and then claim asylum: about 11,000 did so in 2023, and 95% of those claims were refused <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Since May 2024 a UK-Bangladesh agreement lets the UK send back refused asylum seekers, offenders and overstayers by a fast-track procedure <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Since 1 June 2026 the UK has tightened its test of universities whose foreign students are refused visas, and in response at least 42 UK institutions have closed or limited admission for Bangladeshi students, 20 of them for students from the Sylhet region in particular <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. Using a forged document or an illegal route to reach the UK brings a 10-year ban on entering <sup>[N3](#n3), [N4](#n4)</sup>. The Prothom Alo reporting says nothing on work visas, including care-worker visas, so those are open questions below.

<a id="is-it-open-now-and-who-can-go"></a>

### 1.2 Is it open now, and who can go?

Open to students who get past a university's checks and the visa decision, but admission for Bangladeshis is now closed or limited at many universities, as of October 2026 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="status"></a>

#### 1.2.1 Status

- **16 May 2024:** the UK and Bangladesh signed an agreement for the return of Bangladeshis staying in the UK irregularly (see [Removal to Bangladesh](#removal-to-bangladesh)) <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **July 2024:** before the general election, Labour leader Keir Starmer said his government would send so-called illegal migrants back soon after taking power, naming Bangladeshis as an example <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.
- **15 July 2025:** student visas for Bangladeshis became e-visas, with no sticker in the passport (see [Step 3: Set up your e-visa before you fly](#step-3-set-up-your-e-visa-before-you-fly)) <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
- **25 November 2025:** the British High Commissioner in Dhaka announced a 10-year travel ban for anyone using visa fraud or an illegal route (see [Visa fraud and the 10-year ban](#visa-fraud-and-the-10-year-ban)) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
- **1 June 2026:** UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration, the visa department of the Home Office, the UK's interior ministry) began a stricter test of universities that sponsor foreign students (see [Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away](#why-universities-are-turning-bangladeshis-away)) <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **September 2026 intake:** a consultant at a student-recruitment firm said several hundred Bangladeshi students lost their university sponsorship after being called for a UKVI interview <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="the-channels"></a>

#### 1.2.2 The channels

- **Student visa:** the main route in this reporting (see [Student visa](#student-visa)) <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **Commonwealth Scholarship:** UK-government-funded master's study for a small number with an outstanding academic record (see [Commonwealth Scholarship](#commonwealth-scholarship)) <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.
- **Asylum after arriving on another visa:** about 11,000 Bangladeshis in 2023, almost all refused (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Not a legal route:** crossing the English Channel from Europe (see [Irregular entry and removal](#irregular-entry-and-removal)) <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Do Bangladeshis go to the UK on the Skilled Worker visa, including the care-worker route, and does BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which clears Bangladeshis going abroad for work) clear them? How many went each year since 2022?
> - What are the requirements, fees and processing time for a UK visit visa from Dhaka, and what share of Bangladeshi applicants are refused?
> - Can a Bangladeshi student bring a spouse and children as dependants in 2026, and for which courses?

<a id="size"></a>

#### 1.2.3 Size

- **British Bangladeshis:** 451,529 Bangladeshis lived in Britain as British citizens according to the 2011 government statistics, 52% of them born in Britain, three North South University researchers wrote <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.
- **Asylum claims:** about 11,000 Bangladeshis in 2023 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Commonwealth Scholarships:** 1,867 Bangladeshis since 1960, 26 in 2024 <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.
- **Remittance:** see Sending money home <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many study visas did the UK grant to Bangladeshis each year from 2023 to 2026?

<a id="how-a-job-reaches-you-and-who-takes-a-cut"></a>

### 1.3 How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

For students, the way in is a university's admission, often through a student-recruitment agency; the university, not the agency, issues the CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, the reference a student needs to apply for the visa) and can withdraw it (see [Step 2: Apply for the visa](#step-2-apply-for-the-visa)) <sup>[N4](#n4), [N2](#n2)</sup>. Gangs promise a guaranteed visa for extra fees, a job without the right qualifications, or fast processing, and the result is lost money, refusal, a travel ban or trafficking, the British High Commission said <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Leading UK universities want access to UKVI's data on recruitment agents' behaviour <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. For promises that a student can work freely, see "Student visas used as work routes" in the Illegal routes doc.

> **Need to know**
> - What do Dhaka and Sylhet student-recruitment agencies charge students, and how much do UK universities pay them per student?
> - How is a UK care-worker or other work visa sold in Bangladesh, and for how much?

<a id="what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep"></a>

### 1.4 What you pay, and what you earn and keep

Tighter admission, rising tuition fees, the taka's fall against the pound, and higher housing and visa costs in the UK put heavy financial pressure on students, and many now choose other countries, Global Admission's student adviser for the Sylhet region said in October 2026 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What are the UK student visa fee, the Immigration Health Surcharge and the money a student must show in the bank in 2026?
> - How many hours a week may a student work in term time, and what is the minimum wage?

<a id="what-ties-you-to-the-employer"></a>

### 1.5 What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** the university is the visa sponsor; it can reassess an application and withdraw its sponsorship before the visa is decided <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **Universities:** a university that fails UKVI's test may have to take corrective action, and serious or repeated failure risks losing its sponsor licence (its permission to admit foreign students) <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What happens to a student's visa if the university loses its sponsor licence after the student has arrived?
> - Can a care worker or other Skilled Worker visa holder change employer, and how long do they have to find a new sponsor if the job ends?

<a id="what-goes-wrong-most-and-what-people-wrongly-believe"></a>

### 1.6 What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are university offers withdrawn from Bangladeshi students, visa refusals, asylum claims refused and followed by removal, and visa fraud that ends in a 10-year ban <sup>[N2](#n2), [N1 (2024)](#n1), [N3](#n3)</sup>.

<a id="what-people-believe-and-the-facts"></a>

#### 1.6.1 What people believe, and the facts

- **"Claim asylum once you are in."** About 95% of the claims made by Bangladeshis in 2023 were refused, and refused claimants are now returned under the 2024 agreement <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **"Pay extra and the visa is guaranteed."** The High Commission says such promises come from criminal gangs and end in refusal, a travel ban or worse <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
- **"A university offer means you will go."** Universities have withdrawn offers and sponsorship from Bangladeshi students, including after a UKVI interview call <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="irregular-routes-and-where-an-irregular-stay-ends"></a>

#### 1.6.2 Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **The English Channel from Europe:** researchers wrote in May 2024 that many Bangladeshis who reached Italy irregularly tried to cross to the UK <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. The Bangladesh High Commission wrote to Keir Starmer in 2024 that no Bangladeshi had entered the UK illegally by boat <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.
- **Staying on after a refusal or an expired visa:** it ends in removal to Bangladesh (see [Removal to Bangladesh](#removal-to-bangladesh)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

<a id="student-visa"></a>

## 2 Student visa

The student visa is the main legal route in this reporting, and it is getting harder for Bangladeshis <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. In the first six months of 2026 the rate reported for Bangladeshi students' visa applications rose to 23.5% from 7.1% a year earlier, the second highest after Pakistan, according to the Financial Times' analysis of Home Office data; across all countries it rose from 2.3% to 10.5% <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the 23.5% figure for Bangladeshi students the share of applications refused or withdrawn? Prothom Alo calls it a withdrawal rate in a report about refusals.

<a id="why-universities-are-turning-bangladeshis-away"></a>

### 2.1 Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away

Since 1 June 2026 UKVI's Basic Compliance Assessment (its yearly test of universities that sponsor foreign students) allows a university a student visa refusal rate below 5%, down from below 10% <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. At least 95% of students who get a visa must enrol, up from 90%, and the course completion rate must reach 90% from June 2027, up from 85% <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. Universities are withdrawing offers to students from Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nigeria and some other countries to avoid losing their sponsor licence <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. The Home Office says universities must choose students more responsibly <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

Two lists from student-recruitment agencies reviewed by Prothom Alo in October 2026 show restrictions on Bangladeshi students at at least 42 UK institutions <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>:

- **Closed or limited for students from various regions of Bangladesh (22 institutions),** among them Middlesex University, Keele University, De Montfort University, University of East London, University of Roehampton, University of Sunderland, Coventry University and University of Bradford <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **Separate restrictions on students from the Sylhet region (20 institutions),** among them University of Chester, University of Wolverhampton, Canterbury Christ Church University, University of Brighton, Cardiff Metropolitan University, Oxford Brookes University and University of Portsmouth <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **Some restrict only certain courses or campuses** and keep others open <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

The University of East Anglia also said it had started withdrawing offers to students from some countries <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Where can a student check whether a given university currently accepts Bangladeshi applicants, or applicants from Sylhet?

<a id="step-1-get-an-offer-and-a-cas"></a>

### 2.2 Step 1: Get an offer and a CAS

1. Apply to a university and get an offer; the university then issues the CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
2. Expect the university to check everything before it issues the CAS. Russell Group universities (an association of 24 leading UK universities) add an extra approval step and a mandatory review by a second person <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. They check:
   - **academic certificates** directly with the institution that issued them <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>;
   - **English test results** with the test provider <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>;
   - **bank and financial documents** through the bank and third-party verification <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
3. Because of the new rules, universities check qualifications, English and finances far more strictly than before, a consultant at the student-recruitment firm Global Admission said <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which English tests do UK universities and UKVI accept from Bangladeshi applicants in 2026?
> - How much money must be shown, for how long must it be held, and which Bangladeshi banks' statements are accepted?

<a id="step-2-apply-for-the-visa"></a>

### 2.3 Step 2: Apply for the visa

1. Apply for the student visa with the CAS <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
2. You may be called for an interview by UKVI. In the September 2026 intake many students of the recruitment firm Global Admission were called; once the university learned of the interview it reassessed the application and withdrew its sponsorship, which happened to about 20 of the firm's students <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. The firm's consultant claimed several hundred students from Bangladesh faced the same in that intake <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Where is the UK visa application centre in Dhaka (and in Sylhet), what does the visa cost, and how long does a decision take?
> - What does the UKVI interview ask, and what happens to the visa fee if the university withdraws the CAS?
> - Can a refused student visa be appealed or reviewed?

<a id="step-3-set-up-your-e-visa-before-you-fly"></a>

### 2.4 Step 3: Set up your e-visa before you fly

Since 15 July 2025 Bangladeshi students get an e-visa, a digital record of the visa, instead of a sticker (vignette) in the passport; most applicants get an e-visa, but students' dependants (spouse, children) may still get a sticker <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. The UK has already replaced the Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), the old residence card, with the e-visa <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. At the border you need only your passport and your e-visa account details <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. Before you fly:

1. Create a UKVI account and link to it the passport you will travel on <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
2. Check that the e-visa has been issued and is correctly linked to the account <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
3. Carry a printed or digital copy of the e-visa approval letter to show at the airport or at immigration <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
4. If you get a new passport, update its details in your UKVI profile <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What is the web address for creating the UKVI account, and what does an airline at Dhaka airport ask to see for a student with an e-visa?

<a id="commonwealth-scholarship"></a>

### 2.5 Commonwealth Scholarship

Commonwealth Scholarships pay for Bangladeshis to study for a master's degree at UK universities, funded by the UK government through the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. 26 Bangladeshis won one in 2024, for universities including Oxford, Cambridge, SOAS (University of London), Warwick and Edinburgh; 1,867 have received one since 1960 <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. Every scheme is highly competitive and needs an outstanding academic record <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>. In 2024 the British Council held a pre-departure briefing for the year's scholars at the British High Commissioner's residence, with essential information on studying in the UK <sup>[N8 (2024)](#n8)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - When does the Commonwealth Scholarship application open each year, who nominates Bangladeshi candidates, and what does it pay?

<a id="visa-fraud-and-the-10-year-ban"></a>

### 2.6 Visa fraud and the 10-year ban

Anyone who uses visa fraud or an illegal route to go from Bangladesh to the UK gets a 10-year travel ban, the British High Commissioner in Dhaka, Sarah Cooke, said in November 2025 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The Russell Group confirmed in June 2026 that the current penalty for student visa fraud is a 10-year re-entry ban, and proposed harsher ones <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. The High Commission says it welcomes genuine applications made only through official channels <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. For fake admission and visa offers from study-abroad firms, see "Study-abroad firms that take the fee" in the Dalal scams doc.

**Red flags the High Commission names:** a guaranteed visa for an extra fee; a job without the right qualifications; fast processing <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

<a id="asylum"></a>

## 3 Asylum

About 11,000 Bangladeshis who came to the UK on student, visit or work visas claimed asylum in 2023, and the UK refused 95% of these claims <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Refused asylum seekers are now returned to Bangladesh under the May 2024 agreement (see [Removal to Bangladesh](#removal-to-bangladesh)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. While a claim is pending the UK provides housing, food, children's schooling and, if needed, unemployment support under the 1951 Refugee Convention, the North South University researchers wrote; they added that people who went to the UK irregularly face financial hardship from running their cases day after day <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. For claims built on a false story, and the UK's penalties for asylum fraud, see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis claimed asylum in the UK in 2024 and 2025, and what share were granted?
> - Can an asylum seeker work while the claim is pending?

<a id="irregular-entry-and-removal"></a>

## 4 Irregular entry and removal

<a id="crossing-the-channel"></a>

### 4.1 Crossing the Channel

Researchers wrote in May 2024 that a large part of the Bangladeshis who entered Italy irregularly in 2023 then tried to reach the UK across the English Channel, and many of those who entered irregularly claimed asylum <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. The Bangladesh High Commission told Keir Starmer in 2024 that no Bangladeshi had entered the UK illegally by boat and that Bangladesh was not among the top 20 countries of irregular migrants <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>. For the route to Italy, see "Libya to Italy by boat" in the Illegal routes doc.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis arrived in the UK by small boat in 2024 to 2026, according to Home Office statistics?

<a id="removal-to-bangladesh"></a>

### 4.2 Removal to Bangladesh

Under the agreement signed on 16 May 2024, the UK sends back by a fast-track procedure Bangladeshis whose asylum claims are refused, foreign offenders, and people whose visas have expired <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Where the evidence for removal is strong, people can be returned without the interview that used to be mandatory <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The agreement came out of the first UK-Bangladesh joint working group on home affairs, which also committed to keep legal migration open through existing visa routes <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The UK government had also agreed to send a large share of irregular arrivals to Rwanda while their cases ran in the UK; in May 2024 it was not known whether Bangladeshis would be among them <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis has the UK returned under the 2024 agreement, by year, and does the Rwanda plan still exist?
> - Does the Bangladesh High Commission in London give help or legal referrals to Bangladeshis facing removal?

<a id="british-citizenship-and-its-removal"></a>

## 5 British citizenship and its removal

The UK can remove a British citizen's citizenship if the government considers that the person is eligible for another country's citizenship, even if they have never lived there <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. A December 2025 report by the Runnymede Trust and Reprieve, two UK rights and research organisations, said about 9 million people, about 13% of the UK's population, could lose citizenship this way, with people tied to Pakistan, Bangladesh, Somalia, Nigeria, North Africa and the Middle East most at risk <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. Shamima Begum, born in the UK, lost her citizenship on the claim that she was a Bangladeshi citizen, which the Bangladesh government denied <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In December 2025 the Conservative and Reform UK parties were talking of plans that could force lakhs of people living legally in the UK to leave <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="sending-money-home"></a>

## 6 Sending money home

Bangladesh receives more than USD 2 billion a year in remittances through migrants who went to the UK by legal and illegal routes, the North South University researchers wrote in May 2024 <sup>[N5 (2024)](#n5)</sup>. For the latest UK figures and how to send, see "Where it comes from" in the Sending money home doc.

<a id="voting-in-bangladesh-elections-from-the-uk"></a>

## 7 Voting in Bangladesh elections from the UK

For the national election after November 2025, Bangladeshis and British Bangladeshis in the UK could register for a postal vote from 29 November to 3 December 2025 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

**Who could register:** a Bangladeshi by birth or descent with a valid passport or NID (national identity card), proof of a current address abroad, and, if already a voter, the same details updated <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

1. Download the Election Commission's "Nagorik" app on a smartphone <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
2. Enter your NID details, passport number and UK address <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
3. Once the Election Commission accepts the request, the ballot comes by post; fill it in and post it back within the set time <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

The Bangladesh High Commission in London posted the steps, infographics and video tutorials on its website and Facebook page <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Will postal voting from the UK be offered again for later elections, and when does registration open?
