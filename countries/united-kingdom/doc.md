# United Kingdom: migration guide

How Bangladeshis go to the United Kingdom (UK), visa by visa, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## Background

### In one minute

For Bangladeshis the UK is mainly a study destination: in the year to June 2026, 10,021 of 12,468 Bangladeshi student visa applications were granted, against 2,362 of 3,785 work visa applications and 17,568 of 37,254 visitor visa applications [OK0146, OK0147, OK0149]. The care-worker route that drew many South Asians in 2021-2023 closed to applicants abroad in July 2025 [OK0156, OK0104]. Since 1 June 2026 a UK university must keep its foreign students' visa refusals below 5% to pass UKVI's yearly check on sponsors, and at least 42 UK institutions have closed or limited admission for Bangladeshis, 20 of them for students from the Sylhet region [OK0128, XK0060, XK0061]. Many people who came on a visa then claim asylum: 5,769 Bangladeshis claimed in the year to June 2026, 4,789 of them after holding a visa or other permission, and only 11% of Bangladeshi claims decided that year were granted [OK0151, OK0153]. Since May 2024 a UK-Bangladesh agreement speeds up the return of refused asylum seekers, offenders and overstayers [OK0158, OK0159]. Deception in a visa application brings a 10-year ban from the UK [OK0139]. A work visa needs a certificate of sponsorship from a licensed employer; brokers inside the UK sold fake certificates for £10,000 to £20,000, the BBC reported in 2024 [OK0083, XK0093, XK0097]. Advisers also sell made-up asylum stories, the BBC reported in April 2026, and in October 2026 UK immigration officers raided a ring that investigators suspect was linked to a Bangladeshi LGBT (lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender) group in east London; status gained by fraud can be revoked and proven asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0123, XK0131, XK0148, XK0149, XK0145].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open to students who get past a university's checks and the visa decision, though admission for Bangladeshis is now closed or limited at many universities as of October 2026; open to skilled workers earning at least £41,700, but closed to care workers applying from abroad [XK0053, XK0060, OK0087, OK0104].

#### Status

- **16 May 2024:** the UK and Bangladesh signed an agreement for the return of Bangladeshis staying in the UK irregularly (see Removal to Bangladesh) [OK0161, OK0158].
- **June-July 2024:** before the general election, Labour leader Keir Starmer said his government would send so-called illegal migrants back soon after taking power, naming Bangladeshis as an example, and that he would scrap the Conservative government's Rwanda policy [XK0018, XK0085, XK0086]. Labour won the election in July 2024, ending 14 years of Conservative government [XK0089].
- **15 July 2025:** student visas for Bangladeshis became eVisas, with no sticker in the passport (see Step 4: Set up your eVisa before you fly) [XK0025].
- **22 July 2025:** care workers and senior care workers can no longer get a first Health and Care Worker visa from abroad; they can only extend, update or switch inside the UK (see Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa) [OK0104, OK0156].
- **2 March 2026:** people who claim asylum from this date and are granted protection get 30 months' permission at a time instead of 5 years (see Asylum) [OK0166, OK0168].
- **26 March 2026:** the UK began a "visa brake" refusing all student visas from some nationalities whose visa holders often claim asylum; Bangladesh is not on the list (see Visa brake) [OK0143, OK0144].
- **1 June 2026:** UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration, the visa department of the Home Office, the UK's interior ministry) began a stricter test of universities that sponsor foreign students (see Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away) [OK0128].
- **September 2026 intake:** a consultant at a student-recruitment firm said several hundred Bangladeshi students lost their university sponsorship after being called for a UKVI interview [XK0067].
- **6 October 2026:** UK immigration officers raided several places in London over fake gay asylum claims and arrested three people, including a Bangladeshi singer (see False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them) [XK0131].
- **1 January 2027:** the Graduate visa after a degree falls from 2 years to 18 months for applications made from this date (see Graduate visa) [OK0032].

#### The channels

- **Student visa:** the main route; 12,468 Bangladeshi applications and 10,021 grants (86%) in the year to June 2026 (see Student visa) [OK0146].
- **Standard Visitor visa:** 37,254 Bangladeshi applications in the year to June 2026, of which 17,568 (45%) were granted; it does not allow work (see Standard Visitor visa) [OK0149, OK0116].
- **Skilled Worker visa:** 3,091 Bangladeshi applications in the year to June 2026, 64% granted; needs a certificate of sponsorship from a licensed UK employer (see Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa) [OK0148, OK0083].
- **Family visas:** 2,069 Bangladeshi applications in the year to June 2026, 89% granted [OK0150].
- **Scholarships:** Commonwealth, GREAT and Women in STEM scholarships fund a small number of Bangladeshis for postgraduate study (see Scholarships) [XK0022, XK0072, XK0116].
- **Asylum after arriving on another visa:** 5,769 Bangladeshi claims in the year to June 2026, 11% granted (see Asylum) [OK0151].
- **Not a legal route:** crossing the English Channel from Europe; 5 Bangladeshi asylum claimants in the year to June 2026 had come by small boat (see Irregular entry and removal) [OK0153].

BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which clears Bangladeshis going abroad for work) recorded 10,383 Bangladeshis going to the UK in 2023 and 3,550 in 2024 [OK0215].

#### Size

- **Bangladeshis in the UK:** about 652,000 Bangladeshi nationals lived in the UK in 2024, IOM (the International Organization for Migration) counted [OK0217]. 451,529 Bangladeshis lived in Britain as British citizens according to the 2011 government statistics, 52% of them born in Britain, three North South University researchers wrote [XK0008].
- **Asylum claims by Bangladeshis:** 4,483 in 2022, 5,191 in 2023, 7,225 in 2024 and 6,419 in 2025 [OK0152].
- **Commonwealth Scholarships:** 1,867 Bangladeshis since 1960 (scholarships or fellowships), 26 in 2024 [XK0022, XK0020].
- **Remittance:** see Sending money home [OK0216].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

For students, the way in is a university's admission, often through a student-recruitment agency; the university, not the agency, issues the CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, the reference a student needs to apply for the visa) and can withdraw it (see Step 3: Apply for the visa) [OK0009, XK0066]. A university that withdraws a CAS before the visa decision keeps that student out of its refusal rate, the number UKVI uses to decide whether it may keep sponsoring foreign students [OK0132, OK0128]. Gangs promise a guaranteed visa for extra fees, a job without the right qualifications, or fast processing, and the result is lost money, refusal, a travel ban or trafficking, the British High Commission said [XK0035, XK0036]. Leading UK universities want access to UKVI's data on recruitment agents' behaviour [XK0051]. For work visas the root is the certificate of sponsorship: only a licensed employer can issue one, and there is no "registration fee" for a UK work visa, but brokers acting as recruitment agents sold fake ones to foreign students for up to £17,000 each, the BBC reported (see Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa) [OK0083, OK0186, XK0090, XK0093]. For promises that a student can work freely, see "Student visas used as work routes" in the Illegal routes doc.

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

Tighter admission, rising tuition fees, the taka's fall against the pound, and higher housing and visa costs in the UK put heavy financial pressure on students, and many now choose other countries, the Sylhet-region student adviser at Global Admission, a UK student-recruitment firm, said in October 2026 [XK0068].

- **Student visa:** £558, plus the immigration health surcharge of £776 a year, plus proof of one year's tuition and £1,171 to £1,529 a month for up to 9 months (see Step 2: Gather the money, English and TB certificate) [OK0006, OK0048, OK0012, OK0013, OK0014].
- **Skilled Worker visa:** £819 for up to 3 years or £1,618 for longer, plus £1,035 a year surcharge and £1,270 in savings [OK0095, OK0096, OK0049, OK0097].
- **Certificate of sponsorship for a work visa:** issued by the employer, with no fee to the worker; victims of the care-visa fraud reported by the BBC paid from £8,000 to as much as £20,000 for fake ones (see Fake sponsorship certificates) [OK0186, XK0093, XK0101, XK0104, XK0097].
- **Made-up asylum evidence:** investigators allege the ring raided in October 2026 charged £1,000 to £4,500 per claimant; a BBC investigation reported allegations that one firm charged up to £7,000 for a fake claim (see False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them) [XK0133, XK0140].
- **Health care:** the surcharge gives free NHS (National Health Service) treatment from the visa start date, but prescriptions, dental treatment and eye tests are still paid for [OK0053].
- **Pay:** the minimum wage from April 2026 is £12.71 an hour at 21 and over and £10.85 at 18 to 20, rising to £13.21 and £11.27 in April 2027 [OK0182, OK0183].

### What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** the university is the visa sponsor; it can reassess an application and withdraw its sponsorship before the visa is decided [XK0066, OK0132]. If a university loses its licence after the student has arrived, UKVI either lets students finish for up to six months or the end of the academic year, or cuts their permission to 60 days to find a new university and apply again; a student UKVI believes was not genuinely studying can lose permission at once [OK0135, OK0134, OK0136].
- **Workers:** a Skilled Worker or Health and Care Worker cannot change job or employer without applying to update the visa with a new certificate of sponsorship from the new employer [OK0086, OK0109, OK0110].
- **Universities:** a university that fails UKVI's test gets a red rating, and serious or repeated failure risks its sponsor licence [OK0131, XK0053].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are university offers withdrawn from Bangladeshi students, visa refusals, asylum claims refused and followed by removal, visa fraud that ends in a 10-year ban, fake work-visa sponsorship sold to students, and paid-for false asylum stories [XK0053, OK0149, OK0151, OK0158, OK0139, XK0090, XK0123].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Claim asylum once you are in."** Only 11% of Bangladeshi claims decided in the year to June 2026 were granted, and refused claimants are returned under the 2024 agreement [OK0151, OK0159].
- **"Pay extra and the visa is guaranteed."** The High Commission says such promises come from criminal gangs and end in refusal, a travel ban or worse [XK0035, XK0036].
- **"A sponsorship certificate has to be bought."** The certificate is an electronic record a licensed employer issues; there is no registration fee, and in one network the BBC investigated, 86 of 141 paid-for certificates were fake and refused by the Home Office [OK0093, OK0186, XK0098].
- **"A care job in the UK can still be found from Bangladesh."** Since 22 July 2025 care workers cannot get a first visa from abroad [OK0104].
- **"A paid adviser can make an asylum story work."** The Home Office is re-examining more than a hundred claims linked to one suspected ring, status gained by fraud can be revoked, and asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0135, XK0149, XK0145].
- **"A university offer means you will go."** Universities have withdrawn offers and sponsorship from Bangladeshi students, including after a UKVI interview call [XK0053, XK0066].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **The English Channel from Europe:** researchers wrote in May 2024 that many Bangladeshis who reached Italy irregularly tried to cross to the UK [XK0009]. In the year to June 2026, 5 Bangladeshi asylum claimants had come by small boat and 26 hidden in vehicles [OK0153].
- **Staying on after a refusal or an expired visa:** it ends in removal to Bangladesh and a re-entry ban (see Removal to Bangladesh) [OK0159, OK0139].

## Student visa

The student visa is getting harder for Bangladeshis to get, as universities withdraw offers to avoid losing their sponsor licence [XK0053]. In the year to June 2026, 1,635 Bangladeshi student visa applications were refused and 10,021 granted [OK0146]. In the first six months of 2026 the rate reported for Bangladeshi students' visa applications rose to 23.5% from 7.1% a year earlier, the second highest after Pakistan, according to the Financial Times' analysis of Home Office data; across all countries it rose from 2.3% to 10.5% [XK0054, XK0055, XK0056].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the Financial Times' 23.5% figure for Bangladeshi students in January-June 2026 a refusal rate or a withdrawal rate? Prothom Alo calls it a withdrawal rate in a report about refusals.

**Who can apply:** anyone 16 or over with an unconditional offer from a licensed student sponsor, enough money and enough English [OK0001, OK0011]. A degree-level student aged 18 or over can usually stay up to 5 years; below degree level, up to 2 years [OK0004]. Students cannot claim benefits or be self-employed [OK0008, OK0220].

### Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away

For checks applied for from 1 June 2026, UKVI's Basic Compliance Assessment (its yearly test of universities that sponsor foreign students) requires a visa refusal rate below 5%, an enrolment rate of at least 95% and a course completion rate of at least 85%, rising to 90% from June 2027; before June 2026 the limits were below 10%, 90% and 85% [OK0128, OK0130, OK0129]. The refusal rate covers the 12 months before the university applies, and a CAS the university withdraws before the visa decision does not count [OK0133, OK0132]. Universities are withdrawing offers to students from Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nigeria and some other countries to avoid losing their sponsor licence [XK0053]. The Home Office says universities must choose students more responsibly [XK0069].

Two lists from student-recruitment agencies reviewed by Prothom Alo in October 2026 show restrictions on Bangladeshi students at at least 42 UK institutions [XK0060]:

- **Closed or limited for students from various regions of Bangladesh (22 institutions),** among them Middlesex University, Keele University, De Montfort University, University of East London, University of Roehampton, University of Sunderland, Coventry University and University of Bradford [XK0061, XK0062].
- **Separate restrictions on students from the Sylhet region (20 institutions),** among them University of Chester, University of Wolverhampton, Canterbury Christ Church University, University of Brighton, Cardiff Metropolitan University, Oxford Brookes University and University of Portsmouth [XK0061, XK0063].
- **Some restrict only certain courses or campuses** and keep others open [XK0061].

The University of East Anglia also said it had started withdrawing offers to students from some countries [XK0064].

### Step 1: Get an offer and a CAS

1. Check the university is on the Home Office's register of licensed student sponsors on GOV.UK; only a licensed sponsor can issue a CAS [OK0011].
2. Apply to the university and get an unconditional offer; the university then sends the CAS reference number [OK0011, OK0009].
3. Russell Group universities (an association of 24 leading UK universities) check applications before they issue the CAS, with an extra approval step and a mandatory review by a second person [XK0046, XK0048]. They check:
   - **academic certificates** directly with the institution that issued them [XK0049];
   - **English test results** with the test provider [XK0049];
   - **bank and financial documents** through the bank and third-party verification [XK0050].
4. Because of the new rules, universities check qualifications, English and finances far more strictly than before, a consultant at the student-recruitment firm Global Admission said [XK0065].
5. Apply for the visa within 6 months of getting the CAS, and no earlier than 6 months before the course starts [OK0010, OK0002].

### Step 2: Gather the money, English and TB certificate

1. **Money:** hold the first year's tuition (up to 9 months, the amount shown on the CAS) plus £1,529 a month for up to 9 months for a course in London or £1,171 a month elsewhere [OK0012, OK0013, OK0014]. The money must sit in the account for at least 28 days in a row, and the 28-day period must end within 31 days of the visa application [OK0015]. A student with a loan or a sponsor brings a letter from the lender or sponsor [OK0016]. Each partner or child coming too needs £845 a month (London) or £680 a month (elsewhere) on top [OK0030].
2. **English:** for a degree course, show English at level B2 of the CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference) with a SELT (Secure English Language Test) from an approved provider, or, for a degree taught in English outside the UK, an Ecctis assessment code confirming it [OK0019, OK0018, OK0017]. A university may assess a degree-level student's English itself and ask for a different test [OK0020].
3. **Tuberculosis (TB) test:** anyone coming for 6 months or more who has lived in Bangladesh needs a chest x-ray at a Home Office-approved clinic; the certificate is valid 6 months [OK0056, OK0057, OK0058]. Approved clinics:
   - **Dhaka:** Green Crescent Health Services, 60 Park Road, Baridhara Diplomatic Zone, no appointment needed, Saturday to Thursday 9am to 5pm, Tk 5,500 at 11 and over, Tk 3,500 under 11 [OK0060, OK0059, OK0062, OK0061]; IOM Migration Health Assessment Center, UN House, 1st floor, House 1, Road 88, Gulshan 2, Sunday to Thursday 8:30am to 5pm, USD 70 (USD 30 under 11) paid in taka [OK0064, OK0065, OK0063, OK0066].
   - **Chattogram:** Green Crescent Medical Diagnostic, 13th floor, Khan Plaza, 1407 SK Mujib Road, Agrabad [OK0067].
   - **Sylhet:** Green Crescent MediLab, Time Square Building, Shibgonj Point, Sub-City, Sonarpara [OK0068].
   - **What to bring:** passport or NID (national identity card), a photocopy of it, the fee and any earlier chest x-ray or TB reports; the fee is not refunded if the visa is refused [OK0069, OK0070].

### Step 3: Apply for the visa

1. Apply online on GOV.UK with the CAS number; the fee is £558 plus the health surcharge of £776 for each year of the visa (a visa of up to 18 months pays one and a half years) [OK0009, OK0006, OK0048, OK0054].
2. Prove identity, either by scanning the passport with the "UK Immigration: ID Check" app or by giving fingerprints and a photo at a UK visa application centre (listed on GOV.UK's "Find a visa application centre" page); bring the passport, which is returned the same day [OK0023, OK0024]. Book the centre only after applying online; most centres offer free appointments within 5 working days, some charge £76.50 [OK0072, OK0071].
3. Expect a decision in about 3 weeks; UKVI contacts the applicant if documents need verifying or an interview is needed [OK0003, OK0025]. In the September 2026 intake many of Global Admission's students were called for a UKVI interview; once the university was told of the interview it reassessed the application and withdrew its sponsorship, which happened to about 20 of the firm's students [XK0066]. The firm's consultant claimed several hundred students from Bangladesh faced the same in that intake [XK0067].
4. **If you withdraw:** the fee is refunded only if UKVI has not started processing [OK0026].
5. **If refused:** where the refusal letter allows it, ask for an administrative review online within 28 days for £80; it can take 12 months or more, and making any new visa application cancels it [OK0073, OK0074, OK0075].

### Step 4: Set up your eVisa before you fly

Since 15 July 2025 Bangladeshi students get an eVisa, a digital record of the visa, instead of a sticker in the passport [XK0025, OK0027]. Before you fly:

1. Create a UKVI account on GOV.UK ("Get access to your eVisa"), using a phone number and email you will keep, your passport and the visa application number (the GWF or UAN on Home Office emails) [OK0076, OK0077]. Confirming identity needs a smartphone app [OK0078]. Each family member needs their own account [OK0079].
2. Link the passport you will travel on to the account and check the eVisa shows the right status; an airline may refuse boarding if the details are wrong [OK0081, OK0080].
3. If asked for proof at check-in or the border, sign in and get a share code; a travel share code lasts 90 days and can be reused [OK0082].
4. Carry a printed or digital copy of the eVisa approval letter, and update your passport in the UKVI account if you get a new one [XK0031, XK0032].
5. Travel no earlier than the start date on the visa; on a course over 6 months you may arrive up to 1 month before it starts [OK0005].

**Pre-departure briefing:** the British Council holds an online "Study UK Pre-departure Briefing" for Bangladeshi students with UK offers; in 2025 it ran on 29 July from 3:30 to 5:30 pm [XK0109]. UKVI representatives give the latest on the visa process and take questions, alongside universities and BUILA (the British Universities International Liaison Association) [XK0110, XK0112]. It covers packing, budgeting, travel and settling in, and how to use UK health care, insurance and mental health support [XK0111, XK0146]. Students register through a link in the announcement and get joining details by email [XK0113].

### Working during and after your studies

1. A full-time degree-level student at a university with a track record of compliance may work up to 20 hours a week in term-time and full time in breaks; below degree level the limit is 10 hours, and other study allows no work [OK0033, OK0034].
2. Students may not be self-employed, work as entertainers or professional sportspeople, or fill a permanent full-time job [OK0035].
3. Students may also work in a placement that is an assessed part of the course [XK0162].
4. A student on a course of 12 months or longer keeps permission for 4 months after the course ends; to stay on, switch to the Graduate visa in that time (see Graduate visa) [OK0036, OK0032].

### Bringing family

1. Only a government-sponsored student on a course over 6 months, or a postgraduate on a PhD, other doctorate or research degree, can bring a partner and children [OK0029, OK0028].
2. Each pays the £558 fee, the £776 yearly surcharge and shows £845 or £680 a month [OK0007, OK0048, OK0030].
3. A child born in the UK is not automatically British; apply for the child's dependant visa [OK0031].

### Graduate visa

1. Apply from inside the UK, before the Student visa expires, once the university has told the Home Office the course is complete [OK0038, OK0039, OK0037]. The university must have "Student Sponsor - Track Record" on the register, and you must have studied in the UK at least 12 months (or the whole course if shorter) [OK0046, OK0047].
2. Pay £937 and the health surcharge: £2,070 for 2 years or £1,552.50 for 18 months [OK0040, OK0041, OK0042].
3. The visa lasts 2 years if applied for by 31 December 2026, 18 months from 1 January 2027, and 3 years after a PhD [OK0032]. A decision takes about 8 weeks [OK0043].
4. You may work in most jobs, look for work and be self-employed [OK0044]. It cannot be extended; staying on needs a switch to another visa such as the Skilled Worker visa [OK0045].

### Scholarships

Three UK scholarship schemes reported for Bangladeshis pay for postgraduate study: the Commonwealth Scholarship, the GREAT Scholarship and the British Council's Women in STEM scholarship [XK0022, XK0072, XK0116].

#### Commonwealth Scholarship

Commonwealth Scholarships, funded by the UK government through the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC), pay for Bangladeshis to study at UK universities [XK0022]. Each pays return airfare, full tuition, a stipend of £1,712 a month (£2,000 in London) and a study travel grant; scholars must return home afterwards and may not switch to a Graduate or other visa [OK0208, OK0209, OK0210, OK0218]. 26 Bangladeshis won one in 2024, for universities including Oxford, Cambridge, SOAS (University of London), Warwick and Edinburgh; 1,867 have received a Commonwealth Scholarship or Fellowship since 1960 [XK0020, XK0022]. Every scheme is highly competitive and needs an outstanding academic record [XK0023].

**Master's scholarships:**

1. Apply for admission to the UK university first [OK0205].
2. Apply both on the CSC's online system, CSC Central, and to Bangladesh's nominating agency, the Secondary and Higher Education Division of the Ministry of Education, Dhaka (tel +880 258160106, ds_stp@moedu.gov.bd); the CSC takes no direct applications and charges no fee [OK0205, OK0203, OK0204, OK0211].
3. For the 2027/28 round the CSC deadline was 16:00 UK time on 20 October 2026, nominations go to the CSC by December, and results come by July 2027 [OK0206, OK0205, OK0207].

**PhD for public university teachers:** in August 2024 the scheme invited full-time faculty members of Bangladesh's public universities to apply for a UK PhD [XK0078]. The themes were science and technology for development; health systems and capacity; global development; global peace, security and governance; resilience and response to crises; and access, inclusion and opportunity [XK0079]. Applicants needed an upper second-class (2:1) honours degree or equivalent CGPA, or a lower second (2:2) plus a relevant postgraduate or master's degree [XK0080].

1. Get a nomination from your university's Registrar's office [XK0082].
2. Put together all academic certificates and mark sheets, the language test result, one passport-size photo and the hard copy of the nomination [XK0082].
3. Send them to the Secretary, University Grants Commission of Bangladesh (UGC), room 610, Agargaon administrative area, Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, Dhaka, and email a PDF of the documents to iccugcbd@gmail.com; the 2024 deadline was 15 September, with details on www.ugc.gov.bd [XK0083, XK0084].
4. Only shortlisted candidates are called for interview [XK0081].

#### GREAT Scholarship

The GREAT Scholarship is given by the UK government's GREAT Britain Campaign with the British Council and funded by the host universities; each is worth £10,000 towards a postgraduate degree, open to students from 15 countries including Bangladesh [XK0070, XK0072]. For 2025-26 the universities were Aston, Cranfield, Keele, Liverpool John Moores, Loughborough, Robert Gordon, Manchester, Ulster, Exeter, Plymouth and Surrey [XK0073].

**Who can apply:** a Bangladeshi citizen with a completed bachelor's degree, interest in the subject and the English level the university requires, willing to join the GREAT scholars' network and act as an ambassador for the scheme [XK0074, XK0075].

1. Open the British Council's list of participating universities and follow each university's link for its rules [XK0076].
2. Check each university's own deadline on its website; they differ [XK0076].
3. Universities announce results by June, and the chosen university pays out the scholarship [XK0077].

#### Women in STEM scholarship

The British Council's Women in STEM scholarship (STEM is science, technology, engineering and mathematics), part of its Going Global Partnerships programme, fully funds women for a one-year STEM master's at UK universities; 2026 was its sixth year [XK0115, XK0116]. For 2026-27 each award is worth at least £40,000 and covers tuition, a living allowance, travel and visa costs, health care fees and English language support [XK0118]. In 2026 there were 90 awards for 30 countries, 25 of them for South Asia at five UK universities, and applications closed on 30 April [XK0119, XK0116]. The eligible countries and universities are on the British Council's global website, and scholarships open in Bangladesh on the British Council Bangladesh website [XK0120].

### Visa fraud and the 10-year ban

Under the Immigration Rules, an application must be refused where the applicant used deception, such as false documents or false statements, and anyone who used deception is banned from entering the UK for 10 years [OK0138, OK0139]. The British High Commissioner in Dhaka, Sarah Cooke, repeated the 10-year ban for visa fraud or an illegal route in November 2025 [XK0033]. The UK refers applicants and agents caught with forged papers to the Bangladeshi police; in 2011 a visa forgery factory in Sylhet was shut down after a UK officer in Dhaka spotted a fake visa [OK0192, OK0191]. The High Commission says it welcomes genuine applications made only through official channels [XK0034]. For fake admission and visa offers from study-abroad firms, see "Study-abroad firms that take the fee" in the Dalal scams doc.

**Red flags the High Commission names:** a guaranteed visa for an extra fee; a job without the right qualifications; fast processing; a request for payment before the visa application; a request to send documents to someone claiming to be from the Home Office; a job offer that comes with a "work permit" [XK0035, OK0188, OK0187]. A genuine employer directs the worker to apply on GOV.UK [OK0190]. Visa crime can be reported to the Home Office online, anonymously if wished [OK0189].

**Abuses of the UK system exposed before:** English test fraud, student visas through fake colleges, sham marriages for residence, fake job sponsorship and fake documents or financial information [XK0126]. The Home Office says anyone who seeks an immigration benefit with false information will have the application refused and may be removed [XK0128].

## Standard Visitor visa

A visit visa for tourism, family visits, certain business meetings or study of up to 6 months; the holder cannot work, paid or unpaid, claim benefits, or marry [OK0115, OK0116, OK0117]. In the year to June 2026, 21,418 of the Bangladeshi visitor applications decided were refused [OK0149].

1. **Check you qualify:** you must show you will leave at the end of the visit and can pay for the trip and the journey home, or that someone else will [OK0118].
2. **Apply online:** £135 for up to 6 months, no earlier than 3 months before travel; long-term visas cost £506 (2 years), £903 (5 years) or £1,128 (10 years), each allowing up to 6 months per visit [OK0119, OK0120]. The form asks for travel dates, where you will stay, the trip's cost, your income, your parents' names and any offences, and may ask for 10 years of travel history, your employer and who pays [OK0122, OK0123]. Each family member applies and pays separately [OK0126].
3. **Visa application centre:** book the appointment during the online application; give fingerprints, photo and documents, with certified translations of anything not in English, and collect the passport the same day [OK0219, OK0124, OK0127].
4. **Decision:** usually within 3 weeks; the fee is not refunded if refused [OK0125, OK0121]. No health surcharge is charged on a visitor visa [OK0050].

## Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa

To work in the UK a worker needs a confirmed job offer from a Home Office-approved employer, a certificate of sponsorship (CoS) from that employer, an eligible occupation and a minimum salary [OK0083, OK0084]. In 2022 the UK care sector had 165,000 vacancies and the government allowed international recruitment, drawing many applicants from countries such as India, Nigeria and the Philippines; fraud rings exploited this [XK0095]. The 2021-2023 rise in care-worker visas was driven mainly by Indian, Pakistani and Bangladeshi and Sub-Saharan African workers, and visas to care workers fell by over 99% after overseas recruitment ended in July 2025 [OK0157, OK0156].

**Who can still come from Bangladesh:**

- **Skilled Worker:** a job in a "higher skilled" occupation, or a "medium skilled" one on the immigration salary list or temporary shortage list, paying at least £41,700 a year or the job's going rate, whichever is higher [OK0089, OK0087]. Bricklayers, roofers and carpenters qualify; construction labourers do not [OK0090]. The job must also pay at least the minimum wage and follow UK working-hours rules, or the visa is refused [OK0088].
- **Health and Care Worker:** doctors, nurses and other listed health professionals; care workers and home carers (code 6135) and senior care workers (6136) qualify only when extending, updating or switching inside the UK, after at least 3 months working legally for the sponsor [OK0104, OK0105]. The salary floor for care roles is £31,300 or the lower going rate [OK0108].

### Step 1: Check the employer and the certificate

1. Look the employer up in the Home Office's register of licensed sponsors for workers, published on GOV.UK [OK0091].
2. For a care job in England, check the employer is also registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) on cqc.org.uk [OK0092].
3. The certificate of sponsorship is an electronic record with a reference number, not a paper document; it shows the employer's name and sponsor licence number [OK0093, OK0098]. The employer, not the worker, pays for the sponsor licence (£611 for small businesses, £1,682 for larger ones) [OK0103].
4. A request for a "registration fee" or a job offer that comes bundled with a "work permit" are scam signs the British High Commission lists (see Visa fraud and the 10-year ban) [OK0186, OK0187].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a worker ask UKVI or the employer to confirm a CoS reference number is genuine before applying, and how?

### Step 2: Apply

1. Apply online within 3 months of getting the CoS, and no more than 3 months before the job starts [OK0094].
2. Prove English at level B2 and get a TB certificate (see Step 2: Gather the money, English and TB certificate) [OK0102, OK0098].
3. Pay £819 for up to 3 years or £1,618 for longer, plus £1,035 a year health surcharge; a Health and Care Worker pays £324 or £628 and no surcharge [OK0095, OK0096, OK0049, OK0106, OK0107]. Health and Care Worker applicants use the Skilled Worker form and answer "yes" to the Health and Care Worker question [OK0113].
4. Show £1,270 held for 28 days, unless the employer certifies it will cover the first month [OK0097].
5. A decision usually takes 3 weeks [OK0099].

### Changing employer

1. Get a new CoS from the new licensed employer and apply to update the visa; the new job must meet the same rules [OK0109, OK0110].
2. Keep working in the old job while waiting, but do not start the new one until the update is approved [OK0111].
3. A care worker in England can only move to a CQC-registered employer [OK0112].
4. A second job of more than 20 hours a week also needs a visa update [OK0114].

### Bringing family

A care worker or senior care worker cannot bring a partner or children from abroad except in narrow cases, such as a child born in the UK [OK0100]. Other Skilled Workers' dependants need £285 for a partner, £315 for the first child and £200 for each other child [OK0101]. After 5 years a Skilled Worker may apply to settle permanently [OK0085].

### Fake sponsorship certificates

In 2024 the BBC found that brokers acting as recruitment agents sold fake work-visa papers to students already in the UK who wanted care jobs, taking up to £17,000 (Tk 26.7 lakh) each [XK0090, XK0093]. The students found out when they applied for the Skilled Worker visa and the Home Office cancelled the papers [XK0094]. The named cases are a broker's network and two Indian students:

- **Taimur Raza's network:** Raza, a Pakistani national based in Wolverhampton and Birmingham, supplied one agency with papers for 141 applicants at £10,000 to £20,000 each, about £1.2 million, sent as PDFs on WhatsApp, the BBC reported [XK0099, XK0097]. 86 of the 141 were fake and refused; 55 applicants got visas, but the care homes named as sponsors said they had no record of them [XK0098]. Raza called the allegations false and one-sided and has been in Pakistan since December 2023; a Sikh advice centre said he had repaid students £258,000 and that it had passed the case to the National Crime Agency [XK0100, XK0108].
- **Nadia, 21:** paid a broker £8,000 of a £10,000 fee and after six months got papers for a care home in Walsall; when she phoned the care home it said it had issued no certificate and had no vacancy, and the broker blocked her number [XK0101, XK0102].
- **Neela:** paid a training provider in Wolverhampton £15,000 to switch her student visa to a care-worker visa; its staff showed her emails, letters and copies of visas to look genuine, and the Home Office later cancelled her certificate as fake [XK0103, XK0104].

Victims who tried to stay on legally now risk being sent home [XK0105]. Most did not go to the police for fear of the Home Office, said Luke Piper, head of immigration at the Work Rights Centre; many went instead to Sikh gurdwaras in the West Midlands, whose members recovered money for some [XK0106, XK0107].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Care worker or senior care worker visa from abroad:** closed since 22 July 2025 [OK0104, OK0156].

### Visa brake

Since 26 March 2026 the UK refuses all Student visas from outside the UK for nationals of Afghanistan, Cameroon, Myanmar and Sudan, and Skilled Worker visas for Afghans, because many holders later claimed asylum; Bangladesh is not on the list [OK0144, OK0143]. The brake applies even to applicants who already hold a CAS or CoS [OK0145]. Bangladesh was among the six nationalities that made up 46% of asylum claimants in the year to June 2026, and 4,789 of its 5,769 claimants had held a visa or other permission first [OK0155, OK0153].

## Asylum

Asylum claims by Bangladeshis rose from 4,483 in 2022 to 7,225 in 2024 and 6,419 in 2025 [OK0152]. Of the 5,769 Bangladeshis who claimed in the year to June 2026, 4,789 had held a visa or other permission first: 1,083 a study visa, 3,113 a work visa and 497 a visitor visa [OK0153, OK0154]. Of 8,657 Bangladeshis who got a first decision that year, 822 were granted and 7,835 refused, an 11% grant rate [OK0151]. Refused claimants are returned to Bangladesh under the May 2024 agreement (see Removal to Bangladesh) [OK0159]. For claims built on a false story, and the UK's penalties for asylum fraud, see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc.

1. **Claiming:** claim on arrival or as soon as you fear return; a late claim is more likely to be refused [OK0162]. From inside the UK, call the asylum registration appointment line to book a screening interview; the claim is registered only once screening is done [OK0163, OK0164].
2. **While waiting:** no work, unless the claim has waited over 12 months through no fault of yours, and then only in jobs on the Immigration Salary List; apply free on form PTW1 to the Home Office's Permission to Work Team (AsylumPTW@homeoffice.gov.uk) [OK0165, OK0171, OK0172, OK0174]. Someone who claims while a work visa is still valid usually keeps the right to work; claiming after it expired ends it [OK0173]. A destitute claimant can get housing, chosen by the Home Office and unlikely to be in London, and £49.18 a week per person on a debit card [OK0176, OK0175]. Migrant Help UK answers free on 0808 801 0503, Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm, with a 24-hour emergency line [OK0177].
3. **If granted:** people who claimed from 2 March 2026 get 30 months' permission and must apply to renew within 28 days of expiry if they still need protection; earlier claimants get 5 years [OK0166, OK0167, OK0168].
4. **If refused:** leave, appeal within the time allowed, or face detention without warning and removal [OK0169]. A claim may not be considered at all if the person passed through a safe third country [OK0170].

In November 2025 the UK was preparing a major change to its asylum policy, modelled on Denmark's [XK0114].

### False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them

Some advisers sell made-up asylum and settlement cases, the BBC reported in April 2026 [XK0123]:

- **What is sold:** a false story, coaching on gathering evidence and a ready-made application, with letters, photos or medical documents [XK0123]. Undercover reporters posing as Bangladeshi and Pakistani students were told to claim to be gay, atheist or politically persecuted, and to set up fake news websites, join staged political protests and lie to doctors about illness [XK0138, XK0139, XK0140].
- **Other false claims reported:** posing as gay without being so, and posing as a victim of domestic abuse to settle fast under the Migrant Victims of Domestic Abuse Concession [XK0122, XK0121, XK0147].
- **How advisers find people:** online adverts and personal contact offering an "easy route" [XK0124].
- **Prices:** up to £7,000 from one firm; in a recorded conversation with a Bangladeshi-origin lawyer near Mile End Road in east London, £1,500 for preparing the claim plus £2,000 to £3,000 for extra evidence [XK0140, XK0141, XK0142]. The BBC said he qualified as a barrister in 2022 but is not authorised to practise; he denies deliberate wrongdoing [XK0143].

**The Aponjon raids:** on 6 October 2026 immigration officers raided several places in London and arrested three people, including a Bangladeshi singer, who was bailed and denies the allegation, and seized phones, jewellery and several thousand pounds in cash [XK0131, XK0132]. Investigators suspect a ring linked to Aponjon, a Bangladeshi LGBT group in east London, sold fake attestation letters, staged photos and invented letters about same-sex relationships for £1,000 to £4,500 [XK0148, XK0133]. Officers had noticed unusual similarities between applications, and the Home Office is re-examining more than a hundred claims that may be linked [XK0134, XK0135]. Claims citing sexual orientation by Bangladeshis numbered 148 in 2018 and 175 in 2023, by Home Office figures that do not show whether they were false [XK0136, XK0137].

**What happens:** Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced tough action against these rings; status gained by fraud can be revoked, and proven asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0144, XK0149, XK0145]. In April 2026, after the BBC's investigation, Downing Street said the people named in its report were under investigation [XK0127]. A UK immigration lawyer, Monowar Hossain, said unregistered advisers push false information for money, some working under the cover of authorised lawyers, and that many applicants spend large sums and still fail [XK0129, XK0130].

**Checking an adviser:** every immigration adviser must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA) or belong to an approved professional body; search the IAA Adviser Finder (linked from GOV.UK's "Find an immigration adviser") and the government's list of banned advisers [OK0178, OK0179, OK0181]. Money paid to an unregulated adviser cannot be recovered through a complaint [OK0180].

## Irregular entry and removal

### Crossing the Channel

Researchers wrote in May 2024 that a large part of the Bangladeshis who entered Italy irregularly recently tried to reach the UK across the English Channel, and many of those who entered irregularly claimed asylum [XK0009]. Home Office figures show few succeed: of the Bangladeshis who claimed asylum in the year to June 2026, 5 had come by small boat and 26 hidden in vehicles [OK0153]. For the route to Italy, see "Libya to Italy by boat" in the Illegal routes doc.

### Removal to Bangladesh

Under the agreement announced on 16 May 2024, the UK returns refused asylum seekers, foreign offenders and visa overstayers sooner, without the interview that used to be mandatory where there is good evidence for removal [OK0161, OK0159, OK0158]. Labour said in June 2024 that its returns policy covers people with no legal right to stay in the UK, not British Bangladeshis, and that Bangladesh was named only because of the agreement [XK0087, XK0088]. The same working group committed to keep legal migration open through existing visa routes and to share data on visa abuse [OK0160].

**Re-entry bans after a breach of immigration law:** 12 months after leaving voluntarily at one's own expense; 2 or 5 years after leaving at the government's expense; 10 years after being removed at the government's expense; 10 years after using deception in an application [OK0139, OK0140]. An overstay of 30 days or less is ignored if the person left voluntarily at their own expense [OK0141]. Using a false identity, absconding, or working or renting without the right to do so counts as frustrating immigration control [OK0142].

## British citizenship and its removal

The UK can remove a British citizen's citizenship if the government considers that the person is eligible for another country's citizenship, even if they have never lived there [XK0042]. A December 2025 report by the Runnymede Trust and Reprieve, two UK rights and research organisations, said about 9 million people, about 13% of the UK's population, could lose citizenship this way, with people tied to Pakistan, Bangladesh, Somalia, Nigeria, North Africa and the Middle East most at risk [XK0041, XK0043]. Shamima Begum, born in the UK, lost her citizenship on the claim that she was a Bangladeshi citizen, which the Bangladesh government denied [XK0044]. In December 2025 the Conservative and Reform UK parties were talking of plans that could force lakhs of people living legally in the UK to leave [XK0045].

## Voting in Bangladesh elections from the UK

For the national election after November 2025, Bangladeshis and British Bangladeshis in the UK could register for a postal vote from 29 November to 3 December 2025 [XK0037].

**Who could register:** a Bangladeshi by birth or descent with a valid passport or NID, proof of a current address abroad, and, if already a voter, the same details updated [XK0039].

1. Download the Election Commission's "Nagorik" app on a smartphone [XK0038].
2. Enter your NID details, passport number and UK address [XK0038].
3. Once the Election Commission accepts the request, the ballot comes by post; fill it in and post it back within the set time [XK0038].

The Bangladesh High Commission in London posted the steps, infographics and video tutorials on its website and Facebook page [XK0040].

## Online harassment from other Bangladeshis

Several Bangladeshi women living in the UK told the BBC in 2024 that a Bangladeshi man living in the Paris suburbs, Hasan Sayed, with thousands of TikTok followers, trolled them [XK0150]. Victims alleged he took their personal photos and details from social media, put them in trolling videos, and on TikTok live mocked them and threatened rape and murder [XK0151]. What victims tried, and what happened:

- **Police and platform reports:** many complained to police in the UK and France and reported to the platforms, without result [XK0152]. TikTok told one victim, Kamrul Islam of Staffordshire, that the videos did not breach its community guidelines, and the police gave him no hope [XK0153].
- **Mass reporting by followers:** Masuma, a victim in Wales, had a video calling her a sex worker taken down only after her followers reported it again and again, when the damage was already done [XK0161].
- **UK embassy in the harasser's country:** in February 2023 Kamrul went to the UK embassy in Paris, which put him in touch with an English-speaking French lawyer; the lawyer filed a complaint with the Paris public prosecutor's office, and said a final outcome would take a long time [XK0154, XK0155, XK0156].
- **Information Commissioner's Office (ICO, the UK's data-protection regulator):** in April 2023 Kamrul went to the ICO, which found the content very worrying and disagreed with the platforms' reasons [XK0157]. Citing misuse of children's personal data, it told TikTok, YouTube and Facebook to remove the videos within seven days, and the three said they would delete content against their guidelines and block the accounts [XK0158, XK0159]. Kamrul said he still had to contact TikTok almost daily about new accounts, and effective action was not always taken [XK0160].

**Complaining to the ICO:**

1. Complain to the platform or organisation first and keep a copy [OK0212].
2. File online on the ICO website with the organisation's email address and that copy, plus a timeline and screenshots if you have them; the ICO helpline is 0303 123 1113 [OK0212, OK0214].
3. Complaints were being assigned to a case officer within 14 weeks [OK0213].
