# United Kingdom: migration guide

How Bangladeshis go to the United Kingdom (UK), visa by visa, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. This first version is built only from Prothom Alo reporting (2024 to November 2026), so every fact in it is cited as N and still needs an official source. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## Background

### In one minute

In this reporting Bangladeshis reach the UK mainly as students, and some come on student, visit or work visas and then claim asylum: about 11,000 did so in 2023, and 95% of those claims were refused [XK0003]. Since May 2024 a UK-Bangladesh agreement lets the UK send back refused asylum seekers, offenders and overstayers by a fast-track procedure [XK0001]. Since 1 June 2026 the UK has tightened its test of universities whose foreign students are refused visas, and in response at least 42 UK institutions have closed or limited admission for Bangladeshi students, 20 of them for students from the Sylhet region in particular [XK0057, XK0060, XK0061]. Using a forged document or an illegal route to reach the UK brings a 10-year ban on entering [XK0033, XK0046]. A UK work visa, including for care work, needs a certificate of sponsorship from a registered employer, which costs the worker nothing; brokers inside the UK sold fake certificates to foreign students for up to £17,000 each [XK0092, XK0096, XK0093]. Advisers also sell made-up asylum stories, and in October 2026 the Home Office raided a ring it suspects was linked to a Bangladeshi LGBT group in east London; status gained by fraud can be revoked and asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0123, XK0131, XK0148, XK0149, XK0145].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open to students who get past a university's checks and the visa decision, but admission for Bangladeshis is now closed or limited at many universities, as of October 2026 [XK0053, XK0060].

#### Status

- **June-July 2024:** before the general election, Labour leader Keir Starmer said his government would send so-called illegal migrants back soon after taking power, naming Bangladeshis as an example, and that he would scrap the Conservative government's Rwanda policy [XK0018, XK0085, XK0086]. - **November 2025:** the UK was preparing a major change to its asylum and immigration policy, modelled on Denmark's [XK0114].

- **1 June 2026:** UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration, the visa department of the Home Office, the UK's interior ministry) began a stricter test of universities that sponsor foreign students (see Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away) [XK0057].

- **6 October 2026:** UK immigration officers raided several places in London over fake gay asylum claims and arrested three people, including a Bangladeshi singer (see False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them) [XK0131].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

For students, the way in is a university's admission, often through a student-recruitment agency; the university, not the agency, issues the CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, the reference a student needs to apply for the visa) and can withdraw it (see Step 2: Apply for the visa) [XK0048, XK0066]. Gangs promise a guaranteed visa for extra fees, a job without the right qualifications, or fast processing, and the result is lost money, refusal, a travel ban or trafficking, the British High Commission said [XK0035, XK0036]. Leading UK universities want access to UKVI's data on recruitment agents' behaviour [XK0051]. For work visas the root is the certificate of sponsorship: only a registered employer can issue one, and the worker pays nothing for it, but brokers posing as recruitment agents sold fake ones for £10,000 to £20,000 each (see Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa) [XK0096, XK0093, XK0097]. For promises that a student can work freely, see "Student visas used as work routes" in the Illegal routes doc.

> **Need to know**

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

Tighter admission, rising tuition fees, the taka's fall against the pound, and higher housing and visa costs in the UK put heavy financial pressure on students, and many now choose other countries, Global Admission's student adviser for the Sylhet region said in October 2026 [XK0068].

- **Certificate of sponsorship for a work visa:** free to the worker; victims of the care-visa fraud paid £8,000 to £17,000 for fake ones (see Fake sponsorship certificates) [XK0093, XK0101, XK0104].
- **Made-up asylum evidence:** investigators allege the ring raided in October 2026 charged £1,000 to £4,500 per claimant; a BBC investigation found one firm charging up to £7,000 for a fake claim (see False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them) [XK0133, XK0140].

### What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** the university is the visa sponsor; it can reassess an application and withdraw its sponsorship before the visa is decided [XK0066].
- **Workers:** a work visa needs a registered employer to sponsor the worker [XK0096].
- **Universities:** a university that fails UKVI's test may have to take corrective action, and serious or repeated failure risks losing its sponsor licence (its permission to admit foreign students) [XK0059, XK0053].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are university offers withdrawn from Bangladeshi students, visa refusals, asylum claims refused and followed by removal, visa fraud that ends in a 10-year ban, fake work-visa sponsorship sold to students, and paid-for false asylum stories [XK0053, XK0054, XK0003, XK0001, XK0033, XK0090, XK0123].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Claim asylum once you are in."** About 95% of the claims made by Bangladeshis in 2023 were refused, and refused claimants are now returned under the 2024 agreement [XK0003, XK0004].
- **"Pay extra and the visa is guaranteed."** The High Commission says such promises come from criminal gangs and end in refusal, a travel ban or worse [XK0035, XK0036].
- **"A sponsorship certificate has to be bought."** The worker pays nothing for a certificate of sponsorship; paid-for certificates in the BBC's investigation were mostly fake and were cancelled by the Home Office [XK0096, XK0098].
- **"A paid adviser can make an asylum story work."** The Home Office is re-examining more than a hundred claims linked to one suspected ring, status gained by fraud can be revoked, and asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0135, XK0149, XK0145].
- **"A university offer means you will go."** Universities have withdrawn offers and sponsorship from Bangladeshi students, including after a UKVI interview call [XK0053, XK0066].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **The English Channel from Europe:** researchers wrote in May 2024 that many Bangladeshis who reached Italy irregularly tried to cross to the UK [XK0009]. The Bangladesh High Commission wrote to Keir Starmer in 2024 that no Bangladeshi had entered the UK illegally by boat [XK0019].
- **Staying on after a refusal or an expired visa:** it ends in removal to Bangladesh (see Removal to Bangladesh) [XK0001].

## Student visa

The student visa is the main legal route in this reporting, and it is getting harder for Bangladeshis [XK0053]. In the first six months of 2026 the rate reported for Bangladeshi students' visa applications rose to 23.5% from 7.1% a year earlier, the second highest after Pakistan, according to the Financial Times' analysis of Home Office data; across all countries it rose from 2.3% to 10.5% [XK0054, XK0055, XK0056].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the 23.5% figure for Bangladeshi students the share of applications refused or withdrawn? Prothom Alo calls it a withdrawal rate in a report about refusals.

### Why universities are turning Bangladeshis away

Since 1 June 2026 UKVI's Basic Compliance Assessment (its yearly test of universities that sponsor foreign students) allows a university a student visa refusal rate below 5%, down from below 10% [XK0057]. At least 95% of students who get a visa must enrol, up from 90%, and the course completion rate must reach 90% from June 2027, up from 85% [XK0058]. Universities are withdrawing offers to students from Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nigeria and some other countries to avoid losing their sponsor licence [XK0053]. The Home Office says universities must choose students more responsibly [XK0069].

Two lists from student-recruitment agencies reviewed by Prothom Alo in October 2026 show restrictions on Bangladeshi students at at least 42 UK institutions [XK0060]:

- **Closed or limited for students from various regions of Bangladesh (22 institutions),** among them Middlesex University, Keele University, De Montfort University, University of East London, University of Roehampton, University of Sunderland, Coventry University and University of Bradford [XK0061, XK0062].
- **Separate restrictions on students from the Sylhet region (20 institutions),** among them University of Chester, University of Wolverhampton, Canterbury Christ Church University, University of Brighton, Cardiff Metropolitan University, Oxford Brookes University and University of Portsmouth [XK0061, XK0063].
- **Some restrict only certain courses or campuses** and keep others open [XK0061].

The University of East Anglia also said it had started withdrawing offers to students from some countries [XK0064].

> **Need to know**
> - Where can a student check whether a given university currently accepts Bangladeshi applicants, or applicants from Sylhet?

### Step 1: Get an offer and a CAS

1. Apply to a university and get an offer; the university then issues the CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) [XK0048].
2. Expect the university to check everything before it issues the CAS. Russell Group universities (an association of 24 leading UK universities) add an extra approval step and a mandatory review by a second person [XK0046, XK0048]. They check:
   - **academic certificates** directly with the institution that issued them [XK0049];
   - **English test results** with the test provider [XK0049];
   - **bank and financial documents** through the bank and third-party verification [XK0050].
3. Because of the new rules, universities check qualifications, English and finances far more strictly than before, a consultant at the student-recruitment firm Global Admission said [XK0065].

> **Need to know**
> - Which English tests do UK universities and UKVI accept from Bangladeshi applicants in 2026?
> - How much money must be shown, for how long must it be held, and which Bangladeshi banks' statements are accepted?

### Step 2: Apply for the visa

1. Apply for the student visa with the CAS [XK0066].
2. You may be called for an interview by UKVI. In the September 2026 intake many students of the recruitment firm Global Admission were called; once the university learned of the interview it reassessed the application and withdrew its sponsorship, which happened to about 20 of the firm's students [XK0066]. The firm's consultant claimed several hundred students from Bangladesh faced the same in that intake [XK0067].

### Step 3: Set up your e-visa before you fly

Since 15 July 2025 Bangladeshi students get an e-visa, a digital record of the visa, instead of a sticker (vignette) in the passport; most applicants get an e-visa, but students' dependants (spouse, children) may still get a sticker [XK0025, XK0028]. The UK has already replaced the Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), the old residence card, with the e-visa [XK0026]. At the border you need only your passport and your e-visa account details [XK0027]. Before you fly:

1. Create a UKVI account and link to it the passport you will travel on [XK0029].
2. Check that the e-visa has been issued and is correctly linked to the account [XK0030].
3. Carry a printed or digital copy of the e-visa approval letter to show at the airport or at immigration [XK0031].
4. If you get a new passport, update its details in your UKVI profile [XK0032].

**Pre-departure briefing:** the British Council holds an online "Study UK Pre-departure Briefing" for Bangladeshi students with UK offers; in 2025 it ran on 29 July from 3:30 to 5:30 pm [XK0109]. UKVI officers give the latest on the visa process and take questions, alongside universities, BUILA (the British Universities International Liaison Association) and current students and graduates [XK0110, XK0112]. It covers packing, budgeting, travel and settling in, and how to use UK health care, insurance and mental health support [XK0111, XK0146]. Students register through a link in the announcement and get joining details by email [XK0113].

### Scholarships

Three UK scholarship schemes reported for Bangladeshis pay for postgraduate study: the Commonwealth Scholarship, the GREAT Scholarship and the British Council's Women in STEM scholarship [XK0022, XK0072, XK0116].

#### Commonwealth Scholarship

Commonwealth Scholarships pay for Bangladeshis to study for a master's degree at UK universities, funded by the UK government through the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (CSC) [XK0022, XK0024]. 26 Bangladeshis won one in 2024, for universities including Oxford, Cambridge, SOAS (University of London), Warwick and Edinburgh; 1,867 have received one since 1960 [XK0020, XK0022]. Every scheme is highly competitive and needs an outstanding academic record [XK0023]. In 2024 the British Council held a pre-departure briefing for the year's scholars at the British High Commissioner's residence, with essential information on studying in the UK [XK0021].

**PhD for public university teachers:** in August 2024 the scheme invited full-time faculty members of Bangladesh's public universities to apply for a UK PhD [XK0078]. The themes were science and technology for development; health systems and capacity; global development; global peace, security and governance; resilience and response to crises; and access, inclusion and opportunity [XK0079]. Applicants needed an upper second-class (2:1) honours degree or equivalent CGPA, or a lower second (2:2) plus a relevant postgraduate or master's degree [XK0080].

1. Get a nomination from your university's Registrar's office [XK0082].
2. Put together all academic certificates and mark sheets, the language test result, one passport-size photo and the hard copy of the nomination [XK0082].
3. Send them to the Secretary, University Grants Commission of Bangladesh (UGC), room 610, Agargaon administrative area, Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, Dhaka, and email a PDF of the documents to iccugcbd@gmail.com; the 2024 deadline was 15 September, with details on www.ugc.gov.bd [XK0083, XK0084].
4. Only shortlisted candidates are called for interview [XK0081].

> **Need to know**
> - When does the Commonwealth master's application open each year, who nominates candidates who are not university teachers, and what does the scholarship pay?

#### GREAT Scholarship

The GREAT Scholarship is given by the UK government's GREAT Britain Campaign with the British Council and funded by the host universities; each is worth £10,000 towards a postgraduate degree, open to students from 15 countries including Bangladesh [XK0070, XK0072]. For 2025-26 the universities were Aston, Cranfield, Keele, Liverpool John Moores, Loughborough, Robert Gordon, Manchester, Ulster, Exeter, Plymouth and Surrey [XK0073].

**Who can apply:** a Bangladeshi citizen with a completed bachelor's degree, interest in the subject and the English level the university requires, willing to join the GREAT scholars' network and act as an ambassador for the scheme [XK0074, XK0075].

1. Open the British Council's list of participating universities and follow each university's link for its rules [XK0076].
2. Check each university's own deadline on its website; they differ [XK0076].
3. Universities announce results by June, and the chosen university pays out the scholarship [XK0077].

#### Women in STEM scholarship

The British Council's Women in STEM scholarship (STEM is science, technology, engineering and mathematics), part of its Going Global Partnerships programme, fully funds women for a one-year STEM master's at UK universities; 2026 was its sixth year [XK0115, XK0116]. For 2026-27 each award is worth at least £40,000 and covers tuition, a living allowance, travel and visa costs, health care fees and English language support [XK0118]. In 2026 there were 90 awards for 30 countries, 25 of them for South Asia at five UK universities, and applications closed on 30 April [XK0119, XK0116]. The eligible countries and universities are on the British Council's global website, and scholarships open in Bangladesh on the British Council Bangladesh website [XK0120].

### Visa fraud and the 10-year ban

Anyone who uses visa fraud or an illegal route to go from Bangladesh to the UK gets a 10-year travel ban, the British High Commissioner in Dhaka, Sarah Cooke, said in November 2025 [XK0033]. The Russell Group confirmed in June 2026 that the current penalty for student visa fraud is a 10-year re-entry ban, and proposed harsher ones [XK0046]. The High Commission says it welcomes genuine applications made only through official channels [XK0034]. For fake admission and visa offers from study-abroad firms, see "Study-abroad firms that take the fee" in the Dalal scams doc.

**Red flags the High Commission names:** a guaranteed visa for an extra fee; a job without the right qualifications; fast processing [XK0035].

**Frauds the UK has cracked down on before:** English test fraud, student visas through fake colleges, sham marriages for residence, fake job sponsorship and fake documents or financial information [XK0126]. The Home Office says anyone who seeks an immigration benefit with false information will have the application refused and may be removed [XK0128].

## Care-worker and Skilled Worker visa

To apply for a UK work visa, the Skilled Worker visa included, a worker needs a certificate of sponsorship from a registered employer; it states the worker's details, job title and salary, and the worker pays nothing for it [XK0092, XK0096, XK0094]. In 2022 the UK care sector had 165,000 vacancies and the government allowed international recruitment, drawing many applicants from countries such as India, Nigeria and the Philippines; fraud rings exploited this [XK0095].

> **Need to know**
> - How can a worker check with the Home Office or the employer that a certificate of sponsorship is genuine before applying?

### Fake sponsorship certificates

In 2024 the BBC found that brokers acting as recruitment agents sold fake work-visa papers to students already in the UK who wanted care jobs, taking up to £17,000 (Tk 26.7 lakh) each [XK0090, XK0093]. The students found out when they applied for the Skilled Worker visa and the Home Office cancelled the papers [XK0094]. The named cases are Indian students:

- **Taimur Raza's network:** Raza, a Pakistani national based in Wolverhampton and Birmingham, supplied one agency with papers for 141 applicants at £10,000 to £20,000 each, about £1.2 million, sent as PDFs on WhatsApp, the BBC reported [XK0099, XK0097]. 86 of the 141 were fake and refused; 55 applicants got visas, but the care homes named as sponsors said they had no record of them [XK0098]. Raza called the allegations false and one-sided and has been in Pakistan since December 2023; a Sikh advice centre said he had repaid students £258,000 and that it had passed the case to the National Crime Agency [XK0100, XK0108].
- **Nadia, 21:** paid a broker £8,000 of a £10,000 fee and after six months got papers for a care home in Walsall; when she phoned the care home it said it had issued no certificate and had no vacancy, and the broker blocked her number [XK0101, XK0102].
- **Neela:** paid a training provider in Wolverhampton £15,000 to switch her student visa to a care-worker visa; its staff showed her emails, letters and copies of visas to look genuine, and the Home Office later cancelled her certificate as fake [XK0103, XK0104].

Victims who tried to stay on legally now risk being sent home [XK0105]. Most did not go to the police for fear of the Home Office, said Luke Piper of the Work Rights Centre, a UK charity; many went instead to Sikh gurdwaras in the West Midlands, whose members recovered money for some [XK0106, XK0107].

## Asylum

About 11,000 Bangladeshis who came to the UK on student, visit or work visas claimed asylum in 2023, and the UK refused 95% of these claims [XK0003]. Refused asylum seekers are now returned to Bangladesh under the May 2024 agreement (see Removal to Bangladesh) [XK0004]. While a claim is pending the UK provides housing, food, children's schooling and, if needed, unemployment support under the 1951 Refugee Convention, the North South University researchers wrote; they added that people who went to the UK irregularly face financial hardship from running their cases day after day [XK0013, XK0014]. For claims built on a false story, and the UK's penalties for asylum fraud, see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc. In November 2025 the UK was preparing a major change to its asylum policy, modelled on Denmark's [XK0114].

### False asylum stories and the advisers who sell them

Some advisers sell made-up asylum and settlement cases, the BBC reported in April 2026 [XK0123]:

- **What is sold:** a false story, coaching on gathering evidence and a ready-made application, with letters, photos or medical documents [XK0123]. Undercover reporters posing as Bangladeshi and Pakistani students were told to claim to be gay, atheist or politically persecuted, and to set up fake news websites, join staged political protests and lie to doctors about illness [XK0138, XK0139, XK0140].
- **Other false claims reported:** posing as gay without being so, and posing as a victim of domestic abuse to settle fast under the Migrant Victims of Domestic Abuse Concession [XK0122, XK0121, XK0147].
- **How advisers find people:** online adverts and personal contact offering an "easy route" [XK0124].
- **Prices:** up to £7,000 from one firm; in a recorded conversation with a Bangladeshi-origin lawyer near Mile End Road in east London, £1,500 for preparing the claim plus £2,000 to £3,000 for extra evidence [XK0140, XK0141, XK0142]. The BBC said he qualified as a barrister in 2022 but is not authorised to practise; he denies deliberate wrongdoing [XK0143].

**The Aponjon raids:** on 6 October 2026 immigration officers raided several places in London and arrested three people, including a Bangladeshi singer, who was bailed and denies the allegation, and seized phones, jewellery and several thousand pounds in cash [XK0131, XK0132]. Investigators suspect a ring linked to Aponjon, a Bangladeshi LGBT group in east London, sold fake attestation letters, staged photos and invented letters about same-sex relationships for £1,000 to £4,500 [XK0148, XK0133]. Officers had noticed unusual similarities between applications, and the Home Office is re-examining more than a hundred claims that may be linked [XK0134, XK0135]. Claims citing sexual orientation by Bangladeshis numbered 148 in 2018 and 175 in 2023, by Home Office figures that do not show whether they were false [XK0136, XK0137].

**What happens:** Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced tough action against these rings; status gained by fraud can be revoked, and proven asylum fraud carries up to 10 years in prison [XK0144, XK0149, XK0145]. Downing Street said the people named by the BBC were under investigation [XK0127]. Regulators warn that only registered, authorised immigration advisers should be used [XK0125]. A UK immigration lawyer, Monowar Hossain, said unregistered advisers push false information for money, some working under the cover of authorised lawyers, and that many applicants spend large sums and still fail [XK0129, XK0130].

> **Need to know**
> - Where does someone check that a UK immigration adviser is registered (the regulator's name and register)?

## Irregular entry and removal

### Crossing the Channel

Researchers wrote in May 2024 that a large part of the Bangladeshis who entered Italy irregularly in 2023 then tried to reach the UK across the English Channel, and many of those who entered irregularly claimed asylum [XK0009]. The Bangladesh High Commission told Keir Starmer in 2024 that no Bangladeshi had entered the UK illegally by boat and that Bangladesh was not among the top 20 countries of irregular migrants [XK0019]. For the route to Italy, see "Libya to Italy by boat" in the Illegal routes doc.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis arrived in the UK by small boat in 2024 to 2026, according to Home Office statistics?

### Removal to Bangladesh

Under the agreement signed on 16 May 2024, the UK sends back by a fast-track procedure Bangladeshis whose asylum claims are refused, foreign offenders, and people whose visas have expired [XK0007, XK0001]. Where the evidence for removal is strong, people can be returned without the interview that used to be mandatory [XK0002]. Labour said in June 2024 that its returns policy covers people with no legal right to stay in the UK, not British Bangladeshis, and that Bangladesh was named only because of the agreement [XK0087, XK0088]. The agreement came out of the first UK-Bangladesh joint working group on home affairs, which also committed to keep legal migration open through existing visa routes [XK0006]. The UK government had also agreed to send a large share of irregular arrivals to Rwanda while their cases ran in the UK; in May 2024 it was not known whether Bangladeshis would be among them [XK0012, XK0017].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis has the UK returned under the 2024 agreement, by year, and did the Labour government scrap the Rwanda plan as Starmer pledged in June 2024?
> - Does the Bangladesh High Commission in London give help or legal referrals to Bangladeshis facing removal?

## British citizenship and its removal

The UK can remove a British citizen's citizenship if the government considers that the person is eligible for another country's citizenship, even if they have never lived there [XK0042]. A December 2025 report by the Runnymede Trust and Reprieve, two UK rights and research organisations, said about 9 million people, about 13% of the UK's population, could lose citizenship this way, with people tied to Pakistan, Bangladesh, Somalia, Nigeria, North Africa and the Middle East most at risk [XK0041, XK0043]. Shamima Begum, born in the UK, lost her citizenship on the claim that she was a Bangladeshi citizen, which the Bangladesh government denied [XK0044]. In December 2025 the Conservative and Reform UK parties were talking of plans that could force lakhs of people living legally in the UK to leave [XK0045].

## Sending money home

Bangladesh receives more than USD 2 billion a year in remittances through migrants who went to the UK by legal and illegal routes, the North South University researchers wrote in May 2024 [XK0015]. For the latest UK figures and how to send, see "Where it comes from" in the Sending money home doc.

## Voting in Bangladesh elections from the UK

For the national election after November 2025, Bangladeshis and British Bangladeshis in the UK could register for a postal vote from 29 November to 3 December 2025 [XK0037].

**Who could register:** a Bangladeshi by birth or descent with a valid passport or NID (national identity card), proof of a current address abroad, and, if already a voter, the same details updated [XK0039].

1. Download the Election Commission's "Nagorik" app on a smartphone [XK0038].
2. Enter your NID details, passport number and UK address [XK0038].
3. Once the Election Commission accepts the request, the ballot comes by post; fill it in and post it back within the set time [XK0038].

The Bangladesh High Commission in London posted the steps, infographics and video tutorials on its website and Facebook page [XK0040].

> **Need to know**
