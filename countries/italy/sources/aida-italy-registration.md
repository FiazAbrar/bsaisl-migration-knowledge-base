# Registration of the asylum application

## Italy

Country Report: **Registration of the asylum application** Last updated: 14/09/26

### Author

ASGI [Visit Website](http://www.asgi.it)

![](https://asylumineurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/asgi.png)

The Procedures Decree provides that applications for international protection are made by non-EU citizens on the territory of the State, including at the border and in transit zones, and in the territorial waters.[^(\[1\])](#_ftn1)

The Decree also provides for training for police authorities appropriate to their tasks and responsibilities.[^(\[2\])](#_ftn2)

#### Making and registering the application (fotosegnalamento)

Under the Procedures Decree,[^(\[3\])](#_ftn3) the asylum claim can be made either at the Border Police upon arrival or at the Immigration Office (*Ufficio Immigrazione*) of the Police (*Questura*), if the applicant is already on the territory. The intention to seek international protection may be expressed orally or in writing by the person concerned in their own language with the help of a cultural mediator.[^(\[4\])](#_ftn4)

PD 21/2015 provides that asylum seekers who express their wish to apply for international protection before Border Police authorities are requested to approach the competent Questura within 8 working days. Failure to comply with the 8-working-day time limit without justification results in deeming the persons as illegally staying on the territory.[^(\[5\])](#_ftn5) However, there is no provision for a time limit to make an asylum application before the Questura when the applicant is already on the territory.

DL 145/2024 however provided that an asylum application made after 90 days from the irregular entry or from start of the period of irregular stay in Italy has to be channelled into an accelerated procedure, and that in this case applicants do not have access to accommodation measures unless they provide justified reasons for the delay.[^(\[6\])](#_ftn6)

The law does not foresee any financial support for taking public transport to the competent Questura. In practice, NGOs working on the borders provide the train ticket for that journey on the basis of a specific agreement with the competent Prefecture. However, this support is not always guaranteed.

The procedure for the initial registration of the asylum application is the same at the border and at the Questura. The first step is the identification and registration process, which entails fingerprinting and photographing that can be carried out either at the border police or at the Questura. This procedure is called “*fotosegnalamento*”.

The Procedure Decree provides that the registration of the application shall be carried out within 3 working days from the expression of the intention to seek protection or within 6 working days in case the applicant has expressed such willingness before Border Police authorities. That time limit is extended to 10 working days in presence of a significant number of asylum applications due to consistent and tight arrivals of asylum applicants.[^(\[7\])](#_ftn7)

Upon completion of the *fotosegnalamento*, the person receives an invitation (*invito*) to reappear before the Questura to lodge the asylum application.

DL 133/2023 introduced a scenario for the cancellation of the asylum request by introducing Article 6 (3 bis) to the Procedure Decree, according to which in the event asylum applicants do not present themselves at the police station for the verification of the identity declared and the formalisation of the asylum application, the previous expression of the will to seek asylum does not constitute an asylum application and the asylum procedure is not considered initiated.[^(\[8\])](#_ftn8)

#### Lodging the application (*verbalizzazione*)

*Fotosegnalamento* is followed by a second step, consisting in the formal registration of the asylum application, which is carried out exclusively at the Questura within the national territory. The EUAA provides support in this process since 2017.

The formal registration of the application (*verbalizzazione or formalizzazione*) is conducted through the “C3” form (*Modello C3*).[^(\[9\])](#_ftn9) The form is completed with the basic information regarding the applicant’s personal history, journey to reach Italy and reasons for fleeing their country of origin. This form is signed by the asylum applicant and sent to the Territorial Commission before the interview. Asylum applicants receive a copy of the C3 and copies of all other documents submitted to the police authorities.

With the completion of the C3, the formal stage of applying for international protection is concluded. The “*fotosegnalamento*” and the lodging of the international protection application do not always take place at the same time, especially in big cities, due to the high number of asylum application and the shortage of police staff. In practice, the formal registration might take place weeks (or months) after the asylum applicant made the asylum application. This delay still creates difficulties for asylum applicants who, in the meantime, might not have access to the reception system and the national health system, except for emergency health care.

In 2025, the number of lodged asylum applications registered with the support of EUAA personnel in Italy decreased, to 25,584 applications for international protection (30,513 in 2024). Of these, 75% related to the same 10 citizenships: Bangladesh (5,154), Peru (4,000), Egypt (2,079), India (1,538), Pakistan (1,451), Sri Lanka (1,423), Morrocco (1,115), Tunisia (907), Georgia (783) and China (698).[^(\[10\])](#_ftn10)

The Reception Decree provides for the issuance of a “residence permit for asylum applicants” (*permesso di soggiorno per richiesta asilo*), valid for 6 months, renewable.[^(\[11\])](#_ftn11)

#### **Access to the procedure in practice**

Reports of denial of access to the asylum procedure recorded by ASGI have continued since 2021, including in 2025. However, since the early months of 2022, the situation has reached unprecedented critical levels.

When they prevent access to the procedure, Questure do not issue any document attesting the intention of the persons concerned to seek asylum. This exposes them to risks of arbitrary arrest and deportation.

This problem mainly affects people who reach Questure autonomously, after entering in Italy by land or after independent disembarkations or when applying for asylum after staying on the national territory.

On 8 February 2024, the Ministry of Interior decided to stipulate an agreement with UNHCR in order to intervene on the timing of access to the asylum procedure and the reduction of the backlog relating to the number of applications to formalise.[^(\[12\])](#_ftn12)

On 7 March 2025, ASGI, together with other NGOs such as Emergency, Lungo la Rotta Balcanica and the Italian Chamber of Immigration Lawyers (CADUS), lodged two class actions[^(\[13\])](#_ftn13) before the Administrative Court of Veneto (TAR) against the Questure (Police headquarters) of two provinces in the Veneto region, Vicenza and Venice, accused of systematic delays in granting access to the international protection procedure, the average waiting time to apply for international protection in the two offices in 2024 being between 90 and 100 days (as directly indicated by the two Questure).[^(\[14\])](#_ftn14) The organizations used a tool – that of the public class action – that had previously been only seldomly used in immigration law. This tool serves to present to the competent court – in this case the Regional Administrative Court – a dysfunctional and inefficient practice of a Public Administration or of those who manage a service on behalf of it. The court is called to rule on the existence of a systematic violation of a deadline (as in this case) or other forms of non-compliance. If the court finds the violation exists, it can order the administration involved to remedy it, without incurring in greater costs or burdens for the State, but by working on greater efficiency in the use of resources. The class action does not provide for forms of compensation for damages for asylum seekers but is a tool that pushes the administration to work in compliance with the law. The action also aims at pointing out the structural nature of violations of terms and deadlines by the public administration, highlighting the issue on a national basis. By the end of July 2025, other organisations or individuals suffering the same issues accessing the asylum procedure had added their own claims with a formal intervention in the two actions.

Following the hearing of 17 September 2025, the Veneto Regional Administrative Court (TAR) issued two identical interlocutory orders, requesting both the Ministry of the Interior and the two Police Headquarters (Questure) to provide precise data on their offices’ organisation , to better understand the causes of the delays. Only the two Police Headquarters complied, while the central Ministry remained silent. On 18 March 2026, the TAR issued two judgments in which it acknowledged the existence of serious delays regarding effective access to the procedure, and ordered the administrations to restore a function as sensitive as that concerning access to and the conduct of international protection procedures within 90 days from the publication of the judgment. Furthermore, the judgments criticised the inertia of the central administration and the insufficient evidentiary response provided by the peripheral administrations.[^(\[15\])](#_ftn15) The organisations involved are monitoring the level of implementation of the Court orders, which so far remains insufficient.

Since 2015[^(\[16\])](#_ftn16) and up to the present, the problem of delayed registrations also affected people who disembarked, as they had to face the so-called [hotspot](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/italy/asylum-procedure/access-procedure-and-registration/preliminary-checks-of-third-country-nationals-upon-arrival/) procedure, being channelled into the asylum procedure or a return procedure (being sent to a CPR) mainly depending on their nationality and on the base of a “foglio notizie” not translated in their language and fulfilled without an effective assistance from cultural mediators. This continued to happen in 2024, and such practices were still reported mainly concerning Tunisian and Moroccan nationals.

As such, some nationalities face more difficulties in accessing the asylum procedure, both at hotspots and at Questure. In 2025, according to ASGI’s experience, similar difficulties persisted. By the end of 2025 and in the first months of 2026, in Trieste, many people from Nepal were *de facto* denied to access the asylum procedure. They had to file urgent appeals to the Civil Court in order to obtain access to the asylum procedure. The Civil Court of Trieste upheld all the appeals and ordered the Questura to start the asylum procedure.[^(\[17\])](#_ftn17) In some cases, the interim decision on the appeal convinced Questura to allow the access to the procedure, then the Court could close the case.[^(\[18\])](#_ftn18)

In cases where, once in CPR, people managed to submit an asylum application, this was, with few exceptions, considered an instrumental application aimed at avoiding repatriation. Consequently, the detention order was validated as the application was considered as not genuine (see [Detention](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/italy/detention-asylum-seekers/)).

In 2020, the Court of Cassation reaffirmed the close connection between compliance with information obligations and the effectiveness of the right of access to the asylum procedure, both denied by the value attributed to the so-called “*foglio notizie*” or second “*foglio notizie*”, which are often distributed to foreign citizens who arrive at the border without a prior or contextual explanation on the meaning of their signature.[^(\[19\])](#_ftn19)

In early 2024, the Court of Cassation expressed an important principle to better stress the importance of the information obligations as a guarantee of access and respect of the asylum rights. With a decision issued on 5 March 2024 the Court affirmed that “Pursuant to art. 10 ter TUI, complete and effective information on the international protection procedure must be ensured for all foreigners brought for rescue and first aid needs at the hotspots, (..) this obligation also exists in the case in which the foreigner has not expressed the need to request international protection, given that silence or any declaration incompatible with the desire to request it, which must in any case be clearly expressed and not in ambiguous formulas, cannot take on significance if it does not appear that the person has been fully informed in advance.” The Court also remarked that it is not sufficient, in order to consider the fulfilment of the information obligation, that the rejection or detention decree generally indicates that the subject has been fully informed, if nothing appears on this matter from the foglio notizie or from other documents, or evidence offered by the administration; and in particular if nothing is apparent regarding the times and methods with which the information was administered, with specific regard to the language used, the presence of an interpreter or cultural mediator and this in order to allow a verification of the comprehensibility of the information provided.[^(\[20\])](#_ftn20)

On 13 July 2023, the Supreme Court once again intervened on the timing of the registration of the asylum application stating that in compliance with the provisions of the art. 6, of Directive 2013/32/EU, the application for international protection must be registered within the terms established therein (three or six days depending on the office) and that the ten-day extension of the term, provided for by national legislation (last period of ‘art. 26, paragraph 2-bis, Legislative Decree 25/2008, introduced by the national legislator with Legislative Decree 142/2015) must be applied only in the case of a high and proven number of applications following consistent and close arrivals. ^([\[21\]](#_ftn21))

People seeking protection are also affected by the readmission procedures, practices witnessed at the Slovenian border as well as in Adriatic ports (see [Access to the territory](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/italy/asylum-procedure/access-procedure-and-registration/access-territory-and-push-backs/)).

In 2023, there were numerous reports of cases in which access to the asylum procedure was hindered on even on national territory, and practices differed widely between different areas of the territory.

Due to the problems in registering one’s asylum application in Milan, several associations together with ASGI sent a letter to UNHCR.[^(\[22\])](#_ftn22)

Similarly, the high numbers affecting the Questura of Turin, including regarding the impossibility to formalise the asylum applications, lead ASGI to address the Police with a formal letter,[^(\[23\])](#_ftn23) which was then followed by a highly participated-in public protest.[^(\[24\])](#_ftn24) Several months after the demonstration, no substantive change in the rules for submitting asylum applications has been registered.

Between May and June 2023, ASGI carried out monitoring through data collected by its members on 55 of the 107 Italian provinces.[^(\[25\])](#_ftn25) The outcome was that in 40 cities, asylum applicants cannot access the asylum procedure without an official address. The Civil Court of Rome, on 31 July 2023, once again stated that “the Administration does not enjoy full discretionary power, but it is obliged to provide the necessary means to register the application within the times prescribed by the law, moreover, since the pandemic emergency which led to first eliminating and then significantly limiting access to offices has long been overcome”.[^(\[26\])](#_ftn26)

On 28 March 2023, the Civil Court of Milan upheld the urgent appeal submitted by an Egyptian asylum applicant who had tried several times to access the Questura and finally had expressed his intention to seek asylum through a certified mail sent by his lawyer. The Court ordered Questura to register his intention to seek asylum.[^(\[27\])](#_ftn27) As of April 2023 the Questura of Milan decided to allow access to the asylum procedure through a telematic system (Prenotafacile) which, however, requires the use of a passport or of an identification document.[^(\[28\])](#_ftn28) On 9 May 2023, the Civil Court of Milan upheld the urgent appeal submitted by another Egyptian asylum applicant ordering Questura to process his asylum request and evaluating the inadequacy of the Prenotafacile system as it was actually not allowing people to request an appointment.[^(\[29\])](#_ftn29)

Later, the Questura of Milan decided to allow those who do not have passport to book an appointment through some organisations which declared to be available, such as ACLI.

In 2023, the Questure of Sassari and Siracusa declared asking people to submit legalised documents to prove the family bond among parents and children who are, otherwise, prevented from applying.

Regarding the requests of evidence of family bonds, in 2022 some Questure – such as those of Caserta and Rome – started, as reported to ASGI, to ask for a DNA test to prove the family bond.

In 2023, as reported to ASGI, the Questura of Bologna refused to formalise an asylum request of a family lacking family documents.

The Civil Court of Rome, with a decision of 31 March 2023, ordered the immediate access to the asylum procedure for a Georgian citizen, deeming the new practice established in recent weeks by the Questura of Rome of setting appointments for the formalisation of the application months after the request did not comply with regulatory provisions.[^(\[30\])](#_ftn30)

Moreover, on 24 March 2023, the Civil Court of Trieste ordered the Questura of Udine to register within 30 days the international protection request of a Nigerian woman, 62, who, since October 2022, was trying to obtain access to the asylum procedure but had only obtained an expulsion order.[^(\[31\])](#_ftn31)

Also, with an important decision, the Civil Court of Bologna, on 18 January 2023, recognised the right to access the procedure to a group of asylum seekers who, helped by the CIAC association, since August 2022 were denied access to the procedure and accommodation lacking a domicile.[^(\[32\])](#_ftn32)

On 8 April 2024, the report “Attendere prego” from the International Rescue Committee Italy (IRC) Le Carbet, Mutuo Soccorso Milano, Naga ASGI and Intersos, was published confirming the difficulty in accessing the procedure existing in Milan and in others Italian provinces.[^(\[33\])](#_ftn33)

In 2024 ASGI, together with the organisations Spazi Circolari, Progetto Diritti; ARCI; Libellula Italia; A Buon Diritto, Baobab Experience; and CILD, started a collective anti-discrimination lawsuit against the Ministry of the Interior due to the continued issues regarding access to the asylum procedure. After the lawsuit was lodged, the Questura of Rome adopted suitable tools to overcome the problem, allowing, without waiting days, to submit the asylum application. For this reason, on 7 October 2024 the Court closed the case as the matter of dispute ceased.[^(\[34\])](#_ftn34)

Another anti-discrimination lawsuit was filed in 2025 by ASGI against the Police Headquarters (Questura) of Turin, also regarding access to the asylum procedure. The court of Turin found that the organisational model adopted by the Immigration Office of the Turin Questura regarding foreign nationals seeking to formalise an application for international protection constitutes direct discrimination, both individual and collective. During the proceedings, it was demonstrated that in Turin, in order to submit an application for international protection, migrants are forced to queue for months outside the Questura’s offices from the early hours of the night, in the hope of being selected to formalise their requests. Each day, only about 10 people are able to submit an asylum application: the system provides no possibility of booking an appointment and, above all, there are no transparent criteria to understand on what basis the selection for entry is made. Having established there was discrimination, the court ordered the Questura of Turin to design a different organisational model within four months of the ruling’s publication.[^(\[35\])](#_ftn35)

**Access to the procedure from detention**

In practice, the possibility of accessing the asylum procedure inside a pre-removal detention centre (CPR) is limited due to the lack of appropriate legal information and assistance, and to administrative obstacles. According to the Reception Decree, people are informed about the possibility to seek international protection by the managing body of the centre.[^(\[36\])](#_ftn36)

As recorded by ASGI, in 2025, as in previous years, in many cases people detained, who were not informed of the possibility and the way to ask for asylum, could not express this will even before the Judge of the Peace (Giudice di Pace) at the hearing to validate the detention. Only in some cases were they able to submit the asylum request thanks to their lawyers after the detention order had been issued. This was possible, however, mainly in the CPRs, such as that of Gradisca, where mobile phones are not seized.

Regarding the possibility to apply for asylum by applicants serving prison terms, ASGI has recorded significant difficulties still in 2025 (see chapter on [Detention of asylum applicants](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/italy/detention-asylum-seekers/)).

On 14 October 2022, the Civil Court of Turin accepted the appeal lodged by an asylum applicant from Morocco who had obtained access to the asylum procedure just a few days before the end of his prison sentence. The Territorial Commission applied an accelerated procedure and evaluated the asylum request to be manifestly unfounded. Judging on the appeal presented by the applicant, the Court of Turin established that the applicant should be granted special protection, due to his long stay in Italy and to the family ties created in the country.[^(\[37\])](#_ftn37)
