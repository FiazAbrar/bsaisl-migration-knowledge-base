Entering Italy and staying there legally. Here's how.

</div>

<div class="field field--name-field-image-media field--type-entity-reference field--label-hidden field__item"
data-bis_skin_checked="1">

<img
src="./interno-it-decreto-flussi_files/studenti_stranieri_-www.ijobs_.it_.jpg"
title="Entry methods" loading="lazy" width="436" height="265"
alt="Entry methods" />

</div>

<div class="clearfix text-formatted field field--name-body field--type-text-with-summary field--label-hidden field__item"
data-bis_skin_checked="1">

Foreign citizens may enter Italy if they can document the reason and
conditions of their stay, as well as the availability of means to
support themselves during their stay and to return to their country of
origin, except in cases of entry for work. Anyone who does not meet
these requirements, or is considered a threat to national security or
the security of one of the countries with which Italy has signed
agreements for the free movement of persons across internal borders, is
not admitted to Italy. The applicable legislation on immigration and the
status of foreigners is the Consolidated Law on Immigration.

## Regular residence: required documents

To legally enter Italy, you need a passport or other travel document and
an entry visa (for visits and/or tourism, work, study and/or research,
family, etc.), which must be requested from the Italian embassy or
consulate in the non-EU citizen's country of origin or permanent
residence. Entry into Italy is permitted with short-term visas, valid
for up to three months, and long-term visas, which require the granting
of a long-term residence permit with the same reason as the visa. For
stays of less than three months, visas issued by diplomatic authorities
of other countries with which Italy has ratified agreements, or pursuant
to EU regulations, are considered valid.

## Irregular presence and expulsions

Failure to comply with these procedures, or staying longer than three
months or the shorter period indicated on the visa, places the foreigner
in the status of an irregular immigrant and will result in expulsion,
except in cases of force majeure provided by law. Expelled foreign
citizens may not re-enter Italy unless they have special authorization
or the entry ban has expired. Foreigners reported for serious reasons of
public order and national security, or the protection of international
relations, are not admitted to Italy. In practice, the following are
considered irregular:

- the non-EU citizen who enters Italy without documents (passport or
  identification document and visa);
- the non-EU citizen who, having entered Italy legally, has lost the
  necessary requirements for residence.

Foreigners who enter Italy illegally are turned away at the border or,
if they have already entered the country, expelled, unless they must be
held in an immigration center to verify their identity and/or
nationality. The expulsion order is issued by the competent prefecture
and executed by the police headquarters.

## Entry for work reasons

Entry into Italy for employment, including seasonal work, and
self-employment must comply with the entry quotas (Article 21 of the
Consolidated Law) established in periodic (usually annual) decrees, the
so-called "flow decrees," issued by the Prime Minister based on the
criteria set out in the three-year planning document on immigration
policies (Article 3). The flow decrees typically provide for a quota
reserve for citizens from countries with which the government has
concluded agreements to regulate entry flows and readmission procedures.
