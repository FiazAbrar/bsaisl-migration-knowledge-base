Nationality of arrivals

In December, Bangladeshis accounted for 40 per cent of all arrivals by sea (compared to 22% in November), followed by Syrians
(15%, compared to 19% in November) and Pakistanis (14%, compared to 9% in November). Among other nationalities of
significant numbers arriving by sea were Egyptians (6%), Algerians (4%), Malians and Afghans (3% each), Tunisians and Guineans
(2% each). In December, the main nationalities of those departing from Libya were Bangladeshis (50%, 30% in November), Syrians
(19%, 25% in November) and Pakistanis (17%, 11% in November). Most of those departing from Tunisia were Tunisians (22%,
23% in November), Guineans (20%, 27% in November), and Malians (16%, 9% in November). Most of those departing from
Türkiye were Afghans (70%, 52% in November), Iranians (26%, 19% in November), and Iraqis (2%, 14% in November). All those
departing from Algeria were Algerians (100%, as in November).

Country of embarkation

In 2024, 63 per cent of the people reaching Italy via sea departed from Libya (42,279 people in 899 landings), followed by
29 per cent from Tunisia (19,460 people in 656 landings), 5 per cent from Türkiye (3,495 people in 49 landings), and 2 per
cent from Algeria (1,383 people in 143 landings). In December, as in November, Libya was the main country of departure
(81% of all persons arriving in Italy) by sea, followed by Tunisia (11%, down from 20% in November), Türkiye (4%, as in
November) and Algeria (4%, up from 2% in November).

Overview

In 2024, 66,617 refugees and migrants arrived in Italy by sea across 1,747 separate landings, marking a significant
reversal in trends following four consecutive years of steady increases, and representing a 58 per cent
reduction compared to 2023. Notably, 2024 was the fourth lowest year of sea arrivals in the past decade. Despite the
decrease in the number of sea arrivals, 1,718 refugees and migrants lost their lives or went missing at sea while
attempting to cross the Central Mediterranean towards Italy, averaging 1 every 39 people arriving by sea compared to
1 in 83 in 2023--highlighting a rise in the incidence of death and missing persons along this route.2

Routes travelled by refugees and migrants

The significant decrease in arrivals by sea to Italy in 2024 is a direct consequence of the overall decrease in departures
on all routes and in particular from Tunisia. Notably, the number of individuals departing from Tunisia fell by 80 per
cent, from nearly 100,000 in 2023 to under 20,000 in 2024, a downward trend that was already evident in the last
quarter of 2023. Similarly, arrivals from Türkiye decreased by 51 per cent, while those from Libya declined by 19 per
cent, with most departures continuing to originate from Tripolitania. In contrast, the only route to experience an
increase was the one from Algeria, where departures rose by 123 per cent. However, the absolute number of arrivals
from Algeria remained low, with 1,383 individuals reaching Italy--thus not significantly affecting overall figures. No
landings from Cyprus, Lebanon, Greece (since 2022), or Egypt (since 2021) were recorded in 2024.

In 2024, the island of Lampedusa remained the main port of disembarkation in Italy, with over 45,500 sea arrivals,
accounting for 68 per cent of all arrivals by sea--mirroring the proportion recorded in 2023. Other main disembarkation
points included the island of Sicily (9%), Calabria (6%), and Sardinia (2%), followed by smaller shares from other Italian
regions. The proportion of sea arrivals disembarking in Lampedusa remained consistent year-on-year, notwithstanding a
reduction in departures from Tunisia, whose geographic proximity has historically resulted in a high number of arrivals.
This relative stability in arrivals to Lampedusa can be partly attributed to an increased use of small vessels departing

  Nationalities of refugees and migrants

In 2024, individuals of 57 different nationalities were registered at disembarkation sites in Italy. The top six countries of
origin were Bangladesh (21%), the Syrian Arab Republic (19%), Tunisia (12%), Egypt (7%), and Guinea and Pakistan (5%
each). The reduction in departures from Tunisia significantly impacted the nationality composition of arrivals, leading to
a marked decrease in West African nationals. Tunisians became the main nationality departing from Tunisia,
representing 40 per cent of all departures from the country--up from 18 per cent in the previous year. However, in
absolute terms, fewer Tunisian nationals crossed the Mediterranean in 2024 compared to 2023 (7,741 vs. 17,904
individuals). No significant re-routing of West African nationals from Tunisia to Libya was observed. Other nationalities
departing from Tunisia included Guineans, Gambians, Malians, and Sudanese. Nationals from Asia, as well as North and
East Africa, primarily crossed the Central Mediterranean from Libya. On this route, the top five countries of origin were
Bangladesh, the Syrian Arab Republic, Egypt, Pakistan, and Eritrea. Among them, Asian nationals and Syrians reportedly
traveled by air to Benghazi before moving onward to Tripolitania for embarkation. Along the Türkiye route, the main
nationalities recorded were Iranian, Afghan, Iraqi, and Pakistani.

In 2024, there was a notable increase in arrivals from Bangladesh and the Syrian Arab Republic. Despite the overall
reduction in sea arrivals, over 14,200 Bangladeshis (+12% compared to 2023) and 12,550 Syrians (+24% compared to
2023) reached Italy. One of the main reasons for the increase is linked to the existence of smuggling businesses that
rely on flight routes to Benina Benghazi Airport in Tripolitania. Additionally, there were increases in arrivals from other
nationalities, though in smaller numbers: Ethiopians saw a 21 per cent rise (from 1,064 to 1,289 people), and Somalis
increased by 11 per cent (from 584 to 648 people).

  Profiles of refugees and migrants

In line with previous years, in 2024, most individuals arriving in Italy by sea continued to be adult males, accounting for
75 per cent of total sea arrivals. In the same year, the proportion of women among sea arrivals declined significantly,
from 10 per cent in 2023 to 6 per cent in 2024, representing a 73 per cent decrease in absolute numbers 15,967 in
2023 vs. 4,276 in 2024). The reduction was largely attributed to fewer arrivals along routes traditionally used by
women, particularly those from Tunisia, mostly used by Sub-Saharans, and Türkiye, mostly used by Afghans. Likewise,
Libya was less frequently used as a transit country by female refugees and migrants, as well as families.

In 2024, the Syrian Arab Republic was the main country of origin for women arriving in Italy via sea, representing 22
per cent of all female sea arrivals (approximately 1 in 5), with the majority of Syrian women having departed from Libya.
Other countries of origin of women included Tunisia (17%), Guinea (16%), Côte d'Ivoire (6%), and Afghanistan (5%).

In 2024, unaccompanied children (UAC) represented 13 per cent of all sea arrivals to Italy, totalling 8,752 individuals.
This proportion is in line with trends observed in previous years, when UAC arrivals fluctuated between 12 and 15 per
cent. The top nationalities of UAC in 2024 were Tunisia (16%), Guinea (14%), The Gambia (11%), Egypt (10%), and
Eritrea (9%).
