### For Background, "What you pay, and what you earn and keep" → #### Money sent home

Italy is one of Bangladesh's biggest sources of remittances: USD 2,051.5 million in July 2025-June 2026, up from USD 810.9 million five years earlier, and USD 602.24 million in July-September 2026, sixth among all countries [G0007, G0009, G0005]. On Italy's side, Bangladesh received 19.4% of all money transfers sent from Italy in the year to June 2026, more than any other country, about EUR 1,686 million in 2025, the largest amounts from the provinces of Rome (EUR 364.9 million), Milan, Naples and Venice [G0014, G0016, G0019, G0020, G0021, G0022]. Those Italian figures count only cash sent through money-transfer companies, not bank-to-bank transfers, and transfers fell in the first half of 2026, to EUR 795.8 million against EUR 834.9 million a year earlier [G0013, G0018]. Sending from Italy cost 1.27% of the amount on average in April-June 2026, and 1.57% for sums up to USD 200 (how to send: see Sending money home) [G0011, G0078]. One estimate puts unrecorded flows, hundi included, at enough to raise the true total sent from Italy from EUR 8.6 billion to EUR 9.9-12.5 billion [G0023].

## Sending money home

**Send through a licensed money-transfer company, the post office or a bank, never hundi.** Hundi (hawala: money passed outside the banking system through private agents) is illegal in Bangladesh, and in Italy carrying on payment or financial business without Banca d'Italia authorisation is a crime [G0045, G0080]. Bangladesh's Financial Intelligence Unit (BFIU) had frozen or suspended about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling by June 2026, so a family paid through hundi into bKash or Nagad can lose the money [G0044]. Money sent legally earns the family Bangladesh's 2.5% government cash incentive, kept in the FY2026-27 budget, and sometimes a further 2.5% from the bank; hundi gets neither [G0043, G0030]. Italy has no tax on remittances: its 2019 tax of 1.5% on money transfers above EUR 10 to non-EU countries was repealed by the 2021 Budget Law [G0026, G0025, G0027].

> **Need to know**
> - Is any 2026-27 Italian bill or budget amendment proposing to bring back a tax on remittances?

### Your salary and bank account

Since 1 July 2018 every employer in Italy must pay wages, and any advance, through a bank or post office by traceable means (bank transfer, cheque, card), never in cash; an employer who pays cash is fined EUR 1,000 to 5,000 [G0057, G0058]. Payment onto a prepaid card in your name counts, even one without an IBAN (the international bank account number) [G0060]. Your signature on the payslip (busta paga) does not prove you were paid, so keep your bank statements as proof (see If you aren't paid) [G0059].

1. **Open a conto di base (basic bank account).** It is a legal right for everyone staying legally in the EU, including asylum seekers, refugees and homeless people, and banks and Poste Italiane must offer it [G0050]. It gives bank transfers, salary credit, a bank card, cash withdrawals and deposits, and automatic bill payment [G0048]. It is free if your ISEE (indicatore della situazione economica equivalente, the family means indicator) is below EUR 11,600; above that it costs much less than an ordinary account [G0047, G0049]. You cannot open one if you already have another current account in Italy, unless you close it, or use it for a business [G0049].
   You'll need:
   - your codice fiscale (tax code), even a numeric provisional one [G0053];
   - your permesso di soggiorno (residence permit) or the post-office receipt of your permit application or renewal with your photo; an Italian identity card is not needed [G0054, G0055].
   1. Go to a bank branch or post office and ask, in these words: "Vorrei aprire un conto di base. Il mio ISEE è sotto 11.600 euro, quindi deve essere gratuito." ("I want to open a basic account. My ISEE is below EUR 11,600, so it must be free.") [G0047]
   2. Show the codice fiscale and the permit or receipt [G0053, G0054].

   **If it fails:** banks rarely advertise the basic account, and in 2026 Banca d'Italia found that their computer systems shut out migrants with numeric provisional tax codes or permit receipts; only 180,000 basic accounts existed at the end of 2025 against 7 million people eligible [G0051, G0052]. If a bank refuses your numeric codice fiscale, permit or receipt, go to a patronato (union-linked welfare office), a trade union or a migrants' association such as ASGI (Associazione per gli Studi Giuridici sull'Immigrazione) for help, then complain in writing and, if needed, to the ABF (see If it fails, below) [G0056, G0061].

### Step by step: sending money

You'll need:
- your permesso di soggiorno (or receipt) or passport, and codice fiscale, as ID at the counter or in the app (Need to know below);
- for a bKash wallet: the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered [G0031];
- for a bank account: the receiver's name, account number, bank and branch.

1. **Check the company is licensed.** Payment institutions and banks working in Italy must be on Banca d'Italia's registers, searchable at infostat.bancaditalia.it/GIAVAInquiry-public/ng/ (type the company name); working without authorisation is a crime [G0079, G0080]. In 2020 the Guardia di Finanza (Italy's financial police) seized a payment institution and six money-transfer shops in Rome run by Bangladeshis over EUR 20 million of transfers to Bangladesh made without identifying the senders [G0041].
2. **Choose the channel.** bKash lists these partners for sending from Italy straight to a bKash wallet: Ria, Remitly, National Exchange, Placid Express, WorldRemit, Wise, NEC Money, Taptap Send, Western Union, MoneyGram and ACE Money Transfer [G0028]. In the World Bank's August-September 2025 survey, sending EUR 140 (about Tk 19,500) cost [G0033, G0001]:
   - 2.98% (EUR 0.99 fee) online with a debit card through Poste Italiane's Western Union service, for cash pickup, the cheapest [G0035];
   - 4.57% (EUR 2.50 fee) in cash at a National Exchange Company agent, to a bank account [G0036];
   - 4.81% (EUR 3.00 fee) in cash at a Janata Exchange Company branch, to a bank account [G0037];
   - 5.46% (EUR 5.00 fee) in cash at a post office through Poste Italiane's MoneyGram service [G0038];
   - 22.24% at Extrabanca and 33.57% by bank transfer from Intesa Sanpaolo (EUR 25 and EUR 47 fees): do not send small sums by ordinary bank transfer [G0039, G0040].
   The average for EUR 140 was 9.55% [G0034].
3. **Compare the rate, not only the fee.** The total cost is the fee plus the gap between the market rate and the rate the company gives you [G0035]. On 10 November 2026 the market rate was about Tk 139.6 per euro [G0001].
4. **Send.** At the counter, give the bKash number and registered name (or the bank details) and hand over the cash; or send from the company's app with your card [G0031, G0035]. bKash accepts up to Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer, any number of times [G0029].
5. **The family collects.** bKash remittance can be cashed out for Tk 7 per thousand at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs, and the 2.5% government incentive is added on receipt [G0029, G0030].

**If it fails, complain in this order:**
1. The company or bank: a written complaint (reclamo) to its customer service; for payment services it must answer within 15 working days [G0061, G0062].
2. The ABF (Arbitro Bancario Finanziario, the banking ombudsman run with Banca d'Italia), online at arbitrobancariofinanziario.it, if there is no answer or you disagree, within 12 months of your complaint; it costs EUR 20, refunded if you win even in part [G0064, G0063].
3. bKash, for a wallet transfer: +88-02-55663001 from abroad, 16247 in Bangladesh, support@bkash.com [G0032].

### Hundi

Undocumented workers have no formal channel for sending money, which keeps hundi alive (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [G0046, G0045]. Italy has prosecuted Bangladeshi money-transfer networks: the 2020 Rome seizure grew out of a 2019 case against a Bangladeshi ring that, for profit, helped compatriots stay in Italy illegally [G0041, G0042].

> **Need to know**
> - Have the Guardia di Finanza or Bangladeshi police broken up an Italy-Bangladesh hundi network in 2025-2026, and where (Rome, Milan, Venice-Mestre, Naples)?

### Tax on your pay

From 2026 IRPEF (imposta sul reddito delle persone fisiche, Italy's income tax) is charged at 23% on income up to EUR 28,000 a year and 33% from EUR 28,000 to 50,000, after the 2026 Budget Law cut the middle rate from 35% [G0066, G0067, G0065]. Since 2025 a worker who is not an Italian, EU or EEA citizen can no longer claim tax deductions for a wife, children or parents living in Bangladesh; deductions for other relatives now apply only to parents living with you in Italy [G0068, G0069].

## For "Coming home" (with B): customs on both sides

**Leaving Italy:**
- Declare EUR 10,000 or more in cash, or the equivalent in other currencies, to Italian customs on the EU cash declaration form when you leave the EU [G0070, G0073]. Gold bars of at least 99.5% gold and coins of at least 90% gold count as cash [G0071]. Customs can act below EUR 10,000 if they suspect the money is linked to crime, and not declaring is penalised [G0072].

**Arriving in Dhaka:**
- Up to 2 used mobile phones and 1 new phone once a year, without duty or tax [G0074].
- At most 117 g (10 tola) of gold, one bar, once a year, declared at customs [G0075].
- More than USD 10,000 in foreign currency must be declared on the FMJ form [G0076].

> **Need to know**
> - What does Italian customs do in practice at Rome Fiumicino or Milan Malpensa when a Bangladeshi worker carries undeclared cash just over EUR 10,000: seizure, fine, or both?
