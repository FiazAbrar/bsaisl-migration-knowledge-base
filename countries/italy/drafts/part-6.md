## Asylum

Asylum (international protection) is only for someone who cannot go home because they fear persecution or serious harm and cannot get protection from their own country [A0106]. Italy and the EU both list Bangladesh as a "safe country of origin" (a country whose people are presumed not to need protection), and the EU list applies from 12 June 2026 [A0001, A0007, A0008]. In 2025 Italy refused 26,240 of 29,595 Bangladeshi claims at first decision, 88.66% [A0038, A0039]. From January to September 2026 it refused 24,065 of 27,790 and gave refugee status to 375 people [A0042, A0043, A0044, A0045, A0046]. A refusal is also a return order and an entry ban [A0087]. Italy's Ambassador in Dhaka said in November 2025 that most Bangladeshis who arrive irregularly ask for asylum with false papers, fail and have to go home [A0099].

Bangladeshis were still the largest group of applicants in Italy in 2025: 28,365 people, with 37,130 cases waiting at year end [A0037]. First-time applications from Bangladeshis fell from 1,875 in June 2026 to 720 in July, the first full month of the new EU rules [A0047, A0008].

### Bangladesh is a "safe country"

Bangladesh has been on Italy's list of safe countries of origin since a Ministerial Decree of 7 May 2024 [A0002]. In October 2024 the government wrote the list into Article 2-bis of Legislative Decree 25/2008 (the Procedure Decree, Italy's main asylum procedure law), and Bangladesh is still on it [A0003, A0001]. On 23 February 2026 the EU adopted its first common list: Bangladesh, Colombia, Egypt, India, Kosovo, Morocco and Tunisia [A0006, A0007]. It applies from 12 June 2026 with the rest of the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum (the EU's common asylum and return rules), and Italy keeps its own list alongside it [A0008, A0009]. The European Commission can suspend a country from the EU list after an assessment [A0010].

What it means for an applicant:

- **You must prove Bangladesh is unsafe for you personally.** A claim can be rejected as "manifestly unfounded" (obviously without basis) if you have not shown serious reasons why Bangladesh is not safe for you in particular [A0004]. The Questura (the provincial police headquarters, which registers asylum claims) must warn you of this when the claim is lodged [A0005].
- **Fast track.** A nationality with an EU-wide recognition rate of 20% or lower goes into the accelerated procedure, which must end within three months [A0011, A0012]. Across the EU, 4% of Bangladeshi applicants succeeded in 2024 [A0104].
- **Border procedure.** Italy's law implementing the Pact makes the border procedure compulsory for people from countries with an acceptance rate below 20% [A0013]. Italy must examine up to 16,032 claims a year this way between 12 June 2026 and 12 June 2027 [A0014]. During it you must stay at or near the border or in a designated place, which does not count as entering Italy [A0015]; in practice this has amounted to detention [A0082]. The Territorial Commission (the local panel that decides asylum claims) must decide within four weeks, and the whole procedure, appeal included, must end within twelve weeks [A0072, A0018, A0017].

**The August 2025 court ruling:** on 1 August 2025 the EU Court of Justice ruled, in the case of two Bangladeshis taken from the sea to Italy's detention centre in Albania, that a country designated safe by law must be open to review by a court, on sources the applicant can see [A0032, A0033]. It also ruled that a country that does not protect its whole population cannot be listed, but only until the new EU Regulation took effect on 12 June 2026, which allows exceptions for groups of people [A0035, A0034]. The ruling affected transfers to Albania but did not take Bangladesh off either list [A0036, A0001, A0007].

### How to apply

These rules apply to claims made from 12 June 2026; a claim made before that date continues under the old rules until it ends [A0024].

1. **Ask for asylum at the Questura or the border.** Already in Italy, go to the Ufficio immigrazione (Immigration Office) of the nearest Questura; on arrival, ask the Border Police [A0050]. You can ask orally or in writing, with a free interpreter [A0054, A0059]. People in prison or in a CPR (Centro di Permanenza per i Rimpatri, a closed centre where people are held before removal) can also apply [A0051, U0039]. Questure and border police offices registered all asylum claims until 31 October 2026 [A0025].

   You'll need [A0052, A0053, A0062]:
   - your passport, if you have one (the police may ask for it);
   - every document that supports your story;
   - if you want, a written account of your story, in Italian or another language.

   1. Give your personal details and reasons; the police take your photo and fingerprints, which go into Eurodac, the EU fingerprint database [A0052].
   2. Your claim is recorded on form C3 (personal history, journey, reasons for leaving), which you sign; you get a copy [A0066].
   3. You receive a document with your name, a unique identity code, your photo and the details you declared; it proves the claim is registered [A0026].
   4. You are given the National Commission for the Right of Asylum's "Practical guide for applicants for international protection in Italy", which exists in Bengali [A0058].
   5. If you need a place to live, say so now: a reception place must be asked for when you apply (While you wait: reception, work and papers, item 1) [A0089].

   **Apply early:** a claim made without good reason more than 90 days after entering irregularly, or after your permit expired, goes into the accelerated procedure and gives no right to a reception place [A0070, A0071]. **Only one EU country examines your claim,** generally the first one you entered, and you cannot choose [A0103].

   **If it fails:** in big cities registration can take weeks or months after you ask, and a Questura that turns people away gives them no paper, which leaves them open to arrest and deportation [A0067, A0068]. In 2024, waits of 90 to 100 days were recorded at the Questure of Vicenza and Venice [A0069]. For help, in this order:
   1. The free legal service at your reception centre, or a free NGO legal desk [A0056].
   2. The ARCI-UNHCR helpline for refugees and asylum seekers: 800 905 570, or +39 351 137 6335 on WhatsApp or Lycamobile, numeroverderifugiati@arci.it [A0091].

   > **Need to know**

2. **Go to the interview at the Territorial Commission.** Only the Commission judges your claim; the police cannot [A0072]. A Bangla interpreter must be present [A0059]. You must attend, state every relevant fact and hand over all your documents, including your passport [A0062]. Missing the interview can count as withdrawing your claim [A0064].
   - Tell the Questura and the Commission at once of any change of address; otherwise letters, including the decision, count as delivered to your last declared address [A0063].
   - You may bring a lawyer at your own cost; none is needed at this stage [A0060, A0056].
   - What you say is confidential and is not shared with Bangladeshi authorities without your consent [A0055].
   - For people rescued at sea, caught crossing irregularly or held in a CPR, the Questore (head of the Questura) can order a check of their phone and SIM to establish identity, nationality and route; messages are excluded [A0065].

   **Time limits:** a decision is due within 8 months in the ordinary procedure, 4 months in the accelerated procedure and four weeks in the border procedure [A0076, A0018].

### While you wait: reception, work and papers

1. **Reception.** You get a reception place only if you ask for it when you apply, and you cannot choose the town [A0089]. Most people are placed in CAS (Centri di Accoglienza Straordinaria, emergency reception centres), which held 101,106 people on 31 December 2025 [A0095]. Pocket money is EUR 2.50 (about Tk 350) a day, and there is no allowance outside the system [A0093, G0001]. Italian lessons and job orientation are optional services [A0094]. You lose the place if you leave without permission, are violent or have enough money of your own [A0090]. If the centre's manager does not fix a problem, complain to the local Prefettura (the central government's office in each province) [A0092].
2. **Work.** You may work once 90 days have passed since your claim was formalised, if no decision has been made and the delay is not your fault [A0027]. The asylum permit cannot be turned into a work permit, even if you have a job [A0030]. Employers are often wary of hiring someone who holds only an asylum receipt [A0031].

> **Need to know**
> - Under Decree-Law 100/2026, what permit does an asylum seeker hold after formalisation, for how long, and is it renewed at the Questura or the Prefettura?

### The decision

The Commission can grant refugee status or subsidiary protection (both with 5-year permits), find that special protection applies (a 2-year permit), or reject the claim [A0086, A0083, A0087]. For Bangladeshis in January to September 2026, 375 got refugee status and 3,255 special protection, out of 27,790 decisions [A0046, A0045].

**Special protection (protezione speciale):** a national permit for people who cannot be expelled because they risk persecution, torture or inhuman treatment, or because of Italy's constitutional and international obligations [A0083]. Many workers caught by fake job contracts try to regularise through it [N0088]. Since Decree-Law 20/2023 (the "Cutro decree"), it can no longer be asked for directly at the Questura, and your private and family life in Italy no longer counts as a ground [A0084]. The permit lasts two years and is renewable, but can no longer be converted into a work permit [A0085]. Renewals can take 16 to 18 months [A0086].

Victims of labour exploitation can get a separate "special cases" permit (see Caporalato: illegal gangmasters) [A0088].

### If it is refused

The rejection is also a return order and an entry ban, with the effect of an expulsion order, and is challenged in the same appeal [A0087].

1. **Appeal within the deadline.** It runs from the day the decision is notified:
   - ordinary procedure: 30 days (60 if you are abroad) [A0077];
   - accelerated cases under Article 67(7)(a) of the EU Asylum Procedure Regulation: 7 days [A0021];
   - border procedure: 5 days [A0019].

   The appeal goes to the specialised immigration section of the civil court (tribunale) [A0074]. A lawyer is required, and people without money have the right to a free, state-paid lawyer (patrocinio a spese dello Stato) [A0057, A0060].
2. **Ask to stay while the court decides, where needed.** As a rule an appeal stops removal, except in the cases set by Article 68(3) of the Regulation [A0022]. In the border procedure it does not, except for unaccompanied children, so file a request to stay with the appeal; you cannot be removed while the judge decides it [A0019, A0020]. A second or later repeat claim gives no right to stay [A0081].
3. **Wait for the court.** Appeals often take far longer than the 4 months the law sets, in some cases ten times longer [A0075]. After the civil court, only a challenge on points of law at the Court of Cassation remains, within 30 days [A0074].

### After a final refusal

The return order and entry ban stand, with the effect of an expulsion order (see What can get you jailed or deported) [A0087]. On 25 September 2025 an EU-chartered flight returned 29 Bangladeshis, 16 of them from Italy [A0096]. Moving on to another EU country does not help: only one country examines the claim, and the EU rules from June 2026 close that option [A0103, A0098]. For ways to leave, see Going home without valid papers.

### Scams

- **"Political asylum is easy in Italy."** In 2025, 26,240 of 29,595 Bangladeshi claims were rejected at first decision [A0038]. The Italian Ambassador said in 2025 that people paid brokers the equivalent of about EUR 15,000 (about Tk 21 lakh) each to reach Italy [A0100, G0001].
- **Fake papers and bought stories.** Rights groups warn that most Bangladeshi claims may now be dismissed as manifestly unfounded at the outset [A0102]. Giving false information or documents puts you straight into the compulsory border procedure [A0013].
- **Paying for what is free.** The interpreter, legal advice at reception centres and many NGOs, and a court lawyer for people without money are free [A0054, A0056, A0057].

## Irregular routes and undocumented workers

The main irregular way into Italy for Bangladeshis is a legal flight to Benghazi in eastern Libya, then a boat to Italy [U0065, U0070, P0049]. Bangladeshis are the largest nationality landing in Italy by sea: 6,160 of 27,242 people from 1 January to 6 November 2026, 20,164 of 66,296 in 2025, and 13,779 in 2024 [U0001, U0002, U0005, U0006, U0007]. Italy's Ambassador in Dhaka warns that irregular entrants are very likely to be sent back, lose their money and may face future limits on travel to Europe [N0020]. What brokers charge is under Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends.

### The Libya sea route

#### How the route works

1. **Recruitment in Bangladesh.** A village broker, or a relative abroad, promises Italy; the deal is often presented as legal migration, and irregular migration is often the last stage of a failed legal attempt [U0064, U0092]. Loans are easier to get when the money is said to be for Italy [U0088].
2. **The flight.** Most fly Dhaka to Dubai, then to Benghazi directly or through Egypt; other routes run through India, Sri Lanka, Kuwait or Turkey [U0066, U0090]. Some are given USD 500 (about Tk 62,000) in cash to hand to the facilitator in Dubai [U0067, G0077].
3. **Entry to Libya.** People enter Benghazi legally, on entry clearances from the Libyan National Army's Military Investment Authority (the armed force that controls eastern Libya) [U0065]. A local sponsor is needed at Benghazi airport, and migrants describe a special queue for Bangladeshis [U0124, U0068].
4. **Across Libya.** Drivers move them west through Ajdabiya, Sirte and Shwerayif to Zuwara and Zawiya, the main departure points since 2023 [U0069, U0070, P0050].
5. **The boat.** The crossing to Lampedusa, the Italian island closest to Libya, takes 24 to 36 hours in fair weather [U0071].

#### What goes wrong in Libya

- **"Game houses"** (the last holding sites before the boat): smugglers demand more money even from people told their trip was fully paid [U0077].
- **Ransom videos:** families receive calls, photos or videos of their relative being tortured, with instructions to send money through informal channels [U0078]. In July 2026 the families of six young men from Manoharganj, Cumilla, received such videos by imo and WhatsApp and were asked Tk 25 lakh each [U0125, U0097, U0094].
- **Staged kidnappings:** Bangladeshi and Libyan smugglers stage kidnappings together to force new payments; travellers on the cheaper "normal" deal are told they must pay their own release [U0079, U0080].
- **Being sold:** people told they can pay after reaching Europe are made to work in Libya to repay the debt; in one case seven men were sold for USD 17,000 (about Tk 21 lakh) and the new handler asked USD 12,000 (about Tk 15 lakh) each [U0082, U0084, G0077]. Others are promised a "next visa" to Italy and temporary work in Libya, and have their passports taken on arrival [U0083].
- **Caught at sea:** people intercepted by the Libyan Coast Guard report being handed to Bangladeshi networks who demand more money [U0081].
- **Getting out:** the Bangladesh Embassy in Tripoli struggles to get Libyan exit permits; in 2023 it could not bring home about 2,000 people [U0085]. Returns do happen with IOM (International Organization for Migration, the UN migration agency): 175 people came home from Libya on 26 February 2026 [U0101, U0102].

#### Deaths at sea

In 2024, 1,718 people died or went missing crossing the central Mediterranean towards Italy, one for every 39 arrivals [P0054]. IOM counted about 765 deaths there from 1 January to 7 April 2026, over 460 more than in the same period of 2025 [U0106]. In early April 2026 more than 80 people went missing when a boat from Tajoura, Libya, capsized [U0107].

What families have done [U0098, U0093]:

3. **Worked with the Bangladesh Embassy in Libya and IOM,** which organise return flights with the Foreign and Expatriates' Welfare ministries [U0102].

### Landing: Lampedusa and the hotspots

1. **Hotspot.** People rescued at sea or caught crossing are taken to a hotspot (punto di crisi), such as the one on Lampedusa, for first aid, health checks, photos and fingerprints [U0049].
2. **Screening.** Identity checks, fingerprints and transmission to Eurodac must end within 72 hours [U0051]. Refusing identification can mean being held up to 28 days at the hotspot or in a CPR [U0052].
3. **Information.** Staff must explain the border procedure, asylum, relocation and assisted voluntary return [U0050]. What follows an asylum claim is under Asylum.

### Living without papers in Italy

- **It is an offence:** entering or staying without permission is punished by a fine of EUR 5,000 to 10,000 (about Tk 7 to 14 lakh); expulsion is under What can get you jailed or deported [U0045, G0001].
- **Health care:** people without papers get urgent or essential outpatient and hospital care, including continuing care, in public and accredited facilities [U0047]. Going to a hospital cannot lead to a report to the police, except where an Italian would also be reported [U0048].
- **Work:** an employer who hires a worker without a valid permit faces 6 months to 3 years in prison and EUR 5,000 (about Tk 7 lakh) per worker [U0046, G0001].
- **Losing a permit:** people who lost a regular job, and with it their permit, have been detained and sent to the centre in Albania [U0110].
- **No regularisation is open.** The last, the 2020 emersione (regularisation), took 207,542 employer applications, for domestic and care work or farming and fishing only [U0121, U0008, U0009]. Bangladeshis filed 16,102 domestic and care applications, the second most after Ukrainians, and 2,222 in farming [U0008, U0009]; most applied as caregivers [U0011].

### Detention in a CPR

Police hold people awaiting deportation in a CPR [U0039, L0106]. Italy had 11 CPRs with 883 places and 6,835 entries in 2025, and plans six more [U0117, U0119]. Bangladeshis are among those held [U0120].

- **How long:** a judge must validate detention, which then lasts 3 months; it can be extended by 3 months, then in 3-month steps up to 12 more months [U0039, U0122, U0040].
- **Legal help:** at the hearing that validates an expulsion, you can get free legal aid and an interpreter [L0105].
- **Who is detained:** having no valid passport counts as a "risk of flight", which allows removal by police escort [U0032, U0031].
- **Voluntary return from inside:** a detainee admitted to assisted voluntary return stays in the CPR until departure (Going home without valid papers, Step 2, item 1) [U0024].
- **If not removed:** if you cannot be held or removed, the Questore orders you in writing to leave within 7 days [U0041]. Ignoring the order can bring a fine of EUR 10,000 to 20,000 (about Tk 14 to 28 lakh), or EUR 6,000 to 15,000 (about Tk 8.4 to 21 lakh) if the expulsion came with time to leave voluntarily [U0043, U0044, G0001].

#### The Albania centre

Since a decree of 28 March 2025, people already in a CPR in Italy can be moved to the CPR at Gjader, Albania, run by Italy [U0108]. About 90 were held there in late February 2026, and no more than 700 had been transferred by August 2026 [U0109, U0113]. Detainees share a single phone, and anyone to be deported must first be flown back to Italy [U0112, U0111]. In August 2025 a Rome court gave international protection to several Bangladeshis who had been held at Gjader, among them a man tortured for ransom in Libya [U0115, U0116].

### Deportation to Bangladesh

Italy and Bangladesh signed an MoU (Memorandum of Understanding) on Migration and Mobility in Dhaka on 5-6 May 2025, covering returns under the 2017 Bangladesh-EU Standard Operating Procedures for returning people with no right to stay [U0053, U0054, B0030]. About 5,000 Bangladeshis have been returned from Europe under those procedures, many from Italy [U0063].

- Over 120 Bangladeshis were returned from Italy in 2025, to early November [M0034].
- On 31 October 2025 Italy returned 4 Bangladeshis, 2 of whom had arrived from Libya without a visa a month before; they were arrested around Rome and flown from Fiumicino [M0033, U0059].
- The Italian Embassy in Dhaka warns that paying traffickers, brokers and dalals (middlemen) to enter without a proper visa, or with a fraudulent one, leads to immediate repatriation [M0035].
- On 23 October 2026 a charter flight brought 19 Bangladeshis from Italy, with 10 from Greece and 6 from Cyprus [U0060].
- At Dhaka airport, BRAC's Migration Programme meets returned people with emergency help, counselling and money to get home, then training and financial help (see Coming home) [U0061, U0062].

### Scams

- **"Pay later, in Italy."** Debts promised "after arrival" become forced work in Libya (see What goes wrong in Libya) [U0082].
- **"Premium" journeys.** The promise that the smuggler pays any ransom is used to sell a higher price; cheaper travellers face staged kidnappings [U0080, U0079].
- **Italy-based contacts.** A Madaripur man was introduced through a relative to an Italy-based expatriate and a local agent who promised legal migration, and was later held for ransom in Tripoli on a Tk 24 lakh deal [U0092, U0089, U0091]. In February 2025 Italian anti-mafia police arrested a Madaripur man at Fiumicino airport after victims testified that his network held people hostage in Libya while his brother collected ransom in Bangladesh [U0104, U0105]; that network charged about EUR 11,000 (about Tk 15.4 lakh) a person [U0103, G0001].

## Going home without valid papers

Without a valid permit there are three ways out of Italy: assisted voluntary return (AVR), leaving on your own, or deportation [U0012, U0037, U0031]. AVR is free: it pays for the travel document, the ticket and a cash grant, and admission suspends any expulsion order [U0013, U0014, U0015, U0021]. Leaving on your own means no forced expulsion, but you can still be given a re-entry ban of 1 to 3 years [U0037, U0038]. Deportation brings a ban of 3 to 5 years [U0035].

### Step 1: Get a passport or Travel Permit

With a valid Bangladeshi passport, go to Step 2. If it has expired or been lost, get a Travel Permit (a one-way document to return home) from the Embassy of Bangladesh in Rome or the Consulate General in Milan (addresses under Help from the Bangladesh Embassy in Rome and Consulate in Milan); the Embassy in Rome charges EUR 28 (about Tk 3,900) [B0080, G0001]. Under AVR the programme pays for it [U0013].

### Step 2: Choose how to leave

1. **Apply for assisted voluntary return (AVR).** Italy's national AVR project is run by IOM, with local authorities and migrant-support associations; it takes departures until 2 February 2027 [P0129, U0020, P0130].

   You'll need: your passport or, without one, a Travel Permit from the Bangladesh mission, which the programme pays for (Step 1) [U0013].

   1. Contact IOM or a migrant-support association working on AVR; the F.A.S.T.E.R. project gives counselling to prepare departure and reintegration [U0020, P0080].
   2. A representative you give a mandate to can help with the application; since April 2026 the State pays that representative at the end of the procedure [U0025, P0079].
   3. Once you are admitted, the Prefettura tells the Questura, and any expulsion order is suspended [U0021].
   4. The project gives guidance, pays the travel document and the ticket to your home town, gives a cash grant before departure or on arrival, and gives help in Bangladesh to restart work, now payable in goods, by money transfer or in cash [U0017, U0013, U0014, U0015, U0016, P0078].

   **Who can't:** anyone who has used AVR before, or who was expelled as a criminal penalty or is wanted on an arrest warrant [U0018, U0023].

   **If you drop out:** you are removed by police escort at once [U0022].

2. **Leave on your own.** Buy a ticket and leave; a person without papers identified at the border on the way out is not expelled by force, but can be given a re-entry ban of 1 to 3 years from the day of exit [U0037, U0038].
3. **If you already have an expulsion order, ask for time to leave.** Ask the Prefect for a voluntary departure period of 7 to 30 days, which can also be done through AVR [U0027, U0028]. The Questura must explain this option with leaflets in several languages; if you don't ask, you are removed by escort [U0029]. The Questore can ask you to show lawful funds of 1 to 3 months of the assegno sociale (Italy's basic social allowance) [U0030]. Missing the deadline without good reason means removal by escort [U0033].

### Step 3: At the airport

Show your passport or Travel Permit at the police check on departure [U0037]. If you left under a voluntary departure order, keep proof that you left on time: the ban can then be revoked on request [U0036].

### If you are detained

A detainee admitted to AVR stays in the CPR until the flight, within the maximum detention time (see Detention in a CPR) [U0024]. Having no valid passport counts as a risk of flight and leads to removal by escort [U0032, U0031]. The Consulate General in Milan visits Bangladeshis held in Italian jails, contacts their families and advises on legal help (welfare.milan@gmail.com) [B0103].

### Bans after you leave

- **After deportation:** you cannot re-enter without the Interior Minister's special authorisation; the ban lasts 3 to 5 years [U0034, U0035, P0088].
- **Re-entering in breach of the ban:** 1 to 4 years in prison and a new expulsion by escort [U0034].
- **After voluntary departure:** the ban runs from the end of the time given and can be revoked if you prove you left in time [U0036].
- **After leaving on your own and being identified at the border:** 1 to 3 years [U0038].
