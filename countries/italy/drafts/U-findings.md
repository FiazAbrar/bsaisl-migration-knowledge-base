### For Background, "What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe"

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **"Pay in two parts and you go to Italy through Libya."** The standard deal is USD 10,000: USD 3,000 before leaving Bangladesh and USD 7,000 once in Libya; an "all-inclusive" deal is about USD 14,000 [U0072, U0073]. Payments demanded inside Libya often push the total to USD 15,000 to 17,000 [U0074]. Families have paid Tk 18 to 20 lakh to a village broker and then been asked Tk 25 lakh each in ransom [U0095, U0096, U0094]. How the route works and how it ends is under Irregular routes and undocumented workers [U0064, U0077].
- **"It starts as a legal visa."** Many begin with a legal plan and are steered onto the Libya route by the same brokers; one Madaripur man was promised legal migration for Tk 2.4 million and ended up held for ransom in Tripoli [U0064, U0089, U0091].
- **"Italy will regularise us again."** Stories of past regularisations are a main reason people go [U0086]. No regularisation is open now; the last, in 2020, was only for domestic, care and farm work, and its outcome is under Living without papers [U0008, U0009].
- **"Once in Italy, you are safe."** Italy returned over 120 Bangladeshis in 2025, two of them a month after they arrived from Libya, and the Italian Embassy in Dhaka warns that entering through dalals leads to immediate repatriation [U0057, U0056, U0058]. A return order brings a ban on re-entry of 3 to 5 years (see Going home without valid papers, Bans after you leave) [U0035].

## Irregular routes and undocumented workers

The main irregular way into Italy for Bangladeshis is by boat from Libya, after a legal flight into Benghazi [U0065, U0070]. Bangladeshis are the largest nationality landing in Italy: 6,160 of 27,242 people from 1 January to 6 November 2026, 20,164 of 66,296 in 2025, and 13,779 in 2024 [U0001, U0002, U0005, U0006, U0007].

### The Libya sea route

#### How the route works

1. **Recruitment in Bangladesh.** A village broker or a relative abroad promises Italy; the deal is often presented as legal migration [U0064, U0092]. Loans are easier to get when the money is said to be for Italy [U0088].
2. **The flight.** Most fly Dhaka to Dubai, then to Benghazi in eastern Libya directly or through Egypt; other routes run through India, Sri Lanka, Kuwait or Turkey [U0066, U0090]. Some are given USD 500 in cash to hand to the facilitator in Dubai as proof of payment [U0067].
3. **Entry to Libya.** People enter Benghazi legally, on entry clearances issued by the Libyan National Army's Military Investment Authority (the armed force that controls eastern Libya) [U0065]. A local sponsor is needed at Benghazi airport, and migrants describe a special queue for Bangladeshis [U0124, U0068].
4. **Across Libya.** Drivers move them west through Ajdabiya, Sirte and Shwerayif to Zuwara and Zawiya, the main departure points since 2023 [U0069, U0070].
5. **The boat.** The crossing to Lampedusa (the Italian island closest to Libya) takes 24 to 36 hours in fair weather [U0071].

#### What goes wrong in Libya

- **Holding houses and "game houses"** (the last holding sites before the boat): smugglers demand more money even from people told their trip was fully paid [U0077].
- **Ransom videos:** families receive calls, photos or videos of their relative being tortured, with instructions to send money through informal channels [U0078]. In July 2026 the families of six young men from Manoharganj, Cumilla, received such videos by imo and WhatsApp and were asked Tk 25 lakh each [U0125, U0097, U0094].
- **Staged kidnappings:** Bangladeshi and Libyan smugglers stage kidnappings together to force new payments; travellers on the "normal" (cheaper) deal are told they must pay their own release [U0079, U0080].
- **Being sold:** people told they can pay after reaching Europe are made to work in Libya to repay the debt; in one case seven men were sold for USD 17,000 and the new handler asked USD 12,000 each [U0082, U0084]. Others are promised a "next visa" to Italy and temporary work in Libya, and have their passports taken on arrival [U0083].
- **Caught at sea:** people intercepted by the Libyan Coast Guard report being handed to Bangladeshi networks who demand more money [U0081].
- **Getting out:** the Bangladesh Embassy in Tripoli struggles to get Libyan exit permits; in 2023 it could not repatriate about 2,000 people [U0085]. Returns do happen with IOM (International Organization for Migration, the UN migration agency): 175 people came home from Libya on 26 February 2026 [U0101, U0102].

#### Deaths at sea

IOM counted about 765 deaths on the central Mediterranean between 1 January and 7 April 2026, over 460 more than in the same period of 2025 [U0106]. In one wreck in early April 2026, more than 80 people went missing after a boat from Tajoura, Libya, capsized [U0107].

#### If a relative is held for ransom in Libya

Families who paid have gone, in this order [U0098, U0093]:

1. **The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Dhaka:** a written application asking for rescue; the Cumilla families did this in July 2026 [U0098].
2. **A case at the local police station:** in the Madaripur case a case at Turag Police led the PBI (Police Bureau of Investigation) to arrest three suspects on 21 April 2026, and the kidnappers released the man near Tripoli airport [U0093]. In the Cumilla case the local police said no written complaint had been filed [U0099].
3. **The Bangladesh Embassy in Tripoli and IOM,** which organise the return flight [U0102, U0093].

### Landing: Lampedusa and the hotspots

1. **Hotspot.** People rescued at sea or caught crossing are taken to a hotspot (punto di crisi), such as the one on Lampedusa, for first aid, health checks, photos and fingerprints [U0049].
2. **Fingerprints and screening.** Identity checks, fingerprinting and transmission to Eurodac (the EU fingerprint database) must end within 72 hours [U0051]. Refusing identification can mean being held up to 28 days at the hotspot or in a CPR [U0052].
3. **Information.** Staff must explain the border procedure, asylum, relocation and assisted voluntary return [U0050]. What happens after an asylum claim is under Asylum.

### Living without papers in Italy

- **It is an offence:** entering or staying without permission is punished by a fine of EUR 5,000 to 10,000 [U0045].
- **Health care:** people without papers get urgent or essential outpatient and hospital care, including continuing care, in public and accredited facilities [U0047]. Going to a hospital cannot lead to a report to the police, except where an Italian would also be reported [U0048].
- **Work:** an employer who hires a worker without a valid permit faces 6 months to 3 years in prison and EUR 5,000 per worker [U0046].
- **Losing a permit:** people who lost a regular job and with it their permit have been detained and sent to the centre in Albania [U0110].
- **No regularisation is open.** The last one, the 2020 emersione, took 207,542 employer applications, for domestic and care work or agriculture and fishing only [U0121, U0008, U0009]. Bangladeshis filed 16,102 domestic and care applications, the second most after Ukrainians, and 2,222 in agriculture [U0008, U0009]; most applied as caregivers [U0011].

### Detention in a CPR

A CPR (Centro di permanenza per il rimpatrio, a closed centre where people are held before removal) is where police hold people awaiting deportation [U0039]. Italy had 11 CPRs with 883 places and 6,835 entries in 2025, and plans six more [U0117, U0119]. Bangladeshis are among those held [U0120].

- **How long:** a judge must validate detention, which then lasts 3 months; it can be extended by 3 months, then in 3-month steps up to 12 more months [U0039, U0122, U0040].
- **Who is detained:** having no valid passport counts as a "risk of flight", which allows removal by police escort [U0032, U0031].
- **AVR from inside:** a detainee admitted to assisted voluntary return stays in the CPR until departure [U0024].
- **If not removed:** if the person cannot be held or removed, the Questore (head of the provincial police) orders them in writing to leave within 7 days [U0041]. Ignoring the order can bring a fine of EUR 10,000 to 20,000, or EUR 6,000 to 15,000 if the expulsion came with time to leave voluntarily [U0043, U0044].

#### The Albania centre

Since a decree of 28 March 2025, people already in a CPR in Italy can be moved to the CPR in Gjader, Albania, run by Italy [U0108]. About 90 were held there in late February 2026, and no more than 700 had been transferred by August 2026 [U0109, U0113]. Detainees share a single phone, and anyone to be deported must first be flown back to Italy [U0112, U0111]. In August 2025 a Rome court gave international protection to several Bangladeshis who had been held at Gjader, among them a man tortured for ransom in Libya [U0115, U0116].

### Deportation to Bangladesh

Italy and Bangladesh signed an MoU (Memorandum of Understanding) on Migration and Mobility in Dhaka on 5-6 May 2025, which covers returns under the 2017 Bangladesh-EU Standard Operating Procedures for returning people without a right to stay [U0053, U0054, U0055]. About 5,000 Bangladeshis have been returned from Europe under those procedures, many from Italy [U0063].

- Italy returned over 120 Bangladeshis in 2025 [U0057]. Four arrested around Rome were flown from Fiumicino on 31 October 2025 [U0123, U0059].
- On 23 October 2026 a charter flight brought 19 Bangladeshis from Italy, with 10 from Greece and 6 from Cyprus [U0060].
- At Dhaka airport, BRAC's Migration Programme meets returned people with emergency help, counselling and money to get home, then training and financial support (see Coming home) [U0061, U0062].
- Italy carried out 6,443 forced returns and 1,063 assisted voluntary returns of all nationalities from 1 January to 4 November 2026 [U0003].

> **Need to know**

### Scams

- **"Pay later, in Italy."** Debts promised "after arrival" become forced work in Libya (see What goes wrong in Libya) [U0082].
- **"Premium" journeys.** The promise that the smuggler pays any ransom is used to sell a higher price; cheaper travellers face staged kidnappings [U0080, U0079].
- **Italy-based contacts.** A man was introduced through a relative to an Italy-based expatriate and a local agent who promised legal migration [U0092]. In February 2025 Italian anti-mafia police arrested a Madaripur man at Fiumicino airport after victims testified that his network held people hostage in Libya while his brother collected ransom in Bangladesh [U0104, U0105]; that network charged about EUR 11,000 a person [U0103].

## Going home without valid papers

Italy has three ways out for someone without a valid permit: assisted voluntary return (AVR), leaving on your own, or deportation [U0012, U0037, U0031]. AVR is free and pays for the travel document, the ticket and a cash grant, and admission suspends any expulsion order [U0013, U0014, U0015, U0021]. Leaving on your own at the airport means no forced expulsion, but a re-entry ban of 1 to 3 years can still be given [U0037, U0038]. Deportation brings a ban of 3 to 5 years [U0035].

### Step 1: Get a passport or Travel Permit

With a valid Bangladeshi passport, go to Step 2. If it has expired or been lost, ask the Embassy of Bangladesh in Rome or the Consulate General in Milan for a Travel Permit (a one-way document to return home); the fee is EUR 28 (addresses and hours under Contacts) [B-fee]. Under AVR the programme pays this fee [U0013].

> **Need to know**
> - What documents does the Embassy in Rome ask for a Travel Permit (NID, old passport copy, photos, police report for a lost passport)?
> - How many days does a Travel Permit take in Rome and Milan?

### Step 2: Choose how to leave

1. **Apply for assisted voluntary return (AVR).**
   You'll need: your passport or the details for a Travel Permit; your Italian papers if any (expulsion order, old permit).
   1. Ask an AVR project partner, a Prefettura (the government office in each province) or a migrant-support association to put your case forward; the Ministry runs AVR with international organisations, local authorities and associations [U0020].
   2. A representative you sign a mandate for can help with the application; since 2026 the State pays that representative, so you should not pay [U0025, U0026].
   3. Once admitted, the Prefettura tells the Questura (provincial police headquarters), and any expulsion order is suspended [U0021].
   4. The project gives guidance, pays the travel document and the ticket to your home town, gives a cash grant before departure or on arrival, and an in-kind grant in Bangladesh to restart work [U0017, U0013, U0014, U0015, U0016].
   **Who can't:** anyone who has already used AVR, or anyone expelled as a criminal penalty or wanted on an arrest warrant [U0018, U0023].
   **If you drop out:** you are removed by police escort at once [U0022].
2. **Leave on your own.** Buy a ticket and leave; a person without papers identified at the border on the way out is not expelled by force, but can be given a re-entry ban of 1 to 3 years from the day of exit [U0037, U0038].
3. **If you already have an expulsion order, ask for time to leave.** Ask the Prefect for a voluntary departure period, which is 7 to 30 days and can be done through AVR [U0027, U0028]. The Questura must explain this option with multilingual leaflets; if you don't ask, you are removed by escort [U0029]. The Questore can ask you to show lawful funds of 1 to 3 months of the assegno sociale (Italy's basic social allowance) [U0030]. Missing the deadline without good reason means removal by escort [U0033].

### Step 3: At the airport

Show your passport or Travel Permit at the police check on departure [U0037]. If you left under a voluntary departure order, keep proof that you left on time: the ban can then be revoked on request [U0036].

> **Need to know**
> - What proof of exit does Italian border police give (stamp, form), and how is a request to revoke the ban sent from Bangladesh?

### If you are detained

A detainee admitted to AVR stays in the CPR until the flight, within the maximum detention time (see Detention in a CPR) [U0024]. Expulsion orders under AVR are suspended [U0021]. For family visits and help from the Bangladesh mission, see Contacts.

### Bans after you leave

- After deportation, a person cannot re-enter without the Interior Minister's special authorisation; the ban lasts 3 to 5 years [U0034, U0035].
- Re-entering in breach of the ban carries 1 to 4 years in prison and a new expulsion by escort [U0034].
- After voluntary departure, the ban runs from the end of the time given and can be revoked if you prove you left in time [U0036].
- After leaving on your own and being identified at the border, the ban is 1 to 3 years [U0038].
