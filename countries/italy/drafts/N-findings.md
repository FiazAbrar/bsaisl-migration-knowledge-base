# N findings: corridor, chain and money in practice (Italy)

## a. Draft doc text

### In one minute

**Italy is open to Bangladeshi workers, but only through a named Italian employer, and the legal route is narrow: about 10,000 Bangladeshis got Italian work visas in 2025 and about the same number are expected in 2026** [N0010, N0022]. Every legal work visa starts with a nulla osta (Italian for "no objection"), a work authorisation that an Italian employer requests for one named worker on a "click day", and that the immigration office of the Prefettura (the SUI, Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione) issues [N0028, N0021, N0150]. Because a worker in Bangladesh can't apply himself and can hardly find an Italian employer from Dhaka, a middleman steps in, and the nulla osta is sold: workers have paid EUR 7,000 to 20,000, often in two instalments, with the passport held until the last one is paid [N0145, N0115, N0054, N0058, N0059]. In 2023-24 so many nulla osta were bought on fake job offers that Italy froze every unvisaed Bangladeshi nulla osta in October 2024; many of those old cases were later rejected, while new ones are being processed quickly [N0001, N0012, N0011]. Many who paid and arrived found no employer waiting, could not get a residence permit, and became undocumented in debt [N0124, N0086, N0056]. Bangladeshis are now the fifth-largest non-EU community in Italy, 195,523 legal residents at the end of 2024, but only about one in ten new permits they got that year was for work; two-thirds came through asylum (see Asylum) [N0155, N0156, N0064, N0157].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

**Open, through the Decreto Flussi quota and a small training route outside it. New nulla osta issued since late October 2024 are being processed; many of the 2023-24 nulla osta frozen in October 2024 have been rejected** [N0010, N0011, N0040, N0012]. Who can go: anyone an Italian employer names in a nulla osta application; there is no Bangladesh-side skill test for the quota route, but the Article 23 route requires certified training first (see The channels) [N0028, N0029].

#### Status

- **11 October 2024:** Decree-Law 145/2024 suspended every work nulla osta already issued to Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Sri Lankan workers unless the visa had already been issued, and ended "silent consent" (automatic approval after 60 days) for them; their nulla osta are now issued only after the Ispettorato Nazionale del Lavoro (INL, the National Labour Inspectorate) checks the application [N0008, N0006, N0117].
- **17 October 2024:** the Italian Embassy in Dhaka said it would issue a work visa only after the SUI confirmed the suspended nulla osta had been verified, and from 20 October returned the passports of everyone waiting [N0002, N0003]. A suspended nulla osta does not expire while it waits [N0005].
- **August 2025:** about 8,000 of nearly 40,000 pending cases had been resolved, and nulla osta issued after 22 October 2024 were not suspended [N0039, N0040].
- **8 December 2025:** the Embassy said holders of a nulla osta issued or reconfirmed in 2025 register on VFS Global's portal and upload it, and get an appointment within a few days; holders of 2023-24 nulla osta wait until the immigration office in Italy finishes its review, after which VFS contacts them [N0134, N0135, N0136].
- **September 2026:** the Italian Ambassador, Antonio Alessandro, said new authorisations are processed quickly with a very small rejection rate, while the old suspended ones were "eventually rejected in large numbers" [N0011, N0012].

The rule that singles out Bangladesh was written to run, pending a Foreign Ministry list of high-risk countries, until 31 December 2025 [N0007].

#### The channels

- **Decreto Flussi (the yearly quota):** the route nearly every Bangladeshi worker uses: an Italian employer applies on click day for a named worker, the SUI issues the nulla osta, and the worker applies for the visa in Dhaka through VFS Global (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker, Step 3; Step 5) [N0028, N0021, N0134]. Italy's quotas are 164,850 workers for 2026, 165,850 for 2027 and 166,850 for 2028 [N0026]. At the Embassy's November 2026 seminar the organisers said Bangladesh has a reserved quota, in return for cooperating on returns of irregular migrants [N0027]. The quota is usually gone within ten minutes of the click day opening [N0150]. Many in Bangladesh call the seasonal visa the কৃষি ভিসা (agriculture visa) (see Work visa: seasonal worker) [N0149].
- **Article 23 (training first, outside the quota):** a worker who completes a pre-departure course certified by Italy's Ministry of Labour (basic Italian, civic education, job skills) can enter outside the quota, applications run all year with no click day, and the course can be done in Bangladesh [N0029, N0030, N0031]. The first projects in Bangladesh were set up by Italian private firms: Frontiere Skills (its TASTE project trains restaurant and hotel workers at Hotel Regency, Dhaka) and Format (textiles), with about 300 students enrolled in November 2026 [N0024, N0025, N0036]. Format says its training is free [N0032]. The Ambassador says several hundred Bangladeshis go this way each year [N0017].
- **Family reunification:** about 12,000 to 13,000 Bangladeshis a year get family visas, and family members may work once in Italy (see Family reunification) [N0013].
- **Not a work route:** a Schengen visit visa; refusals are high because many applicants submit fake or forged documents (see Visit visa (Schengen)) [N0018]. The irregular route through Libya (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [N0019].

> **Need to know**

> - What is Bangladesh's reserved quota in the 2026-2028 decree, in numbers, and does it cover seasonal and non-seasonal work?

#### Who qualifies

The employer chooses the worker by name; there is no public list of workers and no list of certified agencies an employer must use [N0113, N0120]. The flow is overwhelmingly male: 76.7% of Bangladeshis in Italy are men [N0066]. Italian is the only language at work, and a Bangladeshi migrants' organisation says most who go without the language or skills end up undocumented [N0129, N0161]. Detailed requirements: see Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker, Step 2.

#### Size

- **Workers leaving (BMET, the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training):** 653 in 2021, 7,594 in 2022, 16,879 in 2023, 1,164 in 2024 (the freeze), and 9,316 in 2025 [N0125, N0133].
- **Visas:** about 10,000 Italian work visas for Bangladeshis in 2025, and about the same expected in 2026 [N0010].
- **Applications:** Bangladeshis filed almost 58% of all Decreto Flussi applications in 2023 (352,562) and 45.8% in 2024 (328,877), far ahead of Morocco (15.8%), India (14.2%), Sri Lanka (6.6%), Pakistan (4.8%) and Egypt (4.1%) [N0046, N0044, N0045].
- **Visas against applications:** of 24,151 visas issued for the 2024 quota by 12 December 2024, 35 went to Bangladeshis, 1,866 to Sri Lankans and 41 to Pakistanis; for the 2023 quota, 3,409 Bangladeshis got visas [N0049, N0048]. Bangladeshis accounted for 39,397 rejected, revoked, archived or withdrawn 2024 applications, over half of all failures [N0047].
- **Living in Italy:** 195,523 Bangladeshi citizens held permits at 31 December 2024, up 16.9% in a year [N0155]. Rome holds 37,905 (19.4%), followed by Lombardy (15.1%), Veneto (10.8%), Campania (8.5%) and Sicily (6.8%) [N0065]. Of the 28,045 new permits Bangladeshis got in 2024, the most of any nationality, two-thirds came from asylum and protection claims and only about one in ten from work [N0063, N0157, N0064].

> **Need to know**
> - How many workers did BMET clear for Italy from January to October 2026 (OEP data; see agent B)?

#### What closed it before

- **2012-13:** Italy took Bangladesh off the quota list after Bangladeshi seasonal farm workers stayed on after the season; formal labour migration stopped in 2013 [N0140, N0126].
- **12 October 2020:** Italy put Bangladesh back on the Decreto Flussi list, and workers began going again in 2021 [N0141, N0125].
- **4 June 2024:** Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni filed a complaint with the national anti-mafia prosecutor, Giovanni Melillo, saying most Decreto Flussi entrants came from Bangladesh, where diplomats spoke of jobs being bought and sold, and that in Campania, with 157,000 applications (over half the total), under 3% of those given a seasonal visa went on to sign a contract [N0100, N0099, N0098].
- **October 2024:** Decree-Law 145/2024 froze the unvisaed nulla osta of Bangladeshis, Pakistanis and Sri Lankans; in Dhaka 34,959 of 38,039 applications (92%) were suspended [N0008, N0050]. Applicants held protests at the Embassy and in Dhaka's Kakrail area demanding visas or their passports back [N0152, N0130].
- **May 2025:** Bangladesh and Italy signed a memorandum of understanding on migration and mobility, which both sides cite as the framework for regular routes [N0041, N0023].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A legal job reaches a Bangladeshi worker through a person, not a list: an employer in Italy files for a worker he names, and the name usually comes through a Bangladeshi already in Italy or a broker [N0113, N0142, N0153]. The choke point is the nulla osta: only an employer can request one, a worker can't apply himself, and that is where the middlemen sell their place [N0145, N0112]. The Bangladesh Embassy's labour secretary in Rome put it this way: a group exploits a process that needs "a third party as personal reference" [N0139].

#### The quota and the click day

The government sets the quota; employers then race to file on the click day, and the quota usually fills in the first ten minutes [N0026, N0150]. Applications run at about five times the places: 609,000 for 132,000 in 2023 and 718,000 for 147,000 in 2024, according to the Ero Straniero campaign (a coalition of Italian NGOs) [N0160]. Few of them end in a legal job: of 127,707 quota places for 2023, only 16,188 (about 13%) led to a signed contract and a permit request, and for 2024 only 7.8% [N0052, N0053].

#### Who sells the nulla osta

- **Fake employers in Italy:** investigators in Campania found a ring that filed about 2,000 fictitious nulla osta requests through compliant or invented companies; each migrant paid EUR 7,000 to 8,000 [N0103, N0104]. In a 2026 Lecce-Taranto investigation, businesses filed "simulated" hiring requests only to obtain nulla osta, and 30 people were jailed or put under house arrest; one intercepted call called it "business di bangladesi" [N0109, N0111, N0110].
- **Consultants and patronati in Italy:** in the Campania case a lawyer filed click-day applications through his staff for at least EUR 5,000 a file, and patronato staff (patronati are union-linked advice offices that file paperwork) filed requests for a fee [N0106, N0107].
- **Officials:** in the same case, officials of the Prefettura and the Labour Inspectorate in Naples and Salerno allegedly took EUR 500 to 800 a file [N0105].
- **Bangladeshi brokers in Italy and Bangladesh:** workers describe a broker in Bangladesh with "a relative in Italy who handles legal entries", or a Bangladeshi with an agency in Italy who says he has firms ready to hire [N0059, N0054]. Brokers are called দালাল (dalal) [N0089].

> **Need to know**

> - What does the employer himself get paid per nulla osta?

#### The embassy queue

The queue at the Embassy in Dhaka was itself sold. On 19 February 2025, two Italian Foreign Ministry employees who had worked on visas, Nicola Muscatello and Roberto Albergo, were put under house arrest for corruption, and two Rome-based Bangladeshis, Nazrul Islam and Hossain Md Baleyet Patwari, were jailed, in an investigation by Rome's Guardia di Finanza (financial police) [N0072, N0073]. Prosecutors say the two Bangladeshis charged compatriots a fixed sum plus a share of their future earnings in Italy, and paid the officials in hotel stays, phones, watches and flights to speed up visas [N0074, N0076]. The Financial Express reported that the ring charged EUR 15,000 for a work visa and EUR 7,000 for a tourist visa, and that Nazrul Islam, linked to the Awami League's Italian branch, had brought nearly 1,500 Bangladeshis the year before [N0078, N0079, N0080]. Investigators also say embassy staff paid real Italian firms to join the scheme [N0084].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Rome court ruled on the Muscatello/Albergo/Nazrul Islam case, and were visas issued through the ring revoked?

#### The broker and the village

Many irregular migrants come from Shariatpur and Madaripur, the Ambassador says, and the same districts recur among legal-route victims: a Madaripur pharmacist paid EUR 16,000 for a contract and landed in Naples with no job [N0016, N0082]. The broker is often a relative: a Narsingdi man paid EUR 16,000 to a cousin in Naples, and no employer ever contacted him [N0086]. A Naples farm owner applied for workers through a Bangladeshi already on his farm [N0153].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a village dalal take on an Italy deal, and is he paid by the worker, the main broker or both?

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The official costs are small: a EUR 16 revenue stamp on the application, a visa fee of EUR 50 to 116 and a VFS fee of EUR 38, plus the flight [N0138, N0033]. In practice workers pay brokers EUR 7,000 to 20,000, or Tk 6 to 15 lakh, and many arrive to no job [N0104, N0115, N0054, N0144, N0137, N0124].

#### The cap

Italy sets no migration cost for the worker beyond those fees [N0164, N0138]. Workers told TBS in 2023 that the cost should be no more than Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh, against up to Tk 15 lakh they paid [N0137]. (For any BMET cap on Italy: see Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker, Step 1.)

#### What people really pay

- **USD 10,000 to 15,000:** what brokers charge for the visa, according to the Embassy's November 2026 seminar [N0034].
- **EUR 15,000:** the figure Meloni gave in 2024, and the work-visa price of the 2025 embassy bribery ring [N0101, N0078].
- **EUR 16,000:** what two workers who reached Naples in 2023 paid [N0082, N0086].
- **EUR 18,000 and EUR 20,000:** what two workers told the Ero Straniero campaign they paid [N0057, N0054].
- **Tk 6 to 10 lakh:** the middleman's take in 2023, according to the Daily Star; workers told TBS they paid up to Tk 15 lakh [N0144, N0137].
- **Tk 30 lakh:** the price agreed in May 2026 for a fake Schengen visa to Rome (see Scams) [N0090].

#### How it is paid

In instalments: a first payment to start the paperwork, the rest when the visa comes [N0058, N0115]. A Gazipur man paid over EUR 7,000 to an agency that found him an employer and owes the same again when the visa arrives [N0115]. The passport is held as security: one worker's broker kept it until the last instalment [N0059]. The 2025 bribery ring took part of its fee as a share of the worker's future wages in Italy [N0074].

#### How they borrow

Families sell land, pool savings and take bank loans: the Madaripur man took two bank loans, the Narsingdi man raised EUR 16,000 from relatives, loans and the sale of family land, and another worker said his bank debt grew daily [N0083, N0086, N0061].

> **Need to know**
> - What monthly rate does a village moneylender charge on an Italy loan in 2026, and do PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) migration loans cover Italy?

#### What the job pays

32% of Bangladeshis employed in Italy work in hotels and restaurants and 25.3% in industry [N0158]. The Daily Star puts seasonal and unskilled earnings at most Tk 1 to 1.5 lakh a month and skilled pay at EUR 1,500 to 2,500 [N0146, N0147]. Minimum pay under each national contract: see Your rights at work.

#### What goes unpaid

Those who arrive through a fake or broken offer earn far below the contract: one worker taken from the airport by his broker worked 16 to 17 hours a day, every day, for EUR 400 to 500 a month, and paid EUR 140 of it back to the broker for a shared room [N0060]. Another found his employer couldn't register the contract because it was behind on social security payments (DURC, the certificate of paid contributions), and he could work only for that employer [N0055, N0056].

#### Money sent home

Italy sent Bangladesh EUR 1,397 million in 2024, more than to any other country and 17% of all remittances leaving Italy, up 20% in a year [N0068, N0067]. The Ambassador puts Italy's share at about 5% of Bangladesh's remittances, about USD 1.7 billion [N0015]. Only 77% of Bangladeshi adults in Italy have a bank account (how to send: see Sending money home) [N0070].

#### How long the debt takes

A seasonal worker who paid Tk 6 lakh can't save it back in one nine-month season [N0148]. At EUR 15,000 to 20,000 the debt is far larger, and workers who found no job were still carrying it years later [N0054, N0086, N0082].

> **Need to know**
> - How long does a non-seasonal worker who paid EUR 15,000 and has a real job take to clear the debt, after rent and food?

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The commonest failure is paying for a nulla osta and arriving to no job, no contract and no permit [N0114, N0124, N0056]. A Naples migrant network counts more than 1,200 people caught in one Italy-Bangladesh visa-fraud scheme over four years, and its help desk sees at least 100 Bangladeshis with the same problem every Thursday [N0081, N0085].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A nulla osta means I can go."** It is only an authorisation for the employer to hire you; the Embassy decides on the visa [N0028]. Many 2023-24 nulla osta were frozen and later rejected (see Status) [N0012].
- **"With the visa, a job is waiting."** Many found no employer; one man fell into irregularity in Rome because the company that was to hire him did not exist [N0124, N0086]. Only about 13% of the 2023 quota and 7.8% of the 2024 quota ended in a contract and permit request [N0052, N0053].
- **"I came legally, so Italy will see me as a victim."** A Naples social worker says the state treats such workers as fraudsters, not victims; many try special protection instead (see Asylum) [N0087, N0088].
- **"Paying more gets it done faster."** The Embassy says paying an intermediary can get the visa cancelled and bring legal action [N0043].
- **"Libya is the cheaper way."** Bangladesh is one of the largest sources of irregular arrivals; the Ambassador warns that irregular entrants are very likely to be sent back, lose their money and may face future travel bans (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [N0019, N0020]. About 10,000 of roughly 30,000 people who crossed the Mediterranean in the first half of 2025 were Bangladeshi [N0121].

### Scams (Decreto Flussi worker)

#### Done to workers

- **The fake or empty nulla osta.** Firms in Italy file hiring requests only to sell the nulla osta, with no job behind it (see Who sells the nulla osta) [N0109, N0103]. Check the nulla osta with the employer and the Embassy before paying (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker, Step 5) [N0135].
- **"I know someone at the Embassy."** In the 2025 bribery case, Italian officials and two Bangladeshi middlemen were arrested over exactly this offer (see The embassy queue) [N0072, N0073, N0078]. The Embassy says payments to intermediaries risk cancellation of the visa [N0043].
- **The passport held as security.** Brokers keep the passport until the last instalment; a ring working from a Gulshan-1 travel agency in Dhaka took a woman's passport and returned it only after she paid Tk 4.4 lakh into their bank accounts [N0059, N0162, N0095].
- **Someone else flies on your visa.** In July 2026 the CID (Criminal Investigation Department) arrested seven members of the same ring, who sent another woman to Italy on a woman's family visa; she learned at Dhaka airport that the other woman had flown on 24 April [N0093, N0096]. The broker approached her as she collected her passport from the Embassy for renewal [N0094]. Its alleged leader, Belayet Hossain, had sent about 30 people this way since 2016 [N0097].
- **The Bucharest detour.** Agencies sell Romanian work visas as a way into Europe: one man paid EUR 11,000, was offered undeclared building work, and moved on to Italy without papers [N0122].

#### Tried by workers

- **Fake Schengen visas.** In May 2026 three men flew to Rome on fake Schengen visas with a Biman ground officer's help; two were caught at Rome airport and sent back, after agreeing Tk 30 lakh each and paying Tk 20 lakh up front, and the CID arrested the officer [N0090, N0091, N0092].
- **Someone else's passport and visa.** The Gulshan-1 case was filed under Bangladesh's human trafficking and migrant smuggling law [N0163].
