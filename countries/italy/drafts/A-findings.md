## Asylum

Asylum almost never works for Bangladeshis in Italy, and since 12 June 2026 their claims go through fast-track procedures [A0011, A0013, A0104]. Italy treats Bangladesh as a "safe country of origin" (a country whose people are presumed not to need protection), and so does the EU's own list, which applies from 12 June 2026 [A0001, A0007, A0008]. In 2025 Italy rejected 88.66% of Bangladeshi applications at first decision; in the first nine months of 2026 it rejected 24,065 of 27,790 [A0039, A0042, A0043, A0044, A0045]. A rejection carries a return order and an entry ban [A0087]. Bangladeshis were still the largest group of applicants in Italy in 2025, with 28,365 applicants and 37,130 cases waiting at year end [A0037]. First-time applications from Bangladeshis fell from 1,875 in June 2026 to 720 in July, the first full month of the new rules [A0047].

Asylum (international protection) is for someone who cannot go home because they fear persecution or serious harm and cannot get protection from their own country [A0106]. Italy's Ambassador in Dhaka said in November 2025 that most Bangladeshis who arrive irregularly ask for asylum with false papers, fail and have to go home [A0099].

### Bangladesh is a "safe country"

Bangladesh has been on Italy's list of safe countries of origin since a Ministerial Decree of 7 May 2024 [A0002]. In October 2024 the government wrote the list into law itself, in Article 2-bis of Legislative Decree 25/2008 (the Procedure Decree, Italy's main asylum procedure law), and Bangladesh is still on it [A0003, A0001]. On 23 February 2026 the EU adopted its first common list of safe countries: Bangladesh, Colombia, Egypt, India, Kosovo, Morocco and Tunisia [A0006, A0007]. The EU list applies from 12 June 2026, and Italy keeps its national list alongside it [A0008, A0009]. The European Commission can suspend a country from the EU list after an assessment [A0010].

What it means for an applicant:

- **You must prove the country is unsafe for you personally.** An application can be rejected as "manifestly unfounded" (obviously without basis) if the applicant has not shown serious reasons why Bangladesh is not safe for them in particular [A0004]. The Questura (the provincial police headquarters, which registers asylum claims) must warn a Bangladeshi applicant of this when the claim is lodged [A0005].
- **Fast track.** Under the EU Asylum Procedure Regulation, a nationality with an EU-wide recognition rate of 20% or lower goes into the accelerated procedure, which must end within three months [A0011, A0012]. Across the EU, only 4% of Bangladeshi applicants succeeded in 2024 [A0104].
- **Border procedure.** Italy's law implementing the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum makes the border procedure compulsory for people from countries with an acceptance rate below 20% [A0013]. Italy must examine up to 16,032 claims a year this way between 12 June 2026 and 12 June 2027 [A0014]. During it the applicant must stay at or near the border or in a designated place, which does not count as entering Italy [A0015], and in practice this has amounted to detention [A0082]. The Territorial Commission must decide within four weeks, and the whole border procedure, appeal included, within twelve weeks [A0018, A0017].

**The August 2025 court ruling:** On 1 August 2025 the EU Court of Justice ruled, in a case brought by two Bangladeshis taken from the sea to Italy's centre in Albania, that a country can be designated safe by law only if a court can review that designation against sources the applicant can see [A0032, A0033]. It also ruled that a country that does not protect its entire population cannot be on the list, but that rule lasted only until the new EU Regulation took effect on 12 June 2026, and the new Regulation allows exceptions for groups of people [A0035, A0034]. The ruling slowed transfers to Albania, but it did not take Bangladesh off either list (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [A0036, A0001, A0007].

### How to apply

The rules below apply to claims made from 12 June 2026; a claim made before that date continues under the old rules until it ends [A0024].

1. **Ask for asylum at the Questura or the border.** A person already in Italy goes to the Ufficio immigrazione (Immigration Office) of the nearest Questura; on arrival, to the Border Police [A0050]. You can ask orally or in writing, in Bangla, with an interpreter, who is free [A0054, A0059]. People in prison or a CPR (Centro di Permanenza per i Rimpatri, a pre-removal detention centre) can also apply [A0051]. Until 31 October 2026, Questure and border police offices register all asylum claims [A0025].

   You'll need [A0052, A0053, A0062]:

   - your passport, if you have one (the police may keep it);
   - every document that supports your story;
   - if you want, a written account of your story, in Italian or another language.

   1. Give your personal details and reasons; the police take your photo and fingerprints, which go into Eurodac, the EU fingerprint database [A0052].
   2. Your claim is recorded on form C3 (personal history, journey, reasons for leaving), which you sign; you get a copy of the C3 and of every document you hand in [A0066].
   3. You receive a named document with a unique identity code, your photo and the details you declared; it proves the claim is registered [A0026].
   4. Ask for the National Commission for the Right of Asylum's "Practical guide for applicants for international protection in Italy", which is given at filing and exists in Bengali [A0058].
   5. If you need a place to live, say so now: a reception place must be requested when you apply (While you wait: reception, work and papers, item 1) [A0089].

   **Apply early:** an application made without good reason more than 90 days after entering irregularly or after your permit expired goes into the accelerated procedure and gives no right to a reception place [A0070, A0071]. **Only one EU country examines your claim,** generally the first one you entered, and you cannot choose [A0103].

   **If it fails:** in big cities registration can take weeks or months after you ask, and a Questura that turns people away gives them no paper, which leaves them open to arrest and deportation [A0067, A0068]. Wait times of 90 to 100 days were recorded at the Questure of Vicenza and Venice in 2024 [A0069]. For help, in this order:

   1. The free legal service at your reception centre, or a free NGO legal desk [A0056].
   2. The ARCI-UNHCR helpline for refugees and asylum seekers: 800 905 570, or +39 351 137 6335 on WhatsApp or Lycamobile, numeroverderifugiati@arci.it [A0091].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Where is the Bengali "Practical guide" published online (exact URL)?
   > - Which Questure in Rome, Milan, Naples and Venice take asylum claims by online booking, and how long is the wait in late 2026?
   > - Who registers asylum claims from 1 November 2026, when the Questure's transitional role ends?

2. **Go to the interview at the Territorial Commission.** Only the Territorial Commission (the Ministry of the Interior's local panel that decides asylum claims) interviews you on the merits; the police cannot judge your claim [A0072]. A Bangla interpreter must be present [A0059]. You must attend, state every relevant fact and hand over all your documents, including your passport [A0062]. Missing the interview can count as withdrawing your claim [A0064].

   - Tell the Questura and the Commission at once of any change of address; otherwise letters, including the decision, count as delivered to your last declared address [A0063].
   - You may bring a lawyer at your own cost; a lawyer is not needed at this stage [A0060, A0056].
   - What you say is confidential and is not shared with Bangladeshi authorities without your consent [A0055].
   - For people rescued at sea, caught crossing irregularly or held in a CPR, the Questore can order a check of their phone and SIM to establish identity, nationality and route; messages are excluded [A0065].

   **Time limits:** a decision is due within 8 months in the ordinary procedure and 4 months in the accelerated procedure, and within four weeks in the border procedure [A0076, A0018].

### While you wait: reception, work and papers

1. **Reception.** You get a reception place only if you ask for it when you apply, and you cannot choose the town [A0089]. Most people are placed in CAS (Centri di Accoglienza Straordinaria, emergency reception centres): 101,106 people were in CAS on 31 December 2025 [A0095]. Pocket money is EUR 2.50 a day, and there is no allowance outside the system [A0093]. Italian lessons and job orientation are optional services that many centres do not offer [A0094]. You lose the place if you leave without authorisation, are violent or have enough money of your own [A0090]. If the centre does not fix a problem, complain to the local Prefettura (the provincial government office) [A0092].
2. **Work.** You may work once 90 days have passed since your claim was formalised, if no decision has been made and the delay is not your fault [A0027]. The asylum permit cannot be turned into a work permit, even if you have a job [A0030]. Employers are often wary of hiring someone who holds only an asylum receipt [A0031].

### The decision

The Commission can grant refugee status or subsidiary protection (both with 5-year permits), find that special protection applies (a 2-year permit), or reject the claim [A0086, A0083, A0087]. For Bangladeshis in January-September 2026, refugee status went to 375 people and special protection to 3,255, out of 27,790 decisions [A0046, A0045].

**Special protection (protezione speciale):** a national permit for people who cannot be expelled because they risk persecution, torture or inhuman treatment, or because of Italy's constitutional and international obligations [A0083]. Since Decree-Law 20/2023 (the "Cutro decree", converted into Law 50/2023), it can no longer be requested directly at the Questura, and a person's private and family life in Italy no longer counts as a ground [A0084]. The permit lasts two years and is renewable, but it can no longer be converted into a work permit [A0085]. Renewals can take 16 to 18 months [A0086].

**Victims of labour exploitation:** a separate "special cases" permit exists for victims of labour exploitation (see Work visa: seasonal worker) [A0088].

### If it is refused

The rejection is also a return order and an entry ban; it works as an expulsion order and is challenged in the same appeal [A0087].

1. **Appeal within the deadline.** The deadline runs from the day the decision is notified:
   - ordinary procedure: 30 days (60 if you are abroad) [A0077];
   - accelerated cases under Article 67(7)(a) of the EU Regulation: 7 days [A0021];
   - border procedure: 5 days [A0019].

   The appeal goes to the specialised immigration section of the civil court (tribunale) [A0074]. A lawyer is required, and people without money have the right to a free, state-paid lawyer (patrocinio a spese dello Stato) [A0057, A0060].
2. **Ask to stay while the court decides, where needed.** As a rule an appeal now stops removal, except in the cases set by Article 68(3) of the EU Regulation [A0022]. In the border procedure an appeal does not stop removal, except for unaccompanied children, so you must file a request to stay with the appeal; you cannot be removed while the judge decides it [A0019, A0020]. A second or later repeat application gives no right to stay [A0081].
3. **Wait for the court.** Appeal cases often take far longer than the 4 months the law sets, in some cases ten times longer [A0075]. After the civil court, only a challenge on points of law at the Court of Cassation remains, within 30 days [A0074].

### After a final refusal

After a final refusal the return order and entry ban in the decision stand, with the effect of an expulsion order (see What can get you jailed or deported) [A0087]. On 25 September 2025 an EU-chartered flight returned 29 Bangladeshis, 16 of them from Italy [A0096]. Moving on to another EU country does not help: only one country examines the claim, and the EU rules from June 2026 close that option [A0103, A0098]. For ways home, see Going home without valid papers.

### Scams

- **"Political asylum is easy in Italy."** It is not: in 2025, 26,240 of 29,595 Bangladeshi claims were rejected at first decision [A0038]. The Italian Ambassador said in 2025 that people paid brokers the equivalent of about EUR 15,000 each to reach Italy [A0100].
- **Fake papers and bought stories.** Rights groups warn that most Bangladeshi claims will now be dismissed as manifestly unfounded at the outset [A0102]. Applicants must tell the truth and hand over their documents; giving false information or documents puts a person straight into the compulsory border procedure [A0062, A0013].
- **Paying for what is free.** The interpreter, legal advice at reception centres and many NGOs, and a court lawyer for people without money are free [A0054, A0056, A0057].
