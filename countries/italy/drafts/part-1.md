# Italy: migration guide

How to go to Italy on a Bangladeshi passport, visa by visa, step by step. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO, research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh); EUR is the euro, converted throughout at EUR 1 = about Tk 139.6, the Bangladesh Bank cross rate on 10 November 2026, and USD at USD 1 = Tk 124.42, Bangladesh Bank's rate the same day [G0001, G0077]. The Italian Embassy in Dhaka and VFS Global charge visa fees in cash, in taka, at their own rate of EUR 1 = Tk 140 [M0022]. BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is the Bangladesh government agency that registers job-seekers on its OEP (Overseas Employment Platform) website and gives every worker emigration clearance before departure [B0124, B0132]. The Decreto Flussi ("flows decree") is Italy's quota of foreign workers, set by the Prime Minister: every entry for employed, seasonal or self-employed work must fall within it, except a few routes outside the quota [P0089, P0001, W0074]. A nulla osta (Italian for "no objection") is the authorisation an employer in Italy gets to hire one named foreign worker; it is not permission to enter, and the Embassy decides the visa only after the employer confirms [N0028, W0031, W0040]. The SUI (Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione) is the immigration desk at the Prefettura, the central government's office in each province; it runs the whole hiring procedure and issues the nulla osta [W0030, W0036]. The permesso di soggiorno is the residence permit: a worker asks the Questura, the provincial police headquarters, for it within 8 working days of entering Italy, and the Questura also carries out expulsions [W0060, W0061, P0087]. VFS Global takes every Italian visa application in Bangladesh for the Embassy and handles only the logistics, with no say in decisions [W0099, M0006].

## Background

### In one minute

Italy takes Bangladeshi workers only through an employer in Italy who names them in a nulla osta under the yearly Decreto Flussi quota; about 10,000 Bangladeshis got Italian work visas in 2025, and about as many are expected in 2026 [N0113, P0089, W0091]. A worker can't apply himself and can hardly find an Italian employer from Bangladesh, so a middleman sells the place: workers have paid EUR 7,000 to 20,000 (about Tk 9.8 to 27.9 lakh), against Tk 21,830 in official fees at VFS [N0145, N0115, N0054, M0018, M0019, M0020, G0001]. Bangladesh sets no legal maximum on what an agency may take for Italy [B0049]. So many nulla osta were bought on fake or empty job offers in 2023-24 that Italy froze every unvisaed Bangladeshi nulla osta in October 2024, and the old cases were mostly rejected; new ones go through quickly, but only after the Questura and the labour inspectorate have checked them [M0001, W0094, W0092, D0024, D0025, D0026]. Many who paid and flew found no employer waiting, could not get a permit and fell into irregular status with the debt still owed [N0124, N0086, D0094]. Once you hold a permit for work, losing or leaving the job does not cancel it and you may work for someone else, but the first permit depends on the employer signing your contract within 15 days of your arrival [L0013, L0114, W0041, W0038]. Bangladeshis are also the largest nationality landing by boat from Libya (20,164 in 2025) and the largest group of asylum seekers, and nearly 9 in 10 of their asylum claims are rejected, because Italy and the EU list Bangladesh as a safe country [U0005, A0037, A0039, A0001, A0007].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open as of 10 November 2026, through an employer's nulla osta under the Decreto Flussi [W0091, P0089]. For the 2027 round, employers pre-fill applications until 7 December 2026 (and may finish saved forms until 13 December), and the click day for non-seasonal work is 16 February 2027 (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 3) [P0007, W0007, W0011]. New nulla osta are processed in a relatively short time with very few refusals; the old ones frozen in October 2024 were mostly rejected [W0092, W0093, W0094]. A Bangladeshi worker's nulla osta is never issued by silence: the Questura must approve it, and the INL (Ispettorato Nazionale del Lavoro, the National Labour Inspectorate) first checks the employer [D0024, D0025, D0026].

#### Status

- **11 October 2024:** Decree-Law 145/2024 suspended every nulla osta already issued to Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Sri Lankan workers who had no visa yet, because of the high number of false documents, and made new ones depend on the INL's prior check, with no automatic issue after 60 days [M0001, W0054, W0055].
- **16-20 October 2024:** the Embassy said it would issue a work visa only after the SUI confirmed a suspended nulla osta, and from 20 October returned the passports of everyone waiting; a suspended nulla osta does not expire while it waits [M0002, N0003, M0003].
- **27 May 2025:** over 7,000 suspended cases had ended in a visa or a rejection, and over 70% of entries on the Embassy's work-visa webform carried fake or invalid nulla osta [M0004, M0005].
- **August 2025:** about 8,000 of nearly 40,000 pending cases had been resolved; nulla osta issued after 22 October 2024 were not suspended [N0039, N0040].
- **September 2025:** the SUIs had confirmed 944 nulla osta for Bangladeshis who had not yet applied for a visa, and the Embassy called them to walk-in days at the VFS centres in Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet [M0011, M0010].
- **8 December 2025:** holders of a nulla osta issued or reconfirmed in 2025 register on VFS's portal and get an appointment within a few days; holders of a 2023-24 nulla osta wait until the immigration office in Italy finishes its review (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 5) [M0029, M0031, M0032].

#### The channels

- **Decreto Flussi, non-seasonal work (form B2020):** the route most Bangladeshi workers use: the employer files for a named worker on click day, the SUI issues the nulla osta, and the worker applies at VFS (see Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal)) [W0015, W0030, W0099]. Italy admits 76,200 non-seasonal workers in 2027, 14,000 of them domestic and care workers [P0002]. Bangladesh shares a reserved quota of 25,000 non-seasonal places a year in 2026-2028 with the other countries that have cooperation agreements with Italy, and such reserved quotas go to countries that cooperate on readmitting their returned nationals [W0023, W0024, P0006, P0090]. The quota usually fills in the first ten minutes of click day [N0150].
- **Seasonal farm and tourism work:** 89,000 places in 2027, for at most nine months in twelve; many in Bangladesh call it the কৃষি ভিসা (krishi visa, agriculture visa) (see Work visa: seasonal worker) [P0003, D0035, N0149].
- **Domestic and care work:** the 14,000 places for jobs with a household, plus up to 10,000 out-of-quota carers a year (see Work visa: domestic and care worker) [P0002, D0012, D0058].
- **Article 23 of the immigration law: training first, outside the quota:** a worker who completes a pre-departure course certified by Italy's Ministry of Labour (basic Italian, civic education, job skills) enters outside the quota; applications run all year with no click day, and the course can be done in Bangladesh [N0029, N0030, N0031]. The first projects in Bangladesh were set up by Italian private firms: Frontiere Skills, whose TASTE project trains restaurant and hotel workers at Hotel Regency in Dhaka, and Format, which trains textile workers; about 300 students were enrolled in November 2026 [N0024, N0025, N0036]. Format says its training is free [N0032]. Several hundred Bangladeshis a year now go through such training [W0096]. Separately, the EU-funded Talent Partnership, run by BMET with the ILO (International Labour Organization), had certified 698 trainees by July 2026, mainly for Italy, and many were waiting for employer interviews [B0040, B0041, B0042].
- **Highly skilled (EU Blue Card, Article 27, nulla osta code "L/N"):** outside the quota and only for highly qualified jobs; very few Bangladeshi applicants have succeeded (see Work visa: highly skilled and out-of-quota (EU Blue Card, Article 27)) [M0013, M0014].
- **Self-employment:** 650 places a year for all countries together (see Self-employment visa) [P0004].
- **Family reunification:** about 12,000 to 13,000 family visas a year, and family members may work once they arrive (see Family reunification) [F0086, F0087].
- **Not a work route:** a Schengen visit visa (the short-stay visa for Italy and the other Schengen countries), which allows no work and which the Italian consulate in Dhaka refused to 60% of applicants in 2025 (see Visit visa (Schengen)) [V0044, V0053]; and the boat from Libya (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers) [U0005].

#### Who qualifies

Anyone an employer in Italy names: the employer picks the worker at his discretion, and there is no public list of workers and no list of agencies an employer must use [N0113, N0120]. The Article 23 route needs the certified course first, and the Blue Card a highly qualified job [N0029, W0119]. Few women go: 76.7% of Bangladeshis in Italy are men, and BMET cleared 261 women for Italy from 1 January to 9 November 2026 [N0066, B0009]. Italian is the only language at work [N0129].

#### Size

- **Workers cleared by BMET:** 16,846 in 2023, 1,162 in 2024, 9,365 in 2025 and 8,614 from 1 January to 9 November 2026, when Italy was BMET's 8th-largest destination [B0002, B0003, B0004, B0005, B0026].
- **Month by month:** 4,043 in July 2023, 59 in December 2023, 11 in September 2024, 1,644 in August 2025 and 690 in October 2026 [B0010, B0011, B0013, B0014, B0016].
- **Job titles on OEP:** the largest 2026 titles are the vague "A Casual Worker" (2,842) and "General Labour" (2,427) [B0022, B0023].
- **Work visas:** about 10,000 in 2025, and about as many expected in 2026 [W0091].
- **Applications against visas:** Bangladeshis filed almost 58% of all Decreto Flussi applications in 2023 (352,562) and 45.8% in 2024 (328,877), far ahead of Morocco (15.8%) and India (14.2%) [N0046, N0044]. By 12 December 2024, of 24,151 visas issued for the 2024 quota, 35 had gone to Bangladeshis and 1,866 to Sri Lankans; for the 2023 quota, Bangladeshis got 3,409 [N0049, N0048].
- **Living in Italy:** 195,523 Bangladeshis held permits at the end of 2024, the fifth-largest non-EU community; Rome has 37,905 of them (19.4%), followed by Lombardy, Veneto, Campania and Sicily [N0155, N0156, N0065]. Of the 28,045 new permits Bangladeshis got in 2024, two-thirds came from asylum and protection claims and only just over one in ten from work [N0063, N0157, N0064].
- **By boat:** 20,164 Bangladeshis landed by sea in 2025 and 6,160 from 1 January to 6 November 2026, more than any other nationality [U0005, U0001].

#### What closed it before

- **2012-13:** Italy took away Bangladesh's quota after Bangladeshi seasonal farm workers did not go home after the season, and formal labour migration stopped in 2013 [N0140, B0032].
- **2020-21:** Italy put Bangladesh back on the Decreto Flussi list on 12 October 2020, and workers began going again in 2021, 653 that year [N0141, B0033, N0125].
- **4 June 2024:** Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni took a complaint to the national anti-mafia prosecutor, Giovanni Melillo, saying most Decreto Flussi entrants came from Bangladesh, where diplomats spoke of jobs being bought and sold, and that in Campania, with 157,000 applications, under 3% of those given a seasonal visa signed a contract [N0100, N0099, N0098].
- **October 2024:** the freeze (see Status) suspended 34,959 of 38,039 applications in Dhaka (92%), and BMET cleared only 1,162 workers for Italy that year [N0050, B0003]. Visa processing had already taken over a year in some cases, and applicants had formed a human chain at the Embassy in April 2024 and protested in Dhaka's Kakrail area, demanding visas or their passports back [B0034, N0152, N0130].
- **6 May 2025:** Bangladesh and Italy signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on Migration and Mobility in Dhaka, covering legal migration and the return of people with no right to stay [B0027, B0030].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A legal job reaches a Bangladeshi worker through a person, not a list: an employer in Italy files for a worker he names, and the name usually comes through a Bangladeshi already in Italy or a broker [N0113, N0142, N0153]. The choke point is the nulla osta: only the employer can request it, and a worker can't apply himself and can hardly find an employer from Bangladesh, so a middleman sells the place [N0145, W0031]. The Bangladesh Embassy's labour secretary in Rome has said a group exploits a process that needs "a third party as personal reference" [N0139].

#### The quota and the click day

The government sets the quota; employers pre-fill their applications online, then race to send them on click day, which are handled mostly in order of arrival [P0036, W0142]. Applications ran at about five times the places: 609,000 for 132,000 in 2023 and 718,000 for 147,000 in 2024 [N0160]. Few end in a job: of 127,707 places given out for 2023, only 16,188 (about 13%) led to a signed contract and a permit request, and for 2024 only 7.8% [N0052, N0053]. Since October 2025 a private employer may file at most three requests a year; more must go through an employer organisation or labour consultant, who must keep the number in line with the firm's turnover and staff (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 2) [P0038, P0039, W0034].

#### Who sells the nulla osta

- **Firms filing for jobs that don't exist:** in Campania a ring filed about 2,000 fictitious requests, and each migrant paid EUR 7,000 to 8,000 (about Tk 9.8 to 11.2 lakh) [N0103, N0104, G0001]. In Grosseto in September 2025 a Bangladeshi was arrested as the alleged head of a network that brought in nearly 200 Bangladeshis for fake employers, many of whom did not know their names were used, at EUR 2,000 to 4,000 (Tk 2.8 to 5.6 lakh) an application [D0098, D0100, D0099, G0001]. In June 2026, 30 people were arrested over false applications filed through a CAF (tax assistance centre) in Taranto, mostly for Pakistanis, Bangladeshis and Indians, who paid up to EUR 6,500 (about Tk 9.1 lakh) [D0129, D0127, D0101, D0102, G0001]. In a Lecce-Taranto investigation, businesses filed "simulated" hiring requests only to obtain nulla osta, and one intercepted call called it "business di bangladesi" [N0109, N0110].
- **Consultants and patronati:** in the Campania case a lawyer filed click-day applications through his staff for at least EUR 5,000 (about Tk 7 lakh) a file, and staff of patronati (union-linked welfare advice offices) filed requests for a fee [N0106, N0107, G0001].
- **Officials:** officials of the Prefettura and the Labour Inspectorate in Naples and Salerno allegedly took EUR 500 to 800 (about Tk 70,000 to 1.12 lakh) a file [N0105, G0001].
- **Bangladeshi brokers:** a broker in Bangladesh with "a relative in Italy who handles legal entries", or a Bangladeshi with an agency in Italy who says he has firms ready to hire; workers call them দালাল (dalal) [N0059, N0054, N0089].

#### The embassy queue

The queue at the Italian Embassy in Dhaka was itself sold [N0072, N0078]. On 19 February 2025 two Italian Foreign Ministry employees who had worked on visas, Nicola Muscatello and Roberto Albergo, were put under house arrest for corruption, and two Rome-based Bangladeshis, Nazrul Islam and Hossain Md Baleyet Patwari, were jailed, in an investigation by the Guardia di Finanza (Italy's financial police) [N0072, N0073]. Prosecutors say the two Bangladeshis charged compatriots a fixed sum plus a share of their future earnings in Italy, and that the officials took hotel stays, phones, watches and flights [N0074, N0076]. The ring allegedly charged EUR 15,000 (about Tk 20.9 lakh) for a work visa and EUR 7,000 for a tourist visa, and Nazrul Islam, linked to the Awami League's Italian branch, had brought nearly 1,500 Bangladeshis the year before [N0078, N0079, N0080, G0001]. Investigators also say embassy staff paid real Italian firms to join the scheme [N0084].

#### The broker and the village

Many irregular migrants come from Shariatpur and Madaripur, the Italian Ambassador says, and the same districts recur on the legal route: a Madaripur man paid a broker EUR 16,000 (about Tk 22.3 lakh) for promised contract work [N0016, N0082, G0001]. The broker is often a relative: a Narsingdi man paid EUR 16,000 for a job a cousin in Naples arranged, and no employer ever contacted him [N0086]. A Naples farm owner applied for workers through a Bangladeshi already working on his farm [N0153].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The official costs are small: Tk 21,830 at VFS, a EUR 16 revenue stamp, the flight and BMET's fees [M0018, M0019, M0020, N0138]. Workers pay brokers EUR 7,000 to 20,000 (about Tk 9.8 to 27.9 lakh), and many arrive to no job [N0115, N0054, N0124, G0001].

#### The cap

Bangladesh sets no legal maximum for Italy: the Ministry's 2017 migration-cost caps cover 14 countries, and Italy is not one of them [B0049]. Italy sets no migration cost for the worker either [N0164]. The official costs are:

- visa fee: EUR 116 (Tk 16,240) [M0018];
- VFS service charge: EUR 38 (Tk 5,320), VAT included [M0019];
- bank draft charge: Tk 270 [M0020];
- revenue stamp: EUR 16 (about Tk 2,200) [N0138, G0001];
- the flight to Italy, paid by the worker [N0138];
- BMET's fees: a Tk 5,500 welfare fee, Tk 1,000 insurance and a Tk 250 clearance fee (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 6) [B0140, B0131, B0133].

The contract of stay commits the employer to pay your journey home [W0067]. Workers told a newspaper in 2023 that the whole cost should be no more than Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh [N0137].

#### What people really pay

- **USD 10,000 to 15,000 (about Tk 12.4 to 18.7 lakh):** what brokers charge, the Embassy's November 2026 seminar heard [N0034, G0077].
- **EUR 15,000 (about Tk 20.9 lakh):** what Prime Minister Meloni said migrants paid for click days, and the work-visa price of the 2025 embassy bribery ring [N0101, N0078, G0001].
- **EUR 7,000 up front and as much again on the visa (about Tk 19.5 lakh in all):** a Gazipur man in 2025 [N0115, D0090, G0001].
- **EUR 16,000 (about Tk 22.3 lakh):** two men from Madaripur and Narsingdi [N0082, N0086, G0001].
- **EUR 18,000 and EUR 20,000 (about Tk 25.1 and 27.9 lakh):** two workers who spoke to Ero Straniero, a campaign of Italian NGOs [N0057, N0054, G0001].
- **Tk 6 to 10 lakh:** the middleman's take, with workers reporting up to Tk 15 lakh in all [N0144, N0137].

#### How it is paid

In instalments: a first payment starts the paperwork and the rest is due before or when the visa comes; one worker paid EUR 8,000 (about Tk 11.2 lakh) up front and the rest before the visa [N0058, N0115, G0001]. The passport is held as security: his broker kept it until the last instalment [N0059]. The 2025 bribery ring took part of its fee as a share of future wages in Italy [N0074]. The Embassy tells anyone using an agency to take a receipt for every payment and to know every document filed in their name, and says neither it nor VFS will ever hold a passport against the applicant's will [M0008, M0007].

#### How they borrow

Families sell land, pool savings and take bank loans: the Madaripur man took loans from two banks, the Narsingdi man raised EUR 16,000 from relatives, loans and the sale of family land, and in the Grosseto case some families mortgaged land [N0083, N0086, D0128]. Loans are easier to get when the money is said to be for Italy [U0088]. One worker said his bank debt grew daily with interest [N0061]. The PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank, the government bank for migrants) lends at most Tk 3 lakh (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 1) [B0160].

#### What the job pays

32% of employed Bangladeshis in Italy work in hotels and restaurants and 25.3% in industry [N0158]. Pay is set by the CCNL (contratto collettivo nazionale di lavoro, the national collective contract) for the sector, and is quoted before tax and contributions [L0023, L0034]. The 2026 minimums include:

- **Farm work:** EUR 1,042.43 a month (about Tk 1.46 lakh), the national floor for a common worker [L0032, G0001].
- **Construction:** EUR 9.48 an hour (about Tk 1,320) for a common labourer, plus provincial extras [L0035, G0001].
- **Hotels:** EUR 1,412.60 a month (about Tk 1.97 lakh) at the lowest level, paid 14 times a year [L0040, L0042, G0001].
- **Cleaning:** EUR 1,413.50 a month (about Tk 1.97 lakh) full-time [L0046, G0001].

Full tables are under Your rights at work. Wages must be paid through a bank or post office, never in cash, and an employer who pays cash is fined EUR 1,000 to 5,000 [G0057, G0058]. Income tax and the end of tax deductions for family in Bangladesh: see Sending money home.

#### What goes unpaid

- **Pay far below the contract:** one worker taken from the airport by his broker worked 16 to 17 hours a day for EUR 400 to 500 a month (about Tk 56,000 to 70,000), and paid EUR 140 (about Tk 19,500) of it back to the middleman as rent [N0060, G0001].
- **Payslips that lie:** among Fincantieri shipyard subcontractors, whose workers included Bangladeshis, payslips carried items never paid, and 383 workers took under EUR 7 (about Tk 980) an hour because they needed the job to renew their permit (see Your rights at work) [L0049, L0050, L0051, G0001].
- **Gangmaster pay on farms:** near Naples in 2025 prosecutors found farm workers paid about EUR 2.70 an hour for 10 to 14-hour days (see Caporalato: illegal gangmasters) [D0106].

How to claim: see If you aren't paid.

#### Money sent home

Italy is one of Bangladesh's largest sources of remittances: USD 2,051.5 million (about Tk 25,500 crore) in July 2025 to June 2026, up from USD 810.9 million five years earlier, and USD 602.24 million in July to September 2026, sixth among all countries [G0007, G0009, G0005, G0077]. Bangladesh received 19.4% of all money-transfer flows leaving Italy in the year to June 2026, more than any other country, and the province of Rome alone sent EUR 364.9 million (about Tk 5,100 crore) in 2025 [G0014, G0019, G0001]. Sending from Italy cost 1.27% of the amount on average in April to June 2026 (how to send: see Sending money home) [G0011]. Only 77% of Bangladeshi adults in Italy have a bank account [N0070]. Hundi (money passed outside the banks through private agents) is illegal in Bangladesh, and by June 2026 the BFIU (Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit) had frozen or suspended about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling [G0045, G0044]. Workers without papers have no formal channel, which keeps hundi alive [G0046].

#### How long the debt takes

A seasonal worker who paid Tk 6 lakh can't save it back in one nine-month season [N0148]. A EUR 15,000 deal equals more than 14 months of the farm minimum before tax [N0101, L0032].

### What ties you to the employer

The nulla osta ties you to one employer until you arrive and sign your contract; after that the permit for work is looser: losing or leaving the job does not cancel it, and it lets you do other legal work [W0031, W0041, L0013, L0114].

- **To enter:** only the employer, or its employer organisation or labour consultant, can file the nulla osta naming you, and after the Embassy's checks it must confirm within 15 days or the nulla osta is revoked (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 3; Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 5) [N0113, W0034, P0024, P0025].
- **To get the first permit:** the employer must sign the contratto di soggiorno (contract of stay) with you and send it online to the SUI within 15 days of your entry, or the nulla osta is revoked unless the delay was not your fault; until then you work only on the nulla osta (Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 7) [P0027, P0028, W0038, W0043]. In practice workers have waited up to a year for a Prefettura appointment while employers disappeared, and one worker could work only for the employer that had filed for him, with no permit or health care, because it was behind on its social security payments [D0095, N0055, N0056]. In August 2026 Bangladesh told Italy that several hundred workers who entered in 2022-23 could not change employer or extend their permits because their sponsors could not be reached [B0038].
- **To renew:** the permit lasts as long as the contract, up to 1 year for a fixed-term contract and 2 years for a permanent one, and each renewal up to 3 years; you ask the Questura of the province where you live at least 90 days before it expires (see Your residence permit) [W0057, L0109]. Workers have accepted under EUR 7 an hour because they needed the job to renew [L0051].
- **To quit:** resign only online on the Ministry of Labour form, since a paper letter has no effect; resigning does not cancel the permit, but it loses NASpI (unemployment pay) unless you resign for just cause (see Changing employer or leaving) [L0095, L0015, L0070, L0071].
- **To change employer:** a permit for employment can be used for other legal work [L0114]. Blue Card holders and out-of-quota carers need the labour office's approval to change employer in their first 12 months, and seasonal workers change through SIISL, the government job-matching platform (see Changing employer) [W0083, D0064, D0037].
- **To leave:** the contract of stay commits the employer to pay your journey home, and a seasonal worker must go home when the nine months end (see Leaving at the end of the job) [W0067, D0039].
- **Which contract covers you:** a foreign worker with a valid permit has the same rights at work as an Italian, and pay and hours follow the CCNL for the sector; bars and restaurants have their own contract (FIPE), separate from hotels, and domestic workers theirs (see Your rights at work) [L0019, L0023, L0043, D0074].
- **If you lose the job:** register as available for work and you may stay for the rest of the permit and at least one year (not on a seasonal permit); after that a new permit depends on your income (see If you lose your job) [L0013, L0016].
- **If the permit expires:** while a renewal is pending you may stay and work with the post-office receipt, but if it expired over 60 days ago with no renewal requested, the Prefect (the government's representative in the province) orders expulsion, and staying without a permit is fined EUR 5,000 to 10,000 (about Tk 7 to 14 lakh) [P0040, P0041, L0099, L0098, G0001]. People who lost a regular job, and with it their permit, have been detained and sent to Italy's detention centre in Albania (see What can get you jailed or deported) [U0110].

> **Need to know**
> - When the Decreto Flussi employer never signs the contratto di soggiorno, does the Questura now give the worker a job-seeking permit or accept another employer?
> - Does the Questura accept a different employer at the first renewal?

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The commonest failure is paying for a nulla osta and arriving to no job, no contract and no permit [N0114, N0124, N0056]. A Naples migrant network counts more than 1,200 people caught in one Italy-Bangladesh visa-fraud scheme over four years, and its help desk sees at least 100 Bangladeshis with the same problem every Thursday [N0081, N0085]. Bangladeshis who came on the Decreto Flussi and found no employer, or a scam, have formed a committee in Rome, Tikase [D0094]. Ital-Bangla, a Bangladeshi migrants' association, estimates that about 80% of those who entered on work visas in 2020-2023 became undocumented [B0035]. Before departure the loss is money paid for a nulla osta that is fake or rejected (see Status) [M0005, W0094].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A nulla osta means I can go."** It only authorises the employer to hire you: the Embassy decides the visa, and only after the employer confirms [N0028, W0040, P0025]. Many 2023-24 nulla osta were frozen and then rejected (see Status) [W0094].
- **"With the visa, a job is waiting."** Many found no employer: one man fell into irregular status because the company that was to hire him did not exist [N0124]. Only about 13% of the 2023 quota and 7.8% of the 2024 quota ended in a contract and a permit request [N0052, N0053].
- **"I came legally, so Italy will treat me as a victim."** A Naples social worker says the state treats such workers as fraudsters, and many try special protection instead, a two-year permit that can no longer be turned into a work permit (see Asylum) [N0087, N0088, A0085].
- **"Someone at the Embassy can speed it up."** Officials and middlemen were arrested over exactly this offer in 2025 (see The embassy queue), and the Embassy says paying intermediaries risks cancellation of the visa and legal action [N0072, N0073, K0012].
- **"An L/N 'carta blu' nulla osta is a sure visa."** Brokers sell it, but it is only for highly qualified jobs, holding one does not guarantee a visa, and very few Bangladeshi applicants have got one [M0015, M0013, M0014].
- **"Parents can no longer be brought to Italy."** A Bangla news site reported this in November 2025, but the law in force and the Embassy's July 2026 checklist still allow a dependent parent with no other children in Bangladesh, or a parent over 65 whose other children can't support them for documented serious health reasons (see Family reunification) [F0090, F0004, F0079].
- **"Political asylum is easy in Italy."** Italy and the EU list Bangladesh as a safe country of origin, and Italy rejected 88.66% of Bangladeshi claims at first decision in 2025 (see Asylum) [A0001, A0007, A0039].
- **"Go on a tourist visa and get papers there."** A Schengen visa doesn't allow work, study or applying to stay, and Bangladesh's Ministry warns never to go on a visit visa on a broker's promise (see Changing visa type) [V0044, B0127].
- **"Italy will regularise us again."** Past regularisations are a main reason people go, but the last one, in 2020, was only for domestic and care work and for farming, livestock and fishing [U0086, U0121, U0008, U0009].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **By boat from Libya: it ends at a hotspot, often after ransom, and usually in a refused asylum claim.** People fly Dhaka to Dubai and on to Benghazi, are driven across Libya to Zuwara or Zawiya, and cross to Lampedusa (the Italian island closest to Libya) in 24 to 36 hours [U0066, U0069, U0070, U0071]. The standard price is USD 10,000 (about Tk 12.4 lakh), USD 3,000 before leaving and USD 7,000 in Libya, and extra demands in Libya often raise it to USD 15,000 to 17,000 (about Tk 18.7 to 21.2 lakh) [U0072, U0074, G0077]. Families have paid a broker Tk 18 to 20 lakh and then been asked Tk 25 lakh each in ransom [U0095, U0096, U0094]. Many start with a promise of legal migration from the same brokers [U0064]. On landing, people are taken to a hotspot (a first-reception centre) for fingerprints, and most Bangladeshi asylum claims are rejected (see Irregular routes and undocumented workers; Asylum) [U0049, A0039].

  > **Need to know**
  > - What does the Libya route to Italy cost in taka in late 2026, in Madaripur, Shariatpur, Sylhet or Cumilla, and what do brokers call the two instalments?

- **Fake Schengen visas: it ends at Rome airport.** In May 2026 two men who had agreed Tk 30 lakh each for fake Schengen visas, paying Tk 20 lakh up front, were caught at Rome airport and sent back, and the Biman ground officer who helped them board was arrested [N0090, N0092, N0091]. Rings also paste a genuine Italian visa page into a look-alike's passport, or send another woman to Italy on a woman's family visa [V0139, N0093, N0096].
- **Through another EU country:** agencies sell Romanian work visas as a way in; one man paid EUR 11,000 (about Tk 15.4 lakh), was offered undeclared building work and moved on to Italy without papers [N0122, G0001].
- **Staying without a permit: it ends in a CPR, expulsion and a ban across Europe.** If police can't remove someone at once, the Questore (head of the Questura) can hold them in a CPR (Centro di Permanenza per i Rimpatri, a detention centre before removal) for three months, which a judge can extend [L0106, L0107, L0108]. An expulsion brings a 3 to 5-year ban recorded in the Schengen Information System, which bars every EU and Schengen country [L0103, L0104]. Italy returned over 120 Bangladeshis in 2025, and the Embassy warns that entering through dalals leads to immediate repatriation [M0034, M0035]. Workers who entered legally have ended there too: a Bangladeshi who came in 2023 was refused a permit by the Latina Questura, ordered expelled and held at Rome's Ponte Galeria CPR [D0097].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Visa-free entry:** Bangladeshis need a visa to enter Italy, and an airport transit visa even to change planes in an Italian airport [P0084, V0052].
- **Airport transit without a visa:** closed unless you already hold a valid Schengen or national visa or a residence permit, or fit another listed exemption [V0052].
- **Working holiday visa (vacanze-lavoro):** only for citizens of New Zealand, Canada, Australia, Hong Kong, South Korea and Japan, the countries with an agreement with Italy [V0133].
- **Turning a tourist visa into a stay or a job:** a tourist visa does not allow work, study or applying to stay in Italy (see Changing visa type) [V0044].
