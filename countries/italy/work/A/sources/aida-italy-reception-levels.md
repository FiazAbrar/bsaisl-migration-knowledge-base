# Forms and levels of material reception conditions

## Italy

Country Report: **Forms and levels of material reception conditions** Last updated: 14/09/26

### Author

ASGI [Visit Website](http://www.asgi.it)

![](https://asylumineurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/asgi.png)

**Amounts granted to centres’ management to provide reception conditions and services**

According to the law, the scope of material reception conditions and services offered to asylum applicants shall be defined by decree of the Ministry of Interior to guarantee uniform levels of reception across the territory, taking into account the peculiarities of each type of reception centre.[^(\[1\])](#_ftn1) The latest decree approving the tender specifications schemes (*capitolato d’appalto*) was adopted on 27 March 2024.[^(\[2\])](#_ftn2)

Under the tender specification schemes issued following L. 50/2023, in 2023, the daily amount per person awarded to the centres’ management was increased compared to the 2021 tender scheme: € 37 for non-collective structures up to 50 places (€ 28 previously). However, the items corresponding to the necessary costs have remained almost unchanged (slightly lower amount for staff and slightly higher for management of the structure). Higher costs are instead foreseen for additional and possible services including on-call intervention of a night operator.[^(\[3\])](#_ftn3) This amount does not appear sufficient to favour small facilities. For collective structures, fees are higher (40 euros for collective structures up to 50 places compared to 37 for non-collective) and this confirms the limited interest of the authorities in adopting policies that favour reception in small structures throughout the territory on the model of the SAI system, which avoids ghettoization and favours integration.

The new tender specification schemes guarantee basic needs such as personal hygiene, pocket money, a € 5 phone card but, in line with the changes introduced by Law 50/2023, considers the following services as purely optional and as mere subcategories of social assistance: Italian language courses; orientation to local services; psychological support, legal information service, professional training, leisure activities and job orientation. Some of these services, (job orientation and professional training) are discretionary in CAS, and completely absent for the (few) asylum applicants within the SAI system, that only benefit from first level services.

The new tender schemes distinguish the supply of staff depending on the number of guests and depending on the nature of the centre, i.e. depending on whether they are individual housing units or collective centres.

The table below compares the schemes provided, for example, for reception facilities made of individual housing units and collective centres, hosting from 41 up to 50 guests: in collective centres the demand for personnel and therefore the services requested are lower than the ones in non-collective centres. As a result, linguistic mediation, which is already low in non-collective centres – for centres of 50 guests, it is 28 minutes per person per week – in collective centres it becomes even lower, corresponding to 16 minutes per person. The hours of social workers, 36 per week in non-collective centres and just 14 in collective centres, make clear how the services provided will be scarce and insufficient considering that they social worker hours are to include Italian courses, legal assistance, professional orientation and psychological assistance.

In relation to financial allowances i.e. pocket money for personal needs, each asylum applicant hosted in governmental reception centres and CAS receives €2.50 per day.

Italian law does not provide any financial allowance for asylum applicants who are not within the national reception system.
