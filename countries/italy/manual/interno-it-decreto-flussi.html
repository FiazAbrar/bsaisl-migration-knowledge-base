Entry mode

Entering Italy and staying there legally. Here's how.

Foreign citizens may enter Italy if they can document the reason and conditions of their stay, as well as the availability of means to support themselves during their stay and to return to their country of origin, except in cases of entry for work. Anyone who does not meet these requirements, or is considered a threat to national security or the security of one of the countries with which Italy has signed agreements for the free movement of persons across internal borders, is not admitted to Italy. The applicable legislation on immigration and the status of foreigners is the Consolidated Law on Immigration.

Regular residence: required documents

To legally enter Italy, you need a passport or other travel document and an entry visa (for visits and/or tourism, work, study and/or research, family, etc.), which must be requested from the Italian embassy or consulate in the non-EU citizen's country of origin or permanent residence. Entry into Italy is permitted with short-term visas, valid for up to three months, and long-term visas, which require the granting of a long-term residence permit with the same reason as the visa. For stays of less than three months, visas issued by diplomatic authorities of other countries with which Italy has ratified agreements, or pursuant to EU regulations, are considered valid.

Irregular presence and expulsions

Failure to comply with these procedures, or staying longer than three months or the shorter period indicated on the visa, places the foreigner in the status of an irregular immigrant and will result in expulsion, except in cases of force majeure provided by law. Expelled foreign citizens may not re-enter Italy unless they have special authorization or the entry ban has expired. Foreigners reported for serious reasons of public order and national security, or the protection of international relations, are not admitted to Italy. In practice, the following are considered irregular:

  • the non-EU citizen who enters Italy without documents (passport or identification document and visa);
  • the non-EU citizen who, having entered Italy legally, has lost the necessary requirements for residence.

Foreigners who enter Italy illegally are turned away at the border or, if they have already entered the country, expelled, unless they must be held in an immigration center to verify their identity and/or nationality. The expulsion order is issued by the competent prefecture and executed by the police headquarters.

Entry for work reasons

Entry into Italy for employment, including seasonal work, and self-employment must comply with the entry quotas (Article 21 of the Consolidated Law) established in periodic (usually annual) decrees, the so-called "flow decrees," issued by the Prime Minister based on the criteria set out in the three-year planning document on immigration policies (Article 3). The flow decrees typically provide for a quota reserve for citizens from countries with which the government has concluded agreements to regulate entry flows and readmission procedures.

Regulatory references
Consolidated law on immigration, Legislative Decree no. 286/1998
