## 1 Background

<a id="in-one-minute"></a>

### 1.1 In one minute

Bangladeshis go to Canada as students and visitors, and some on work permits tied to a job offer <sup>[N4](#n4), [N2](#n2), [N5](#n5)</sup>. Since 2024 Canada has been cutting back: it capped international students, tied the post-study work permit to listed subjects and a language score, and planned to roughly halve new students in 2026 <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6), [N1 (2024)](#n1), [N7](#n7)</sup>. Many people got Canadian visit visas after COVID-19 with fake documents and then crossed into the United States or claimed asylum, so Canada now refuses far more visas, stops suspect travellers before they board, and from March 2026 made asylum much harder to get <sup>[N2](#n2), [N8](#n8)</sup>. A false document or false statement, even one an agent put in the application, can bring a ban of at least five years <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In October 2026 CBSA charged a ring that filed 243 visit-visa applications for 209 Bangladeshis with fake hosts and forged papers, and a Saskatchewan court jailed two Bangladeshi-origin restaurant owners for trafficking a Bangladeshi woman promised a job and permanent residence <sup>[N3](#n3), [N5](#n5)</sup>. When Canada sought powers in 2025 to cancel visas en masse, its internal documents named India and Bangladesh as "specific challenging countries", and 10 of the 45 Bangladeshis given visitor visas for the 2026 FIFA World Cup claimed asylum <sup>[N10](#n10), [N11](#n11)</sup>. Students arrive to a weak job market: in early 2026 unemployment among 15- to 24-year-olds was 14.1% <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Fake Canadian job offers sold in Bangladesh are covered in the Dalal scams doc (see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc).

<a id="is-it-open-now-and-who-can-go"></a>

### 1.2 Is it open now, and who can go?

Open for students, visitors and workers with a genuine job offer, but with fewer places and far stricter checks than before 2024 <sup>[N7](#n7), [N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="status"></a>

#### 1.2.1 Status

- **22 January 2024:** Canada announced a two-year cap on international students: at most 360,000 a year, about 35% fewer than in 2023, because of a housing crisis (see [Student visa](#student-visa)) <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.
- **March 2024:** Canada began re-checking visas for fraud, after which more than 2,000 Indian and Bangladeshi nationals were stopped from boarding flights to Canada, according to a Global Affairs Canada document <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **June 2024:** with enhanced visa checks, the refusal rate for new Canadian visa applications reached 63%, according to the same document <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **1 November 2024:** new rules for the post-graduation work permit took effect (see [Step 4: Apply for the post-graduation work permit](#step-4-apply-for-the-post-graduation-work-permit)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **4 November 2025:** the 2025 federal budget cut the 2026 target for new temporary residents to 385,000, about 43% below 2025 <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
- **Early 2026:** 178 programs were removed from the post-study work permit list and 119 fields added <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
- **November 2025:** Bill C-12, before Parliament, included a power to cancel visas en masse; internal documents named India and Bangladesh (see [Visa cancellation powers](#visa-cancellation-powers)) <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **January-February 2026:** Canada lost about 109,000 jobs, 64,000 of them held by young people (see [Can you work?](#can-you-work)) <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>.
- **26 March 2026:** Bill C-12, the Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act, took effect and made asylum much harder to get (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.
- **19 June 2026:** new asylum laws were proposed, which many expect to tighten the system further <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.
- **June-July 2026, FIFA World Cup:** 10 of the 45 Bangladeshis given World Cup visitor visas claimed asylum after arriving (see [World Cup visitors who claimed asylum](#world-cup-visitors-who-claimed-asylum)) <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.
- **1 September 2026:** for study permit applications from this date, a single applicant outside Quebec must show at least CAD 23,448 for living costs on top of tuition (see [Step 2: Apply for the study permit](#step-2-apply-for-the-study-permit)) <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. Parts of Quebec's secularism law took effect the same day, banning prayer rooms at public universities and colleges (see [Step 1: Choose the institution and program](#step-1-choose-the-institution-and-program)) <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **22 October 2026:** CBSA announced charges against five people over a Bangladeshi visit-visa fraud ring (see [Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case](#fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case)) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the student cap still in force for 2027, and what is the number for 2027?
> - Were the asylum laws proposed on 19 June 2026 passed, and what exactly do they change?

<a id="the-channels"></a>

#### 1.2.2 The channels

- **Student visa (study permit), then the post-graduation work permit:** the route most Bangladeshis use (see [Student visa](#student-visa)) <sup>[N4](#n4), [N13](#n13)</sup>.
- **Visit visa (temporary resident visa, TRV):** for visits; now refused far more often (see [Visit visa](#visit-visa)) <sup>[N3](#n3), [N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **Work permit with a job offer:** through the Temporary Foreign Worker Program, with an LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment, the employer's proof that no Canadian was found for the job) (see [Work permit](#work-permit)) <sup>[N7](#n7), [N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **Permanent residence:** later, for some temporary workers and graduates, not on arrival (see [Permanent residence](#permanent-residence)) <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
- **Not a legal route:** going on to the United States across the land border (see [Irregular routes and undocumented stay](#irregular-routes-and-undocumented-stay)) <sup>[N4](#n4), [N2](#n2)</sup>.

<a id="size"></a>

#### 1.2.3 Size

- **Bangladeshi students:** 11,948 went in the period covered by Canada's figures published in March-April 2024, and 7.5% of them never contacted their university or college after arriving <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **Post-graduation work permits:** more than 105,000 were issued in the first half of 2024, 64% of them to college program graduates, according to the Toronto Star <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Stopped before boarding:** in 2024 CBSA recommended that about 11,050 passengers with suspicious travel documents not be boarded, 493 of them Bangladeshis (475 suspected of fraud) <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.
- **World Cup visitor visas:** 45 issued from Bangladesh for the 2026 tournament, out of 26,111 FIFA-related permits or visas in all <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.
- **Canada's 2026 targets:** 155,000 new international students and 230,000 temporary foreign workers, and 380,000 new permanent residents <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many study permits, visit visas and work permits did IRCC issue to Bangladeshis in 2024, 2025 and 2026?
> - Does BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) clear any Bangladeshi workers for Canada, and how many in 2025?

<a id="how-a-job-reaches-you-and-who-takes-a-cut"></a>

### 1.3 How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

In this reporting a Bangladeshi reaches Canada through a college or university admission, a visit visa backed by a Canadian host, or a job offer through the temporary foreign worker process, and the cut is taken by people who fake one of those papers <sup>[N4](#n4), [N3](#n3), [N5](#n5)</sup>. The 2026 CBSA case shows the visit-visa version: men in Montreal and Toronto and a partner in Bangladesh are accused of building applications for 209 Bangladeshis with fake Canadian hosts and forged documents (see [Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case](#fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case)) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. The Saskatchewan case shows the job version: two Bangladeshi-origin restaurant owners promised a woman a restaurant job and permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process, then worked her without a valid permit (see [The Saskatchewan restaurant case](#the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case)) <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. On the student side, an immigration consultant can be the weak point: one Bangladeshi's first asylum claim was rejected because a fraudulent consultant filed it <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What do Bangladeshi brokers charge in 2026 for a Canadian visit visa file with a host's invitation letter, and for an LMIA job offer?

<a id="what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep"></a>

### 1.4 What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **Fake papers cost more than money:** a false document in any application can mean refusal, a ban of at least five years and a permanent fraud record (see [Lying on an application](#lying-on-an-application)) <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Unpaid wages:** in the Saskatchewan case the court ordered the two owners to pay the woman CAD 5,885.16 and CAD 12,268 as unpaid wages and restitution <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **Tuition:** international undergraduates paid on average about CAD 41,746 a year in 2025-26, about CAD 49,800 in Ontario and about CAD 18,900 in Newfoundland and Labrador, according to Statistics Canada; engineering and computer science can cost well above the average <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **Money to show for a study permit:** first-year tuition, first-year living costs (at least CAD 23,448 for a single applicant outside Quebec from 1 September 2026) and travel (see [Step 2: Apply for the study permit](#step-2-apply-for-the-study-permit)) <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **What part-time work covers:** daily costs at most, not tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote in November 2026 (see [Can you work?](#can-you-work)) <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **Baggage:** on Biman, students flying from Dhaka to study in Canada were charged Tk 20,000 to 25,000 for a bag one pound overweight (see [Flying to Canada](#flying-to-canada)) <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What do Bangladeshi students pay study-abroad agencies in 2026, and what does a Canadian college diploma cost a year?
> - What is the study permit fee, the visit visa fee and the biometrics fee in 2026?

<a id="what-ties-you-to-the-employer"></a>

### 1.5 What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** work off campus is limited to 24 hours a week during classes, full time in scheduled breaks (see [Can you work?](#can-you-work)) <sup>[N13](#n13), [N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **Graduates:** the post-graduation work permit is open, so the holder can work for any employer <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
- **Workers on a temporary foreign worker permit:** in the Saskatchewan case the woman's permit was later issued for one named restaurant, in Elrose <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **Anyone without a work permit:** cannot work legally anywhere in Canada <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Can a worker on an LMIA-based work permit change employer, and how long does a new permit take?

<a id="what-goes-wrong-most-and-what-people-wrongly-believe"></a>

### 1.6 What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are fake documents in visit-visa files, students who never start their courses, asylum claims that no longer succeed, and workers held without valid permits <sup>[N3](#n3), [N4](#n4), [N8](#n8), [N5](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="what-people-believe-and-the-facts"></a>

#### 1.6.1 What people believe, and the facts

- **"Come on a visit or student visa, then claim asylum."** Asylum claims by temporary visa holders fell 84% from April 2024 to April 2026, and a claim that contradicts what was said in the visa application is now examined strictly (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.
- **"The agent filled in the form, so the agent is responsible."** The applicant is responsible for everything in the application, whoever prepared it <sup>[N9](#n9), [N3](#n3)</sup>.
- **"A study permit lets me go and work."** A Prothom Alo education article said that chasing work instead of study makes staying in Canada hard, and work off campus is capped at 24 hours a week during classes <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6), [N13](#n13)</sup>.
- **"Part-time work will pay the tuition."** Part-time income helps with daily costs but is not enough for tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote; a Bangladeshi student in Manitoba said hundreds now queue for a job in an ordinary grocery shop <sup>[N14](#n14), [N12](#n12)</sup>.
- **"Studying in Canada leads to a job and permanent residence."** It guarantees neither; that depends on skills, experience and the immigration policy of the time <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
- **"A work permit leads to permanent residence."** A woman was promised permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process and was instead trafficked (see [The Saskatchewan restaurant case](#the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case)) <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="irregular-routes-and-where-an-irregular-stay-ends"></a>

#### 1.6.2 Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **Crossing into the United States:** many who came on visit visas after COVID-19 crossed into the US illegally, according to a Canada-based journalist; one Bangladeshi refugee claimant who crossed, he says by mistake, was held by US immigration and Canada would not take him back (see [Irregular routes and undocumented stay](#irregular-routes-and-undocumented-stay)) <sup>[N2](#n2), [N15](#n15)</sup>.
- **Staying without status:** no legal work, and a person without status who falls ill and goes to public health care may be caught by police, a Prothom Alo article said (see [Living without status](#living-without-status)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="visas-not-open-to-bangladeshis"></a>

## 2 Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Open work permit for a student's spouse, below master's level:** since the 2024 cap, only spouses of master's, PhD and postdoctoral students get open work permits <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.
- **Post-graduation work permit after a college program outside the listed fields:** tourism, hospitality and business studies are not on the list (see [Step 1: Choose the institution and program](#step-1-choose-the-institution-and-program)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

<a id="student-visa"></a>

## 3 Student visa

Canada capped new international students in 2024 because of a housing crisis, cut the 2026 target to about 155,000, and took steps against small private colleges accused of low-quality teaching <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6), [N7](#n7)</sup>. Immigration Minister Marc Miller said some provinces would see about 50% fewer international students, and students already in Canada whose visas expire before they finish are not counted in the cap <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

<a id="step-1-choose-the-institution-and-program"></a>

### 3.1 Step 1: Choose the institution and program

1. Start 10 to 12 months ahead: the main intake is September (some institutions also take students in January or May), applications for September usually open late in the year before, and international deadlines are often earlier <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
2. Check the entry requirements: a Bangladeshi HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) is enough to apply for a three- to four-year bachelor's degree; each university's website shows how it rates the HSC, the subjects it requires (higher mathematics and physics for engineering) and the minimum marks; a GPA 5 alone does not guarantee admission or a scholarship, because competitive programs look at subject marks <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
3. Take an English test: most English-medium universities accept IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT or another test they recognise; many bachelor's programs ask for IELTS 6.5 overall, with minimums for each part that vary by department, and French-language institutions may require French <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
4. Check that the program leads to a post-graduation work permit if you want to work in Canada afterwards:
   - a university bachelor's degree qualifies in any field, for a permit of up to three years <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>;
   - a college program qualifies only if it is on IRCC's list: 966 programs in agriculture and agri-food, health care, STEM (science, technology, engineering and mathematics), skilled trades and transportation <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>;
   - tourism, hospitality and business studies are not on the list <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>;
   - since 2024 only programs on IRCC's approved Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) list qualify; 119 fields were added and 178 programs removed from early 2026 <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
   - not every institution or program qualifies, so check before admission <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
5. For a diploma or certificate, confirm the program's CIP code, and re-check it if you change program midway <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
6. Look for scholarships early: universities offer entrance and merit scholarships and some offer need-based aid, but full bachelor's scholarships are few and very competitive, and many scholarship deadlines close before the admission deadline <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
7. Choose a reputable institution after researching it, as a Prothom Alo education article advised; it also said competition would be high in Ontario and British Columbia, so other provinces are worth trying <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

**Quebec:** parts of Quebec's new secularism law took effect on 1 September 2026, banning prayer rooms at public universities and colleges there; institutions' published guidance also bars praying on campus and covering the face <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. Many universities outside Quebec have multifaith prayer rooms, and Muslim, Hindu and Bangladeshi student associations that help newcomers settle and find housing <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.

An admission letter can be from a college that does not really exist: an Indian student who arrived in Brampton found that the address on his was a small office with no classrooms (see [Done to students](#done-to-students)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a student check that a Canadian college is a designated learning institution (DLI), and where is IRCC's CIP list published?

<a id="step-2-apply-for-the-study-permit"></a>

### 3.2 Step 2: Apply for the study permit

1. Apply to the university: most Ontario universities take applications through the Ontario Universities' Application Centre (OUAC); in other provinces apply to the institution directly <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. Send SSC (Secondary School Certificate) and HSC certificates and transcripts, your passport, the language test result and, where asked, a personal statement; some programs add a questionnaire, portfolio or interview <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
2. If selected, you get a letter of acceptance <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
3. Get a provincial attestation letter (PAL): because of the national cap on study permit applications set in 2024, most bachelor's applicants need one, usually obtained through the university after admission, and without it IRCC does not process the study permit application <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>. For Quebec you also need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
4. Prove the money, separately for each: first-year tuition, first-year living costs and travel. For applications from 1 September 2026, a single applicant outside Quebec must show at least CAD 23,448 for living costs on top of tuition; Quebec has its own rules <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.
5. Apply online to IRCC for the study permit <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.

Every document must be genuine and every answer true: a forged education certificate or hidden fact is misrepresentation (see [Lying on an application](#lying-on-an-application)) <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

> - Where does a Bangladeshi give biometrics for a study permit (VFS Global Dhaka?), what does the permit cost, and how long does a decision take in 2026?
> - What is the refusal rate for Bangladeshi study permit applications in 2025 and 2026?

<a id="step-3-study-full-time-in-canada"></a>

### 3.3 Step 3: Study full time in Canada

1. Stay enrolled full time for the whole program; part-time is allowed only in the last semester <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
2. Study in Canada: online study from outside Canada no longer counts towards the post-graduation work permit for programs starting after September 2024 <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
3. Attend: under the International Student Compliance Regime, institutions report twice a year whether students are attending classes as their permits require <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. In the figures published in March-April 2024, about 50,000 international students had come on study permits without starting their studies, and 7.5% of 11,948 Bangladeshi students never contacted their institution after arriving <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="can-you-work"></a>

### 3.4 Can you work?

1. During regular semesters a student may work up to 24 hours a week off campus, and full time during scheduled breaks such as summer or winter <sup>[N13](#n13), [N14](#n14)</sup>. No separate work permit is needed for part-time work on or off campus <sup>[N17 (2024)](#n17)</sup>.
2. Working without authorisation can cancel student status and lead to refusal of the post-graduation work permit <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

**Finding work:** in April 2026 Statistics Canada put unemployment among 15- to 24-year-olds at 14.1%, more than double the 6.7% national rate; for South Asian youth, including Bangladeshis, it was 13%, against 11.2% for white youth <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Canada lost about 109,000 jobs in January and February 2026, half of them full time, including 64,000 held by young people <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Analysts said employers now prefer experienced workers, and newcomers without professional networks often get no interview <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Ashiq Zaman, a Bangladeshi student at the University of Manitoba, said white classmates find jobs through references while South Asians send CVs to hundreds of places without reply <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Competition is highest in Toronto, Montreal, Edmonton, Vancouver and Calgary, where many immigrants live, while smaller cities have fewer jobs <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Part-time pay helps with daily costs but does not cover tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote <sup>[N14](#n14)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What on-campus work may a student do, and does it count towards the 24 hours?

<a id="step-4-apply-for-the-post-graduation-work-permit"></a>

### 3.5 Step 4: Apply for the post-graduation work permit

The post-graduation work permit (PGWP) is an open work permit for graduates of Canadian institutions, valid for up to three years depending on the length of study <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. Students who applied for or got a study permit before 1 November 2024 fall under the old rules; later applicants under the new ones <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

**You'll need:**
- proof of program completion (degree, diploma or transcript) <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>;
- proof of full-time enrolment <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>;
- proof that the program is eligible <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>;
- language test results from an approved test in English or French, no more than two years old: CLB (Canadian Language Benchmarks) 7 or NCLC 7 for university graduates, CLB 5 or NCLC 5 in reading, writing, listening and speaking for college graduates <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N13](#n13)</sup>;
- medical results, where needed <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>;
- a passport valid long enough: the permit cannot run past the passport's expiry, so renew it first, the article advised <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

1. Apply online within 180 days of getting proof that you completed the program; after that the application is usually refused <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
2. Show you will leave Canada when the permit ends; family ties, property or a job at home help <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
3. Medical problems, insufficient funds, a criminal record, a security risk or false information can make you inadmissible, and so can a family member's inadmissibility <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.

<a id="bringing-a-spouse"></a>

### 3.6 Bringing a spouse

Since the 2024 cap, only spouses of master's, PhD and postdoctoral students get open work permits <sup>[N6 (2024)](#n6)</sup>.

<a id="scams-and-mistakes"></a>

### 3.7 Scams and mistakes

<a id="done-to-students"></a>

#### 3.7.1 Done to students

- **Fake colleges:** many Indian students found on arrival that their college was fake or lacked facilities, according to the Economic Times <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. One from Haryana dealt with his agent only by phone, and after he paid 4 lakh rupees the agent assured him he could pay the rest from part-time work; he had paid 4.2 lakh rupees of a 12 lakh rupee "tuition fee" before finding the college was an empty office <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **Fraudulent consultants:** see Asylum <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

<a id="done-by-students"></a>

#### 3.7.2 Done by students

- **Using the study permit to work:** some students used the student visa to get into Canada for work, exploiting the rule that tuition need not be paid in advance <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. Canada now counts students who never start (see [Step 3: Study full time in Canada](#step-3-study-full-time-in-canada)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **Using the study permit to claim asylum:** asylum claims by foreign students hit a record in 2024, and at least 10% of student visa holders were unaccounted for (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="visit-visa"></a>

## 4 Visit visa

The visit visa is the temporary resident visa (TRV) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. After COVID-19 Canada issued visit visas in large numbers, and lakhs of people from Bangladesh, India and other countries got them with fake documents, a Canada-based journalist wrote <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. Canada now screens temporary visa applications more carefully, and entry is limited for anyone whose purpose is unclear or suspicious <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. (For people sold Canadian visit visas as a way to work, see "Visit and tourist visas sold as jobs" in the Illegal routes doc.)

> **Need to know**
> - Where does a Bangladeshi apply for a Canadian visit visa, what documents and funds are required, what does it cost, and how long does it take in 2026?

<a id="visa-cancellation-powers"></a>

### 4.1 Visa cancellation powers

Documents obtained by CBC News in 2025 show IRCC and CBSA officials set up a working group with US partners to find and cancel fraudulent visitor visa applications and to widen their powers to do so <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. The internal documents named India and Bangladesh as "specific challenging countries" and listed "visa holders of specific countries" among the cases where visas could be cancelled en masse <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. In public, Immigration Minister Lena Diab cited only pandemics or war, and IRCC said the proposal was not aimed at specific groups; the power was put in Bill C-12 <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. More than 30 civil society groups objected, the Migrant Rights Network calling it a "mass deportation machine" <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. IRCC said tighter checks on TRV applications from "countries with the highest rates of abuse" had cut irregular crossings from the US into Canada by 97% since June 2024, and that visa cancellations for fraud were up 25% on a year earlier <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Did the mass-cancellation power pass in Bill C-12, and has it been used against Bangladeshi visa holders?

<a id="world-cup-visitors-who-claimed-asylum"></a>

### 4.2 World Cup visitors who claimed asylum

Canada issued 26,111 FIFA-related temporary resident permits or visas for the 2026 World Cup matches in Toronto and Vancouver, and by 20 July 2026, 175 of the holders had claimed asylum after arriving, according to IRCC data reported by The Globe and Mail <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. Only 45 of the visas went to Bangladeshis, and 10 of them claimed asylum, about one in five <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. Ghana and China had 25 claimants each; Egypt, Colombia and Kenya 15; Bangladesh, Nigeria and Senegal 10; Pakistan, Nepal, Burundi and Ecuador 5 <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. IRCC had warned that ticket holders at risk of not going home could be stopped at the border, and immigration analysts fear the claims will make visas harder for genuine fans at future events <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.

<a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case"></a>

### 4.3 Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case

1. <a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case-item-1"></a>**The case:** on 22 October 2026 CBSA announced charges against five people for running an organised visit-visa fraud ring with forged papers and false information <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
2. <a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case-item-2"></a>**The applications:** 243 TRV applications in the names of 209 Bangladeshis showed fake Canadian citizen hosts on invitation letters and used forged documents and false information <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
3. <a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case-item-3"></a>**The accused:** Russell Mirza and Mohammad Aminul Islam of Montreal, Md Mudassar Alam Bhuiyan of Toronto, and Kazi Nafiz Alam appeared in the Valleyfield court in Quebec on 22 October 2026; the fifth, Md Rakibul Hasan, is believed to be in Bangladesh, and an arrest warrant was issued for him <sup>[N3](#n3), [N18](#n18)</sup>. The charges have not yet been tested in court <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.
4. <a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case-item-4"></a>**How it came out:** on 3 July 2024 CBSA, with local police and fire officials, inspected a residential building in Mercier, Quebec, found signs of immigration breaches, and opened an investigation in August 2024; on 3 June 2025 it searched four sites in Quebec and Ontario and seized computers and documents <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. Investigators said the ring worked in an organised way, and they focused on the 79 applications with the strongest evidence <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>.
5. <a id="fake-hosts-and-invitation-letters-the-cbsa-case-item-5"></a>**The charges:** under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA), sections 117(1) and 117(3) (organising illegal entry by conspiracy), 126 and 128(a) (helping create false information and hide facts in applications) and 131 (aiding a breach of immigration law) <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree said anyone who exploits the immigration system through fraud faces severe consequences <sup>[N18](#n18)</sup>. CBSA warned that fake bank statements, fake invitation letters or false information must never be used, and that the applicant can face a five-year ban even if an agent prepared the application <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

<a id="stopped-before-boarding"></a>

### 4.4 Stopped before boarding

Since March 2024 Canada has re-checked visas for fraud and stopped more than 2,000 Bangladeshi and Indian travellers from boarding flights to Canada, according to a Global Affairs Canada document <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. In 2024 CBSA recommended that 493 Bangladeshis not be boarded, 475 of them suspected of fraud <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Where is a Bangladeshi passenger stopped (at Dhaka check-in or at a transit airport), who tells them, and does the airline refund the ticket?

<a id="work-permit"></a>

## 5 Work permit

Prime Minister Mark Carney said in September 2025 that the Temporary Foreign Worker Program must be restructured to match real labour demand in specific sectors and regions, and Canada cut its 2026 target for temporary foreign workers by 37% to 230,000 <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. From January to August 2025 only 42% of the targeted temporary foreign workers arrived <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. How a genuine Canadian work permit and LMIA work, and how fake ones are sold in Bangladesh, are set out in the Dalal scams doc (see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc).

> **Need to know**
> - Do Bangladeshi recruiting agencies hold any genuine Canadian LMIA job orders, and does BMET issue permission letters for Canada?

<a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case"></a>

### 5.1 The Saskatchewan restaurant case

1. <a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case-item-1"></a>**The promise:** between 1 August 2022 and 31 March 2023, a woman who had worked about 18 years in a government job in Bangladesh was promised a restaurant job in Canada and permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
2. <a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case-item-2"></a>**What happened:** she was made to work at Sohel Haider's restaurant in Gull Lake, Saskatchewan, then at Mohammad Masum's restaurant in Tisdale, without a valid work permit for them; told her permit papers were being processed, she worked long hours six days a week without proper pay <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
3. <a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case-item-3"></a>**The passport and the abuse:** Masum took her Bangladeshi passport and sexually assaulted her several times, which the judge called rape <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
4. <a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case-item-4"></a>**The way out:** when she got a different work permit for a restaurant in Elrose and arrived there, local Canadians who saw her condition helped her escape and get legal help <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
5. <a id="the-saskatchewan-restaurant-case-item-5"></a>**The sentence:** on 22 October 2026 Saskatoon Provincial Court sentenced Masum, 45, to 14 years for human trafficking and three sexual assaults, and Haider, 56, to the minimum 4 years for human trafficking, and ordered them to pay her CAD 12,268 and CAD 5,885.16 <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="permanent-residence"></a>

## 6 Permanent residence

Canada's 2026 target is 380,000 new permanent residents: 239,800 economic (64%), 84,000 family reunification and 56,200 refugee and humanitarian <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>. The 2025 budget planned permanent residence for up to 33,000 temporary work permit holders in 2026 and 2027, with priority for rural and remote regions hit by labour shortages and tariff changes <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Which programs give permanent residence to the 33,000 work permit holders, and who qualifies?

<a id="lying-on-an-application"></a>

## 7 Lying on an application

Under IRPA, forging or altering a passport, visa, education certificate or similar document is misrepresentation, and so is hiding important information or giving false information <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. The documents most often forged are passports and travel documents; visas; diplomas, degrees and trade or apprenticeship certificates; birth, marriage, divorce, annulment, separation and death certificates; and police clearance certificates <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. The applicant is responsible for everything in the application even when an agent or consultant filled it in <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

**What can happen:**
- the application is refused at once <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>;
- a ban of at least five years on entering or applying to Canada <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>;
- a permanent record of fraud <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>;
- for someone already in Canada, loss of status, removal and possibly criminal charges <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="asylum"></a>

## 8 Asylum

Asylum claims by temporary visa holders (visitors and students) fell from 10,095 in April 2024 to 1,580 in April 2026, an 84% drop, according to IRCC <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. People used to enter on a visit or student visa and claim asylum later; now any mismatch between the reasons given for the visa and the asylum claim is examined strictly <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. Bill C-12, in force since 26 March 2026, made asylum eligibility much harder, especially for those who claim long after entering Canada <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. Claims are now filed through a digital portal with strict deadlines, and more are being dismissed or declared abandoned <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. Many visit-visa holders who claimed asylum after COVID-19 still had cases in the courts in June 2025 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>. Of the 45 Bangladeshis given visitor visas for the 2026 World Cup, 10 claimed asylum (see [World Cup visitors who claimed asylum](#world-cup-visitors-who-claimed-asylum)) <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. (see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc).

One Bangladeshi refugee claimant's first claim was rejected because it was filed through a fraudulent immigration consultant (see [Crossing into the United States](#crossing-into-the-united-states)) <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

<a id="irregular-routes-and-undocumented-stay"></a>

## 9 Irregular routes and undocumented stay

<a id="crossing-into-the-united-states"></a>

### 9.1 Crossing into the United States

Many people who came on visit visas after COVID-19 crossed illegally into the US, and Indian authorities said some students did the same <sup>[N2](#n2), [N4](#n4)</sup>. A Global Affairs Canada document said stricter visa rules for travellers from Mexico, India and Bangladesh cut illegal crossings from Canada into the US by 84% between June and November 2024 <sup>[N2](#n2)</sup>.

Mahin Shahriar, a Bangladeshi-origin refugee claimant who his lawyer says has lived in Canada since 2019, shows how a crossing can end <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>:
1. On 12 May 2025 he followed directions sent to a friend's phone to an address near Montreal that was very close to the border, and crossed into the US without knowing it, he said <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
2. US ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) held him in Buffalo; ICE was ready to return him but CBSA did not approve his entry, and CBSA said every entry decision is made case by case <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
3. ICE could not send him to Bangladesh because he had no travel document, and US authorities accepted that he might be tortured there, his lawyer said <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
4. His lawyer, Wasim Ahmed, said the Safe Third Country Agreement has a rule to return such an asylum seeker to Canada within 14 days, and asked the Federal Court for an urgent hearing on humanitarian grounds <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What did the Federal Court decide in Mahin Shahriar's case, and does Canada take back its refugee claimants who cross into the US?

<a id="living-without-status"></a>

### 9.2 Living without status

Without a work permit no one can work legally in Canada <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. A Prothom Alo article said health care in Canada is public, so a person without status who falls ill must go to public care, where police may catch them <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

## 10 Flying to Canada

Biman Bangladesh Airlines flies Dhaka to Toronto with a stopover in Rome; one flight had a technical fault before Rome and passengers were delayed for a long time <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>. A US-based columnist who flew it in 2026 found the flight full, with many first-time flyers sitting about 20 hours at a stretch <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>:

- at the Toronto counter he was asked to pay several hundred dollars for a bag one pound overweight <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>;
- at Dhaka, students going to Canada were charged Tk 20,000 to 25,000 for one pound over <sup>[N16](#n16)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
