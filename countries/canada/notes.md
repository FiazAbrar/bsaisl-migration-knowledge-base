# Canada: research notes

## What this version is built from

14 Prothom Alo articles (January 2024 to October 2026) from the archive chunk xchunk-canada.jsonl, read in full; 13 used, 110 facts logged (XC0001 to XC0110), all cited [N] as news. No official source has been fetched yet, and no network research was done for this version.

The reporting covers the 2024 student cap, the post-graduation work permit (PGWP) rules of November 2024 and their common refusal reasons, student no-shows, visa fraud checks and boarding denials, the 2026-2028 levels plan targets, the March 2026 asylum law (Bill C-12), IRCC's misrepresentation warning, the October 2026 CBSA case over 209 Bangladeshi visit-visa applicants, the October 2026 Saskatchewan trafficking sentence, a Bangladeshi refugee claimant stuck in US detention, NID services at the High Commission, and Biman's Dhaka-Toronto flight. How a genuine LMIA work permit works and the fake Canada job offers sold in Bangladesh are already in the global Dalal scams doc ("Fake Australia and Canada work visas"); the April 2024 reporting on visit-visa asylum claimants is in the global Illegal routes doc. This doc links to both rather than repeating them.

The reporting says nothing on study permit fees, proof of funds, the visa application centre in Dhaka, BMET clearance for Canada, LMIA job orders through Bangladeshi agencies, or the High Commission's address and helplines; those are "Need to know" items in the doc.

## Resolved conflicts

- **Number of accused in the CBSA case (updated in the second pass):** the 24 October 2026 Nagorik article (pa-2026-10-24-d2rpy8qwuq) says five were charged but names four, says the four were presented in court while listing Rakibul Hasan (in Bangladesh) among them, and mentions one more accomplice. The second Prothom Alo report of the same CBSA release (pa-2026-10-24-94ztp26a3b) names all five: Russell Mirza, Mohammad Aminul Islam, Md Mudassar Alam Bhuiyan and Kazi Nafiz Alam appeared in the Valleyfield court on 22 October, and Md Rakibul Hasan, in Bangladesh, is under an arrest warrant. The second report is internally consistent (five charged, four in court, one abroad) and names the "accomplice", so it wins; the doc now names all five [XC0093 for residences, XC0135 for the court appearance and the fifth name] and no longer says "one more accomplice".
- **Prosecutor's mention of "Sohel and Mahfuz":** the Saskatchewan article once names the accused as "Sohel and Mahfuz", against "Sohel Haider and Mohammad Masum" everywhere else; treated as a misprint and not used.
- **Boarding denials:** "more than 2,000 Indian and Bangladeshi nationals" stopped since March 2024 (Global Affairs document) and "493 Bangladeshis" recommended not to board in 2024 (CBSA) are different measures and periods; both kept with their sources.
- **84% figures:** an 84% drop in illegal crossings to the US (June to November 2024, Global Affairs) and an 84% drop in asylum claims by temporary visa holders (April 2024 to April 2026, IRCC) are separate statistics; both kept with their periods.
- **Student cap numbers:** 360,000 a year for 2024-2025 (January 2024) and a 2026 target of about 155,000 (November 2025 levels plan) are different years, not a conflict.
- **Safe Third Country Agreement "14 days":** stated by the article as a rule; the doc attributes it to the lawyer's argument, since the article frames it as his belief about what should apply.
- **Student work hours (held item R3-countries-canada-1):** a July 2024 Prothom Alo education report (pa-2024-07-04-6ngsgospii) gives 20 hours a week off campus during studies; the November 2025 and November 2026 reports [XC0054, XC0158] give 24 hours. The newer, specific figure wins (the 2024 article predates the change); only the article's still-current point, that no separate work permit is needed, is used [XC0166].

**Method notes:**
- canada.ca hangs on fetch.py's browser user agent but answers curl's; pages were saved with fetch.py's own `fetch()` called with the curl user agent (`work/f.py`). bs4 is not installed, so fetch.py made no .txt copy; `work/totxt.py` makes the same kind of plain-text copy from the saved HTML (needed for tables pandoc drops, such as the fee list and the living-cost table).
- Study permits for Bangladeshis by year (OC0014-OC0017) are sums of IRCC's monthly open-data rows, each rounded to the nearest 5; the sums are approximate and written to `sources/ircc-od-study-permits-citz.bangladesh.txt`. This counts permits that became effective, a different measure from Prothom Alo's 11,948 "students who went" [XC0021].
- Processing times are IRCC's published figures for Bangladesh on 18 November 2026 (study 4 weeks, visit 30 days, work 30 weeks); they move weekly.
- "No labour wing" at the Ottawa High Commission is read from its full service-email directory (OC0145), not from a statement.
