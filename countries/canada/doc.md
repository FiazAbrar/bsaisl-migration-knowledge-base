# Canada: migration guide

How Bangladeshis go to Canada, visa by visa, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

CAD is the Canadian dollar; Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000). IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) is Canada's immigration, refugee and citizenship agency [XC0010]; CBSA (the Canada Border Services Agency) screens travellers and investigates immigration fraud [XC0035, OC0136]. BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) is the Bangladesh government body that clears workers to go abroad [OC0003].

## Background

### In one minute

Bangladeshis go to Canada as students and visitors, and some on work permits tied to a job offer [XC0021, XC0036, XC0105]. Since 2024 Canada has been cutting back: it capped international students, tied the post-study work permit for college graduates to listed subjects, required a language score for it, and planned to roughly halve new students in 2026 [XC0001, XC0013, XC0017, XC0065]. Many people got Canadian visit visas after COVID-19 with fake documents and then crossed into the United States or claimed asylum, a Canada-based journalist wrote [XC0036, XC0037]. Canada now refuses far more visas, stops suspect travellers before they board, and from March 2026 made asylum much harder to get: a claim made more than a year after first entering Canada is no longer heard [XC0034, XC0033, OC0098]. A false document or false statement, even one an agent put in the application, can bring a ban of at least five years [OC0124, OC0125]. In October 2026 CBSA charged five people over a scheme that filed 243 visit-visa applications for 209 Bangladeshis with Canadian citizens presented as hosts and fraudulent documents [OC0136], and a Saskatchewan court jailed two Bangladeshi-origin restaurant owners for trafficking a Bangladeshi woman promised a job and permanent residence [XC0094, XC0095, XC0103, XC0105]. When Canada sought powers in 2025 to cancel visas en masse, its internal documents named India and Bangladesh as "specific challenging countries", and 10 of the 45 Bangladeshis given visitor visas for the 2026 FIFA World Cup claimed asylum [XC0112, XC0115, XC0131]. Students arrive to a weak job market: in early 2026 unemployment among 15- to 24-year-olds was 14.1% [XC0121]. Study permits that took effect for Bangladeshis fell from about 9,810 in 2023 to about 5,860 in 2025 [OC0014, OC0016], and BMET cleared only 147 workers for Canada in 2025 [OC0007]. Fake Canadian job offers sold in Bangladesh are covered in the Dalal scams doc (see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc).

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open for students, visitors and workers with a genuine job offer, but with fewer places and far stricter checks than before 2024 [XC0064, XC0065, XC0034].

#### Status

- **22 January 2024:** Canada announced a two-year cap on international students: at most 360,000 a year, about 35% fewer than in 2023, because of a housing crisis (see Student visa) [XC0001, XC0002, XC0003].
- **March 2024:** Canada began re-checking visas for fraud, after which more than 2,000 Indian and Bangladeshi nationals were stopped from boarding flights to Canada, according to a Global Affairs Canada document [XC0033].
- **From June 2024:** Canada introduced enhanced visa checks, after which the refusal rate for new Canadian visa applications reached 63%, according to the same document [XC0034].
- **1 November 2024:** new rules for the post-graduation work permit took effect (see Step 4: Apply for the post-graduation work permit) [XC0010].
- **4 November 2025:** the 2025 federal budget cut the 2026 target for new temporary residents to 385,000, about 43% below 2025 [XC0063, XC0064].
- **November 2025:** Bill C-12, before Parliament, included a power to cancel visas en masse; internal documents named India and Bangladesh (see Visa cancellation powers) [XC0115, XC0112].
- **2026:** IRCC froze the list of fields of study that lead to a post-graduation work permit; nothing is added or removed in 2026 (see Step 1: Choose the institution and program) [OC0083].
- **January-February 2026:** Canada lost about 109,000 jobs, 64,000 of them held by young people (see Can you work?) [XC0123].
- **26 March 2026:** Bill C-12, the Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act, became law: asylum claims made more than a year after first entry are no longer heard (see Asylum), and the government can cancel visas in bulk (see Visa cancellation powers) [OC0101, OC0098, OC0104].
- **June 2026:** new asylum regulations were proposed; they came into force on 3 November 2026 with a single online claim process (see Asylum) [OC0106].
- **2026 FIFA World Cup:** 10 of the 45 Bangladeshis given World Cup visitor visas claimed asylum after arriving (see World Cup visitors who claimed asylum) [XC0131].
- **1 September 2026:** for study permit applications from this date, a single applicant outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 a year for living costs on top of tuition (see Step 2: Apply for the study permit) [OC0032]. Parts of Quebec's secularism law took effect the same day, banning prayer rooms at public universities and colleges (see Step 1: Choose the institution and program) [XC0162].
- **22 October 2026:** CBSA announced charges against five people over a Bangladeshi visit-visa fraud ring (see Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case) [OC0136].
- **November 2026:** the 2027-2029 Immigration Levels Plan set up to 150,000 new international students and 220,000 new workers a year for 2027 to 2029 [OC0018].

#### The channels

- **Student visa (study permit), then the post-graduation work permit:** see Student visa [XC0021, XC0050].
- **Visit visa (temporary resident visa, TRV):** for visits; now refused far more often (see Visit visa) [XC0094, XC0034].
- **Work permit with a job offer:** an employer-specific work permit, usually backed by an LMIA (labour market impact assessment), a document the Canadian employer gets to prove a foreign worker is needed (see Work permit) [OC0111, OC0112].
- **Permanent residence:** later, for some temporary workers and graduates, not on arrival (see Permanent residence) [XC0062].
- **Not a legal route:** going on to the United States across the land border (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XC0030, XC0037].

#### Size

- **Study permits for Bangladeshis:** about 9,810 took effect in 2023, 6,735 in 2024, 5,860 in 2025 and 4,305 from January to September 2026 [OC0014, OC0015, OC0016, OC0017].
- **Bangladeshi students who never showed up:** 11,948 went in the period covered by Canada's figures published in March-April 2024, and 7.5% of them never contacted their university or college after arriving [XC0021].
- **Workers cleared by BMET:** 548 in 2023 (196 women), 534 in 2024, 147 in 2025 and 64 from January to 23 November 2026, by BMET's online OEP clearance report [OC0003, OC0004, OC0005, OC0007, OC0009]. In 2023, 420 of the 548 were recorded under the job category "Worker"; in 2024 the largest named trade was cook (30) [OC0011, OC0013].
- **Post-graduation work permits:** more than 105,000 were issued in the first half of 2024, 64% of them to college program graduates, according to the Toronto Star [XC0019].
- **Stopped before boarding:** in 2024 CBSA recommended that about 11,050 passengers with suspicious travel documents not be boarded, 493 of them Bangladeshis (475 suspected of fraud) [XC0035].
- **World Cup visitor visas:** 45 issued from Bangladesh for the 2026 tournament, out of 26,111 FIFA-related permits or visas in all [XC0131, XC0129].
- **Canada's 2026 targets:** 155,000 new international students and 230,000 temporary foreign workers, and 380,000 new permanent residents [XC0065, XC0066].
- **Canada's 2027-2029 targets:** each year, up to 150,000 new international students, 220,000 new workers and 380,000 permanent residents [OC0018, OC0019].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

In this reporting a Bangladeshi reaches Canada through a college or university admission, a visit visa backed by a Canadian host, or a job offer through the temporary foreign worker process, and each reported fraud or abuse case turns on one of those routes [XC0021, XC0095, XC0105]. The 2026 CBSA case shows the visit-visa version: men in Montreal and Toronto and a partner in Bangladesh are accused of building applications for 209 Bangladeshis with fake Canadian hosts and forged documents (see Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case) [XC0093, XC0094, XC0095]. The Saskatchewan case shows the job version: two Bangladeshi-origin restaurant owners promised a woman a restaurant job and permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process, then worked her without a valid permit (see The Saskatchewan restaurant case) [XC0105, XC0107]. Immigration consultants can also cheat: one Bangladeshi refugee claimant's first asylum claim was rejected because a fraudulent immigration consultant filed it [XC0046].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **Government fees:** study permit CAD 150, visitor visa CAD 100, work permit CAD 155, open work permit holder fee CAD 100 on top, biometrics CAD 85 per person (at most CAD 170 for a family applying together) [OC0034, OC0037, OC0038, OC0039, OC0035, OC0036]. The post-graduation work permit costs CAD 255 [OC0081].
- **Visa centre extras:** booking a biometrics appointment is free; the premium lounge (Tk 8,409) and courier (Tk 5,096 a package) are optional (see Step 2: Apply for the study permit) [OC0048, OC0050].
- **Fake papers cost more than money:** a false document in any application can mean refusal, a ban of at least five years and a permanent fraud record (see Lying on an application) [OC0124].
- **Unpaid wages:** in the Saskatchewan case the court ordered the two owners to pay the woman CAD 5,885.16 and CAD 12,268 as unpaid wages and restitution [XC0104].
- **Tuition:** international undergraduates paid on average about CAD 41,746 a year in 2025-26, about CAD 49,800 in Ontario and about CAD 18,900 in Newfoundland and Labrador, according to Statistics Canada; engineering and computer science can cost well above the average [XC0152, XC0153].
- **Money to show for a study permit:** first-year tuition, first-year living costs (CAD 23,448 for a single applicant outside Quebec for applications from 1 September 2026) and travel (see Step 2: Apply for the study permit) [OC0029, OC0032].
- **What part-time work covers:** daily costs at most, not tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote in November 2026 (see Can you work?) [XC0159].
- **Baggage:** on Biman, students flying from Dhaka to study in Canada were charged Tk 20,000 to 25,000 for a bag one pound overweight (see Flying to Canada) [XC0090].

### What ties you to the employer

- **Students:** work off campus is limited to 24 hours a week during classes, unlimited in scheduled breaks (see Can you work?) [OC0068, OC0069].
- **Graduates:** the post-graduation work permit is open, so the holder can work for any employer [XC0050].
- **Workers on an employer-specific permit:** the permit names the employer, place and job [OC0111]. To change employer the worker needs a new work permit, and a worker who loses the job must stop working until it is approved (see Changing employer) [OC0114, OC0115]. A worker who is abused can get an open permit to leave (see If you are abused or not paid) [OC0121].
- **Anyone without a work permit:** cannot work legally anywhere in Canada [OC0113].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The reported problems are fake documents in visit-visa files, students who never start their courses, asylum claims that are now examined strictly and harder to win, and workers held without valid permits [XC0094, XC0021, XC0072, XC0073, XC0107].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Come on a visit or student visa, then claim asylum."** Asylum claims by temporary visa holders fell 84% from April 2024 to April 2026, and a claim that contradicts what was said in the visa application is now examined strictly; IRCC says asylum is not a shortcut to immigrate or stay longer (see Asylum) [XC0070, XC0071, XC0072, OC0097].
- **"The agent filled in the form, so the agent is responsible."** The applicant is responsible for everything in the application, whoever prepared it [OC0125].
- **"A study permit lets me go and work."** A Prothom Alo education article said that chasing work instead of study makes staying in Canada hard, and work off campus is capped at 24 hours a week during classes [XC0009, OC0068].
- **"Part-time work will pay the tuition."** Part-time income helps with daily costs but is not enough for tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote; a Bangladeshi student in Manitoba said hundreds now queue for a job in an ordinary grocery shop [XC0159, XC0126].
- **"Studying in Canada leads to a job and permanent residence."** It guarantees neither; that depends on skills, experience and the immigration policy of the time [XC0161].
- **"A work permit leads to permanent residence."** A woman was promised permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process and was instead trafficked (see The Saskatchewan restaurant case) [XC0105, XC0103].

#### Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

- **Crossing into the United States:** many who came on visit visas after COVID-19 crossed into the US illegally, according to a Canada-based journalist; one Bangladeshi refugee claimant who crossed, he says by mistake, was held by US immigration and Canada would not take him back (see Irregular routes and undocumented stay) [XC0037, XC0041].
- **Staying without status:** no legal work, and a person without status who falls ill and goes to public health care may be caught by police, a Prothom Alo article said (see Living without status) [XC0028, XC0029].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **Open work permit for a student's spouse, below master's level:** since the 2024 cap, only spouses of master's, PhD and postdoctoral students get open work permits [XC0006].
- **Post-graduation work permit after a college program outside the listed fields:** tourism, hospitality and business studies are not on the list (see Step 1: Choose the institution and program) [XC0015].

## Student visa

Canada capped new international students in 2024 because of a housing crisis, cut the 2026 target to about 155,000, and took steps against small private colleges accused of low-quality teaching [XC0003, XC0065, XC0005]. Immigration Minister Marc Miller said some provinces would see about 50% fewer international students, and students already in Canada whose visas expire before they finish are not counted in the cap [XC0004].

### Step 1: Choose the institution and program

1. Plan at least 10 to 12 months ahead, a November 2026 study guide advises, because September applications open late in the previous year and international deadlines are often earlier [XC0167]. The main intake is September (some institutions also take students in January or May), applications for September usually open late in the year before, and international deadlines are often earlier [XC0140].
2. Check the entry requirements: a Bangladeshi HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) is enough to apply for a three- to four-year bachelor's degree; each university's website shows how it rates the HSC, the subjects it requires (higher mathematics and physics for engineering) and the minimum marks; a GPA 5 alone does not guarantee admission or a scholarship, because competitive programs look at subject marks [XC0141, XC0142, XC0143].
3. Take an English test: most English-medium universities accept IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT or another test they recognise; many bachelor's programs ask for IELTS 6.5 overall, with minimums for each part that vary by department, and French-language institutions may require French [XC0144, XC0145].
4. Check the school is a designated learning institution (DLI), a school a province has approved to host international students: search IRCC's DLI list on canada.ca by province, school name or city, note the school's DLI number (it goes on the study permit application) and check whether the list shows it as offering programs that lead to a post-graduation work permit [OC0066, OC0067].
5. Check that the program leads to a post-graduation work permit if you want to work in Canada afterwards:
   - a bachelor's, master's or doctoral degree qualifies in any field [OC0074];
   - a college, polytechnic or other non-university program qualifies only if its field of study is on IRCC's list, for study permits applied for from 1 November 2024: about 966 programs in agriculture and agri-food, health care, STEM (science, technology, engineering and mathematics), skilled trades and transportation [OC0075, XC0013, XC0014];
   - tourism, hospitality and business studies are not on the list [XC0015];
   - to check, find the program's 6-digit CIP (Classification of Instructional Programs) code on the school's website, from the school, or in Statistics Canada's CIP catalogue, and search it on IRCC's "Field of study requirement" page; the code must be on the list when you apply for the study permit or the work permit, and the list is frozen for 2026 [OC0084, OC0085, OC0083];
   - programs a private college delivers on behalf of a public college, and programs done more than half online, do not qualify [OC0076];
   - re-check the CIP code if you change program midway [XC0052].
6. Look for scholarships early: universities offer entrance and merit scholarships and some offer need-based aid, but full bachelor's scholarships are few and very competitive, and many scholarship deadlines close before the admission deadline [XC0156, XC0157]. Applying for a scholarship is usually free, and nobody can guarantee one (see Done to students) [OC0128].
7. Choose a reputable institution after researching it, as a Prothom Alo education article advised; it also said competition would be high in Ontario and British Columbia, so other provinces are worth trying [XC0008, XC0007].

**Quebec:** parts of Quebec's new secularism law took effect on 1 September 2026, banning prayer rooms at public universities and colleges there; institutions' published guidance also bars praying on campus and covering the face [XC0162]. Many universities outside Quebec have multifaith prayer rooms, and Muslim, Hindu and Bangladeshi student associations that help newcomers settle and find housing [XC0163, XC0164].

An admission letter can be from a college that does not really exist: an Indian student who arrived in Brampton found that the address on his was a small office with no classrooms (see Done to students) [XC0024].

### Step 2: Apply for the study permit

Study permit applications from Bangladesh are decided by the High Commission of Canada in Singapore, not in Dhaka; the Dhaka, Chittagong and Sylhet visa application centres (VACs), run by VFS Global, only take fingerprints and passports [OC0051, OC0044].

1. Apply to the university: most Ontario universities take applications through the Ontario Universities' Application Centre (OUAC); in other provinces apply to the institution directly [XC0146]. Send SSC (Secondary School Certificate) and HSC certificates and transcripts, your passport, the language test result and, where asked, a personal statement; some programs add a questionnaire, portfolio or interview [XC0147].
2. If selected, you get a letter of acceptance (LOA); IRCC asks the school to confirm it is genuine, and if the school does not answer in time IRCC returns the application and refunds the fee [OC0022, OC0153].
3. Get a provincial attestation letter (PAL), a letter from the province confirming a study space for you: accept the offer and pay part or all of the tuition, then ask the school how to apply; the school provides it [OC0023, OC0024]. Master's and doctoral students at a public DLI do not need one from 1 January 2026 [OC0025]. A PAL issued in 2026 is valid until 31 December 2026 unless it shows an earlier date, and after an approval or refusal you need a new one to apply again [OC0026, OC0027]. For Quebec, get a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) from the Government of Quebec instead; the school explains how [OC0028].
4. Prove the money, without counting any work in Canada: first-year tuition, living costs and travel both ways, and a plan for paying the later years [OC0029, OC0030]. For applications from 1 September 2026, living costs outside Quebec are CAD 23,448 for one person, CAD 29,192 for two and CAD 35,888 for three [OC0032, OC0031]. Accepted proof includes a tuition and housing receipt from the school, an education loan, a scholarship letter, a sponsor's letter with their job, relationship and amount, and 6 months of bank statements with the source of the money [OC0033].
5. Prepare the Bangladesh extras the Singapore office asks for [OC0051]:
   - a one-page study plan: why this program in Canada, why not a similar one at home, how it helps your job prospects at home, and your ties to Bangladesh; a CV (curriculum vitae, a summary of your education and work) if you left full-time study more than two years ago [OC0052];
   - notarized copies of diplomas and transcripts [OC0053];
   - original police certificates for every country you lived in six months or more since age 18 [OC0054];
   - bank statements showing at least 4 months of history and balance, with a written explanation of where the money came from [OC0055];
   - each parent's employment letter (employer, date of hire, position, salary) or two years of income tax records; for a parent's business, the registration, two years' business tax and audited accounts [OC0056];
   - IELTS Academic results from the last year, strongly recommended [OC0057].
6. Apply online in an IRCC account, with a scanner or phone camera for the documents and a credit or debit card; pay CAD 150 for the permit and CAD 85 for biometrics at the same time [OC0040, OC0043, OC0034, OC0035, OC0041].
7. Give biometrics: within 30 days of IRCC's Biometric Instruction Letter, book a free appointment with VFS Global and go to a VAC, Sunday to Thursday, 09:00 to 14:00 [OC0041, OC0049, OC0048, OC0047]:
   - **Dhaka:** 4th floor, Delta Life Tower, Plot 37, Road 90, North Gulshan Avenue, Gulshan-2 [OC0045];
   - **Chittagong:** 5th floor, World Trade Centre, 102-103 Agrabad C/A [OC0046];
   - **Sylhet:** Merchant Tower, East Mirabazar [OC0046].
   Biometrics given once last 10 years [OC0042].
8. Wait: IRCC's published processing time for study permits from Bangladesh was 4 weeks in November 2026 [OC0059, OC0062]. An interview may be required [OC0058].
9. If approved, IRCC asks for your passport through the VAC and puts a visitor visa in it, and sends a port-of-entry letter of introduction; the study permit itself is issued at the Canadian airport on arrival [OC0049, OC0063].

**If refused:** the letter gives the reasons, for example no valid acceptance letter, not enough money, a failed medical, or not convincing the officer that you mainly want to study and will leave at the end [OC0064]. Reapplying with the same information does not change the decision [OC0065]. Fees paid to a school before approval are paid at your own risk [OC0058].

Every document must be genuine and every answer true: a forged education certificate or hidden fact is misrepresentation (see Lying on an application) [OC0124, OC0126].

### Step 3: Study full time in Canada

1. Stay enrolled full time every semester; part-time is allowed only in the last semester [OC0073].
2. Study in class in Canada: for students whose study permit application or program start is on or after 1 September 2024, at least half the program must be done in class in Canada, and time studied outside Canada is cut from the post-graduation work permit [OC0077].
3. Attend: under the International Student Compliance Regime, institutions report twice a year whether students are attending classes as their permits require [XC0022]. In the figures published in March-April 2024, about 50,000 international students had come on study permits without starting their studies, and 7.5% of 11,948 Bangladeshi students never contacted their institution after arriving [XC0020, XC0021].

### Can you work?

1. Off campus: a full-time student in a post-secondary program of at least 6 months leading to a degree, diploma or certificate may work up to 24 hours a week during terms, without a work permit, once the program has started and with a social insurance number (SIN), the 9-digit number needed to work [OC0068].
2. During scheduled breaks such as summer and winter holidays, off-campus hours are unlimited [OC0069].
3. On campus (for the school, a faculty member, a student group or a business on campus), hours are unlimited, in term and in breaks [OC0072].
4. Students only in English or French language courses or courses needed to enter a program cannot work off campus [OC0071].
5. Working more than 24 hours a week in term breaks the permit conditions: you can lose student status, be refused future permits and have to leave Canada [OC0070].

**Finding work:** in April 2026 Statistics Canada put unemployment among 15- to 24-year-olds at 14.1%, more than double the 6.7% national rate; for South Asian youth, including Bangladeshis, it was 13%, against 11.2% for white youth [XC0121, XC0122]. Canada lost about 109,000 jobs in January and February 2026, half of them full time, including 64,000 held by young people [XC0123]. Analysts said employers now prefer experienced workers, and newcomers without professional networks often get no interview [XC0124, XC0125]. Ashiq Zaman, a Bangladeshi student at the University of Manitoba, said white classmates find jobs through references while South Asians send CVs to hundreds of places without reply [XC0126]. Competition is highest in Toronto, Montreal, Edmonton, Vancouver and Calgary, where many immigrants live, while smaller cities have fewer jobs [XC0127]. Part-time pay helps with daily costs but does not cover tuition, a Canada-based researcher wrote [XC0159].

### Step 4: Apply for the post-graduation work permit

The post-graduation work permit (PGWP) is an open work permit for graduates of Canadian institutions [XC0050]. It lasts as long as a program of 8 months to under 2 years, 3 years after a program of 2 years or more, and 3 years after any master's of at least 8 months [OC0078, OC0079]. It is issued only up to the passport's expiry date, so renew the passport first [OC0080]. Nobody gets a second PGWP [OC0076].

**You'll need:**
- proof of program completion (degree, diploma or transcript) [XC0058];
- proof of full-time enrolment [XC0058];
- proof that the program is eligible [XC0058];
- language test results from an approved test in English or French, no more than two years old: CLB (Canadian Language Benchmarks) 7 or the French equivalent, NCLC 7, in all four skills for degree graduates; CLB 5 or NCLC 5 for college graduates [OC0074, OC0075, XC0056];
- medical results, where needed [XC0058].

1. Apply online within 180 days of the school's confirmation that you completed the program, while your study permit is still valid; the fee is CAD 255 [OC0073, OC0081].
2. If the study permit has already expired, you have 90 days after expiry to apply for the PGWP and pay to restore your student status; you cannot work until approved, and after 90 days you must leave Canada [OC0082].
3. Medical problems, insufficient funds, a criminal record, a security risk or false information can make you inadmissible, and so can a family member's inadmissibility [XC0061].

### Bringing a spouse

Since the 2024 cap, only spouses of master's, PhD and postdoctoral students get open work permits [XC0006].

### Scams and mistakes

#### Done to students

- **IRCC's warning signs:** someone who offers a study visa without a DLI acceptance letter, sells an acceptance letter, offers cheaper tuition for a fee, charges to guarantee a scholarship, says every program leads to a work permit, or promises a job or permanent residence [OC0128]. Pay tuition only to the school, and have the school send the acceptance letter directly to you [OC0129].
- **Undeclared or unlicensed agents:** any person who helps with the application must be declared on it, paid or not; hiding one counts as lying [OC0130]. A paid consultant in Canada must be a member of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, a lawyer a member of a provincial law society; check the name on their websites [OC0131].
- **Fake colleges:** many Indian students found on arrival that their college was fake or lacked facilities, according to the Economic Times [XC0023]. One from Haryana dealt with his agent only by phone, and after he paid 4 lakh rupees the agent assured him he could pay the rest from part-time work; he had paid 4.2 lakh rupees of a 12 lakh rupee "tuition fee" before finding the college was an empty office [XC0026, XC0025, XC0024].
- **If it already happened:** a student who came to study genuinely, unknowingly used fake documents and has a removal order may get a temporary resident permit; contact IRCC's Client Support Centre and report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre [OC0134].

#### Done by students

- **Using the study permit to work:** some students used the student visa to get into Canada for work, exploiting the rule that tuition need not be paid in advance [XC0027]. Canada now counts students who never start (see Step 3: Study full time in Canada) [XC0022].
- **Using the study permit to claim asylum:** asylum claims by foreign students hit a record in 2024, and at least 10% of student visa holders were unaccounted for (see Asylum) [XC0031].
- **Reversing the fee payment:** cancelling a credit-card payment of the visa fee (a chargeback) is fraud and can bring a ban of up to 10 years [OC0127].

## Visit visa

The visit visa is the temporary resident visa (TRV), a sticker IRCC puts in the passport [OC0086]. After COVID-19 Canada issued visit visas in large numbers, and lakhs of people from Bangladesh, India and other countries got them with fake documents, a Canada-based journalist wrote [XC0036]. Canada now screens temporary visa applications more carefully, and entry is limited for anyone whose purpose is unclear or suspicious [XC0075]. (For people sold Canadian visit visas as a way to work, see "Visit and tourist visas sold as jobs" in the Illegal routes doc.)

### How to apply

Visit visa applications from Bangladesh are also handled by the Singapore visa office [OC0152].

1. Check you can convince an officer of three things: ties at home (job, home, assets or family) that will bring you back, that you will leave at the end of the visit, and enough money for the stay [OC0087].
2. Make sure your passport is valid for at least 6 months from the travel date [OC0088].
3. Gather the documents the Singapore office asks for [OC0089, OC0090, OC0091, OC0092, OC0093]:
   - copies of birth and, if married, marriage certificates [OC0154];
   - bank statements for the last 4 months and proof of income (employment letter, pay slips, business papers); if someone else pays, their signed letter, ID and a bank certificate, all dated within three months;
   - the Family Information Form (IMM 5707), filled in, dated and signed, required for residents of Bangladesh;
   - if visiting family or friends: the host's invitation letter with the purpose and length of the visit, proof of your relationship, and a copy of the host's Canadian citizenship or immigration status document;
   - if employed: a signed letter on company letterhead granting leave, with your position, salary and date of hire; if self-employed: business registration, licence and accounts;
   - provisional flight and hotel bookings.
4. Apply online and pay CAD 100 for the visa and CAD 85 for biometrics [OC0037, OC0035].
5. Give biometrics at a VAC in Dhaka, Chittagong or Sylhet within 30 days of IRCC's letter (see Step 2: Apply for the study permit, item 7) [OC0041, OC0044].
6. Wait: IRCC's published processing time for visitor visas from Bangladesh was 30 days in November 2026 [OC0060, OC0062].
7. If approved, IRCC asks for your passport; you cannot travel until the visa is in it [OC0095]. If refused, the letter in your IRCC account gives the reasons [OC0095].
8. At the border the officer decides how long you may stay, normally up to six months [OC0096].

A complete file does not guarantee a visa, and false statements or fraudulent documents mean immediate refusal and a possible 5-year ban [OC0094].

### Visa cancellation powers

Documents obtained by CBC News in 2025 show IRCC and CBSA officials set up a working group with US partners to find and cancel fraudulent visitor visa applications and to widen their powers to do so [XC0111]. The internal documents named India and Bangladesh as "specific challenging countries" and listed "visa holders of specific countries" among the cases where visas could be cancelled en masse [XC0112, XC0113]. In public, Immigration Minister Lena Diab cited only pandemics or war, and IRCC said the proposal was not aimed at specific groups; the power was put in Bill C-12 [XC0114, XC0117, XC0115]. More than 30 civil society groups objected, the Migrant Rights Network calling it a "mass deportation machine" [XC0116]. IRCC said tighter checks on TRV applications from "countries with the highest rates of abuse" had cut irregular crossings from the US into Canada by 97% since June 2024, and that visa cancellations for fraud were up 25% on a year earlier [XC0118, XC0119].

The power became law on 26 March 2026: when it is in the public interest, including fraud, IRCC may cancel, suspend or change a large group of visas or study and work permits, or pause applications, by an order in council that is published in the Canada Gazette [OC0104]. It does not touch asylum claims or permanent or temporary resident status [OC0105].

### World Cup visitors who claimed asylum

Canada issued 26,111 FIFA-related temporary resident permits or visas for the 2026 World Cup matches in Toronto and Vancouver, and by 20 July 2026, 175 of the holders had claimed asylum after arriving, according to IRCC data reported by The Globe and Mail [XC0129, XC0128]. Only 45 of the visas went to Bangladeshis, and 10 of them claimed asylum, about one in five [XC0131, XC0132]. Ghana and China had 25 claimants each; Egypt, Colombia and Kenya 15; Bangladesh, Nigeria and Senegal 10; Pakistan, Nepal, Burundi and Ecuador 5 [XC0130]. Canada's immigration authorities had warned that ticket holders at risk of not going home could be stopped at the border, and immigration analysts fear the claims will make visas harder for genuine fans at future events [XC0134, XC0133].

### Fake hosts and invitation letters: the CBSA case

1. **The case:** on 22 October 2026 CBSA announced charges against five people over a scheme that submitted 243 visitor visa (TRV) applications for 209 Bangladeshis, presenting Canadian citizens as hosts and using fraudulent documents and false statements [OC0136].
2. **The accused:** Rashel Mirza, Mohammed Aminul Islam, Md Mudassar Alam Bhuiyan and Kazi Nafeez Alam appeared at the Valleyfield courthouse in Quebec on 22 October 2026; the fifth, Md Rakibul Hassan, is reported to be in Bangladesh and is under an arrest warrant [OC0137]. Prothom Alo reported the first two live in Montreal and Bhuiyan in Toronto [XC0093].
3. **How it came out:** a CBSA check at a home in Mercier, Quebec on 3 July 2024 led to an investigation; CBSA searched four places in Quebec and Ontario on 3 June 2025 and focused on the 79 strongest applications [OC0138].
4. **The charges:** under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA), conspiracy to organise entry (section 117), inducing people to misrepresent facts (sections 126 and 128(a)) and inducing breaches of the Act (section 131); the court has yet to rule on them [OC0139].

CBSA warned that fake bank statements, fake invitation letters or false information must never be used, and that the applicant can face a five-year ban even if an agent prepared the application [XC0101, OC0125]. Anyone can report suspected immigration fraud to CBSA's Border Watch line, 1-888-502-9060 (Monday to Friday, 9 am to 9 pm Eastern time), without giving a name [OC0135].

## Work permit

Prime Minister Mark Carney said in September 2025 that the Temporary Foreign Worker Program must be restructured to match real labour demand in specific sectors and regions, and Canada cut its 2026 target for temporary foreign workers by 37% to 230,000 [XC0068, XC0065]. From January to August 2025 only 42% of the targeted temporary foreign workers arrived [XC0069]. The 2027-2029 plan sets 220,000 new workers a year [OC0018]. BMET cleared 147 Bangladeshi workers for Canada in 2025 and 64 in 2026 up to 23 November, spread over dozens of job titles (see Size) [OC0007, OC0009, OC0012]. How fake Canadian job offers are sold in Bangladesh is set out in the Dalal scams doc (see "Fake Australia and Canada work visas" in the Dalal scams doc).

### How a genuine work permit is issued

1. The Canadian employer checks whether the job needs an LMIA, applies for one if it does, and gives the worker the LMIA letter and its Annex A [OC0112]. The employer must not be on IRCC's list of non-compliant employers [OC0155].
2. The worker applies online to IRCC with the job offer and LMIA papers, pays CAD 155, and gives biometrics (CAD 85) at a VAC in Dhaka, Chittagong or Sylhet (see Step 2: Apply for the study permit, item 7) [OC0038, OC0035, OC0044].
3. IRCC's published processing time for work permits from Bangladesh was 30 weeks in November 2026 [OC0061, OC0062].
4. The permit lists the employer, location and job, and the worker must keep to them [OC0111].
5. The employer must give a signed employment agreement with the job, conditions, maximum weekly hours and pay; keep a copy [OC0118].

### Rights at work

Temporary foreign workers are covered by Canada's labour laws: the employer must pay for the work, keep the workplace safe and cannot take the worker's passport or work permit [OC0116]. Each province has an employment or labour standards office for pay, hours and conditions; the worker needs no permission to contact it and cannot be punished or deported for doing so [OC0117].

### Changing employer

1. Get a new job offer, then apply for a new employer-specific work permit from inside Canada [OC0114].
2. To start the new job before the decision, send IRCC's web form with the priority code PPCHANGEWORK2020 and the set text from IRCC's "Changing jobs or employers" page [OC0114].
3. If you lost the job, stay in Canada and do not work for anyone until the new permit or IRCC's work authorization email arrives [OC0115].

### If you are abused or not paid

1. In danger, call 911 [OC0132].
2. Report the employer to Service Canada online or on the 24-hour tip line 1-866-602-9448 (live agents in over 200 languages, Monday to Friday 6:30 am to 8 pm Eastern time); the employer is never told who reported [OC0119]. Abuse includes threats to your status, being kept at the work site, the employer keeping your passport, and wrong pay [OC0120].
3. If you are on a valid employer-specific permit, apply from inside Canada for the free open work permit for vulnerable workers, which lets you leave and work for almost any other employer; abuse includes a third party charging you for the job or making false promises [OC0121, OC0122, OC0123].
4. For trafficking, call the Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-833-900-1010 [OC0132].

### The Saskatchewan restaurant case

1. **The promise:** between 1 August 2022 and 31 March 2023, a woman who had worked about 18 years in a government job in Bangladesh was promised a restaurant job in Canada and permanent residence through the temporary foreign worker process [XC0105].
2. **What happened:** she was made to work at Sohel Haider's restaurant in Gull Lake, Saskatchewan, then at Mohammad Masum's restaurant in Tisdale, without a valid work permit for them; told her permit papers were being processed, she worked long hours six days a week without proper pay [XC0106, XC0107].
3. **The passport and the abuse:** Masum took her Bangladeshi passport and sexually assaulted her several times, which the judge called rape [XC0108].
4. **The way out:** when she got a different work permit for a restaurant in Elrose and arrived there, local Canadians who saw her condition helped her escape and get legal help [XC0109].
5. **The sentence:** on 22 October 2026 Saskatoon Provincial Court sentenced Masum, 45, to 14 years for human trafficking and three sexual assaults, and Haider, 56, to the minimum 4 years for human trafficking, and ordered them to pay her CAD 12,268 and CAD 5,885.16 [XC0103, XC0104].

## Permanent residence

Canada's 2026 target is 380,000 new permanent residents: 239,800 economic (64%), 84,000 family reunification and 56,200 refugee and humanitarian [XC0066]. The 2025 budget planned permanent residence for up to 33,000 temporary work permit holders in 2026 and 2027, with priority for rural and remote regions hit by labour shortages and tariff changes [XC0062, XC0063, XC0067]. The 2027-2029 plan keeps 380,000 a year, over 64% economic [OC0019]. In 2027 a one-off measure will move up to 200,000 people already in Canada to permanent residence: up to 100,000 workers nominated by provinces through extra Provincial Nominee Program places, and up to 100,000 in a humanitarian group [OC0020, OC0021].

## Lying on an application

Under IRPA, forging or altering a passport, visa, education certificate or similar document is misrepresentation, and so is hiding important information or giving false information [XC0078, XC0080]. IRCC treats as fraud any false or altered passport, visa, language result, job offer, acceptance letter, tuition receipt, transcript, diploma, birth, marriage or death certificate, or police certificate [OC0126]. The applicant is responsible for everything in the application even when an agent or consultant filled it in [OC0125].

**What can happen** if the applicant, the agent or an interpreter sends false documents or information [OC0124]:
- the application is refused;
- a ban of at least five years from Canada;
- a permanent record of fraud with IRCC;
- for someone already in Canada, loss of status and removal.

For someone already in Canada, criminal charges are also possible [XC0085]. Reversing a fee payment by chargeback can bring a ban of up to 10 years [OC0127].

**If you hold a fake document:** report it to CBSA's Border Watch line and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, and to local police [OC0133].

## Asylum

Asylum claims by temporary visa holders (visitors and students) fell from 10,095 in April 2024 to 1,580 in April 2026, an 84% drop, according to IRCC [XC0070]. People used to enter on a visit or student visa and claim asylum later; now any mismatch between the reasons given for the visa and the asylum claim is examined strictly [XC0071, XC0072]. IRCC says asylum is not a shortcut to immigrate or stay longer: people found not to need protection must leave, and lying on a claim can bring a ban on returning [OC0097]. From January to August 2026, asylum claims fell 52% from the same period of 2025 [OC0107]. Many visit-visa holders who claimed asylum after COVID-19 still had cases in the courts in June 2025 [XC0037]. Of the 45 Bangladeshis given visitor visas for the 2026 World Cup, 10 claimed asylum (see World Cup visitors who claimed asylum) [XC0131]. How false asylum stories are sold is covered in the Illegal routes doc (see "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the Illegal routes doc).

One Bangladeshi refugee claimant's first claim was rejected because it was filed through a fraudulent immigration consultant (see Crossing into the United States) [XC0046].

**Who is turned away before any hearing.** Under Bill C-12, law since 26 March 2026 and applied to claims made from 3 June 2025, a claim is not heard if it is made [OC0101, OC0098]:
- more than one year after the person first entered Canada after 24 June 2020, even if they left and came back;
- more than 14 days after crossing into Canada between official border posts on the US land border.
Also ineligible: anyone who ever made a claim in Canada that was rejected, withdrawn or abandoned, or claimed in a country that shares information with Canada [OC0099], and, under the Canada-US Safe Third Country Agreement, anyone arriving from the US at a land crossing, who is returned to the US unless an exception applies [OC0151]. Unaccompanied minors, and people who register their intention to claim in the online portal within their first year, are exempt from the one-year rule [OC0109]. People caught by the new rules still get a pre-removal risk assessment (PRRA) before removal [OC0102].

**How a claim works since 3 November 2026** [OC0106, OC0108]:
1. State the intention to claim in the IRCC Portal.
2. Attend the in-person appointment IRCC gives you, where the claim is formally made.
3. If the claim is eligible, submit the completed application and documents within 60 days (one 30-day extension on request).
4. At the Immigration and Refugee Board (IRB) hearing, prove a fear of persecution for race, religion, nationality, political opinion or social group, or a danger of torture or risk to life, and that your own authorities cannot or will not protect you [OC0100].

Claimants can get a free work permit after submitting a complete claim and passing the immigration medical exam [OC0110]. A claimant who goes back to Bangladesh before the decision has the claim treated as abandoned [OC0103].

## Irregular routes and undocumented stay

### Crossing into the United States

Many people who came on visit visas after COVID-19 crossed illegally into the US, a Canada-based journalist wrote, and Indian authorities said some students did the same [XC0037, XC0030]. A Global Affairs Canada document said stricter visa rules for travellers from Mexico, India and Bangladesh cut illegal crossings from Canada into the US by 84% between June and November 2024 [XC0032, XC0034].

Mahin Shahriar, a Bangladeshi-origin refugee claimant who his lawyer says has lived in Canada since 2019, shows how a crossing can end [XC0040, XC0045]:
1. On 12 May 2025 he followed directions sent to a friend's phone to an address near Montreal that was very close to the border, and crossed into the US without knowing it, he said [XC0042].
2. US ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) held him in Buffalo; his lawyer said ICE was ready to return him but CBSA did not approve his entry, and CBSA said every entry decision is made case by case [XC0040, XC0043, XC0044].
3. ICE could not send him to Bangladesh because he had no travel document, and US authorities accepted that he might be tortured there, his lawyer said [XC0047, XC0049].
4. His lawyer, Wasim Ahmed, said the Safe Third Country Agreement has a rule to return such an asylum seeker to Canada within 14 days, and asked the Federal Court for an urgent hearing on humanitarian grounds [XC0048, XC0045].

### Living without status

Without a work permit no one can work legally in Canada [XC0028]. A Prothom Alo article said health care in Canada is public, so a person without status who falls ill must go to public care, where police may catch them [XC0029].

## If a Bangladeshi dies in Canada

To send the body home, the family or friends get a free death certificate for repatriation from the Bangladesh High Commission in Ottawa, with its form, a copy of the deceased's passport, the Canadian death certificate, and the next of kin's address and phone number in Bangladesh [OC0149, OC0150].
