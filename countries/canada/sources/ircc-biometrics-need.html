Find out if you need to give biometrics

To be able to access all our features, you need a browser that supports JavaScript, and it needs to be enabled.

To find out if you need to give your biometrics, refer to Biometrics: Who needs to give their fingerprints and photo.

In most cases, you need to give biometrics when you apply for

  • temporary residence (except US nationals)
  • permanent residence
  • an extension of your stay in Canada, unless you’re from a visa-exempt country
  • refugee or asylum status

However, some applicants are exempt from giving biometrics.

Who doesn’t need to give biometrics

You don’t need to give biometrics if you’re

  • a Canadian citizen or citizenship applicant (including passport applicants)
  • a permanent resident, unless you’re in both of the following situations:
    • You’re applying to renew or replace your permanent resident card.
    • You were exempt from giving biometrics because of your age when you applied for permanent residence.
  • a visa-exempt visitor applying for an electronic travel authorization
  • a citizen of a visa-exempt country applying to extend your stay in Canada (visitor record)
  • a child aged 13 or under when you submit your application
  • aged 80 or over when you submit your application, unless you’re an asylum claimant
    • If you were 79 when you submitted your application and you turn 80 while your application is being processed, you must still give your biometrics.
  • a head of state or head of government coming to Canada temporarily
  • an applicant who qualifies for or holds a diplomatic or official visa
  • a valid US visa holder transiting through Canada to or from the US
  • applying for a visitor visa, a study permit or a work permit and you’ve already provided biometrics for a permanent residence application that’s still being processed
