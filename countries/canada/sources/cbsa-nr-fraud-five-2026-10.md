# **CBSA investigation leads to charges against five individuals involved in an immigration fraud scheme**

From: [Canada Border Services Agency](/en/border-services-agency.html)

## News release

CBSA investigation leads to charges against five individuals involved in an immigration fraud scheme

**October 22, 2026**\
**Valleyfield, Quebec**

An investigation by the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) has resulted in three charges being laid against four individuals and a fifth accomplice in connection with an immigration fraud scheme.

Rashel Mirza, Mohammed Aminul Islam, Md Mudassar Alam Bhuiyan, and Kazi Nafeez Alam appeared at the Valleyfield courthouse on October 22, 2026. The fifth person Md Rakibul Hassan is currently reported to be in Bangladesh, a warrant has been issued for his arrest. 

On July 3, 2024, the CBSA conducted an operation involving a compliance and control check on the occupants of a private residence in Mercier with officers and representatives of the Town of Mercier police department and fire department. Having noted a number of indications of non-compliance with the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Act,* the CBSA began an investigation into the activities of the accused in August of that year.

The investigation identified 209 Bangladeshi nationals for whom 243 temporary resident visa (TRV) applications had been submitted for the purpose of visiting Canadian citizens presented as hosts. The investigators found that an elaborate scheme had been established to enable these foreign nationals to obtain a TRV by means of fraudulent documents and false statements.

CBSA investigators executed four search warrants in Quebec and Ontario on June 3, 2025. The evidence gathered, including documents and computer equipment, pointed to the involvement of five individuals in carrying out the scheme. Given the scale and complexity of the scheme, the CBSA focused its efforts on the 79 most probative TRV applications.

As a result, the accused have been charged with:

- Conspiracy to organize or facilitate the coming into Canada of one or more persons, contrary to subsection 117(1) of the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Act* (IRPA), thereby committing an offence under subsection 117(3) of the IRPA;
- Knowingly inducing, aiding or abetting persons to misrepresent or hide material facts relating to immigration applications, contrary to section 126 of the IRPA, thereby committing an offence under paragraph 128(a) of the IRPA;
- Knowingly inducing, aiding or abetting persons to contravene the IRPA, contrary to section 131 of the IRPA, thereby committing an offence under subsection 117(3) of the IRPA.

The charges are subject to validation by the court.

## Quick facts

- The CBSA is responsible for identifying, investigating and pursuing the prosecution of individuals and entities connected to violations of the *Customs Act* and the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Act* (IRPA). 

- Individuals who violate Canada’s immigration laws are subject to serious consequences, including administrative actions and even criminal charges that can lead to fines, periods of probation or imprisonment, as well as a criminal record.

- In 2025, the CBSA opened 191 criminal investigations into suspected offences under the IRPA; and between January 1, 2026, and June 30, 2026, the CBSA opened 55 criminal investigations into suspected offences under the IRPA. 

- If you suspect immigration fraud—such as unauthorized work/study, living in Canada without valid status, or entry based on false documents—you can report it directly to the CBSA: 

  - - By phone: Call the Border Watch toll-free line at 1-888-502-9060 (available Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. ET).
    - Online: Report suspicious cross-border activities using the [CBSA secure web form](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/security-securite/bwl-lsf-eng.html).

  *Note: Information provided is confidential, and you are not required to give your name. *
