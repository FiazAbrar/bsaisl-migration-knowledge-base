# Who can apply

You can’t claim asylum if you have an active [removal order](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/security-securite/rem-ren-eng.html#s02) (opens in a new tab).

Before you start an asylum claim, check if it might be ineligible.

## What makes an asylum claim ineligible

Your asylum claim is ineligible if you:

- already have protected person status in Canada
- have ever made an asylum claim in Canada that was rejected, ineligible, withdrawn or abandoned
- have already made an asylum claim in a country that shares information with us
- can return to a country where you’re a Convention refugee
- are not allowed into Canada (inadmissible) for [any of these reasons](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/admissibility-enforcement/inadmissibility/reasons.html)  (opens in a new tab):
  - security
  - human or international rights violations
  - organized crime
  - serious crime
- enter Canada from the United States (US) [along the land border](#stca)
- make your claim more than one year after you first entered Canada after June 24, 2020—even if you’ve left and returned since then **[Some exceptions apply](#c12)**
- make your claim more than 14 days after you entered Canada in between ports of entry along the Canada–US land border [**Some exceptions apply**](#c12)

If you enter Canada from the US

- at an official land border crossing
- anywhere along the border, including rivers, lakes and other waterways, and start an asylum claim within 14 days of entry

your asylum claim is ineligible **and you’ll be returned to the US**, unless you meet an exemption or exception.

This is because of the Canada–US Safe Third Country Agreement (STCA), which helps both governments manage asylum claims along our shared border.

[Learn more about the STCA.](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/mandate/policies-operational-instructions-agreements/agreements/safe-third-country-agreement.html)

Exceptions to rules introduced on March 26, 2026

These rules don’t apply if

- you’re under 18 and don’t have a parent or legally responsible adult in Canada when you claim asylum
- you submit the start of your claim online before the one-year cut-off

### What happens to ineligible claims

Ineligible claims are sent to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) for next steps.

[Learn more about ineligible claims](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/refugees/asylum/ineligible.html).

## Show that you meet the definition of a refugee

Eligible claims get sent to the Immigration and Refugee Board (IRB), which schedules a hearing to decide if you need Canada’s protection.

During the hearing, you will need to prove that you are physically in Canada and that you meet the definition of a **Convention refugee** or a **person in need of protection**.

### A Convention refugee

If you’re a Convention refugee, it means you fear returning to your country because you will be persecuted for one of these reasons:

- race
- religion
- nationality
- political opinion
- being part of a particular social group

### A person in need of protection

If you are a person in need of protection, it means you fear returning to your country because:

- you’re in danger of torture
- there’s a risk to your life
- there’s a risk of cruel and unusual treatment or punishment

Also, as a **Convention refugee** or **person in need of protection**:

- you have to prove authorities in your country can’t or won’t protect you
- you may have to show you can’t safely live in another part of your country
