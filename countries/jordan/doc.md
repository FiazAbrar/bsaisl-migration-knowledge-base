# Jordan: migration guide

How to go to Jordan from Bangladesh, visa by visa, step by step, for Bangladesh passport holders, as of November 2026. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed. "You'll need:" lists what to have before starting a procedure.

JD is the Jordanian dinar; Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000). Jordan's work-permit rules are quoted from the Ministry of Labour's (MOL) Comprehensive Guide of Principles for Issuing Work Permits (Arabic edition of 20 August 2026, English edition of 3 July 2025) and its domestic worker regulation [D0096, A0108, A0123].

*Money in this doc.* JD 1 is about Tk 170, the rate BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government-owned recruiting agency) uses in its 2026 Jordan notices [A0018]. The Central Bank of Jordan has kept the dinar fixed to the US dollar since 1995, at about JD 0.709 to USD 1 (so JD 1 is about USD 1.41), and Bangladesh Bank's rate on 19 October 2026 was USD 1 = Tk 123.65 [A0017, KA0001, A0016]. Amounts are given in the currency the source uses.

## Background

### In one minute

Jordan is a women's corridor: of the 13,829 workers BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared for Jordan from 1 January to 8 November 2026, 12,784 were women, and Jordan is the second destination for Bangladeshi women after Saudi Arabia [A0001, A0003]. The main route is BOESL, whose biggest market is Jordan's export garment factories, which sell to the United States under a free trade agreement; Bangladeshis are over half of the migrant workers in that industry [A0061, A0062, A0022, A0019]. Since 1 June 2026 Jordan takes new workers from abroad only for these garment factories, homes, specialised skills and a few other cases [A0125, D0096]. BOESL tests women on sewing machines in Dhaka and sends those selected almost free because the factory pays [A0078, A0071]. The pay is set by the garment sector's collective agreement: JD 230 a month, of which a migrant gets at least JD 130 in cash and JD 100 is counted as the food and dormitory bed the factory provides [A0023]. Jordan raised its national minimum wage to JD 290 in 2025 but left garment workers and foreign domestic workers out [A0024]. A garment worker can move only to another garment factory, and anyone caught working without a valid permit or for a different employer is deported [A0108, A0135]. The traps are brokers who sell places in BOESL's test, fake "garment visas" sold in a real factory's name for Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh, and housemaid jobs that end in confinement or trafficking [A0104, A0156, KA0007, A0145].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

#### Status

*9 November 2026: open for garment and domestic work, closed to new recruits for most other jobs.* MOL stopped recruitment of foreign workers from abroad in most sectors from 1 June 2026 [A0125]. Employers may now recruit from abroad only for garment and textile factories set up or registered in development zones, Qualified Industrial Zones (QIZ, the export-garment zones) or free zones; women domestic workers (men under separate conditions); specialised-skill professions; firms contracted to the Jordanian-Palestinian agricultural marketing company; and a replacement for a worker who has left Jordan for good [D0096, A0126, A0127]. Workers already in Jordan legally keep their jobs and renew their permits [A0128]. BMET's 2026 clearances to 8 November (13,829) are already above the whole of 2025 (13,375) [A0001, A0005].

From 28 February to the end of March 2026, during the Iran war, Iran fired at least 262 missiles and drones at sites in Jordan; most international flights to Jordan stopped, but Amman airport stayed open [A0138, A0140, A0139].

#### The channels

Three routes take Bangladeshis to work in Jordan:
- garment and factory jobs in the export zones, almost all for women machine operators, through BOESL or private agencies (see Work visa: garment and factory worker) [A0057, A0078, A0084];
- domestic work in Jordanian homes, recruited through Jordanian offices licensed by MOL (see Work visa: domestic worker) [A0132];
- other work permits, now mostly closed to new recruits from abroad; the free work permit for day labourers in farming and construction is issued only to workers already in Jordan (see Other work permits) [A0125, D0096, D0118].

#### Which channel most people use

Jordan is BOESL's main market: 1.06 lakh of the 1.43 lakh workers it sent abroad in 14 years to mid-2024 went there, and of the 82,668 it had sent by mid-2022 all but 902 were women [A0061, G0218]. 42 garment companies in Jordan hire through it [A0062]. BMET's permission letter (its approval for an agency to recruit for one job order) for a BOESL Jordan order names the factory, the block of visas and the wage; private agencies get them too, such as Al-Sourav Overseas' 2023 letter for 100 men at Friends Apparels in Aqaba [A0100, A0101, A0084]. BMET's miscellaneous-information page lists these letters by memo and RL (recruiting licence) number, not by country, so a search there for "Jordan" or "জর্ডান" returns nothing [A0162, A0163]. The biggest garment groups pick workers by practical test and interview at BMET's training centres in Dhaka, among them Classic Fashion, Aseel Universal Garments and Rainbow Textile, and Classic Group employed more Bangladeshi women than any other garment group in Jordan in 2021 [A0051, A0052].

#### Who qualifies

BOESL's November 2026 notice takes women aged 20 to 35 who can run plain and overlock sewing machines, and its March 2026 notice tested only women, in person at the Bangladesh-Korea and Mohila (women's) TTCs (Technical Training Centres) in Mirpur, Dhaka [A0072, A0080, A0172]. Its November 2026 notice for Tusker Apparel takes no one who has worked abroad before [A0076]. The age on a passport is not always true: in all three suspected child-labour cases Better Work Jordan (the programme of the ILO, the International Labour Organization, and the IFC, the World Bank's International Finance Corporation, that inspected garment exporters) found in 2024, the passports had falsified dates of birth, and a worker told the ILO in 2018 she had changed the age on hers at 16 [A0047, A0088]. Who qualifies for domestic work is under Work visa: domestic worker [A0132].

#### Who is there

BOESL said in February 2026 that about 30,000 Bangladeshi women work in Jordan's garment sector [A0158]. The exporting garment industry employed about 84,100 workers in mid-2025, three-quarters of them migrants on two- to three-year contracts, and women are over 75% of its production workforce [A0020, A0021]. Government sources counted about 75,000 Bangladeshi women and 25,000 men in Jordan in 2020 [A0143]. Of about 50,000 domestic workers in Jordan in 2014, around 30,000 were Bangladeshi, and the Bangladesh Embassy estimated 50,000 Bangladeshi domestic workers in 2017 [A0147, A0150]. At the end of 2025, 12% of the 337,103 non-Jordanians with valid work permits, about 40,000 people, were Bangladeshi, against 50% Egyptian [R0192].

#### The size of the flow

From 2004 to May 2025, 235,292 workers went to Jordan, 1.69% of all Bangladeshi overseas employment; 212,667 of them were women, 17% of all Bangladeshi women sent abroad in that time [A0014, A0015]. Jordan was BMET's seventh destination in 2024, 2025 and 2026 to date, and its second destination for women every year from 2023 [A0008, A0005, A0001, A0011, A0009, A0006, A0003]. It took 19% of the women BMET cleared in 2025 and about 24% of those cleared in 2026 to 8 November [A0006, A0007, A0003, A0004]. #### Other origin countries

In the export garment industry, Bangladeshis are over half of the migrants, followed by Indians, Sri Lankans, Nepalis and workers from Myanmar, with about 450 Syrians [A0019]. India and Sri Lanka send fewer workers than Bangladesh but more skilled staff and professionals [A0059]. Indian workers were the most likely to have paid recruitment fees in 2024 [A0027]. When Indian, Nepali and Sri Lankan workers could not fly during COVID-19, factories leaned heavily on Bangladeshis [A0053, A0169]. Egyptians held 133,650 valid work permits in August 2023, mostly in farming and then construction [A0142, A0141]. MOL counted about 327,000 valid permits for non-Jordanians in June 2026 [A0130].

#### What has closed it before
- **2006 to 2010.** Jordan banned recruiting Bangladeshi workers in 2006 and reopened in 2010 for women garment workers only [A0065].
- **Late 2011.** After reports of sexual abuse of women workers and strikes, which Jordan saw as a threat to its US trade agreement, it tightened again and departures fell from 700-800 a month to under 100 [A0066, A0067].
- **November 2014.** Bangladesh's embassy in Amman suspended sending domestic workers in protest at a Jordanian insurance decision [A0148].
- **2020.** Clearances fell to 3,769 [A0014].
- **July 2022 to June 2023.** Garment orders fell after the Russia-Ukraine war and BOESL's garment departures more than halved, to 7,014 [A0054, A0055].
- **May 2025.** MOL stopped recruitment from abroad after a three-month window, except for domestic workers [A0131, A0132].

#### Where it may grow

In May 2025 Bangladesh's Expatriates' Welfare Adviser asked Jordan's Labour Minister to take more Bangladeshi men in agriculture, tourism and construction [A0159]. In March 2026 BOESL called for 5,000 women garment workers for Jordan at no cost [A0078]. Bangladesh and Jordan signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) on manpower in 2012, under which recruitment follows the legal procedures of both countries [KG0005].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A Jordan garment job starts as a block of visas a factory gets within its quota of foreign workers, and reaches the worker through BOESL's test in Dhaka, where officially nobody takes a cut; brokers sell access around it [A0111, A0102, A0071, A0104]. The choke points are BOESL's own selection for garments and the Jordanian recruitment office for domestic work [A0074, A0132].

#### How a job becomes a visa

MOL lets each sector recruit within quotas for Jordanian and foreign workers, and garment factories may hire up to 75% of their workforce from abroad [A0111, A0049]. The factory's approved block appears in BMET's permission letter: for example block visa JO_1315, 125 visas issued on 21 April 2025 to Jerash Garments & Fashion Manufacturing in Amman, recruited by BOESL [A0102, A0101]. QIZ employers must keep an annual bank guarantee with MOL to secure workers' rights, and must send recruited workers home when the contract ends [A0110, A0109]. Each step, from the notice to the flight, is in Work visa: garment and factory worker [A0078].

#### Who sells, and how
- **Brokers selling a place in BOESL's test.** In 2018 a dalal (broker) asked one woman Tk 1.3 lakh for a Jordan garment job through BOESL and took Tk 65,000; a worker alleged that dalals bribe BOESL interviewers and tell their candidates to wear a set nail-polish colour [A0104, A0087]. BOESL says it has no agents or sub-agents, chooses workers itself and takes no cash [A0074, A0075].
- **Fake garment visas.** In June 2026 the police CID (Criminal Investigation Department) arrested three at Surah Travels and Traders in Uttar Badda, Dhaka, which had no recruiting licence, used the name and seal of Jerash Garments of Amman and took Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person [A0156, A0157, A0160]. It came to light when the factory complained through the Bangladesh Embassy in Jordan [A0161].
- **Private agencies with a real permission.** Al-Sourav Overseas' 2023 permission let it charge each man Tk 1,74,300, by cheque, bank draft or pay order, for a JD 125-a-month machine-operator job in Aqaba [A0084, A0085].
- **Domestic work.** A Jordanian household recruits through an MOL-licensed office, whose guarantee for the worker lasts 90 days from her arrival [A0132, A0137]. Women report paying Dhaka agencies Tk 20,000 to Tk 1,20,000 for Jordan housemaid jobs [A0097, A0096, A0099]. A woman recruited by a fellow villager, who never knew which agency sent her, was taken from Amman airport and sold on a two-year "contract" to a man who sent women to hotels and houses (2020) [A0144, A0145].

#### The free work permit

MOL's free work permit for day labourers in farming, construction, loading and housing projects is issued only to a worker already in Jordan and lets the holder work for any employer in the same trade; it costs JD 800 a year plus JD 50 and is issued only through an entity MOL designates [D0118, A0120, D0121, A0122].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

Through BOESL a garment worker should pay almost nothing, but workers have reported paying Tk 40,000 to 60,000 in all and brokers' clients far more; she then earns JD 130 a month in cash plus food and a dormitory bed, more with overtime [A0071, A0089, A0104, A0023].

#### The cap
- **BOESL garment jobs.** In BOESL's November 2026 notice the company bears all costs; the worker pays Tk 200 for fingerprints and Tk 200 for PDO (pre-departure orientation), repaid by the company before departure [A0071]. A July 2026 notice also charged Tk 100 to apply online and Tk 1,000 for the medical [A0082, A0081].
- **Private agency, men.** Tk 1,74,300 in Al-Sourav's 2023 permission [A0085].
- **Women domestic workers.** The agencies the Ministry approved in September 2025 to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from them [A0106].

#### What people really pay
- Through BOESL, Tk 14,000 to 25,000 in 2018, part of it refunded after arrival [A0103].
- Women in Jordan's garment factories, through BOESL or a broker, Tk 40,000 to 60,000 and sometimes Tk 80,000 (2023), for passport, medical and urine tests, fingerprints and personal shopping [A0089].
- Through a broker, Tk 65,000 (2018) [A0104].
- For a fake garment visa, Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh (2026) [A0156].
- For a housemaid job, Tk 20,000 to Tk 1,20,000 [A0096, A0097].

#### How they pay

BOESL takes no fee before final selection and all fees go through a bank [A0075]. Private agencies must take the permitted amount by cheque, bank draft or pay order [A0085]. Women in Jordan's garment factories who borrowed reported interest as high as Tk 100 a day, and loans from the broker himself were the hardest to repay [A0090, A0091].

#### What the job pays
- **The floor.** The garment collective agreement sets JD 230 a month: at least JD 130 cash and JD 100 in kind for housing and food [A0023]. The national minimum of JD 290 from January 2025 does not apply to garment workers or foreign domestic workers [A0024]. Under the 2025 agreement migrants earning JD 230 or more got no rise, and the union signed it without consulting its members [A0025, A0026].
- **BOESL's notices.** JD 130 basic in March 2026; USD 183.35 basic in November 2026 [A0018, A0072].
- **What workers report.** Women told at BOESL training they would get JD 125 to 150 received JD 99 to 130 (2023) [A0092]. An operator's basic was about Tk 18,000, or Tk 25,000 to 30,000 with overtime (2023) [A0058].

At JD 130, about Tk 22,000, a Tk 40,000 to 60,000 recruitment cost equals two to three months' cash pay [A0023, A0018, A0089].

#### Overtime and hours

Jordan's labour law sets no daily or weekly limit on overtime; the sector agreement caps it at four hours a day, a 72-hour six-day week [A0040]. In 2024, 54% of factories broke that cap and migrant workers averaged 59 hours a week [A0041, A0039].

#### Deductions

Wage information, use and deductions was the most common pay violation in 2024, found in 29% of factories [A0044]. Because social security is counted on a lower base wage, a migrant garment worker on a three-year contract effectively forgoes 11.75% (JD 25.85) of each month's earned wage, by an ILO calculation in 2022 [A0094]. When she leaves Jordan for good the SSC (Social Security Corporation) pays her a lump sum (see Leaving at the end of the job, step 5) [KC0074, KC0075].

#### Food and the dormitory

Factories must house all migrant workers, mostly in dormitories [A0037]. In 2024, 66% of factories were cited on dormitory safety and health, and 36% for lacking cooking space, so workers cook in their rooms, a practice linked to dissatisfaction with factory food [A0036, A0038].

#### Money sent home

Bangladeshis in Jordan sent USD 127.87 million in the year to June 2023 (see Sending money home) [A0060].

### What ties you to the employer

A Jordan work permit ties you to one employer and, in garments, to one sector: a QIZ garment worker can move to another garment factory but not out of garments, and any transfer needs the permit cancelled at the labour directorate in front of both worker and employer [A0108, A0118]. Better Work Jordan calls this the kafala (sponsorship) system, which limits switching employer or leaving without repercussions [A0034].

- **Entering.** You enter on the factory's or household's approved recruitment, and the employer pays the ticket to Jordan [A0102, A0170, A0124].
- **Renewing.** Workers in Jordan legally renew their permits even under the 2026 recruitment stop; how is under Your work and residence permits [A0128].
- **Quitting and changing employer.** A transfer is done by cancelling the permit in the presence of worker and employer; garment workers can move only within garments, and holders of a free work permit can leave that category only for a specialised-skills permit [A0118, A0108, A0173]. The steps are under Changing employer or leaving [A0118].
- **Leaving Jordan.** The QIZ employer must send you home at the end of the contract, and BOESL's notices put the return ticket after three years on the employer [A0109, A0170]. A domestic worker's employer pays her return ticket [A0124].

#### Which law covers you

Factory jobs follow Jordan's labour law and the garment sector agreement; domestic workers have their own regulation (No. 90 of 2009), which sets at most eight working hours a day [A0171, A0040, A0123].

> **Need to know**
> - Is a QIZ garment worker who leaves before her contract ends barred from coming back to Jordan, and for how long?

#### Absconding reports

MOL calls leaving work without notice "desertion"; in Jordan such workers are called "runaways" [A0114, A0168]. The employer can report a worker who leaves, and a worker MOL finds has left the employer is deported; garment factories in development zones are exempt from the cap on how many workers an employer may report, and a worker can also be deported for refusing to work [A0113, KC0037, A0116, A0119]. The rules and what to do are under If your employer reports you absent ("absconding") [KC0097].

#### Working without a valid permit

Any worker caught without a valid permit, or working for a different employer or in a different job, is deported, and MOL deported 7,000 such workers from 2025 to March 2026 [A0135, A0129]. A worker under a deportation decision cannot be recruited to Jordan again unless the status is corrected [A0112]. Until 1 December 2026 a Cabinet regularisation lets workers in breach in most sectors, domestic workers included, put their papers right at half the past permit fees or leave Jordan for good with past fees and fines waived, but it leaves out most zone garment workers; the terms are under Going home without valid papers, The 2026 regularisation [KA0002, KA0004, KA0005]. MOL estimates up to 500,000 undocumented foreign workers in Jordan [D0174].

#### Passports kept by employers

In 2024 Better Work Jordan found passport confiscation in three factories, and passport confiscation was the second most common complaint from women workers in 2022 to Tamkeen Fields for Aid (Tamkeen), a Jordanian legal-aid group for migrant workers [A0031, A0154].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

Withheld wages and confiscated passports are the most common complaints; inside the factories the problems are long overtime, verbal abuse, poor dormitories and sexual harassment that workers fear reporting [A0153, A0154, A0041, A0070, A0036, A0043].

#### What workers complain about
- Tamkeen received 291 complaints from women workers in 2022: 139 about withheld wages, 114 passport confiscation, 76 each about lost days off and long hours, 75 no social security and 69 unpaid overtime; Bangladeshis filed 23 [A0153, A0154, A0155].
- In 2024 Better Work Jordan found forced labour in five factories, including passport confiscation, bonded labour and refusal to let a worker end her contract; half the cases were in Aqaba's smaller factories, where some workers left for informal work [A0031, A0032, A0033].
- Cases of withheld documents and suicides among migrant garment workers drew public concern in 2024 [A0035].
- 47% of factories were cited for verbal abuse, and over 20% of workers felt it extremely risky to report quid-pro-quo sexual harassment [A0070, A0043].
- Termination violations rose to 20% of factories in 2024, and once a worker has left Jordan nobody can check whether she was really paid her final dues [A0046, A0045].
- During COVID-19 lockdowns some workers got half pay or none [A0095].
- MOL's online complaint platform received 6,938 labour complaints in 2024, 3,171 of them about unpaid wages, 216 overtime, 47 forced labour and 11 kept documents [D0157].

Better Work Jordan, which inspected the garment exporters, paused its operations on 22 June 2025 for lack of funding and was still paused in November 2026; in July 2026 MOL and the ILO agreed new ILO support for the garment sector (see Work visa: garment and factory worker, Step 6, item 8) [A0048, KB0011, KB0009].

#### What people wrongly believe

*"BOESL jobs are free, so the broker must be the one who gets you through."* BOESL says it has no agents or sub-agents, selects workers itself and takes no cash [A0074, A0075]. Brokers still sell places, claiming they can pay off interviewers (2018) [A0104, A0087].

*"A visa in a real factory's name is a real job."* Surah Travels sold Jordan jobs in Jerash Garments' name with no licence and no link to the factory [A0156, A0160]. A real Jordan garment job comes with a BMET permission letter naming the agency, employer, number of posts and wage (see Work visa: garment and factory worker) [A0101, A0100].

*"Jordan's minimum wage is JD 290."* Not for garment workers or foreign domestic workers [A0024]. A garment worker's JD 230 includes JD 100 in kind for food and housing, so the cash floor is JD 130 [A0023].

*"Overtime is optional."* BOESL's notices call it voluntary [A0073]. Migrant garment workers averaged 59 hours a week in 2024 and 54% of factories broke the overtime cap [A0039, A0041].

*"If the factory is bad, leave and find work outside."* Garment workers cannot move out of the garment sector, and workers caught working for another employer are deported [A0108, A0135].

#### How the irregular routes end
- **Leaving the employer.** Workers caught are detained and referred to the security services before deportation (2017), and 7,000 were deported from 2025 to March 2026 [A0151, A0129]. Bangladeshi domestic workers were the largest group leaving employers; one who left after a month lived on temporary jobs until illness confined her to bed, then waited for a pardon and deportation (2017) [A0149, A0166, A0167, A0174].
- **Trafficking.** The woman sold on arrival in 2020 was beaten when she refused sex work, and was rescued by the embassy after the Bangladeshi rights group Ain o Salish Kendra wrote to the Foreign Ministry and the Bangladesh ambassador (see Work visa: domestic worker, Scams) [A0164, A0165, A0146].

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

Jordan's Ministry of Interior (MOI, which issues visas and residence permits) sorts nationalities into "restricted" countries, whose nationals need MOI's prior approval before they travel, and non-restricted ones; Bangladesh is on the restricted list [V0001, V0002]. So every route below that skips prior approval is closed to a Bangladeshi passport holder living in Bangladesh:

- **Visa on arrival and MOI's instant e-visa** (bought online and printed before travel; JD 40 for a three-month single-entry visa): only for foreigners who do not need prior approval [V0004, V0005, V0006].
- **Jordan Pass** (the Ministry of Tourism's prepaid ticket to over 40 tourist sites, which also waives the visa fee for a stay of at least three nights): not sold to Bangladeshi nationals [V0012, V0013, V0010].
- **Tourist groups without prior approval:** since October 2021 only groups of at least five from Nigeria, Libya, the Philippines, Colombia and Pakistan, on an approved programme [V0014].
- **Visa at a Jordanian embassy or on arrival for nationalities taken off the list** (Albania and Moldova in 2021): Bangladesh was not taken off [V0015, V0002].

Since January 2025 foreign nationals living in a Gulf country, the US, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea or Europe with a residence there valid at least four months may enter without prior approval, but in 2022 Jordan's embassy in Japan excluded Bangladeshi passport holders from the then version of this exception for residents of Japan (see Entry without prior approval: Gulf residents and Schengen or US visa holders) [KE0006, KE0010].

## Work visa: garment and factory worker

This is the main route to Jordan (numbers under Background) [G0105]. The factory gets a recruitment approval from MOL at the Investment Window, pays the work permit fee in advance and gets the entry visa through MOI; in Bangladesh the worker passes BOESL's sewing test, gives fingerprints, takes the medical and the PDO, and gets BMET's emigration clearance; in Jordan the factory completes the work permit and residence after a medical there [G0052, G0056, G0071, G0008, G0005, G0166, G0058]. On BOESL's notices the factory pays almost everything, including the airfare both ways [G0003, G0005]. A licensed private agency can also hold a Jordan garment job order, as Al-Sourav Overseas (RL1028) did for 100 male machine operators in 2023, and may then charge the worker up to the amount the Ministry sets [G0042, G0045].

### Who pays what

#### Before paying: what the law allows

- **Jordan:** to recruit for a garment factory under the QIZ rules [G0217], the employer must file a certificate from the home country's labour ministry that the worker was recruited through a licensed agency and paid no fees beyond those its law allows, and a copy of the newspaper job advert stating the same [G0053, G0054].
- **The work permit fee** is JD 225 a year for a worker of a garment company registered in a development zone (JD 350 in other sectors), plus a JD 50 surcharge collected "from the employer or the worker, as applicable" [G0063, G0064].
- **The factory sector's own rule:** Better Work Jordan's zero-recruitment-fee policy, endorsed by Jordan's national stakeholders, took effect on 1 January 2019 [G0077].
- **Getting home:** the employer must return the worker to Bangladesh when the contract ends, and backs this with a bank guarantee that also covers unpaid rights and fines for a residence permit it failed to renew [G0061, G0062].
- **Who pays the permit:** under the sector's unified contract the employer gets the work and residence permits for the whole contract without any cost to the worker [H0085].

#### The Bangladesh maximum

The government may fix by order the most a worker pays to go abroad [G0129]. What it set for Jordan depends on the channel:

- **Through BOESL:** the Jordanian employer pays BOESL's service charge, the emigration tax, 15% VAT, BOESL's registration fee, life insurance and the welfare fee; the worker pays at most the medical (Tk 1,000), the PDO (Tk 305, Tk 200 on one), fingerprints (Tk 200 or 220) and, on some notices, Tk 500 for the undertaking stamp [G0005, G0013, G0016, G0023, KB0003]. On some notices the factory repays even these, before departure or in Jordan [G0023, G0013].
- **Through a private agency:** in the Ministry's 2023 permission for Al-Sourav Overseas, the agency could take Tk 1,74,300 from each male machine operator, only after the visa and flight were confirmed [G0045].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a current general government ceiling for Jordan for private agencies, or is it set letter by letter, as for Al-Sourav [G0045, G0129]?

#### How to pay

- **BOESL:** nothing is paid to BOESL until you are finally selected, every fee goes through a bank, and BOESL takes no cash from anyone [G0010, G0018].
- **A private agency:** by cheque, bank draft or pay order, which the agency must show BMET [G0045]; keep every receipt and don't pay middlemen [G0162, G0163].

**What happens in practice:** in 2022-23 BOESL recruits said they spent Tk 40,000 to 60,000 in all, some about Tk 80,000, mostly on passports, the medical and urine test, fingerprints at BOESL and shopping [G0092, G0091]. Some borrowed at up to Tk 100 a day in interest [G0095].

### Step 1: Find a real job

1. **Register on OEP.** Agencies must pick workers from BMET's database, and BMET gives no clearance to anyone outside it [G0155, G0154].

   You'll need [G0157, G0158]:
   - your NID (national ID card);
   - a mobile phone that can receive codes;
   - a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-banking account to pay.

   1. In Google Chrome open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp (or scan the QR code on BMET's guide) and press "অনুগ্রহ করে এখানে ক্লিক করুন" (Please click here) twice [G0158].

   3. Type the OTP (one-time code) sent to your mobile and press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue), and fill in the full form [G0158].

   5. Download your Registration Card [G0158].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What is the OEP registration fee in 2026 [G0157]?

   **If it fails:** call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [G0118], or Probash Bondhu, the 24-hour call centre of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB, the government's welfare body for migrant workers): 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [G0119].

2. **Get the skills and a certificate.** Most BOESL Jordan notices want experienced machine operators: real factory experience, skill on two machines (plain and overlock, for example; flatlock, overlock and feed-of-the-arm for Mas Al Safi) and, on one, two years' experience [G0002, G0012, G0022, KB0001]; the March 2026 call for 5,000 women also took semi-skilled machinists [G0024, G0025]. BOESL gives priority to holders of a certificate in the trade from a BMET-run TTC [G0009].

   You'll need: your OEP registration, and a current photo in your OEP profile that matches your face today [G0159].

   1. Log in on OEP with the code sent to your mobile, open Training portal from your profile, then Training, then Trade/Skills; choose your division, district and training centre and press Search [G0159].
   2. Pick the trade and press Apply; check your photo, press Continue, then Process to Enroll and Confirm; "My Course" lists your applications [G0159].
   3. Pay the fee BMET sets for short courses against a money receipt; courses run by development partners or projects are free and pay a monthly stipend [G0160].

   **Scams:** if anyone offers to get you into or through a TTC, or to get your clearance, for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement office [G0161].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which TTCs run a sewing machine operation course, and does BOESL treat that certificate as passing its own test [G0009]?

3. **Find a BOESL Jordan notice.** BOESL posts every notice on boesl.gov.bd [G0106]; Jordan garment notices are filed as "জর্ডানে কর্মসংস্থান সংক্রান্ত নোটিশ" (Jordan employment notice) [G0029]. In November 2026 the open Jordan notices were for five factories: Sana Garments, United Creation, Mas Al Safi, Prestige Apparel and Tusker Apparel [G0028]. A real notice gives:
   - the factory, the post and the number of posts: for example 28 sewing machine operators for Sana Garments, 25 for Prestige Apparel, 100 machine operators for Mas Al Safi [G0001, G0015, KB0001];
   - the monthly basic pay: JD 130, or USD 183.35 to 183.43 [G0012, G0015, KB0001, G0001, G0022];
   - who may apply: women aged 20-35 (21-39 on some), with an NID, and nobody with a court case in Bangladesh or Jordan [G0025, KB0002, G0002, G0004];
   - the terms: 8 hours a day, 6 days a week, voluntary overtime, a 3-year renewable contract, free housing, food, first aid and transport, and the factory pays the flight out and the flight home after three years [G0003];
   - what you pay, where and when the test is, and what to bring (sub-step 5) [G0005, G0006].

   Notices differ on returnees: Prestige's and Tusker's November 2026 notices take no one who has worked abroad before, yet both give priority to workers back from war-torn countries, and Tusker's July 2026 notice was only for women who had worked at Tusker in Jordan [G0017, KB0006, G0020, G0108].

4. **Apply online, if the portal is open.** BOESL's portal, brms.boesl.gov.bd, has been closed for all job applications since 11 October 2026 because of a technical fault; BOESL says to follow its notice board and Facebook page [G0030]. Its 9 November 2026 Mas Al Safi notice still says an online application gets you a chance at the interview, not a job [KB0005]. When it works:
   1. Open brms.boesl.gov.bd and click Register; enter your name, mobile number and a password, and confirm with the OTP sent to your mobile [G0031, G0032].
   2. Click "Go to Account" and log in with the same number and password [G0033].
   3. Complete your profile: name, parents' names, date of birth, passport details, marital status, address, CV, education, work experience and photo [G0034].
   4. Click "Start Applying" and apply for a job you qualify for [G0035].
   5. Pay the fee by Nagad, bKash or card, then print the CV and invoice the system gives you and take them to BOESL's interview [G0036]. The fee for the Tusker job in July 2026 was Tk 100, and applying does not guarantee an interview [G0107].

   > **Need to know**
   > - While the portal is closed, do walk-in candidates at the Friday tests need any application or receipt?

5. **Go to the test.** All five November 2026 notices set the interview and test for Friday 13 November 2026, by 8 am, at the Mohila (women's) TTC, Darus-Salam Road, Mirpur, Dhaka-1216 [G0006, G0014, KB0008, KB0007, KB0004]; the March 2026 call ran tests every Friday at 7 am from 3 April at the Bangladesh-Korea TTC and the Mohila TTC, Mirpur [G0026]. Representatives of the Jordanian factories take the interviews [G0102].

   Bring [G0007, G0006, G0021, KB0004, G0008, G0015]:
   - 4 colour passport-size photos, with a white background on most notices;
   - your original passport, valid at least 1 year, and all your passports if you have more than one;
   - copies of the passport photo page: 5 colour sets (Sana, United Creation) or 1 colour and 4 black-and-white sets (Prestige, Tusker, Mas Al Safi);
   - your education or experience certificates, if any;
   - a copy of your NID (voter ID);
   - the original ID card (or attendance card) of the factory where you work now.

   No CV is needed [G0019]. You must pass the trade test [G0008]. Recruits describe the BOESL interview, then several days at "the technical" (a sewing test and briefing, six days for one woman) [G0089]; some are thrown by how fast the test centre's machines run [G0205]. From test to visa most waited about three months, some nearly six [G0090].

   **For questions:** BOESL, 02-48319125, 02-48317515, 02-58311838, or Probash Bondhu, 16135 [G0006]; BOESL's office is on the 5th floor of Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka-1000, info@boesl.gov.bd, Sunday to Thursday 9 am to 4 pm [G0011].

6. **If a private agency offers the job, check the agency and the job order.**
   1. Check its licence on OEP's agency list, www.oep.gov.bd/agencies (free): find the row with its RL number and read the License Status (Active, Expired, Cancelled, Suspended or Server Locked) [G0120, G0122]. A real row reads, for example, "RL2434, BTS OVERSEAS LIMITED, … Active, 02/May/2029" [G0121]. A licence can show "Cancelled" while its validity date is years ahead, and recruiting on a cancelled licence carries 1 to 2 years' prison [G0123, G0124].
   2. Ask for BMET's Recruitment Permission Letter for the job order and read it [G0040, G0041]:
      - **permission ID** (for example JO-RL1028-2023-0010184), date and the number of workers allowed [G0040, G0042];
      - **block visa number, number of visas and visa issue date**: the visas the factory already holds in Jordan [G0042];
      - **employer name and city** (Amman, Aqaba) and **Attested: Yes** [G0042, G0037];
      - **job, number of posts, salary and contract length** (for example 125 sewing machine operators at 183.61 for 3 years) [G0038];
      - **F/H/M/A/O**, marked Y or N for each [G0041].
   3. Ask for the Ministry's permission letter behind it, which states the cost you may be charged and the conditions: for Al-Sourav, JD 125, 3 years, 3 months' probation, 8 hours a day, 1 day off a week, free medical care, housing, food and return airfare, and Tk 1,74,300 by cheque, bank draft or pay order [G0044, G0045].
   4. Check the agency has signed the undertaking, on Tk 300 non-judicial stamp paper, that you will get the job and pay promised and that it will pay your food, lodging and return if anything goes wrong [G0050].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What do F, H, M, A and O stand for on BMET's letters (food, housing, medical, airfare and overtime?) [G0041]?
   > - Where does BMET post Jordan permission letters now, given that its list's titles name only the memo and RL number?

   **If a broker brings you the job:** an agency may appoint only a registered person as its sub-agent; acting as one without registration carries 1 year's prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [G0126, G0127]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents at all [G0018].

### Step 3: The factory gets the approval, pays the permit fee and gets the visa

This happens in Jordan, between the factory, MOL and MOI [G0052, G0058].

1. **The factory applies to MOL.** Its representative files a written request with MOL's commissioner at the Investment Window, giving each worker's name as in the passport, date of birth, nationality and job, with the home-country certificate and newspaper advert (Who pays what) [G0052, G0053, G0054]. Garment factories are one of the few sectors allowed to recruit from abroad, and only into listed jobs such as machine operator, sewing, cutting, finishing, ironing, packing and quality inspection [G0067, G0068]. The commissioner decides within a week [G0055].
2. **It pays the work permit fee in advance.** After approval it pays the fee (JD 225 plus JD 50, Who pays what); the approval lasts four months, and the permit's year starts from your entry into Jordan [G0056, G0063, G0064].
3. **It gets your entry visa from MOI.** Jordan's visas are issued only through MOI's e-services, eservices.moi.gov.jo, and since July 2023 work visas and work residence are applied for online there [G0071, G0072]. BMET's letter records the factory's block visa number, the number of visas and their issue date [G0037].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Can a worker check her own Jordan work visa on eservices.moi.gov.jo, and with which number?
   > - If MOL refuses the factory's recruitment approval or MOI the visa, is there an appeal, and is the worker told?
   > - Is MOL's list of nationalities "restricted by the Residence Law", for which its approval lasts four months instead of two, the same as MOI's restricted list that includes Bangladesh [G0069, V0002]?

4. **The Bangladesh Embassy in Amman attests the demand.** On OEP's process the Bangladesh mission verifies the employer and attests the job demand and visa; BMET's letters then show "Attested: Yes", and the original demand letter and power of attorney must be filed at BMET before clearance [G0154, G0037, G0049].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How does the Embassy in Amman attest a garment factory's demand letter: papers, fee and time?
   > - Can a worker confirm with the Embassy that a factory's demand is real?

5. **If you don't travel,** the factory may replace you once within the four months, after cancelling your visa, for a JD 100 fee [G0057, G0065].

**Check the job is real:** have BMET check the visa and demand letter free within 3 working days: bring the visa and, where it applies, a copy of the passport page with the visa to BMET in Dhaka or your district employment and manpower office [G0188, G0189, G0190].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET's visa check cover Jordan visas (its online check lists only Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and the UAE) [G0188]?

### Step 4: Medical, fingerprints, PDO, contract and BMET clearance

Once selected you pay, through the bank, only the items on your notice (Who pays what) [G0010, G0005].

1. **The medical in Bangladesh** costs Tk 1,000 [G0005, G0016]. Recruits report a urine test as part of it [G0091]. Factories were cited for pregnancy-testing new recruits, and Jordan's garment exporters asked the Bangladesh Embassy and BOESL to end the practice; in 2024, 18% of factories were still cited for it [G0079, G0080].

   > **Need to know**
   > - At which centres is the Bangladesh medical for Jordan done, and what does it test?

2. **Fingerprints** cost Tk 200 or 220 and are taken at BOESL [G0023, G0005, G0091].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What are the fingerprints for: BMET's smart card, Jordan's visa, or both?

3. **Do the compulsory 3-day PDO** at a BMET training institute: apply on OEP and pay against a money receipt; BOESL's notices put the fee at Tk 305 (Tk 200 on one) [G0164, G0165, G0005, G0023].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What is the click path for booking the PDO on OEP?

4. **Read the contract before you sign.** Jordan's garment factories use one unified contract for migrants [G0076]. Under the sector's 2025 agreement the minimum pay is JD 230 a month, of which a migrant gets at least JD 130 in cash and JD 100 as housing and food [G0074]. With a private agency, the contract must be signed before clearance and you must be given a Bangla translation attested by BMET; check the pay, hours and leave against Your rights at work [G0048].

   **What happens in practice:** recruits say the contract was in English and not read to them, or only explained aloud at BOESL [G0097, G0210]; women told at BOESL they would get JD 125 to 150 were paid JD 99 to 130 [G0096].

5. **The undertaking.** Some notices charge Tk 500 for the undertaking stamp [G0005].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does the worker's undertaking (অঙ্গীকারনামা) for Jordan say, and who keeps it?

6. **BMET clearance (the smart card).** The smart card is your emigration clearance, and it makes you a member of the Welfare Board with its insurance [G0144, G0173].
   1. **The agency applies** (BOESL or a private agency) with the visa copy, the attested contract, an attested copy of your registration card, its undertaking on job certainty on Tk 300 stamp paper, the bank pay order for the welfare and smart card fees and the source-tax challan; BMET's emigration branch clears within 3 working days [G0166, G0167].
   2. **Fees:** welfare fee Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026; insurance premium Tk 1,000 once; clearance fee Tk 250; source income tax Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [G0172, G0173, G0168]. On BOESL's notices the factory pays the welfare fee, life insurance and emigration tax [G0005].
   3. **Check it is real** at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [G0144]; welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards before departure [G0176].

   **If it goes wrong:** BMET's Director (Emigration), +8802222222415, 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd; then the Additional Director General (Employment), adgemp@bmet.gov.bd [G0171].

7. **Know your insurance.** Workers who leave with BMET clearance are insured by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC, the state life insurer) for Tk 10 lakh for 5 years from the card's date, ages 18 to 55, for the one-time Tk 1,000 [G0173, G0142]. It pays on death and disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months, but not if you went entirely at the employer's cost [G0174, G0143].

   You can download the certificate from JBC's e-service; claims are under If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job [G0175].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does the job-loss cover exclude BOESL's garment workers, whose employer pays almost all costs [G0143, G0016]?

### Step 5: Loan and the flight

1. **If you need a loan,** PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank, the government bank for migrants) lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest a year, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days of complete papers [G0181]. Women have a separate loan on the same terms [G0148]. You must be 18 to 60 and not in default, and you and your guarantors must live permanently in the branch's area [G0178].

   You'll need [G0179, G0180]:
   - the free application form, 3 recent photos;
   - copies of your NID, passport, visa and BMET card, and the contract if you have one;
   - 3 signed MICR (machine-readable) cheque leaves from your own active bank account, with its statement;
   - a PKB savings account opened with at least Tk 500;
   - two guarantors, each with a photo, NID copy and proof of address: one financially solvent person (with 3 signed cheques, a statement and proof of income) and one family member.

   1. Apply at the PKB branch for your permanent address, or online on mygov.bd, uploading your photo, passport, visa, BMET card and NID and your guarantors' NIDs and photos; you pay nothing to submit [G0178, G0146, G0147].
   2. After approval, pay processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each plus VAT, Tk 900 per lakh for risk cover, and stamps [G0182].

   **If it goes wrong:** PKB helpline 16238, +880 9677-787878 from abroad [G0182]; loans department 01716-175560, migration@pkb.gov.bd [G0183]; grievance officer, gm2@pkb.gov.bd, +88-02-48321195 [G0184].

2. **The flight** is paid by the factory [G0003, G0027]. With a private agency, a representative must fly with each group to Amman or meet it at the airport, and must report each group's arrival to the Ministry, BMET and the Embassy in Amman [G0046, G0047]. BOESL has given recruits a paper for Dhaka airport stating what they must pay there and that they should pay nothing more, and caps of one colour so a group can find each other [G0211, G0087].

   Before you go, check you have [G0185]:
   - a valid passport and the work visa;
   - the medical certificate;
   - the demand letter, and the contract with the destination country's embassy attestation;
   - the agency's name, address and phone;
   - the BMET smart card;
   - the air ticket.

   Keep one set of copies of all papers at home and at least 3 with you [G0186]. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know [G0187].

### Step 6: The first weeks in Jordan

1. **Arrival.** You fly to Amman; with a private agency, an agency representative flies with the group or meets it at the airport (Step 5) [G0046].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Who meets BOESL's groups at Queen Alia airport, and what does the worker show at immigration?

2. **The medical in Jordan.** The factory needs a medical examination from a health centre approved by Jordan's Ministry of Health to complete your work permit [G0058].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does the Jordan medical test for, what does it cost, who pays, and what happens to a worker found unfit?

3. **The work permit and residence.** The factory's representative completes the permit at the labour directorate with your contract (two copies), two photos, a passport copy valid at least a year, the medical, MOI's approval for your annual residence and its social security certificate, and pays JD 10 for the form; renewal, the residence card and what to do if it isn't issued are under Your work and residence permits [G0058, G0059]. A real permit shows your name as in the passport, nationality and permitted job, the employer and factory, the start and expiry dates, a receipt number and date, and the labour director's seal and signature [G0060].

4. **Your passport.** Factories have been found keeping passports, one for 16 months [G0085, G0088]; your rights are under Your rights at work.

5. **The dormitory.** Factories must house all migrant workers, mostly in dormitories [G0083]. In 2024 two in three factories failed the accommodation standards, mostly on cleanliness and cooking space, so many workers cook in their rooms [G0081, G0082]. One group of new arrivals slept on the stairs for three days [G0212].

6. **Pay and deductions.** Your cash pay is at least JD 130 [G0074]; your social security share, 7.5% of the wage, is deducted each month, which workers call "social money" [H0042, G0100]. Recruits report 11 to 12-hour days with overtime [G0099].
7. **Bank account, e-wallet and SIM.** About two-thirds of garment workers are paid in cash; the others are paid into a bank account or an e-wallet (a mobile money account), and some factories let the worker choose [R0255, KG0002]. Some BOESL recruits paid a "bank draft" fee in Bangladesh into a new account into which their wages were later paid [G0098]. An e-wallet needs a smartphone and a local SIM card; since 2020 e-wallets can be opened online with an electronic identity check [KG0003, KG0004]. A foreigner may register at most 3 mobile lines in his own name with each phone company [KG0001]. Sending money home is under Sending money home [R0141].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which papers (passport, residence card, work permit) does a phone company in Jordan ask a new worker for to register a SIM, and does the factory arrange it?
   > - Which banks or e-wallets do garment factories use for Bangladeshi workers, and who opens the account?

8. **Orientation and help.** Better Work Jordan has been paused since 22 June 2025 for lack of funds [G0073, KB0011]. In July 2026 MOL and the ILO agreed a stop-gap programme from July 2026 and a longer one to January 2028 on fair recruitment, migrant-worker protection and complaints, including a worker welfare and support centre [KB0009, KB0010]. The ILO-supported Al-Hassan Workers' Center serves garment workers in the Al-Hassan Industrial Zone [G0213]. The Bangladesh Embassy in Amman is mission.amman@mofa.gov.bd; its full details are under Contacts [G0117].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there any arrival orientation for new garment workers in 2026, and who runs it?
   > - Is the Al-Hassan Workers' Center open in 2026, and what does it offer new arrivals?

9. **If you left without BMET clearance,** you can join the Welfare Board from Jordan: apply online at portal.wewb.gov.bd, then take the printed application, your passport and proof of legal work to the Embassy's labour wing, and pay the equivalent of Tk 6,500, life insurance included [G0145]. You get a card with a QR code [G0177].

### Scams

Complain about an agency or a broker to BMET or the Ministry (below), and about a factory in Jordan as in If something goes wrong [G0191, G0194].

**If someone cheats you:** taking money on a false promise of a job abroad carries up to 5 years and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh, and buying or selling a visa 2 to 7 years [G0149, G0150, G0152]; people cheated in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [G0153].

1. **Complain to BMET:** a self-explanatory application with your proof to BMET's Director General, filed at the workers' complaint cell; free, meant to be settled within 30 working days [G0191]. BMET holds a hearing and recommends; the Ministry decides [G0192].
2. **Or to the Ministry's enforcement branch:** an application with proof, your full address and the agency's RL number and address, to sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd (02-41030255); action starts within 3 to 5 working days [G0194, G0195].
3. **Mobile courts** give immediate remedies to people about to go, free, within 7 working days [G0197].
4. **From Jordan,** email the Ministry's Complaint Resolution Cell, minister.expat@gmail.com [G0198].
5. **If it isn't settled,** go up BMET's ladder: the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544; then the Ministry's Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd; then www.grs.gov.bd [G0193].

#### Done to workers

**Paying a broker to "get you into BOESL":** BOESL's Jordan route costs a worker a few thousand taka [G0005, G0016], yet brokers (dalal) charge for it: in one case Tk 130,000, lowered to Tk 65,000 and borrowed from three people, for a woman who believed she could not pass BOESL's test without him [G0200, G0201, G0202]. A worker claimed some brokers bribe BOESL's interviewers and tell candidates to wear a certain nail-polish colour on a certain finger to be recognised [G0203, G0204]. Brokers tell clients to hide the deal from the factory [G0206]. Recruits say brokers run off with money and passports [G0093]. **Protection:** BOESL has no agents or sub-agents, takes no cash and charges nothing before final selection [G0018, G0010]; apply as in Step 1.

**Touts at the test centres:** Women gather in Dhaka's Technical area for BOESL's free Jordan service, and cheats there took as much as Tk 5 to 10 lakh from many of them; in October 2026, 20 were arrested in Shyamoli and sentenced by a mobile court [G0214, G0113, G0215, G0114]. **Protection:** pay no one at the centre; BOESL says to avoid any transaction [G0027].

**Fake Jordan factory jobs:** In June 2026 the CID arrested three people of Surah Travels and Traders, Uttar Badda, Dhaka, an unlicensed travel agency that advertised on Facebook, forged the name, logo and seal of Jerash Garments of Amman, and took Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh per person; 55 passports and contracts on stamp paper were seized [G0109, G0110, G0111, G0112]. The factory itself complained through the Embassy [G0111]. **Protection:** check the agency on OEP and the permission letter (Step 1, item 6); a real BOESL job is on boesl.gov.bd [G0120, G0106].

**Unlicensed agency, cancelled licence or unregistered broker:** Advertising overseas jobs without government or BMET approval is an offence [G0151]. An agency's OEP row can read "Cancelled" though its date is years ahead [G0123]. Only a registered sub-agent may collect workers for an agency [G0125, G0126]. **Protection:** check the agency and the broker as in Step 1, item 6 [G0120, G0127].

**Wage lower than promised:** Women told at BOESL they would get JD 125 to 150 were paid JD 99 to 130; others were told about Tk 20,000 and got about Tk 15,000 [G0096, G0209]. **Protection:** read the contract and the BMET letter (Step 1, item 6; Step 4, item 4); complaints in Jordan are under If something goes wrong.

**Pregnancy tests:** New recruits have been pregnancy-tested, which Jordan's garment exporters asked the Embassy and BOESL to stop [G0079, G0080].

**Loans at crushing interest:** Recruits borrowed at up to Tk 100 a day, or Tk 200 per thousand [G0095]. **Protection:** PKB's loan at 9% a year (Step 5) [G0181].

#### Tried by workers

**A changed age on the passport:** BOESL's notices take women aged 20 to 39, depending on the notice, and Jordan's recruitment age for migrant workers is 18 [G0025, G0002, G0086]. One woman was 16 when she arrived after changing the age on her passport, and another paid a Tk 10,000 bribe for her passport because she looked young [G0208, G0207]. In 2024 Better Work found three suspected child-labour cases, all with falsified dates of birth in the passport, and reported them to MOL [G0084]. Getting a visa or work permit by illegal means is a crime in Bangladesh [G0152].

**A second passport or a hidden court case:** BOESL asks you to bring all your passports if you have more than one, and rejects anyone with a case in Bangladesh or Jordan [G0004, G0021].

### Your rights at work

#### Contract

**Which law covers you:** Jordan's Labour Law (Law No. 8 of 1996, as amended) applies to all workers and employers except public servants and municipal employees; agricultural workers and domestic workers, cooks and gardeners fall under a separate regulation (see Work visa: domestic worker) [KC0001, KC0002]. Any clause in which you give up a right the law gives you is void [KC0003]. Garment factories are also bound by the sector's collective bargaining agreement (CBA, a written agreement on pay and conditions between employers' associations and the union), which covers every worker in a covered factory whether or not they joined the union [H0004, H0016].

**Your contract:** The law requires a further copy of the contract in a foreign language for a worker who is not of an Arab nationality [KC0004]. Garment factories use the Unified Work Contract for Migrant Workers, issued by the Jordan Garments, Accessories & Textiles Exporters Association, the Association of Owners of Factories, Workshops and Garments and the garment workers' union [H0007]. It is made in Arabic and a language you choose, in three copies: one for you, one for the employer and one kept by MOL for your work permit [H0006]. It runs 3 years from the day you arrive, and the employer pays for your residence and work permits [H0008, H0009]. You pay no fee to anyone for the job or travel except the official costs in Bangladesh (see the steps for this visa) [H0045]. The employer may move you to another of its branches in Jordan with one week's written notice, without cutting your pay [H0010]. Probation lasts at most 3 months at no less than the minimum wage, and during it the employer may end your job without notice or end-of-service pay [KC0005, KC0006].

#### Pay and deductions

**Minimum wage:** The national minimum wage of JD 290 a month, in force from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2027, does not apply to garment workers, whose minimum is set by the sector CBA [KC0113, H0013, H0014]. The 2025 CBA set it at JD 230 from 1 January 2025 [H0015]. For migrant workers this is split into at least JD 130 cash and JD 100 "in kind" for accommodation and food [H0019]. Migrant workers already on JD 230 or more were excluded from the 2025 rise, while Jordanian workers on JD 230-400 got a one-off JD 10 seniority bonus in 2025 and, from 1 January 2026, JD 5 more after one year's service [H0020, H0021]. The union signed the 2025 CBA without consulting its members [H0022]. In practice JD 100 is taken from the JD 230 automatically, and of the January 2025 rise of JD 10 only JD 5 reached workers' cash pay [H0023, H0024]. An employer paying below the minimum is fined JD 500-1,000 per case and must pay the difference; the fine doubles on each repeat [H0026, KC0007].

> - What do Bangladeshi garment workers actually take home a month in 2026, with overtime, after the JD 100 food-and-housing deduction?
> - Is the JD 100 in-kind deduction still the figure in 2026, or has a later CBA changed it?

**When and how you are paid:** Wages and overtime must be paid within 7 days of falling due [KC0008]. Under the unified contract the employer pays in cash or by bank or electronic transfer, as agreed with you [H0030]. With each payment you get a payslip showing wage, allowances, deductions and overtime hours and pay, in a language you understand [H0031, H0032]. Signing a wage sheet or receipt does not waive your right to any higher amount owed [KC0009]. If the factory has to stop work for a while for a reason beyond its control, you get full pay for the first 10 days in a year and half pay after that, up to 60 paid days a year [KC0069].

> **Need to know**
> - Does any MOL decision require garment wages to be paid electronically (bank account or e-wallet)?

**Deductions:** The employer may deduct only [KC0010, KC0011, H0042]:
- advances, at most 10% of the wage per instalment;
- money paid to you by mistake;
- your social security share (7.5% of the wage);
- a savings fund you joined;
- housing and other services at the rate you agreed;
- debts ordered by a court;
- fines and damages within the limits below.

A fine is allowed only for an offence on the penalty list the Minister of Labour has approved, may not exceed 3 days' wage a month, and you must be heard first [KC0014, KC0012]. You can object to the labour inspector within one week of being told of a fine [KC0013]. Deductions for tools or products you lost or damaged by your fault may not exceed 5 days' wage a month [KC0015]. In 2024, 29% of assessed garment factories broke the wage-information and deduction rules, mostly by deducting the daycare allowance from working mothers who were absent [H0043]. At Fine Apparel, a factory run by the Needle Craft group, a 21-year-old Bangladeshi worker, Tureza Akter, was reportedly fined many times for missing excessive targets before she died by suicide; the factory's buyers include Under Armour, Columbia Sportswear and American Eagle Outfitters [H0044, KG0006].

#### Hours, rest and leave

**Hours and overtime:** At most 8 hours a day and 48 a week, not counting meal and rest breaks [KC0016]. Overtime needs your consent and is paid at least 125% of your normal hourly wage; the unified contract says it is optional [KC0017, H0049]. The Labour Law sets no daily or weekly overtime limit, but the CBA caps overtime at 4 hours a day, which means at most 12 hours a day and 72 a week over six days [H0254, H0050]. In 2024, 54% of assessed factories broke that cap, and migrant workers averaged 59 hours a week against 41 for Jordanians [H0051, H0052]. At Fine Apparel, workers reported staying until 10pm or midnight but being paid for at most three hours' overtime [H0053].

**Rest day and public holidays:** Friday is the weekly rest day, on full pay, unless the work needs another fixed day [KC0019, KC0020, H0055]. Work on your rest day, a religious feast or an official holiday is paid at least 150% of your normal wage [KC0018].

> **Need to know**
> - Which official and religious holidays must garment factories give with pay, and does the factory or MOL announce them each year?

**Leave:**

- **Annual leave:** 14 days on full pay a year, 21 after 5 continuous years with the same employer; weekly rest days and holidays don't count [KC0021]. The employer may not refuse your request, and leave you have not taken is paid in cash when the job ends; you can't sign it away [KC0022, KC0023, KC0024].
- **Sick leave:** 14 days a year on full pay with a report from the factory's approved doctor, plus 14 more on full pay if you are in hospital; factories with more than 20 workers use a medical committee to approve reports [KC0025]. If the factory has no approved doctor, a report from any licensed doctor or clinic is accepted [H0061].
- **Maternity:** 10 weeks on full pay (paid by social security) and paid nursing breaks of up to an hour a day for a year [H0062, KC0071, KC0072]. You can't be dismissed from the sixth month of pregnancy or during maternity, annual or sick leave [KC0028, KC0029]. In 2024, 18% of factories were cited because migrant women had been given pregnancy tests [H0067].
- **Paternity:** 3 days on full pay [KC0026].
- **Hajj:** 14 days on full pay, once, after 5 continuous years with the employer [KC0027].

**Heat:** MOL's heat-wave guidance covers open-air sites in direct sun at peak hours, where the employer may move the hours to morning or evening [H0068, H0069]. In 2024, 20% of assessed garment factories' dormitories were not adequately protected against heat or properly ventilated [H0070].

#### Housing, food and health care

**Housing and food:** Garment factories must house all their migrant workers, mostly in dormitories [H0071]. The unified contract gives you three adequate meals a day, shared housing that meets Jordan's standards, and free transport if the dorm is 1 km or more from the factory [H0033, H0034]. In 2024, 66% of assessed factories broke the accommodation rules; the main failures were cleanliness and cooking space, which forces workers to cook in their rooms [H0072, H0073]. Researchers describe rooms of 8 to 10 workers, a gate shut by a security guard at the 10pm curfew, and employer-appointed dorm leaders who report on workers [H0074, H0075, H0076]. Women have been sent home for breaking curfew, for a relationship with a man or after being accused of theft [H0076]. One Bangladeshi worker who had JD 95 a month taken for board and lodging cooked her own food because she found the canteen food poor [H0025].

> **Need to know**
> - What are MOL's binding dormitory standards (space per worker, beds, toilets, kitchens), and where does a worker report a bad dorm?

**Health care:** The employer arranges your medical checks, and in an emergency the factory's medical staff must send you to a specialist or hospital at the employer's expense [H0081]. The 2025 CBA requires a medical unit at the factory as the Labour Law sets out, and MOL says this care covers migrant workers [H0082, H0017]. The CBA also obliges the employer to refer you to a psychological care centre if you ask [H0083].

> **Need to know**

#### Your passport

The employer may not keep your passport, residence permit or work permit [H0084]. An employer that forces anyone to work, including by keeping their travel document, is fined JD 500-1,000 [KC0030]. Under the Passports Law anyone found holding a passport unlawfully faces 6 months to 3 years in prison and/or JD 500-1,000 [KC0076, KC0077, KC0078]. Handing your own passport to someone for unlawful use carries the same penalty [KC0079]. In practice passports are still kept: in 2024 Better Work Jordan found passport confiscation in three factories, half of all its forced-labour cases were in Aqaba, and Fine Apparel workers reported passports withheld [H0091, H0092, H0093]. Workers who complained to Tamkeen in 2024 reported having to pay to get their passports back [H0094]. To report a kept passport, use MOL's complaint channels under If something goes wrong [H0190].

#### End-of-service pay and social security

The Labour Law's end-of-service pay (a month's wage per year) is only for workers outside social security [KC0031]. The SSC covers every worker aged 16 or over regardless of nationality, so as a garment worker you are in social security instead [KC0073]. It insures you for work injury, old age, disability, death, maternity and unemployment; your share is 7.5% of your wage and the employer's 14.25% [H0098, H0041]. When you leave Jordan for good, SSC pays you a lump sum: with 120 monthly contributions or fewer (10 years), it is 10% of your insured wages for each year you contributed [KC0075, KC0074, R0183]. How to claim it is under Leaving at the end of the job, step 5, and how to check your employer is paying in is under If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job, Check you are covered [KC0095, R0219].

#### Trade union and Better Work Jordan

Jordan's law allows only a single union structure and Jordan has not ratified the ILO's convention on freedom of association [H0105]. For garment workers it is the General Trade Union of Workers in Textile, Garment and Clothing Industries (JTGCU) [H0106]. You have the right to join it without harassment or loss of rights, and an employer may not make your job depend on not joining [H0110, KC0070]. Joining is optional: in 2024, 74% of factories were cited because workers signed a union form with their contract without understanding that [H0107]. Each factory has a Union-Labour Committee (worker representatives who meet management), but the union does not fully represent migrant workers and struggles to communicate with them in their languages [H0109, H0108]. In 2018 the union said it had four field offices taking workers' complaints [H0111].

Better Work Jordan has been compulsory for exporting garment factories since 2010 [H0117]. It paused operations on 22 June 2025 after a funding cut, with no staff left in Jordan, and was still paused in November 2026; queries go to betterwork@ilo.org [H0113, H0115, KB0011, H0114]. On 9 July 2026 MOL and the ILO agreed new ILO support for the garment sector (see Work visa: garment and factory worker, Step 6, item 8) [KB0009].

### Your work and residence permits

#### How long you can stay

Your contract is for 3 years, but your work permit lasts one year at a time and must be renewed; each renewal runs from the date the last permit expired [H0008, KC0032, KC0033]. The employer applies to renew at least one month before expiry [KC0092]. The permit fee for a garment worker is JD 225 a year plus JD 50 for the Vocational and Technical Skills Development Commission [KC0083, KC0084]. The fees are paid in advance, and under the unified contract the employer pays for your work and residence permits for the whole contract, even if it ends early [KC0088, H0085]. If a permit is not renewed within 90 days of expiry, a late fine of 50% of the fee is added [KC0085]. You also need an annual residence permit; not applying to renew it within a month of expiry costs JD 90 a month (JD 3 a day) [H0160]. QIZ employers lodge an annual bank guarantee with MOL, JD 30,000 to 150,000 depending on their workforce, that covers workers' unpaid rights, return tickets and overstay fines caused by the employer not renewing permits [KC0091]. In practice some factories have let permits lapse for months or years, leaving workers unable to go home without paying overstay fines [H0162, H0163].

#### Leaving Jordan during the contract and going home on leave

Your annual leave is under Your rights at work [KC0021]. To leave Jordan and come back you need an exit and return visa; the procedure is under Exit and return visa [V0097]. The unified contract gives a ticket to Jordan and a ticket home at the end of the job, and says nothing about a ticket for leave [H0240].

#### Lost passport or work permit

A replacement work permit card costs JD 10 [KC0086, KC0087]. Falsely reporting your passport lost, or destroying it on purpose, is a crime with 6 months to 3 years in prison and/or JD 500-1,000 [KC0080, KC0076, KC0077]. The Embassy's labour wing has handled passport renewals for garment workers; its contacts are under If something goes wrong, and a new passport or travel permit is under Going home without valid papers, Step 1 [H0223, R0029].

#### Bringing your family

Migrant garment workers are not allowed to bring their families or children to Jordan; for the general rules see Family residence [H0078]. Having a baby in Jordan is under Having a child in Jordan, and why an asylum claim does not work is under Asylum [V0193, V0175].

### What can get you jailed or deported

#### Working without a permit or for another employer

It is illegal to work without a permit, on a permit expired up to 90 days, for an employer other than the one on your permit without MOL permission, or in another job [KC0034, KC0035, KC0036]. MOL orders deportation of a worker who breaks these rules, including one it finds has left their employer, and the deported worker can't be hired in Jordan again for at least 5 years [KC0037, KC0039]. The deportation is paid by the employer the worker was caught with, and anyone who employs a worker unlawfully is fined at least twice the permit fees [KC0038, KC0040]. The ministries of interior and labour and the Public Security Directorate (PSD, the national police) said in November 2025 that any worker found without a work permit or in another job is deported immediately [R0150]. Until 1 December 2026 a government decision (the 2026 regularisation) waived most past permit fees and fines for workers in breach who put their papers right or left for good, but it excludes workers in garment and textile factories in the QIZs, development zones and free zones (see Going home without valid papers, The 2026 regularisation) [R0152, R0175, R0153]. From 1 January 2027 MOL puts an electronic deportation flag on every worker who has not regularised [R0176]. Any foreigner staying more than two weeks must register with the PSD, and breaking this costs JD 200 [R0148, R0149]. Anyone who shelters or employs a runaway domestic worker can be prosecuted, including for human trafficking [R0151].

#### Overstaying

Not renewing your residence permit within a month of expiry costs JD 3 a day, raised from JD 1.5 in 2025; for leaving with overstay fines see Going home without valid papers [H0160, H0161].

#### Absent from work

Being absent without good reason for more than 10 days in a row, or more than 20 days in a year, lets the employer dismiss you without notice, after a warning by registered mail and in a newspaper; absconding reports are under If something goes wrong [KC0061, KC0062].

#### Strikes and protests

A strike needs at least 14 days' written notice to the employer, and is banned while the dispute is before MOL's conciliation or the labour court [KC0041, KC0042]. A worker in a banned strike is fined at least JD 50 for the first day and JD 5 a day after, and loses pay for those days [KC0043]. After Tureza Akter's death, Fine Apparel workers struck despite the law, and the company agreed to sack the supervisor and two managers they named [H0167, H0168]. After stoppages or strikes, managements have kept paperwork showing that workers "resigned" [H0186, H0187].

#### Drugs and alcohol

- Using or holding the main drugs for your own use: 1 to 3 years in prison and JD 1,000-3,000; a first offence is not recorded as a criminal record [KC0081, KC0082].
- Being drunk in a public place and causing trouble: up to 3 months and/or JD 100-200 [H0171, H0172].
- Bringing alcohol or drugs into the workplace is banned, and being found clearly drunk or drugged at work lets the employer dismiss you without notice [H0174, KC0044].

#### Cheques and debts

Writing a cheque in bad faith without enough money in the account: one year in prison and JD 100-200; if the cheque is later paid, a fine of 5% of its value (JD 100-5,000) [H0175, H0176].

> **Need to know**
> - Can an ordinary unpaid debt (for example to a shop or a co-worker) lead to a travel ban for a migrant worker?

#### Public behaviour and relationships

- An indecent act or gesture in public: up to 1 year and JD 200 [H0177].
- Sex outside marriage (zina): 1 to 3 years, prosecuted only if a spouse or the woman's guardian complains [H0178, H0179].
- In the dorms, a relationship with a man has got women sent home (see Your rights at work) [H0076].

#### Online posts

Under the Cybercrime Law (Law 17 of 2023):
- false news targeting national security or social peace, or insulting or defaming anyone online: at least 3 months in prison and/or JD 5,000-20,000 [H0180];
- posts stirring strife or hatred, or showing contempt for religion: 1 to 3 years and/or JD 5,000-20,000 [H0181];
- posting someone's private photo, recording or video to shame them: at least 3 months and JD 20,000-40,000 [H0182].

#### False documents

Using someone else's identity or forged papers lets the employer dismiss you without notice [KC0045]. Recruiting a worker under 18 is treated as human trafficking; in 2024 Better Work Jordan found three suspected child workers in garment factories, all with falsified dates of birth in their passports [H0184, H0185].

#### Bans on coming back

A worker deported under the Labour Law can't be recruited or employed in Jordan for at least 5 years [KC0039].

### If something goes wrong

#### If you aren't paid, are abused or your passport is taken

You can't be dismissed or punished for complaining to the authorities [KC0046]. Workers still fear retaliation and distrust the complaint channels inside factories [H0226]. You may also leave without notice and keep your rights if the employer cuts your pay, beats, humiliates or sexually harasses you, or ignores an MOL notice to obey the law, but you must tell MOL within two weeks of leaving [KC0056, KC0057, KC0058, KC0059]. An employer who beats or sexually harasses workers is fined JD 2,000-5,000 [KC0060]. Labour Law claims expire two years after they arise [KC0053].

1. **Keep proof:** you get your own copy of the contract and a payslip with every wage payment; these show what you were promised and paid [H0006, H0031].
2. **Inside the factory:** the unified contract sends a complaint first to the factory's union labour committee, then to the union if not settled in five days; you can skip both and go straight to MOL [H0189].
3. **MOL's Hemayah ("protection") complaint platform:** file at complaint.hemayeh.jo and keep the complaint number to follow it up [H0249, H0190]. You can mark the complaint confidential; if you are paid below the minimum, choose "minimum wage" rather than "unpaid wages" [H0197].

   Hemayah was built to work in 10 languages including Bengali, and embassies, unions and NGOs can file a complaint for a worker (2022) [D0199, D0200].

4. **Or call or message MOL** [H0191, H0192, H0253, H0251, H0252, H0193, H0194, H0250]:
   - national call centre 06-5008080, 24 hours;
   - complaints by WhatsApp 06-2221020 or 06-2221239;
   - free line for permits and the Labour Law 080022208;
   - inspection and migrant labour 06-5802681, inspection WhatsApp 06-5802660 or 06-5802661;
   - in person: MOL, 11 Issa Al-Naouri Street, Amman, Sunday to Thursday 8:30am-3:30pm, phone +962 6 2221020.
5. **The labour inspector** can order the employer to fix a violation within 7 days, after which the Minister can close the factory [KC0047].
6. **Unpaid or cut wages: the Wage Authority (in Amman) or the magistrates' court (elsewhere).** MOL's Wage Authority hears wage claims in Amman governorate: short pay, illegal deductions, late pay, unpaid overtime [H0200]. File while still employed, or within six months of leaving if you ended the contract; it also mediates if asked within six months [H0201, H0202]. You, or the union for you, file in writing, and several workers of one factory can file one claim [KC0048]. It can add compensation up to the amount withheld [KC0049]. If you don't attend, the case is dropped [H0203]. It is free, and the award is enforced like a court judgment, without instalments [H0204, H0205]. In 2024 it decided 354 of 368 cases [H0206]. Outside Amman, wage and other individual claims go to the magistrates' court (mahkamat al-sulh, the lowest court), which must decide within three months and charges no fees [KC0050, KC0051, KC0052].
7. **Dismissed unfairly:** sue within 60 days of dismissal; the court can order your job back or compensation of half a month's wage per year, at least two months' wage [KC0054, KC0055]. If a fixed-term contract is ended early by the employer, you are owed the wages for the rest of the term [KC0064, H0243].
8. **Tamkeen** gives migrant workers legal advice, mediates with employers and refers cases to the Anti-Trafficking Unit; from January to November 2024 it received 153 complaints from migrant workers [H0095, H0094]. It is at Madinah Street, Building 197, 3rd floor, offices 306-307, Amman; +962 6 5539501; info@tamkeen-jo.org [R0234].

9. **The Bangladesh Embassy's labour wing:** First Secretary (Labour), office +962 6 552 9192, mobile +962 7896 06415, fsamman@probashi.gov.bd [H0219, H0221, H0220]. In the case of Achilles Apparel, a garment company that laid off its workers in December 2023, it took up workers' complaints from November 2022 with MOL, the garment industry bodies and the union, and after about 18 months, in February 2024, handed 454 Bangladeshi workers their overdue wages and social security pay at MOL [H0225, H0224, H0222]. It also handled their work permits, passport renewals and return home; complaints to the Ministry in Dhaka are under Contacts [H0223].

After several factory closures, including the Aseel factory, MOL, employers and the union set up a committee in March 2024, and a legal study it commissioned recommended an early warning system so workers are paid when a factory closes [H0228, H0255, H0227].

#### If you are injured or fall ill

In an emergency the factory must send you to a doctor or hospital at its expense [H0081]. Work-injury treatment and pay are covered by SSC; see If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job [H0098]. Sick leave is under Your rights at work [KC0025].

#### If your employer reports you absent ("absconding")

**What it is:** An absconding report (in Arabic tableegh firar, a "runaway" report) is filed by the employer with MOL when a worker leaves the job [KC0097]. The employer must first file it at a police directorate or security centre and bring the stamped report, and may file only if it has paid all your social security contributions [KC0099, KC0100]. It may not file one in the last two months of your work permit [KC0101]. Garment factories are exempt from the 10% cap on how many workers an employer may report [KC0102].

**What it can lead to:** MOL deports a worker it finds has left their employer, with a ban of at least 5 years [KC0037, KC0039]. Separately, more than 10 days' unexplained absence lets the employer dismiss you without notice [KC0061]. If the report turns out to be false, the employer is blocked from all MOL permits and you are given a permit with another employer [KC0103, KC0104].

1. **If you leave for one of the legal reasons** (assault, harassment, a pay cut, or the employer ignoring an MOL notice), tell MOL within two weeks through Hemayah or the numbers above, and keep the complaint number [KC0056, H0190].
2. **If your employer ended your job,** tell the labour directorate that issued your permit at once; otherwise you can't get a permit with any other employer [KC0098].
3. **Ask the Embassy's labour wing** for help (contacts above) [H0219].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### If you want to change employer

The garment sector in the development zones, the QIZs and the free zones is a closed sector: you can't move to a job in any other sector, but you may move to another garment employer [KC0106, KC0107, KC0110]. For a recruited worker the rules are [KC0111, KC0108, KC0109]:
- while your permit is valid: not before 6 months with the first employer, and only with the consent of MOL and both employers; the new employer pays for a new one-year permit;
- after your permit expires: within 60 days of expiry you need a clearance (bara'at dhimma) from the old employer; after 60 days, or if your permit was cancelled, the employer died or the company was dissolved or liquidated, you need none.

The old permit is cancelled at the labour office with both you and the employer (or its agent) present [KC0105]. If you move after your permit has expired, the new employer pays the permit fee back-dated to the end of your last one [KC0093].

> **Need to know**
> - Do QIZ garment factories apply the six-month rule, or do the QIZ instructions set their own?
> - How often does a garment factory actually release a Bangladeshi worker to another factory?

**Notice:** On an open-ended contract either side gives at least one month's written notice [KC0063]. The unified contract is fixed-term (3 years): if you leave early without one of the legal reasons under If something goes wrong, the employer can claim up to half a month's wage for each month left, and pays only part of your ticket home in proportion to the time you worked [H0008, KC0065, H0241].

**If it is blocked:** Researchers say the system in practice makes it impossible for garment workers to change employer during their 3 years, and the ILO has urged changing the CBA so they can [H0079, H0230]. If you are owed pay or are abused, use the steps under If something goes wrong; the legal reasons let you leave without notice and keep your rights [KC0056].

#### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Final pay:** all wages and overtime within 7 days of your last day, with housing and meals until you travel [H0242]. Unused annual leave is paid in cash [KC0023].
2. **Papers:** on request the employer gives you a service certificate, certified by MOL, and returns any papers you deposited [KC0066, KC0067, KC0068].
3. **Ticket:** the employer pays your ticket home and must send you back and prove you left [H0240, KC0089].
4. **Cancel the permit:** the permit is cancelled at the labour office with you and the employer present; the final departure (exit without return) document costs JD 10 [KC0105, KC0086].
5. **Social security lump sum:** under MOL's rules SSC pays only after MOL stamps a no-objection, which costs JD 10; apply for it within 3 months of your permit expiring, or the full permit fee is charged first [KC0095, KC0086, KC0094]. Holding the no-objection no longer stops you being issued a work permit in Jordan later [KC0096]. During the 2026 regularisation period SSC also takes the claim online under "Permanent Departure from the Country with No Return", while you are still in Jordan, without a visit to MOL or prior approvals, with a form for having the money sent abroad [H0102, H0101, H0104, H0103].
6. **Fines:** late-permit and overstay fines (see How long you can stay) are dealt with under Going home without valid papers; what you can bring home is under Coming home [KC0085, H0160].

> **Need to know**

> - How long does SSC's transfer of the lump sum to a Bangladeshi bank take, and what does it cost?

**Employer tricks at the end of the job:**

- Being made to "resign" and sent home before a valid 3-year contract ends [H0231].
- Termination papers not in your language, and final settlements wrong or missing [H0232].
- Papers saying you were paid in full that can't be checked once you have left [H0233].
- Being "sent home" over dorm rules, a relationship or illness [H0076, H0077].

Signing a receipt or wage sheet does not waive any higher amount owed under the law or contract, and if you ended the contract yourself, a wage claim can still be filed within six months of leaving (see If something goes wrong) [KC0009, H0201].

## Work visa: domestic worker

Domestic workers are housemaids, cooks, gardeners, patient companions and the like, doing household tasks such as cleaning, cooking, ironing, caring for family members, shopping for the home and gardening [D0001]. They are outside Jordan's Labour Law and come under Regulation No. 90 of 2009 on domestic workers, cooks, gardeners and the like, and its amendments [D0003, D0002]. Their recruitment is run by private offices licensed under Regulation No. 63 of 2020 [D0045]. MOL allows domestic workers to be recruited from six countries: Ethiopia, Ghana, Bangladesh, the Philippines, Nepal and Sri Lanka [KD0013].

**Is the route open?** Yes on the Jordanian side: when MOL suspended the recruitment of foreign workers in almost every sector from 1 June 2026, it exempted domestic workers [D0091, D0092]. Recruiting from abroad for the domestic sector is allowed for women; for men MOL applies its own conditions [D0096, D0097]. In 2021 about 7,009 of Jordan's 52,221 documented women domestic workers were Bangladeshi, and Bangladeshis were growing in number because they cost less to recruit [D0150, D0151]. Bangladesh lets only approved agencies send women domestic workers and sets stricter age and training rules (Steps 1 to 3) [D0081, D0074].

> **Need to know**
> - Has BMET approved any Jordan housemaid job orders since 2025, and through which agencies?

### Who pays what

The worker pays nothing in Jordan: the recruitment office and the household may not take any money from the worker or deduct recruitment costs from her wage, and the office may not work with any party that charges workers for jobs in Jordan [D0054, D0055]. MOL sets the total recruitment cost for each nationality, covering the work-permit fee, tickets, the office's fee and the medical tests in both countries, and an office may not charge the household more [D0052]. For a Bangladeshi worker MOL's figure is JD 2,200, or JD 1,700 when the household is exempt from the work-permit fee [D0005, D0006]. A household charged more can report the office to MOL's Domestic Workers Directorate, in Ministry building no. 3, Abdali, Amman [D0198, KD0005]. The household also pays the residence and work permits every year, the ticket to Jordan, and a ticket home once the worker has served at least two years [D0023, D0028].

In Bangladesh an agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from them, and signs a pledge saying so [D0082, D0084]. In practice women still pay village brokers: Nazma Begum of Moheshpur, Jhenaidah, paid brokers Tk 50,000, borrowed from relatives and neighbours, for a Jordan housekeeping job [D0185].

### How a Jordanian household recruits

Only through an office licensed by MOL [D0045]. Licensing of new offices, frozen since 2012, reopened in May 2024 [KD0006, KD0005]. Each office lodges a JD 60,000 bank guarantee with MOL, which MOL can seize if the office breaks its obligations [D0046]. MOL publishes each office's name, address, phone number and status (active, suspended or closed) on www.mol.gov.jo, under the expatriate labour portal [D0014].

1. **The household goes to an office** with a copy of its family book (pages 1 to 7) and a bank authorisation for the office [D0010]. A foreign resident must also lodge a JD 2,000 bank guarantee and give a marriage contract and residence permit copy [D0011]. Anyone convicted of, or sent to court for, assaulting a worker, and anyone who cannot afford to pay one, may not recruit [D0012].
2. **The office signs a contract with the household** on MOL's form, and must display its recruitment costs by nationality at its premises and on its website [D0050]. It may recruit only through a licensed agency in a country that has an agreement or MoU with Jordan [D0047].
3. **The household pays the permit fees up front and the rest on handover.** The office collects the recruitment cost only when it hands the worker over, except the work-permit fee, and must give a receipt for every payment, signed by its manager and showing the office's name, registration number, address and seal [D0053, KD0009]. The fees are paid to the treasury against a "financial receipt" in the household's name, valid eight months [D0231, D0232]. If the worker has not entered Jordan within two months of the contract, the office must cancel her visa [D0059].
4. **Medical and handover in Jordan.** Within three working days of the worker's arrival the office takes her for the Ministry of Health medical, and the Ministry gives the result within three working days [D0062, D0060]. The office may hand her to the household only once the result says she is fit [D0020]. A worker found pregnant or with a contagious disease is sent home within seven working days [D0061].
5. **Permits.** The office applies for her work permit and annual residence permit within three working days of the medical result, and takes out an insurance policy for her with a licensed insurer [D0062, D0066].

#### The guarantee period

For the first 90 days after the worker enters Jordan (or 30 days after she moves to a new household), the office carries the risk [KD0007]. If in that time she refuses to work, runs away, or is found ill or pregnant, the household can choose a refund from the office within 14 days, less a set allowance for the days she worked, or a replacement worker from abroad or from inside Jordan at no extra charge from the office [KD0007, KD0008, D0233]. A refusal because of physical abuse proven by a Ministry of Health forensic report, or because of unpaid wages, is treated differently: the office may move her to another household (see If you aren't paid or are abused) [KD0007, D0065]. If she refuses to work and wants to go home in that period, the office pays her ticket [D0017, D0063]. If she runs away in that period, the office repays the household's full recruitment cost [D0019]. After the 90 days the office's guarantee ends, but the household can still use its financial receipt to get a second worker from the same or another office [KD0012]. The receipt allows two replacements within its eight months [D0016]. MOL charges a replacement fee of JD 100 when the first worker never arrived and JD 175 when the replacement is someone already in Jordan [D0069].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

**The "housekeeping job" that is trafficking:** A village broker promised Nazma Begum a JD 250 housekeeping job in Jordan; in Amman she was taken from the airport, held captive and "sold" on a two-year contract to a man who sent the women in his house to hotels for sex work [D0184, D0186]. Her family went to Ain o Salish Kendra, which wrote to the foreign ministry and the ambassador, and the Embassy rescued her [D0187]. Recruitment to Jordan runs largely through unlicensed rural brokers [D0189, D0195].

**The false passport age:** A 15-year-old girl whose passport said she was 27 was sent to Jordan as a housemaid through a registered agency, MH International, in 2017, and was repeatedly raped there [D0190]. Recruiting a worker under the agreed age, or with forged papers, can cost a Jordanian office its licence for good [D0056, D0057, KD0010].

**Being hired out to other houses:** Domestic workers complain of being made to work in other homes without pay [D0154]. An office may not send a worker to do daily work or to work for anyone but the household, and making her work by the day or seizing her wages can cost it its licence for good [D0049, D0058, KD0010].

**Unlicensed recruiters and fake job adverts:** MOL tells households to check its website before using an office, to avoid fake recruiters' adverts [D0196]. In Bangladesh, the unlicensed Surah Travels and Traders sold Jordan jobs in a Jordanian company's name for Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person (see Work visa: garment and factory worker, Scams, Fake Jordan factory jobs) [D0211]. Some agencies take money for residence and work permits without processing them, leaving workers undocumented and liable for overstay fines [D0223].

#### Tried by workers

Travelling on a passport with a false age is the trick in the case above, and BMET clearance needs proof that a woman domestic worker is 25 to 45 [D0190, D0087]. A worker who leaves the house without the household's consent or good cause becomes liable for the contract's costs and her own ticket home [D0031].

### Your rights at work

These come from Regulation 90 of 2009 and its amendments [D0002].

#### Hours, rest and leave

- **Hours:** at most eight hours of actual work a day, not counting idle time, rest or meals [D0033].
- **Sleep:** at least eight hours a day [D0034].
- **Weekly day off:** agreed with the household; if you are made to work on it you get another day off [D0035].
- **Annual leave:** 14 paid days a year, which you can agree to take at the end of the contract [D0036].
- **Sick leave:** 14 paid days a year [D0037].

#### Pay

Paid within seven days of the due date, with proof [D0024]. The household may deduct for loss or damage proven to be your fault, but never more than five days' wage in a month [D0030].

#### Care, contact and religion

The household must treat you with respect and give you clothing, food, drink, a lit and ventilated room, rest and privacy [D0026]. It must provide health care and let you practise your religion in a way that does not conflict with public order and morals [D0029]. It must let you call home once a month at its cost [D0021]. If the family moves abroad, it may take you only with your consent and after telling the Bangladesh Embassy [D0027].

#### Passport

Holding someone else's passport is a crime under Jordan's Passport Law, punished with six months to three years in prison and a fine [D0156, D0221]. Passport confiscation is still among the commonest complaints: 114 of the 291 complaints from women workers to the legal-aid NGO Tamkeen in 2022 [D0155].

#### In practice

Tamkeen received over 730 complaints from domestic workers in 2020-2022, including being stopped from travelling, unpaid wages, no health care, holidays or food, being cut off from family, violence and long hours [D0154].

### Bringing your family

A domestic worker who married a Jordanian and switched to a housewife's residence may get a domestic work permit again after divorce, her husband's death, or if she wants to work [D0178]. Having a baby in Jordan, including the risks for an unmarried worker, is under Having a child in Jordan; the overstay, drug, cheque and online-post offences under What can get you jailed or deported, in the garment worker section, apply to domestic workers too [V0193, H0160, KC0081].

### If something goes wrong

#### If you aren't paid or are abused

1. **If you are assaulted, leave.** If the household sexually or physically assaults you or seriously violates your basic rights, you may leave at once and claim your rights [D0042]. A physical-abuse case is proved with a report from the Ministry of Health's forensic doctor, and on that report, or for unpaid wages, the office may move you to another household [D0065].
2. **Complain to MOL** by phone, WhatsApp or the Hemayah platform (complaint.hemayeh.jo), as in Work visa: garment and factory worker, If something goes wrong, steps 3 and 4; the Embassy or an NGO can file for you [H0190, D0199]. In person, MOL's citizen service centre takes domestic-worker requests: phone 06 2221258, info@mol.gov.jo, Sunday to Thursday 8:30 am to 3:30 pm [D0070].
3. **What MOL does.** It calls you and the household in, hearing you separately and in confidence [D0039]. A complaint about where you live leads to a visit by a male and a female inspector, with the household's consent [D0040]. A household found in breach gets a week to fix it, then can be barred from hiring [D0041].
4. **Trafficking.** The Ministry of Social Development's Dar Karama shelter takes trafficking victims, including people who refer themselves, and gives psychological, medical and legal help; stays are up to two months, can be extended, and do not depend on helping the police [D0158, D0161, D0160]. It has 35 places, 20 of them for women, and served 133 people in 2024 [D0159]. MOL and the police anti-trafficking unit run the hotline, but interpreters for Asian languages are not always there [D0164].
5. **The Bangladesh Embassy** in Amman: +962 6 5529192/3, mission.amman@mofa.gov.bd [D0148, D0149]. WEWB lists a Bangladesh safe house for women in Jordan [D0089].
6. **From Bangladesh.** Your family can ask WEWB for safe-house help, free, directly or through the district commissioner's expatriate welfare section or the District Employment and Manpower Office (DEMO); WEWB writes to the Embassy (call centre 16135; +8809610102030 from abroad) [D0090]. You or a close relative can report harassment to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare at aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd with your passport, smart card and residence permit copies and the agency's details; it is free and dealt with in 7 working days [D0085].
7. **Legal aid.** Tamkeen, Madinah Munawwarah Street, building 197, 3rd floor, offices 306-307, Amman; +962 6 5539501 [D0204, D0205].

**What happens in practice.** Rights groups say domestic servitude victims are pressured to drop complaints against employers [D0171]. Migrant victims often prefer their own embassy's shelter because they mistrust the authorities, and Jordan often lacks money for their flights home [D0162, D0163]. In 2020 the ambassador said she wanted a shelter at the Embassy like those in Saudi Arabia and Oman [D0193].

#### The office's duties when things go wrong

The office must provide private housing for domestic workers and shelter them, but may not house them at its own premises [D0051, D0048]. In the first 90 days it pays the ticket home of a worker who refuses to work [D0017]. Anyone else who shelters a worker who has left her household is liable under the regulation [D0044].

#### If your household reports you absent ("runaway")

1. **How it works.** A household whose worker leaves gets an "escape circular" (تعميم هروب) from a police security centre, which lets it replace her on its financial receipt [D0071, D0016]. A false report by the household is itself a violation [D0043].
2. **What it does to you.** You must tell the household before leaving the house [D0032]. Workers who leave before the contract ends are treated as illegal residents and fined, detained or deported [D0166]. MOL and the police raid for runaway domestic workers and take action against anyone who employs or shelters them [D0182]. About 30,000 domestic workers in Jordan are undocumented [D0152].
3. **The 2026 regularisation.** Until 1 December 2026, a Cabinet decision lets domestic workers in breach, and their households, settle at half the work-permit fees for all past periods, with late-renewal fines and overstay fines waived [KD0001, KD0002, R0175]. Under it, an absent worker who has been replaced can take a permit with a new household without the old one's consent once the absence circular is lifted; one who was not replaced can do so two years after the report [D0175, D0176]. A worker who wants to go home instead can leave for good with all past permit fees and fines waived (see Leaving Jordan) [KD0003]. From 1 January 2027 anyone who has not regularised gets a deportation flag, and MOL and the police deport violators they catch [R0175, KD0004].

### Changing employer or leaving

#### Changing household

Domestic workers may not move to another sector [D0018]. Since June 2026, a domestic worker whose last permit expired or was cancelled more than two years ago may move to another sector without the old household's consent [D0177, KD0002]. Within the domestic sector you can move to a new household: the new household pays the transfer cost MOL sets (JD 2,200 for a Bangladeshi, or JD 1,700 if the permit is fee-exempt) [D0007]. If you move in the first year of the recruitment contract with a valid permit, the office pays the old household JD 1,100 once you have been with the new household a month; in the second year it pays JD 700 if your permit is valid and JD 600 if it has expired; once a two-year contract has ended, the office owes the old household nothing [D0008, D0009]. A July 2024 rule created a way to change household without the old one's consent, but only four domestic workers were allowed to use it in 2024-25 [D0167, D0168].

#### Leaving Jordan

1. **Settle your rights first.** At the end of the contract you and the household sign a clearance of your rights before MOL, each keeping a copy, before you leave [D0038].
2. **Get MOL's clearance.** Domestic workers need a clearance certificate from MOL to leave; without permits you pay fines for the time without them [D0207].
3. **Your ticket home** is the household's after two years' work [D0028].
4. **If you are out of status.** Until 1 December 2026, a domestic worker in breach who leaves Jordan for good (exit without return) pays no past work-permit fees or fines and no overstay fines, once her file is settled [KD0003, R0175].

### Bans and suspensions

Jordan banned recruitment from Bangladesh in 2006, reopened it for women garment workers only in 2010, and halted it again in late 2011 after abuse reports and strikes [D0208]. The Minister of Labour can open recruitment from any country or close it, giving offices up to three months to finish earlier orders [D0067, KD0011]. Domestic workers were left out of the June 2026 suspension [D0092].

## Other work permits

Outside garments and households, a foreigner may work in Jordan only with the Minister of Labour's approval, in work that Jordanians cannot fill, and with a work permit obtained before being recruited or employed, valid one year or part of a year [D0130, KC0032]. Bangladeshis are about 15.9% of Jordan's estimated 1.5 million migrant workers, after Egyptians at 53.3% [D0222]. Bangladesh asked Jordan in May 2025 to hire Bangladeshi men in agriculture, tourism and construction [D0210].

### Is it open now?

**Status (9 November 2026): closed for new recruitment from abroad, except a few routes.** Since 1 June 2026 MOL has suspended recruitment of foreign workers in all sectors, exempting only domestic workers, garments in the development zones, QIZs and free zones, and specialised-skill professions [D0091, D0092]. Approvals issued before the suspension are still processed [D0093]. MOL reopens a sector only when it sees a clear shortage of workers [D0094]. Even before the suspension, recruitment from abroad was allowed only for zone garments, domestic workers, specialised skills, firms contracted to the Jordanian-Palestinian agricultural marketing company, and a replacement for a worker who has left Jordan for good [D0096, D0097].

So a man in Bangladesh cannot now be recruited for a new construction, farm, cleaning, bakery or petrol-station job in Jordan [D0091, D0092, D0096].

### Professions closed to foreigners

MOL's current guide (updated 20 August 2026) keeps 14 professions closed to non-Jordanians [D0103]:

- office work: printing, secretarial, data entry and administration [D0100]
- all selling, wholesale, retail and distribution [D0102]
- interior and exterior decoration; switchboards; electrical wiring; car repair and upholstery [D0100]
- women's and children's hairdressing; parking attendants; office messengers; lottery-ticket selling [D0100]
- security guards, except building guards, who count as cleaners [D0101]
- all jobs in firms renting out compressors or construction equipment; household maintenance and electrical-appliance repair [D0100]
- education, except teachers, faculty and researchers [D0100]

Imams, church staff, schoolteachers, writers and artists, journalists, air hosts and singers need another authority's approval first [D0104].

### Where a foreigner can work

MOL's sector table lists the jobs foreigners may do and how many Jordanians an employer must employ for each foreigner, in agriculture, industry, bakeries, construction, fuel stations, car washes, hotels and restaurants [D0105]. Fuel stations, for example, need four Jordanians for each foreign pump operator [D0107]. Farm numbers follow the Ministry of Agriculture's classification of the holding [D0106].

Some sectors lock you in. Bakeries, domestic gas distribution, mechanical trades (oil change, tyre repair, car wash) and agriculture are closed sectors: you can change employer inside them but not leave them, except that a farm worker may become a domestic gardener [D0108, D0109]. QIZ garments are closed both ways [D0110]. A building maintenance worker can only leave for the day-labour free permits; building cleaners and cleaners can move to other open sectors [D0111].

**Fees.** MOL charges the employer JD 350 a year per worker [D0113, D0114, D0132], plus JD 50 for the Technical and Vocational Skills Development Commission and a JD 10 audit fee [D0115, D0139]. Building services workers cost JD 700 and specialised-skills permits JD 1,500 a year [D0112, D0117]. A permit renewed more than 90 days late costs 50% extra [D0116].

### The free (flexible) work permit for day labourers

**What it is.** A one-year permit, called the "free work permit" for day labourers and often the "flexible permit", that lets you work for any employer in the same trade anywhere in Jordan [D0118, D0123]. It comes in four types: agricultural, construction, loading and unloading, and housing projects [D0119].

**Who can get it.** Only a foreign worker already inside Jordan [D0118, D0119]. You can switch to the construction, loading or housing-projects permit from an open sector; from a closed sector (such as agriculture or bakeries) only once your permit has expired and only if you were not recruited after 1 January 2025 [D0126].

**Who it is not for.** It is not a way into Jordan from Bangladesh [D0118]. It does not cover domestic workers or QIZ garment workers, who cannot leave their sectors [D0018, D0110]. It is separate from the Syrian refugees' "flexible work permit", which is issued through cooperatives or the General Federation of Jordanian Trade Unions [D0128].

**How to apply.**

1. Pass the medical for expatriate workers so that the result shows "healthy" on the electronic system [D0120].
2. Apply to the entity MOL has appointed to organise and receive these applications, on MOL's form, with a copy of your valid passport; MOL (the Minister or his delegate) issues the permit, and the card names that entity in the employer box [D0120, KD0016].
3. Pay JD 800 a year (or part of a year) plus JD 50 for the Technical and Vocational Skills Development Commission [D0121, D0122].

**Rules once you have it.** You may work only in the trade on the card, and cannot move into other sectors; you can leave this permit only for a one-year specialised-skills permit [D0124, D0127]. The final-departure and exit-and-return rules do not apply to you [D0125].

**Scams and help.** The free permit sold in Bangladesh is a scam (see Scams below); for help see Where to get help below [D0118].

> **Need to know**
> - Which entity has MOL appointed to receive free-permit applications in 2026, where is its office, and is it still issuing new free permits during the June 2026 recruitment suspension?

### Egyptian and Syrian competition

Egyptians are the largest group of foreign workers and mostly work on farms [D0222]. They can enter Jordan without a visa, and some 100,000 work in construction on farm permits [D0227, D0226]. Day labourers gather at pick-up points such as Sweileh, hoping for JD 20 to 30 a day, sometimes unpaid, and share rooms at JD 20 a month each [D0218, D0219]. Syrians, Iraqis, spouses of Jordanians and Jordanian temporary-passport holders can move between any sectors, which Bangladeshis cannot [D0129].

### How men get these jobs, and what they pay

There is no route from Bangladesh into these sectors now (see Is it open now?) [D0091, D0096]. Among Egyptians the trade runs on bought permits: brokers, called "simsar", sell employer-specific permits for up to JD 3,000, and the worker then finds his own work [D0214]. Farm owners inflate their needs and sell spare agricultural permits for JD 300 to 500, and the workers move on to construction [D0225]. About 25,000 Bangladeshi men were working in Jordan in 2020 [D0188].

> **Need to know**

### Steps for a non-garment job (where one is open)

1. **Check the route is open.** Ask for the Jordanian MOL recruitment approval and check its date: only approvals issued before 1 June 2026, or jobs in the exempt sectors, can go ahead [D0093, D0092].
2. **Check the job is not on the closed list** above [D0103].
3. **The employer applies to MOL** with the recruitment form, a work contract in two signed copies, a valid professional licence (or an Agriculture Directorate letter for farms), your passport copy and a Social Security statement [D0135]. The employer pays the permit fee in advance after approval and lodges a guarantee of JD 300 per worker for nationalities restricted by the Residence Law [D0136, D0138]. The approval is valid two months (four for restricted nationalities), and the permit runs from the day you enter [D0137].
4. **Do the Bangladesh-side steps** on OEP (registration, training, the 3-day PDO, emigration clearance) as in Work visa: garment and factory worker, Steps 1 and 4 [D0234].
5. **On arrival** you give a non-conviction certificate from Bangladesh and pass the medical at a Ministry of Health-approved centre [D0136, D0135].
6. **Work only for the employer and trade on your permit.** Working without a permit (or on one expired up to 90 days), for another employer without MOL's permission, or in another trade is a violation [KC0034, KC0035, KC0036]. The Minister orders the worker deported at the cost of whoever he was caught working for, and he may not be recruited or employed in Jordan again for at least five years; the employer or broker is fined at least twice the sector's permit fee [KC0037, KC0038, KC0039, KC0040].
7. **Changing employer.** In agriculture and construction you can move within the sector during your permit with both employers' and MOL's agreement and a new one-year permit; in other sectors only after six months [D0140, D0141]. Within 60 days of your permit's expiry you need a clearance (bara'at dhimma) from the old employer to move; after 60 days you need none [KC0108, KC0109].
8. **If you are reported absent.** An employer may report you only if your social security is paid, not in the last two months of your permit, and for no more than 10% of its foreign workers; a false report blocks the employer, and you get a permit with a new one [D0144, D0143, D0145].
9. **Leaving.** Your permit is cancelled at the labour directorate with you and the employer present [D0146].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

**The "free visa" or free permit sold in Bangladesh:** A "free visa" is a visa you pay a broker for that is tied to no real job and no legal right to work [D0212]. In Jordan the same thing is a bought permit: workers are led to believe it lets them work anywhere, but not working for the sponsor in the permit's sector is grounds for deportation [D0215]. One worker paid JD 2,600 and never met his employer; another paid JD 3,100 and the broker kept his passport until he repaid [D0217, D0216]. MOL has seen workers pay around JD 2,000 to come and be left without a job or permit [D0229]. The real free permit cannot be bought from Bangladesh at all: it is issued only to workers already in Jordan [D0118].

**Fake Jordan jobs:** Unlicensed outfits sell fake Jordan jobs, as Surah Travels and Traders did for Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person (see Work visa: garment and factory worker, Scams, Fake Jordan factory jobs) [D0211]. Agencies may also take permit money and not process it [D0223].

#### Tried by workers

Buying a farm permit and then working in construction or a café is common, and most foreign day labourers work outside their permit's sector or without one [D0225, D0220]. Paying into social security does not replace a work permit [D0183]. Workers caught without a valid permit, or who changed employer or trade, are deported, and from 1 January 2027 those who have not regularised are flagged for deportation [A0135, R0176].

## Visit visa (through a host in Jordan)

Bangladesh is on MOI's list of restricted countries, so a Bangladeshi can't get a visa on arrival and needs MOI's approval before travelling [V0002, KE0001]. A Bangladeshi visiting a relative, friend or company in Jordan gets it as a visit visa that a host inside Jordan applies for on MOI's e-services, while the visitor is still outside Jordan [V0023, V0024, V0025]. The host can be a person (a Jordanian, or a foreigner with legal residence) or a company [V0032, V0033, V0034]. The visa never allows work (see Can you work?) [V0073].

### Cost and time

- MOI service charge: JD 2 per person for a regular application [V0039].
- Urgent application: JD 100 for up to five people, plus JD 15 for each extra person [V0040].
- Pay through eFawateercom (Jordan's online bill-payment system) or by direct payment [V0041].
- The visa fee itself is charged on top, at the rate in force on the day the application is processed [KE0003, KE0011]. MOI's published fees are JD 40 for one entry within 3 months, JD 60 for two entries within 3 months and JD 120 for multiple entries within 6 months [V0006, V0008, V0007]. Visa fees are set by reciprocity under the Minister's instructions [V0067].
- Processing takes about two weeks, and MOI may ask for more information meanwhile [KE0002, KE0005, KE0012].

> **Need to know**
> - Does a Bangladeshi pay the same visa fee as MOI's published rates (JD 40 single entry), and is it paid online with the approval or in cash at the airport? [V0006, KE0003]
> - What guarantee (كفالة, a deposit or bank guarantee) does MOI ask a host to pay for a restricted-nationality visitor, and how is it refunded? [V0049]

### Step 1: The host opens an MOI e-services account

1. **Register** at eservices.moi.gov.jo/MOI_EVISA or in the MOI e-services app (Android or iOS): click the user registration link and choose "individuals" (or "institutions" for a company) [V0026, V0042, V0043].
2. **Activate the account once:** a host in Amman goes to MOI's Directorate of Nationality (MOI is in Arjan, Amman); outside Amman, to his governorate office; a company must go to MOI itself [V0044, V0059]. An account opened from outside Jordan is activated by a link sent to the holder [KE0015].
3. **If the account data is wrong,** contact MOI by WhatsApp or email (see Where to get help) [V0053].

### Step 2: Gather the papers

The host uploads copies of [V0027, V0028, V0029, V0030, V0031, V0032, V0033, V0034]:

- the visitor's passport, valid at least 6 months;
- proof of the relationship and the reason for the visit;
- if the visitor lives outside Bangladesh (for example a worker in Saudi Arabia), his residence permit there, valid at least 6 months, and his exit and re-entry visa;
- a COVID-19 vaccination certificate (still on MOI's list);
- a host applying in person: his ID card and, if a foreigner, proof of his legal residence;
- a company: its valid professions licence.

> **Need to know**

> - Does MOI still ask for the COVID-19 certificate in practice? [V0031]

### Step 3: Apply and pay

1. **Submit** the application on the website, in the app or at an MOI service counter [V0025].
2. **Pay** the amount MOI tells you electronically (Cost and time) [V0039, V0041].
3. **Wait** while MOI consults its partner agencies; it may call the host for a personal interview or ask for more details [V0036, V0035].
4. **Read the decision** in the e-services account, by SMS or by email [V0037].

### Step 4: Check the approval and travel

1. **Print the approval notice** from the account and carry it [V0038, KE0004]. It carries no signature, because the approval is recorded electronically with the PSD, which also runs the borders, and with Jordanian embassies [V0046, V0047, V0048]. Since 19 March 2023 visas are issued by MOI online, not by embassies [G0071].
2. **Travel within two months** of the approval: a visa issued by MOI must be used to enter within two months, one issued by a Jordanian embassy within one month [V0065].
3. **Carry** a passport the Jordanian government recognises, valid at least 6 months [V0068, V0027].

### Step 5: After you arrive

1. **If you stay more than two weeks,** go to the PSD or the nearest police station (markaz amni) with your passport and fill in the declaration of your personal data and address [V0070].
2. **The host must report you** to the PSD within 48 hours of your arrival at his home, and again when you leave [V0071].
3. Missing either step is fined JD 200 [V0072].

### How long you can stay

A visitor can be legally present up to three months after arrival (one month, plus two more if he applies for an extension), and can ask for a further three-month extension [V0167].

No. A non-Jordanian found working without a work permit, or in a job other than the one on his permit, is deported immediately, and the employer or homeowner is fined up to twice the permit's value [V0073, V0074]. For whether a visit visa can become a work visa, see Changing visa type [R0178].

## Entry without prior approval: Gulf residents and Schengen or US visa holders

In January 2025 MOI let foreign nationals living in a GCC country (the Gulf Cooperation Council: Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain), the US, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea or any European country enter without prior approval if they hold a residence there valid at least four months, down from six [KE0006]. For nationals who still need prior approval, the decision names only Arab nationals: those living in a country whose citizens need no approval are treated like that country's citizens, if their residence there is valid four months [KE0007]. Before the change, Jordan's embassy in Japan told residents of Japan that the residence exception did not cover Bangladeshi passport holders, who still needed prior approval [KE0010]. MOI's own visit-visa rules still expect a Bangladeshi living in a third country to come through a host's application, attaching his residence there and his exit and re-entry visa [V0028, V0029].

Two other rules from the same decision:

- **Schengen or US visa holders** may enter whether or not they have used that visa [KE0008].
- **Domestic workers** who travel with a Jordanian employer living in the Gulf, or with a GCC-national employer, enter with the employer and get a three-month residence at the border [KE0009].

## Asylum

A Bangladeshi who came to Jordan to work cannot register as an asylum seeker: since 23 January 2019, by a government decision, UNHCR may not register new asylum seekers, and the bar covers anyone who entered for work, tourism, study or medical treatment [V0170, V0175]. Jordan is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention and has no refugee law [V0161].

### Why it doesn't work

- **No route to settle:** Jordan's 1998 agreement with UNHCR (amended 2014) only lets an asylum seeker stay while UNHCR decides; a recognised refugee must be resettled to a third country or go home [V0162, V0163, V0164].
- **Thousands are stuck:** UNHCR knew of 5,500 non-Syrians who wanted to register but could not since 2019; undocumented, many risk deportation [V0176, V0177].
- **Deportation:** in December 2015 Jordan deported about 800 Sudanese asylum seekers, saying asylum did not apply because they had entered on medical visas; many held UNHCR papers [V0179, V0180, V0181].
- **Fines and bans continue:** even refugees registered with UNHCR were charged overstay fines, now JD 3 a day, and those who leave without paying can be barred for five years [V0165, R0147, V0166].
- **Arrest:** a foreigner not legally present can be arrested and deported unless registered with UNHCR [V0169].

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi registered with UNHCR Jordan, and what happened to them?

### If you fear for your life at home

UNHCR's services are free, and every registration service needs an appointment through its helpline, 06 400 8000; urban registration is at its Khalda centre in Amman [V0171, KE0020, V0173]. Leaving Jordan, including going home, can close a UNHCR file unless you hold an MOI exit and re-entry permit [V0174].

## Sending money home

### Use a bank or licensed exchange house, never hundi

In Jordan only a company holding a final licence from the Board of the Central Bank of Jordan (CBJ) may deal in foreign currency, and a licensed exchange company may send and receive money transfers [R0130, R0133, R0131]. Hundi (informal money transfer outside banks and licensed exchange houses) runs outside this licence; practising exchange business without a licence is punished with one month to one year in prison and a JD 3,000-20,000 fine, doubled for a repeat [R0132]. Bangladesh Bank says that sending foreign currency outside the banking channel, by hundi or any other illegal route, is a punishable offence [R0137]. Bangladesh's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2012 counts keeping abroad money that could have been brought into Bangladesh as money smuggling, lists smuggling of local and foreign currency as an offence, and punishes money laundering with 4 to 12 years in prison and a fine of twice the money involved or up to Tk 10 lakh, whichever is more [R0134, R0135, R0136]. Bangladesh's government asks workers to send money only through legal channels [R0090].

People use hundi because it gives a better exchange rate and the family gets the money almost at once [R0139]. Money sent through a legal channel earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with a further 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps the government incentive [R0004, R0091]. Bangladesh Bank also acts at the receiving end: in 2022 it suspended the accounts of 480 mobile financial service agents over hundi, many of them bKash and Nagad agents [R0257, R0138].

You'll need:
- For a bKash wallet: the receiver's bKash number and full name exactly as registered [R0003].
- For a bank account: your own or a relative's account in Bangladesh [R0001].

1. **Get your wage into your hands or your wallet.** About two-thirds of garment workers in Jordan are still paid in cash; the rest are paid into a bank account or an e-wallet (a mobile money account) [R0255]. Jordan's e-wallets work together through JoMoPay (the Jordan Mobile Payments Switch), and CliQ is Jordan's instant payment system between banks and wallets [R0143, R0142]. All migrant garment workers surveyed sent part of their wages home, 62% at least two-thirds every month, after cashing out their wages [R0140, R0256].
2. **Choose the channel.** To send straight into a bank account, use a bank or exchange house that has a "drawing arrangement" (a remittance agreement) with a Bangladeshi bank [R0001]. Dutch-Bangla Bank lists Al Alami Exchange in Jordan among its drawing-arrangement partners, and money sent by Western Union can be collected at any Dutch-Bangla Bank branch in Bangladesh [KF0043, KF0044]. Through eFawateercom, a migrant can open a Western Union account, move money into it and send from it [R0141]. At some garment factories an exchange-house representative comes to the dormitory, so workers can send money without going into town [R0144].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which other banks and exchange companies in Jordan have drawing arrangements with Bangladeshi banks or are bKash partners, and where does a worker see the CBJ's list of licensed exchange companies [R0130]?
   > - Can a Jordanian e-wallet (through JoMoPay or CliQ) send money directly to a bKash or Nagad account in Bangladesh [R0142, R0143]?
3. **Send larger amounts less often: it costs less.** Across all countries, sending up to USD 200 cost 2.94% on average in April-June 2026, USD 201-500 cost 0.94%, and above USD 500 cost 0.50% [R0092].

   > **Need to know**

4. **Send.** If your exchange house is a bKash partner, give the receiver's bKash number and full name as registered, and hand over the cash; up to Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer [R0003]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [R0002].

> **Need to know**

**If something goes wrong:**
- bKash helpline: 16247, or +88-02-55663001 from a country not listed [R0005].

### Saving and pension in Bangladesh

#### NRCTA (Non-Resident Convertible Taka Account)

A taka savings, current or fixed-deposit account for Bangladeshis abroad, funded by remittances, at a scheduled bank's AD (Authorised Dealer, licensed for foreign exchange) branch or Offshore Banking Unit, under Bangladesh Bank's Circular 41 of 8 October 2026 [R0008, R0009]. A worker can open one before departure with no initial deposit, or during a visit home [R0010]. It earns market interest, and principal and interest can be sent abroad freely [R0011]. Banks may let someone in Bangladesh operate it under a power of attorney and may send the money to a non-resident nominee if the holder dies, and the account can be kept after coming home for good [R0012]. Banks may lend in taka against it, including to buy a home for your own use (not for relending, farming or real estate business) [R0013]. The trade-off: money sent into an NRCTA generally gets no 2.5% incentive unless specifically stated [R0014].

#### Wage Earner Development Bond (WEDB)

A 5-year government bond [R0093].
- Sizes: Tk 25,000, 50,000, 1 lakh, 2 lakh, 5 lakh, 10 lakh and 50 lakh [R0093].
- Where sold: Bangladesh Bank, any scheduled bank's AD branch, and abroad at branches of Bangladeshi banks, exchange houses and exchange companies [R0093].
- Who buys: a legal wage earner, in taka, with remittance sent through legal channels, in his own name or a named person's, with no ceiling [R0094].
- Profit at maturity, paid every six months: 12% for holdings up to Tk 15 lakh, 11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh and 9% above [R0095].
- Cashing early pays less (up to Tk 15 lakh): 8.70% after 6-12 months, 9.45% after 12-18, 10.20% after 18-24 and 11.20% after 24-60 [R0096].
- Other terms: profit and investment are income-tax free; you can name a nominee and borrow against the bond; it has a 40-50% death-risk benefit; and the principal can be taken in US dollars [R0097].

## Going home without valid papers

In Jordan the fine for staying without a valid residence permit is JD 90 a month, and JD 3 a day for part of a month; it is collected when you leave, and you cannot board until it is paid [KF0007, R0145, R0246]. Working without a valid work permit, or for another employer or in another job than the permit names, gets a worker deported [KC0037, R0150, R0177]. Until 1 December 2026 a Cabinet decision lets workers in breach leave Jordan for good without paying past work-permit fees and fines; garment workers in the export zones are left out (see The 2026 regularisation, below) [KF0001, KA0004, R0153].

### Help from Bangladesh

A close relative in Bangladesh can ask the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment to help bring a woman or man worker home where the case qualifies; the Ministry acts through the Embassy, WEWB or BMET, free, within 7 working days of receiving the letter [R0098]. The relative gives [R0098]:
- Proof that the person is a migrant worker.
- The workplace and residence address in Jordan.
- The recruiting agency's name and address.
- The employer's mobile number.

The family can also call WEWB's Probash-Bondhu call centre: 16135 in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad, 24 hours [R0042].

### The 2026 regularisation: leaving free before 1 December 2026

Jordan's Cabinet decided on 8 June 2026, and the Labour Minister announced on 14 June, a period for workers of all nationalities in breach of the labour and residence laws to put their status right or leave, running to 30 September 2026 [KF0001, R0152]. On 6 October 2026 the Cabinet extended it to 1 December 2026 for workers in all sectors, domestic workers included, who came to Jordan to work and held a work permit before [KF0001, KF0002]. MOL said about 49,000 workers in breach were registered with it; by 20 August it had issued or renewed about 64,000 work permits, and about 2,400 workers had left Jordan for good [R0166, R0170, R0169].

**If you leave for good ("exit without return"):**
- All work-permit fees and fines for past periods are waived up to the day you leave [KA0004, R0158].
- 100% of residence overstay fines are waived, including for domestic workers, provided your file is settled [KF0009].
- You can draw your SSC money directly (see If you lose the job) [R0160].
- MOL said a worker who leaves in the period pays nothing and only has to buy a ticket and leave [R0172].
- The cost: you cannot come back to Jordan for five years [R0165].

**If you stay and regularise:**
- 50% off the work-permit fees for all past periods, and 100% of late fines (for renewing an expired permit or moving employer) and residence overstay fines waived [KA0002, KA0003].
- You pay the fee for the current year's permit [R0157].
- Someone who came on a tourist, study or treatment visa and never had a work permit gets a one-year permit by paying the full permit fee plus JD 500 for all past periods; the employer or household applies at MOL [KF0003, R0179].
- A worker reported as a runaway (absconding) can use the decision without the old employer's approval if the work permit expired more than a year ago [R0161].
- No clearance letter from the old employer is needed to move when the permit has expired or been cancelled [R0163].
- Workers recruited from abroad who entered Jordan after 25 February 2025 may not use it to move to another sector [R0164].

**Who is left out:** non-Jordanian workers in garment and textile factories in the QIZs, development zones and free zones, and holders of specialised-skills permits [R0153]. Garment workers from those zones whose permit expired or was cancelled two or more years ago may move to any employer without a clearance [R0162].

### Step 2: Settle residence and work-permit fines

1. **Know what you owe in residence fines.** A foreigner who entered legally but has no temporary residence permit, an overstayer, or an annual-permit holder who did not apply to renew within one month of expiry is fined JD 90 for each month, and JD 3 a day for part of a month [KF0007, R0145, R0146]. 2. **Know what you owe in work-permit fees.** If a past employer never got you a permit, you cannot draw your social security money on leaving until all past permit dues and the penalty are paid [R0196].
3. **Use the waiver while it lasts.** Until 1 December 2026, leaving for good wipes these fees and fines, as above [KA0004, KF0009, KF0001].

### Step 3: Check for fines and travel bans before the airport

1. **Fines are collected at departure.** The residence fine is usually charged at the airport and you cannot leave until it is paid [R0246].
2. **Court cases.** Jordanian courts can impose prison for money disputes that elsewhere would be civil cases [R0248].

### If you are detained

Jordan's Minister of Interior can, on the PSD Director's recommendation, order any foreigner deported and detained until removal, without a court order [R0187]. Jordan has no separate immigration detention centres: people are held in police stations and prisons, called Reform and Rehabilitation Centres [R0189]. Bangladeshi women have been held at the Juweideh (Jwaideh) centre in Amman [R0193]. There is no time limit: the administrative governor decides case by case, and detention can last months or years [R0188]. An appeal to the Administrative Court is possible but too costly for most without free legal aid [R0191].

In July 2020, 103 Bangladeshi women were held at Juweideh: 87 for administrative reasons, 2 for judicial reasons and 14 convicted [R0193]. Those held for administrative reasons were usually held 15-20 days while their papers to go home were completed [R0194]. The Embassy has taken clothes and other necessities to Bangladeshi women held there [R0129]. When MOL deports a worker caught working in breach of the Labour Law, the deportation is carried out at the expense of the employer he was caught working for [KC0038].

### Bans after you leave

- **Leaving under the 2026 decision:** no return for five years [R0165].
- **Deportation flag:** no entry for five years [R0173].
- **Deported under the Labour Law:** no one may recruit or employ you again in Jordan for at least five years from the deportation [KC0039].

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Everyone aged 16 or over working under Jordan's Labour Law is covered by the SSC whatever their nationality, except workers whose relationship with the employer is irregular (casual) [KC0073, KF0031]. Work-injury cover includes accidents on the usual way to and from work, and the employer pays for it, at 2% of wages [KF0030, KF0013]. WEWB pays grants and helps with the migrant insurance for workers who left with BMET clearance or joined the Board [R0031, R0032]. The Embassy's labour wing pursues compensation; Bangladesh's labour wings recovered Tk 127.6 crore (1 crore is Tk 10 million) in 1,852 cases of death compensation, wages and benefits in 2023-24 [R0099].

### Check you are covered

1. **SSC.** Your employer must pay 14.25% of your wage into the SSC and deduct 7.5% from you [R0218]. Check that it does: register at eservices.ssc.gov.jo/registerIndividual with your personal number, the 10-digit number on a white sticker in your passport and on your residence permit [R0219, R0220].
2. **WEWB.** The death grant needs BMET emigration clearance or Board membership, and death in the country the clearance was for [R0031, R0033]. Workers with a BMET clearance card are members automatically; check at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check, and join online there if you left without clearance [R0034, R0035].
3. **Migrant insurance.** Workers who left with a BMET smart card from 1 January 2023 have the JBC migrant insurance: Tk 10 lakh on death, for 5 years from the card's date, for a one-time Tk 1,000 premium [KF0045, R0100]. Those who left between 19 December 2019 and 31 December 2022 were insured for 2 years only, for Tk 2 lakh (to 10 December 2020) or Tk 4 lakh [KF0046].

   > **Need to know**

### If you are injured or fall ill

1. **Treatment.** After a work accident the employer must take you to a treatment provider the SSC approves, report injuries the SSC lists to the nearest police station within 48 hours, notify the SSC in writing with the first medical report within 14 working days, and inform MOL [KF0033, KF0042, KF0015, R0210]. Most hospitals in Jordan are private and often ask for cash or a deposit first [R0247].
2. **If the employer does not report it,** you or your family can notify the SSC yourselves within four months of the injury; in the garment zones, report to the SSC office in the industrial zone [KF0034, R0213]. Keep every paper: receipts for the doctor, prescriptions, medicines, laboratory tests, hospital admission and transport to hospital [R0214].
3. **What the SSC pays.** The SSC covers treatment, hospital stay, transport to and from treatment and rehabilitation including artificial limbs, and pays a daily allowance of 75% of your insured daily wage while the injury keeps you off work, in hospital or at home on medical decision; the employer pays the first three days [KF0032, KF0014, KF0035, KF0019]. If the injury happened because the employer broke safety rules, the employer must repay the SSC all the medical costs [KF0039].
4. **Permanent injury.** SSC medical committees, a first-level one and an appeal one, assess the disability [KF0025]. Total permanent disability (75% or more) gives a monthly pension of 75% of your insured wage, plus 25% if you need someone's help in daily life [KF0037, KF0021]. Partial disability of 30% or more gives that share of the full pension; below 30%, a one-off payment of that share of the full pension times 36 months [KF0038, KF0022, KF0023]. As a non-Jordanian you can ask the SSC to replace the pension with a lump sum: the SSC lump sum or 36 months of the pension, whichever is more [KF0041, KF0027].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How does a worker who has gone home to Bangladesh apply to the SSC to swap a disability pension for the lump sum, and how is it paid [KF0041]?
5. **Sick leave and medical costs.** You get 14 fully paid sick days a year with a doctor's report, plus 14 more if you are in hospital or a medical committee certifies it [R0216]. In the garment sector the collective agreement makes the employer pay doctor's fees, tests and medicines, including outside the zone, and forbids deducting them from wages [R0215].
6. **Going home sick or injured.** On the worker's or family's application, with proof of clearance or membership and a passport copy, WEWB asks the Embassy to bring the worker home and in special cases helps with hospital admission [R0036]. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports takes injured or sick returnees on request [R0037].
7. **Treatment aid in Bangladesh.** Returnees with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment; apply within 1 year of return with medical reports and prescriptions, a bank statement, a copy of the arrival stamp and the passport [R0038, R0039].
8. **The insurance.** It pays 100% of Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability, and for partial disability [R0101, R0102, R0103]:
   - 90%: whole arm or leg.
   - 75%: arm at the elbow or leg below the knee.
   - 50%: one eye or one hand at the wrist.
   - 25%: lower jaw, or four fingers with thumb.
   - 15%: thumb, index or middle finger, or all toes.
   - 10%: ring or little finger, the big toe, any one other toe, or four toes including the big toe.

   If the worker later dies, the death claim pays the sum insured minus what was paid for the partial disability [R0104]. Claims go through WEWB to JBC, as in If a worker dies, step 8 [R0105].

### If you lose the job

1. **Your social security money.** At the end of a contract, or if either side ends it early, you get a lump sum from the SSC of 10% of your total insured wage for each year (12% after 120 monthly contributions, 15% from 216) [KC0074, R0217]. If you have drawn it once before, you can draw it again only after 24 months under the new contract, and not a third time before pension age [R0226]. If the employer never enrolled you in the SSC, it must pay you one month's last wage for each year worked [R0223].
2. **Claim it online before you fly.** During the 2026 regularisation period a worker leaving Jordan for good can apply online, while still in Jordan, under the SSC's "Permanent Departure from the Country with No Return" option, without going to MOL (otherwise MOL's no-objection is needed first, as in Work visa: garment and factory worker, Leaving at the end of the job, step 5); the form to have the money sent abroad is in the Forms and Applications section of the SSC website's electronic library, and you then follow the SMS instructions [R0183, R0184, R0185, R0186, KC0095].
3. **If the factory closes or stops paying.** Garment factory owners deposit a bank guarantee with MOL, which MOL can cash to pay wages, dues and tickets home; the Garment Union or the labour office in the zone asks MOL to do it [R0224, R0225]. When Achilles Apparel laid off workers in December 2023, 454 Bangladeshi workers got their overdue wages and social security after the Embassy pressed the case with ministries and international organisations for 18 months [R0232, R0233].
4. **Changing employer instead of going home:** see Work visa: garment and factory worker, Changing employer or leaving. Under the 2026 decision no clearance from the old employer is needed once the permit has expired [R0163].
5. **Job-loss insurance.** Tk 50,000 if you went from 1 January 2023, lose the job and return within 6 months, but not if you went entirely at the employer's cost [KF0047, R0053, R0103]. Claim through WEWB with proof of clearance or membership and a passport copy; free [R0049].
6. **From Bangladesh,** you or your family can apply to the Ministry about unpaid wages, allowances or insurance, with the incident, proof of status and the employer's details [R0108].

### What you can bring home

Bangladesh's Non-tourist Passenger Baggage Rules 2025 (SRO 261 of the National Board of Revenue, gazetted 3 June 2025) set what a returning migrant worker may bring [R0061].

#### Duty-free

- Baggage: a passenger aged 12 or over may bring up to 65 kg free of all duty and tax, plus up to 35 kg more of clothes, personal items, books and study materials [R0062].
- One of each [R0063]: a desktop or laptop (with one UPS, an uninterruptible power supply), printer, scanner, ordinary camera, oven or microwave, rice or pressure cooker, blender or juicer, sewing machine, fan, sports equipment, a TV up to 29 inches, a music system, one carpet up to 15 sq m and 1 carton (200) of cigarettes.
- Phones: 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one, the new one only once a year [R0063, R0064].
- Jewellery: declared gold and silver jewellery, once a year, no more than 12 pieces of one type [R0065, R0066].

#### With duty

- Gold or silver bar, once a year, declared: one gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola), at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g, or a silver bar of up to 234 g (20 tola); anything over, or a hidden bar, is confiscated, and an undeclared bar can lead to arrest [R0068, R0069].
- Bigger items, one of each [R0070]:
  - TV 30-36 inches: Tk 10,000 (37-42 inches: Tk 20,000).
  - Refrigerator or deep freezer: Tk 5,000.

  - Split AC up to 18,000 BTU (cooling capacity): Tk 15,000.
  - Washing machine or dishwasher: Tk 3,000.

#### Not allowed

A Bangladeshi passport holder may not bring in any alcohol [R0071, R0072].

If you missed it, you can still declare on the Schedule 1 form to a customs officer of at least Assistant Commissioner rank within 30 days of arrival [R0112].
