# India: migration guide

What happens to Bangladeshis who go to India, or are sold a way to go there, for Bangladesh passport holders, as of November 2026. It is built from Prothom Alo reporting (2024 to November 2026) and from official Indian and Bangladeshi sources: the High Commission of India in Dhaka, the Indian Visa Application Centres, India's immigration law, the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi and Bangladesh's own laws. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh); Rs is the Indian rupee, and an Indian lakh is likewise Rs 100,000; dollar amounts are converted to taka as the source converted them. The BSF (Border Security Force) is India's border guard; BGB (Border Guard Bangladesh) is Bangladesh's. A flag meeting is a meeting of the two forces at the border, where people held by one side are handed to the other [XI0040, XI0080]. A "push-in" is India forcing people across the border into Bangladesh without a handover [XI0031, XI0036]. The BJP (Bharatiya Janata Party) has governed the state of West Bengal since May 2026 [XI0111, XI0102]. Women and girls trafficked across the land border into Indian brothels are covered in the Illegal routes doc (see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc); this doc covers what is specific to living, working or being held in India.

## Background

### In one minute

India gives Bangladeshis no work visa for ordinary jobs: its employment visa is only for professionals appointed by the Indian government or sent on assignment by established organisations, with an invitation from a registered Indian company [OI0001, OI0002, OI0009]. What is open is short-stay visas, free of any Indian government fee, applied for at five Indian Visa Application Centres (IVAC) for a Tk 1,500 processing fee; India limited them from mid-2024 and reopened tourist visas from 28 June 2026 [OI0037, OI0038, OI0039, XI0023, OI0104]. The people who work in India crossed the land border with brokers' help, one Satkhira family paying a broker about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700), and have worked as labourers and masons in Delhi, Haryana and Kolkata; couples in Kolkata earned about USD 10 to 12 (about Tk 1,230 to 1,470) a day [XI0120, XI0113, XI0041, XI0081, XI0114, XI0121]. Under India's Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, entering without a passport and visa carries up to five years in jail, and the person accused must prove they are not a foreigner [OI0046, OI0048]. Some lived there 20 to 25 years and got Indian Aadhaar identity cards [XI0034]. Since May 2025 Indian states have rounded up people they call illegal Bangladeshis and pushed many across the border, and since 20 May 2026 West Bengal arrests people it treats as illegal entrants, targeting Muslims from Bangladesh; its chief minister said 4,800 had been sent back from detention centres by 7 June 2026 [XI0031, XI0044, XI0102, XI0115, XI0126]. Many of those caught have served jail terms in India and waited for Bangladesh to verify their nationality before being handed back at a flag meeting or land port, some to face a case at home for crossing illegally [XI0063, XI0038, XI0087, XI0064, XI0090]. A job offer in India has also been a lure for kidney removal and for sex trafficking (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Not for work: the High Commission of India lists medical, tourist, student and family visas for Bangladeshis, but India's employment visa is only for professionals, and the people who work in India crossed irregularly (see Which visas Bangladeshis can get) [OI0003, OI0005, OI0007, OI0008, OI0001, OI0002, XI0120].

#### Status

- **July-August 2024:** India tightened visas for Bangladeshis after the political change in Bangladesh, and by November 2024 limited tourist and other visas except for emergency medical treatment, bankers said [XI0023, XI0026]. Bangladeshi credit-card spending in India fell from Tk 92 crore in June 2024 to Tk 51 crore in September, by Bangladesh Bank data, and India fell from first to sixth place for such spending within six months [XI0024, XI0025].
- **May 2025:** push-ins resumed; 167 people were pushed in through five districts in four days (see Push-ins) [XI0031].
- **1 September 2025:** an order under India's Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, which replaced the Foreigners Act 1946, exempted Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian people from Bangladesh who sought shelter in India for fear of religious persecution and entered by 31 December 2024 from the passport and visa rules (see What the law says) [OI0102, OI0052].
- **20 May 2026:** West Bengal put the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) into force in the state and began arresting people it treats as illegal entrants (see West Bengal since May 2026) [XI0102, XI0108].
- **June 2026:** Bangladesh's state minister for foreign affairs, Shama Obaed, said Bangladesh had sent Delhi 12 to 13 letters and that India must follow the established mechanism; BGB raised push-ins at the 57th BGB-BSF director-general-level border conference in Delhi [XI0127, XI0128, XI0133].
- **28 June 2026:** India's High Commissioner Dinesh Trivedi said tourist visa applications could be submitted again from this date, at the five IVACs [OI0104, OI0037].

#### The channels

- **Medical, tourist, student and family visas:** the visas the High Commission of India in Dhaka lists for Bangladeshis (see Which visas Bangladeshis can get) [OI0003, OI0005, OI0007, OI0008].
- **Employment visa:** professionals only (see Work visas: professionals only) [OI0001, OI0002].
- **Crossing the land border through a broker:** the way people who work in India went, by their own accounts and those of BGB (see Crossing the border without papers) [XI0120, XI0081].

The figures below are of people held, pushed in or sent back:

- West Bengal's chief minister said on 7 June 2026 that 4,800 had been sent back from the state's temporary detention centres and 836 more were held [XI0126].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

No legal recruiting chain to India exists for ordinary jobs; the money goes to brokers who take people across the border [OI0001, XI0018, XI0120].

#### The broker at the border

- **Who:** brokers operate on both sides of the border [XI0018]. Women trafficked from Bangladesh cross by land or river with the help of agents at the border, and the ring leaders in both countries pay the brokers, an Indian law-enforcement official said in August 2025 [XI0068]. In September 2025 six men from Khaliajuri, Netrokona, caught on their way to India for Durga Puja, said three members of a trafficking ring from Jamburachhara, Sreemangal, offered to take each of them to India for Tk 10,000 [XI0075, XI0074]. In January 2026 BGB arrested a man at the Bokchar border, Chapainawabganj, accused of sending three men to Chennai to work as masons [XI0091, XI0092].
- **Price:** Tk 10,000 a person for six men going for Durga Puja, caught in Habiganj in September 2025; about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700) for a family from Satkhira that went to work, by its own account in June 2026 [XI0074, XI0113, XI0112]. People who left Bangladesh illegally after the fall of the Sheikh Hasina government in August 2024 paid brokers Tk 50,000 to Tk 25-30 lakh a person, according to their families and colleagues [XI0018].
- **Papers:** after women trafficked from Bangladesh arrive, local identity papers are made for them and they are sent to different cities, an Indian law-enforcement official said in August 2025 [XI0069]. Some people who crossed illegally got brokers to put a Bangladesh exit stamp and an Indian entry stamp in their passports, several sources told Prothom Alo in September 2024 [XI0022]. In India, using a forged or fraudulently obtained passport or visa carries two to seven years in jail and a fine of Rs 1 lakh to 10 lakh [OI0097, OI0098].
- **The broker's own risk in Bangladesh:** arranging anyone's exit through a place other than an official exit point carries up to ten years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 5 lakh, and taking money with a false promise of a job abroad up to five years and at least Tk 1 lakh, under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 [OI0093, OI0092, OI0091].

#### Offers that hide a crime

A job in India has been offered as a lure: a Dhaka ring promised jobs and took kidneys, and a man arrested in Chattogram confessed to taking women garment workers to India with offers of parlour jobs, where they were forced into sex work, police said (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **To cross:** about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700) for a Satkhira family that went to work (see The broker at the border) [XI0113].
- **Pay:** a Satkhira couple working as masons near Kolkata earned about USD 10 (about Tk 1,230) a day together; another couple in construction in Kolkata earned about USD 12 (about Tk 1,470) a day [XI0114, XI0121]. Raisul Islam of Satkhira said wages in India were higher than what he earned in Bangladesh [XI0112].
- **Housing:** Raisul Islam's family rented a room on the outskirts of Kolkata [XI0113].

### What ties you to the employer

The people in the reporting entered India illegally and worked as labourers and masons, many with no valid papers [XI0081, XI0114, XI0120]. What ended it was the authorities, local people or a landlord: Raisul Islam's family surrendered fearing harassment by local people and police, and after West Bengal's new government came to power a landlord told Mirajul Gazi's family to leave, and fearing attacks by local people they decided to go home [XI0119, XI0123]. Hospitals treating a foreigner as an in-patient must report them to India's Registration Officer [OI0103].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

#### What goes wrong most

- **Arrest and jail in India,** then a wait for Bangladesh to verify nationality, some since 2020 (see Jail and the wait for nationality verification) [XI0038].
- **Being pushed across the border** with no food or shelter, sometimes into no man's land, under threat of shooting and with rubber bullets fired (see Push-ins) [XI0045, XI0059, XI0060].
- **A case at home on return,** for crossing illegally (see Cases at home) [XI0090, XI0099].
- **Robbery and death on the crossing:** in August 2024 a retired judge said those taking him across took the Tk 60-70 lakh he carried and beat him, and a former student leader died of strangulation, Meghalaya police said, while crossing at Kanaighat, Sylhet [XI0020, XI0021].
- **Trafficking:** kidney removal after people were taken to Delhi (one victim's kidney was removed in Gujarat), and women held in hotels and brothels (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0010, XI0014, XI0067].
- **Visa-stage fraud:** people posing as High Commission staff sell "guaranteed" appointment slots, and applicants have submitted fake dollar endorsements and bank statements (see Scams at the visa stage) [OI0040, OI0033].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Years in India and an Aadhaar card make you safe."** Bangladeshis who had lived in India 20 to 25 years with Aadhaar cards and other documents had those papers kept by Indian police or the BSF and were pushed into Bangladesh without their families, according to BGB [XI0034, XI0035].
- **"A job offer in India is a job."** In the reported cases it led to kidney removal and to hotels where women were forced into sex work (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].
- **"An Indian work visa can be bought through an agent."** India's employment visa needs an invitation and proof of employment from a registered Indian company, and is for professionals only (see Work visas: professionals only) [OI0009, OI0010, OI0001].
- **"A court will hear you first."** West Bengal's chief minister said detainees need not be taken to court before expulsion [XI0116].

## Indian visas

### Which visas Bangladeshis can get

The High Commission of India in Dhaka lists these visas for Bangladeshis:

- **Tourist:** to visit relatives, friends or children, or for sightseeing, pilgrimage or any other purpose [OI0003, OI0004].
- **Medical and medical attendant:** for specialised treatment with an advance appointment from a recognised doctor, clinic or hospital; the patient's closest family members can get attendant visas (see Medical visa) [OI0005, OI0006].
- **Student:** for students admitted to a government or government-approved university, college or school in India [OI0007].
- **Entry (X):** for Bangladeshis married to Indian citizens or to their children; the applicant brings a marriage certificate or nikahnama apostilled (certified for use abroad) by Bangladesh's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and attested by the High Commission, plus the Indian spouse's voter card or Aadhaar card [OI0008, OI0020, OI0021].
- **Double entry (X-3):** for going to India to attend a visa appointment at a foreign embassy there that also covers Bangladesh; someone going on to a job in a third country shows the embassy's appointment confirmation and the employment contract [OI0022, OI0024, OI0023].
- **Transit:** Bangladeshi seamen joining a ship from an Indian port apply for a transit visa [OI0025].
- **Employment:** for professionals only (see Work visas: professionals only) [OI0001].

Since July 2024 India had limited tourist and other visas for Bangladeshis, except for emergency medical treatment, bankers said in November 2024 [XI0023]. In December 2024 Prothom Alo reported tourist visas closed, with medical and higher-education visas given only on a limited scale [XI0141]. In May 2026 five people who travel abroad regularly told Prothom Alo that India's visa had been closed for nearly two years [XI0142]. On 25 June 2026 India's High Commissioner said tourist applications could be submitted from 28 June 2026 and that urgent medical cases would still be helped on humanitarian grounds [OI0104, OI0105].

> **Need to know**
> - The IVAC site still showed an advisory in November 2026 that only limited appointment slots for urgent medical and emergency visas were open: are tourist visa appointments open to everyone in November 2026, and how long is the wait for a slot?

### How to apply

1. **Fill in the online form, book and pay:** fill in the online visa application form, then book an appointment and pay the Tk 1,500 IVAC processing fee at appointment.ivacbd.com; the Government of India charges Bangladeshis no visa fee [OI0027, OI0045, OI0038, OI0039].
2. **Gather the papers** for a tourist visa (other visa types have their own lists):
   - passport valid for at least six months with at least two blank pages, plus all old passports [OI0011, OI0012];
   - one recent 2x2 colour photo on a white background, and a copy of the national ID card or birth certificate [OI0011, OI0013];
   - a copy of an electricity, gas or landline telephone bill as proof of address [OI0014];
   - proof of funds: a six-month bank statement, or a dollar endorsement of USD 200 per person no older than three months, or an international credit or travel card with its statement [OI0015];
   - proof of profession: a government order, an employer's certificate, the business registration, a student ID card or retirement papers [OI0016].
   The High Commission can ask for more [OI0026].
3. **Submit in person** at the IVAC with the printed form and papers; the High Commission does not take applications itself, and forms by email are not accepted [OI0027, OI0028]. The IVACs, run by the State Bank of India, are in Dhaka, Chittagong, Rajshahi, Sylhet and Khulna [OI0028, OI0037]. Mobile phones, electronic devices and bags are not allowed inside and cannot be left there; bring only a plastic bag with your papers, and come alone [OI0043, OI0044].
4. **Wait 5 to 10 working days,** depending on the visa type [OI0029]. To check progress, contact the IVAC where you applied with the sticker number and passport number, or email visahelp.dhaka@mea.gov.in with a copy of the receipt [OI0030].
5. **Collect the passport** at the IVAC; if it comes back without a visa, the High Commission's decision is final [OI0031].

### Medical visa

- **Papers:** in addition to the general papers (see How to apply, item 2), the local doctor's medical documents and reports, an appointment letter from a hospital in India recognised by the Indian government or its medical accreditation bodies, and a copy of the invitation letter [OI0017, OI0018, OI0106].
- **Organ transplants** (kidney, liver and so on) need clearance from Bangladesh's Law Ministry and Ministry of Foreign Affairs [OI0019].
- **In India:** go only to the hospital named on the visa; changing hospital needs permission from the FRRO (Foreigners Regional Registration Office), or future travel to India may be restricted [OI0042].

### Work visas: professionals only

India's employment visa is for Bangladeshi professionals appointed by the Government of India, or on regular assignment with reputed organisations of both countries [OI0001, OI0002]. The applicant needs an invitation letter from the registered Indian company, firm or organisation stating the job, and proof of the employment, contract or engagement in India [OI0009, OI0010].

### Scams at the visa stage

- **Paid appointment slots:** agents pose as High Commission staff and sell "guaranteed" appointment slots; no one from the High Commission or its Assistant High Commissions offers slots by phone, social media or middlemen, and any demand beyond the Tk 1,500 IVAC fee for a slot or faster delivery is fraud [OI0040, OI0038]. Report such offers to 01325089975 [OI0041].
- **Fake bank papers:** the High Commission has caught applications with fake dollar endorsements and bank statements, and rejects at once any application with false information or fake papers [OI0033, OI0034].

### What can get a visa cancelled

- **Speaking against India:** on 25 March 2025 Indian authorities cancelled the visa of Md Azadur Rahman of Magura and sent him back through Changrabandha immigration, Cooch Behar, after an argument with a taxi driver at the stand by the checkpoint, over allegations that he spoke against India [XI0027, XI0028]. As the situation grew heated, Mekhliganj police took him to the police station, and an immigration official, quoted by Indian media, said his visa was cancelled after consulting the FRRO in Kolkata [XI0030, XI0029].
- **Political campaigning:** in 2019 Bangladeshi actor Ferdous Ahmed, in India on a business visa, joined a road show for a candidate of the Trinamool Congress, an Indian political party, in Raiganj, according to media reports; India's Home Ministry cancelled his visa and told him to leave, and reports say he was blacklisted [XI0134, XI0135, XI0136].
- **Changing hospital on a medical visa** without FRRO permission (see Medical visa) [OI0042].

### Overstaying

A foreigner who stays in India beyond the visa period, or breaks its conditions, can be jailed for up to three years, fined up to Rs 3 lakh, or both [OI0099, OI0047]. The offence can be compounded (closed by paying a sum set by the Indian government) before or during trial [OI0100]. Bangladeshi actor Gazi Abdun Noor stayed about nine months after his visa expired on 6 July 2018; India's Home Ministry told him to leave in 2019 and to apply for a new visa from Bangladesh [XI0137, XI0138].

> **Need to know**
> - What sums has India notified for compounding an overstay, and is a Bangladeshi who overstays barred from future visas?

## Crossing the border without papers

For women and girls taken across the border and sold, see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc.

### What the law says

- **In India:** a foreigner who enters without a valid passport and visa can be jailed for up to five years, fined up to Rs 5 lakh, or both, and any police officer of head constable rank or above can arrest a suspect without a warrant [OI0096, OI0046, OI0101]. If it is disputed whether someone is a foreigner, that person must prove they are not [OI0048].
- **The exemption:** Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian people from Bangladesh who sought shelter in India for fear of religious persecution and entered on or before 31 December 2024 are exempt from the passport and visa requirement, whether they came without papers or their papers have expired [OI0052].
- **In Bangladesh:** no one may leave Bangladesh without a valid passport, and doing so carries up to six months in jail, a fine of up to Tk 2,000, or both (see Cases at home) [OI0082, OI0107].

### Where people cross

- Families and colleagues of people who fled after August 2024 named Sylhet, Akhaura (Brahmanbaria), Netrokona, Haluaghat (Mymensingh), the Jashore area, Lalmonirhat and other northern border areas as the most used [XI0018].
- In reported attempts: border pillar 1961/7-S, Chunarughat, Habiganj (September 2025); Bokchar, Chapainawabganj Sadar (January 2026); Uttar Jhaukuti, Nageshwari, Kurigram, into Dhubri district, Assam (April 2026) [XI0073, XI0091, XI0094].
- Of West Bengal's 2,200 km of border with Bangladesh, 1,600 km is fenced and about 600 km is not, its chief minister said in May 2026 [XI0109]. India is giving BSF border posts devices to take fingerprints and eye scans of undocumented Bangladeshis for its Foreigners Registration Office, according to Indian security sources quoted by PTI (Press Trust of India) in July 2025 [XI0062]; Indian orders can require any foreigner to give photographs and biometrics [OI0050].

### What happens on the way

- **Robbed and beaten:** retired Appellate Division justice Shamsuddin Chowdhury (Manik), caught by local people at the Kanaighat border, Sylhet, on 23 August 2024, said he had a Tk 15,000 deal to cross, and those helping him took the Tk 60-70 lakh he carried and beat him on the other side [XI0019, XI0020].
- **Killed:** Ishaque Ali Khan (Panna), a former student leader, died on the night of 24 August 2024 crossing through the Dona border, Kanaighat; Meghalaya police sources said he was strangled [XI0021].

### Caught on the Bangladesh side

BGB hands people caught crossing to the local police, and a case is filed against them:

- 21 September 2025: six men from Boail, Khaliajuri, Netrokona, going for Durga Puja, caught before dawn at Chunarughat, Habiganj; a case was filed against them for attempting to cross illegally [XI0073, XI0074, XI0076].
- 14 January 2026: three men going to Chennai as masons, and the man accused of sending them, arrested 200 yards inside the zero line (the border line itself) at Bokchar, Chapainawabganj; a case was filed against all four [XI0091, XI0092, XI0093].

## Working in India without papers

### Where people work

- **Delhi:** 24 people (8 men, 8 women and 8 children) brought back through Phulbari, Kurigram, in May 2025 had gone to Delhi for work [XI0040, XI0041].
- **Haryana:** most of 31 people handed back at Darshana, Chuadanga, in October 2025 had lived and worked as labourers in Haryana until Haryana police detained them [XI0081, XI0082].
- **Kolkata:** a couple from Satkhira and another couple worked as masons and construction workers in and around the city [XI0113, XI0114, XI0121].
- **Chennai:** masons' work was the plan of three men caught at Bokchar in January 2026 [XI0092].

Men, women and children from Bangladesh enter India illegally because of poverty and live by presenting themselves as Bengalis until they are caught, an Indian police officer said in August 2025 [XI0070].

## Arrest, detention and being sent back

Anyone living illegally in India faces action under local law, whatever their nationality, and must be repatriated under law, procedure and the two countries' arrangements, India's foreign ministry said in 2025 and 2026 [XI0039, XI0101]. The law is India's Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, which replaced the Foreigners Act 1946 and lets the Indian government hand its powers to state governments and their officers [OI0102, OI0049]. Orders under it can require a foreigner to live in a set place and restrict their movements [OI0051].

### Jail and the wait for nationality verification

1. **Arrest and sentence:** 22 Bangladeshis handed back at Tamabil, Sylhet, in August 2025 had been arrested by the BSF and Indian police and served terms in Tura jail, Meghalaya; many of 19 handed back at Chauddagram, Cumilla, in October 2025 had served sentences in Indian jails [XI0063, XI0087].
2. **Nationality verification:** India sends Bangladesh the details of the people it holds, more than 2,800 by June 2026 according to media, and each must be verified as a Bangladeshi citizen before return [XI0130, XI0038, XI0089]. Some on India's list of 2,369 had waited since 2020, and in May 2026 more than 2,860 cases were pending with Bangladesh, some for over five years, India's foreign ministry said [XI0038, XI0100]. In Assam's own affidavit to the Supreme Court, cited by a local lawyer, those whose Bangladesh address is unknown cannot be deported until their nationality is verified and a travel permit arranged [XI0048, XI0049].
3. **Travel Permit:** for a detainee or trafficking victim being sent home, the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi issues a Travel Permit (a travel document) on an application with proof of nationality, the Indian court release order or deportation order, two 35x45 mm photos and a fee of Rs 1,800 paid by debit or credit card; it issues the permit only after the appropriate authority in Bangladesh has verified the person's nationality [OI0063, OI0064, OI0065]. India's home ministry says a deportee is first given a travel document by their own country's embassy so that its officials can receive them [OI0056].
4. **Handover:** at a land port or flag meeting (see Through a flag meeting or land port) [XI0064, XI0086].

Bangladesh's Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 gives a migrant worker, especially one detained abroad, the right to return home and to help from the Bangladesh mission; the cost of bringing someone home can be recovered from that person [OI0088, OI0089].

> **Need to know**
> - Which Bangladeshi body verifies the nationality of people held in India (the Ministry of Home Affairs, the police Special Branch or the district administration), how long it takes, and how can a family in Bangladesh send papers to speed up a relative's case?

### Assam: detention camp and Foreigners Tribunals

- **Detention:** Assam police began a crackdown on "foreigners" on 23 May 2025 and took those detained to Matia, Goalpara district, the state's largest detention camp for alleged illegal migrants, according to Al Jazeera [XI0043].
- **Who counts as a foreigner:** Bengali-speaking residents must prove they came to Assam before 24 March 1971, and Foreigners Tribunals can declare people foreigners over minor spelling mismatches in documents [XI0046, XI0047].
- **The law used:** Assam's chief minister cites a 1950 law giving district commissioners power to expel certain undocumented migrants; Assam High Court lawyer Waliullah Laskar said it applies only to those who entered illegally or overstayed a visa [XI0050, XI0051].
- **Hindus:** Assam told police not to send the cases of non-Muslims, especially Hindus, who entered before 31 December 2014 to the tribunals [XI0056]. India's 2019 Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) gives Hindu, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist, Parsi and Christian people from Bangladesh, Pakistan and Afghanistan who came by 31 December 2014 because of religious persecution a faster route to citizenship (five years' residence instead of eleven); it does not cover Muslims [OI0053, OI0055, OI0054]. The same six communities who entered by 31 December 2024 are exempt from the passport and visa rules (see What the law says) [OI0052].

### West Bengal since May 2026

- **The rule:** on 20 May 2026 chief minister Suvendu Adhikari put the CAA into force in West Bengal, so everyone who entered India after 31 December 2024 is treated as illegal [XI0102]. He said India's government had sent the state a guideline on 14 May 2025 to hand illegal Bangladeshi entrants directly to the BSF, which the previous state government did not act on [XI0104, XI0105]. The communities covered do not include Muslims from Bangladesh (see Assam: detention camp and Foreigners Tribunals) [OI0054]. Adhikari said police cannot harass those covered [XI0110].
- **"Detect, delete, deport":** state police arrest and detain those not covered and hand them to the BSF, which arranges deportation with Bangladesh's border force, Adhikari said [XI0108, XI0103]. The drive targets only Muslim Bangladeshis, and detainees need not be taken to court before expulsion, Adhikari said [XI0115, XI0116]. Since India's Supreme Court ruled in December 2025 that foreigners have hardly any rights under the constitution, people facing expulsion must prove why they should not be sent to Bangladesh, according to Al Jazeera [XI0117]; India's 2025 Act puts the burden of proving they are not a foreigner on the person accused [OI0048].
- **Detention centres:** temporary detention centres were set up in every district; by 7 June 2026, 4,800 people had been sent back from them and 836 were held, Adhikari said [XI0126]. Thousands were detained across the state in the two weeks after his order [XI0118].
- **Hakimpur:** since late May 2026 alleged Bangladeshi migrants have gathered at Hakimpur, about 80 km from Kolkata, and other border checkpoints; a police officer there said 250 to 300 arrive daily and have their citizenship checked and biometrics taken for a digital list [XI0124, XI0125]. Raisul Islam's family surrendered on their own, fearing harassment by local people and police, and security forces took them to a detention centre 18 km away [XI0119, XI0132].
- **Consular help:** the Bangladesh Deputy High Commission in Kolkata is at 9 Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujib Sarani, Kolkata 700017, phone +91 33 4012 7500, email mission.kolkata@mofa.gov.bd [OI0070, OI0071, OI0072].

Human Rights Watch's Asia director Elaine Pearson said detainees should have the right to legal aid even without valid papers, and called the process illegal [XI0131].

### Other states

- **Gujarat:** Ahmedabad police said they had identified more than 250 people they said were Bangladeshis living illegally, and expulsion was under way, a police officer told local media [XI0052, XI0053]. 78 people left by the BSF on a Sundarbans char (a river island) in May 2025 said they had been flown and shipped blindfolded from Gujarat [XI0032, XI0033].
- **Maharashtra:** police detained seven Muslims as foreigners in May 2025 and handed them to the BSF; West Bengal's government intervened and they were brought back from the border on 15 June, a Trinamool Congress legislator said [XI0054, XI0055].
- **Haryana and Meghalaya:** Haryana police detained Bangladeshi labourers and handed them to the BSF, and Bangladeshis arrested in Meghalaya served terms in Tura jail (see Jail and the wait for nationality verification) [XI0082, XI0063].

### Push-ins

- **May 2025:** 167 people were pushed in through Khagrachhari (73), Kurigram (46), Sylhet (23), Moulvibazar (15) and Chuadanga (10) from 4 to 7 May; on 9 May the BSF left 78 people on Mandarbaria char in the western Sundarbans, ill after days without food, one with a broken hand and several with marks of torture [XI0031, XI0032]. By BGB's information those pushed in included Indian, Myanmar and Bangladeshi nationals [XI0034]. Bangladesh protested with a diplomatic note on 9 May [XI0036].
- **In no man's land:** people pushed out of Assam on 27 May 2025 stood knee-deep in water for 12 hours with no food or shelter; one of them, Ali, said the BSF threatened to shoot them if they tried to go back toward India, and that Indian security personnel fired rubber bullets when they begged not to be pushed [XI0045, XI0059, XI0060].
- **June 2026:** BGB said it had stopped at least 18 BSF push-in attempts involving about 180 people since 4 June [XI0129].
- **Evidence:** the BSF is getting about 5,000 body cameras to record the sending back of undocumented Bangladeshis, according to Indian security sources in July 2025 [XI0061].
- **Before:** push-ins were frequent around 2002-03, when the BJP-led NDA (National Democratic Alliance) governed India, writer Kallol Mustafa wrote in May 2025 [XI0037].

Faisal Mahmud, spokesperson of the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi, said in June 2025 that Bangladesh had told India the BSF's push-ins did not follow international procedure [XI0058]. BGB's Cumilla battalion commander said in October 2025 that BGB had asked the BSF not to push anyone across and to send it the names of any illegal Bangladeshi it held [XI0088].

## Trafficked and held in India

Women and girls sold into Indian brothels are covered in the Illegal routes doc (see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc, and "Where victims and families get help" in the same doc). What follows is what happened to people inside India.

### Kidney removal

Dhaka Metropolitan Police (DMP) said in May 2024 that a ring took poor people to India on promises of jobs, held them in Delhi and took their kidneys, and had done this to 10 people [XI0001]. The case of one victim, Robin, by DMP's account:

1. **The offer:** in April 2023, at a tea stall behind Shah Ali Market, Mirpur-10, Robin was talking about his family's hardship when Md Masum, sitting nearby, said he had a business in India and could give him a job; they kept in touch by phone until Robin agreed [XI0004, XI0005, XI0006].
2. **The "medical":** Masum said going to India needed medical tests, took him to a Dhaka hospital in September 2023, and after the check the ring took his passport for the visa [XI0007].
3. **In India:** Robin was taken to Faridabad, Delhi, persuaded to sell a kidney for a promised Tk 6 lakh, tested at a New Delhi hospital, and kept in a house in Muktinagar, Gujarat [XI0008, XI0009].
4. **The surgery:** on 4 March 2024 the ring, using threats, had a kidney removed at a hospital in Gujarat; he was released after four days, held 10 to 11 more days, and learned the ring had sold the kidney to brokers for a large sum [XI0010, XI0011, XI0013].
5. **Payment and return:** ring members in Bangladesh paid his wife Tk 3 lakh, half the promised sum, and he came home [XI0008, XI0012].

Robin filed a case at Dhanmondi police station, and police arrested three members in Dhanmondi and Bagerhat on 11 and 12 May 2024; 10 to 12 people named in the case, including Masum, were absconding [XI0002, XI0003]. An Indian medical visa for an organ transplant needs clearance from Bangladesh's Law Ministry and Ministry of Foreign Affairs [OI0019].

### Women held for sex work

- **Hotels in Jharkhand:** women garment workers in Chattogram were taken across the Jashore border to hotels in Jharkhand with offers of higher-paid parlour jobs, forced into sex work and tortured if they refused, according to the police; one woman and a friend escaped, and her case led to the arrest of Md Tarek (34) on 24 June 2024 [XI0014, XI0015, XI0017, XI0139].
- **Brothels in Hyderabad:** police in Hyderabad rescued a Bangladeshi teenage girl from forced sex work in August 2025, the fourth such rescue in the city, from red-light areas in Khairatabad, Chaderghat and Bandlaguda [XI0066, XI0067]. Trafficking ring leaders operate in Hyderabad's Attapur, Bandlaguda, Chintalmet, Himayatsagar Road and Champapet, Prothom Alo reported [XI0072].
- **Sold in other cities:** women trafficked from Bangladesh are sold to brokers in Kolkata, Delhi, Hyderabad, Mumbai and other cities, and a study by India's Central Social Welfare Board found 2.7% of women commercially sexually exploited in India are Bangladeshi [XI0071].
- **Jailed after rescue:** in November 2010 the family of a 19-year-old Faridpur woman learned she was held in Dum Dum Central Jail, Kolkata, after police rescued her from a brothel; she later killed herself in that jail [XI0077].

A rescued victim is sent home on the same Travel Permit as a detainee (see Jail and the wait for nationality verification, item 3) [OI0063]. Bangladesh's 2012 trafficking law has been replaced by the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 [OI0095].

## Coming home

### Through a flag meeting or land port

1. **The handover:** the BSF hands people to BGB at a flag meeting on the zero line, or Indian immigration hands them over at a land port such as Tamabil, on the Bangladeshi authorities' application [XI0080, XI0064].
2. **Verification:** the BSF sends BGB the names, and BGB checks them through various means and confirms each person is Bangladeshi [XI0089].
3. **The police station:** BGB usually hands them to the local police station [XI0083, XI0097, XI0090].
4. **Home or court:** what follows has varied:
   - handed to families (Phulbari, Kurigram, May 2025) or after the legal process (Tamabil, August 2025; Kachakata, Kurigram, April 2026) [XI0042, XI0065, XI0097];
   - kept at the union parishad (the local council office) while police tell relatives and help them home (Darshana, Chuadanga, October 2025) [XI0084];
   - a case filed by BGB and sent to court the next day (Chauddagram, Cumilla, October 2025) [XI0090];
   - sent to court after the legal process: two men caught by the BSF on 27 April 2026 trying to come home without passports at Boirchuna, Pirganj, Thakurgaon [XI0098, XI0099].

Two men who crossed into Assam on 12 April 2026, by their families' account after losing their way in bad weather, were handed back after their families told the Chouddoghori BGB outpost, BGB wrote to the BSF, and the two forces held a flag meeting [XI0094, XI0095, XI0096].

For help after reaching home, see "Help after you get home" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.

### Cases at home

- **The offence:** the Bangladesh Passport Order 1973 bars anyone from leaving Bangladesh without a valid passport, and breaking it carries up to six months in jail, a fine of up to Tk 2,000, or both [OI0082, OI0107]. The Control of Entry Act 1952 is not the law for returnees: it covers Indian citizens entering Bangladesh [OI0084, OI0085].
- **Legal aid:** the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 counts a Bangladeshi who has come back from abroad, employed there or not, as a migrant worker, and gives migrant workers and people defrauded in the name of migration the right to reasonable legal aid [OI0086, OI0087].
- **Against the broker:** the same Act punishes whoever arranged the crossing (see The broker at the border), and a migrant harmed by a breach of it can also file a civil case for compensation [OI0093, OI0090].

## If you lose your passport in India

To get home, apply in person for a Travel Permit at the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi with an application to the Head of Mission (Consular), a copy of the police FIR (first information report), a photocopy of the lost passport, a copy of the visa, two passport-size colour photos and the Form C your hotel gave you; the fee is Rs 1,800 by debit or credit card [OI0068, OI0069, OI0064].

## If someone dies in India

To bring a body home, the relative sends the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi (consular.newdelhi@mofa.gov.bd) a request letter, an undertaking from the relative and the undertaker, the hospital's death certificate, passport and visa copies of the deceased and the relative, and a no-objection certificate from the local police station; there is no fee [OI0067, OI0066, OI0059].
