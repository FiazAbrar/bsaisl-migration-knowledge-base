# India: migration guide

What happens to Bangladeshis who go to India, or are sold a way to go there, for Bangladesh passport holders, as of November 2026. This first version is built only from Prothom Alo reporting (2024 to November 2026) and still needs official sources. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

Tk is the Bangladeshi taka (1 lakh is Tk 100,000; 1 crore is 100 lakh); dollar amounts are converted to taka as the source converted them. The BSF (Border Security Force) is India's border guard; BGB (Border Guard Bangladesh) is Bangladesh's. A flag meeting is a meeting of the two forces' local commanders at the border, where people held by one side are handed to the other [XI0040, XI0080]. A "push-in" is India forcing people across the border into Bangladesh without a handover [XI0031, XI0036]. The BJP (Bharatiya Janata Party) has governed the state of West Bengal since May 2026 [XI0111, XI0102]. Women and girls trafficked across the land border into Indian brothels are covered in the Illegal routes doc (see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc); this doc covers what is specific to living, working or being held in India.

## Background

### In one minute

The reporting shows no legal route for Bangladeshis to go to India for work, only short-stay visas and irregular crossing [XI0023, XI0120]. Since July 2024 India has limited visas for Bangladeshis to little more than emergency medical treatment, according to bankers [XI0023]. The people who work there go across the land border through brokers, for Tk 10,000 a person or about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700) for a family, and have worked as labourers and masons in Delhi, Haryana and Kolkata; couples in Kolkata earned about USD 10 to 12 (about Tk 1,230 to 1,470) a day [XI0074, XI0113, XI0041, XI0081, XI0114, XI0121]. Some lived there 20 to 25 years and got Indian Aadhaar identity cards [XI0034]. Since May 2025 Indian states have rounded up people they call illegal Bangladeshis and pushed many across the border, and since 20 May 2026 West Bengal arrests anyone it treats as an illegal entrant who is not covered by India's Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), which excludes Muslims from Bangladesh; its chief minister said 4,800 had been sent back from detention centres by 7 June 2026 [XI0031, XI0044, XI0102, XI0106, XI0108, XI0126]. Those caught are jailed in India, wait for Bangladesh to verify their nationality, and are handed back at a flag meeting, some to face a case at home for crossing illegally [XI0063, XI0038, XI0087, XI0090]. A job offer in India has also been a lure for kidney removal and for sex trafficking (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Not for work: the reporting describes no work visa or recruitment channel for Bangladeshis in India, only visas for treatment and other short visits, and irregular crossing [XI0023, XI0120].

#### Status

- **July-August 2024:** India tightened visas for Bangladeshis after the political change in Bangladesh, and by November 2024 limited tourist and other visas except for emergency medical treatment, bankers said [XI0023, XI0026]. Bangladeshi credit-card spending in India fell from Tk 92 crore in June 2024 to Tk 51 crore in September, by Bangladesh Bank data, and India fell from first to sixth place for such spending within six months [XI0024, XI0025].
- **May 2025:** push-ins resumed; 167 people were pushed in through five districts in four days (see Push-ins) [XI0031].
- **20 May 2026:** West Bengal put the CAA into force in the state and began arresting people it treats as illegal entrants (see West Bengal since May 2026) [XI0102, XI0108].
- **June 2026:** Bangladesh's state minister for foreign affairs, Shama Obaed, said Bangladesh had sent Delhi 12 to 13 letters and that India must follow the established mechanism; BGB raised push-ins at the 57th BGB-BSF director-general-level border conference in Delhi [XI0127, XI0128, XI0133].

#### The channels

- **Medical and other short-stay visas:** the visas still issued, according to bankers in November 2024 (see Short-stay visas) [XI0023].
- **Study:** a Bangladeshi father was in India in March 2025 to bring home his son, who studies in Kurseong, Darjeeling [XI0028].
- **Crossing the land border through a broker:** the way people who work in India went, by their own accounts and those of BGB (see Crossing the border without papers) [XI0120, XI0081].

#### Size

No count of Bangladeshis living in India appears in the reporting. The figures are of people sent back:

- India's foreign ministry said on 22 May 2025 it had a list of 2,369 Bangladeshis ready to be sent back, and on 7 May 2026 that more than 2,860 nationality verification cases were pending with Bangladesh [XI0038, XI0100].
- West Bengal's chief minister said on 7 June 2026 that 4,800 had been sent back from the state's temporary detention centres and 836 more were held [XI0126].
- Assam's chief minister said more than 300 Muslims had been pushed into Bangladesh from May 2025; most of those pushed in were Indian citizens, according to Al Jazeera [XI0044, XI0140].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis does Bangladesh's High Commission in New Delhi estimate live in India without papers, and how many were returned through flag meetings in 2025 and 2026?

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

There is no recruiting chain for India in the reporting: no visa is sold. The cut goes to brokers who take people across the border [XI0018, XI0120].

#### The broker at the border

- **Who:** brokers operate on both sides of the border [XI0018]. Women trafficked from Bangladesh cross by land or river with the help of agents at the border, and the ring leaders in both countries pay the brokers, an Indian law-enforcement official said in August 2025 [XI0068]. In September 2025 six men from Khaliajuri, Netrokona, said three men from Jamburachhara, Sreemangal, whom BGB described as members of a trafficking ring, offered to take each of them to India for Tk 10,000 [XI0075]. In January 2026 BGB arrested a man at the Bokchar border, Chapainawabganj, accused of sending three men to Chennai to work as masons [XI0091, XI0092].
- **Price:** Tk 10,000 a person in Habiganj in September 2025; about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700) for a family from Satkhira, by its own account in June 2026 [XI0074, XI0113]. Awami League leaders and others fleeing after August 2024 paid Tk 50,000 to Tk 25-30 lakh a person, according to their families and colleagues [XI0018].
- **Papers:** after women trafficked from Bangladesh arrive, local identity papers are made for them and they are sent to different cities, an Indian law-enforcement official said in August 2025 [XI0069]. Some people who crossed illegally got brokers to put a Bangladesh exit stamp and an Indian entry stamp in their passports, several sources told Prothom Alo in September 2024 [XI0022].

#### Offers that hide a crime

A job in India has been offered as a lure: a Dhaka ring promised jobs and took kidneys, and a Chattogram couple promised parlour jobs and forced women into sex work (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].

> **Need to know**
> - How do workers who cross find jobs in Delhi, Haryana or Chennai: through relatives, contractors at the border, or the brokers themselves, and do the brokers take a cut of wages?

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

- **To cross:** Tk 10,000 a person, or about USD 250 (about Tk 30,700) for a family (see The broker at the border) [XI0074, XI0113].
- **Pay:** a Satkhira couple working as masons near Kolkata earned about USD 10 (about Tk 1,230) a day together; another couple in construction in Kolkata earned about USD 12 (about Tk 1,470) a day [XI0114, XI0121]. Raisul Islam of Satkhira said wages in India were higher than what he earned in Bangladesh [XI0112].
- **Housing:** families rented a room on the outskirts of Kolkata [XI0113].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Bangladeshi labourer earn a day in Delhi, Haryana and Chennai in 2026, and how is money sent home: hundi (informal transfer), or carried across?

### What ties you to the employer

The people in the reporting entered India illegally and worked as labourers and masons, many with no valid papers, so no employer sponsored their stay [XI0081, XI0114, XI0120]. What ended it was the authorities, local people or a landlord: Raisul Islam's family surrendered fearing harassment by local people and police, and after West Bengal's new government came to power a landlord told Mirajul Gazi's family to leave, and fearing attacks by local people they decided to go home [XI0119, XI0123].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

#### What goes wrong most

- **Arrest and jail in India,** then a wait for Bangladesh to verify nationality, some since 2020 (see Jail and the wait for nationality verification) [XI0038].
- **Being pushed across the border** with no food or shelter, sometimes into no man's land, with shots fired (see Push-ins) [XI0045, XI0059, XI0060].
- **A case at home on return,** for crossing illegally (see Coming home) [XI0090, XI0099].
- **Robbery and death on the crossing:** in August 2024 a retired judge said those taking him across took the Tk 60-70 lakh he carried and beat him, and a former student leader died of strangulation, Meghalaya police said, while crossing at Kanaighat, Sylhet [XI0020, XI0021].
- **Trafficking:** kidney removal in Delhi and Gujarat, and women held in brothels and hotels (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0010, XI0014].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"Years in India and an Aadhaar card make you safe."** Bangladeshis who had lived in India 20 to 25 years with Aadhaar cards and other documents had those papers kept by Indian police or the BSF and were pushed into Bangladesh without their families, according to BGB [XI0034, XI0035].
- **"A job offer in India is a job."** In the reported cases it led to kidney removal and to hotels where women were forced into sex work (see Trafficked and held in India) [XI0001, XI0014].
- **"A court will hear you first."** West Bengal's chief minister said detainees need not be taken to court before expulsion [XI0116].

## Short-stay visas

### Who gets a visa now

Since July 2024 India has limited tourist and other visas for Bangladeshis, except for emergency medical treatment, bankers said in November 2024 [XI0023]. Travel to India for treatment and tourism fell sharply as a result, people in the sector said in March 2025 [XI0026].

> **Need to know**
> - Where does a Bangladeshi apply for an Indian visa in 2026 (the Indian Visa Application Centres in Bangladesh), what does each visa cost, and how long does a medical visa take?

### What can get a visa cancelled

- **Speaking against India:** on 25 March 2025 Indian authorities cancelled the visa of Md Azadur Rahman of Magura and sent him back through Changrabandha immigration, Cooch Behar, after an argument with a taxi driver at the stand by the checkpoint, over allegations that he spoke against India [XI0027, XI0028]. Police took him from a protesting crowd to the police station, and an immigration official said his visa was cancelled after consulting the FRRO (Foreigners Regional Registration Office) in Kolkata [XI0030, XI0029].
- **Political campaigning:** in 2019 Bangladeshi actor Ferdous Ahmed, in India on a business visa, joined a Trinamool Congress candidate's campaign in Raiganj; India's Home Ministry cancelled his visa and told him to leave, and reports say he was blacklisted [XI0134, XI0135, XI0136].
- **Overstaying:** Bangladeshi actor Gazi Abdun Noor stayed about nine months after his visa expired on 6 July 2018; India's Home Ministry told him to leave in 2019 and to apply for a new visa from Bangladesh [XI0137, XI0138].

> **Need to know**
> - What fine or ban does India impose on a Bangladeshi who overstays a visa by days or months in 2026?

## Crossing the border without papers

For women and girls taken across the border and sold, see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc.

### Where people cross

- Families and colleagues of people who fled after August 2024 named Sylhet, Akhaura (Brahmanbaria), Netrokona, Haluaghat (Mymensingh), the Jashore area, Lalmonirhat and other northern border areas as the most used [XI0018].
- In reported attempts: border pillar 1961/7-S, Chunarughat, Habiganj (September 2025); Bokchar, Chapainawabganj Sadar (January 2026); Uttar Jhaukuti, Nageshwari, Kurigram, into Dhubri district, Assam (April 2026) [XI0073, XI0091, XI0094].
- Of West Bengal's 2,200 km of border with Bangladesh, 1,600 km is fenced and about 600 km is not, its chief minister said in May 2026 [XI0109]. India is giving BSF border posts devices to take fingerprints and eye scans of undocumented Bangladeshis for its Foreigners Registration Office, according to Indian security sources quoted by PTI (Press Trust of India) in July 2025 [XI0062].

### What happens on the way

- **Robbed and beaten:** retired Appellate Division justice Shamsuddin Chowdhury (Manik), caught by local people at the Kanaighat border, Sylhet, on 23 August 2024, said he had a Tk 15,000 deal to cross, and those helping him took the Tk 60-70 lakh he carried and beat him on the other side [XI0019, XI0020].
- **Killed:** Ishaque Ali Khan (Panna), a former student leader, died on the night of 24 August 2024 crossing through the Dona border, Kanaighat; Meghalaya police sources said he was strangled [XI0021].

### Caught on the Bangladesh side

BGB hands people caught crossing to the local police with a case for attempting to cross illegally:

- 21 September 2025: six men from Boail, Khaliajuri, Netrokona, going for Durga Puja, caught before dawn at Chunarughat, Habiganj; the brokers fled [XI0073, XI0074, XI0076].
- 14 January 2026: three men going to Chennai as masons, and the man accused of sending them, arrested 200 yards inside the zero line at Bokchar, Chapainawabganj; a case was filed against all four [XI0091, XI0092, XI0093].

## Working in India without papers

### Where people work

- **Delhi:** 24 people (8 men, 8 women and 8 children) brought back through Phulbari, Kurigram, in May 2025 had gone to Delhi for work [XI0040, XI0041].
- **Haryana:** most of 31 people handed back at Darshana, Chuadanga, in October 2025 had lived and worked as labourers in Haryana until Haryana police detained them [XI0081, XI0082].
- **Kolkata:** a couple from Satkhira and another couple worked as masons and construction workers in and around the city [XI0113, XI0114, XI0121].
- **Chennai:** masons' work was the plan of three men caught at Bokchar in January 2026 [XI0092].

Men, women and children from Bangladesh enter India illegally because of poverty and live by presenting themselves as Bengalis until they are caught, an Indian police officer said in August 2025 [XI0070].

> **Need to know**
> - Which other Indian cities and trades employ Bangladeshis without papers in 2026 (brick kilns, rag-picking, domestic work), and what do they earn?

## Arrest, detention and being sent back

Anyone living illegally in India faces action under local law, whatever their nationality, and must be repatriated under law, procedure and the two countries' arrangements, India's foreign ministry said in 2025 and 2026 [XI0039, XI0101].

### Jail and the wait for nationality verification

1. **Arrest and sentence:** 22 Bangladeshis handed back at Tamabil, Sylhet, in August 2025 had been arrested by the BSF and Indian police and served terms in Tura jail, Meghalaya; many of 19 handed back at Chauddagram, Cumilla, in October 2025 had served sentences in Indian jails [XI0063, XI0087].
2. **Nationality verification:** India sends Bangladesh the details of the people it holds, more than 2,800 by June 2026 according to media, and each must be verified as a Bangladeshi citizen before return [XI0130, XI0038, XI0089]. Some on India's list of 2,369 had waited since 2020, and in May 2026 more than 2,860 cases were pending with Bangladesh, some for over five years, India's foreign ministry said [XI0038, XI0100]. In Assam's own affidavit to the Supreme Court, cited by a local lawyer, those whose Bangladesh address is unknown cannot be deported until their nationality is verified and a travel permit arranged [XI0048, XI0049].
3. **Handover:** at a land port or flag meeting (see Coming home) [XI0064, XI0086].

> **Need to know**
> - Who in Bangladesh verifies nationality (the Ministry of Home Affairs, the Special Branch, the High Commission), how long it takes, and how can a family in Bangladesh speed up the case of a relative jailed in India?

### Assam: detention camp and Foreigners Tribunals

- **Detention:** Assam police began a crackdown on "foreigners" on 23 May 2025 and took those detained to Matia, Goalpara district, the state's largest detention camp for alleged illegal migrants, according to Al Jazeera [XI0043].
- **Who counts as a foreigner:** Bengali-speaking residents must prove they came to Assam before 24 March 1971, and Foreigners Tribunals can declare people foreigners over minor spelling mismatches in documents [XI0046, XI0047].
- **The law used:** Assam's chief minister cites a 1950 law giving district commissioners power to expel certain undocumented migrants; Assam High Court lawyer Waliullah Laskar said it applies only to those who entered illegally or overstayed a visa [XI0050, XI0051].
- **Hindus:** Assam told police not to send the cases of non-Muslims, especially Hindus, who entered before 31 December 2014 to the tribunals [XI0056]. India's 2019 Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) eases citizenship for non-Muslims who faced religious persecution in neighbouring Muslim-majority countries and entered before that date [XI0057].

### West Bengal since May 2026

- **The rule:** on 20 May 2026 chief minister Suvendu Adhikari put the CAA into force in West Bengal, so everyone who entered India after 31 December 2024 is treated as illegal [XI0102]. He said India's government had sent the state a guideline on 14 May 2025 to hand illegal Bangladeshi entrants directly to the BSF, which the previous state government did not act on [XI0104, XI0105]. The CAA's seven communities do not include Muslims from Bangladesh; since a 2024 change, members of those communities who entered by 31 December 2024 are treated as legal citizens [XI0106, XI0107]. Adhikari said police cannot harass those covered [XI0110].
- **"Detect, delete, deport":** state police arrest and detain those not covered and hand them to the BSF, which arranges deportation with Bangladesh's border force [XI0108, XI0103]. The drive targets only Muslim Bangladeshis, and detainees need not be taken to court before expulsion, Adhikari said [XI0115, XI0116]. Since India's Supreme Court ruled in December 2025 that foreigners have hardly any rights under the constitution, people facing expulsion must prove why they should not be sent to Bangladesh, according to Al Jazeera [XI0117].
- **Detention centres:** temporary detention centres were set up in every district; by 7 June 2026, 4,800 people had been sent back from them and 836 were held, Adhikari said [XI0126]. Thousands were detained across the state in the two weeks after his order [XI0118].
- **Hakimpur:** since late May 2026 alleged Bangladeshi migrants have gathered at Hakimpur, about 80 km from Kolkata, and other border checkpoints; a police officer there said 250 to 300 arrive daily and have their citizenship checked and biometrics taken for a digital list [XI0124, XI0125]. Raisul Islam's family surrendered on their own, fearing harassment by local people and police, and security forces took them to a detention centre 18 km away [XI0119, XI0132].

Human Rights Watch's Asia director Elaine Pearson said detainees should have the right to legal aid even without valid papers, and called the process illegal [XI0131].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a Bangladeshi held in a West Bengal holding centre contact the Bangladesh Deputy High Commission in Kolkata, and does anyone give detainees legal aid?

### Other states

- **Gujarat:** Ahmedabad police said they had identified more than 250 people they said were Bangladeshis living illegally, and expulsion was under way [XI0052, XI0053]. 78 people left by the BSF on a Sundarbans char in May 2025 said they had been flown and shipped blindfolded from Gujarat [XI0032, XI0033].
- **Maharashtra:** police detained seven Muslims as foreigners in May 2025 and handed them to the BSF; West Bengal's government intervened and they were brought back from the border on 15 June [XI0054, XI0055].
- **Haryana and Meghalaya:** Haryana police detained Bangladeshi labourers and handed them to the BSF, and Bangladeshis arrested in Meghalaya served terms in Tura jail (see Jail and the wait for nationality verification) [XI0082, XI0063].

### Push-ins

- **May 2025:** 167 people were pushed in through Khagrachhari (73), Kurigram (46), Sylhet (23), Moulvibazar (15) and Chuadanga (10) from 4 to 7 May; on 9 May the BSF left 78 people on Mandarbaria char in the western Sundarbans, ill after days without food, one with a broken hand and several with marks of torture [XI0031, XI0032]. By BGB's information those pushed in included Indian, Myanmar and Bangladeshi nationals [XI0034]. Bangladesh protested with a diplomatic note on 9 May [XI0036].
- **In no man's land:** people pushed out of Assam on 27 May 2025 said they stood knee-deep in water for 12 hours with no food or shelter; the BSF threatened to shoot them if they turned back and fired rubber bullets [XI0045, XI0059, XI0060].
- **June 2026:** BGB said it had stopped at least 18 BSF push-in attempts involving about 180 people since 4 June [XI0129].
- **Evidence:** the BSF is getting about 5,000 body cameras to record the sending back of undocumented Bangladeshis, according to Indian security sources in July 2025 [XI0061].
- **Before:** push-ins were frequent around 2002-03, when the BJP-led NDA (National Democratic Alliance) governed India, writer Kallol Mustafa wrote in May 2025 [XI0037].

Faisal Mahmud, spokesperson of the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi, said in June 2025 that Bangladesh had told India the BSF's push-ins did not follow international procedure [XI0058]. BGB's Cumilla battalion commander said in October 2025 that BGB had asked the BSF not to push anyone across and to send it the names of any illegal Bangladeshi it held [XI0088].

## Trafficked and held in India

Women and girls sold into Indian brothels are covered in the Illegal routes doc (see "India: women and girls taken across the land border" in the Illegal routes doc, and "Where victims and families get help" in the same doc). What follows is what happened to people inside India.

### Kidney removal

Dhaka Metropolitan Police (DMP) said in May 2024 that a ring took poor people to India on promises of jobs, held them in Delhi and took their kidneys, and had done this to 10 people [XI0001]. The case of one victim, Robin, by DMP's account:

1. **The offer:** in April 2023, at a tea stall behind Shah Ali Market, Mirpur-10, Robin was talking about his family's hardship when Md Masum, sitting nearby, said he had a business in India and could give him a job; they kept in touch by phone until Robin agreed [XI0004, XI0005, XI0006].
2. **The "medical":** Masum said going to India needed medical tests, took him to a Dhaka hospital in September 2023, and after the check the ring took his passport for the visa [XI0007].
3. **In India:** Robin was taken to Faridabad, Delhi, persuaded to sell a kidney for a promised Tk 6 lakh, tested at a New Delhi hospital, and kept in a house in Muktinagar, Gujarat [XI0008, XI0009].
4. **The surgery:** on 4 March 2024 the ring, using threats, had a kidney removed at a hospital in Gujarat; he was released after four days, held 10 to 11 more days, and learned the ring had sold the kidney to brokers for a large sum [XI0010, XI0011, XI0013].
5. **Payment and return:** ring members in Bangladesh paid his wife Tk 3 lakh, half the promised sum, and he came home [XI0008, XI0012].

Robin filed a case at Dhanmondi police station, and police arrested three members in Dhanmondi and Bagerhat on 11 and 12 May 2024; Masum and 10 to 12 others named in the case were absconding [XI0002, XI0003].

### Women held for sex work

- **Hotels in Jharkhand:** women garment workers in Chattogram were taken to India with offers of higher-paid parlour jobs and kept in hotels, forced into sex work and tortured if they refused, according to the police; one woman and a friend escaped, and her case led to the arrest of Md Tarek (34) on 24 June 2024 [XI0014, XI0017, XI0139].
- **Brothels in Hyderabad:** police in Hyderabad rescued a Bangladeshi teenage girl from forced sex work in August 2025, the fourth such rescue in the city, from red-light areas in Khairatabad, Chaderghat and Bandlaguda [XI0066, XI0067]. Trafficking rings operate in Attapur, Bandlaguda, Chintalmet, Himayatsagar Road and Champapet [XI0072].
- **Sold in other cities:** women trafficked from Bangladesh are sold to brokers in Kolkata, Delhi, Hyderabad, Mumbai and other cities, and a study by India's Central Social Welfare Board found 2.7% of women commercially sexually exploited in India are Bangladeshi [XI0071].
- **Jailed after rescue:** in November 2010 the family of a 19-year-old Faridpur woman learned she was held in Dum Dum Central Jail, Kolkata, after police rescued her from a brothel; she later killed herself in that jail [XI0077].

## Coming home

### Through a flag meeting or land port

1. **The handover:** the BSF hands people to BGB at a flag meeting on the zero line, or Indian immigration hands them over at a land port such as Tamabil, on the Bangladeshi authorities' application [XI0080, XI0064].
2. **Verification:** BGB checks names and addresses and confirms each person is Bangladeshi [XI0089].
3. **The police station:** BGB hands them to the local police station [XI0083, XI0097].
4. **Home or court:** what follows has varied:
   - handed to families (Phulbari, Kurigram, May 2025) or after the legal process (Tamabil, August 2025; Kachakata, Kurigram, April 2026) [XI0042, XI0065, XI0097];
   - kept at the union parishad while police tell relatives and help them home (Darshana, Chuadanga, October 2025) [XI0084];
   - a case filed by BGB and sent to court the next day (Chauddagram, Cumilla, October 2025) [XI0090];
   - sent to court after the legal process: two men caught by the BSF on 27 April 2026 trying to come home without passports at Boirchuna, Pirganj, Thakurgaon [XI0098, XI0099].

Two men who crossed into Assam on 12 April 2026, by their families' account after losing their way in bad weather, were handed back after their families told the Chouddoghori BGB outpost, BGB wrote to the BSF, and the two forces held a flag meeting [XI0094, XI0095, XI0096].

For help after reaching home, see "Help after you get home" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.
