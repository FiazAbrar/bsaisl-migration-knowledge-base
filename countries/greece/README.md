Sources checked up to 23 Nov 2026.

## 1 Background

<a id="in-one-minute"></a>

### 1.1 In one minute

Greece has two doors for Bangladeshis. The legal door is the 2022 MoU: Greece is to give 4,000 Bangladeshis seasonal work visas a year, and to give 15,000 Bangladeshis already living there without papers temporary legal status for five years <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The regularisation happened: more than 10,000 were given legal residence, and many went home to Bangladesh as soon as they got their resident card, the Bangladesh ambassador said <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2), [N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. In April 2024 Bangladesh was still asking Greece to implement the MoU's second part, and Greece had no embassy in Dhaka <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. The other door is a rubber boat from Libya to Crete, sold to young men from Sunamganj and Habiganj for Tk 12 to 15 lakh, part of it paid only once the young man is in Libya <sup>[N6](#n6), [N7](#n7), [N5](#n5), [N8](#n8)</sup>. Brokers call the crossing the "game" <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. In March 2026 a boat lost its way, drifted six days, and at least 22 people died for lack of food and water, at least 13 of them from Sunamganj; the bodies were thrown into the sea <sup>[N10](#n10), [N7](#n7), [N11](#n11), [N9](#n9)</sup>. Those who reach Crete are registered and held in camps, and since September 2025 Greek law says that people not entitled to asylum must be sent home or detained for at least 24 months and fined up to EUR 10,000 <sup>[N3](#n3), [N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="is-it-open-now-and-who-can-go"></a>

### 1.2 Is it open now, and who can go?

The legal route exists on paper through the 2022 MoU, and Bangladesh was still asking Greece to implement its second part in April 2024; the boat from Libya to Crete, now the main entry point for asylum seekers into Greece, was still carrying Bangladeshis at the end of October 2026 <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2 (2024)](#n2), [N6](#n6), [N5](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="status"></a>

#### 1.2.1 Status

- **9 February 2022:** Greek migration minister Notis Mitarachi visited Bangladesh and signed the MoU with Bangladesh's Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **12 January 2023:** Greece opened an online platform for the regularisation of Bangladeshis already in Greece and began issuing residence permits in phases (see [Regularisation of Bangladeshis already in Greece](#regularisation-of-bangladeshis-already-in-greece)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **16 April 2024:** the Greek foreign minister, George Gerapetritis, told Bangladesh's foreign minister in Athens that Greece will recruit many Bangladeshi workers in agriculture, tourism, hospitality and construction, according to a Bangladesh foreign ministry press release; Bangladesh asked Greece to implement the MoU's second part <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **July 2025:** Greece temporarily suspended asylum applications from people arriving from North Africa <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **3 September 2025:** Greece's parliament passed a law toughening penalties for migrants whose claims are rejected and speeding up their return (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.
- **June 2026:** the new EU migration and asylum pact was due to take effect <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi received a Greek seasonal work visa under the 2022 MoU, and how many in each year since?
> - Is the MoU's "second part" the 4,000 seasonal work visas a year, and has it started?
> - Did Greece open its embassy in Dhaka after April 2024? If not, where does a Bangladeshi apply for a Greek visa?

<a id="the-channels"></a>

#### 1.2.2 The channels

- **Seasonal work visa under the MoU:** 4,000 a year on paper (see [Work visa: seasonal worker under the 2022 MoU](#work-visa-seasonal-worker-under-the-2022-mou)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Regularisation:** for Bangladeshis already in Greece without papers (see [Regularisation of Bangladeshis already in Greece](#regularisation-of-bangladeshis-already-in-greece)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Not a legal route:** the boat from Libya to Crete (see [Irregular entry: Libya to Crete by boat](#irregular-entry-libya-to-crete-by-boat)) <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.
- **Not a legal route:** overland from the Gulf through Iran and Turkey (see [Irregular entry: overland through Turkey](#irregular-entry-overland-through-turkey)).

<a id="size"></a>

#### 1.2.3 Size

- **Regularised:** 10,833 had registered with the Bangladesh embassy in Greece in the first phase by January 2024, and Bangladesh's foreign minister said in April 2024 that more than 10,000 had been given legal residence <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1), [N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **Arrivals of all nationalities:** about 61,000 in the year to August 2025 and 42,000 in the following 12 months, a fall of about 31%, according to Greek migration ministry figures reported by ANA, the Greek news agency <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>.
- **Bangladeshis on the boats:** of two boats of 56 people each that reached Crete in early August 2026, one carried 25 Bangladeshis and the other 47 <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshis live in Greece legally today, and how many without papers?
> - How many Bangladeshis did BMET (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) clear for Greece each year from 2022 to 2026?
> - How many Bangladeshis arrived in Greece irregularly in 2025 and 2026?

<a id="how-a-job-reaches-you-and-who-takes-a-cut"></a>

### 1.3 How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

For the legal seasonal route the reporting gives no chain at all; for the boat route it names brokers living in Libya, one broker's father collecting the money at home, brokers based in Dhaka, and payments split between home and Libya <sup>[N11](#n11), [N9](#n9), [N5](#n5), [N7](#n7), [N8](#n8)</sup>.

- **The broker in Libya.** Akhlus Mia, father of Shayek Ahmad, said he paid broker Azizul Islam of Ichgaon village Tk 12 lakh to send his son, and that Azizul lives in Libya and sends local young men from Libya to Greece <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>. Mohibur Rahman of Chhatak upazila went through Nabi Hossain of Pagla, Shantiganj upazila, who is also in Libya <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **The collector at home.** Nabi Hossain's father, Abdul Mannan, handled all the money in Bangladesh; he met Mohibur through masonry work and encouraged him to go to Greece, a cousin said <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Brokers in Dhaka.** The families of six missing Habiganj men said they had been trying to reach brokers based in Dhaka <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **Brokers spread across three countries.** Of the 9 brokers named in two cases filed in Sunamganj in March 2026, some were in Libya, Greece and Italy <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a Greek farm employer find a Bangladeshi seasonal worker under the MoU: through BMET, BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the government's recruiting company), private agencies, or only by naming a person?

<a id="what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep"></a>

### 1.4 What you pay, and what you earn and keep

The boat route costs Tk 12 to 15 lakh per person, raised by selling land and cattle and borrowing at high interest <sup>[N5](#n5), [N8](#n8), [N9](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="what-people-really-pay-for-the-boat"></a>

#### 1.4.1 What people really pay for the boat

- **Derai, Sunamganj:** Tk 12 lakh each, with half paid after reaching Libya, the brother of one man who died said <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
- **Jagannathpur, Sunamganj:** Tk 12 lakh in two instalments: Tk 4.5 lakh before leaving, then Tk 7.5 lakh demanded from Libya before the boat <sup>[N11](#n11), [N8](#n8)</sup>.
- **Chhatak, Sunamganj:** Tk 13 lakh, of which Tk 5 lakh was borrowed from a moneylender at interest <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Madhabpur and Lakhai, Habiganj:** Tk 12 to 15 lakh each, raised by selling land or borrowing at high interest <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

How brokers stage payments to keep families paying is explained under "The staged-payment trap, and no receipts" in the Dalal scams doc, and the moneylender loans under "Loan sharks and land mortgages" in the same doc.

<a id="the-work"></a>

#### 1.4.2 The work

A significant number of Bangladeshi workers live in western Manolada and work mainly on Greek farms: strawberries, olives, oranges and other crops <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The embassy said regularised workers now send money home through legal channels <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Bangladeshi farm worker in Manolada earn a day or a month, with papers and without?
> - What does the seasonal visa cost, and what may an agency charge for it?

<a id="what-ties-you-to-the-employer"></a>

### 1.5 What ties you to the employer

Regularised Bangladeshis work freely in various firms, where before they were kept in hardship on farms and in factories, the January 2024 report said <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the seasonal work visa tied to one employer, and can a seasonal worker change employer or stay on after the season?
> - Is the five-year regularisation permit tied to an employer, and can it be renewed after five years?

<a id="what-goes-wrong-most-and-what-people-wrongly-believe"></a>

### 1.6 What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

<a id="what-people-believe-and-the-facts"></a>

#### 1.6.1 What people believe, and the facts

- **"The broker will put you on a proper boat."** Mohibur Rahman's father said the broker promised a wooden boat and put his son on a plastic one <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. The March 2026 boat was rubber, and the 31 October 2026 one an inflatable rubber boat <sup>[N10](#n10), [N5](#n5)</sup>.
- **"There will be food for the trip."** These boats leave with food for 24 hours, and on the March 2026 boat the food and water ran out after one day, Shayek Ahmad's uncle said <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.
- **"The broker will tell the family the truth."** Broker Nabi Hossain told Mohibur's family he had reached Greece and was in a camp where he could not talk, while his body was already in the sea (see [If a relative dies or goes missing on the crossing](#if-a-relative-dies-or-goes-missing-on-the-crossing)) <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>.
- **"Once in Greece, you can claim asylum."** In July 2025 Greece suspended asylum applications from people arriving from North Africa, and since September 2025 those not entitled to asylum must be returned or detained (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="irregular-routes-and-where-an-irregular-stay-ends"></a>

#### 1.6.2 Irregular routes, and where an irregular stay ends

The boat crossing from Libya ends in death at sea for some (see [Deaths on the crossing](#deaths-on-the-crossing)), in a camp on Crete for those who arrive (see [Arrival on Crete](#arrival-on-crete)), and, under the September 2025 law, in return or detention for those not entitled to asylum (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N10](#n10), [N3](#n3), [N4](#n4)</sup>. Before the regularisation, life without papers meant hiding from police raids in Athens and the risk of being sent home (see [Living without papers and deportation](#living-without-papers-and-deportation)) <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

<a id="work-visa-seasonal-worker-under-the-2022-mou"></a>

## 2 Work visa: seasonal worker under the 2022 MoU

Under the MoU signed on 9 February 2022, Greece is to give 4,000 Bangladeshi workers seasonal work visas each year <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. In April 2024 the Greek foreign minister said Greece would recruit many Bangladeshis in agriculture, tourism, hospitality and construction, and Bangladesh's foreign minister asked Greece to implement the MoU's second part <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>. Opening an embassy in Dhaka was on Greece's priority list at that time <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Where does a Bangladeshi apply for the Greek seasonal work visa, and through whom: a Greek embassy (in Dhaka or New Delhi), VFS Global (the company that runs visa application centres for many European embassies) or another visa centre, BMET or a recruiting agency?
> - What papers does it need (a job offer from a named Greek employer, a contract, a skill certificate), what does it cost, and how long does it take?
> - How long is a season, what does the job pay, and who pays the air ticket?
> - Must the worker get BMET clearance, and is Greece one of the countries with a migration cost cap?

<a id="regularisation-of-bangladeshis-already-in-greece"></a>

## 3 Regularisation of Bangladeshis already in Greece

The MoU gives 15,000 Bangladeshis living in Greece without papers temporary legal status for five years <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Greece opened an online application platform on 12 January 2023 and issued residence permits in phases <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. By January 2024, 10,833 people had registered with the Bangladesh embassy in Greece in the first phase <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Bangladesh's ambassador to Greece, Asud Ahmad, said the first phase had been implemented after two years of diplomatic effort and that regularisation was ongoing <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

**What it changed.** Mohammad Kamal, regularised after 10 years in Greece, said he could not go home while irregular even when relatives died; with his card he came home, married, planned to go back after three months and could now come every year <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. The ambassador said many went to Bangladesh as soon as they got their resident card <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the regularisation still open in 2026, and to whom: only people who were in Greece before a cut-off date?
> - What papers and fees did an applicant need, and what role did the Bangladesh embassy's registration play?
> - What happens when the five-year permit ends?

<a id="irregular-entry-libya-to-crete-by-boat"></a>

## 4 Irregular entry: Libya to Crete by boat

Crete and the small island of Gavdos south of it are now the main entry point for asylum seekers into Greece; they are about 320 km from the Libyan coast <sup>[N6](#n6), [N12](#n12)</sup>. Arrivals by this route rose in 2025 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. The route to Libya itself, the detention houses and ransom there are covered under "Libya to Italy by boat" in the Illegal routes doc.

<a id="how-it-is-sold"></a>

### 4.1 How it is sold

- **The deal.** The Derai men each agreed Tk 12 lakh with a broker to go to Greece, left home in February 2026 and paid half after reaching Libya <sup>[N7](#n7)</sup>.
- **The way to Libya.** Shayek Ahmad (23) went through Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and other countries to Libya; Mohibur Rahman (20) went through Saudi Arabia, and stayed in Saudi Arabia and Libya with Maruf Ahmad, another Sunamganj man who left Bangladesh with him <sup>[N8](#n8), [N9](#n9), [N13](#n13)</sup>. How brokers route people through a third country is under "A flight to a different country first" in the Dalal scams doc.
- **More money in Libya.** From Libya the broker demanded Tk 7.5 lakh more before Shayek would be put on the boat, and his father sold land and cows to pay it <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. Both Shayek's and Mohibur's fathers said the broker beat their sons for money before the boat <sup>[N8](#n8), [N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Wanting to come home.** Mohibur messaged his family from the journey begging to be brought home because he was suffering, his cousin said <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>.

<a id="the-crossing"></a>

### 4.2 The crossing

- **The "game".** Local people and brokers call the rubber-boat crossing from Libya to Greece a "game" <sup>[N7](#n7), [N9](#n9)</sup>.
- **Where from.** The March 2026 boat left Tobruk, the eastern Libyan port city, on 21 March <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>.
- **Food.** These boats are sent off with food for 24 hours, Shayek Ahmad's uncle said <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>. People are sent from Libya to Greece in small boats with limited food, a Bangladeshi in a Greek camp said <sup>[N11](#n11)</sup>.
- **Boarding.** Shayek hurt his leg trying to board on the first day and could not go, and was put on the next "game" on 21 March <sup>[N8](#n8)</sup>.

<a id="pushbacks-at-sea"></a>

### 4.3 Pushbacks at sea

A BBC analysis of 15 incidents between May 2020 and May 2023 found that 43 people died, more than 40 of them after the Greek coast guard forced them out of Greek waters or pushed them back to sea after they had reached Greek islands; witnesses said at least 9 were thrown into the sea <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. In four incidents people were sent back after reaching Greek islands, and in some they were pushed out to sea on dinghies without engines, witnesses said <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. The coast guard denied the allegations and said its own investigations found no wrongdoing <sup>[N14 (2024)](#n14)</sup>. In 2025 Frontex said it was reviewing 12 possible human rights violations by Greece, and human rights groups accuse Athens of pushing migrants back at sea and on land <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. Since 2019 the government has built a fence on its northern border and stepped up sea patrols <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

<a id="arrival-on-crete"></a>

### 4.4 Arrival on Crete

1. <a id="arrival-on-crete-item-1"></a>**Spotted and brought in.** In August 2026 a Frontex aircraft spotted a boat of 40 that was then brought into Ierapetra port with the help of a coast guard patrol ship and a fishing boat; later that month an EU drone spotted two boats near Kali Limenes port in south Crete, and the coast guard rescued 79 people from them near Crete, Bangladeshis and at least two women and two children among them <sup>[N3](#n3), [N6](#n6)</sup>.
2. <a id="arrival-on-crete-item-2"></a>**Registered.** At the port the migrants are registered and identity checks begin <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>.
3. <a id="arrival-on-crete-item-3"></a>**Held in a camp.** In August 2026 all those rescued were held at a temporary shelter camp at Heraklion port, as numbers on Crete rose; at least 202 people reached Crete from Libya in under 48 hours that month <sup>[N3](#n3)</sup>. A Habiganj man who arrived on 6 March 2026 was kept in a camp, and the survivors of the 27 March boat were brought to the same camp <sup>[N15](#n15)</sup>.
4. <a id="arrival-on-crete-item-4"></a>**Health check, then asylum.** About 540 people rescued south of Crete in December 2025, Bangladeshis among them, were taken to Rethymno on Crete, where their asylum process was to begin after health checks <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>.
5. <a id="arrival-on-crete-item-5"></a>**Hospital.** Two seriously ill survivors of the March 2026 boat were taken to hospital on Crete <sup>[N10](#n10), [N15](#n15)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - How long are new arrivals held in the Crete camps, and where are they sent next?
> - Can a Bangladeshi in a camp phone home, and how do families find out who is in which camp?

<a id="deaths-on-the-crossing"></a>

### 4.5 Deaths on the crossing

- **March 2026, from Tobruk.** The boat left on 21 March, lost its way and drifted six days without food or water; at least 22 people died, and survivors said the bodies were thrown into the sea on the order of one of the smugglers <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. The fuel ran out, and the bodies were kept two days on the boat until they began to smell <sup>[N9](#n9), [N11](#n11)</sup>. A Frontex ship rescued 26 people about 53 nautical miles south of Ierapetra on 27 March: 21 Bangladeshis, 4 South Sudanese and a Chadian <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. Sunamganj police confirmed 12 dead from the district, six from Derai, five from Jagannathpur and one from Dowarabazar, and the family of Mohibur Rahman of Chhatak later learned he had also died on the boat <sup>[N11](#n11), [N9](#n9)</sup>. Greek authorities arrested two South Sudanese men, aged 19 and 22, suspected of being the smugglers, who were under investigation for illegal entry and negligent homicide <sup>[N10](#n10)</sup>. More on this boat is under "Deaths" in the Illegal routes doc.
- **31 October 2026, Habiganj.** Seven young men from Madhabpur and Lakhai upazilas left Libya for Greece through brokers on an inflatable rubber boat with about 50 passengers; it sprang a leak mid-sea and sank <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. Of the seven, only Masum Mia of Puraikala village survived: he floated for two days, washed ashore unconscious and reached Greece with the help of local people, his father said <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>. The other six, aged 25 to 30, were still missing in mid-November 2026 <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

<a id="if-a-relative-dies-or-goes-missing-on-the-crossing"></a>

### 4.6 If a relative dies or goes missing on the crossing

**How families find out.** In the reported cases, news came from a survivor or a man from the same area already in Greece, not from the broker: Rohan Ahmad, on the March boat, phoned Tarapasha village; Sohel Ahmad, living in Greece, went to the camp and phoned Shayek's family; Masum Mia phoned his own family, and his father told the families of the six missing <sup>[N7](#n7), [N8](#n8), [N5](#n5)</sup>. Broker Nabi Hossain hid Mohibur's death for two days, and only confirmed he had been put on the boat when the family went to his house in Pagla <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. A survivor who travelled with Mohibur then phoned home to say he had died first <sup>[N13](#n13)</sup>. The Habiganj families could not reach the brokers at all <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

**Getting the authorities to search.** The Madhabpur UNO said in November 2026 that families must apply formally to the relevant government ministry, after which a search can be arranged abroad through the foreign ministry and the embassy <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

**Cases against brokers.** After the March 2026 deaths the fathers of two of the dead filed two cases against 9 brokers at Jagannathpur and Derai police stations on 30 March 2026 <sup>[N9](#n9)</sup>. The Sunamganj Deputy Commissioner said the district administration would also file cases, and the Jagannathpur UNO said a list of brokers was being made for the police <sup>[N11](#n11), [N8](#n8)</sup>. Who to go to and what the law says are under "Where victims and families get help" and "The law in Bangladesh" in the Illegal routes doc, and "File a trafficking case" in the Dalal scams doc.

> **Need to know**
> - Which ministry takes the formal application for a missing migrant, and what papers must the family bring?
> - Can the family get the death of a man whose body was thrown into the sea recognised in Bangladesh, and does the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the Bangladesh government board for the welfare of migrant workers and their families) pay anything for a man who left without BMET clearance?

<a id="asylum"></a>

## 6 Asylum

In 2025 Greece temporarily suspended asylum applications from people arriving from North Africa, and said hundreds of irregular migrants had been returned since the suspension in July <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. On 3 September 2025 its parliament passed a law under which undocumented migrants who are not entitled to asylum must be returned to their own country, or detained for at least 24 months and fined up to EUR 10,000 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. UNHCR said the law could expose people who need protection to punishment <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. Greece planned return flights for some migrants from Pakistan, Bangladesh and Egypt in September 2025 <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>.

Under the new EU migration and asylum pact, due in June 2026, Greece gets EU help with migration, and Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said returning those whose asylum claims are rejected will be a priority <sup>[N12](#n12)</sup>. Greece is in talks with Germany and other EU countries about return hubs outside the EU <sup>[N6](#n6)</sup>. What happened to one Bangladeshi who claimed asylum in Greece as a Rohingya is under "From the Gulf overland through Iran and Turkey to Greece" in the Illegal routes doc, and how false asylum stories are sold is under "Asylum claims built on a false story" in the same doc.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the suspension of asylum applications from North Africa arrivals still in force, and does it cover Bangladeshis who sailed from Libya?
> - Does Greece treat Bangladesh as a safe country of origin, and what share of Bangladeshi asylum claims does it reject?

## 7 Living without papers and deportation

Before the regularisation, Bangladeshis without papers in Greece lived in inhuman conditions and could not get treatment when sick <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. In 2022 Greek police were rounding up irregular migrants in Athens every day, and the government sent more than fifty Bangladeshis home in several rounds <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. In September 2025 Greece planned return flights that included Bangladeshis (see [Asylum](#asylum)) <sup>[N4](#n4)</sup>. What happens on landing in Dhaka is under "Deportees and outpass holders" in the Departure bottlenecks doc.

> **Need to know**
> - How does a Bangladeshi without papers in Greece get a travel permit from the embassy to go home, and what does it cost?
> - Does Greece run a voluntary return programme with IOM (the International Organization for Migration) for Bangladeshis?

<a id="work-and-life-in-greece"></a>

## 8 Work and life in Greece

A significant number of Bangladeshi workers live in western Manolada, working mainly on Greek farms: strawberries, olives, oranges and other crops <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. Since the regularisation, regularised Bangladeshis work freely in various firms, and the embassy said they now send money home through legal channels <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>. How to send money and what each way costs is under "Ways to send money and what each costs" in the Sending money home doc.

> **Need to know**
> - What are the minimum wage, working hours and housing for farm workers in Manolada, and where does a worker complain if not paid?

<a id="contacts"></a>

## 9 Contacts

- **Bangladesh Embassy in Greece (Athens):** registered Bangladeshis for the regularisation; the ambassador in January 2024 was Asud Ahmad <sup>[N1 (2024)](#n1)</sup>.
- **Greek embassy in Bangladesh:** none as of April 2024; opening one in Dhaka was on Greece's priority list <sup>[N2 (2024)](#n2)</sup>.
- **In Bangladesh, for a missing relative:** the upazila nirbahi officer, then a formal application to the relevant ministry (see [If a relative dies or goes missing on the crossing](#if-a-relative-dies-or-goes-missing-on-the-crossing)) <sup>[N5](#n5)</sup>.

> **Need to know**
> - The Bangladesh Embassy in Athens: address, phone, email and labour wing hours.
> - Which embassy handles Greek visas for Bangladeshis, and its address.
