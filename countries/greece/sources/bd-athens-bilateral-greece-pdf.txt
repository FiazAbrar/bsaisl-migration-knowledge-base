                    Bangladesh-Greece Bilateral Relations




      Greece recognized Bangladesh immediately after its independence in 1972. Earlier,
Bangladesh maintained its representation to Athens through concurrent accreditation
from Cairo initially and later from Rome. At a later stage, Bangladesh – as part of
creating diplomatic footprints around the world – established a resident Mission in
Athens on 31 July 2009. H.E. Mrs Nahida Rahman Shumona has been serving as
Bangladesh Ambassador in Greece since September 2024 (5th resident Ambassador).
The Hellenic Republic, on the other hand, is yet to open a Mission in Dhaka. At present
their Embassy in New Delhi is concurrently accredited to Bangladesh. H. E. Mrs. Aliki
Koutsomitopoulou has recently joined as the Ambassador.


      Greece and Bangladesh have established a strong relationship due to their shared
democratic values and the presence of a large Bangladeshi diaspora in Greece. This
diaspora, mostly in agriculture and hospitality, has expanded cooperation opportunities
for both countries. The signing of an MOU on migration and mobility has been a positive
development in bilateral relations. As the two countries share common values of
democracy, human rights and peaceful coexistence in compliance principles enshrined
in the UN charter, they necessarily have commonalities of views on major regional and
global issues including migration, counter-terrorism and climate change, global peace
and security etc. This has been buttressed by support to each other’s candidature in the
multilateral for a and cooperation in global platform/ conferences like COP, ASEM, CVF
etc. Bilateral relations primarily focus on trade and investment, but due to Greece's
financial crisis and its small consumer society, trade is insignificant. Greece's focus on
the EU, Turkey, and Balkan region hinders expansion. Despite these constraints, efforts
are being made to tap potential in trade and investment.
