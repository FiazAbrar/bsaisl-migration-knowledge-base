 Background

1. An annual progress report on the technical cooperation programme (TCP) between the ILO and the
       Government of Qatar has been published every year since 2018, and these reports are accessible on the ILO
       Qatar website.1

2. The objective of the annual progress report is to present key developments and priorities and keep interested
       parties informed about the broad scope of work undertaken by the ILO Project Office in Doha. This report
       covers the period from November 2023 to December 2024. In the past, the report was published around
       November each year to align with the sessions of the ILO's Governing Body. From now, it will be published in
       January to provide coverage for the full calendar year.

3. To further deepen the impact of the labour reforms adopted in recent years and to strengthen labour market
       institutions and policies, the TCP transitioned from Phase 2 (2021-2024) to Phase 3 (2024-2028). In March 2024,
       a Joint Work Programme (JWP) was signed by the ILO Director General and the Minister of Labour of Qatar.
       This four-year programme includes three strands: (i) Phase 3 of the TCP; (ii) a dedicated project to support the
       work of the international trade unions in Qatar; and (iii) a secondment programme for Ministry of Labour
       (MOL) officials to the ILO.2

4. Phase 3 of the TCP is in alignment with the recently launched National Development Strategy (2024­2030) and
       the Qatar National Vision (QNV) 2030, and also reflects the findings of an independent final evaluation of
       Phase 2 carried out in 2023. Phase 3 covers three pillars, which also form the structure of this report:

                      Pillar 1. Workforce policies
                      Pillar 2. Labour market governance
                      Pillar 3. International cooperation and exchange of experience

5. The administrative data presented in this report comes from Government of Qatar sources unless specified
       otherwise. Some elements of this report also reflect developments that are not strictly part of the TCP between
       the ILO and the Government of Qatar. However, they are included to provide further context on the progress
       of the labour reforms.

 The context in 2024

6. The work of the TCP in 2024 has been shaped by a number of factors. Most importantly, by the third National
       Development Strategy (NDS3) for 2024-2030, which was published in January 2024.

                    In reflecting on the previous period and the goals outlined in the QNV 2030, the NDS3
                        refers to the labour reforms in the section on Human Development: "Notable progress has
                        also been made towards a more fair and equitable labour market, with the introduction of
                        reforms that set a minimum wage and improve worker mobility and protection."3

                    The NDS3 includes a strategic national outcome on a "Future-Ready Workforce" and labour
                        market goals are prominent throughout the entire strategy. The emphasis is on fostering
                        a more skilled and productive workforce by attracting and retaining talent from abroad,
                        upskilling Qatari nationals, and increasing the number of nationals in the private sector.
                        In addition, the NDS3 highlights the importance of reforms to immigration policy, enabling
                        new ways of working and further facilitating labour mobility.

                    There are also relevant objectives in the outcome on "Sustainable Economic Growth,"
                        which identifies target sectors for diversification and focuses on creating an enabling
                        environment including through creating a "level playing field," more "transparent and
                        enforceable laws," and ensuring "quality and efficiency" in government services.4 These
                        points are mentioned in the context of creating a more business-friendly environment, but
                        would also benefit workers and employers.

7. The National Planning Council (NPC) was established in May 2024 and will play a central role in overseeing
       and monitoring implementation of the ND3, including through endorsing and supporting national strategies
       and plans. This body replaces the Planning and Statistics Authority (PSA).

8. Moreover, a Workforce Planning Committee (WPC) was formed with the Minister of Labour as the Chair, and
       the President of the Civil Service and Government Development Bureau (CGB) and Secretary General of the
       NPC as the Vice Chair. The Committee includes representatives from the Ministries of Labour, Education and
       Higher Education, Commerce, Interior, Defense, Finance, the NPC, and the CGB. It also includes Qatar Central
       Bank and Qatar Energy. The Committee will support coordination for effective workforce planning in the
       public and private sector and identify challenges and set priorities to address them.

9. In November 2024, the Ministry of Labour launched the National Strategy for an Efficient and Highly
       Productive Workforce (2024-2030). The event was held under the patronage and presence of the Prime
       Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs of the State of Qatar. The Strategy is in harmony with the NDS3 and
       focuses on (i) enhancing the efficiency and productivity of the workforce; (ii) empowering citizens and
       increasing their participation in the labour market; and (iii) improving the working environment and
       conditions in Qatar. The Strategy also prioritizes the enhancement of the Ministry's institutional performance
       and effectiveness, including by improving the use of technology and digital transformation.

10. The legislative framework is evolving. A law on job localization in private sector was adopted in October 2024,
       reflecting a top national priority. The NDS3 also sets a clear target for Qatari women and men to make up 20
       per cent of the workforce in the private and semi-private sector. Another significant piece of legislation under
       consideration would make joint committees mandatory for companies. This would greatly enhance workers'
       voice, strengthen relations between workers and management at the enterprise level, and also establish a
       basis for social dialogue at the sectoral and national level.

11. Reflecting on the suite of legislation that was adopted between 2018 and 2021, there is broad acceptance of
       the legislation on the minimum wage, on occupational heat stress, and on the removal of exit permits. While
       there is increased recognition of the value of labour mobility, including in the NDS3, it is far from universal,
       and there is still resistance to it from many employers. Continued practices that contribute to restricting
       mobility risk undermining its positive impact for workers, employers and the economy overall. In particular,
       concerns have been raised on the extent to which domestic workers can change jobs or leave the country
       without their employers' permission; and, relatedly, the high costs that employers incur when recruiting
       domestic workers.

12. In line with the NDS3, a number of structural reviews are underway to identify gaps in the labour law related
       to implementation and the quality of services. Assessments are underway or planned in the areas of labour
       mobility, the labour inspection and occupational safety and health systems, dispute resolution systems,
       employment contracts, and social protection. The recommendations that emerge could inform labour laws
       and policies, and also the adoption of operating systems and procedures across MOL departments and units
       to ensure the consistency and quality of services.

13. These reviews also seek to further digitalize the work of the MOL and enhance online services, an area that
       has seen a major step forward in the past year. The digital transformation is an explicit policy of the MOL and
       the Government more broadly, as set out in the NDS3. This is evident in the ongoing development of the
       Labour Market Information System (LMIS), the launch of several online job-matching platforms, and
       revamping of the various e-services on the MOL website.

14. An encouraging feature of 2024 has been the significant increase in engagement with migrant community
       leaders by the MOL and the Workers' Support and Insurance Fund (WSIF). The community leaders are
       associated with embassies, the National Human Rights Committee (NHRC), international trade unions, and
       NGOs, among other institutions. This heightened community outreach has increased awareness among
       migrant workers of their rights and obligations, as well as the range of services available to them. It has also
       enabled greater cooperation with the various government and non-government entities by creating
       opportunities for feedback from the community leaders and workers' representatives to the MOL and others.

15. The MOL continues to engage with international trade unions on a range of issues. Under the JWP, a dedicated
       project to support workers' activities is being finalized with the unions and is expected to begin in early 2025
       (see paragraph 3).

16. Discussions are ongoing about the ILO Project Office in Doha becoming an ILO Country Office. The functions
       of the Country Office would build on the JWP with the MOL and seek to enhance partnerships with Qatar-
       based institutions to further advance the decent work agenda in the country, in the region, and globally.

Pillar 1: Workforce policies

Labour market information system (LMIS)

17. The Ministry of Labour has prioritized the development of a labour market information system (LMIS), which
       will be a tool to anticipate labour market changes and inform policymaking. It will draw on a wide range of
       data sources to enable informed and impactful employment policies.

18. In May 2024, the MOL invited the ILO and other partners, including the United Nations Economic and Social
       Commission for Western Asia (ESCWA), to participate in a two-week workshop to discuss the development of
       the LMIS platform. The discussion covered the application of the various use cases of the LMIS, labour market
       indicators, strategies, and best practices for conducting surveys and structuring questionnaires. Progress
       updates on the LMIS are presented to the Workforce Planning Committee.

19. The ILO continues to work closely with the MOL's Information and Labour Market Research Department and
       the Transformation Management Office on two main inputs to the LMIS:

                    The first Establishment Skills Survey (ESS) in Qatar, which will provide critical insights into
                        Qatar's current and future workforce needs. The ESS is a comprehensive survey designed
                        to collect data directly from employers across various sectors to identify existing skills
                        gaps, shortages, and anticipate short-term future demands. This information is crucial for
                        enhancing workforce capabilities and shaping effective labour market policies, supporting
                        NDS3 goals related to economic diversification and the creation of a highly skilled
                        workforce. In December 2024, the MOL and ILO organized a workshop titled "Shaping the
                        Future Workforce: A Quick Dive into the Establishment Skills Survey (ESS)" facilitated by the
                        International Training Center of the ILO (ITC-ILO) to begin work on the design of the survey
                        instrument and methodology.

                    The TCP is working with the Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) on a sectoral study on skills in
                        Qatar's financial sector that complements the broader ESS. While the ESS covers a wide
                        range of sectors, this study zeroes in on the financial industry, identifying current and
                        short-term future skills requirements, particularly considering the ongoing digital
                        transformation. This data-driven approach will enable targeted upskilling strategies and
                        align education and training programs with industry demands.

20. Another key input to the LMIS is the labour force survey. Representatives from the MOL and the National
       Planning Council participated in an online training course on designing a labour force survey in May and June
       2024 delivered by the ITC-ILO. Officials from the MOL also participated in an online course on LMIS by the ITC-
       ILO in June 2024. While the ESS provides insights into employers' skills demand, the labour force survey serves
       as a valuable tool for understanding the skills supply, among other key aspects. The LMIS acts as the
       framework through which these data points are effectively communicated to their intended audiences,
       whether it is the Government for policymaking or other entities for information and dissemination purposes.

21. The ILO has been sharing tools, approaches, and international good practices with the Higher Education
       Department of Qatar Foundation (QF) as they work on a tracer study of QF graduates. QF has already
       presented the approach to other higher education institutions in the country and found that there is interest
       in replicating it.

Labour mobility

35. It has been four years since the adoption of the legislation removing the legal requirement for workers to
       obtain permission from their employers, in the form of a No Objection Certificate (NOC), to change jobs. This
       has resulted in greater labour mobility with workers able to find new and better opportunities, and employers
       able to identify and recruit workers more easily within Qatar.

                      Until the end of September 2024, 50,127 men and 10,124 women have had their
                        applications to change jobs approved. As in previous years, the overall proportion of
                        women who changed jobs in this period mirrors the overall proportion of women in the
                        workforce (16.8 per cent).

36. Throughout 2024, the ILO and MOL collaborated with Strategy Hub to assess the kafala reforms, with a focus
         on the labour mobility reforms. This study examined the impact of the reforms on the labour market, on
         workers, and on employers of different sizes in different sectors in Qatar. The study draws from different
         sources of information:

                      MOL and Ministry of Interior (MOI) data from September 2018 to August 2023 including
                        on employment change, labour permits, employment contracts, and QID cancellation.
                        Employment change approvals for workers increased by around 830 per cent in a year
                        since the reforms were adopted in September 2020 until August 2021, in comparison to
                        the year before the reforms. The data analysed showed that there has been a gradual
                        steady increase in number of female workers who have applied to change jobs.

                      An enterprise survey, which was administered through multiple channels to ensure broad
                        participation from companies across various sectors. The survey was disseminated by the
                        MOL, various international business councils, and associations of human resources (HR)
                        professionals. It assessed factors such as recruitment practices, job matching, cost
                        implications, and investments in skills development of workers before and after the
                        reforms were adopted in September 2020 to offer a comparative view. The survey findings
                        highlighted that although respondents were aware of the labour reforms, a significant
                        majority of employers asked for NOCs or equivalent documents when recruiting workers
                        locally.

                      The two main reasons provided for this requirement were to mitigate delays in the process
                        of employment transfer, and to avoid potential conflicts with the former employer. The
                        findings also reflected that while large enterprises still relied mainly on recruiting workers
                        from abroad,6 SMEs reported a 20 per cent decrease in their cross-border hiring between
                        2019-2023. The enterprise survey also looked at the retention rate amongst workers
                        recruited locally versus workers recruited from abroad during the period.

37. The MOL launched a new revised version of the electronic employment change platform in May 2024. The
         new system enables both workers and new employers to initiate the employment change (EC) application.
         Upon the submission of the application by the new employer, the system notifies the worker for his/her
         approval. The system no longer requires the submission of the EC paper form which previously needed to
         be filled, signed, and uploaded into the previous system by the worker. This aims to enhance efficiency,
         reduce the burden on workers, and address challenges related to omissions or inaccurate information filled
         in the paper form. This is substantial as omissions or technical problems were among the main reasons for
         rejection of the EC application in the old system.

38. Under this system, the new employer needs to input the working and living conditions including the contract
         period, job title, probation period, basic monthly wage and other financial entitlements. The worker then is
         able to review the information and approve or reject the application. This avoids any changes made to the
         terms and conditions of the new job after the EC application has been approved. The platform is now
         functioning more efficiently than when the system was first introduced. In September, the average number
         of days to process an EC application was 9, compared to 6 in September 2023, but down from 22 days in
         August 2021.

39. The MOL has published user guides on the new system for employers and workers in Arabic and English. The
         MOL also presented the new platform to the community liaison officers (CLOs) of international trade unions

40. Since the adoption of Law No. 13 of 2018 and Ministerial Decision No. 95 of 2019 by the Ministry of Interior,

Attracting and retaining talent

41. The ILO and the MOL collaborated with Fragomen in Qatar to initiate a study to assess whether the
         immigration and work permit systems address the current and future needs of the labour market. In early
         2024, companies of various sizes and across sectors were consulted in a roundtable including: (i) local Qatari
         enterprises; (ii) foreign business councils and their members; and (iii) semi-governmental entities and
         relevant government entities.

 42. A comprehensive benchmarking and comparative analysis were conducted to understand the differences
         and similarities between immigration systems worldwide. This also included analyzing regional and global
         jurisdictions focusing on long-term residency schemes, nationalization policies, remote working schemes,
         intracompany transfers, and trusted employer schemes.

                      In line with QNV 2030, the study puts forward a set of 26 main recommendations revolving
                        around economic growth and retention of workers, addressing the unique challenges and
                        opportunities within Qatar's labour and immigration frameworks. Some of the key
                        recommendations include improving pre-departure immigration processes, ensuring
                        effective and transparent employment change process, and introducing flexible visa quota
                        requirements.

                      One recommendation of the study is to establish a `Trusted Employer Scheme' in Qatar
                        through which eligible enterprises that have demonstrated compliance with national
                        legislation, including labour and immigration laws, can be incentivized through
                        streamlined immigration processes. Beneficiaries could see reduced visa processing times
                        and facilitated recruitment and transfer of employees across borders. They would also be
                        required to demonstrate adherence to laws, with periodic reviews to ensure that they
                        maintain compliance. The scheme would also promote transparency by maintaining up-
                        to-date records on workers' legal status, wages, and working conditions, with real-time
                        reporting to build trust with regulatory bodies. The design of the Trusted Employer
                        Scheme is currently underway with a view to initiating a pilot phase in 2025.

Fair recruitment

 43. The ILO has been providing technical inputs to the MOL to review the legal framework on regulating private
         recruitment agencies in Qatar. In 2024, the ILO conducted a comprehensive review of the Ministerial Decision
         No. 8 of 2005 regarding the conditions and procedures for obtaining a license to recruit foreign workers for
         others, and Ministerial Decision No. 21 of 2021 amending certain provisions of Ministerial Decision No. 8 of
         2005 in line with international labour standards and good practices. Some key recommendations include:

                      Expanding the criteria to obtain a license to recruit workers to require that applicants have
                        the necessary qualifications and are not involved in crimes related to fraud, violence,
                        forced labour and trafficking in persons.

                      Requiring the applicant to submit documents related to internal company policies and
                        systems to ensure compliance with relevant national legislation.

                      To address concerns of workers and employers, it has also been recommended to include
                        a legal provision on mandatory inspection of the accommodation of private recruitment

         global strategy on fair recruitment. The State of Qatar has been a member of the committee since its
         inception.

Pillar 2: Labour market governance

Labour inspection

 50. Up until September 2024, the MOL's Labour Inspection Department inspected 23,438 companies, 12,094
         worksites (i.e. construction sites), and 5,063 accommodation sites.

 51. The MOL is initiating a comprehensive review of the labour inspection framework, with the view to
         establishing an `intelligence-based system' that leverages technology and best practice. Procedures will be
         re-engineered to enhance compliance with the labour laws to protect workers' rights, providing a clear and
         consistent regulatory environment, and reducing administrative burdens. The inspection functions cover
         working conditions, occupational safety and health, accommodation, recruitment agencies, and compliance
         with the new nationalization law. The ILO has provided inputs on the scope of the review and will be working
         closely with the MOL throughout the process in 2025.

 52. A series of trainings are planned in collaboration with the International Organization for Migration (IOM) on
         the subject of forced labour and trafficking in persons (FL/TIP) for MOL officials. The trainings aimed at
         providing the MOL and other officers, including inspectors, with the necessary technical knowledge to
         identify and investigate possible cases of FL/TIP and refer those to the mandated public prosecution bodies
         in Qatar.

Occupational safety and health (OSH)

53. The Ministry of Labour has initiated the establishment of a National OSH Committee that would have a
       number of responsibilities, including enhancing coordination on OSH matters among government and private
       sector entities. The Committee could look into reviewing OSH legislation, the accreditation of OSH
       professionals in the country, and also the possible ratification of ILO OSH conventions. One of the outstanding
       priorities remains the collection and analysis of data on occupational injuries and diseases.

Wages

61. The Wage Protection System (WPS) enables the Government of Qatar to monitor salaries and allowances paid
       to workers, and to detect violations. The system can reduce wage disputes and alleviate some of the workload
       of the Dispute Resolution Department and the Labour Dispute Settlement Committees. In September 2024,
       102,786 companies sent their salary information files to the WPS.

62. A block is automatically imposed on a company when the system detects that a worker has not been paid or
       has been paid less than the minimum wage (the system cannot yet detect whether the wage paid according
       to the salary information file is less than specified by the employment contract). Sometimes these events are
       administrative reporting errors on the part of the company. In such instances, the MOL will communicate with
       the company to obtain additional information. If the violation is confirmed and not immediately rectified, a
       violation notice is issued. A legislative amendment is currently being considered to automatically issue fines
       in case of non-payment of wages.

119. Following the ILO's successful organization of two side events with the UN at the Doha Forum 2023, the ILO
       also supported two high-level events during the Forum in December 2024. The first was on the future of work
       co-organized with the MOL; and the other, co-organized by the ILO and QFFD, focused on "Women and Girls
       in Conflict and Post-Conflict Situations." This followed a joint webinar held in July 2024 between the ILO and
       QFFD on the ILO's work at the intersection of the humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding nexus.

120. The JWP signed between the ILO and the MOL includes a component on secondments. Currently, two MOL
       officials are temporarily assigned to departments in the ILO in Geneva. MOFA also has a Junior Professional
       Officer (JPO) programme. In June 2024, the ILO participated in a briefing alongside other UN agencies to
       introduce potential JPOs to the ILO's mandate and operations.

121. In February 2024, the MOL and the ILO co-hosted a high-level event to mark the World Day for Social Justice,
       the first such celebration in Doha.

122. In 2025, Qatar will host the second World Summit for Social Development (WSSD), a milestone event for the
       ILO and the global development community.
