## Overview of the Maldives’ 2026 "Localisation & Digitisation" Overhaul

Following the enactment of the comprehensive *Regulation on Employment of Expatriates in the Maldives (Regulation No. 2025/R-120)*, the landscape for foreign recruitment has fundamentally shifted. The Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology has moved decisively away from lenient regularisation pathways towards strict digital enforcement, worksite compliance, and a structured phase-out of foreign labour in key sectors.

**Author:** Nas Zakaria — Founder & CEO of [LabourBooking.com](%5Bhttps://labourbooking.com%5D(https://labourbooking.com)). His expertise spans labour policy, ethical recruitment, and platform development.

For recruitment agencies supplying the critical tourism, construction, and healthcare sectors, the 2026 system is defined by four operational pillars:

1.  **Track 1: The Digital E-Visa Mandate:** The complete abolition of physical visa stickers in favour of the Xpat Online System.
2.  **Track 2: Phased Localisation Quotas:** A sunset timeline for specific expatriate occupations.
3.  **Track 3: Unskilled & Industrial Labour:** Extended 10-year permits tied to rigorous worksite inspections.
4.  **Track 4: MSME Exemptions:** Newly introduced 2026 quota fee waivers for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises.

## Track 1: The Digital E-Visa Mandate (Xpat System)

As of late 2025 and moving strictly into 2026, the Maldives Immigration authority has entirely digitised the work visa process, making it the first country in the region to fully implement such a system for expatriate labour.

### The End of Physical Stickers

Physical work visa stickers endorsed in passports are no longer legally recognised. The sole valid document for an expatriate worker is the **e-Visa**, which is dynamically linked to the validity of their approved Work Permit in the government’s Xpat portal. Agencies must ensure candidates travel with valid e-Visas, as expired e-Visas will result in denied entry or exit at the border.

### The "15-Day" Arrival Window

The arrival procedure has been tightened. Newly arriving expatriates must hold a valid **Entry Pass** issued by the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology prior to boarding. Upon entering the Maldives, the employer (or their designated agency representative) has a strict **15-day window** to apply for the official Work e-Visa via the Xpat system. Failure to do so results in immediate non-compliance.

## Track 2: Phased Localisation & Prohibited Roles

The most critical strategic change for recruitment agencies in 2026 is the phased removal of foreign worker quotas. The government is actively reserving specific job categories exclusively for Maldivian citizens.

### Immediate Quota Bans

The Ministry will no longer issue *any* new quotas for the following roles. Agencies must immediately remove these from their Maldives supply pipelines:

- Cashiers in retail businesses (fruits/vegetables).
- Taxi and rental vehicle drivers.
- Aircraft co-pilots, first officers, and vessel captains.
- Photographers, videographers, and entertainers.

### The 2-to-5 Year Phase-Out Timeline

A sunset clause is now in effect for various skilled and semi-skilled professions. Once the respective deadline passes, existing quotas will be cancelled, and no renewals will be granted.

- **Within 2 Years:** Electricians, accountants, beauticians, hairdressers, personal trainers, tour guides, and English-speaking guest relation officers.
- **Within 3–5 Years:** Nurses, HR managers, dive instructors, site supervisors, and front office managers.

> **Recruiter Tip:** Agencies supplying the resort sector must urgently pivot their sourcing strategies. Focus on roles that remain exempt from localisation timelines, or transition your business model to supplying high-end, short-term consultancy rather than permanent operational staff for these phasing-out roles.

## Track 3: Unskilled Labour & Worksite Compliance

While skilled roles are being localised, the Maldives continues to rely heavily on South Asian labour for construction, agriculture, and municipal services.

### The 10-Year Permit Extension

In a move to stabilise the industrial workforce, the maximum work permit duration for **unskilled labourers** has been extended from five years to **ten years**. This provides significantly better return on investment for employers regarding initial mobilisation costs.

### Aggressive Worksite Enforcement

This extension comes with drastically tightened oversight. The Ministry has mandated that all expatriate workers must be tied to a specific, declared worksite. By mid-2026, all employers must submit notarised *Work Site Declaration Forms*.

- **Unannounced Inspections:** Ministry inspectors now possess the authority to enter any worksite or accommodation facility without prior notice to verify that the deployed workers match the Xpat quota declarations.
- **Financial Penalties:** Assigning a worker to an undeclared site or a different occupation carries a fine of MVR 50,000 per migrant worker, with general regulatory violations attracting fines up to MVR 100,000.

## Track 4: MSME Exemptions & Financial Linking

The financial architecture of the quota system underwent a major overhaul in March 2026, directly impacting employer costs and agency billing structures.

### The MSME Quota Fee Exemption

Following a March 2026 parliamentary amendment to the Employment Act, the Cabinet is now empowered to exempt Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) from paying the standard **MVR 2,000 annual quota fee** per expatriate worker. For agencies, this makes smaller local businesses much more viable clients, as their upfront statutory costs have been drastically reduced to encourage innovation and market competitiveness.

### The MIRA Tax Compliance Link

Conversely, for larger enterprises, quotas are now weaponised to enforce tax compliance. The Ministry directly evaluates a business’s GST and income tax filing history with the Maldives Inland Revenue Authority (MIRA) over the past three years. If a client is non-compliant with their taxes, their expatriate quotas can be unilaterally reduced or cancelled without refund.

## Corridor Summary & Source Markets

The Maldives operates a highly managed, quota-centric immigration model rather than a nationality-blind one. Employers must secure a permanent or project-based quota \*before\* initiating recruitment.

- **Primary Source Markets:** Bangladesh, India, Sri Lanka, and Nepal remain the dominant sources for construction, retail, and foundational hospitality roles.
- **Hospitality & Specialised Skills:** The Philippines, Indonesia, and various Eastern European nations remain highly targeted for high-end resort operations, though agencies must carefully navigate the newly introduced localisation phase-outs (e.g., guest relation officers and HR roles).
- **Amnesty Removal:** The previous chapters allowing for the easy "regularisation" of undocumented workers have been entirely removed from the 2025/2026 regulations. The Ministry will only run ad-hoc, publicly announced amnesty programmes at its own discretion.

## Recruiter Value-Add and Opportunities in 2026

In the Maldives, the days of merely "supplying numbers" and relying on in-country regularisation to fix paperwork errors are over. The value of a recruitment agency is now entirely predicated on **digital compliance and risk mitigation**.

- **Xpat System Management:** Agencies must take the lead in managing the employer’s Xpat dashboard, ensuring eFaas verifications are correct, and securing the 15-day e-Visa conversion upon candidate arrival to prevent instant illegal status.
- **Worksite Declaration Auditing:** With MVR 50,000 fines per worker for worksite mismatches, recruiters should offer compliance audits, ensuring clients have submitted their notarised declaration forms before the mid-2026 deadlines.
- **MSME Advisory:** Capitalising on the March 2026 Employment Act amendments, agencies should actively target local Maldivian MSMEs, helping them navigate the new cabinet exemptions to save on the MVR 2,000 quota fees.
