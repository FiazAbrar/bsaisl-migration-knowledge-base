## a. Draft doc text

### (under Work visa: company worker) Bringing your family

A company worker cannot bring a spouse, children or parents to live with him: only a Maldivian citizen or a foreigner on a professional-category work visa may sponsor a family member, and the Dependent Visa is only for the family of professional-category work visa holders [F0001, F0013]. The same rule covers a sponsored family visit, because a sponsor of a visit must also be a Maldivian or a professional-category work visa holder [F0014, F0013]. Which jobs count as professional is under Work visa: professional; the Dependent Visa steps are under Dependent visa (see Dependent visa) [F0001]. Without a sponsor, a relative can come only as a visitor for up to 30 days, and may not work (see Tourist visa) [F0116, F0117].

> **Need to know**
> - Does a Bangladeshi on a skilled-category work permit (electrician, chef, resort supervisor) count as "professional category" for a Sponsorship Declaration, or only MQA (Maldives Qualifications Authority) Level 7 posts?
> - Do Bangladeshi wives of company workers get entry on a tourist visa to visit their husbands, or are they refused at Velana airport as suspected job-seekers?

## Dependent visa

The Dependent Visa lets the spouse, children under 18 or parents of a foreigner working on a professional-category work visa live in the Maldives with him or her [F0001, F0002]. It is not open to the family of company workers on ordinary work permits (see Bringing your family) [F0001, F0013]. The visa costs MVR 750 [F0007]. The worker in the Maldives does every step on IMUGA, the Maldives Immigration online portal (imuga.immigration.gov.mv); the family's part is to send the papers and travel [F0003, F0009, F0031].

### Step 1: Check you qualify

1. **Check your own visa category.** You can sponsor only if you hold a valid professional-category work visa; your Work Permit Card or Employment Approval is attached to the sponsorship [F0013, F0015].
2. **Check who you can bring.** A spouse, children below 18, or parents [F0002].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a minimum salary, housing standard or employer letter for a professional-category worker to sponsor a family, and does the employer have to sign as sponsor?
> - How long is a Dependent Visa issued for, and is the MVR 750 charged per visa or per 3 months (the 2007 Immigration Act's fee annex prices permits per 3 months)?
> - May a dependent spouse take a job, or must she get her own work permit through an employer?

### Step 2: Get the family's papers ready in Bangladesh

1. **Collect the documents for each family member** [F0118, F0004, F0119, F0006]:
   - passport copy (biodata page) and a passport-size photo to the Immigration photo standard;
   - proof of relationship in English: the marriage certificate (nikahnama) for a spouse, the birth certificate for a child;
   - a medical report and expatriate health insurance (needed for the visa after arrival).

> **Need to know**
> - Does Maldives Immigration want a Bangladeshi marriage or birth certificate attested by the Bangladesh Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or by the Maldives High Commission in Dhaka, before it accepts it?
> - Must the medical report be done in Bangladesh before travel or at an approved clinic in the Maldives after arrival?

### Step 3: Submit the Sponsorship Declaration on IMUGA

1. **Apply at least 7 working days before the family's arrival,** while they are still outside the Maldives; the option disappears once they are in the country [F0030, F0020]. Processing normally takes 5 working days [F0016].
   You'll need: form IM20 signed by you, the family member's passport copy, your Work Permit Card or Employment Approval, and the marriage or birth certificate [F0121, F0120, F0015, F0022].
   1. Log in to IMUGA, go to **Visa** and select **Apply for a Visa** [F0021].
   2. Confirm the person is outside the Maldives, select **Sponsorship** and choose the category [F0021].
   3. State the purpose as bringing in a dependent: a sponsorship filed for another purpose (such as a visit) does not qualify the person for a Dependent Visa later [F0008].
   4. Fill in the form, upload the documents, review and submit [F0021].
   **What you sign:** on form IM20 the sponsor takes full responsibility for the person's whole stay, including accommodation, food and medical costs [F0023].
2. **Download the Sponsorship Approval Slip** from IMUGA once approved [F0019].
3. **Book the flight only after approval,** and make sure the family enters the Maldives within 30 days of the approval date [F0018, F0017].

### Step 4: Apply for the Dependent Visa after arrival

1. **Apply on IMUGA once the family has arrived on the approved sponsorship** [F0031].
   You'll need: application form IM20, photo, passport biodata page, a copy of your work permit or work visa, the family member's last arrival stamp (new applicants) or last visa page (renewals), proof of relationship in English, medical report and expatriate health insurance; fee MVR 750 [F0032, F0118, F0005, F0012, F0004, F0119, F0006, F0007].
   1. Go to imuga.immigration.gov.mv, select **Visa**, sign in and click **Apply for a Visa** [F0009].
   2. In the prerequisite field confirm that the person is in the Maldives [F0009].
   3. Choose visa type **Dependent Visa** and the right reason/subtype, fill in the details, attach the documents and submit [F0009].
2. **Download the eVisa** from IMUGA when it is approved [F0010].

**If it fails:** call the Immigration hotline 9199157 or 3330487, or email permits@immigration.gov.mv [F0011, F0024].

## Marriage visa (spouse of a Maldivian)

The Marriage Visa is a resident visa for a foreigner legally married to a Maldivian citizen [F0033]. The marriage must first be lawful under the Family Act (Act No. 4/2000) and registered with the Family Court [F0036, F0053, F0055]. The first visa is for one year, then renewals of up to five years at a time [F0040, F0041]. Marriage gives residence, not citizenship: under the Constitution a foreigner becomes a citizen only in accordance with the law, and a non-Muslim never can [F0033, F0061, F0060].

### Step 1: Check the marriage is allowed

1. **Religion.** A Maldivian woman may not marry a non-Muslim man [F0053].
2. **Money (foreign men only).** A foreign man marrying a Maldivian woman must be able, as the court judges, to maintain a family, and signs a declaration accepting the Family Act's obligations before the marriage [F0055, F0056]. The Family Court's 2012 regulation set this at MVR 15,000 a month, shown by a salary letter (government job) or six months of bank statements (private job); the rule does not apply to foreign women marrying Maldivian men [F0076, F0077].
3. **Legal status.** Since September 2024 the Family Court wants a copy of the foreigner's valid visa and valid passport with the marriage application, so an undocumented worker cannot marry until his status is regular [F0072, F0075, F0073].

> **Need to know**
> - What monthly income does the Family Court require of a foreign man in 2026 (still MVR 15,000?), and how does a resort or construction worker prove it?
> - Does the Family Court ask a Bangladeshi for a no-objection or single-status certificate from the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé?

### Step 2: Apply to the Family Court to marry

1. **File the marriage application with the Registrar of Marriages at the Family Court, together with your Maldivian partner, at least 7 days before the wedding date** [F0054].
   You'll need: a copy of your valid passport and valid visa, and proof of income if you are a man [F0072, F0075, F0076].

> **Need to know**
> - Where exactly is the Family Court's marriage counter in Malé, what form is used, what does registration cost, and how long does approval take?

**Marrying abroad (for example in Bangladesh).** It is allowed, but the couple must follow the Family Court's procedure and get prior approval [F0081]. Where the country has a Maldivian embassy, the embassy must stamp a document confirming the marriage was performed by someone authorised to conduct Muslim marriages [F0078].

### Step 3: Get the Sponsorship Declaration, or change visa inside the Maldives

1. **If you are outside the Maldives,** your Maldivian spouse files a Sponsorship Declaration on IMUGA and Immigration approves it before you travel [F0035, F0034]. The steps and timing are the same as in Dependent visa (Step 3, item 1) [F0021, F0016, F0017].
2. **If you are already in the Maldives on another valid visa** (for example your work visa), apply to change visa category after the marriage is registered [F0036].

### Step 4: Apply for the Marriage Visa on IMUGA

1. **Apply on IMUGA** [F0071].
   You'll need: form IM20, your passport copy, latest arrival stamp (first-time) or latest visa page (if living in the Maldives), the marriage certificate, a copy of your spouse's National Identity Card, a passport photo, and, the first time only, a valid medical report and health insurance [F0044, F0037, F0038, F0039].
   1. Log in, go to **Visa** and select **Apply for a Visa** [F0071].
   2. Say whether you are in the Maldives, then select **Resident Visa** and choose **Marriage Visa** [F0071].
   3. Fill in the form, upload the documents, review and submit [F0071].
2. **Download the eVisa** after approval [F0043].
3. **Renew in time.** Apply within the two months before expiry; keeping the visa valid is your responsibility [F0042, F0045].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the Marriage Visa fee, and can a Marriage Visa holder work without a separate work permit?

**If the marriage ends.** A foreign spouse divorces or is divorced under the Family Act, and a Maldivian husband who divorces a foreign wife must pay her travel home [F0058, F0057]. After divorce you may keep residence only if you have a Maldivian child; after your spouse's death, only if you still meet the rules [F0046, F0047]. Your children from an earlier marriage, under 18 and not Maldivian, can get a special visa to stay [F0027].

**Scams:** Marriage applications between Maldivians and foreigners rose in 2024 as Immigration started tracing and deporting undocumented workers, and the Family Court responded by checking that the foreigner is legally resident [F0074, F0073]. When the income rule was announced, the Family Court's registrar cited a Bangladeshi husband who fled the country with his Maldivian wife's jewellery [F0079, F0076].

## Resident visa

A Resident Visa is long-term residence, given only to a foreigner married to a Maldivian, a foreigner divorced from a Maldivian who has a Maldivian child, a widow or widower of a Maldivian, or a large investor [F0052, F0046, F0047, F0048]. Spouses use the Marriage visa route (see Marriage visa (spouse of a Maldivian)) [F0033]. The investor route, the Corporate Resident Visa, needs one of the following and is applied for on ONEGov, the government's online services portal [F0048, F0049, F0050, F0051]:

- at least USD 1,000,000 invested in the Maldives in the past three financial years;
- a USD 250,000 fixed deposit in a Maldives-licensed bank for five years;
- at least USD 250,000 invested in a government-approved project.

Years of work in the Maldives are not among the grounds for a Resident Visa [F0052].

## Asylum

The Maldives offers no asylum: it has not signed the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol, and it has no asylum system or refugee protection body [F0082, F0083]. A foreigner whose visa has expired must leave the country [F0029]. Asking for protection does not stop detention: the Immigration Controller can hold people in immigration detention, potentially indefinitely, without a judge reviewing it, without a lawyer and without an interpreter [F0085, F0084]. Claiming asylum is not a way to stay or to avoid deportation [F0083, F0029].

What does exist is narrower:

- **Trafficking victims** could get a special visa to stay and work while their case is investigated and prosecuted, or where there was evidence of forced labour by a previous employer [F0086]. How to report is under If you aren't paid and Undocumented workers: regularisation [F0086].
- **A worker with a case in court** to claim a right (for example unpaid wages) can be given a special visa to stay until the case ends [F0028].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker apply for the trafficking-victim special visa (form IM22? through the police or the Labour Relations Authority?), and have any Bangladeshis received one?

## Having a child in the Maldives

A child born in the Maldives to two Bangladeshi parents does not become Maldivian: citizenship comes only from a Maldivian parent [F0059]. The child must be registered both in the Maldives and with Bangladesh [F0063, F0068].

1. **Register the birth with the island or city council within 7 days** [F0063]. The health worker or attendant who delivered the baby fills in the birth form, and the council must register the birth within a week of the application [F0065, F0066]. The duty is on the parents, and missing the 7 days brings a MVR 10,000 fine [F0067, F0064]. The law is the Birth and Death Registration Act (Law No. 23/2022) [F0062].
2. **Register the child as a Bangladeshi at the High Commission in Malé.** Bangladesh's Births and Deaths Registration Act 2004 requires a Bangladeshi born abroad to be registered with a Bangladesh mission [F0068]. At the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur the child and both parents must come in person with the local birth certificate attested by that country's foreign ministry, both parents' passport copies, the attested marriage certificate, the online application form and two photos [F0069, F0070]. The child's passport is under High Commission services [F0068].
3. **Get the child a visa.** A child under 18 can get a Dependent Visa only if a parent holds a professional-category work visa (see Dependent visa) [F0002, F0001].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé ask for to register a baby born in the Maldives, and must the Maldivian birth certificate first be attested by the Maldives Ministry of Foreign Affairs?
> - What happens to a baby born to a company worker or an undocumented mother, who cannot get a Dependent Visa: is the baby given any visa, or are mother and child sent home?
> - Do councils (Malé City Council) register births to foreign parents on the same form and fee as Maldivians?

## Work visa: domestic worker

Few Bangladeshis do domestic work in the Maldives: a 2023 survey by RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, Dhaka) found 2% of Bangladeshis there in domestic work, against 33% in hotels and resorts and 25% in construction [F0098]. Some South Asian women in domestic service in the Maldives are in forced labour, and Bangladesh's own rules for women going abroad as housemaids apply [F0087, F0106]. In 2010 Bangladesh's then High Commissioner stopped attesting work permits for Bangladeshi women after repatriating a girl forced into prostitution in the Maldives [F0093].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé attest demand letters or work permits for Bangladeshi women housemaids in 2026, and has BMET cleared any women for Maldives domestic work since 2024?
> - What do Maldivian households pay a Bangladeshi housemaid in 2026, and is housing and food on top?

### Step 1: Check you qualify (Bangladesh side)

1. **Age and guardian.** For BMET clearance a woman going abroad as a domestic worker must show proof she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0106].
2. **Training.** BMET makes housekeeping training at one of 41 TTCs (Technical Training Centres) compulsory for women going to Hong Kong or the Middle East for domestic work [F0107]. The course is one month (24 working days), for women aged 25 to 45 (returnees up to 50); a woman whose youngest child is under 6, who is pregnant, or who has a contagious or incurable disease is not admitted [F0113, F0114, F0115].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET require the 30-day housekeeping certificate for a woman going to the Maldives, which is neither Hong Kong nor the Middle East?

### Step 2: Go only through an approved agency

1. **Use one of the agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved to send women domestic workers** [F0109]. Each has lodged a Tk 15 lakh security deposit with BMET, must have at least one woman on its office staff, and may take no migration cost from you; it signs an undertaking that its licence may be cancelled if you are abused at work [F0111, F0112, F0110, F0108]. The ministry's order approved 21 agencies [F0109].

> **Need to know**
> - Do any of the 21 approved women-domestic-worker agencies send women to the Maldives, and through which Maldivian agents?

### Step 3: The employer gets your permit (Maldives side)

1. **Only a Maldivian citizen can employ you in a home,** and one person may register up to five foreign workers under the December 2025 Regulation for Immigrant Workers [F0101, F0100, F0099]. The household is registered as a worksite on the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology's Expat system, which only verified eFaas (Maldives digital ID) users can use, and you may not be put to work at an unregistered worksite [F0102, F0105, F0103]. The employer pays a quota fee of MVR 2,000 per worker for 12 months [F0104].
2. **Check the permit and entry pass are real** before you fly, as in Work visa: company worker (Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real); the permit must be for the registered household where you will work [F0102, F0103].

### Scams

- **Spa, massage and "salon" jobs.** Traffickers advertise spa and similar jobs on social media and bring South Asian women on tourist visas or as massage therapists, then force them into commercial sex [F0089, F0092]. Police have reported a rise in Bangladeshi women brought in on tourist visas for this [F0091]. A tourist visa does not allow any paid work (see Tourist visa) [F0117, F0088].
- **If you are found at such a venue,** the police arrest and immediately deport foreigners rather than treat them as victims [F0090].
- **Housemaid on paper, thrown out in practice.** Immigration has said many undocumented people came as housemaids and were thrown out by their employers, and in 2014 it froze housemaid permits for three months for that reason [F0096, F0095].
