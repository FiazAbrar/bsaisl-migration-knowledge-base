### For the intro (rate line)

Money: MVR 1 = about Tk 8.06, from the Maldives Monetary Authority (MMA, the central bank) reference rate of MVR 15.42 per US dollar (30 September 2026) and Bangladesh Bank's rate of Tk 124.35 per US dollar (8 November 2026) [G0015, G0014].

(Lead: arithmetic is 124.35 / 15.42 = 8.064. MVR 1,000 = about Tk 8,064; USD 1 = Tk 124.35. The MMA rate has been fixed at 15.42 all of 2025-2026 [G0015]; the street rate is far higher, see Sending money home.)

### For Background, What you pay, and what you earn and keep: #### Money sent home

The Maldives sent USD 38.05 million to Bangladesh in July-September 2026, 21st among sending countries [G0016]. Bangladesh Bank figures reported in the press put the year to June 2026 at about USD 118 million, down 16.4% from USD 141.1 million a year earlier [G0002]. On the Maldives side, Bangladeshi workers sent over USD 112 million through formal channels in 2024, 72% of all money sent out by migrant workers [G0003]. Much of it goes informally: in a 2023 study, 66% of Bangladeshi workers in the Maldives sent money through hundi (an unlicensed agent who pays the family in taka) and 64% had no bank account [G0004]. The cause is that wages are paid in rufiyaa and dollars are scarce (see Sending money home) [G0005, G0006].

### For the Coming home section (customs both sides; agent B owns the rest of Coming home)

- **Leaving the Maldives with cash.** Declare cash or cheques worth USD 10,000 or more in any currency to Maldives Customs, online at [customs.gov.mv/CashDeclaration/Index](https://www.customs.gov.mv/CashDeclaration/Index) [G0037]. Above USD 10,000 you must also show where the money came from, or it can be confiscated [G0038]. Bags are searched: in one case Velana airport security found USD 81,030 hidden in a cornflakes box in a Bangladeshi worker's bag for Dhaka, which he said someone at a guest house had given him [G0036].
- **Souvenirs you may not take out** without a permit: black coral and other coral, triton shells, pearl oysters, lobsters, turtles and turtle shells, conch shells, giant clams and shark fins [G0039].
- **Customs in Bangladesh.**
  - Duty-free: 65 kg of baggage, 2 used phones and 1 new phone a year, and once a year, if declared, 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [G0061, G0062, G0063].
  - Once a year you may bring a declared gold bar up to 117 g (10 tola), paying Tk 5,000 duty per tola; more is confiscated, and an undeclared bar means arrest [G0064, G0066].
  - No alcohol on a Bangladeshi passport [G0065].
  - Declare foreign currency over USD 10,000 on the FMJ form [G0067].
  - Customs emergency line at Dhaka airport: +88 01319-056591 [G0068].

  > **Need to know**

(For Step 7 / arriving in the Maldives, if W or V wants it: arriving passengers may bring personal goods up to MVR 10,000, clothes, toiletries, a personal laptop and a reasonable amount of jewellery duty-free [G0041]; alcohol and pork need a permit, and religious idols, materials "contrary to Islam", narcotics, vapes and e-cigarettes, and pornography are banned outright [G0040]; declare USD 10,000 or more in cash on arrival too [G0037].)

## Sending money home

**Send through a bank or a licensed money-transfer company, never hundi** (an unlicensed agent who pays your family in taka) [G0057]. Three things make it hard from the Maldives [G0005, G0029, G0011]:

- **You are paid in rufiyaa, but money leaves the Maldives only in US dollars.** Banks and exchange houses are reluctant to take rufiyaa, so many workers buy dollars on the street first [G0052, G0006, G0005].
- **The street price of a dollar is far above the official one.** The official rate is MVR 15.42 per dollar [G0015]; on the black market a dollar cost a record MVR 22.45 on 13 August 2026, 45.59% more [G0029]. In September 2026 an MP calculated that workers buying dollars at MVR 21-22 lose 35-40% before sending, and that a worker sending MVR 5,000 (about Tk 40,300) a month loses about Tk 12,500 a month [G0031, G0032, G0015, G0014].
- **Banks cap what a foreign worker can send each month,** because the MMA gives each bank only a limited monthly dollar allocation [G0011]. In September 2026 Habib Bank's cap was USD 300 a month, Bank of Ceylon had stopped opening new remittance accounts, and State Bank of India allowed Indian workers USD 150 [G0007, G0008].

**Your wages now go into a bank account.** Since about 24 September 2026, employers must pay all foreign workers' salaries and benefits into a bank account in the worker's own name at a bank licensed by the MMA (opening one: see Work visa: company worker, Step 7) [G0017, G0018, G0019]. Paying wages in cash is no longer allowed; an employer who breaks the rule risks losing work-permit quota, a fine, blacklisting on Xpat (the government's online work-permit system) and a police referral [G0020, G0021].

> **Need to know**
> - Is any tax taken in 2026 when a foreign worker sends money abroad? (A 3% remittance tax was repealed from 1 January 2020, but an August 2026 report says a 3% tax applies to transfers.)
> - What is the monthly cap for a foreign worker paid in rufiyaa at Bank of Maldives, Maldives Islamic Bank and Commercial Bank of Maldives in November 2026?
> - What does a worker do if the employer keeps paying cash after the new rule: where and how to report it?

You'll need:
- the receiver's bKash number (a Bangladeshi mobile-money account) and full name exactly as given when the account was opened [G0043].

1. **Choose a channel bKash lists for the Maldives:** NBL Money Transfer Maldives, Commercial Bank of Maldives, Western Union or MoneyGram [G0042].
   - **NBL Money Transfer (Maldives)** is owned by Bangladesh's National Bank PLC and licensed by the MMA; it has operated since December 2011 at Gadhamoo, Boduthakurufaanu Magu, Malé [G0046, G0047]. In 2023 it was the only Bangladeshi exchange house in the Maldives [G0048].
   - **Commercial Bank of Maldives** sends to bKash instantly through its "Bangla eRemit" link with Commercial Bank of Ceylon in Bangladesh [G0050].
   - Workers on outer islands have to travel to an exchange house in a town to send money formally [G0049].
2. **Send.** At the counter, give the bKash number and the registered name and hand over the money; the limit is Tk 2.5 lakh per transfer, as many times as you like [G0043, G0044].
3. **The family collects.** Money sent legally gets the government's 2.5% cash incentive, kept in the FY2026-27 budget, and sometimes a 2.5% bank incentive too; it can be cashed out at Tk 7 per thousand at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs [G0056, G0044]. Money sent by hundi gets no incentive [G0033].

> - What ID does a Maldives remitter ask for (passport, work visa card, work permit), and can a worker whose visa has expired still send money legally?
> - What reference number is on the receipt, and how does the family trace a transfer that hasn't arrived?

**Hundi and street dollars: the risks.**
- In Bangladesh, the Financial Intelligence Unit (BFIU) froze about 55,000 mobile-money accounts linked to digital hundi and online gambling in 2026 and decided in August 2026 to freeze about 14,000 more [G0058].
- In the Maldives, since 1 September 2026 selling or offering dollars above the MMA rate is fined MVR 25,000 to MVR 1,000,000, advertising such rates MVR 25,000 to MVR 500,000, and criminal charges can follow [G0026, G0027]. Money changers now need an MMA licence [G0028].
- In August 2026 police and immigration detained four Bangladeshi and two Indian workers for trading currency; MVR 54.4 million and USD 1.62 million had passed through their accounts in three years [G0034]. Police warned that anyone who knowingly lets someone else use their bank account for illegal transactions can be charged as an accomplice [G0035].

**What is being done.** The Bangladesh High Commission in Malé has asked banks to let Bangladeshi workers send at least USD 50-100 a month [G0009]. On 8 October 2026 Bangladesh Bank proposed to the MMA a rufiyaa settlement through the Asian Clearing Union (a regional payment system), taka cards and a currency swap, and is exploring branches of Bangladeshi banks such as BRAC Bank and National Bank in the Maldives; none had been agreed by late October 2026 [G0055, G0054].

### Saving at home

**The Wage Earner Development Bond** is a 5-year government bond for workers abroad, bought in taka or foreign currency in your own name or a named beneficiary's in Bangladesh, at authorised-dealer bank branches in Bangladesh or Bangladeshi banks' branches and exchange houses abroad, from Tk 25,000 to Tk 50 lakh, with no upper limit [G0059]. It pays 12% simple profit on holdings up to Tk 15 lakh (11% for Tk 15-30 lakh, 10% for Tk 30-50 lakh, 9% above), paid every six months, tax-free, and the principal can be taken back in US dollars [G0060].

**What happens in practice.**
- The single biggest problem for Bangladeshi workers is getting dollars, not the transfer itself. Wages are in rufiyaa, banks and exchange houses won't take rufiyaa or cap monthly dollar sales, so workers buy dollars on the curb market at MVR 21-22.5 (official 15.42) or use hundi [G0005, G0029, G0031]. This is the reason formal remittances fell 16.4% in FY2025-26 [G0002].
- The monthly caps are bank-by-bank and keep falling: SBI USD 700 -> 500 -> 400 -> 150 (Oct 2025) [G0010, G0008]; Habib USD 1,000 -> 300 (by Sept 2026); Bank of Ceylon stopped new remittance accounts [G0007]; 2024 ranges USD 250-300 [G0013]. No cap was found for BML, MIB or CBM.
- The salary-into-bank rule (gazetted 25 Aug 2026, effective about 24 Sept 2026) matters because a 2025 op-ed and a 2023 study say most workers have no bank account [G0051, G0004]; undocumented workers cannot open one, which is why the 2016 version of the rule stopped being enforced (Raajje, not logged as fact). Agent W/U may want this.
- Bangladesh-side efforts are all proposals as of 8 Nov 2026: BB letter of 8 Oct 2026 (ACU rufiyaa settlement, taka cards, swap), branches of BRAC Bank / National Bank, the HC's June 2026 talks with Bank of Maldives about direct rufiyaa-to-taka transfers (BSS 23 June 2026, page not saved, see c.) and Sept 2026 requests to SBI/BoC/Habib [G0054, G0055, G0009]. Sonali Bank planned an exchange house in Malé in 2023 [news-tbs-sonali-exchange-house-maldives]; no evidence it opened.
- Crackdown on informal dollar trading: First Amendment to the Foreign Currency Act (in force 1 Sept 2026) adds fines for selling above the MMA rate; police/immigration task force detained 4 Bangladeshis + 2 Indians on 30-31 Aug 2026 [G0027, G0034]. The Home Minister has blamed the rate on "criminal networks" (Adhadhu headline seen, not saved).
