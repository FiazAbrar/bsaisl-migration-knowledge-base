## a. Draft doc text

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open, and busy: the Maldives was Bangladesh's fourth-largest destination in 2026, with 32,408 workers cleared by BMET from 1 January to 7 November, after Saudi Arabia, Singapore and Qatar [N0001, N0002]. The Maldives has shut Bangladeshi recruitment twice since 2019, first over undocumented workers, then in 2024 after a surge of arrivals, trafficking flags and forged company papers (see What closed it before) [N0012, N0020, N0026].

#### Status

- **New work visas:** being issued. In October 2026 the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé told people coming on a new work visa to collect their BMET smart card (the emigration clearance card) before travelling (Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight) [N0104].
- **Fraud warning, 23 October 2026:** the High Commission warned of fraud, false promises and illegal money collection in the name of jobs, visas and work permits, and asked anyone cheated to report it rather than stay silent [N0103]. It told people not to pay anyone who promises a guaranteed job, a quick work permit or to "regularise" a visa, and not to pay more than the government-set cost (see Scams) [N0105].
- **Quotas closed for some jobs:** in December 2025 the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology (the Maldivian ministry that issues work quotas and permits) stopped issuing quotas at once for taxi drivers, co-pilots, ship captains, entertainment workers and shop cashiers, with more jobs phased out over two to five years [N0106].
- **Demand:** in July 2026 the Maldives High Commissioner in Dhaka said the government is regularising undocumented workers and wants more skilled Bangladeshis, such as nurses and accountants [N0101, N0102].

> **Need to know**
> - What changed in 2025 to let 40,159 Bangladeshis go, when in March 2025 private employers were limited to 10 Bangladeshi workers each and the rest went only to government projects: was the 10-worker limit lifted, or did a few large employers and state projects take most of them?
> - Is any restriction on Bangladeshi unskilled quotas in force on 8 November 2026 (a per-employer limit, a sector list, a ceiling set by the Cabinet under the 2024 amendment)?

#### The channels

- **A relative or acquaintance already in the Maldives, or a village broker:** how almost everyone goes. In a 2023 survey of 370 current and returnee workers, 59% went through sub-agents or dalals (দালাল, brokers), 36% through relatives, and only 4% through a licensed recruiting agency [N0073, N0122].
- **A licensed agency with a BMET permission letter:** rare. BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) issued recruitment permissions for the Maldives such as ZM Tours and Travels' May 2024 permission for 100 construction labourers for Sanken Overseas Maldives (Pvt) Ltd (Step 1: Find a real job) [N0108].
- **Professionals:** the Maldives says it wants more skilled Bangladeshis such as nurses and accountants (see Work visa: professional (teachers, doctors, nurses, engineers)) [N0102].
- **No legal route:** coming on a tourist visa to find work. Brokers sell it as an "open visa" or "free visa"; it is a tourist visa that does not allow work (see What people believe, and the facts) [N0076, N0096, N0030].

> **Need to know**
> - In 2025-26, what share of BMET clearances for the Maldives were individual ("single visa") clearances against agency permission letters?

#### Who qualifies

Whoever a Maldivian employer with a valid quota names on a work permit; the employer must register the workplace, hold a quota and get a valid work permit before the worker can work (Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and entry pass) [N0036]. In 2025 a private employer could bring at most 10 Bangladeshi workers, and only if it was paying its fees and was not blacklisted [N0022].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there an age limit, education requirement or skill test for a Maldives unskilled work permit, and does BMET demand a skill certificate for the Maldives?

#### Size

- **People:** about 100,000 Bangladeshis live and work in the Maldives, about a fifth of its roughly 5 lakh population [N0101, N0093]. Immigration's border data showed 90,624 Bangladeshi workers in the country in December 2023, against 139,220 Bangladeshis with "active" work permits, because a permit stays active after the worker runs away or leaves unless the employer cancels it [N0015, N0016].
- **Undocumented:** estimates run from at least 60,000 foreigners of all nationalities (US State Department, 2025) and 63,000 (Maldives Immigration, 2023) to the High Commission's late-2024 estimate of 10,000 to 15,000 Bangladeshis (see Undocumented workers: regularisation) [N0058, N0018, N0092].
- **Departures:** BMET cleared 7,020 workers for the Maldives in 2024, 40,159 in 2025 (5th destination) and 32,408 in 2026 to 7 November (4th) [N0005, N0004, N0001]. About 104,000 went from 2004 to 2018 and 4,411 in 2019, before the ban [N0007].
- **Other nationalities:** Indians, Sri Lankans and Bangladeshis make up most of the 62,000 foreign workers who entered in 2023, and the 2019 ban on Bangladeshis brought a surge of Indian workers [N0115, N0116].

#### What closed it before

- **18 September 2019:** the Ministry of Economic Development banned new unskilled Bangladeshi workers to tackle undocumented workers [N0012].
- **After the ban:** the sixth amendment to the Employment Act, under President Solih, capped workers from any one country at 100,000 [N0014, N0013].
- **17 December 2023:** the new government reopened unskilled hiring from Bangladesh, and paused unskilled hiring from other countries [N0009]. 14,689 permits went to 3,174 employers almost at once [N0019].
- **30 March 2024:** recruitment from Bangladesh stopped after employers brought people in large numbers and some transactions were flagged for trafficking [N0020]. The minister said companies had submitted forged documents [N0026]. The High Commission's labour wing said some companies brought workers in on false job offers and left them jobless, possibly to extort them, and a Bangladeshi syndicate was implicated; the workers had proper visas [N0027, N0028]. 6,436 had BMET clearance by 22 May 2024, and many could not fly [N0008].
- **29 June 2024:** Bangladeshis allowed again, but only for government projects; later, private employers paying their fees could bring up to 10 each [N0021, N0022].
- **September 2024:** the eighth amendment replaced the fixed 100,000 cap with numbers and sectors set by regulation on the Cabinet's advice; the minister said the total would follow demand, "whether it be 100,000, 110,000 or 120,000" [N0035, N0037, N0038].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

The quota is the root: a Maldivian employer pays the state for each foreign-worker slot [N0041]. Researchers found corruption running from agents in Bangladesh and the Maldives up to "quota providers who sell their yearly quotas" to the agents [N0069]. The choke point is the Ministry of Homeland Security's expatriate system, where quotas and permits are issued and which the Malaysian company Bestinet is taking over [N0040, N0047].

#### The quota

- **What it costs the employer:** MVR 6,200 a year per worker, made up of a MVR 2,000 quota fee and a MVR 350 monthly work permit fee (fees and deposit: see Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and entry pass) [N0041].
- **Paper workers:** of 221,231 active work permits, only about half are regularly paid; in December 2023 only 39,004 of 139,220 Bangladeshi permits were [N0044, N0017].
- **Quota fraud under investigation:** since 2019 Maldives Police and the Anti-Corruption Commission have investigated 22 recruitment agencies, including current or former immigration officers, for manipulating the quota system; 20 cases were still pending in 2025 [N0053].
- **"Unnecessary workers":** a Malé-based Bangladeshi journalist describes syndicates among employers, Bangladeshi agencies and middlemen that profit from hiring workers no one needs [N0029].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Maldivian employer or "quota provider" charge per Bangladeshi slot in 2026, and is it paid once or yearly?

#### Who controls the system: Bestinet

- **The deal:** the Malaysian company Bestinet signed a 15-year agreement in October 2016 to run the Maldives' expatriate system; it lapsed in 2019 when Bangladeshi recruitment stopped [N0040, N0121]. In 2024 the government chose to give Bestinet a new contract rather than pay USD 13.7 million in compensation plus further claims [N0045].
- **The money:** Bestinet takes MVR 1,542 of each worker's yearly MVR 6,200, about MVR 6.9 billion over 15 years, and workers pay USD 50 (MVR 771) each for medical insurance under the deal [N0042, N0048, N0049].
- **The system:** it adds biometrics, face recognition and ten-fingerprint capture; in February 2026 it was being tested with two state-owned companies for launch on 1 April 2026 [N0046, N0047].
- **Allegations:** Adhadhu reports that Bestinet faces allegations of corruption, fraud and deception in Malaysia, Bangladesh and Nepal, and former Home Minister Umar Naseer has called for an investigation of the deal [N0050, N0051].

> **Need to know**
> - Did the Bestinet system go live in April 2026, and does it replace Xpat Online for work permits and permit checks?

#### Company visa and "free visa"

A Maldives work permit ties you to the company that applied for it [N0030]. A "free visa" is a permit from one company sold to a worker who then works elsewhere; the High Commission says there is no such thing, and breaking the rule can mean arrest at any time and deportation (If your employer reports you absconded) [N0030, N0031]. Workers who leave the employer named on the permit lose their legal status, because the employer files a "missing" report [N0090]. Some do it because the promised job was never there [N0118].

#### The broker and the village

The dalal is the main link: he processed 59% of migrations in 2023 [N0073]. Only about 10% of workers had any contact afterwards with whoever sent them, so the broker answers to no one when the job fails [N0066].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a village dalal take per Maldives worker in 2026, and who in Malé supplies him the permits?

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

#### The cap

(See Step 1: Find a real job for the government's cost cap and the cap in each permission letter.) Maldivian rules ban agencies from charging workers recruitment fees or holding their passports, with fines of MVR 5,000 to 50,000 [N0060, N0061].

#### What people really pay

- **2023 survey:** an average of Tk 205,997, from Tk 32,000 to Tk 3,75,000 [N0123, N0062].
- **2024 press:** Tk 3 to 4 lakh, and one Chattogram man's deal was Tk 4.6 lakh (Tk 2.6 lakh paid, Tk 2 lakh more due on the visa) [N0097, N0094].
- **Maldives-side survey, 2024:** over 94% of 847 Bangladeshi workers had paid recruitment fees of up to USD 2,200 [N0083].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the street price of a Maldives construction or resort work permit in Dhaka in 2026, and what is it called (চুক্তি, কন্ট্রাক্ট)?

#### How it is paid

In parts: one 2024 deal took Tk 2.6 lakh first, with Tk 2 lakh more due when the visa came [N0094]. Workers who had paid when recruitment stopped in 2024 were left waiting with no visa (If an agency or broker takes your money: see Step 1: Find a real job) [N0008, N0094].

#### How they borrow

- **2023:** 53% borrowed from friends and relatives, 37% used family savings, 19% sold assets, 10% took NGO or microcredit loans, and only 4% a bank loan [N0063, N0064].
- **2024:** more than half of the workers in the Maldives-side survey had borrowed to pay [N0083]. The Chattogram man borrowed Tk 1 lakh at monthly interest [N0095].

#### What the job pays

- **Average pay:** MVR 6,760 a month, with over 70% working more than 54 hours a week (2024) [N0086]. In 2023 Bangladeshi workers earned an average of Tk 35,147 a month: Tk 37,066 documented, Tk 25,650 undocumented [N0072].
- **No minimum wage for migrants:** the Maldives minimum wage did not cover migrant workers (see Your rights at work for the current rule) [N0077, N0079].
- **Undercutting:** a water-sports guide says undocumented workers who work for food and a bed push pay down, so people who "deserve 12,000-14,000 MVR" work for MVR 5,000 [N0087, N0088].
- **Housing:** undocumented workers spend about 35% of their earnings on accommodation [N0080].

> **Need to know**
> - What do a resort worker, a construction labourer and a shop worker on a 2026 permit take home after food and housing?

#### What goes unpaid

- **Less than promised:** 47% got less than promised, Tk 18,203 a month on average against Tk 29,743 promised [N0067].
- **Overtime:** 36% were not paid for overtime [N0068].
- **Months unpaid:** a Bangladeshi crew building a resort on an island 75 km from Malé went unpaid for two months [N0082].

How to claim: If you aren't paid. The 2024 amendment fines employers MVR 50,000 for each case of neglecting a foreign worker, including non-payment [N0113].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The Maldives government names withheld passports and unpaid or underpaid wages as the commonest signs of trafficking, and in 2024 companies brought workers in for jobs that did not exist [N0056, N0027]. In 2024, 70% of workers surveyed did not hold their own passports, and 40% said an agency or agent had taken them [N0085]. Most arrive legally: over 70% entered through legal channels, but most fell into irregular status afterwards [N0084].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A free visa or open visa lets you work anywhere."** No visa does; the permit is for one company, and the "open visa" is a tourist visa [N0030, N0076].
- **"I've got a real visa, so a job is waiting."** In 2024 workers with proper visas arrived to find no job [N0028]. The High Commission tells workers to check the visa and the employer before coming (Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real) [N0124].
- **"Come as a tourist; the permit gets done there."** One Maldivian employer brought a returnee back on a tourist visa planning to arrange a permit, and failed [N0091]. Bangladeshis suspected of coming to work are refused entry at the airport (Tourist visa; Changing visa type) [N0092].
- **"Once there I can run a shop."** Business is illegal for migrant workers; Bangladeshi shop owners have been selling up to Maldivians [N0089].

#### Irregular routes and how they end

- **Tourist visa to work:** brokers use the visa on arrival for Bangladeshi tourists to send workers without the High Commission's attestation; those workers are being identified [N0096]. Some land via Kuala Lumpur, Chennai or Malé holding what they think is a 12-month work visa but is a 3-month tourist visa [N0070].
- **Leaving the permit employer:** the employer files a missing report; the worker can be detained and deported with little chance to put his case (If your employer reports you absconded) [N0078, N0090]. The way back is the company withdrawing the report or a Labour Relations Authority letter (see If your employer reports you absconded) [N0120].
- **How it ends:** from November 2023 to January 2025, 6,315 foreigners were deported and 7,375 left after their permits were cancelled (Going home without valid papers) [N0025].

### Scams (Work visa: company worker)

#### Done to workers

Report to the High Commission in Malé as under the 23 October 2026 notice, and in Bangladesh as under Step 1: Find a real job [N0103].

- **Jobs that don't exist.** Companies brought workers in on real visas, left them jobless, possibly to extort them; a Bangladeshi syndicate was implicated [N0027, N0028].
- **"Free visa" and "open visa".** Sold as a right to work anywhere; it can mean arrest and deportation [N0030, N0031, N0076].
- **Paying to "regularise" or for a fast permit.** The High Commission warns against paying anyone who promises this [N0105]; regularisation is done only as in Undocumented workers: regularisation.
- **Passports taken by the agent.** 40% of workers surveyed said agencies or agents took their passports; agencies are banned from doing this [N0085, N0060].

#### Tried by workers

Forged documents and falsified passports: traffickers target Bangladeshis entering on work visas and falsified passports [N0059], and forged documents submitted by companies led to the 2024 halt [N0026].
