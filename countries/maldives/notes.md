**Searched.** immigration.gov.mv (work visa, FAQs, downloads, press announcements, news 8556); xpat.egov.mv (homepage, announcements list, public verify pages, PDF guides 81, 159, 164, 166, 167, 168); one.gov.mv (Work Permit Missing Report service, needs login); gazette.gov.mv (2025/R-120 not found in English); male.mofa.gov.bd (menus are JavaScript only); English news: Atoll Times, Edition, MMTV, PSM, Raajje, Plus.mv, Corporate Maldives, Maldives Independent, Adhadhu, AsiaNews, Daily FT; Bangla: Khobor Sangjog, bdnews24, Daily Star Bangla, Jago News (blocked), BSS, TBS. **Key findings.**
- Mass Legalisation Programme: announced 18 Dec 2025 (U0023), opened 1 Jan, deadline 2 Apr extended to 2 May 2026 and declared final (U0024, U0025). No fee for the worker is published; the employer pays the work permit deposit (U0020). Final enforcement phase 2 Apr 2026 to 1 May 2027 (U0028); deportations of violators targeted before 17 Nov 2026 (U0035).
- Missing-report system fully documented in the Xpat guides of 10 Sep 2026 (U0066-U0084): MVR 1,000 to file, MVR 2,000 negligence fine, MVR 10,000 to withdraw outside the grace period; the LRA investigates. The Oct 2024 news (MVR 1,000 / 2,000 / 10,000, 30-day free withdrawal 6 Oct-4 Nov 2024) matches.
- Immigration publishes a list of 8,576 passports of absconding or missing workers held at Immigration, 8,543 Bangladeshi (U0109). A strong insider detail: workers can look up whether their passport is there.
- Work visas linked to expired permit fees show as invalid (U0097): the mechanism by which an employer's non-payment makes a worker undocumented.
- Regulation 2025/R-120 (gazetted c. 14-16 Dec 2025) replaced 2023/R-111; standing legalisation chapter removed (U0091), employer change after one month of unpaid fees with an NOC (U0088), employer change for proven negligence (U0089). Official text not saved (Dhivehi on gazette.gov.mv).

**Conflicts.**
- Raajje 185843 says immediate deportation "starting from January 2025"; MMTV 10640 (6 Jul 2026, U0034) says "from next year", meaning 2027. Raajje is evidently a typo; I used MMTV.
- Deportation totals vary with the counting period: 2,013 (Nov 2023-Jul 2024, U0134), 4,054 (to 17 Sep 2024), 9,021 (two years to Nov 2025, U0048), 9,697 (to Dec 2025, plus.mv), 12,552 (to 13 Jun 2026, U0037). These are not conflicts; I used the latest.
- Number without biometrics: 2,933 (minister, Apr 2026, U0031) vs 2,895 phase-3 target (U0038). Probably updated counts; both cited.
- Kurangi start: 2 May 2024 (Atoll Times, Maldives Independent) vs "2 May 2025" in MMTV 6894. MMTV is wrong; not used.
- Ban length: 10 years (2013, U0118) vs "will not be allowed to return" (2024, U0117) vs "varies by offence" (PILC, U0061). No current official figure, so it stays a Need to know.

**Practice.**
- PILC 2024 survey (847 Bangladeshis): 60% undocumented, 56% of them became undocumented after legal entry (U0058, U0059). Workers are told their visa became an "open visa" (U0060).
- UN CERD (Nov 2025): no judicial review, release negotiated between employers and Immigration (U0114, U0115).

**Findings and practice:**
- The visa on arrival is open to Bangladeshis; no 2024-2026 suspension for tourists found. The friction is at Velana (fake bookings, no address) [V0084-V0086] and in the High Commission's repeated warnings that tourist entry cannot become a work visa (17 Aug 2025 notice [V0079-V0081]; 31 Oct 2026 "no free visa" notice [V0082-V0083]).
- Somoy TV ran "বাংলাদেশিদের মালদ্বীপ ভ্রমণে সতর্কতা জারি" (Facebook video, around Sep/Oct 2026) and Star News "৩ মাসের ভিসায় মালদ্বীপে, ৮ মাসেও কাজ নেই" (Dream International case): both Facebook-only, not saved; they suggest tourist/short-visa job fraud is current.
- Visit sponsorship: the professional-category restriction [V0054] means ordinary Bangladeshi workers cannot bring relatives on a sponsored visit; relatives must come as tourists with paid hotels. F may want this for Bringing your family.
- Fee reading: the Act's Annex text layer misaligns labels; reading in order gives Tourist extension 750, Student 300, Business 750, Dependant 750 (V0027, V0028 notes). The Dependent visa page (F's) shows MVR 750, consistent. The 2020 second amendment to the Act is Dhivehi only; not checked.
- Tourist extension: official page (V0046-V0049) gives the IMUGA process but no fee or documents list; fee from the Act (V0027); US State Department says money plus hotel or sponsor letter (V0090). Wording "Immigration has also asked for proof..." in the draft rests on V0090 (third party): the lead may prefer to phrase it as "Travellers report being asked for...".

**What happens in practice.**
- The single biggest problem for Bangladeshi workers is getting dollars, not the transfer itself. Wages are in rufiyaa, banks and exchange houses won't take rufiyaa or cap monthly dollar sales, so workers buy dollars on the curb market at MVR 21-22.5 (official 15.42) or use hundi [G0005, G0029, G0031]. This is the reason formal remittances fell 16.4% in FY2025-26 [G0002].
- The monthly caps are bank-by-bank and keep falling: SBI USD 700 -> 500 -> 400 -> 150 (Oct 2025) [G0010, G0008]; Habib USD 1,000 -> 300 (by Sept 2026); Bank of Ceylon stopped new remittance accounts [G0007]; 2024 ranges USD 250-300 [G0013]. No cap was found for BML, MIB or CBM.
- The salary-into-bank rule (gazetted 25 Aug 2026, effective about 24 Sept 2026) matters because a 2025 op-ed and a 2023 study say most workers have no bank account [G0051, G0004]; undocumented workers cannot open one, which is why the 2016 version of the rule stopped being enforced (Raajje, not logged as fact). Agent W/U may want this.
- Bangladesh-side efforts are all proposals as of 8 Nov 2026: BB letter of 8 Oct 2026 (ACU rufiyaa settlement, taka cards, swap), branches of BRAC Bank / National Bank, the HC's June 2026 talks with Bank of Maldives about direct rufiyaa-to-taka transfers (BSS 23 June 2026, page not saved, see c.) and Sept 2026 requests to SBI/BoC/Habib [G0054, G0055, G0009]. Sonali Bank planned an exchange house in Malé in 2023 [news-tbs-sonali-exchange-house-maldives]; no evidence it opened.
- Crackdown on informal dollar trading: First Amendment to the Foreign Currency Act (in force 1 Sept 2026) adds fines for selling above the MMA rate; police/immigration task force detained 4 Bangladeshis + 2 Indians on 30-31 Aug 2026 [G0027, G0034]. The Home Minister has blamed the rate on "criminal networks" (Adhadhu headline seen, not saved).

### Work visa: company worker, Step 1: Find a real job

2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET lists every TTC (Technical Training Centre) and IMT (Institute of Marine Technology) with contacts on its website [B0005].    2. Open the PDF; check the employer, your job, the pay and the maximum fee against what you were told [B0157, B0159].

### (under Work visa: company worker) Bringing your family

A company worker cannot bring a spouse, children or parents to live with him: only a Maldivian citizen or a foreigner on a professional-category work visa may sponsor a family member, and the Dependent Visa is only for the family of professional-category work visa holders [F0001, F0013]. The same rule covers a sponsored family visit, because a sponsor of a visit must also be a Maldivian or a professional-category work visa holder [F0014, F0013]. Which jobs count as professional is under Work visa: professional; the Dependent Visa steps are under Dependent visa (see Dependent visa) [F0001]. Without a sponsor, a relative can come only as a visitor for up to 30 days, and may not work (see Tourist visa) [F0116, F0117].

## Dependent visa

The Dependent Visa lets the spouse, children under 18 or parents of a foreigner working on a professional-category work visa live in the Maldives with him or her [F0001, F0002]. It is not open to the family of company workers on ordinary work permits (see Bringing your family) [F0001, F0013]. The visa costs MVR 750 [F0007]. The worker in the Maldives does every step on IMUGA, the Maldives Immigration online portal (imuga.immigration.gov.mv); the family's part is to send the papers and travel [F0003, F0009, F0031].

### Step 1: Check you qualify

1. **Check your own visa category.** You can sponsor only if you hold a valid professional-category work visa; your Work Permit Card or Employment Approval is attached to the sponsorship [F0013, F0015].
2. **Check who you can bring.** A spouse, children below 18, or parents [F0002].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a minimum salary, housing standard or employer letter for a professional-category worker to sponsor a family, and does the employer have to sign as sponsor?
> - How long is a Dependent Visa issued for, and is the MVR 750 charged per visa or per 3 months (the 2007 Immigration Act's fee annex prices permits per 3 months)?
> - May a dependent spouse take a job, or must she get her own work permit through an employer?

### Step 2: Get the family's papers ready in Bangladesh

1. **Collect the documents for each family member** [F0118, F0004, F0119, F0006]:
   - passport copy (biodata page) and a passport-size photo to the Immigration photo standard;
   - proof of relationship in English: the marriage certificate (nikahnama) for a spouse, the birth certificate for a child;
   - a medical report and expatriate health insurance (needed for the visa after arrival).

> **Need to know**
> - Does Maldives Immigration want a Bangladeshi marriage or birth certificate attested by the Bangladesh Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or by the Maldives High Commission in Dhaka, before it accepts it?
> - Must the medical report be done in Bangladesh before travel or at an approved clinic in the Maldives after arrival?

### Step 3: Submit the Sponsorship Declaration on IMUGA

1. **Apply at least 7 working days before the family's arrival,** while they are still outside the Maldives; the option disappears once they are in the country [F0030, F0020]. Processing normally takes 5 working days [F0016].
   You'll need: form IM20 signed by you, the family member's passport copy, your Work Permit Card or Employment Approval, and the marriage or birth certificate [F0121, F0120, F0015, F0022].
   1. Log in to IMUGA, go to **Visa** and select **Apply for a Visa** [F0021].
   2. Confirm the person is outside the Maldives, select **Sponsorship** and choose the category [F0021].
   3. State the purpose as bringing in a dependent: a sponsorship filed for another purpose (such as a visit) does not qualify the person for a Dependent Visa later [F0008].
   4. Fill in the form, upload the documents, review and submit [F0021].
   **What you sign:** on form IM20 the sponsor takes full responsibility for the person's whole stay, including accommodation, food and medical costs [F0023].
2. **Download the Sponsorship Approval Slip** from IMUGA once approved [F0019].
3. **Book the flight only after approval,** and make sure the family enters the Maldives within 30 days of the approval date [F0018, F0017].

### Step 4: Apply for the Dependent Visa after arrival

1. **Apply on IMUGA once the family has arrived on the approved sponsorship** [F0031].
   You'll need: application form IM20, photo, passport biodata page, a copy of your work permit or work visa, the family member's last arrival stamp (new applicants) or last visa page (renewals), proof of relationship in English, medical report and expatriate health insurance; fee MVR 750 [F0032, F0118, F0005, F0012, F0004, F0119, F0006, F0007].
   1. Go to imuga.immigration.gov.mv, select **Visa**, sign in and click **Apply for a Visa** [F0009].
   2. In the prerequisite field confirm that the person is in the Maldives [F0009].
   3. Choose visa type **Dependent Visa** and the right reason/subtype, fill in the details, attach the documents and submit [F0009].
2. **Download the eVisa** from IMUGA when it is approved [F0010].

**If it fails:** call the Immigration hotline 9199157 or 3330487, or email permits@immigration.gov.mv [F0011, F0024].

## Marriage visa (spouse of a Maldivian)

The Marriage Visa is a resident visa for a foreigner legally married to a Maldivian citizen [F0033]. The marriage must first be lawful under the Family Act (Act No. 4/2000) and registered with the Family Court [F0036, F0053, F0055]. The first visa is for one year, then renewals of up to five years at a time [F0040, F0041]. Marriage gives residence, not citizenship: under the Constitution a foreigner becomes a citizen only in accordance with the law, and a non-Muslim never can [F0033, F0061, F0060].

### Step 1: Check the marriage is allowed

1. **Religion.** A Maldivian woman may not marry a non-Muslim man [F0053].
2. **Money (foreign men only).** A foreign man marrying a Maldivian woman must be able, as the court judges, to maintain a family, and signs a declaration accepting the Family Act's obligations before the marriage [F0055, F0056]. The Family Court's 2012 regulation set this at MVR 15,000 a month, shown by a salary letter (government job) or six months of bank statements (private job); the rule does not apply to foreign women marrying Maldivian men [F0076, F0077].
3. **Legal status.** Since September 2024 the Family Court wants a copy of the foreigner's valid visa and valid passport with the marriage application, so an undocumented worker cannot marry until his status is regular [F0072, F0075, F0073].

> **Need to know**
> - What monthly income does the Family Court require of a foreign man in 2026 (still MVR 15,000?), and how does a resort or construction worker prove it?
> - Does the Family Court ask a Bangladeshi for a no-objection or single-status certificate from the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé?

### Step 2: Apply to the Family Court to marry

1. **File the marriage application with the Registrar of Marriages at the Family Court, together with your Maldivian partner, at least 7 days before the wedding date** [F0054].
   You'll need: a copy of your valid passport and valid visa, and proof of income if you are a man [F0072, F0075, F0076].

> **Need to know**
> - Where exactly is the Family Court's marriage counter in Malé, what form is used, what does registration cost, and how long does approval take?

**Marrying abroad (for example in Bangladesh).** It is allowed, but the couple must follow the Family Court's procedure and get prior approval [F0081]. Where the country has a Maldivian embassy, the embassy must stamp a document confirming the marriage was performed by someone authorised to conduct Muslim marriages [F0078].

### Step 3: Get the Sponsorship Declaration, or change visa inside the Maldives

1. **If you are outside the Maldives,** your Maldivian spouse files a Sponsorship Declaration on IMUGA and Immigration approves it before you travel [F0035, F0034]. The steps and timing are the same as in Dependent visa (Step 3, item 1) [F0021, F0016, F0017].
2. **If you are already in the Maldives on another valid visa** (for example your work visa), apply to change visa category after the marriage is registered [F0036].

### Step 4: Apply for the Marriage Visa on IMUGA

1. **Apply on IMUGA** [F0071].
   You'll need: form IM20, your passport copy, latest arrival stamp (first-time) or latest visa page (if living in the Maldives), the marriage certificate, a copy of your spouse's National Identity Card, a passport photo, and, the first time only, a valid medical report and health insurance [F0044, F0037, F0038, F0039].
   1. Log in, go to **Visa** and select **Apply for a Visa** [F0071].
   2. Say whether you are in the Maldives, then select **Resident Visa** and choose **Marriage Visa** [F0071].
   3. Fill in the form, upload the documents, review and submit [F0071].
2. **Download the eVisa** after approval [F0043].
3. **Renew in time.** Apply within the two months before expiry; keeping the visa valid is your responsibility [F0042, F0045].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the Marriage Visa fee, and can a Marriage Visa holder work without a separate work permit?

**If the marriage ends.** A foreign spouse divorces or is divorced under the Family Act, and a Maldivian husband who divorces a foreign wife must pay her travel home [F0058, F0057]. After divorce you may keep residence only if you have a Maldivian child; after your spouse's death, only if you still meet the rules [F0046, F0047]. Your children from an earlier marriage, under 18 and not Maldivian, can get a special visa to stay [F0027].

**Scams:** Marriage applications between Maldivians and foreigners rose in 2024 as Immigration started tracing and deporting undocumented workers, and the Family Court responded by checking that the foreigner is legally resident [F0074, F0073]. When the income rule was announced, the Family Court's registrar cited a Bangladeshi husband who fled the country with his Maldivian wife's jewellery [F0079, F0076].

## Resident visa

A Resident Visa is long-term residence, given only to a foreigner married to a Maldivian, a foreigner divorced from a Maldivian who has a Maldivian child, a widow or widower of a Maldivian, or a large investor [F0052, F0046, F0047, F0048]. Spouses use the Marriage visa route (see Marriage visa (spouse of a Maldivian)) [F0033]. The investor route, the Corporate Resident Visa, needs one of the following and is applied for on ONEGov, the government's online services portal [F0048, F0049, F0050, F0051]:

- at least USD 1,000,000 invested in the Maldives in the past three financial years;
- a USD 250,000 fixed deposit in a Maldives-licensed bank for five years;
- at least USD 250,000 invested in a government-approved project.

Years of work in the Maldives are not among the grounds for a Resident Visa [F0052].

## Asylum

The Maldives offers no asylum: it has not signed the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol, and it has no asylum system or refugee protection body [F0082, F0083]. A foreigner whose visa has expired must leave the country [F0029]. Asking for protection does not stop detention: the Immigration Controller can hold people in immigration detention, potentially indefinitely, without a judge reviewing it, without a lawyer and without an interpreter [F0085, F0084]. Claiming asylum is not a way to stay or to avoid deportation [F0083, F0029].

What does exist is narrower:

- **Trafficking victims** could get a special visa to stay and work while their case is investigated and prosecuted, or where there was evidence of forced labour by a previous employer [F0086]. How to report is under If you aren't paid and Undocumented workers: regularisation [F0086].
- **A worker with a case in court** to claim a right (for example unpaid wages) can be given a special visa to stay until the case ends [F0028].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker apply for the trafficking-victim special visa (form IM22? through the police or the Labour Relations Authority?), and have any Bangladeshis received one?

## Having a child in the Maldives

A child born in the Maldives to two Bangladeshi parents does not become Maldivian: citizenship comes only from a Maldivian parent [F0059]. The child must be registered both in the Maldives and with Bangladesh [F0063, F0068].

1. **Register the birth with the island or city council within 7 days** [F0063]. The health worker or attendant who delivered the baby fills in the birth form, and the council must register the birth within a week of the application [F0065, F0066]. The duty is on the parents, and missing the 7 days brings a MVR 10,000 fine [F0067, F0064]. The law is the Birth and Death Registration Act (Law No. 23/2022) [F0062].
2. **Register the child as a Bangladeshi at the High Commission in Malé.** Bangladesh's Births and Deaths Registration Act 2004 requires a Bangladeshi born abroad to be registered with a Bangladesh mission [F0068]. At the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur the child and both parents must come in person with the local birth certificate attested by that country's foreign ministry, both parents' passport copies, the attested marriage certificate, the online application form and two photos [F0069, F0070]. The child's passport is under High Commission services [F0068].
3. **Get the child a visa.** A child under 18 can get a Dependent Visa only if a parent holds a professional-category work visa (see Dependent visa) [F0002, F0001].

> **Need to know**

## Work visa: domestic worker

Few Bangladeshis do domestic work in the Maldives: a 2023 survey by RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, Dhaka) found 2% of Bangladeshis there in domestic work, against 33% in hotels and resorts and 25% in construction [F0098]. Some South Asian women in domestic service in the Maldives are in forced labour, and Bangladesh's own rules for women going abroad as housemaids apply [F0087, F0106]. In 2010 Bangladesh's then High Commissioner stopped attesting work permits for Bangladeshi women after repatriating a girl forced into prostitution in the Maldives [F0093].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé attest demand letters or work permits for Bangladeshi women housemaids in 2026, and has BMET cleared any women for Maldives domestic work since 2024?
> - What do Maldivian households pay a Bangladeshi housemaid in 2026, and is housing and food on top?

### Step 1: Check you qualify (Bangladesh side)

1. **Age and guardian.** For BMET clearance a woman going abroad as a domestic worker must show proof she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0106].
2. **Training.** BMET makes housekeeping training at one of 41 TTCs (Technical Training Centres) compulsory for women going to Hong Kong or the Middle East for domestic work [F0107]. The course is one month (24 working days), for women aged 25 to 45 (returnees up to 50); a woman whose youngest child is under 6, who is pregnant, or who has a contagious or incurable disease is not admitted [F0113, F0114, F0115].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET require the 30-day housekeeping certificate for a woman going to the Maldives, which is neither Hong Kong nor the Middle East?

### Step 2: Go only through an approved agency

1. **Use one of the agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved to send women domestic workers** [F0109]. Each has lodged a Tk 15 lakh security deposit with BMET, must have at least one woman on its office staff, and may take no migration cost from you; it signs an undertaking that its licence may be cancelled if you are abused at work [F0111, F0112, F0110, F0108]. The ministry's order approved 21 agencies [F0109].

> **Need to know**
> - Do any of the 21 approved women-domestic-worker agencies send women to the Maldives, and through which Maldivian agents?

### Step 3: The employer gets your permit (Maldives side)

1. **Only a Maldivian citizen can employ you in a home,** and one person may register up to five foreign workers under the December 2025 Regulation for Immigrant Workers [F0101, F0100, F0099]. The household is registered as a worksite on the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology's Expat system, which only verified eFaas (Maldives digital ID) users can use, and you may not be put to work at an unregistered worksite [F0102, F0105, F0103]. The employer pays a quota fee of MVR 2,000 per worker for 12 months [F0104].
2. **Check the permit and entry pass are real** before you fly, as in Work visa: company worker (Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real); the permit must be for the registered household where you will work [F0102, F0103].

### Scams

- **Spa, massage and "salon" jobs.** Traffickers advertise spa and similar jobs on social media and bring South Asian women on tourist visas or as massage therapists, then force them into commercial sex [F0089, F0092]. Police have reported a rise in Bangladeshi women brought in on tourist visas for this [F0091]. A tourist visa does not allow any paid work (see Tourist visa) [F0117, F0088].
- **If you are found at such a venue,** the police arrest and immediately deport foreigners rather than treat them as victims [F0090].
- **Housemaid on paper, thrown out in practice.** Immigration has said many undocumented people came as housemaids and were thrown out by their employers, and in 2014 it froze housemaid permits for three months for that reason [F0096, F0095].

## Sending money home

**Send through a bank or a licensed money-transfer company, never hundi** (an unlicensed agent who pays your family in taka) [G0057]. Three things make it hard from the Maldives [G0005, G0029, G0011]:

- **You are paid in rufiyaa, but money leaves the Maldives only in US dollars.** Banks and exchange houses are reluctant to take rufiyaa, so many workers buy dollars on the street first [G0052, G0006, G0005].
- **The street price of a dollar is far above the official one.** The official rate is MVR 15.42 per dollar [G0015]; on the black market a dollar cost a record MVR 22.45 on 13 August 2026, 45.59% more [G0029]. In September 2026 an MP calculated that workers buying dollars at MVR 21-22 lose 35-40% before sending, and that a worker sending MVR 5,000 (about Tk 40,300) a month loses about Tk 12,500 a month [G0031, G0032, G0015, G0014].
- **Banks cap what a foreign worker can send each month,** because the MMA gives each bank only a limited monthly dollar allocation [G0011]. In September 2026 Habib Bank's cap was USD 300 a month, Bank of Ceylon had stopped opening new remittance accounts, and State Bank of India allowed Indian workers USD 150 [G0007, G0008].

**Your wages now go into a bank account.** Since about 24 September 2026, employers must pay all foreign workers' salaries and benefits into a bank account in the worker's own name at a bank licensed by the MMA (opening one: see Work visa: company worker, Step 7) [G0017, G0018, G0019]. Paying wages in cash is no longer allowed; an employer who breaks the rule risks losing work-permit quota, a fine, blacklisting on Xpat (the government's online work-permit system) and a police referral [G0020, G0021].

### Your rights at work

The Employment Act (Law 2/2008) covers every worker in the state and private sector except the military and police [L0001]. The Labour Relations Authority (LRA) enforces it [L0093]. Foreign workers can take any breach of their contract to the Employment Tribunal [L0071]. Any clause in a contract or regulation that takes away a right in the Act is void [L0002]. Forced work is banned [L0003]. Since the 8th amendment (1 September 2024), a foreigner may work only with authorisation under the Act and its regulations [L0085]. The 9th amendment, in force since 14 March 2026, tightened the rules on ending a job and on quota fees [L0037, L0038].

Since 15 December 2025 the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology's Regulation for Immigrant Workers has set out the rights and duties of foreign workers and their employers. It replaced the 2023 expatriate regulation [L0099, L0100]. Its appendices cover accommodation standards and what happens when a worker is reported missing ("absconding") [L0104].

#### Contract and probation

- Probation is at most 3 months [L0004]. During it either side can end the job without notice [L0005], but the rules on hours, leave and pay still apply to you [L0184].
- The employer must give you a written job description within one month of starting work [L0006]. If it doesn't, you can still enforce the terms and any verbal promises made to you [L0007].
- Under the 2023 regulation the contract had to be signed within 45 days of arrival [L0098].
- A recruitment agency may not take any fee from you, directly or indirectly [L0072].

> **Need to know**
> - Under the December 2025 regulation, how many days after arrival must the contract be signed, and must it be in a language the worker understands?

#### Pay, service charge and deductions

- **Minimum wage: none for foreigners.** The minimum wage, in force since 1 January 2022, covers every worker except expatriates [L0068]. The law leaves it to the Minister to announce when it will apply to foreigners, and no minimum has been set for migrant workers [L0069, L0070].
- **When and how.** You must be paid at least monthly, in cash or by cheque on a Maldivian bank, to you or someone you name [L0051, L0052].
- **Payslip.** With every payment the employer must give a statement of total pay, each deduction with its reason, and net pay [L0059]. If it doesn't, you can complain to the Employment Tribunal [L0060].
- **Banned.** The employer may not make you pay wages back, sign a receipt for more than you got, or buy from its shop [L0061, L0062, L0064].
- **Deductions allowed** [L0063, L0186]:
  - by law or court order;
  - with your written consent, for housing, goods, a loan or an advance, never more than one third of your wage;
  - for leave, medical costs, or insurance or provident-fund payments.
- **Absence.** For time off work the employer may deduct pay for that time only and impose no other penalty [L0009].
- **Service charge (resorts, hotels, guesthouses, tourist restaurants).** Tourism businesses must add at least 10% service charge to every bill [L0054]. It must be shared equally among all staff, and last month's share must be paid before the end of this month [L0055, L0056]. The employer may keep only 1% [L0057]. LRA can fine an employer up to MVR 100,000 for not collecting or not sharing it [L0067].
- **Ramadan allowance.** For Muslim foreign workers it is at the employer's discretion [L0053].
- **Fines on bad employers.** An employer that fails to pay wages, puts you in a job that isn't on your permit, or otherwise exploits you faces a MVR 50,000 fine [L0112]. For repeated wage breaches the Minister can tell government offices to stop serving the employer [L0066].

#### Hours, rest and overtime

- **48 hours a week** at most, not counting overtime [L0024].
- **A day off.** After 6 days in a row you must get 24 hours off in a row [L0025]. On resorts, tourist boats and industrial islands, the contract may let these days off build up and be taken together [L0026].
- **Overtime** only if your contract allows it [L0032]. It is paid at 1.25 times the hourly wage on normal days and 1.5 times on Fridays and public holidays [L0033]. On resorts, boats and industrial islands, 2 extra hours a day are allowed, paid as overtime [L0027].
- **Breaks:** at least 30 minutes for a meal after 5 hours in a row, and 15 minutes for each prayer time [L0030, L0031].
- **Records.** The employer must keep records of your normal hours, overtime and overtime pay [L0028].
- **Free to leave after work.** The employer may not keep you at the worksite, on the island or on the boat after working hours [L0029]. If you need a vehicle or boat to come and go, the employer or site owner must allow it [L0185].

#### Leave and public holidays

- **Annual leave:** 30 paid days after one year [L0040]. The employer sets the start date after talking to you [L0041]. If you ask, leave pay must be paid by your last working day before the leave [L0042]. Any agreement to give up your leave is void, and unused leave must be paid before the job ends [L0044, L0043].
- **Sick leave:** at least 30 paid days a year [L0045]. You need a certificate from a licensed doctor, handed in on your first day back [L0046]. 15 of the 30 days can be taken without a certificate, at most 2 days in a row [L0047]. You cannot be dismissed for being temporarily off work through illness or injury [L0180].
- **Other paid leave:** 10 days a year for family responsibilities, such as caring for a sick relative [L0049]; 3 days for fathers when a child is born [L0050]; 60 days' maternity leave [L0048]. Since March 2026, parents also get time off for childcare until the child turns two [L0039].
- **Public holidays:** every Friday, the Prophet's birthday, the day the Maldives embraced Islam, Independence Day, National Day, the first day of Ramadan, Eid ul-Fitr, Victory Day, Republic Day, Hajj Day and Eid ul-Adha [L0035]. Since 2026, 1 May (Labour Day) too [L0036]. Working normal hours on a public holiday earns at least an extra half-day's wage on top of overtime [L0034].

#### Your passport

Employers may not take your passport or identity documents [L0087]. Under the Anti-Human Trafficking Act (12/2013), taking or holding someone's identity or travel document as part of trafficking carries 5 years in prison [L0088, L0089]. Even so, employers holding passports is one of the most common abuses reported [L0143, L0150].

**If your passport is taken,** in this order:

1. Ask for it back in writing (WhatsApp or a letter) and keep a copy: "I ask you to return my passport no. [number], which you have kept since [date]. The law does not allow an employer to keep a worker's passport. [Name, work permit no., date]" [L0087].
2. Complain to LRA on its online form under "Noncompliance of fundamental rights" (see If you aren't paid, item 2) [L0127].
3. Tell the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing in Malé (see Contacts) [lead: cite B's labour-wing fact].

> **Need to know**
> - Have the Maldives Police or Immigration ever recovered a passport for a Bangladeshi worker, or charged an employer for keeping one?

#### Housing, health and safety

- The employer must provide housing [L0136], and it must meet the standards set by regulation [L0075]. The 2021 regulation required board as well as lodging [L0132].
- You must have medical insurance from an approved insurer before your work permit is issued [L0090]. The employer may deduct insurance payments from your wage [L0186].
- The employer must provide safety measures and safety equipment free of charge [L0073]. You may refuse work you reasonably believe is a serious danger to your life or health [L0076].
- LRA employment officers can inspect workplaces without warning [L0078]. The employer must let you talk to them freely [L0080], and LRA may not tell the employer who complained [L0079].

> **Need to know**
> - What do the December 2025 accommodation standards require (space per person, toilets, cooking), and who inspects labour quarters?
> - Who pays the work permit medical insurance in practice, and what does it cover (hospital, repatriation of the body)?

#### Pension

Foreign workers do not have to join the Maldives Retirement Pension Scheme. You can join only if your employer agrees to pay the employer's share and handle the paperwork [L0173]. If you join, you can withdraw your savings once you decide to leave the Maldives. The money is paid in rufiyaa into an account at a Maldivian bank [L0174, L0175]. The law gives migrant workers no other old-age, disability or death benefit [L0137].

> **Need to know**
> - How many Bangladeshi workers are members of the pension scheme, and how long does a withdrawal take before departure?

#### Unions

You may form or join a union without prior approval (Industrial Relations Act 1/2024) [L0092]. Being a union member, or taking part in its lawful activities, is not a reasonable cause for dismissal [L0188]. Protests are a different matter (see What can get you jailed).

### What can get you jailed

**Drugs.** Since March 2026 the amended Drugs Act punishes even small amounts more harshly [L0161]. Trafficking a Schedule 1 drug (which includes cannabis and heroin) brings 20 years and a fine of MVR 75,000 to 7.5 million [L0156]. Bringing in more than 350 g of cannabis, 250 g of heroin or 100 g of another Schedule 1 drug at the airport can bring the death penalty [L0157]. Owners, tenants and occupants of a room used for drug-taking can be liable if they knew or had reason to know [L0158]. Never carry a package or bag for anyone else [L0160].

**Alcohol.** It is sold only on resort islands. Having it, drinking it or being drunk anywhere else can mean arrest and deportation [L0153].

**Religion and Ramadan.** Practising any religion other than Islam in public is illegal and can mean prison [L0151]. In Ramadan, outside resorts, do not eat, drink or smoke in public during the day [L0152].

**Sex.** Same-sex relations are illegal [L0155].

**Protests and politics.** A visa condition bans migrants from "political activities" [L0141]. In July 2024 the government said it would arrest and deport the Bangladeshis who organised a peaceful protest in G.Dh. Thinadhoo [L0140]. In December 2024 Immigration detained 55 workers, 17 of them Bangladeshi, who had protested near Muliaage in Malé over unpaid wages. Immigration said it would act under the Immigration Act once the investigation was finished [L0144, L0146].

**Papers.** You may work only with authorisation under the Employment Act [L0085]. You may not work at a worksite that is unregistered or cancelled [L0105], and you may not be moved to another business without the ministry being told [L0102]. More than 2,000 migrants were deported between November 2023 and July 2024 [L0142]. Overstaying, raids and detention are covered in "Going home without valid papers" and "If your employer reports you absconded".

> **Need to know**
> - What does the Drugs Act now give for a first offence of using or possessing a small amount of cannabis, and are foreigners deported after serving the sentence?
> - When a Bangladeshi is arrested, is the High Commission told, and how does the family find out where he is held?

### If you aren't paid

The employer must pay at least monthly [L0051]. When a job ends, everything owed must be paid within 7 days [L0065]. In a dispute over dismissal it is the employer who must prove the reason [L0018].

**Collect proof first.** Photos of your passport, work permit, letter of appointment or contract, job description, payslips or wage register, and a daily record of your hours [L0059, L0028]. The LRA form asks whether you have a written contract and job description [L0126].

Complain in this order:

1. **Write to the employer.** If the employer doesn't fix a breach of the Act or your contract within the time your notice gives, you can resign, and the law treats that as dismissal without reasonable cause [L0017]. Send it by WhatsApp or letter and keep a copy: "To [employer]: I have not been paid my wages for [months], [amount]. The Employment Act requires wages to be paid at least monthly. Please pay by [date]. [Name, passport no., work permit no., date]" [L0051, L0017].
2. **Complain to LRA.** It is free, and LRA may not tell the employer who complained [L0079].

   You'll need [L0125, L0126]:
   - your name, email, phone and address in the Maldives;
   - your passport number;
   - your job title and start date;
   - the employer's name, address and phone, and your supervisor's name and phone.

   1. Open lra.gov.mv/submit-a-complaint-form [L0125].
   2. Fill in "Personal Information" (choose "Bangladeshi" under Nationality), then click "Next" [L0125].
   3. Under "Employment Information" give your designation and start date, and tick whether you have a written contract and job description [L0126].
   4. Under "Employers Information" give the employer's details and say whether it is still in business [L0126].
   5. Under "Employment Disputes" choose "Non Payment of Wages", "Non Payment of Overtime/ allowances", "Breach of Contract", "Failure to grant leaves", "Working Environment Issue", "Noncompliance of fundamental rights" or "Others", and write the details: months unpaid, amounts, names [L0127].

   **In practice:** in a 2022-23 case of 49 workers unpaid for months in Hulhumalé, LRA wrote that it could not make progress because the company would not cooperate, and told them to go to the Tribunal [L0148].
3. **File a case at the Employment Tribunal.** You can file yourself, or someone can file for you, about any breach of your employment agreement [L0071]. On an island outside Malé, file through the island's court [L0081]. The Tribunal can hold hearings by audio or video if travel costs or distance would delay the case [L0084]. A dismissal complaint must be filed within three months of the dismissal [L0019]. Tribunal decisions are final, though either side can appeal to the High Court within 60 days [L0082]. If the employer ignores a decision, the Tribunal sends it to court for enforcement [L0083], through the Civil Court [L0094].
4. **Get free legal help.** The Public Interest Law Centre (PILC) filed the Tribunal case for the 49 Hulhumalé workers [L0147].
5. **Tell the Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing in Malé** (see Contacts) [lead: cite B's labour-wing fact].

**If the company goes bankrupt or closes:** the contract ends within a month [L0183]. Workers are paid before the government and other creditors, starting with wages, overtime and service charge for the last 27 weeks [L0022, L0023].

**Protests.** Workers who protested over unpaid wages in December 2024 were detained by Immigration. LRA looked into their claims at the Immigration Detention Centre [L0144, L0145].

> **Need to know**

### If you want to change employer

You have the right to change employer, but only through the ministry's procedure [L0095]. Working for another business without that procedure is illegal [L0102].

**When you can move:**
- **With a no objection letter** (NOC) from your current employer, issued within the last 3 months [L0118].
- **A new work permit holder** can apply straight away if the permit has at least 8 days left [L0123].
- **Under the December 2025 rules,** a change is possible once the work permit fee has gone unpaid for a month, together with a no objection letter [L0106]. A worker reported missing during a period the ministry announces can change employer if it is proven the employer neglected him [L0107].

**Why it matters:** if your employer doesn't pay its foreign worker fees, the ministry cancels the permits, and they come back only after full payment [L0114]. In October 2025 only 7,900 of the 68,000 employers registered on the Expat Portal were paying regularly, and more than 8,000 had been suspended [L0115].

**How the move happens:**

1. **Get the NOC.** It must give the old employer's details and your details, and may give your work permit number [L0118]. Ready wording for the old employer: "No Objection Letter. [Company name, registration no., address, phone] has no objection to [worker's name], [nationality], passport no. [ ], work permit no. [ ], changing employer to [new employer]. [Date, name and signature of authorised person, company stamp]" [L0118].
2. **Get a medical report no older than 45 days** [L0119].
3. **The new employer applies.** It uses "Change Employer" on oneGov (one.gov.mv), which redirects to the Xpat Portal [L0116]. The new employer needs a quota slot valid for at least 45 days, linked to an active worksite [L0117].

   You'll need [L0120, L0119, L0118]:
   - your passport bio-data page and a passport-size digital photo;
   - a letter of appointment from the new employer, following the template on the Xpat home page;
   - the Immigration IM30 form (visa applicant information);
   - the medical report and the NOC;
   - certificates, for professional jobs.
4. **Wait for approval.** It takes 3 working days once the application is complete [L0121]. After approval, the new employer pays the work permit deposit and the new permit is issued [L0122]. If the new employer's quota slot came from regularisation and doesn't show, it should call 1500 [L0124].
5. **Don't start at the new site** until the new permit is issued and the site is registered [L0105, L0102].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a worker file "Change Employer" himself on Xpat, or only the new employer or its agent?
> - Under the December 2025 regulation, can a worker change employer without an NOC when wages go unpaid or the employer has disappeared, and how is that applied for?
> - What do brokers in Malé charge a worker to arrange an NOC or a "transfer", and is it legal for the old employer to demand money for an NOC?

### Leaving at the end of the job

1. **Give or get notice in writing** [L0014]. For a contract with no fixed end date, the notice is [L0011, L0012, L0013]:
   - two weeks after more than six months' service;
   - one month after more than one year;
   - two months after more than five years.

   The employer can skip notice only by paying the wages and benefits for the notice period [L0181]. For redundancy the notice is one month under one year of service, two months for 1 to 4 years and three months over 4 years [L0182].
2. **Collect everything owed within 7 days** [L0065]. That includes unused annual leave [L0043] and your share of service charge [L0056]. If it isn't paid, see If you aren't paid.
3. **Ask for a service record** within 6 months of leaving [L0016]. It must give the employer, your dates of service, workplace, job title, salary and benefits, and no reason for leaving unless you ask [L0016]. Wording: "Please give me a written performance record under section 24 of the Employment Act. [Name, passport no., dates of employment]" [L0016].
4. **Ticket home.** The 2021 regulation made the employer arrange the return trip when the job or permit ends [L0128]. If the government orders a worker sent home and the employer doesn't act, the government sends him home and acts against the employer, using the employer's deposit [L0129, L0135].
5. **Pension savings,** if you joined: apply to withdraw them before you go [L0174].
6. **Permit cancellation.** When the employer records on Xpat that you have left the country, your work permit is cancelled [L0109]. No exit permit is needed [L0097].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the December 2025 regulation still make the employer pay the ticket home, and what can a worker do if the employer refuses?
> - How can a worker check on Xpat or IMUGA that his permit was cancelled properly, so he is not later listed as absconded?

### Going home on leave

1. **Agree the dates.** After a year you are owed 30 days' paid leave, and the employer sets the dates after talking to you [L0040, L0041].
2. **Ask for your leave pay before you fly.** If you ask, it must be paid by your last working day [L0042]. No one can make you give up your leave [L0044].
3. **Come back within four months.** A work permit is cancelled if you stay outside the Maldives more than four months while it is valid [L0108].
4. **Check your permit and visa before you book.** They must stay valid while you are away (see Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real) [L0108].

> **Need to know**
> - Who pays the ticket for annual leave in usual Maldives contracts, and does the work visa stay valid during leave, or is a re-entry step needed?
> - What does a worker on leave need from BMET in Bangladesh to fly back (re-entry clearance or smart card), and what does it cost?

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (in force since July 2024), a worker injured at work, or the heirs of a worker killed at work, are owed by the employer: medical costs, wages lost during treatment, compensation for disability, and compensation for death [L0169, L0091]. The Bangladesh side (Wage Earners' Welfare Board grants, the BMET insurance, the body at the airport) is in Coming home [lead: cite B's Welfare Board facts].

### If you are injured or fall ill at work

1. **Get treatment.** The employer must provide or arrange medical care for a work injury, at no cost to you [L0074, L0139]. You have medical insurance from your work permit [L0090].
2. **Make sure it is reported.** The employer must tell the Minister within 48 hours of an injury needing more than first aid [L0077].
3. **Sick leave and your job.** You have 30 paid sick days a year [L0045], and you cannot be dismissed for being temporarily off through illness or injury [L0180].
4. **Compensation.** LRA's Workplace Safety and Health Protection Regulation (October 2024) covers all medical costs, wages for the days off for treatment, and damages for temporary or permanent disability [L0162, L0163]. Permanent disability compensation runs for ten years, from MVR 1,260,000 to MVR 19 million depending on age and degree of disability [L0164].
5. **If the employer won't pay,** follow "If you aren't paid" (LRA, then the Tribunal).

> **Need to know**
> - Has any Bangladeshi worker received disability compensation under the 2024 regulation, and through whom was it claimed?
> - Who pays for an injured worker's flight home with an escort: the employer, the insurer or the High Commission?

### If a worker dies

**In the Maldives.**

1. **The employer's duties.** The employer must notify the Minister within 48 hours [L0077]. Under the 2021 regulation it must also tell the family and the Bangladesh High Commission and pay for burial, or send the body home if the family wishes, arranged with the family [L0130, L0131]. The 2023 regulation also made the employer pay burial costs [L0138].
2. **The High Commission** works with Maldivian authorities to send bodies home [L0171]. Eight Bangladeshis died in 10 days in July and August 2026, and five died in a fire on Dhigurah Island in March 2026 [L0170, L0172].
3. **Compensation for a death at work:** at least MVR 50,000 even when the employer was not negligent. Where the employer was negligent, from MVR 1.46 million to MVR 15.4 million depending on age, wage and degree of negligence [L0165, L0166].
4. **Wages and dues:** all money owed must be paid within 7 days of the job ending [L0065].
5. **Pension savings,** if the worker was a member: the heirs claim from the Maldives Pension Administration Office with a court judgment or order from Bangladesh naming the heirs [L0176].

**In practice:** when Bangladeshi WAMCO worker Ameen Mia, 24, went missing on Thilafushi landfill in January 2026, no missing person report was filed for two days, and he was found dead under garbage [L0167, L0168].

> **Need to know**
> - What papers does the High Commission in Malé need to issue the NOC for a body, and how long does repatriation take?
> - Who pays for sending the body home in practice (employer, work permit insurance, Welfare Board), and what happens when the worker was undocumented?
> - Has any Bangladeshi family received the MVR 50,000 or higher compensation, and how was it claimed from Bangladesh?

### If you lose the job

- **No unemployment benefit** exists for migrant workers [L0187].
- **Notice or pay in lieu** (see Leaving at the end of the job, item 1) [L0181, L0182].
- **Unfair dismissal:** complain to the Employment Tribunal within three months [L0019]. The employer must prove the cause [L0018]. The Tribunal can order reinstatement or compensation [L0021]. Workers dismissed during probation cannot use this route [L0020].
- **A new employer can take you over** through Change Employer on oneGov and Xpat (see If you want to change employer) [L0116].
- **If the company closed or went bankrupt:** workers are paid first (see If you aren't paid) [L0022].

## a. Draft doc text

### Is it open now, and who can go?

Open, and busy: the Maldives was Bangladesh's fourth-largest destination in 2026, with 32,408 workers cleared by BMET from 1 January to 7 November, after Saudi Arabia, Singapore and Qatar [N0001, N0002]. The Maldives has shut Bangladeshi recruitment twice since 2019, first over undocumented workers, then in 2024 after a surge of arrivals, trafficking flags and forged company papers (see What closed it before) [N0012, N0020, N0026].

#### Status

- **New work visas:** being issued. In October 2026 the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé told people coming on a new work visa to collect their BMET smart card (the emigration clearance card) before travelling (Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight) [N0104].
- **Fraud warning, 23 October 2026:** the High Commission warned of fraud, false promises and illegal money collection in the name of jobs, visas and work permits, and asked anyone cheated to report it rather than stay silent [N0103]. It told people not to pay anyone who promises a guaranteed job, a quick work permit or to "regularise" a visa, and not to pay more than the government-set cost (see Scams) [N0105].
- **Quotas closed for some jobs:** in December 2025 the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology (the Maldivian ministry that issues work quotas and permits) stopped issuing quotas at once for taxi drivers, co-pilots, ship captains, entertainment workers and shop cashiers, with more jobs phased out over two to five years [N0106].
- **Demand:** in July 2026 the Maldives High Commissioner in Dhaka said the government is regularising undocumented workers and wants more skilled Bangladeshis, such as nurses and accountants [N0101, N0102].

> **Need to know**
> - What changed in 2025 to let 40,159 Bangladeshis go, when in March 2025 private employers were limited to 10 Bangladeshi workers each and the rest went only to government projects: was the 10-worker limit lifted, or did a few large employers and state projects take most of them?
> - Is any restriction on Bangladeshi unskilled quotas in force on 8 November 2026 (a per-employer limit, a sector list, a ceiling set by the Cabinet under the 2024 amendment)?

#### The channels

- **A relative or acquaintance already in the Maldives, or a village broker:** how almost everyone goes. In a 2023 survey of 370 current and returnee workers, 59% went through sub-agents or dalals (দালাল, brokers), 36% through relatives, and only 4% through a licensed recruiting agency [N0073, N0122].
- **A licensed agency with a BMET permission letter:** rare. BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) issued recruitment permissions for the Maldives such as ZM Tours and Travels' May 2024 permission for 100 construction labourers for Sanken Overseas Maldives (Pvt) Ltd (Step 1: Find a real job) [N0108].
- **Professionals:** the Maldives says it wants more skilled Bangladeshis such as nurses and accountants (see Work visa: professional (teachers, doctors, nurses, engineers)) [N0102].
- **No legal route:** coming on a tourist visa to find work. Brokers sell it as an "open visa" or "free visa"; it is a tourist visa that does not allow work (see What people believe, and the facts) [N0076, N0096, N0030].

> **Need to know**
> - In 2025-26, what share of BMET clearances for the Maldives were individual ("single visa") clearances against agency permission letters?

#### Who qualifies

Whoever a Maldivian employer with a valid quota names on a work permit; the employer must register the workplace, hold a quota and get a valid work permit before the worker can work (Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and entry pass) [N0036]. In 2025 a private employer could bring at most 10 Bangladeshi workers, and only if it was paying its fees and was not blacklisted [N0022].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there an age limit, education requirement or skill test for a Maldives unskilled work permit, and does BMET demand a skill certificate for the Maldives?

#### Size

- **People:** about 100,000 Bangladeshis live and work in the Maldives, about a fifth of its roughly 5 lakh population [N0101, N0093]. Immigration's border data showed 90,624 Bangladeshi workers in the country in December 2023, against 139,220 Bangladeshis with "active" work permits, because a permit stays active after the worker runs away or leaves unless the employer cancels it [N0015, N0016].
- **Undocumented:** estimates run from at least 60,000 foreigners of all nationalities (US State Department, 2025) and 63,000 (Maldives Immigration, 2023) to the High Commission's late-2024 estimate of 10,000 to 15,000 Bangladeshis (see Undocumented workers: regularisation) [N0058, N0018, N0092].
- **Departures:** BMET cleared 7,020 workers for the Maldives in 2024, 40,159 in 2025 (5th destination) and 32,408 in 2026 to 7 November (4th) [N0005, N0004, N0001]. About 104,000 went from 2004 to 2018 and 4,411 in 2019, before the ban [N0007].
- **Other nationalities:** Indians, Sri Lankans and Bangladeshis make up most of the 62,000 foreign workers who entered in 2023, and the 2019 ban on Bangladeshis brought a surge of Indian workers [N0115, N0116].

#### What closed it before

- **18 September 2019:** the Ministry of Economic Development banned new unskilled Bangladeshi workers to tackle undocumented workers [N0012].
- **After the ban:** the sixth amendment to the Employment Act, under President Solih, capped workers from any one country at 100,000 [N0014, N0013].
- **17 December 2023:** the new government reopened unskilled hiring from Bangladesh, and paused unskilled hiring from other countries [N0009]. 14,689 permits went to 3,174 employers almost at once [N0019].
- **30 March 2024:** recruitment from Bangladesh stopped after employers brought people in large numbers and some transactions were flagged for trafficking [N0020]. The minister said companies had submitted forged documents [N0026]. The High Commission's labour wing said some companies brought workers in on false job offers and left them jobless, possibly to extort them, and a Bangladeshi syndicate was implicated; the workers had proper visas [N0027, N0028]. 6,436 had BMET clearance by 22 May 2024, and many could not fly [N0008].
- **29 June 2024:** Bangladeshis allowed again, but only for government projects; later, private employers paying their fees could bring up to 10 each [N0021, N0022].
- **September 2024:** the eighth amendment replaced the fixed 100,000 cap with numbers and sectors set by regulation on the Cabinet's advice; the minister said the total would follow demand, "whether it be 100,000, 110,000 or 120,000" [N0035, N0037, N0038].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

The quota is the root: a Maldivian employer pays the state for each foreign-worker slot [N0041]. Researchers found corruption running from agents in Bangladesh and the Maldives up to "quota providers who sell their yearly quotas" to the agents [N0069]. The choke point is the Ministry of Homeland Security's expatriate system, where quotas and permits are issued and which the Malaysian company Bestinet is taking over [N0040, N0047].

#### The quota

- **What it costs the employer:** MVR 6,200 a year per worker, made up of a MVR 2,000 quota fee and a MVR 350 monthly work permit fee (fees and deposit: see Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and entry pass) [N0041].
- **Paper workers:** of 221,231 active work permits, only about half are regularly paid; in December 2023 only 39,004 of 139,220 Bangladeshi permits were [N0044, N0017].
- **Quota fraud under investigation:** since 2019 Maldives Police and the Anti-Corruption Commission have investigated 22 recruitment agencies, including current or former immigration officers, for manipulating the quota system; 20 cases were still pending in 2025 [N0053].
- **"Unnecessary workers":** a Malé-based Bangladeshi journalist describes syndicates among employers, Bangladeshi agencies and middlemen that profit from hiring workers no one needs [N0029].

> **Need to know**

#### Who controls the system: Bestinet

- **The deal:** the Malaysian company Bestinet signed a 15-year agreement in October 2016 to run the Maldives' expatriate system; it lapsed in 2019 when Bangladeshi recruitment stopped [N0040, N0121]. In 2024 the government chose to give Bestinet a new contract rather than pay USD 13.7 million in compensation plus further claims [N0045].
- **The money:** Bestinet takes MVR 1,542 of each worker's yearly MVR 6,200, about MVR 6.9 billion over 15 years, and workers pay USD 50 (MVR 771) each for medical insurance under the deal [N0042, N0048, N0049].
- **The system:** it adds biometrics, face recognition and ten-fingerprint capture; in February 2026 it was being tested with two state-owned companies for launch on 1 April 2026 [N0046, N0047].
- **Allegations:** Adhadhu reports that Bestinet faces allegations of corruption, fraud and deception in Malaysia, Bangladesh and Nepal, and former Home Minister Umar Naseer has called for an investigation of the deal [N0050, N0051].

#### Company visa and "free visa"

A Maldives work permit ties you to the company that applied for it [N0030]. A "free visa" is a permit from one company sold to a worker who then works elsewhere; the High Commission says there is no such thing, and breaking the rule can mean arrest at any time and deportation (If your employer reports you absconded) [N0030, N0031]. Workers who leave the employer named on the permit lose their legal status, because the employer files a "missing" report [N0090]. Some do it because the promised job was never there [N0118].

#### The broker and the village

The dalal is the main link: he processed 59% of migrations in 2023 [N0073]. Only about 10% of workers had any contact afterwards with whoever sent them, so the broker answers to no one when the job fails [N0066].

### What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe

The Maldives government names withheld passports and unpaid or underpaid wages as the commonest signs of trafficking, and in 2024 companies brought workers in for jobs that did not exist [N0056, N0027]. In 2024, 70% of workers surveyed did not hold their own passports, and 40% said an agency or agent had taken them [N0085]. Most arrive legally: over 70% entered through legal channels, but most fell into irregular status afterwards [N0084].

#### What people believe, and the facts

- **"A free visa or open visa lets you work anywhere."** No visa does; the permit is for one company, and the "open visa" is a tourist visa [N0030, N0076].
- **"I've got a real visa, so a job is waiting."** In 2024 workers with proper visas arrived to find no job [N0028]. The High Commission tells workers to check the visa and the employer before coming (Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real) [N0124].
- **"Come as a tourist; the permit gets done there."** One Maldivian employer brought a returnee back on a tourist visa planning to arrange a permit, and failed [N0091]. Bangladeshis suspected of coming to work are refused entry at the airport (Tourist visa; Changing visa type) [N0092].
- **"Once there I can run a shop."** Business is illegal for migrant workers; Bangladeshi shop owners have been selling up to Maldivians [N0089].

#### Irregular routes and how they end

- **Tourist visa to work:** brokers use the visa on arrival for Bangladeshi tourists to send workers without the High Commission's attestation; those workers are being identified [N0096]. Some land via Kuala Lumpur, Chennai or Malé holding what they think is a 12-month work visa but is a 3-month tourist visa [N0070].
- **Leaving the permit employer:** the employer files a missing report; the worker can be detained and deported with little chance to put his case (If your employer reports you absconded) [N0078, N0090]. The way back is the company withdrawing the report or a Labour Relations Authority letter (see If your employer reports you absconded) [N0120].
- **How it ends:** from November 2023 to January 2025, 6,315 foreigners were deported and 7,375 left after their permits were cancelled (Going home without valid papers) [N0025].

### Scams (Work visa: company worker)

#### Done to workers

Report to the High Commission in Malé as under the 23 October 2026 notice, and in Bangladesh as under Step 1: Find a real job [N0103].

- **Jobs that don't exist.** Companies brought workers in on real visas, left them jobless, possibly to extort them; a Bangladeshi syndicate was implicated [N0027, N0028].
- **"Free visa" and "open visa".** Sold as a right to work anywhere; it can mean arrest and deportation [N0030, N0031, N0076].
- **Paying to "regularise" or for a fast permit.** The High Commission warns against paying anyone who promises this [N0105]; regularisation is done only as in Undocumented workers: regularisation.
- **Passports taken by the agent.** 40% of workers surveyed said agencies or agents took their passports; agencies are banned from doing this [N0085, N0060].

#### Tried by workers

Forged documents and falsified passports: traffickers target Bangladeshis entering on work visas and falsified passports [N0059], and forged documents submitted by companies led to the 2024 halt [N0026].

### 3. Bangladesh universal pension: Probash scheme

Suggested place: Your rights at work, #### Pension (after the Maldives pension paragraph) or Sending money home.

Bangladesh's National Pension Authority runs a Probash scheme for any Bangladeshi working or living abroad, paid in foreign currency; after coming home you can keep paying in taka or switch schemes, and the pension is paid in taka [R0010]. Monthly contributions are Tk 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 7,500 or 10,000 [R0011]. People who join between 18 and 50 pay until age 60; those over 50 pay for 10 years [R0022]. The pension starts at 60 and is paid monthly into the bank account or mobile wallet given at registration [R0017]. The amount depends on investment returns, so the figures below are projections [R0024]: after 10 years of paying, Tk 1,530 a month (on Tk 1,000) up to Tk 15,302 (on Tk 10,000); after 20 years, Tk 4,927 up to Tk 49,268 [R0012, R0013].

1. **Register at www.upension.gov.bd** and choose Probash; you get a pension ID and password to check your deposits [R0021].
2. **Pay from abroad** in foreign currency by debit or credit card or another legal banking channel [R0015]. Contributions sent in foreign currency earn the government's remittance incentive on top, credited to the pension account [R0016]. Back in Bangladesh you can also pay at any Sonali or Agrani Bank branch counter [R0021].
3. **Know the limits.** You cannot take the money out before pension age; you can only borrow up to 50% of your deposits, with the Authority's approval, and repay in at most 24 instalments [R0018]. If you stop paying you still get a pension on what you paid in; if you die before 60 your nominee gets the whole amount back in one sum [R0019]. If you die while drawing the pension, your nominee gets the same monthly pension until the date you would have turned 75 [R0020].
4. **For family at home:** you can open a Surokkha scheme account for a family member aged 18 or over (spouse, parent, sibling) and pay in foreign currency, using their NID, bank account number and nominee details [R0014].

Help: National Pension Authority helpline 16131 or +88 09610 900800 (9am-5pm Bangladesh time), support@upension.gov.bd [R0023] (R0023's quote proves the email; the phone numbers are in Bangla digits in the same page footer: re-log with a Bangla quote if the lead wants the phone cited separately).

### 6. Appeal or review of a refusal or revocation

For Changing visa type, Going home without valid papers, or each visa's "If refused":

The Controller of Immigration and Emigration can revoke any visa, with or without notice, if the application had false information or documents, the holder falls in a barred category, the reason for the visa no longer exists, or the holder broke any Maldivian law or regulation [R0001, R0002, R0003]. ### (goes under "Work visa: company worker") If your employer reports you absconded

**What it is.** An employer reports a worker missing in Xpat (the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology's online system for work permits) and pays a non-refundable MVR 1,000 within 3 days, and the work permit status becomes "Reported Missing" [U0066, U0067]. The employer types the date you went missing and remarks on how you left, and can file without any supporting documents by declaring it cannot provide them [U0076, U0075]. It can file long after you left [U0074]. Working for anyone other than the employer named on your permit is illegal, and switching without approval leads the employer to file the report [U0054, U0056].

**What follows.**
- After a 3-day hold in which the employer can withdraw, the Labour Relations Authority (LRA) investigates whether you absconded or the employer was negligent, looking at the employer's violation history and earlier missing reports [U0068, U0069, U0071].
- If the LRA finds you absconded, the report is approved and your permit becomes "Missing Abscond" [U0077, U0019].
- If it finds employer negligence, the employer is fined MVR 2,000 and you can get a new permit with another employer through the employer-change process ("Missing Negligence") [U0072, U0018, U0089].
- After 30 days the employer may cancel your permit and free its quota slot while the investigation goes on [U0070].
- If you do not cooperate with the investigation for 10 days, your permit is revoked [U0090].
- Your passport, if the employer hands it in, is kept by Immigration, which publishes the list of passports of absconding or missing workers it holds (8,576 entries in November 2026, 8,543 of them Bangladeshi) [U0109, U0110].
- Without a valid permit you can be detained and deported at any check (see Going home without valid papers) [U0029, U0116].

**What you can do, in this order:**
1. **Check your status** on Xpat (see Undocumented workers: regularisation, Step 1) [U0098].
2. **Ask the employer to withdraw the report** if you are willing to go back [U0079]. The employer does it in Xpat: Work Permit, View Work Permit, Details, "Missing" tab, "New Request" in the Missing Withdrawal table, then (if the report was already approved) a "Re-Issue Work Permit" application and deposit payment, after which the permit shows "Issued (Arrived)" [U0079, U0080, U0084]. Within the grace period it is free; outside it the employer pays MVR 10,000 [U0078]. There is no time limit for now, but a blacklisted worker's report cannot be withdrawn [U0083, U0082]. A "Missing Negligence" report can be withdrawn only if you agree to return and have a valid contract under the Employment Act (2/2008) [U0081]. The Bangladesh High Commission's labour wing says companies that filed reports must withdraw them and take the worker back [U0057].
3. **If the employer refuses, go to the LRA** and explain why you left; if the LRA accepts your reasons it gives a letter on which another company can get you a work permit [U0055]. Say, in writing:
   > "I am [full name], passport [number], work permit [number]. My employer [company name] reported me missing on [date]. I left because [my salary was not paid from (month) / my passport was taken / I was not given food or housing]. I ask the LRA to find that my absence was caused by the employer's negligence so that I can change employer." [U0069, U0071, U0089]
4. **Appeal** a missing-report decision to the LRA if it goes against you [U0073].
5. **If you left over unpaid wages or mistreatment,** as many construction workers do, file the wage complaint too (see If you aren't paid) [U0085].

## Undocumented workers: regularisation

**Status on 8 November 2026: no legalisation window is open.** The Mass Legalisation Programme ran from 1 January 2026, was extended once from 2 April to 2 May 2026, and the Ministry called that extension the final opportunity [U0023, U0025, U0024]. The final phase of Operation Kurangi (the government's registration and enforcement drive) runs from 2 April 2026 to 1 May 2027 and is for deportation [U0028]. Since 2 May 2026 any worker found with unpaid fees is to be deported immediately, and the government says that from 2027 anyone found in breach will be deported with no further chance to regularise [U0029, U0034]. The standing legalisation chapter was removed from the expatriate employment rules in December 2025; the rules now in force are Regulation 2025/R-120 (Regulation on Expatriate Employment) [U0091, U0086].

### How workers become undocumented

Most undocumented Bangladeshis entered on a work permit and lost their status after arrival [U0051, U0059]. The main causes are an employer not paying the work permit fees, a visa not renewed, and a missing report filed by the employer [U0052, U0064]. A work visa whose permit fees have lapsed shows as invalid in Immigration's system [U0097]. Workers are sometimes told their work visa has become an "open" visa, which is in fact a tourist visa, and so do not seek a remedy [U0060]. About 70 percent of Bangladeshi workers do not hold their own passports [U0053]. Many learn their status only when they try to fly home [U0063].

### Step 1: Check your status

1. **Check your work permit** at xpat.egov.mv/Home/WorkPermitVerify: type your work permit number and first name, then "Verify" [U0098].
2. **Check your fingerprints are on record** at xpat.egov.mv/Home/RequiresFingerprint: type your passport number, choose "Bangladesh", then "Check" [U0100]. Workers without fingerprints taken under Operation Kurangi were the target of the 2026 raids [U0049].
3. **If you were regularised before,** check at xpat.egov.mv/Home/RegularizationVerify with your regularisation number (it looks like RGZ/2026/...) and first name [U0099, U0021].
4. **Ask your employer** to show your permit in Xpat, which lists your status and the due dates of every fee [U0033].

> **Need to know**
> - What status words does the public Work Permit Verification page show (for example "Reported Missing", "Missing Abscond", fees overdue), and can a worker without his permit number check at all?

### Step 2: Give your fingerprints (Operation Kurangi)

Operation Kurangi took ten fingerprints and a photo of every foreign worker, without checking legal status at that stage [U0001, U0002]. By April 2026, 2,933 of about 206,000 foreigners had not given them [U0031]. Phase three, launched by Immigration on 9 May 2026, hunts down those people, about 2,895 of them, for deportation [U0039, U0049, U0038].

You'll need: yourself (no one can go for you); your passport or work permit number and accommodation address if you have them, though you can register without them [U0007, U0008].

1. **Go without an appointment** to the Operation Kurangi Joint Operation Center, NCIT Building, Malé, Saturday to Thursday, 8 am to 10 pm except public holidays, or outside Greater Malé to your island council office in government hours [U0009, U0003, U0004, U0005].
2. **If it fails,** call 7690228 or email operation.kurangi@mohst.gov.mv [U0006].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the NCIT centre still take fingerprints in November 2026, or does a worker without fingerprints now go straight to deportation?

### The 2026 Mass Legalisation Programme (closed)

It was open to workers registered under Operation Kurangi who were living illegally, including those in a dispute with their employer and those with no employer [U0027]. Who qualified [U0010, U0011, U0012, U0013, U0014, U0017]:
- entered legally on a work permit or Work Permit Entry Pass;
- physically in the Maldives;
- work permit expired, or reported missing or "Missing Abscond" before 31 December 2025;
- not blacklisted for criminal or security reasons;
- fingerprints given under Operation Kurangi;
- not legalised in this programme before (one chance only).

Only a new employer, never the last one, could apply, through Xpat [U0015, U0016, U0065]. The employer clicked "+ New Regularized Work Permit", fetched the worker by first name, passport country and number, chose the quota slot, entered occupation and salary in USD, uploaded documents and paid the deposit; an unpaid deposit cancelled the application [U0022, U0020]. Workers without all ten fingerprints first went through a regularisation request and got an RGZ number [U0021]. Workers marked "Missing Negligence" were not legalised but could change employer instead [U0018]. The Bangladesh High Commission told workers only those who had given Kurangi fingerprints could apply [U0124]. Under Operation Kurangi the fully documented share of foreign workers rose from 28 to 62 percent [U0032].

### Routes still open

1. **Employer withdraws a missing report** and re-issues your permit (see If your employer reports you absconded) [U0079, U0092].
2. **Employer negligence found by the LRA:** a "Missing Negligence" worker can move to a new employer [U0018, U0089].
3. **Permit fees unpaid for over a month:** you can change employer with a no-objection letter from the current one [U0088].

> **Need to know**
> - Regulation 2025/R-120 reportedly keeps "an article on legalising expatriates living and working against regulations": does any case-by-case legalisation exist after 2 May 2026, and who applies?
> - What do brokers in Malé charge to "fix papers" for an undocumented worker in 2026, and what do they actually do?

### Scams

Legalisation was done only by the new employer through Xpat, and a detained worker loses the chance to legalise [U0016, U0116]. An "open visa" is in fact a tourist visa, and working for anyone but the employer on your permit is illegal [U0060, U0054].

### The crackdown

- **Who is caught.** Raids target businesses, food courts and markets; Bangladeshis are mainly detained for running businesses on a work visa [U0123, U0036, U0045]. Locals can report workers anonymously on the "Immigration Watch" website [U0050].
- **Phase three, 9 May 2026.** More than 200 Immigration and 100 police officers swept 26 zones of Malé, detained 254 people and gave notices to 44 Maldivian employers [U0133, U0040, U0041]. Those detained are deported at the earliest opportunity [U0042].
- **Since then.** 27 of 88 workers checked at the Malé West Harbour food courts, 12 of 20 in Hulhumalé, 20 of 55 at Hulhumalé's Rashu Market and 13 of 426 in Greater Malé [U0036, U0044, U0045, U0043]. In April 2026 police handed 38 Bangladeshi men to Immigration for deportation over drugs and gambling [U0046].
- **Numbers.** 12,552 people were deported between 17 November 2023 and 13 June 2026 [U0037]. The government aims to finish removing violators by 17 November 2026 [U0035].
- **After arrest.** The minister said release will be difficult "no matter who calls", though problems with visa-fee payments are considered [U0030].

## Going home without valid papers

Staying in the Maldives without a valid permit is prohibited, and a foreigner must leave when the permit expires or is cancelled [U0102, U0103]. The Immigration Act lets the authorities fine a foreigner MVR 15,000 to 50,000 for breaking it, deport him and ban him from returning for a set period [U0101]. In 2026 people found without papers are detained and deported (see The crackdown) [U0029, U0042].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the MVR 15,000-50,000 fine actually charged to a deported or departing worker, and who pays it?
> - Is any voluntary departure scheme open in 2026 (like the 2019-2022 voluntary return, under which 8,938 workers went home) [U0127]?

### Step 1: Get your passport or a travel permit

1. **If your employer handed your passport to Immigration,** look for your name and passport number in Immigration's list "Passports of absconding or missing expatriates" (immigration.gov.mv, Downloads) [U0109]. Immigration logs such passports on form IM06, which has boxes for detained, absconding and "voluntary repatriating" workers [U0110].
2. **If you have no valid passport,** get a travel permit from the Bangladesh High Commission (see Contacts) [U0131].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker collect his passport from Immigration's list: which counter, which papers, and is he detained when he comes?

### Step 2: Leave

Irregular status is often discovered at the airport when the worker tries to leave [U0063]. In 2020 Immigration let any foreigner with a valid passport and no travel ban leave through the airport [U0120].

> **Need to know**
> - Can an undocumented worker or one with a missing report simply buy a ticket and fly out of Velana airport in 2026, or is he stopped and detained?
> - Which Immigration counter handles voluntary departures now, and what must be paid there?

**After you leave.** If you resigned and left but your employer never cancelled your permit, you can cancel it yourself on the "Employee Self Requested Work Permit Cancellation Form", ticking a reason such as "Employer did not pay work permit related fees" and attaching your passport's photo page [U0094, U0095, U0096]. You then can't get a permit with another employer for 6 months unless the old employer gives a no-objection document [U0094].

> **Need to know**
> - Where is the self-cancellation form sent from Bangladesh, and is it still used under Regulation 2025/R-120?

### If you are detained

People awaiting deportation are held in the immigration detention centre in Hulhumalé, which takes about 200 [U0111, U0113]. They are sent home as their documents and tickets are ready, paid from the deposit the employer lodged with the state [U0112, U0062]. A worker with no record in the system must get identity papers from his embassy before he is deported [U0131]. Once detained, a worker can no longer legalise [U0116]. The UN anti-racism committee reported in 2025 that detainees are often not brought before a judge, lack lawyers and interpreters, and that release sometimes depends on deals between employers and Immigration [U0115, U0114].

**What family or a lawyer can do:**
1. **Ask to visit:** fill in form IM11 (Detainee Visitation Request Form, immigration.gov.mv Downloads) with the detainee's name, nationality, date of birth and passport number, attach your ID (a spouse adds a marriage certificate copy), choose in-person or video call, and email it to ecdadmin@immigration.gov.mv [U0105, U0107, U0106].
2. **Send a lawyer:** the lawyer fills in form IM10 (Legal Representation Request Form) with a copy of his licence [U0108, U0105].
3. **Call** Immigration's call centre, +960 1434 [U0132].
4. **Tell the Bangladesh High Commission,** which asks undocumented workers in need to contact it (see Contacts) [U0122].

> **Need to know**
> - How long are workers held in Hulhumalé before deportation in 2026, and can a detainee pay for his own ticket to leave sooner?
> - Does the High Commission visit the Hulhumalé centre, and how does it learn that a Bangladeshi has been detained?

### Bans after you leave

The Immigration Act lets Immigration ban a deported foreigner for a set period, and the length depends on the offence [U0101, U0061]. In 2024 Immigration said deportees were sent "with the condition that they will not be allowed to return" [U0117]. In 2013 the ban for deported undocumented workers was 10 years, against six months for those who went home at their own expense [U0118, U0119]. A blacklisted worker can't be legalised or have a missing report withdrawn [U0013, U0082].

> **Need to know**
> - How long is the entry ban for a worker deported in 2026, and how can he check it before applying for a new Maldives job?
> - Can a deported worker return on a new work permit, and after how many years?

# V findings: visits and short visas (Maldives)

## a. Draft doc text

## Visas not open to Bangladeshis

- **A work visa on arrival, or a switch from a tourist visa to a work visa:** since a 2011 Cabinet decision a Bangladeshi worker must hold the work visa before arriving, and a new worker is let in only on an Entry Pass (work permit) issued by the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology [V0075, V0074]; the Bangladesh High Commission in Malé says a person who comes as a tourist can never get a work visa (see Changing visa type) [V0079].
- **"Free visa":** no such visa category exists in the Maldives [V0082].
- **A visit sponsored by a relative who is a company worker:** only a Maldivian citizen or a foreigner on a professional-category work visa can sponsor a visitor (see Tourist visa, Visiting a relative) [V0054].
- **A multiple-entry tourist visa:** the Immigration Act allows multiple-entry permits for every visa type except the tourist and special visas [V0115].
- **Diplomatic visa:** only for people the Maldives government considers entitled to diplomatic immunity [V0034].
- **Entry on an Israeli passport:** refused since 15 April 2025 (not a Bangladeshi passport issue, listed for completeness) [V0012].

## Tourist visa

Bangladeshis, like all other nationalities except Israeli passport holders, get a tourist visa on arrival at the airport with no application in advance [V0001, V0012]. It is free and allows up to 30 days from entry [V0025]. It is only for visitors with no intention to work or earn money in the Maldives (see Can you work?) [V0114]. The Bangladesh High Commission in Malé asks Bangladeshis travelling as tourists to follow the visa-on-arrival conditions strictly [V0076].

**Status for Bangladeshis (8 November 2026):** visa on arrival open; no suspension or quota for Bangladeshi tourists is published [V0001, V0012]. Bangladeshis are nonetheless turned back at Velana International Airport for fake hotel bookings and for not knowing where they will stay (see Step 3: At the airports) [V0084, V0085, V0086].

### Step 1: Prepare

You'll need:

- a machine-readable passport (with the two coded lines at the bottom of the photo page) valid at least 1 month; a passport whose validity was extended by hand is refused [V0002, V0003]; airlines in Dhaka often ask for 6 months' validity at boarding [V0098];
- a confirmed return ticket [V0004];
- a prepaid, confirmed booking at a registered hotel, guesthouse or resort for the whole stay, or an Immigration-approved sponsorship instead (see Visiting a relative) [V0004, V0085];
- enough money for the stay: there is no published minimum, and the officer judges it against your days and hotel cost [V0004, V0096];
- for a child, the child's own passport and own declaration [V0008, V0007];
- a yellow fever vaccination certificate only if arriving from, or recently through, a yellow-fever-risk country (children under one exempt) [V0009];
- what your next destination requires, if you are flying on [V0010].

1. **Carry the printed confirmation of a prepaid, confirmed booking at a registered facility;** many Bangladeshis are sent back for carrying fake booking copies [V0004, V0085].

### Step 2: Fill in the Traveller Declaration on IMUGA

A declaration is also required within 96 hours of departure [V0020].

   You'll need: your passport, an email address you can open on your phone, your flight details and the address where you will stay [V0016, V0018].

   1. Open the portal and choose Traveller Declaration [V0005].

   5. Submit; the confirmation, with a QR code, comes by email; keep a screenshot and a print-out [V0016, V0097].

2. **Show the declaration with your passport at immigration on arrival** [V0005].

### Extending your stay

### Visiting a relative (sponsored visit)

A relative or friend in the Maldives can sponsor your visit through a Sponsorship Declaration, which replaces the hotel booking [V0004, V0055]. Only a Maldivian citizen or a foreigner on a valid professional-category work visa can sponsor; a Bangladeshi construction, resort or shop worker cannot (see Visas not open to Bangladeshis) [V0054].

1. **Send the sponsor a copy of your passport, and proof of relationship if any** (marriage or birth certificate) [V0056].
2. **The sponsor applies on IMUGA while you are still outside the Maldives** [V0062, V0057].

   You'll need (the sponsor): form IM20, your passport copy, the sponsor's National Identity Card (Maldivian) or Work Permit Card or Employment Approval (foreigner), proof of relationship, and the company seal if a company sponsors [V0056].

   1. Log in to IMUGA, go to Visa, then Apply for a Visa [V0057].

   3. Fill in the form, upload the documents and submit [V0057].

3. **Wait about 5 working days;** the sponsor downloads the Sponsorship Approval Slip from IMUGA [V0058, V0059].
4. **Buy your ticket only after approval, and enter within 30 days of it** [V0061, V0060]. Fill in the Traveller Declaration as in Step 2: Fill in the Traveller Declaration on IMUGA [V0005].

### Can you work?

No. A tourist visa is for people with no intention to work or earn [V0114]. Every foreigner must stay within the conditions of the visa, and an employer may not hire anyone without a work visa [V0042, V0041]. Breaking the Immigration Act costs a foreigner a fine of MVR 15,000 to 50,000, and Immigration may deport the person and bar re-entry for a set period [V0044, V0045]. Immigration has prosecuted foreigners who worked at resorts on the wrong visa, such as sixty musicians and photographers in 2021 [V0089]. The High Commission warns that people who try to work on a tourist visa face jail and a fine, and that anyone who enters on one visa and works elsewhere is arrested and sent back to Bangladesh [V0080, V0083].

**Overstaying:** a foreigner must leave when the visa ends [V0043]. Staying without a valid visa is prohibited and punished as above [V0040, V0044]; staying over 60 days without permission has brought fines of MVR 15,000 or deportation [V0091]. For what happens after arrest, see Going home without valid papers.

### Scams

#### Done to travellers

- **Paid "IMUGA" or "Maldives visa" sites:** the visa on arrival and the declaration are free; sites and people charging for them are scams [V0006, V0015, V0092].
- **Fake hotel bookings sold with "packages":** travellers carrying fake bookings are sent back from Velana [V0085].
- **A "job" or "free visa" on a tourist visa:** no free visa exists, a tourist can never switch to a work visa, and working on the wrong visa ends in arrest and removal [V0082, V0079, V0083].

#### Tried by travellers

Giving false information or false documents gets the visa revoked, with or without notice [V0039]; after revocation without notice you must leave within one day [V0117].

**If someone sold you a "job on a tourist visa", or you are stranded in the Maldives,** in this order:

1. **Bangladesh High Commission, Malé, labour welfare wing:** emergency hotline +960 7648006 (WhatsApp) [V0077]. (Address and consular contacts: see Contacts.)
2. **Report the broker to Maldives Immigration:** call centre 1434 (8.30 am to 2 pm, Sunday to Thursday), or anonymously through Immigration Watch on IMUGA [V0021, V0023].
3. **In Bangladesh,** complain about the broker or agency (see Work visa: company worker (construction, resort, shop), Scams).

Sample to the High Commission: "I, [name, passport no.], paid [broker, address, phone] Tk [amount] on [date] for a job in the Maldives. I was sent on a tourist visa on [date] and [what happened]. I am now at [place], phone [number]."

## Business visa

A business visa is for a visitor doing approved business, not for working as an employee [V0032]. It allows multiple entries and a total stay of up to 90 days in any 12 months [V0063]. A locally registered Maldivian business or organisation must invite you and apply for you [V0064].

### Step 1: The Maldivian company applies

You'll need (to send the company): a clear colour scan of your passport photo page, the passport valid at least 6 months, and a photo taken within the last 3 months that meets Immigration's photo standard [V0067, V0068].

1. **The company submits the application on the Business Portal of the Ministry of Economic Development and Trade,** [business.egov.mv](http://business.egov.mv) [V0119].
2. **A General Service Request (GSR) number is generated** and the status shows "Submitted" [V0065].
3. **The Ministry approves, then Immigration verifies the application** [V0066].
4. **The approved eVisa is downloaded from the Business Portal** [V0066].

Do not travel before approval [V0069]. Approval does not guarantee entry [V0070].

### Can you work?

No: a business visa covers business or work approved by the government, not employment in the Maldives [V0032]. Working as an employee breaks the visa (see Tourist visa, Can you work?) [V0042, V0044].

## Meeting visa

The Meeting Visa is a 14-day visa on arrival for attending a meeting, conference or convention [V0071]. The event must be for a business registered with the Registrar of Businesses, for government use, or approved by the relevant government authority [V0072]. You'll need what a tourist needs: a confirmed return ticket, a prepaid hotel booking, proof of money, a machine-readable passport valid 1 month, and the Traveller Declaration within 96 hours before the flight (see Tourist visa, Step 2: Fill in the Traveller Declaration on IMUGA) [V0120, V0005, V0002]. It cannot be extended, and you cannot switch to a business visa inside the Maldives [V0073, V0121].

## Transit and medical treatment

**Transit:** a transit traveller is one flying on to another country through Malé without passing immigration [V0013]. Anyone who passes immigration, even for one night, enters on a tourist visa and needs everything in Tourist visa, Step 1: Prepare [V0001, V0004].

**Medical treatment:** there is no separate medical visa; a patient comes on a tourist visa (or a meeting, business or sponsored visit) [V0024, V0001]. Advanced care is only in Malé (ADK Hospital, Tree Top Hospital and the government's IGMH), public hospitals ask for payment or proof of insurance up front, and unpaid bills can bar future travel [V0093, V0094, V0095]. If treatment is still going on when the visa ends, a special visa can be granted to continue it, on form IM22 signed by a sponsor who takes full responsibility; it costs MVR 750 for each three months or part [V0035, V0052, V0037].    - whether the job is permanent or on contract, and the contract length.

   **Check what it says:**

   - The salary must be at least what the employer advertised on the National Job Center, because the employer may hire abroad only after advertising there for at least 7 days, and a quota worker is owed the advertised salary and benefits [W0168, W0170].
   - The job must not be one the Maldives gives no quota for: taxi and other fare-collecting drivers, co-pilots, vessel captains, photography and videography, entertainment, and cashiers in fruit, vegetable and food shops [W0138]. For jobs being closed to foreigners, check the date (see How long you can stay) [W0139, W0140, W0141].
   - The work site must be named: a foreign worker may not work at a site that is not registered with the ministry [W0133].

   **Ask for it in writing.** Send the employer or agent: "Please send me a copy of the letter of appointment you uploaded in Xpat for my work permit, showing my basic salary in USD and MVR, work site, working hours and contract length, before I pay anything." [W0057, W0060]

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does a worker on a new work permit sign the letter of appointment, as the employer-change template has required since 7 January 2026 [W0183]?
   > - Do Bangladeshi workers usually see the letter of appointment before leaving Dhaka, or only the entry pass?

2. **Know what you must not pay for.** The employer pays the quota fee (MVR 2,000 per worker for 12 months), the work permit fee (MVR 350 a month) and the security deposit, which the ministry last published as MVR 8,000 for a Bangladeshi [W0135, W0024, W0025, W0064]. Regulation 2025/R-120, the Regulation on Expatriate Employment, puts every permit fee, service fee and fine on the employer [W0162]. Under the Employment Act an employment agency may charge the employer, but no fee may be taken directly or indirectly from a prospective employee [W0125]. Quota and work permit fees are not refunded [W0177]. What workers really pay in Bangladesh is in What you pay, and what you earn and keep (see What people really pay).

   **If someone in the Maldives asks you for these fees:** report it to the Labour Relations Authority (LRA) on 332 8282 or through its online complaint form [W0163]. The Bangladesh-side complaint routes are in Step 1: Find a real job.

3. **Sign the written contract within 45 days of arrival and keep your copy.** The regulation gives the employer 45 days from your arrival to sign a contract with you and give you a copy [W0151]. The Employment Act requires a written agreement that states your name, passport number and emergency contact, whether the job is permanent or temporary, the start date, pay and benefits, how salary is calculated, pay day, leave days, disciplinary rules, appraisal and how dismissal works [W0126, W0127]. The employer must give you a signed copy, and an employer who does not is fined MVR 2,000 to MVR 20,000 [W0128, W0129]. A fixed-term contract may last at most two years, and probation at most three months [W0130, W0131]. You must also get a written job description within 20 days of starting [W0152]. Before the employer can make the first work permit fee payment, it uploads a notarised agreement between itself and you [W0026].

   **Check it is real:** the salary in USD and MVR, the work site and the working hours must match the letter of appointment (item 1) [W0057].

   **If it fails:** a lower salary or no contract is a wage complaint (see If you aren't paid); your rights under the contract are in Your rights at work.

   > **Need to know**
   > - If the contract signed in the Maldives pays less than the letter of appointment uploaded in Xpat, which one does the LRA enforce?
   > - Is the contract given only in English or Dhivehi, or also in Bengali?

**Scams:** since 15 September 2026 nobody who enters on a business visa or a special visa can get a work permit without leaving; a real job needs a Work Permit Entry Pass issued before you fly [W0032]. A "business visa" or "special visa" sold as a work visa is therefore not a route to a work permit [W0032].

### Step 3: The employer gets your work permit and entry pass

Everything in this step is done by the employer on Xpat, logged in with a verified eFaas account (the Maldives government digital ID) [W0049]. The office in charge is the Department of Foreign Labour at the Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology, on 1485 and xpat@home.gov.mv [W0040, W0041]. The rules are Regulation 2025/R-120, published on 14 December 2025 [W0047]. Whether new permits are being issued for Bangladeshis at all is in Is it open now, and who can go? (see Status).

1. **The employer registers the work site and gets a quota.** Before bringing a worker it must register the work site, obtain a quota and obtain a work permit; bringing a worker without a quota, or moving a worker to another business without telling the ministry, is illegal [W0132, W0134]. It first advertises the job on the National Job Center for at least 7 days and may use a quota only if no suitable Maldivian applied [W0168]. The ministry checks the employer's finances against its tax records [W0176]. The quota costs MVR 2,000 per worker for 12 months, except that micro, small and medium businesses get up to 20 quota slots free [W0135, W0048]. The quota slot must still have at least 145 days of validity when the work permit is requested [W0010].

2. **You send the employer your documents; it applies for the work permit.** You must be 18 or older [W0011].

   You'll need:
   - passport bio-data page, valid at least 12 months (and at least one year on arrival) [W0013, W0166];
   - a digital passport photo to Maldives Immigration standards [W0004];
   - a police report from Bangladesh less than 6 months old (see Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates) [W0164];
   - proof of the vaccinations the Ministry of Health requires [W0165];
   - for a professional job, your certificates with certified English translations, and any registration the job needs (see Work visa: professional (teachers, doctors, nurses, engineers)) [W0016].

   The employer adds the letter of appointment and the Maldives Immigration Visa Applicant Information Form (IM30) [W0014, W0015]. You must travel on the same passport used in the application; if you get a new passport after the entry pass is issued, the employer must cancel it and apply again [W0017]. A completed application is processed in 3 working days, and the employer is notified by email and SMS and tracks it under "My Applications" [W0021, W0023].

3. **The employer pays the deposit; the ministry issues your Work Permit Entry Pass.** Once approved, the application moves to "Pending Payment"; after payment the Work Permit Entry Pass is issued [W0018]. The deposit is set for each nationality and is no more than the cost of your ticket home [W0153]. The ministry's last published table puts it at MVR 8,000 for Bangladesh, against MVR 3,500 for India, MVR 3,800 for Pakistan, MVR 4,000 for Sri Lanka and MVR 8,000 for Nepal, and says rates can change without notice [W0064, W0065, W0066]. The employer pays it online by BML (Bank of Maldives) Gateway or its Xpat wallet; government employers are exempt [W0181, W0182]. When the deposit is refunded it goes back to the employer's Xpat wallet [W0045]. After payment the permit shows "Issued (Pending Arrival)" [W0180].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What deposit does Schedule 5 of Regulation 2025/R-120 set for a Bangladeshi worker in 2026, and is the 2021 table (MVR 8,000) still applied?
   > - Do agents in Dhaka or Malé charge workers for the deposit, and under what name?

4. **Pass the pre-departure medical, if it runs in Bangladesh.** The Maldives Ministry of Health requires everyone seeking a work permit to take a Pre-Departure Health Assessment (PDHA) in their home country, where the PDHA system is running [W0085]. It covers your medical history, a physical examination, a chest X-ray, and tests for HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C and syphilis, plus filariasis and malaria tests for people from areas where they are common [W0087]. You also get a measles-rubella (MR) vaccination [W0088]. The clinic must be designated by the Ministry of Health Maldives [W0096]. At registration the clinic checks your face against your passport and takes your photo and fingerprints [W0095, W0092]. You are counselled and sign a consent form in a language you understand [W0094]. Grade A means you are cleared to work in the Maldives; Grade B means a significant abnormal finding and you are not eligible [W0089]. The clinic uploads your fingerprints and result within 2 days, and the result is valid for 3 months from the examination [W0093, W0090].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is the PDHA running in Bangladesh in November 2026; if so, which Dhaka clinics are designated and what do they charge?
   > - If it is not running, what medical (if any) do Maldives employers require before a Bangladeshi worker flies?

5. **Fly within 90 days.** You must enter the Maldives within 90 days of the entry pass being issued, and you must hold it on arrival [W0019, W0009]. The flight and BMET clearance are in Step 6: Orientation, BMET clearance and the flight.

**How long, in order:**

1. Job Center advert: at least 7 days [W0168].
2. Work permit decision: 3 working days once complete [W0021].
3. Deposit paid: entry pass issued [W0018].
4. You fly within 90 days [W0019].
5. On landing you hold a 30-day conditional work permit [W0148].
6. Medical within 15 days of arrival, and the work e-Visa within 15 days of arrival (Step 7) [W0043, W0003].
7. Full work permit within 30 days, extendable by 15; if no permit is issued within 45 days you may move to another employer without your employer's consent [W0169, W0149].
8. Contract within 45 days of arrival [W0151].

The regulation counts these days without government holidays [W0150].

**What you get back:** a Work Permit Entry Pass and a work permit number in the form WP00000000, which you need for every check in Step 5 [W0018, W0055].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a refused quota or work permit be appealed, and by whom?
> - What does the entry pass document look like (PDF, QR code, fields), and does BMET check it before clearance?

### Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real

1. **Check the work permit on Xpat before you pay the rest or fly.**

   You'll need: your work permit number (WP followed by 8 digits) and your first name as on the permit, or your passport number [W0055, W0054].

   1. Open [xpat.egov.mv/Home/WorkPermitVerify](https://xpat.egov.mv/Home/WorkPermitVerify), or click "Work Permit Verification" on the xpat.egov.mv home page [W0051].
   2. Type the number in the "Work Permit Number" box [W0055].
   3. Type your first name or your passport number in the "Name or Passport Number" box [W0054].
   4. Press "Verify" [W0054].

   **Check it is real:** the employer and job must match your letter of appointment [W0057]. Once the deposit is paid, the employer's Xpat record shows "Issued (Pending Arrival)"; ask the employer for a screenshot of it before you fly [W0180].

   **If it fails:** "Employee not found. Please Enter correct details" means no match; check the number for typing errors and try your passport number instead of your name [W0158]. If it still fails, call the Department of Foreign Labour on 1485 or email xpat@home.gov.mv [W0040, W0041].

2. **Check the employer is not on the negligence list.** Xpat publishes the names of employers with confirmed negligence cases since 15 September 2025, with their registration number and number of cases [W0052, W0056]. Open [xpat.egov.mv/EmploymentApproval/EmploymentApprovalMissingPerson/MissingNegligenceEmployerList](https://xpat.egov.mv/EmploymentApproval/EmploymentApprovalMissingPerson/MissingNegligenceEmployerList) and type the company name or registration number in "Search" [W0056]. Since 15 September 2025 an employer that abandons or neglects foreign workers it brought in is fined MVR 50,000 per worker [W0046, W0035].

3. **After arrival, check the e-Visa.** Once approved, an e-Visa logo appears in the Visa section of Xpat and the e-Visa can be viewed or downloaded at any time [W0005]. The employer holds the Xpat account, so ask it for a copy [W0160]. The e-Visa is valid as long as the work permit, and it shows as invalid if the work permit fee has not been paid [W0030, W0006].

4. **Use the Xpat MV app as a second check.** There is no physical work permit card; the official Xpat MV app holds a digital card, added with your work permit number and passport number, and its QR code checks the permit against Xpat [W0156]. The app was last updated in 2023, so use the verification page in item 1 if it fails [W0157].

5. **Check whether you must give fingerprints.** The Fingerprint Requirement page on xpat.egov.mv needs only your passport number and country, and tells you only whether you must enrol fingerprints [W0159, W0051].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the Work Permit Verification result show (employer, occupation, expiry date, status)?
> - Does Maldives Immigration have a public check for a work e-Visa or entry pass by passport number?

**If a check fails, in this order:**

1. Department of Foreign Labour, Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology: 1485, xpat@home.gov.mv, H. Pamelia, Waterfront Building, Boduthakurufaanu Magu, Malé [W0040, W0041, W0042].

### Step 7: After arrival

1. **Let the employer meet you at the airport.** Employers must receive their workers on arrival in person or send someone [W0020]. Immigration lets you in on the entry pass; if the passport number on your papers does not match your passport, your passport can be held for checks before entry [W0002, W0008]. From landing you hold a 30-day conditional work permit (Step 3, How long) [W0148].

2. **Do the work permit medical within 15 days.** New arrivals must submit a work permit medical within 15 days of arrival [W0043]. It must come from a provider registered with the ministry [W0172]. Registered providers include government hospitals such as Indira Gandhi Memorial Hospital and Hulhumalé Hospital and the atoll and regional hospitals, and private ones such as ADK Hospital, Medica Hospital, Tree Top Hospital and Jibon Clinic; the list was last updated on 18 August 2026 [W0076, W0077, W0078, W0079]. The employer uploads the report in Xpat, choosing the provider from a list and giving the issue and expiry dates [W0081, W0082]. After approval your work permit card shows "fit", "unfit" or "needs isolation" [W0083]. If you are unfit or need isolation the employer contacts the Health Protection Agency (HPA) [W0084]. If you are found unfit no permit is issued and the employer must arrange your return; a treatable condition that is not contagious may get a conditional permit [W0155]. The medical is valid for 1 year from the doctor's final approval and must be renewed [W0044, W0062]. If a permit is cancelled while the medical has lapsed, a fine of MVR 2,000 per year applies, which is the employer's to pay [W0061, W0162].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is the Maldives medical due within 15 days of arrival (Xpat) or 30 days (Ministry of Health guideline) [W0043, W0086]?
   > - Which results make a worker unfit in the Maldives medical (HIV, hepatitis B, TB), and who pays the flight home?
   > - What do registered clinics charge for the work permit medical in 2026, and do employers deduct it from wages?

3. **Get the health insurance from day one.** You must be insured from the date of arrival [W0091]. The insurance must come from a registered provider, and your permit lapses if the insurance or medical expires [W0172, W0154]. The ministry's minimum policy [W0067, W0068, W0069, W0070, W0071, W0072, W0073, W0074]:

   - at least MVR 100,000 cover for 12 months, inside the Maldives, for people aged 65 and below;
   - outpatient care MVR 2,000 a year;
   - pre-existing conditions covered;
   - repatriation of your body home, or burial in the Maldives, if you die there;
   - cashless claims at all government hospitals and health facilities, ADK Hospital and Medica Hospital; no co-payment for inpatient care at government centres;
   - premium at most MVR 800 a person.

   The registered insurers in 2021 were Amana Takaful, Dhivehi Insurance Company, Solarelle Insurance and Allied Insurance Company of the Maldives [W0075].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Are the MVR 100,000 minimum and MVR 800 premium cap still in force under Regulation 2025/R-120, and which insurers are registered in 2026?
   > - Do workers receive their own policy card or number?

4. **Make sure the employer applies for your work e-Visa within 15 days.** The employer must apply for and obtain your work e-Visa within 15 days of your arrival on the entry pass, through Xpat [W0003, W0029]. It needs your photo and a colour copy of your passport bio page [W0004]. Since 23 November 2025 visa stickers in passports are not valid; only an approved e-Visa counts, and you need it to leave and re-enter [W0001, W0002]. Ask the employer for a copy once it is approved (Step 5, item 3) [W0160]. If you get a new passport, make sure the visa shows the new passport number before you travel [W0007].

5. **Give your fingerprints if Xpat says you must.** A permit may not be extended until your biometrics are updated [W0161]. People whose permit was issued while they were already in the country (for example on another employer's permit) must enrol, because biometrics are not taken at the border for them [W0039]. In Greater Malé the employer books a slot at [xpatappointment.home.gov.mv](https://xpatappointment.home.gov.mv/), 2pm to 4pm on working days; elsewhere it arranges this with the island council office [W0037, W0038].

6. **Live where the employer houses you.** The employer must provide housing for the whole job, to the standard in the regulation, and gives its details in the permit application [W0174, W0171]. Housing complaints are in Your rights at work.

7. **Open a bank account in your own name.** Since 25 September 2026 your salary must be paid into an account in your own name at a bank licensed by the Maldives Monetary Authority (MMA) [W0033, W0147]. In an LRA wage case, salary counts as paid only if it went into that account [W0034].

   You'll need (Bank of Maldives, BML) [W0097, W0102, W0100]:
   - your passport, original shown for checking;
   - a work permit with at least one month left;
   - an "Employment Verification Letter" from your employer stating your permanent and present address, gross salary, length of service and work permit number [W0101].

   Apply for your first account online at [app.bankofmaldives.com.mv/join](https://app.bankofmaldives.com.mv/join/) [W0099]. The BML expatriate account in rufiyaa needs a minimum deposit of MVR 200 and costs MVR 75 a month [W0098]. Sending money home is in Sending money home.

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which banks do employers of Bangladeshi construction and resort workers use, and do employers open the accounts themselves?
   > - Does the employer keep the worker's bank card or PIN in practice?

8. **Get a SIM card.** Dhiraagu needs a valid passport or work permit from a foreigner [W0104]. Foreign workers may hold at most three mobile numbers with each operator [W0103].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does Ooredoo Maldives ask from a work-permit holder, and what does a basic prepaid SIM cost?

9. **Keep your passport.** Whether the employer may keep it, and what to do if it does, is in Your rights at work.

### How long you can stay

**As long as the fee is paid, one year at a time.** Your work permit lasts for the months your employer has paid at MVR 350 a month, paid at least one month ahead [W0024, W0027]. Your work e-Visa lasts as long as the permit, renewed for at most one year at a time, and it shows as invalid once the fee runs out [W0030, W0031, W0006]. If the fee goes unpaid you may move to another employer without consent, and after 6 months unpaid the ministry can cancel the permit and send you home [W0173]. The permit also lapses if your yearly medical or your insurance expires, and it cannot be extended until your biometrics are updated [W0154, W0044, W0161].

**The upper limit.** A non-professional or unskilled work permit can be issued for up to 10 years in total, up from 5 years before December 2025 [W0136, W0145].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a maximum total stay for professional-category work permits?
> - Can a worker who reaches 10 years return on a new permit after leaving?

**Jobs closing to foreigners.** Regulation 2025/R-120 cancels quotas for some jobs a set time after 14 December 2025 [W0047]:

- after 2 years: hairdressers, beauticians, personal trainers, electricians, English-speaking guest relations officers, tour guides, accountants and imams (babysitters were taken off this list on 17 December 2025) [W0139, W0175];
- after 3 years: divers, nurses, building inspectors, surveyors, site supervisors, pilots and photographers [W0140];
- after 4 years: electrical engineers and dive instructors [W0141];
- after 5 years: housekeeping, human resources and front office managers, and primary and secondary teachers [W0141].

**Time abroad.** A work permit is voided if you stay outside the Maldives for more than four months while it is valid [W0143]. If your permit expired while you were abroad, the employer can get a temporary 15-day approval with a medical done abroad, and you then need a Maldives medical within 15 days of return [W0063]. Going home on leave is in Going home on leave.

**Other ways a permit ends.** A permit can be cancelled for a change of employer, breaking the regulation, a court order, long absence, or missing or false documents [W0137]. It is voided if you are told to leave for health reasons and do not leave in time, and revoked if you do not cooperate with an investigation for 10 days [W0144, W0146]. What happens when the job ends is in Leaving at the end of the job.

> **Need to know**
> - After a work permit is cancelled, how many days does a worker have to leave or change employer before counting as overstaying?

## Work visa: professional (teachers, doctors, nurses, engineers)

A professional uses the same route as a company worker: the employer gets a quota, the work permit and the entry pass on Xpat, and then the work e-Visa (see Work visa: company worker (construction, resort, shop), Step 3) [W0018, W0003]. Xpat classes each occupation as Professional or Non-Professional: registered nurses, medical officers, primary school teachers and civil and electrical engineers are Professional, while carpenters, masons and waiters are Non-Professional [W0184, W0185, W0186, W0187, W0188]. A professional or managerial permit needs an MQA (Maldives Qualifications Authority) level 7 qualification in the field, or MQA level 5 with 5 years' relevant experience and an MQA recognition report [W0012]. On the MQA scale, level 7 is a bachelor's degree and level 5 a diploma [W0189, W0190]. The employer also uploads your certificates, with certified English translations, and any registration the occupation needs [W0016]. Quotas for nurses end three years after 14 December 2025, for electrical engineers four years after, and for primary and secondary teachers five years after (see How long you can stay) [W0140, W0141, W0047].

### Step 1: Get your certificates attested in Bangladesh

1. **Attest every certificate at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.** MQA recognition needs your degree, transcript or mark sheet, earlier qualifications, and SSC, HSC or school-leaving certificates, all attested by the foreign ministry of your country [W0106, W0107]. A provisional certificate is not accepted [W0108]. How to get Bangladeshi certificates attested is in Step 4: Passport, police clearance and certificates.

   **Scams:** the employer signs a declaration that the certificates are genuine and takes full responsibility if forged ones are submitted [W0112].

### Step 2: The employer gets your qualification recognised by MQA

1. **The employer applies to MQA, one form per certificate.** The form is the "Application Form for Qualification Recognition/Validation (for Foreigners)" [W0105].

   You'll need (the employer submits): the attested copies from Step 1, an English translation of any certificate not in English, your passport bio page, and the employer's company registration [W0106, W0107, W0192, W0193, W0191].

   1. The employer hands in the form at MQA, Velaanaage, 2nd Floor, Malé, between 9am and 12 noon on working days [W0110, W0111].
   2. It pays MVR 500 per validation and MVR 250 for each extra copy [W0109].
   3. MQA's phone is +960 3344077 and email info@mqa.gov.mv [W0111].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is the MQA foreigner form now submitted online, what does it cost in 2026, and how long does recognition take?

### Step 3: Register with your professional council

1. **Doctors: register with the Maldives Medical and Dental Council (MMDC).** The MMDC's published fees are MVR 500 for two years' temporary basic registration for a foreign doctor, MVR 500 for a licence valid two years, and MVR 3,000 for each attempt at its licensing exam [W0113, W0114, W0115].

   You'll need (as BOESL, the Bangladesh government's recruiting company, listed for Ministry of Health jobs): passport, your Bangladesh medical council registration, undergraduate and postgraduate degrees, internship certificate, certificate of good standing, experience certificates and the MMDC pre-registration form [W0123, W0124].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Must a Bangladeshi MBBS pass the MMDC licensing exam, and are all BMDC-recognised Bangladeshi medical colleges on the MMDC list?
   > - What are the MMDC fees in 2026 (the published table dates from 2015)?

2. **Nurses: register with the Maldives Nursing and Midwifery Council (MNMC).** Foreign nurses get a temporary registration and licence for two years, in line with their contract, and renew every two years [W0116, W0121].

   You'll need [W0117, W0118, W0119, W0122]:
   - two years' clinical experience within the last five years, with references that describe your clinical area and a hospital of at least 150 beds;
   - IELTS 5.5 or an equivalent English certificate;
   - police clearance;
   - copies notarised by the institution that issued the certificate, an embassy or consulate of the issuing country, or a government office of that country [W0120].

3. **Teachers: get a teaching licence.** Since July 2023 all local and foreign teachers must hold a licence from the Teacher Registration Board at the Ministry of Education [W0178, W0179].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How does a foreign teacher apply for the teaching licence (portal, documents, fee), and can the licence be obtained before arrival?

4. **Engineers.**
