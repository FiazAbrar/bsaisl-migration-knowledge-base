# Going abroad from Bangladesh: FAQ, dalal scams, departure bottlenecks and illegal routes

What people in Bangladesh who want to work abroad need to know across every country, as of November 2026: the questions they ask most, the tricks brokers use, where they get stuck or asked for bribes when leaving and returning, and how the irregular routes end. Each country's own doc, in the `countries` folder, gives the full steps for that country. A raised number such as 3 is an official source; N3 is a news, NGO (non-governmental organisation), research or guide source, listed under "Needs an official source"; a year, like N3 (2022), marks a source older than 2025. "Need to know" marks a point no source has yet confirmed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Deciding

#### Which countries can I go to for work right now?

As of November 2026:
- **Saudi Arabia:** open, and the largest destination, though departures from January to 17 October 2026 were 45% below the same period of 2025 [F0016, F0015].
- **Singapore:** open; BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared 66,657 workers for it from January to 7 November 2026, the second destination [F0016].
- **Qatar:** open, the third destination, with 53,611 cleared from January to 6 November 2026 [F0017].
- **The Maldives:** issuing new work visas; 32,408 cleared from January to 7 November 2026 [F0018, F0019].
- **Kuwait:** open for company jobs; closed to new Bangladeshi domestic workers, since Kuwait's June 2026 list of approved countries leaves out Bangladesh and the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0020, F0021, F0022, F0197].
- **Jordan:** open for garment factories and for women in domestic work, closed to new recruits in most other jobs since 1 June 2026 [F0023, F0175].
- **Italy:** only through an employer in Italy who names you under the yearly quota; about 10,000 Bangladeshis got work visas in 2025 [F0176, F0177].
- **The UAE:** work and visit visas for Bangladeshis have been restricted since July 2024, with no published ban [F0178, F0179].
- **Malaysia:** a restart was announced on 15 October 2026, but the medical tests it needs had not been approved by 4 November 2026 [F0180, F0181].
- **Oman:** new visas for Bangladeshis suspended since 31 October 2023, apart from a few categories [F0182, F0183, F0184].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** BMET cleared only 11 workers from January to 9 November 2026 [F0185].
- **Cambodia:** BMET cleared none in October 2026, and the Ministry told BMET not to clear workers for 12 countries, Cambodia among them, without its own approval [F0186, F0187].

Each country doc opens with its current status under "Is it open now, and who can go?" (for example, see the Qatar doc, Background, Status).

#### Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?

On 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET not to issue clearances for 12 countries without the Ministry's recruitment permission: Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [F0187]. The State Minister has named Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya as high-risk countries where visas must be strictly checked [F0012]. In Cambodia, a study by BRAC (the Bangladeshi development organisation) found 98% of returnees got no job and 90% were forced into online scams [F0189].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the list's Congo actually Kosovo, and is Cambodia on it or Croatia, as other reports of the same 4 November 2026 order give [F0188, F0187]?

#### Which countries can a woman go to?

- **Saudi Arabia:** as a domestic worker, aged 25 to 45, and she should pay nothing (see Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?) [F0190, F0191].
- **Jordan:** garment factories, through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government's recruiting agency); 12,784 women went from January to 8 November 2026, the second destination for women after Saudi Arabia [F0192, F0193].
- **Qatar:** domestic work, with the same 25 to 45 age rule for BMET clearance; in August 2025 BMET let one agency send 100 women housemaids to a Qatar employer [F0195, F0233].
- **Kuwait:** company jobs only (230 women from January to 8 November 2026); the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0196, F0197].
- **The Maldives:** women can go, but few do (148 in 2026 to 7 November) [F0198].
- **Singapore:** almost none: 126 women of 70,832 workers cleared in 2025 [F0199].
- **Oman:** 3 women cleared in 2026 to 8 November, after 6,636 in 2023 [F0200, F0201].

> **Need to know**
> - Are UAE recruitment offices getting domestic-worker entry permits for Bangladeshi women in November 2026, or are they refused at the visa stage?

#### Is there a job for someone with no skills?

Saudi Arabia's skill certificate is not required of domestic workers, though women going to the Middle East for domestic work must take the housekeeping course [F0204, F0094]. Saudi Arabia's skill test, the SVP (Skills Verification Program, run by Takamol), is compulsory for listed trades, and the list includes street, hospital and office cleaners, loading and unloading workers and construction workers [F0202, F0203]. Qatar requires a training and test certificate only for professions its labour ministry lists [F0205]. A Singapore construction job needs a skills test at one of five centres in Bangladesh approved by Singapore's Building and Construction Authority, until the test moves to Singapore in January 2028 [F0024, F0025]. BMET's TTCs (Technical Training Centres) teach 55 trades, and project-funded courses are free and pay a monthly stipend [F0026].

#### How much does it really cost to go?

People pay several times the legal maximum (see What is the most an agency may charge me?): workers who went to five Gulf countries on a "free visa" (a visa bought from a sponsor with no real job behind it) spent Tk 4,99,802 on average, against a government-set average of Tk 1,16,224 [F0356]. By country:
- **Saudi Arabia:** about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [F0027].
- **Qatar:** Tk 4 to 7 lakh for a "free visa" [F0028].
- **Malaysia:** Tk 5.44 lakh on average in 2022-24 [F0029].
- **Singapore:** first-time workers are asked for S$9,000 to 13,000, about Tk 8.7 to 12.6 lakh, and some pay up to Tk 16 lakh [F0030, F0357, F0031].
- **Kuwait:** a visa is sold through brokers for KD 2,000 to 2,200, about Tk 8 to 9 lakh [F0032].
- **The UAE:** at least Tk 3.5 lakh for driving jobs, the State Minister said in May 2026 [F0033].
- **The Maldives:** about Tk 3 to 4 lakh in 2024 [F0034].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person, by the CID's (Criminal Investigation Department's) 2024 estimate [F0035].
- **Italy:** one worker paid an intermediary EUR 20,000 [F0036].
- **Jordan garment jobs through BOESL:** the factory pays; the worker pays Tk 200 for fingerprints and Tk 200 for the orientation, and the company repays these before departure [F0206].

#### What is the most an agency may charge me?

The government fixes the maximum by order [F0207]. If BMET has issued a permission letter for your job, the most is the amount written on it (see What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?) [F0208]. Otherwise the caps reported for each country, with the year each was set or reported, are:
- Saudi Arabia: Tk 1,65,000, set in 2016 [F0209].
- Singapore: Tk 2,62,270, as reported by Bangladesh Bank in 2019 [F0210].
- The UAE: Tk 1,07,780, set in 2017 [F0211].
- Kuwait: Tk 1,06,780, as stated by the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait in August 2025 [F0212].
- Malaysia: Tk 78,990, the cap for the 2022-24 recruitment [F0029].
- Italy: none; the 2017 caps don't cover it [F0213].

A woman going as a domestic worker may be charged nothing [F0191].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Ministry issued the new country-by-country maximums the State Minister promised in October 2026 [F0214]?

#### Which fees do I pay to the government?

The main Bangladesh-side fees (the full list for Saudi Arabia is in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Who pays what):
- OEP (Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's website) registration: Tk 200 [F0215].
- A 48-page, 5-year e-passport: Tk 4,025 (regular) [F0217].
- Police clearance: Tk 1,500 on the portal [F0218].
- Welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [F0219].
- Migrant insurance: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card (clearance) fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax at clearance: Tk 250 to 1,200, depending on the visa [F0222].

#### How much will I earn?

- **Saudi Arabia:** cleaners and general workers earn SR 800 to 1,000 a month; there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0223, F0224].
- **Qatar:** the legal minimum is QR 1,000 a month, plus QR 500 for housing and QR 300 for food if the employer doesn't provide them [F0225].
- **Malaysia:** the minimum wage is RM1,700 a month [F0226].
- **Singapore:** construction basic pay averages S$820 a month for Bangladeshis [F0227].
- **Kuwait:** the Bangladesh Embassy attests a government-contract cleaner's or labourer's visa only if it pays at least KD 90 a month [F0358, F0228].
- **Jordan garment factories:** JD 230 a month, of which at least JD 130 is cash and JD 100 is paid in kind as housing and food [F0229].
- **The Maldives:** migrant workers surveyed in 2024 averaged MVR 6,760 a month, and there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0230, F0231].
- **Oman:** a minimum of RO 90 a month was agreed between the two governments but is not rigorously enforced [F0232].

#### How long will it take to pay back what I spend?

In 2020 men took 19 months or more on average to recover what they spent [F0359]. A Saudi worker on SR 800 with free housing who buys his own food (SR 500 or more a month) and phone (SR 50 or more) keeps at most SR 250, about Tk 8,300 at Tk 33.20 a riyal [F0223, F0037]; at that rate a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes about 54 months to repay before interest, against a usual two-year contract [F0038]. Many low-skilled Gulf workers struggle to send even Tk 15,000 to 20,000 a month; at that rate the same debt takes 23 to 30 months [F0039]. Malaysia workers who paid high fees stayed in debt for two to three years (2024) [F0040].

#### Who pays for the air ticket?

It depends on the job. On Saudi jobs with a BMET permission letter, the letter can put the airfare on the employer, as Bonanza Overseas' September 2025 letter does [F0360]. On BOESL's Jordan garment jobs the factory bears all the costs [F0206]. On a visa a relative sends from Qatar, the worker usually buys the ticket through the Dhaka broker [F0041].

#### Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?

BOESL has sent 1.43 lakh workers in 14 years, 1.06 lakh of them to Jordan, where it sends women to garment factories at no cost [F0042, F0193]. In November 2026 it advertised 112 zero-cost jobs for skilled men at DHL in Malaysia, with applications on OEP [F0043]. Recruitment under the October 2025 Saudi agreement goes through private agencies, not BOESL [F0044], and BOESL lists no Qatar recruitment [F0045].

### Agencies and dalals

#### How do I check whether an agency is real?

1. Ask for the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and find it on OEP's agency list at www.oep.gov.bd/agencies, which shows its name, address, office phone, licence status and validity date [F0046].
2. Read the status, not the date: a licence can show "Cancelled" while its validity date is years ahead [F0047]. Recruiting after cancellation is a crime carrying 1 to 2 years' prison [F0048].
3. Check the job too, because licensed agencies also cheat: workers paid agencies Tk 3 to 4.2 lakh for Qatar jobs and three of them got fake contract letters [F0049, F0050, F0051].

The most active agencies, with their cited red flags, are in the knowledge base's Recruiting agencies doc; the steps for one country are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1.

#### Is it legal to go through a dalal?

A dalal (দালাল, broker) is legal only if a licensed agency has registered and appointed him as its sub-agent [F0052, F0053]. Acting as a sub-agent without registration carries 1 year's prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh, and the agency that uses one risks 1 year or Tk 1 to 5 lakh [F0054, F0055]. Most people still go through one: 56% of 1,084 Gulf migrants in a 2025 OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) survey relied on informal or illegal agents [F0056, F0057], and in the Maldives 59% went through a sub-agent or dalal [F0058]. The broker is often a friend or relative [F0059].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a public list of registered sub-agents that a worker can check a dalal's name against [F0053]?

#### What is a demand letter?

A demand letter is the employer's written request for workers; for Qatar, the company or its agent files it at the Bangladesh Embassy with the government's visa approval, and the Embassy attests it [F0060]. The Bangladesh mission checks the employer and attests the job demand and visa before the agency selects workers [F0061]. Ask the agency to show you the attested copy (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4) [F0060].

#### What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?

It is BMET's approval for an agency to send a set number of workers to a named employer; it shows the job, the salary, the contract terms, who pays the migration cost, and the most the agency may take [F0233, F0234, F0208]. It also makes the agency promise, on stamp paper, to pay the worker's food, lodging, return and compensation if the job is not as promised [F0235]. BMET posts these letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, but the newest one there is dated 10 December 2025 [F0236]. A single visa (up to eight workers for one employer on one demand letter) needs no Ministry recruitment permission, so it has no such letter [F0237].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET still issue permission letters after 10 December 2025, and can a worker get a copy from BMET [F0236]?

#### A relative abroad is sending me a visa. Is that safe?

Many are bought "free visas", sold on by a sponsor who will not employ the worker (see "A free visa lets me work for anyone") [F0361]. From Cumilla and other districts about 80% go on visas from relatives and friends [F0238]. Relatives working abroad often get the visa by paying the employer or others and pass it on to people at home [F0239]. A bought visa also shows as genuine on Qatar's visa checker, so check that the company on the visa is the one you will work for and that the job matches [F0240]. Buying or selling a visa inside Bangladesh carries 2 to 7 years' prison [F0241]. What a relative may be sending is explained in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 5.

#### How should I pay the agency, and when?

Pay through the bank into the agency's own account and keep every receipt [F0242]. BMET asks people not to go to middlemen and to pay only through the proper channel against a receipt [F0243]. BMET's September 2025 permission letter for Saudi kitchen workers, for example, let the agency take its fee only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and only after the visa, clearance and flight were confirmed [F0208]. In practice most workers pay sub-agents in instalments before the visa is confirmed and get the contract only at the airport [F0244].

#### Is there a list of good agencies?

One agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment: Bonanza Overseas (RL-0579) was the Bangladeshi agency in a fair-recruitment pilot that the ILO (International Labour Organization) began in 2018 with QDVC, a construction company in Qatar [F0245]. The Recruiting agencies doc lists the most active agencies with their red flags.

#### The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?

1. Write down who took the money, from whom, when and how much; a Ministry joint secretary said in 2025 that action follows when these are given [F0246].
2. Apply to BMET's Director General with your proof at the workers' complaint cell; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [F0247].
3. Or send an application with proof, your address and the agency's RL number to the Ministry's enforcement branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd; action starts within 3 to 5 working days [F0062, F0063].
4. Mobile courts give immediate remedies to people about to go and returned migrants, free, within 7 working days [F0064].
5. If nothing happens, go up BMET's grievance ladder to the Ministry's Secretary and then the Cabinet Division's system at www.grs.gov.bd [F0065].

The full route is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Scams, If the agency cheats you.

#### Is cheating a migrant a crime?

Yes. Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, keeping a migrant's passport or papers, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay carries up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [F0066, F0067]. Publishing an overseas job advert without government or BMET approval carries up to 1 year [F0068]. Migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [F0069]; OKUP gives free legal advice and help filing cases on 01842773300 [F0070].

### Papers

#### How do I get a passport, and what does it cost?

Apply for an e-passport exactly as your NID (national ID card) reads; anyone over 20 must use the NID [F0071]. A 48-page, 5-year e-passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express) [F0217]. Pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay, or at a bank [F0072], and bring the original NID to the passport office [F0073]. Delivery after biometrics is within 15 working days (regular), 7 (express) or 2 (super express) [F0074].

#### How much validity must my passport have?

It depends on the step: the police clearance needs at least 3 months [F0218], and the Qatar Visa Center (QVC, where Qatar-bound workers sign the contract, give fingerprints and take the medical) books only passports with more than 195 days left [F0367, F0075]. Immigration's hotline gets calls from people asking whether four months is enough to travel [F0002]. Since 19 August 2026 the Special Branch runs a 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport, 01320007588, for questions on passport validity and travel bans [F0001].

#### How do I get a police clearance certificate?

Apply at pcc.police.gov.bd: register, fill in your details and addresses, upload the scans and submit [F0218, F0076]. Pay Tk 1,500 by treasury challan at Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank, or online by card [F0218]. The police verify you at your present address; track it by texting "PCC S <reference number>" to 26969, and collect it with a copy of your passport [F0077]. The PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825 [F0077]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the fee Tk 1,500 (the portal) or Tk 500 (Bangladesh Police's guidance) [F0218, F0078]?

#### What is attestation, what is an apostille, and which do I need?

An apostille is a single certificate from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) that member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention accept; it is applied for on mygov.bd at Tk 200 a page and takes 5 working days [F0079, F0080]. MoFA lists 114 countries that accept it, including Oman, Saudi Arabia and Italy, but not Qatar [F0081, F0082]. For a country that doesn't accept it, each document is first verified by its issuing body and then attested by MoFA: SSC and HSC (Secondary School Certificate and Higher Secondary Certificate) certificates by the Education Board, then the Ministry of Education, then MoFA [F0083, F0084, F0085]. The Qatar steps, including legalisation by the Qatari mission, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2, item 3; the apostille steps in the Italy doc, Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 4.

#### How do I register on OEP?

Registration on OEP is the first step: agencies must pick workers from BMET's database, and BMET clears no one outside it [F0248, F0249]. Open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp in Google Chrome, type your details exactly as on the NID, enter the code sent to your mobile, fill in the form, and pay by bKash, Nagad or another mobile wallet [F0250, F0251]. The fee is Tk 200 [F0215]. Download your Registration Card at the end [F0250]. If it fails, call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [F0252].

#### What is the medical, and how do I book it?

For the Gulf countries it is the Wafid check, booked online and done at a medical centre assigned by your city; people still call it "GAMCA", after the system Wafid replaced [F0253, F0254]. Book the slip yourself at wafid.com: USD 10 for a centre assigned to you, or USD 25 to choose the centre and date [F0255]. The whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 [F0256], yet most workers pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 [F0257]. Check the result on wafid.com's status search; it can take up to 26 days [F0258, F0259]. Qatar-bound workers take their medical at the QVC instead (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4); the Wafid steps are in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

#### Can I pay to be passed "fit" in the medical?

The Daily Star reported in July 2025 that agencies offer "fit" packages, Tk 20,000 against a Tk 10,000 standard one [F0260], and that a syndicate reportedly charges Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed fit certificate [F0261]. A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [F0262]. The same report said the approved centres are allegedly controlled by a few owners who issue fake certificates [F0263].

#### What if I'm declared unfit?

You can't retake the Wafid test for three months [F0086]. If you think the result is wrong or the centre demanded extra money, complain in writing to the Gulf Health Council within two weeks of the test; anonymous complaints are refused [F0087, F0088]. Overcharging can also be reported to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's enforcement branch with your receipts and the centre's name and address (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4) [F0089].

#### Do I need a skill test?

- **Saudi Arabia:** the SVP is compulsory for listed trades; it costs USD 85 per attempt, runs at 27 TTCs and BRTC Gazipur (the Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation's training institute), and a score of 50% passes [F0090, F0091, F0092, F0093]. Steps: the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 3.
- **Singapore construction:** a skills test at a Bangladesh test centre until January 2028, when workers will be tested in Singapore after arrival [F0025].
- **Qatar:** only for professions on the labour ministry's list [F0205].
- **Domestic workers:** no Saudi skill test, but women must take the housekeeping course (see What is the housekeeping course?) [F0204, F0094].

There is no way to pass the Saudi test by paying extra, and a worker caught paying has the account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [F0095, F0096].

#### How do I join a TTC course?

Log in on OEP, open Training portal, then Training, then Trade/Skills, choose your division, district and centre, pick the trade and press Apply [F0097]. Pay the BMET fee for short courses against a money receipt; project-run courses are free and pay a stipend, and courses last from 3 days to 4 years [F0026]. If anyone offers to get you into or through a TTC for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0098].

#### What is the pre-departure orientation (PDO)?

It is a compulsory 3-day course at one of 70 training institutes, booked on OEP; the institute sets the fee and gives a money receipt [F0094, F0099]. Booked through the Ami Probashi phone app, the enrolment fee is Tk 100 plus a Tk 100 platform charge [F0216].

#### How do I check my visa is real?

Take copies of the visa and the passport's visa page to BMET in Dhaka or your district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [F0362]; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [F0101]. BMET checks Saudi, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and UAE work visas online, and has a visa-check app on Google Play [F0101]. The State Minister said in 2026 that BMET can verify visas online for only four or five countries [F0011]. Each country doc gives its own online check (for example, see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7).

### BMET clearance and the smart card

#### What is the smart card, and do I need it?

The smart card is BMET's emigration clearance, and welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check it before departure [F0102]. It also makes you a member of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB, the Ministry's welfare body for migrants), whose members get help when sick, easier loans from Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), scholarships for their children and Tk 3 lakh for the heirs if they die [F0103, F0355]. It gives you the migrant insurance too [F0220]. Families of workers without clearance or membership don't get the Welfare Board's death grant [F0104].

#### How do I get BMET clearance?

- **Through an agency:** the agency applies after the visa, with the visa copy, the attested contract, your registration card and the fees; BMET clears within 3 working days [F0105, F0194].
- **On a visa you got yourself:** apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office with the visa copy, the contract if any, your registration card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and the fees; 3 working days [F0106].

Since July 2023 the application can also be made online through the Ami Probashi phone app, and the card downloaded, without going to BMET [F0365]. The full list of papers is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 6, item 3.

#### How much does clearance cost?

- Welfare fee: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026, up from Tk 3,500 [F0219].
- Insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax: Tk 400 on a single visa not attested by the embassy, Tk 250 if attested, and Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [F0222]; Tk 500 on a Saudi single visa [F0107].

#### How long does clearance take?

BMET's charter promises clearance within 3 working days of a complete application, whether the agency applies or you do [F0105, F0106].

#### How do I check that my smart card is real?

Check it at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [F0108]. Airport welfare desks checked 1,391,988 cards in 2023-24 and stopped 1,098 travellers with no or a fake clearance [F0109]. If something is wrong, contact BMET's Director (Emigration), 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [F0110].

#### Do I have to pay a bribe to get clearance?

BMET's charter lists only the fees above, and BMET asks anyone offered clearance for money to tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0222, F0098]. The State Minister said in 2026 that some dishonest BMET officials give clearance on fake papers [F0013]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of passengers paying police at the airport to get through are true [F0010].

#### I went abroad without clearance. Can I still join the Welfare Board?

Yes. Apply online at portal.wewb.gov.bd; the application is processed through the Bangladesh embassy or high commission [F0111]. Members get a card with a QR code and the Board's benefits [F0112]. The steps for Qatar, including the fee, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7, Welfare Board sign-up if you left without BMET clearance.

### Loans

#### Can I get a government loan to go abroad?

PKB, the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days of complete papers [F0113]. Women migrants have a separate loan on the same terms [F0114]. You must be 18 to 60, not in default, and live permanently in the branch's area with your guarantors [F0115]. You need two guarantors, one financially solvent and one family member [F0116]. Helpline: 16238 [F0117].

#### When can I apply for the PKB loan?

Only once you hold the visa and the BMET smart card, near the end of the process [F0118]. The bank doesn't arrange visas, and finding guarantors stops many people from borrowing [F0119, F0120]. You can apply at the branch or online on mygov.bd, paying nothing when you submit; processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each come after the decision [F0121, F0122].

#### Should I borrow from a moneylender?

In a 2020 government survey, 81% of men had borrowed to go abroad [F0123]. In a 2020 IOM (International Organization for Migration) survey, most family loans were interest-free, NGO and bank loans carried 10-15%, and 62% of moneylender debt carried 50-150% interest [F0124]. A microcredit NGO may charge at most 24% a year [F0125]. A quarter of borrowers risk losing the collateral they pledged if they can't repay [F0264], and families mortgage land and sell cattle: a Natore farmer mortgaged his land and sold four cows, then could not fly [F0265]. A former expatriates' welfare secretary said what workers send home often doesn't even cover the interest [F0014].

#### Can I borrow abroad?

In Singapore, a worker found borrowing from an unlicensed moneylender has his Work Permit revoked and is sent home [F0266]. Many workers in Malaysia borrow at 10% a month to renew their permits, an economist of the CPD (Centre for Policy Dialogue, a Dhaka think tank) said in October 2025 [F0267].

### At the airport

#### What should I carry?

Before you go, check you have [F0268]:
- a valid passport;
- the work visa;
- the medical certificate;
- the demand letter;
- the contract attested by the destination country's embassy;
- the agency's name, address and phone;
- the BMET smart card;
- the air ticket.

Keep one set of copies at home and at least three with you [F0269]. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know: drug offences abroad can carry the death penalty [F0270].

#### What does immigration ask?

Immigration police ask about your purpose and family, and they compare your visa, BMET clearance, papers and answers [F0271, F0004]. A woman with BMET clearance who said she was joining her husband in Oman was questioned closely, because Oman's general worker visas and women's domestic-worker recruitment are closed, and was stopped when the same spouse-visa approval turned out to have sent five women to Oman [F0008]. The Special Branch's 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport is 01320007588 [F0001].

#### Why do people get offloaded?

Immigration police can stop a traveller they doubt from flying and stamp "offload" in the passport [F0271]. The usual reasons are a visa that doesn't match the BMET clearance, fake papers, a breach of visa conditions, or answers that don't match the papers [F0004]. The Special Branch turned back 81,500 passengers from 2021 to June 2026, 7,819 of them in the first half of 2026 [F0005, F0006]. People with valid papers have been stopped too: a man with a valid Malaysia visa and BMET clearance was turned back and then let through after a senior officer intervened [F0009]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of people stopped unfairly are true [F0010].

#### Who helps me at the airport?

WEWB's welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards, help returnees with customs and lost luggage, send the sick to hospital by ambulance and take complaints from abused or cheated returnees [F0102, F0272, F0273]. The Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport gives departing and returning workers a short stay; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd [F0274].

### In the destination

#### My salary isn't paid. What do I do?

The routes:
- **Qatar:** complain to the labour ministry, which must first try to settle the dispute; a Qatari official advised complaining within a month, because 3 to 4 months' unpaid wages become hard to recover [F0275, F0276]. An unpaid worker may ask to change employer or end the contract, with the journey home at the employer's cost [F0277].
- **Saudi Arabia:** file a friendly-settlement claim with the labour ministry within 12 months; unsettled claims go to the labour court [F0363, F0364]. After 3 months unpaid you may move to another employer without consent [F0279].
- **Every country:** your family can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-1 branch in Dhaka with proof of your status, the employer's name and address and the amounts owed; it is free and the Ministry writes to the labour wing within 7 working days [F0280].

The steps for each country are under "If you aren't paid" in its doc (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, If something goes wrong, If you aren't paid, are abused or your passport is taken).

#### My employer has taken my passport. Is that allowed?

No, though it is common:
- **Qatar:** the employer must return it once the residence permit is done, unless you ask in writing for the employer to keep it, and hand it over whenever you ask; keeping it is punishable by a fine of up to QR 25,000 [F0281, F0282].
- **Saudi Arabia:** your passport and iqama (residence card) may never be withheld [F0283].
- **The UAE:** passport confiscation is banned, and you need no employer permission to leave the country [F0284].
- **Kuwait:** keeping it is illegal but widespread; complain to the PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) office where your residence was stamped [F0285, F0286].
- **Singapore:** employers should not keep workers' passports, yet over 31% of Work Permit holders didn't hold their own in 2024 [F0287, F0288].

In Bangladesh, withholding a migrant's passport without lawful reason carries up to 5 years' prison [F0066, F0067].

#### Can I change my employer?

- **Qatar:** since 2020 the law lets you move without the employer's consent, under the labour ministry's rules [F0289].
- **Saudi Arabia:** after 12 months, with 90 days' notice, if your contract is on Qiwa (the labour ministry's platform); sooner if you go 3 months unpaid or your work permit or visa expires [F0290, F0279].
- **The UAE:** transfers between companies have been restricted for Bangladeshis since July 2024 [F0291].
- **Kuwait:** normally after one year and with the employer's consent [F0292].
- **Malaysia:** the PLKS (Temporary Employment Visit Pass, the work pass) doesn't let you change employer or sector [F0293].
- **Singapore:** you may work only for the employer and in the job on your Work Permit [F0294].
- **Jordan garment factories:** only to another garment factory [F0295].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** the permit can't be moved to another employer [F0296].
- **Italy:** losing or leaving the job doesn't cancel your permit [F0297].

In Saudi Arabia you may not be employed in a profession other than the one on your work permit [F0298].

#### I arrived and there is no job, or the visa was fake. What now?

This is the most common failure of a bought visa: in OKUP's 2025 survey, 21% of free-visa workers paid about Tk 1.5 lakh more after arriving to get a work permit, and 14% never got one and stayed on illegally [F0299, F0300]. BMET says 90% of migrants' complaints in 2025 concerned Saudi Arabia, mostly no iqama, no work or unpaid wages [F0301]. The embassy can advise and help in a labour dispute (in Qatar, for example) [F0302], and your family can complain about the agency in Bangladesh (see The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?) [F0247]. If you can't get home, a relative can ask the Ministry for help to bring you back [F0126].

#### What happens if I overstay?

- **Saudi Arabia:** without a valid iqama you are undocumented, and police detain and deport undocumented workers who go out [F0127, F0366]; overstaying a visit or Umrah visa brings a 3 to 5 year ban [F0128].
- **The UAE:** AED 50 a day after the grace period that follows the end of a residence permit [F0129].
- **Qatar:** overstaying a visit visa costs QR 200 a day, up to QR 12,000 [F0130].
- **Cambodia:** USD 10 a day, and deportation can take more than six months [F0131].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** a fine, and an expulsion brings a 2-year entry ban [F0132, F0133].

Countries sometimes open grace periods: Qatar gave three months from 9 February 2025 for overstayers to leave [F0134]. How to get home is under "Going home without valid papers" in each country doc.

#### How do I send money home safely?

Use a bank, a licensed exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, not hundi (unlicensed money agents) [F0135]. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with another 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps it [F0136, F0137]. Sending more at once costs less: in April to June 2026 the average cost was 2.94% for up to USD 200 and 0.50% above USD 500 [F0138]. For bKash, give the partner branch the receiver's bKash number and full registered name [F0139]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [F0140]. The channels for each country are in its doc, under Sending money home.

#### Is hundi illegal?

Yes, in Bangladesh sending money outside banking channels is a punishable offence [F0141]. Abroad it can cost you the job: in Singapore a worker involved in hundi has his permit revoked [F0142].

#### Who can I call for help while abroad?

- Probash-Bondhu, WEWB's 24-hour call centre: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [F0143].
- The Ministry's Complaint Resolution Cell: minister.expat@gmail.com [F0144].
- The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in your country (for example, in Saudi Arabia, 8001000125 free) [F0145].

The labour wing's numbers are under Contacts in each country doc.

### Women

#### Can a woman go abroad for work without her guardian's permission?

For domestic work, no: BMET clears a woman domestic worker only with proof that she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0190]. A woman going on a single visa also needs her guardian's no-objection letter on stamp paper [F0146]. The housekeeping course asks for the same letter [F0303].

#### Who can go as a domestic worker?

A woman aged 25 to 45 when she enrols in the housekeeping course (returnees up to 50), who has passed class 5 (which can be relaxed) and is fit [F0304, F0305]. She can't enrol if she is pregnant, if her youngest child is under 6, or if she has a contagious or incurable disease [F0306].

#### What is the housekeeping course?

It is compulsory for women going to the Middle East for domestic work: one month (24 working days), residential, at one of 41 TTCs [F0094, F0307, F0308]. Apply on OEP's training portal and pay the set fee [F0309]. You must attend 90% of sessions and score 70% in the final test, and the course teaches how to contact the police and the Bangladesh embassy abroad [F0308, F0310]. The certificate is valid two years for a first departure [F0311]. Brokers have sold course certificates for money [F0312].

#### Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?

No. An agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from her [F0191], and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker [F0313]. The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi worker [F0314]. Yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [F0315].

#### Which agencies may send women domestic workers?

Only agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved, each with a Tk 15 lakh security deposit and a woman on the staff; the September 2025 order approved 21 [F0316]. Ask for the approval letter and check the agency's name and RL number are in it (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Step 2) [F0316].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Ministry publish one current list of all agencies approved to send women domestic workers?

#### The agent wants to change my age on the passport. Is that a problem?

Yes: agents have raised girls' ages with a second birth certificate, in one case giving a girl under 13 a passport showing 27 [F0317, F0318]. Check the birth record your passport will use at everify.bdris.gov.bd with the 17-digit registration number and your real date of birth [F0319]. The Probashi call centre, 16135 (free from Bangladesh), gives workers and families advice and help [F0320].

#### What are a domestic worker's rights?

Saudi Arabia's rules are an example:
- at most 10 hours' work a day, with at least 8 hours' continuous rest [F0321];
- one paid day off a week [F0147];
- the employer may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [F0148];
- the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy [F0149].

The rules for other countries are in each country doc's domestic-worker section (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Your rights at work).

#### What if I'm abused? Where can I go?

In Saudi Arabia, the embassy labour wing's free line is 8001000125, and the labour wings run safe homes for women in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [F0145, F0150]. The labour ministry's abuse line, 1919, takes reports round the clock in confidence [F0151]. A relative in Bangladesh can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) for help or to bring you home; it is free and the Ministry acts within 7 working days [F0152, F0153]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash at the airport welfare desk [F0102].

#### Can women get garment jobs in Jordan?

Yes, through BOESL: its November 2026 notice took women aged 20 to 35 who can run plain and overlock sewing machines, at no cost to the worker [F0322, F0206]. In June 2026 the CID arrested three people who used a Jordan garment company's name and seal to take Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person without a licence [F0323]. Steps: see the Jordan doc, Work visa: garment and factory worker.

### Problems and coming home

#### What does the migrant insurance cover?

Workers who left with BMET clearance from 1 January 2023 are insured for Tk 10 lakh by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC), for workers aged 18 to 55, for a one-time Tk 1,000 [F0324]. The cover lasts 5 years from the smart card's date [F0220]. It pays the full Tk 10 lakh on death or total permanent disability, 10% to 90% for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going [F0325]. Job loss is not covered if you went entirely at the employer's cost [F0326]. WEWB helps the worker or family claim [F0327].

#### If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?

1. **At the airport:** a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial for every body that arrives, free, collected within 3 months of the body's arrival [F0328].
2. **From the Welfare Board:** a Tk 3 lakh grant, if the worker had BMET clearance or membership and died in the country the clearance was for [F0104, F0329]. If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the body was buried abroad, apply within 1 year [F0330].
3. **From the insurance:** Tk 10 lakh [F0324].
4. **From the employer:** labour wings recover death compensation, wages and benefits, and the Board pays the heirs; 1,852 cases brought in Tk 127.6 crore in 2023-24 [F0331].

The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents [F0332]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job.

#### How is the body brought home?

The family applies to the Welfare Board, which takes their wish (body home or burial abroad) to the embassy and follows the case, free [F0333, F0334]. The Ministry also brings home bodies of irregular, undocumented and destitute workers: the next of kin applies with the cause of death, proof of status and the death certificate; free, 7 working days [F0335]. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports is available on request [F0336].

#### Why do so many families get no compensation from the employer?

Many deaths are recorded as "natural causes": 71% of deaths of Bangladeshis in Qatar from November 2016 to October 2020, by Amnesty International's reading of Bangladeshi government records [F0337]. Amnesty found in 2021 that without investigation or post-mortem, this rules out compensation from employers [F0338]. The Welfare Board's grant is paid anyway [F0104].

#### What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?

The Welfare Board asks the embassy to bring a sick or injured worker home on the worker's or family's application [F0339]. Back home, workers with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment, applying within 1 year of return [F0340, F0341]. The insurance pays for permanent disability (see What does the migrant insurance cover?) [F0342].

#### What help is there when I come back?

Airport welfare desks give food and water to returnees from jail, help with customs and lost luggage, and take complaints [F0272, F0273]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [F0102]. PKB's rehabilitation loan lends returnees up to Tk 50 lakh for a project at 9% simple interest, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral [F0343]. It needs a trade licence and a guarantor, and in 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees got PKB's special reintegration loan [F0344, F0345].

#### What can I bring home without paying duty?

Under the Baggage Rules 2025 a returning worker aged 12 or over may bring up to 65 kg free, plus 35 kg more of clothes and personal items [F0346, F0347]. One of each of a list of goods is duty-free, including a laptop, an oven, a rice cooker, a fan and a TV up to 29 inches [F0348]. You may bring 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one, the new one once a year [F0349]. Bigger items such as a fridge or air conditioner pay a fixed duty [F0350]. A Bangladeshi passport holder may bring no alcohol [F0351]. The full list is in the Qatar doc, Coming home, What you can bring home.

#### How much gold and cash can I bring?

Up to 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery in all, declared, once a year, with no more than 12 pieces of one type, is duty-free [F0352]. One gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) is allowed once a year if declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; anything more, or a hidden bar, is confiscated [F0353, F0354]. Up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency needs no declaration [F0154].

> **Need to know**
> - Do customs officers still apply USD 5,000, the figure on Bangladesh Customs' own page [F0155]?

#### Is there help for my children?

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC level) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [F0156].

### Myths

#### "A free visa lets me work for anyone."

Brokers sell it as "go and work wherever you like," for Tk 5 to 7 lakh [F0157]. In fact a Saudi employer may not employ a worker in a profession other than the one on the work permit [F0298], and buying and working on a free visa is illegal and punishable in Qatar [F0158]. The company named can cancel the visa at any time, making the worker illegal [F0159]. Of 10 men one Cumilla broker sent to Saudi Arabia, three got a proper job with a contract (2024) [F0160].

#### "A visit visa can be changed to a work visa there."

- **Saudi Arabia:** a visit visa can't be converted to an iqama, and offers to convert one for a fee are fraud [F0161].
- **Qatar:** you must leave and re-enter on a work visa [F0162].
- **Oman:** conversion inside the country stopped for all nationalities on 31 October 2023 [F0182].
- **The UAE:** a visit visa gives no right to work, and working on one brings fines [F0163].

Working on a tourist or Umrah visa in Saudi Arabia brings a ban of 3 to 10 years [F0128].

#### "The agency guarantees the job."

The Bangladesh High Commission in the Maldives warned in October 2026 against paying anyone who promises a guaranteed job or a quick work permit [F0164]. A Bangladeshi construction worker in the UAE told a 2019 study that the contract signed in Bangladesh differs from the one the company keeps [F0165]. In Cambodia, 98% of returnees BRAC studied found no job [F0189].

#### "Pay a little extra and the medical will be fit."

A bought "fit" result can still get you deported: a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and sent home [F0262].

#### "Pay extra and you'll pass the skill test."

There is no way to pass Saudi Arabia's SVP for extra money; a worker caught paying has the account locked [F0095, F0096].

#### "Saudi Arabia abolished the kafala."

The kafala (sponsorship system) still stands: the October 2025 story began in an article by a UK think tank and cited no Saudi source [F0166].

#### "The iqama is the worker's cost."

The employer pays the iqama, the work permit and their renewals [F0167]. A Bangladesh embassy official confirmed the employer should pay, though employers push it onto workers [F0168].

#### "If I have a job, my visa is safe."

In the UAE an employer can cancel a visa online without the worker knowing [F0169]. The UAE began cancelling many Bangladeshis' visas in July 2026, and on 13 October 2026 the Law Minister said 5,000 had been cancelled [F0170, F0171].

#### "Malaysia has opened again."

Recruitment was announced on 15 October 2026, but on 4 November 2026 the minister said the medical tests it needs had not yet been approved [F0180, F0181]. BOESL ran its own call for 112 DHL jobs (see Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?) [F0043].

#### "Oman is opening soon."

In August 2026 the Bangladesh Embassy in Muscat said Oman had made no formal announcement on recruiting Bangladeshis, and in September 2026 the State Minister said visas remain restricted [F0172, F0184].

#### "Kyrgyzstan is a door to Europe."

A man who paid a broker Tk 4.8 lakh on a promise of Italy was taken to Bishkek and spent 44 days in a Central Asian prison [F0173, F0174].

## Dalal scams: the usual tricks and how to spot them

### In one minute

Most Bangladeshis who go abroad pay a dalal (দালাল, an unlicensed broker or middleman) somewhere along the way: in a 2023 national survey, 52% of migrants paid their migration costs to brokers, and 58% borrowed to go [S0088, S0114]. An ILO (International Labour Organization) consultant says the dalal is often a friend or relative, because people trust them with large sums more than strangers [S0089]. Behind the village dalal sit more middlemen, a licensed agency and, abroad, a sponsor or broker who sells the visa itself; one agency owner counted five to six levels of brokers [S0091, S0064, S0068]. Each scam below works by selling something that is not what it seems: a visa with no job behind it, a tourist visa called a work visa, a contract that changes on arrival, a medical or skill-test "pass" for money, a fake clearance card, a European work permit that was never issued, or a marriage that is really trafficking [S0068, S0121, S0075, S0080, S0077, S0118, S0002, VS0004]. Getting the money back is hard: in the 2026 Cambodia cases the CID (Criminal Investigation Department of the police) said many returnees were reluctant to go to law and others bargained with brokers, and complaints without documents are rejected [S0122, S0060, S0061]. Taking money on a false job promise or holding someone's passport is a crime for anyone, not only agencies, with up to 5 years in prison, and trafficking carries at least 5 years up to life [S0159, S0052, VS0007]. Before paying, the official checks are free: BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that licenses agencies and clears workers) checks a job visa or demand letter in 3 working days, and Saudi Arabia, Singapore and other destinations let you check a visa or permit online [S0158, S0066, S0154].

### Who a dalal is

The word dalal means broker [S0213]. Since 2023 the law recognises a "sub-agent", a person registered with BMET who collects workers for a licensed agency; anyone else who does that work, or claims to, commits a crime [S0133, S0170]. A 2024 study of Bangladeshi migrants named recruitment through a sub-agent or dalal among the top risk factors they face [S0214].

#### The chain from the village to the visa seller

1. **The visa seller abroad.** In Saudi Arabia, a Bangladeshi agent in the trade told Bonik Barta in 2026 that sponsors (the employer or household that legally owns a worker's visa) take out work permits for workers they do not need and sell them; the worker enters as that sponsor's employee but then works for another company [S0068]. In Kuwait, brokers lure Kuwaiti citizens into obtaining worker visas, and a visa that costs KWD 6 (Kuwaiti dinars) in processing and attestation fees is sold for KWD 2,000-2,200 (about Tk 8-9 lakh), though the employer also spends about KWD 250 on the work permit and other costs [S0113, S0112, VS0025, VS0026]. The joint general secretary of the Bangladesh Press Club in Kuwait said in July 2025 that an organised broker ring there controls the visa trade, which is why Bangladeshis pay more than other South Asians [S0198].
2. **The licensed recruiting agency in Dhaka.** It may take workers only through registered sub-agents, from BMET's database or directly [S0094]. In reviews from 835 Bangladeshi migrants on the ITUC's (International Trade Union Confederation) Recruitment Advisor, 78% had relied on a sub-agent to reach the agency [S0092].
3. **Layers of middlemen.** A visa passes through six or seven hands before reaching the worker, according to TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) [S0064]. One agency owner said that with five to six levels of brokers, agencies cannot control the cost [S0091].
4. **The village dalal.** This is often a friend or relative, according to an ILO consultant [S0089]. In one 2025 Cambodia case the first meeting was at a tea stall across the highway from Dhaka airport; the man paid Tk 1 lakh to the dalal and the rest to a manpower agency in Fakirapool, Dhaka [S0192, S0194].
5. **The online dalal.** Many dalals now work through Facebook pages and WhatsApp, with no signboard, no trade licence and sometimes no real name; they call themselves "recruitment consultant", "visa processing expert" or a foreign company's "official representative" [S0171, S0173].

#### The law on sub-agents

- **Only registered sub-agents are legal.** Since the Overseas Employment and Migrants (Amendment) Act 2023, an agency may appoint only a registered person as its sub-agent or representative, and BMET issues the registration [S0093, S0097]. Under section 35 of the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013 (the OEM Act), an agency that appoints an unregistered one faces 1 year's prison or a Tk 1-5 lakh fine, and the unregistered dalal faces 1 year or a Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh fine [S0132, S0170].
- **What a registered sub-agent must show.** He must run an office with a signboard giving his name, registration number and date, the agency's name and licence number, and his addresses and phone numbers, and must display his registration certificate [S0096]. BMET issues the certificate, and BMET or the DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office) publishes sub-agents' details on its website [S0097].
- **Where the list should be.** Each agency must publish its list of registered sub-agents on its own website, and may take workers only through registered sub-agents, from BMET's database or directly [S0094].
- **Who pays if he cheats.** Under the 2025 licence and sub-agent rules, the agency and its sub-agent are each and jointly liable for whatever the sub-agent does [S0095]. Each sub-agent lodges Tk 3 lakh security with the agency by pay order, and a worker cheated by him is compensated from it once the irregularity is proved [S0098]. BMET's Director General can suspend or cancel a sub-agent's registration, after an inquiry and hearing, if a complaint by a worker, a relative or a well-wisher is proved [S0099].

> **Need to know**
> - Has BMET registered any sub-agents yet, and where exactly is the public list (which page of bmet.gov.bd or OEP)?
> - Do agencies actually publish their sub-agent lists on their websites, and how many do?

#### What people pay, and to whom

- **The average bill.** A 2020 survey by the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics with the ILO found a worker spends Tk 416,789 on average to go abroad [S0115]. An OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) survey put spending at five times the government-set cost on average [S0178].
- **To whom.** In the 2023 survey, 52.03% of migrants paid their migration costs to brokers [S0088]. Broker use was highest in Sylhet (58.53%) and Mymensingh (57.44%) divisions and lowest in Barishal (28.83%) [S0090].
- **Example of the split.** In the 2025 Cambodia case above, the dalal asked Tk 7 lakh in all for a job he said would pay Tk 80,000 a month; the man paid Tk 1 lakh to the dalal and the rest to the agency [S0193, S0194].
- **The official figure.** The ministry fixed the official migration cost for Saudi Arabia in 2016 at Tk 165,000 [S0055]. In one BMET permission letter for Saudi kitchen workers, the agency could take at most Tk 50,000 per worker [S0058].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a village dalal keep per worker on a typical Saudi or Malaysia visa in 2026, and what does the sub-agent above him keep?

### Fake visa, demand letter or job offer

A demand letter is the employer's written request for workers, attested by a Bangladesh mission abroad [S0064].

**How it works:**

1. A dalal or unlicensed office shows a visa, demand letter or job offer, takes the passport and money, and the worker is sent to the airport or never hears again [VS0023, S0024].
2. Some papers are entirely forged: one Dhaka ring printed Oman visas on a computer and supplied fake tickets and fake BMET clearance [S0024]. Another office held fake Fiji demand letters and fake contracts [S0022].
3. Some are real papers that lead nowhere: a broker told Bonik Barta in 2024 that some Saudi citizens issue visas under shell companies, and brokers disappear once paid [S0072]. A former BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) official said in 2026 that some Saudi visas first appear in the online system and later disappear, and industry insiders said the Saudi labour ministry had cancelled several thousand applications filed on fake demand letters [S0065, VS0028].
4. An attested demand letter is not proof: TIB found some Bangladeshi mission officials attest demand letters without checks or for bribes [S0064].

**Red flags:**

- an office with no recruiting licence that advertises online and collects passports [VS0023];
- a Saudi visa whose company cannot be found in the Ministry of Commerce's commercial register (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 1: Find a real job, Before you pay anyone) [S0204].

**Check before paying:**

1. **At BMET or your DEMO:** take a copy of the visa or demand letter (and, where relevant, the passport page with the visa) to BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, or your DEMO; the check is free and takes 3 working days [S0158, S0063]. BMET checks Saudi, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and UAE visas online, and its visa-check app is on Google Play [S0067].
2. **On the destination's own system:**
   - Saudi Arabia: KSA Visa Track, with the visa or application number and your passport number (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 7: Check the visa yourself) [S0066].
   - Malaysia: MyIMMs application status, with the employer's registration number and application number (Malaysia doc: Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 5: Check the visa yourself) [S0206].
   - Qatar: the Interior Ministry's Visa Inquiry (Qatar doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 5: Check the visa yourself) [S0152].
   - UAE: the job offer, by its reference number, on the enquiry service of MOHRE (the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation), inquiry.mohre.gov.ae (UAE doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 5: Check your visa and permit are real) [S0153].
   - Singapore: the "Check work pass and application status" service of MOM (the Ministry of Manpower), no login needed (Singapore doc: Work visa: Work Permit (construction, marine shipyard, process and other sectors), Step 5: The employer applies; check your IPA) [S0154].
   - Maldives: Work Permit Verification on xpat.egov.mv (Maldives doc: Work visa: company worker (construction, resort, shop), Step 5: Check your permit and visa are real) [S0155].
   - Kyrgyzstan: Check status on the e-visa portal with the reference number (Kyrgyzstan doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 5: The work visa, and checking it is real) [S0156].
   - Oman: the visa status shows only in the sponsor's own account, so only the employer can check it (Oman doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 6: The employer gets the employment visa from the ROP; check it) [S0157].
   - Kuwait: BMET's check covers the visa and demand letter (Kuwait doc: Work visa: private-sector worker (Article 18), Step 5: Medical check and checking your visa is real) [S0158].

**Cases:**

- 15 Sept 2020: a RAB (Rapid Action Battalion, an elite police unit) mobile court found that "Dream Visa", Road 4, Banani, had no recruiting licence, seized 32 passports, fake contracts and fake Fiji demand letters, and jailed the owner for six months after he admitted fraud [VS0023, S0022, VS0024].
- Aug 2022: RAB arrested members of an unlicensed Dhaka ring that, according to RAB, took Tk 4 lakh each and supplied fake Oman visas, fake tickets and fake BMET clearance; victims were turned back at the airport [S0024].

**What to do:** taking money on a false job promise is an offence under section 31 of the OEM Act (see File a police case) [S0159]; complain to BMET too (see Complain to BMET) [S0043].

### Visa trading and the "free visa"

A "free visa" (ফ্রি ভিসা) is a real visa bought from a sponsor who has no job for you [S0068, S0070].

**How it works:**

1. A sponsor abroad takes out a visa in its own name and sells it through brokers [S0068, S0113].
2. Dalals sell it in Bangladesh, though it gives only permission to enter, with no job guaranteed [S0179, VS0027].
3. On arrival in Saudi Arabia you are the sponsor's employee in name only: you must find your own work and housing, may pay the sponsor a monthly cut, and can be deported for breaking your visa's conditions [S0070, S0069].

**What dalals say:** "free visa" (ফ্রি ভিসা), "easy job" (সহজ চাকরি), "quick departure" (কম সময়ে যাত্রা) [S0179]. In Malaysia the same trick is sold as a "calling visa" [S0107].

**Red flags:**

- the words "free visa": BMET says there is no such thing [S0071];
- a household named as the employer on a work visa, with other signs listed in Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Avoid a "free visa" [S0070];
- a price far above the official cost: a Kuwait visa that costs KWD 6 in fees is sold for Tk 8-9 lakh [S0112, VS0025].

**Check before paying:** BMET says to have any so-called free visa and its papers checked at BMET in Dhaka or the DEMO, and to confirm the exact job, pay and benefits [S0203].

**Cases:**

- Reported Sept 2026: hundreds of people from Munshiganj and Cumilla paid about Tk 4-6 lakh each after Facebook ads for Saudi "free visa" jobs, and many found the visa was not a work permit [S0172].
- Reported July 2024: a Pirojpur man sold his business, mortgaged land and borrowed heavily to go to Oman on a free visa through a dalal, and got no work for a year [S0181].
- Reported Jan 2026: many who went to Malaysia on a calling visa or free visa found no work on arrival [S0107].

**What to do:** obtaining a demand letter, visa or work permit by illegal means and buying or selling it inside Bangladesh is an offence under section 33 of the OEM Act, with 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 3 lakh [S0054, VS0042]. The government legal aid helpline is 16699 (see Free legal help) [S0051].

### A visit or tourist visa sold as a job

**How it works:**

1. The dalal promises a work visa but the passport carries a tourist or visit visa [S0121, S0073].
2. The worker enters legally, the visa runs out, and the worker is illegal with no job or employer [S0073].

**What dalals say:** that a one- or two-month tourist visa is a two-year work visa [S0121], or that a 3-month tourist visa will become a 15-month iqama (Saudi residence permit) [S0073].

**Red flags:**

- a job offered on a tourist or visit visa: the ministry warns not to go to work on one on a broker's promise [S0117];
- destinations in the ministry's 5 Feb 2025 warning: the Middle East, Russia, Greece, Libya, Lebanon, Egypt, Cambodia and Malaysia [S0116].

**Check before paying:** the visa type shows in the destination's own visa check (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 7: Check the visa yourself) [S0066], and BMET's free check says whether it is a work visa [S0158].

**Cases:**

- Apr 2023 to May 2026: a Keraniganj woman, then 20, promised a Tk 50,000-a-month restaurant job in Dubai, was given a visit visa, had her passport taken on arrival and was made to work in a dance bar for three years, she told Samakal [S0189].
- Reported July 2026: Cambodia victims travelled on one- or two-month tourist visas their brokers called two-year work visas [S0121].

**What to do:** see File a police case and, if you were forced to work, File a trafficking case [S0159, VS0003].

### Contract substitution: a different job or wage on arrival

**How it works:**

1. The contract shown in Bangladesh names one employer, job and wage [S0075].
2. On arrival the worker signs a new contract at the real, lower wage, or is sent to a different employer [S0076, S0075].

**What dalals say:** that the job title on the visa is temporary and will change once the worker proves himself [S0195].

**Red flags:**

- a job title on the visa that differs from the job promised: in a 2025 Cambodia case the visa named a construction job and the broker called it temporary [S0195].

**Check before paying:** compare the employer, job and salary on the destination's record with what you were promised. Singapore's IPA (In-Principle Approval letter) states the job and salary, and MOM's online check shows Work Permit holders their salary information (Singapore doc: Work visa: Work Permit (construction, marine shipyard, process and other sectors), Step 5: The employer applies; check your IPA) [S0151, S0154].

**Cases:**

- Oct 2022 (reported Sept 2024): a Narsingdi man who paid a broker Tk 4 lakh for a Saudi pharmacy job with a 15-month iqama got a restaurant cleaner's job with a 3-month iqama, and became undocumented [S0074].
- Apr-May 2026: an overseas agent sent a Singapore-bound worker from India a screenshot of an "approved" pass, took about S$8,000, then sent an IPA with a different job and lower salary [S0151].
- July 2024 (reported Feb 2026): a Bangladeshi paid more than EUR 10,000 for a job with a Romanian transport company; on arrival there was no sign of the company, and he was sent to a cement manufacturer [S0007, S0008].
- Nov 2026: the minister told Parliament that complaints of wages below the contract are among those being investigated [S0185].

**What to do:** being sent to a different job is grounds for a BMET complaint (see Complain to BMET) [S0043]; luring someone with false promises of higher pay is an offence under section 31 of the OEM Act [S0159].

### The staged-payment trap, and no receipts

**How it works:**

1. The dalal takes money in instalments, each tied to a promised step: "processing", the visa, the medical, the ticket [S0174].
2. The passport may be kept until the last instalment [VS0029].
3. Money goes in cash or by mobile money, with no receipt [S0175, S0061].
4. When it fails, there is no proof of who was paid what [S0061].

**What dalals say:** fees for "registration" (রেজিস্ট্রেশন), "visa processing" (ভিসা প্রসেসিং) or "document verification" (ডকুমেন্ট ভেরিফিকেশন) [S0174].

**Red flags:**

- cash only, or payment to a personal bKash or Nagad number [S0175];
- money asked before the visa, BMET clearance and flight are confirmed, which a BMET permission letter forbids [S0058].

**Check before paying:** BMET says to deal with the agency through a bank and keep receipts [S0196, S0059]. In one BMET permission letter, the agency could take money only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and only after the visa, BMET clearance and flight were confirmed [S0058].

**Cases:**

- Reported Feb 2025: a Bangladeshi who paid about EUR 18,000 to reach Italy said the man handling his papers in Bangladesh kept his passport until the last instalment [VS0030, VS0029].
- Aug 2022: a Dhaka ring gave refund cheques that bounced [S0025].

**What to do:** BMET rejects complaints not backed by documents, and workers without written papers often fail to get compensation [S0060, S0061]. Keep every message and transfer record and complain (see Complain to BMET) [S0044].

### The passport kept by the dalal

**How it works:**

1. The dalal or office collects the passport "for the visa" [S0021, S0101].
2. The passport becomes a lever: to get it back, the worker must pay more or accept the deal [VS0029, S0123].

**Red flags:**

- an office collecting many passports before any visa exists: in Sept 2026 a BMET mobile court seized three boxes of passports from one agency [S0101];
- a request for the passport to arrange a medical: the ministry warned in Oct 2026 not to hand over a passport or money without confirming official approval [S0102].

**Check before paying:** some steps need only the passport details, not the passport: for example, the Wafid medical slip is printed by entering the passport number and nationality [VS0031].

**Cases:**

- 10 Sept 2026: a BMET mobile court sealed Triple A Overseas (RL-2630), fined it Tk 50,000 and seized three boxes of passports taken irregularly from workers, the ministry said, amid complaints that some agencies were collecting passports with promises of Malaysian visas [S0101].
- July 2026: in Cambodia a broker still held workers' passports and demanded USD 500 more from each [S0123].

**What to do:** holding a migrant's passport, visa or papers without lawful reason is an offence under section 31(c) of the OEM Act: up to 5 years and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh (see File a police case) [S0159, S0160].

### The training-centre and skills-test racket

**How it works:**

1. Saudi-bound workers take a skill test under the SVP (Skill Verification Program) run by Takamol, for a set fee of USD 50 (about Tk 6,200); dalals ask more, promising a pass [VS0051, VS0050].
2. Jordan garment jobs go through a government channel that is meant to be free, and cheats charge for it [S0128].

**What dalals say:** that they can fix the result; one ILO study found dalals telling candidates how to identify themselves to the interviewer, for example with a nail-polish colour on a set finger [S0137].

**Red flags:**

- any payment beyond the USD 50 test fee: BMET says paying extra cannot buy a Saudi skill-test pass [VS0050, S0077];
- a fee for a government-to-government job: BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited, the state recruiting company) has no agent or sub-agent and takes no cash [S0130].

**Check before paying:** see Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 3: Pass the skill test, for the official booking and fee [S0077].

**Cases:**

- Reported 9 Oct 2026: 20 people arrested in a raid in Dhaka's Shyamoli were sentenced by a mobile court for taking Tk 5-10 lakh for Jordan garment jobs that are free through the government channel [S0128, S0129].

**What to do:** a worker caught paying for a pass has the Takamol account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [S0078]. BMET asks people to hand anyone taking money for a pass to the police (emergency number 999) [VS0051, S0087]. BMET's Takamol and Japan Support Centre is at BMET Bhaban 4th floor, Kakrail, Dhaka (01901165981, 01901165982) [S0079].

### The medical "slip" and "unfit, pay to pass"

Wafid is the Gulf states' online booking for the pre-departure medical; people still call it GAMCA, after the system it replaced [S0208, S0209]. The "slip" is the appointment confirmation [S0211].

**How it works:**

1. The slip costs USD 10 (standard) or USD 25 (premium) on wafid.com [S0199].
2. Agencies sell a "package": in a 2025 case a Paltan agency offered Tk 10,000 standard, or Tk 20,000 with a guaranteed fit certificate [S0080].
3. Centres are alleged to declare candidates unfit, then ask for a bribe for a fit report [S0082].
4. Unauthorised centres test and issue reports without approval [S0103].

**What dalals say:** "fit guaranteed" [S0080, S0210].

**Red flags:**

- any "guaranteed fit": a syndicate reportedly charged Tk 25,000-30,000 for one in 2025 [S0210];
- a total well above about Tk 8,500-10,000 for a Gulf medical, the range reported as the proper cost in 2025 [S0210, VS0033]; for Malaysia the ministry set Tk 8,000 in Oct 2026 [S0200];
- a centre not on the destination's approved list [S0103].

**Check before paying:** book the slip yourself and check the result with your passport number or slip number on Wafid (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 4: Pass the Wafid medical check) [S0209, S0211]. The Gulf country that issued the visa examines you again on arrival [S0081]; in a 2025 case a man who paid an agent Tk 25,000 for a fit certificate was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [VS0032].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the official total for the Gulf (Wafid) medical in Bangladesh in 2026, and where is it published?

**Cases:**

- 1 Oct 2026: a mobile court sealed Human Diagnostic and Medical Center, Paltan, Dhaka, which the ministry said had tested 14 Malaysia-bound people without government authorisation [S0103].
- Sept 2025: a former BAIRA official said only 2-5% of workers pay the set fee; the rest pay at least Tk 17,000-20,000 [S0083].

**What to do:** the ministry asks people to email secretary@probashi.gov.bd about illegal or unauthorised medical tests, with the date, place, names and evidence [VS0034]; the Gulf Health Council complaint route is in Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, If you are declared unfit or overcharged [S0081].

### Fake BMET cards and fake clearance

BMET clearance (the "smart card" or emigration clearance) is the government's permission to leave as a worker; airport desks check it [S0111].

**How it works:** rings put fake visas in passports, then obtain BMET smart cards for them or forge the clearance itself, and send people to the airport [S0003, S0024].

**Red flags:**

- a BMET card or clearance supplied by the dalal or office itself [S0024];
- a "clearance" for a country where the ministry has stopped clearances without its approval: since 4 Nov 2026 these include Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [S0127].

**Check before paying:** whether a BMET emigration clearance or card exists can be checked on the page of the WEWB (Wage Earners' Welfare Board), portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [VS0035]. Police clearance certificates are forged too, with QR codes that lead to a fake website: apply only on pcc.police.gov.bd (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 6: Give the agency your papers for the visa (start before Step 5)) [S0084].

**Cases:**

- FY2023-24: airport desks checked 1,391,988 clearances and stopped 1,098 travellers with no or fake BMET clearance [S0111].
- July 2026: the CID said that after checks tightened, some recruiters allegedly used fake BMET cards to send workers to Cambodia [S0118].
- Reported Oct 2022: workers who went to the UAE on fake smart cards got neither legal status nor jobs [S0147].

**What to do:** see File a police case [S0159].

### Loan sharks and land mortgages

**How it works:**

1. The family raises the money by selling land, pawning jewellery or borrowing at high interest [S0176].
2. In an OKUP survey, 72% borrowed, 11% mortgaged land and 6% sold family assets to go [S0178].
3. Some dalals lend the money themselves: in a 2023 study of Bangladeshi women working in Jordan, loans from the brokers were the most problematic [S0136].
4. What the worker sends home may not even cover the interest [S0183].

**Red flags:**

- a loan arranged by the person selling you the job: in July 2026 the CID arrested an NGO loan officer accused of helping a woman take a Tk 1 lakh loan, keeping the money and her passport, and trafficking her to Cambodia [S0186, S0187];
- a bribe asked for a government loan: in July 2026 the state minister said complaints allege migrants are asked for Tk 10,000-20,000 in bribes for Tk 3 lakh loans from Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB, the state migrant bank), whose loan steps are in Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, If you need to borrow [S0086].

**Cases:**

- Early 2023 (reported June 2026): a Madaripur man took loans from two banks to pay a broker EUR 16,000 for promised work in Italy [S0140, S0141].
- July 2024 (reported Feb 2026): a Bangladeshi sold land and borrowed from relatives to raise more than EUR 10,000 for a Romania transport job that wasn't there on arrival [S0007, S0008].

> **Need to know**
> - What monthly interest do village moneylenders charge migrants in 2026, and is land usually mortgaged by a written deed or informally?

### Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes

**How it works:**

1. A broker arranges a marriage to a foreigner and promises a rich husband, a big house abroad or money for the family [S0021, VS0019].
2. The woman travels on a family visa, such as China's Q-1 family-reunion visa [S0019].
3. Abroad, the woman may be forced into sex work: a Chuadanga mother alleged in a 2024 case that this happened to her daughter in China [VS0020].

**What dalals say:** "the husband is rich" (স্বামী বিত্তশালী) [S0021]; in one case the family was told the Chinese groom had become Muslim, worked in a Gazipur factory and would stay in Bangladesh [S0014].

**Red flags:**

- a foreign groom found through "arrangers": middlemen allegedly dealt in up to Tk 10-20 lakh per woman sent to China [VS0018, S0017];
- fake addresses, forged papers and suspect marriage documents [S0020];
- a job or study offer that turns into a marriage: in a May 2024 case, a Rangamati woman's sister alleged she was taken to Dhaka with a promise of a nursing course and forced to marry a Chinese man [VS0022, S0018].

**Cases:**

- Mar 2024: a Chuadanga mother filed a case at a Dhaka tribunal alleging her daughter, married to a Chinese man in July 2023, was taken to China and forced into sex work; the daughter said Bangladeshi traffickers had sold her to him for Tk 10 lakh; the accused include a Jatrabari marriage registrar (kazi), and the CID was investigating in June 2024 [VS0020, S0015, S0016, VS0021].

At least 4,226 Bangladeshi women took China's Q-1 family visa in 2020-2025: 35 in 2022, 1,016 in 2023, 1,378 in 2024 and 1,721 in 2025, according to data presented at an anti-trafficking-law workshop; the figure is not a count of trafficking victims [S0019, VS0015, VS0016, VS0017].

**What to do:** exploitation through a fraudulent marriage counts as trafficking under the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 (see File a trafficking case) [VS0004, VS0014].

> **Need to know**
> - Are forged nikahnamas (Muslim marriage contracts) used for Gulf or Italian family visas, and how does a family check one against the kazi's register?

### Fake European work permits

**How it works:**

1. A dalal or agency sells a work permit for a European country such as Serbia, North Macedonia or Romania [S0001, S0148].
2. The permit is forged; when the visa is refused, the ring attaches a counterfeit visa sticker, gets a BMET card for it, and sends the person to the airport [S0003].
3. Most are stopped at immigration [S0004].
4. Even real permits can lead to a different job, lower pay or no employer at all [S0008, S0009, S0013].

**What dalals say:** that approval and a sticker visa will come within a month [S0006].

**Red flags:**

- an agency that controls your online application login: one Serbia applicant found the agency had changed his password [S0006];
- a contract wage higher than what is really paid: in Romania, a legal expert said in 2026 that the contract filed for the visa often differs from the one signed on arrival and shows a much higher salary [S0009]; in Malta, an Identity Malta source said in 2024 that recruits earn much less than their contracts declare [S0013];
- Romania sold as a stepping stone: in 2023 the Bangladesh ambassador to Romania said agencies encourage people who go there to move on to another country and process any file that pays more [S0150].

**Check before paying:** BMET's free visa check (see Fake visa, demand letter or job offer) [S0158]. Since 4 Nov 2026, BMET must not clear workers for Serbia without the ministry's approval [S0127].

**Cases:**

- Reported 28 Oct 2025: the embassies of Serbia and North Macedonia, having found fake work permits in visa applications, had issued no work permits to Bangladeshis for two months, and diplomatic sources said about 90% of the permits Bangladeshi applicants submitted to North Macedonia's embassy were fake, with the same situation in Serbia [S0001, S0002].
- July 2024 (reported Feb 2026): a Bangladeshi paid EUR 8,000 commission to a Bangladeshi agency for a Romania transport job; on arrival the company was not there [S0007, S0008].
- May 2023: private agencies were reported to charge Tk 6-8 lakh for Romania against BOESL's Tk 2.31 lakh, and the president of the Chamber of Commerce Romania-Bangladesh said he had handled at least 200 cases of workers who didn't get the promised job or pay [S0148, S0149].
- July 2024: The Shift reported that Dhaka agencies were offering Maltese employers workers at EUR 600 a month, below Malta's EUR 925 minimum wage, plus a EUR 1,500 "gift" per worker [VS0046, VS0045, S0012].
- May 2023: a Sylhet man said a broker took Tk 6 lakh for Poland, left him in Kyrgyzstan and disappeared [S0202].

**What to do:** in Romania a worker has a 90-day window by law to find a new job and get a new work permit, and a migrant caught working illegally is deported under escort [S0010, S0011]. In Bangladesh, see File a police case [S0159].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Serbia, Croatia or Malta work permit sell for in Dhaka in 2026?
> - Did Croatia suspend work visas for Bangladeshis, and is it still suspended?

### Online job adverts, Facebook and WhatsApp scams, and fake government sites

**How it works:**

1. A Facebook page or WhatsApp group posts jobs with foreign flags, company logos and photos of "successful" migrants [S0171, S0172].
2. Contact moves to WhatsApp, payment goes by mobile money [S0175].
3. When the page closes or the number changes, the worker has nowhere to go [S0175].

**What dalals say:** "free visa" (ফ্রি ভিসা), "guaranteed job" (নিশ্চিত চাকরি), "Tk 1 lakh a month salary" (মাসে এক লাখ টাকা বেতন) [S0171].

**Red flags:**

- an overseas job advert without government or BMET approval: publishing one is an offence under section 32 of the OEM Act, with up to 1 year in prison and a fine of at least Tk 50,000 [S0162, VS0043];
- computer operator, typist, call-centre or data-entry jobs in Cambodia, the typical bait for scam compounds [S0119, S0124];
- a travel agency advertising jobs: in a June 2026 Jordan case, the CID found the advertiser was a travel agency with no recruiting licence [S0138];
- an offer in the name of a government body: in Aug 2025 BOESL warned that a ring was using its name to promise Malaysia jobs; BOESL has no agent and takes no fee until final selection, then only by bank pay order [VS0052, S0110].

**Check before paying:** check the agency's licence and the visa as in Fake visa, demand letter or job offer [S0158]. For Cambodia jobs, see Cambodia doc: Scam compounds and trafficking [S0119].

**Cases:**

- 2025 (reported Sept 2026): a Cumilla graduate recruited through a WhatsApp group advert for data entry in Phnom Penh paid about Tk 450,000, and his passport was taken within 48 hours of landing [S0124].
- Jan 2023: the Daily Star found 30 Facebook groups and pages offering Cambodia jobs [S0120].
- Aug 2026: a ring sold fake police clearance certificates with QR codes leading to a fake website [S0084].

**What to do:** see File a police case [S0162].

### "Your visa is stuck, pay more"

**How it works:**

1. After the first payment, the dalal reports a problem: the visa is stuck, approval needs more money, or the worker must pay to leave [S0006, S0123].
2. Sometimes the worker is already abroad and held, and the family is told to pay [S0029, S0030].

**What dalals say:** in a 2025 case, hostages in Nepal were forced at gunpoint to tell families "we have reached Canada; the company has arranged 15 days' training" (আমরা কানাডায় পৌঁছে গিয়েছি। কোম্পানির পক্ষ থেকে ১৫ দিনের ট্রেনিংয়ের ব্যবস্থা করা হয়েছে।) [S0031].

**Red flags:**

- "pay after you arrive" offers routed through Nepal, where Bangladeshis get a visa on arrival [VS0037, S0033];
- photos of a visa and ticket in the passport sent to the family as proof of the trip [VS0038].

**Check before paying:** the destination's own visa check, or BMET's (see Fake visa, demand letter or job offer) [S0158].

**Cases:**

- Oct 2025: according to BRAC, three Sylhet men promised Canada jobs, to be paid for after arrival, were taken to Nepal on 13 Oct and held at gunpoint; families were told to pay a local broker Tk 5 lakh and then Tk 12 lakh more each; after a trafficking case at Sylhet's Kotwali police station, a joint police-CID raid arrested the local broker, and the traffickers released the men near Kathmandu airport [VS0037, S0029, S0030, S0032, VS0039].
- Reported July 2026: in Cambodia a worker was told to pay USD 1,500 to quit, and the broker held workers' passports and demanded USD 500 more from each [S0123].
- Reported July 2026: a Cumilla father said he paid Tk 20 lakh for his son's trip to Italy via Libya, where the son was held for ransom [S0143].

**What to do:** call 999, then see File a trafficking case; holding someone and demanding ransom counts as exploitation under the trafficking law [S0087, VS0006].

### Hundi and money mules

Hundi is the informal, illegal money-transfer system that bypasses banks [S0028].

**How it works:** hundi agents take money abroad and pay the family at home through mobile money accounts such as bKash and Nagad, often at a better rate than banks [S0125, S0126].

**Red flags:** a rate better than the bank's (in Dec 2024, about Tk 115 a dollar against about Tk 108), with cash delivered to the doorstep [S0125, VS0036].

**Cases:**

- Mar 2024: the CID arrested five people accused of laundering Tk 4 billion abroad through digital hundi using mobile financial services (MFS) [S0027].
- Sept 2022: the CID ran operations against alleged MFS agents running digital hundi [S0028].

> **Need to know**
> - Are migrants or their families asked to lend their bank or bKash accounts to move other people's money, and has anyone been charged for it?

### Refund refusals after a failed departure

**How it works:**

1. The worker doesn't go, or comes back without the job [S0104].
2. The agency or dalal delays, offers a fresh visa "soon", or gives cheques that bounce [S0025].
3. Many victims bargain with the broker instead of going to law: the CID said so of Cambodia returnees in July 2026 [S0122].

**Red flags:** a promise of a new visa instead of the money back [S0025].

**Cases:**

- 2024: agencies failed to send about 17,777 workers with BMET clearance to Malaysia by its 31 May 2024 deadline, and after a ministry probe the agencies were directed to refund them by 18 July 2024 [S0104]. In July 2024 the state minister said BAIRA reported 70% refunded but he could not yet say how many had been paid, and in Jan 2025 the ministry secretary said 81% of agencies had refunded [S0105, S0106].
- 15 Nov 2026: the minister told Parliament 56 agencies' licences had recently been cancelled for irregularities and fraud [S0184].

**What to do:** in a 2025 OKUP study only 39% of the complainants' BMET cases were resolved, typically with Tk 20,000 to just over Tk 1 lakh, though workers had paid three to six times the government cost [S0057]. A civil suit can claim the full loss (see Sue for compensation) [S0161].

### Trafficking disguised as a job, especially for women

**How it works:**

1. A woman is offered a well-paid job: housekeeping, a restaurant, customer care [S0135, S0189, S0186].
2. She is often sent on a visit visa [S0189].
3. On arrival her passport is taken, and she may be sold or forced into sex work [S0189, S0135, VS0049].

**Red flags:**

- a recruiter she already owes money to [S0186];
- a job with no BMET clearance or on a visit visa [S0117, S0189].

**Cases:**

- Reported Oct 2020: a Jhenaidah woman, lured by a fellow villager with a housekeeping job in Jordan at JD 250 (Jordanian dinars) a month, said she paid two brokers Tk 50,000, was sold in Amman and was held in a house from which women were sent out for sex work [VS0048, S0135, VS0047, VS0049].
- 15 Sept 2020: a RAB mobile court team arrested two owners of Fatema Overseas, Road 10, Banani, on a charge of trafficking a young woman to Saudi Arabia [S0205].
- 14 May 2026: a Keraniganj woman made to work in a Dubai dance bar for three years was rescued at Dhaka airport as the ring tried to take her back; investigators found at least 14 more women held in Dubai [S0189, S0190].
- July 2026: the CID arrested an NGO loan officer accused of trafficking a woman to Cambodia; she came home on 1 July with help from the BRAC Migration Welfare Centre [S0186, S0188].
- The CID's trafficking unit had 122 cases under investigation in 2025, against 114 in 2022, 86 in 2023 and 100 in 2024 [S0191].

**What to do:** see File a trafficking case [VS0002].

### Where to complain and get help

#### Complain to BMET

1. **Online:** since 30 July 2026, file on OEP's Online Complaint Management Module (OEP is the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system), upload documents and track progress [S0045].
2. **On paper:** BMET's complaint form (Form Ka) asks for your name, NID (national ID card), passport and BMET smart card numbers, addresses and the agency's or broker's details; hand it in at BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, or your DEMO [S0044, S0063].
3. **What happens:** free; an inquiry committee holds a hearing, BMET recommends and the ministry decides, within 30 working days [S0043].
4. **If nothing happens:** BMET's complaint officer, the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 (3 months); then the ministry Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd (1 month); then grs.gov.bd (3 months) [S0046].

Under section 41 of the OEM Act the inquiry must finish within 30 working days, and a proven complaint may be settled by order or arbitration within 3 months of the inquiry's end [S0167, S0168, VS0041]. If an agency refuses to pay an arbitration award, BMET may deduct it from the agency's security deposit, according to a 2026 study by RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) [VS0040]. In practice many complaints stay unresolved for years [S0056].

#### Complain to the Ministry

- **Enforcement-2 branch** of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare: application with proof, your full address and the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and address; free; action in 3-5 working days, settlement in 30; 02-41030255, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd [S0047].
- **Mobile courts** under the OEM Act give immediate remedies; free; 7 working days [S0139].
- **Hotline:** 16135 (toll free), +880 9610102030 from abroad [S0048].

#### File a police case

- **Section 31 of the OEM Act:** taking or trying to take money on a false promise of a job abroad, holding a migrant's passport or papers without lawful reason, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay: up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [S0159, S0160]. It applies to any person, not only agencies [S0052].
- **Section 33:** obtaining a demand letter, visa or work permit by illegal means and buying or selling it in Bangladesh: 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and at least Tk 3 lakh [S0054, VS0042].
- **Section 35:** an unregistered sub-agent: 1 year or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [S0170].
- **Section 34:** sending someone out other than through an official exit point: up to 10 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [S0164].
- **How it is handled:** sections 31, 33 and 34 are cognisable (police can register a case and arrest without a warrant), non-bailable and can't be settled privately; sections 32, 33A, 35 and 35A can be tried by a mobile court [S0165, S0166]. Cases are tried by a first-class judicial or metropolitan magistrate [S0169].
- **Emergency:** 999 [S0087].

#### File a trafficking case

- **The law:** the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 replaced the 2012 trafficking act [VS0014, VS0001].
- **What counts:** recruiting, moving or holding someone by deceit or by exploiting their vulnerability, for sexual, labour or other exploitation inside or outside Bangladesh, is trafficking (section 5) [VS0002]. So is luring or helping someone to go abroad for work knowing they may be exploited [VS0003]. Exploitation includes a fraudulent marriage, forcing someone into online scamming, and holding someone for ransom [VS0004, VS0005, VS0006].
- **Penalty:** at least 5 years' rigorous imprisonment up to life and at least Tk 50,000; for an organised group, death, life or at least 7 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [VS0007, VS0008]. Migrant smuggling, getting someone illegally into a country where they are not a citizen or resident, carries 3 to 10 years and at least Tk 1 lakh [VS0044].
- **Where:** a complaint can go to a police station or directly to the Tribunal, and police must protect the complainant and keep their identity secret (section 28) [VS0009]. Until Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Suppression Tribunals are set up, the government can appoint the district's Women and Children Repression Prevention Tribunal judge, or the district and sessions judge, to hear these cases [VS0010].
- **Compensation:** on conviction the Tribunal can order the trafficker to pay the victim (section 46) [VS0011].

#### Sue for compensation

Anyone harmed by a breach of the OEM Act or the employment contract can file a civil suit for compensation alongside a criminal case (OEM Act section 28) [S0161]. A trafficking or smuggling victim has the same right under section 56 of the 2026 trafficking act [VS0012]. In practice workers often can't keep a case going for lack of money and legal aid [S0109].

#### Free legal help

- **The right:** migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to reasonable legal aid (OEM Act section 27) [S0134].
- **Government legal aid helpline:** 16699 [S0051].
- **OKUP:** free, confidential legal advice and help filing a case, 01842773300 [S0049].

## Departure bottlenecks: offloading, airport checks, bribes and deportation

### In one minute

Before a worker boards, five offices each hold a stamp he cannot travel without: the passport office, the police (for the police clearance), an approved medical centre (for the Gulf, one approved by the Gulf Health Council), the destination's embassy, and BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, which issues the emigration clearance or "smart card") [D0024, D0011, D0160, D0131, D0001]. At each one, surveys, news reports and an anti-corruption case show people paying on top of the official fee: TIB (Transparency International Bangladesh) found three in four households that used the passport office in November 2024 to October 2025 paid a bribe or unauthorised money, medical centres have been reported to threaten fit workers with an "unfit" result and to sell guaranteed "fit" reports, and in July 2025 the Anti-Corruption Commission filed a case against BMET officials it accuses of clearing women on fake passports [D0134, D0135, D0147, D0157, D0154]. The last gate is immigration at the airport, run by the police's Special Branch (SB), which turned back 81,500 travellers from 2021 to June 2026, often over mismatched visas, clearance discrepancies, forged papers or answers that did not add up [D0059, D0028, D0031]. Some were stopped with valid papers, most visibly three adult women flying to Nepal on 8 August 2026 who, their lawyer says, were asked why they were unmarried; on 29 September 2026 the High Court asked the government to explain why stopping them should not be declared illegal [D0041, D0042, D0046]. A Home Ministry official has admitted that some complaints of people paying police to get through, or being unjustly stopped, are true [D0036]. Even past Dhaka, visitors can be refused on arrival, as hundreds of Bangladeshis were at Kuala Lumpur in 2025 [D0179, D0113]. Workers who come back deported or on an outpass are met by the government's airport welfare desk, though in 2020 some deportees were jailed on arrival, and destinations such as Kuwait and Malaysia bar deportees from returning for years or for life [D0079, D0188, D0122, D0119].

### Passport and police clearance

#### The passport office

**The rule.** An e-passport (48 pages, 5 years) costs Tk 4,025 regular, Tk 6,325 express or Tk 8,625 super express, VAT included [D0024]. Regular delivery is within 15 working days of biometric enrolment, express within 7 and super express within 2 [D0025]. The application must match the NID (national identity card) or online birth certificate exactly, and anyone over 20 must use the NID [D0026].

**What happens.** In TIB's National Household Survey 2025 (November 2024 to October 2025), passport services were the most corrupt sector: 84.4% of households using them met corruption and 76.6% paid bribes or unauthorised money [D0134, D0135]. The average bribe was Tk 4,532, and 39.7% reported harassment by brokers ("dalals") [D0136]. Most irregularities happened while preparing and submitting the application (76.8%), and the biometric stage carried the highest average bribe, Tk 6,680 [D0137]. People reissuing an old MRP (machine-readable passport) paid the most, Tk 12,444 on average [D0138]. Barishal (93.3%) and Sylhet (91.7%) divisions were the worst [D0139]. TIB says that despite online services many people still depend on brokers, which keeps bribery alive [D0146].

**Police verification.** TIB counts the abolition of police verification for passports among recent reforms [D0140]. In a 2017 study, TIB had found that three in four new-passport applicants paid bribes or irregular money during police verification, and its executive director said there is "a trap of harassment" at every step from application to delivery [D0104, VD0001, D0105]. In the 2025 survey (November 2024 to October 2025), passport verification was the police service with the most corruption (80.7%) and bribery (77.5%) [D0141].

> **Need to know**

#### Police clearance

**The rule.** The police clearance certificate (PCC) is applied for online at pcc.police.gov.bd and verified by police at your present address [D0011, D0012]. The fee was raised from Tk 500 to Tk 1,500 by a Security Services Division circular of 4 December 2024 [D0103]. You track it by texting "PCC S <reference number>" to 26969; the PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825, help.pcc@police.gov.bd, Sunday to Thursday 9 am to 5 pm [D0012].

**What happens.** TIB's 2017 study found police took Tk 500 to 1,000 per visa for a clearance [D0106]. In TIB's 2025 survey, 66.1% of households dealing with the Special Branch paid unauthorised money, though police clearance had the lowest average bribe of any police service, Tk 1,279 [D0142, D0143].

### The Gulf medical (Wafid, formerly GAMCA)

#### The rule and the fee

Workers going to the Gulf states must pass a medical at a centre approved by the Gulf Health Council (GHC), booked through its Wafid system; the approved centres were previously known as GAMCA (GCC-approved medical centres, the GCC being the Gulf Cooperation Council) [D0160]. The agency buys a USD 10 referral token from Wafid, and the whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 [D0160]. Wafid says the result appears within 26 days, and a certificate is valid two months for issuing the visa [D0097, D0096]. Centres are licensed first by Bangladesh's health ministry, then approved by the GHC [D0169]. Qatar uses its own centres: only two are approved, in Dhaka and Sylhet, and the fee is at least USD 137 [D0171].

#### The "unfit, then pay" scheme and the slip racket

- **Unfit, then pay.** TIB reported in 2017 that some centres threaten fit workers with an "unfit" result to charge more, or make them repeat tests [D0147]. A worker told TBS (The Business Standard) in 2025 that, found unfit in Chattogram for Tk 8,500, he was declared fit at a Panthapath centre in Dhaka for Tk 50,000, then re-tested in Kuwait for 5 dirhams [D0167].
- **"Fit" packages.** A Dhaka agency offered one worker a Tk 10,000 "standard" medical that could end "unfit", or a Tk 20,000 package that guaranteed "fit" [D0157]. Syndicate centres reportedly charge Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed "fit" [D0162]. A former BAIRA (recruiting agencies' association) leader said only 2-5% of workers pay the set fee, and the rest pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 [D0165].
- **Why people pay.** An "unfit" result leaves the passport unusable for a Gulf medical until it expires, and workers fear a lifetime blacklist, so many fix a "contract" with a broker in advance [D0158, D0166].
- **Who controls the slips.** Industry insiders say about 20 of more than 200 approved centres get nearly 90% of referrals because syndicate-linked centres pre-book slots [D0161]. Approved centres grew from 26 in 2017 to 260, against 167 in India [D0168].
- **Reports without tests.** Some centres issue certificates without testing, or upload reports from a passport photocopy, and some tests done in Dhaka are filed under district centres' names [D0148, D0170, D0163].
- **What it costs later.** A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for a "fit" certificate failed the re-test in Saudi Arabia and was deported [D0159].

In July 2025 the ministry's monitoring wing said it had heard of extra charges but received no formal complaints, and had contacted the Gulf Cooperation Council; by September 2025 the ministry had written to the GHC asking it to act against irregular centres [D0164, D0173].

> **Need to know**

#### Malaysia's medical

The ministry fixed the Malaysia-bound medical at Tk 8,000 on 31 October 2026, and no centre may take more [D0194]. No fee is due for the MiGRAMS slip (the booking from Malaysia's recruitment system), no centre may work under a particular agency, and a worker may test at any approved centre [D0102, D0195]. If a worker changes agency after the slip, the first agency must send the medical report on without delay [D0101]. BAIRA said that over the previous three years a syndicate of about 100 agencies had charged Tk 11,000 against a Tk 6,500 fee, taking Tk 355 crore extra [D0172].

**Complain:** email the ministry at secretary@probashi.gov.bd if a centre asks more than Tk 8,000 or harasses you [D0196].

### Attestation and visa stamping

Every document going abroad needs attestation by Bangladesh's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA), after the issuing body and, for Arabic translations, a notary, the Islamic Foundation and the Law Ministry [D0027]. For Saudi Arabia, the employer sends visa numbers online, the agency submits the passports, and since 31 October 2022 passports, stamping and fingerprints go through Tasheer, a visa service centre at Jamuna Future Park, not the embassy [D0131, D0130]. Stamping can take one week to two months after fingerprints in some cases [D0132]. In 2022 an agency could submit at most 30 passports a week, with no cap for women workers [D0176].

**What happens.**
- In 2017 TIB reported delays, harassment and illegal charges by some host-country embassies at stamping, and that a file with any error is returned [D0149, D0150].
- In 2022 agencies said they paid USD 220-250 per Saudi visa application through middlemen, which raised workers' costs by at least Tk 20,000; the embassy said only a small fee for Enjaz (the online Saudi visa application) applies and brokers may misuse its name [D0174, D0175, D0177].
- TIB has reported that some officials at Bangladeshi missions abroad attest demand letters without proper checks or for bribes [D0108].

### BMET clearance and the smart card

#### The rule and the fee

The emigration clearance, printed as a smart card, is BMET's permission to leave for work [D0001]. If an agency recruited you, it applies; if you found the visa yourself, you apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [D0001, D0004]. Either way it takes 3 working days [D0001, D0004]. Fees (the welfare, insurance and smart card fees by bank pay order, the tax by treasury challan) [D0002]:
- welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [D0007];
- insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once [D0015];
- clearance (smart card) fee: Tk 250 [D0005];
- source income tax: Tk 400 for an unattested single visa, Tk 250 attested, Tk 500 for a Saudi visa; Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [D0003].

For women going as domestic workers, BMET needs proof of age 25 to 45 and a guardian's no-objection letter [D0002].

#### Bribes, brokers and fake clearances

- TIB's 2017 study found Tk 100-200 taken unofficially per clearance at BMET, and Tk 5,000-15,000 for low- and semi-skilled Malaysia-bound workers [D0106, D0151].
- TIB reported in 2017 that some workers buy the pre-departure training certificate through brokers without attending [D0107].
- In 2016 only 301 workers used BMET's one-stop service to get their own clearance [D0152].
- On 3 July 2025 the ACC filed a case against four BMET officials and the owners or officials of five agencies; it says its 29 May 2025 raid on BMET found clearances for Gulf domestic work approved on another woman's passport number without a check against BMET's database, and issued to four women under 25 [D0154, D0155, D0156].
- In 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare said some BMET officials clear fake documents, that BMET can check visas for only four or five countries, and that a fake-visa clearance case for Serbia was under inquiry [D0038, D0037]. BMET's charter lists online visa checks for Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and the UAE [VD0004].

**Check it is real** at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [D0014]. The welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards before departure [D0008].

**Complain:**
1. BMET's Director (Emigration), +8802222222415, 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd; then the Additional Director General (Employment), 02-48321949, adgemp@bmet.gov.bd [D0006].
2. Against an agency: a written complaint with proof to BMET's Director General, free, handled within 30 working days [D0010, VD0005].
3. If BMET fails: its grievance ladder, then the ministry's Secretary (secretary@probashi.gov.bd), then the Cabinet Division's GRS at grs.gov.bd [D0009].
4. For a bribe: the ACC (see Where to complain) [D0094].

### Departure day at the airport

#### Who checks what

1. **Papers to carry.** BMET's checklist: valid passport, work visa, medical certificate, demand letter, the contract attested by the destination's embassy, the agency's name and phone, the BMET smart card and the air ticket [D0016]. Keep one set of copies at home and three with you [D0017].
2. **Airline check-in.** Air Arabia requires passengers from Bangladesh holding a copy of a UAE visa or e-visa to have an "OK to Board" message on the booking, done at least 24 hours before departure for AED 15 to 20 [D0124, D0125].
3. **The welfare desk.** The Probashi Kallyan (expatriate welfare) desk sits directly opposite immigration at Dhaka; a worker with a BMET card who gets its seal clears immigration faster, according to the airport [D0076, D0075]. The desks check smart cards and help fill boarding cards [D0008, D0078].
4. **SB screening.** In 2021, before the immigration counter, SB officers at a separate desk held the passports of first-time Middle East travellers and interviewed them [D0087].
5. **Immigration counter.** Counters are open 24 hours; the officer stamps the passport and may ask about your travel plans [D0074].

TIB found in 2017 that some workers are handed their job contract only on reaching the airport [D0153].

#### Offloading: who decides and on what law

Immigration at Bangladesh's airports is run by the Special Branch of Bangladesh Police under the Ministry of Home Affairs, headed by an Additional Inspector General of Police (Additional IGP) [D0059, D0060, D0061]. "Offloading" is immigration's refusal to let a checked-in passenger leave; in cases reported in 2021 it was marked with an "offload" stamp or note in the passport [D0082, D0086]. SB lists the laws it applies, including the Passport Order 1973 and the Emigration Ordinance 1982 and Rules 2002 [D0063]. The Passport Order bars leaving without a valid passport or travel document, and lets a passport be impounded if a court has barred its holder from leaving [D0064, D0065]. The Ordinance, as SB quotes it, makes emigration for work lawful for a citizen with a work permit, employment visa or appointment letter, or selected through a recognised agent, and punishes departing otherwise with up to one year's simple imprisonment or a Tk 5,000 fine [D0066, D0067]. In the three women's case below, their lawyers argued that an adult woman's marital status, or travelling without a parent or guardian, cannot by itself be a lawful ground to stop her [D0197].

#### Who gets stopped

Reported reasons include mismatched visas, clearance discrepancies, forged documents and conflicting answers at the interview [D0031]. Cases show who else gets stopped:
- **Women travelling alone.** Women report officers asking whether their husband knows, and phoning husbands or parents to check [D0056, D0049]. A rights activist says officers are most suspicious of women going to Thailand or Nepal [D0057].
- **Nepal.** Police officers told Prothom Alo that Nepal has been flagged as a trafficking route and, because it gives visas on arrival, Nepal travellers' papers get special scrutiny [D0053].
- **Spouse visas to closed markets.** A woman with BMET clearance going to her "husband" in Oman, where worker and housemaid visas are shut, had no registered nikahnama; the same spouse-visa approval had been used for five women [D0032].
- **Students and first-time visitors.** A student with a valid Thai visa was told his trip would be cancelled until he named his university [D0034]. A first-time Malaysia traveller with a valid visa and BMET clearance was stopped and a GD (police general diary) started [D0035].
- **Risky destinations.** BMET was told to check visas strictly for Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya [D0039]. Immigration police link visa fraud by Bangladeshis to at least 23 countries, including Thailand, India, Malaysia and Saudi Arabia [D0033].

### Refused entry at the destination

Destinations also turn Bangladeshis back on arrival. Over 3,500 were refused entry and sent home in January to April 2025 [D0178]. Border officers flag visitors with little money, unclear plans or conflicting papers [D0181].

- **Malaysia (KLIA, Kuala Lumpur International Airport).** Immigration refused 80 Bangladeshis with valid tourist visas in July 2025 for lacking "satisfactory justification", put 123 on the "Not to Land" list the same month, and refused 204 on 13 August and 98 on 15 August 2025 [D0112, D0115, D0179, D0114]. Reasons given were fake hotel bookings, no return ticket, too little money and no clear purpose [D0114, D0110]. Travellers are told to carry RM500 to 1,000, and officers may look at photos on phones [D0116]. More than 200 refused at KLIA were deported in 2025 [D0113]. In 2024 a "counter setting" syndicate was reported to sell entry through bribed officers at RM200 to 2,500 a head; Malaysia's anti-corruption agency arrested 60 people, including 49 immigration officers [D0117, D0118].
- **UAE.** The UAE tightened visitor and employment visas for Bangladeshis from July 2024 [D0180].

## Illegal routes

### In one minute

Every irregular route sold in Bangladesh ends in one of a few places: a Libyan cell with a phone held to your ear while your family is asked for Tk 25 lakh, a guarded compound in Cambodia or Myanmar where you scam strangers or are beaten, a Russian trench, a Gulf or Maldives street with no permit, a Thai or Malaysian immigration depot, or an airport counter where a fake visa is found and you are sent home [I0021, I0346, I0088, I0311, I0265, I0185, I0206, I0148, I0146, I0040]. The dalal (broker) usually sells it as something legal: a "work visa", a "European work permit", a "free visa", "computer operator" or "construction" work, or "Italy, with some work in Libya first" [I0017, I0305, I0202, I0117, I0132]. Common signs: a tourist or visit visa instead of a work visa, a route through Dubai, money split before and after, and a promise that papers will be fixed on arrival [I0092, I0290, I0344, I0212]. Many victims left with real emigration clearance from BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) and still ended up in scam compounds [I0068, I0073]. Taking the boat to Italy is often the last stage of an attempt at legal migration that failed [I0019, I0282]. In Bangladesh, selling a fake job, trading visas, forging papers for smuggling and smuggling people are crimes with prison terms, and a trafficking victim cannot be charged for illegal entry or a false travel document that resulted from the trafficking [I0197, I0189, VI0007, VI0006, VI0019]. Families can complain to the CID (the police's Criminal Investigation Department), local police, BMET and the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, and BRAC and IOM (the International Organization for Migration, the UN migration agency) help with returns (see Where victims and families get help) [I0361, I0356, I0224, I0222, I0027, I0228].

### Libya to Italy by boat

#### How it is sold

The offer is Italy, and the price quoted is usually USD 10,000 (about Tk 12.4 lakh): USD 3,000 before leaving and USD 7,000 after reaching Libya [I0007, I0067]. An "all-inclusive" package is about USD 14,000 (about Tk 17.4 lakh) [I0009, I0067]. In 2026 the Italian Embassy's migration expert said young Bangladeshis pay traffickers Tk 20 to 30 lakh, and families from Cumilla and Chuadanga paid brokers Tk 18 to 34 lakh [I0010, I0023, I0022, I0347]. A common pitch is half the money before departure and the rest "after reaching Italy" [I0344]. Others are told they will travel legally, with temporary work in Libya while they wait for the "next visa"; their passports are taken on arrival [I0017]. Some are told they are going to Kuwait or Malaysia, and find out Libya was the destination only when their passports are taken in Dubai or Cairo [I0292]. A man left unpaid in Dubai was offered Libya by a facilitator there [I0283]. In 2025 two men promised jobs in Greece for Tk 15 lakh each were flown to Libya instead [I0362].

#### The path

1. **Dhaka to Dubai** on a tourist visa [I0290].
2. **Dubai to Benghazi**, directly or through Egypt; when checks at Dhaka airport tighten, groups are sent through India and Sri Lanka instead [I0003, I0291]. One 2026 victim was routed Chennai, Sri Lanka, Dubai, Alexandria, Benghazi and Tripoli; another group went through Mumbai, Saudi Arabia and Egypt [I0020, I0349].
3. **Across Libya by road**, west through Ajdabiya, Sirte and Shwerayif to Zuwara and Zawiya, the main departure points since 2023 [I0004, I0005]. Entry to Benghazi is on clearances from the Libyan National Army's Military Investment Authority, the armed force that controls the east [I0376].
4. **The boat to Lampedusa**, Italy's southern island, usually 24 to 36 hours in fair weather [I0006].

The chain is run in pieces: brokers in Bangladesh recruit and collect money, facilitators in the UAE, Egypt, Kuwait, Turkey, India and Sri Lanka move groups, and Libyan airport sponsors, armed groups, drivers and coastal smugglers control everything inside Libya [I0289].

#### What happens

- **Ransom.** Smugglers call Bangladeshis "gold" because a family at home will answer ransom calls [I0293]. Men are held, tortured and forced to phone home for more money; five Chuadanga men were held 10 months in a room with 57 others [I0346, I0343, I0345]. In July 2026 families of six men from Manoharganj, Cumilla, were asked Tk 25 lakh per person, and in November 2026 the same sum was reportedly being demanded for each of more than 300 men from Bhairab, Kishoreganj [I0021, I0024, I0348]. Bangladeshi and Libyan smugglers stage kidnappings together [I0014]. People are sold between gangs: seven Bangladeshis were sold for USD 17,000 together, and the buyer demanded USD 12,000 each [I0294].
- **Extra charges.** Charges added in Libya push the total to USD 15,000 to 17,000 (about Tk 18.7 to 21.2 lakh) [I0008, I0067]. In the last holding houses before the sea ("game houses") smugglers demand more even from people told the trip was fully paid [I0013]. Those told they can pay after reaching Europe are made to work in Libya to pay off the debt [I0016].
- **Paying does not free you.** In a 2025 case the families paid Tk 21.8 lakh and Tk 16 lakh in ransom, and the men were then handed to Libyan police and held 45 days [I0363, I0364]. People caught at sea by the Libyan Coast Guard report being handed to Bangladeshi networks on the coast who demand more [I0015].
- **Death.** In May 2020, according to Libya's then government, a trafficker's family killed 26 Bangladeshis and four Africans in Mizda, Libya [I0332]. In November 2025 a boat with 26 Bangladeshis capsized off Al Khums and four died [I0337]. In March 2026 at least 18 Bangladeshis died on a boat that left Tobruk, Libya, for Greece, carrying 38 Bangladeshis among 43 people [I0333, VI0024, I0334]. The State Minister for Expatriates' Welfare alleged that smugglers abandon weak or sick passengers at sea and often throw bodies into the water [I0335].
- **Arrival in Italy.** People landing are taken to a hotspot (a first-reception centre) for checks, photos and fingerprints [I0032]. Italy lists Bangladesh as a safe country of origin and rejected 88.66% of Bangladeshi asylum claims at first decision in 2025 [I0034, I0033]. Italy returned over 120 Bangladeshis in 2025 up to early November, including four on 31 October, two of whom had arrived from Libya without a visa a month before [I0035, I0066].
- **Coming home broke.** Men who went this way and failed have come home with debts of more than Tk 10 lakh [I0284]. Five Chuadanga families lost about Tk 1.47 crore in fees and ransom [I0343].

#### The numbers

- **Arrivals in Italy by sea:** 20,164 Bangladeshis in 2025 and 6,160 from 1 January to 6 November 2026, more than any other nationality both years [I0002, I0001].
- **Share of the route:** Bangladeshis were 50% of people leaving Libya in December 2024 and 39% in December 2025; 88% of all arrivals in Italy in 2025 left from Libya [I0054, I0056, I0057]. Frontex, the EU's border agency, counted 2,510 Bangladeshis among 8,489 crossings on the Central Mediterranean route in January to April 2026 [I0285].
- **Over the years:** 4,141 Bangladeshis arrived by this route in 2020, 7,838 in 2021 and 15,228 in 2022; Frontex counts about 110,000 from 2009 to June 2026 [I0287, I0351].
- **All irregular routes to Europe:** IOM counted 24,318 Bangladeshis reaching Europe by irregular sea and land routes in 2025, up 59% on 2024 [I0301].
- **Deaths:** 1,718 people died or went missing on the Central Mediterranean in 2024, one for every 39 arrivals, and about 765 by 7 April 2026, over 460 more than a year earlier [I0055, I0029].
- **Returns:** at least 10,000 Bangladeshis have been flown home from Libya with IOM's help since 2017, including 175 on 26 February 2026 [I0288, I0026]. In 2023 the Embassy in Tripoli could not get Libyan exit permits to send home about 2,000 people [I0018].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** under the 2026 Act, organising this journey is migrant smuggling, holding people for ransom is a form of exploitation under the trafficking offence, and the Act also covers offences committed abroad against Bangladeshis (see The law in Bangladesh) [VI0006, VI0003, VI0020].
- **Libya:** in April 2026 a Libyan court jailed four traffickers for up to 22 years [I0059].
- **Italy:** entering or staying without permission is an offence fined EUR 5,000 to 10,000 (about Tk 6.98 to 13.96 lakh) [I0049, VI0031]. If removal can't happen at once, the Questore (head of the provincial immigration police) can hold the person in a CPR, a detention centre before removal, for three months [I0051, I0052]. Expulsion brings a 3 to 5-year ban entered in the Schengen Information System, which bars every EU and Schengen country [I0047, I0048]. An Italian court did recognise one Bangladeshi kidnapped and tortured in Libya until his family paid as a trafficking victim [I0031].

#### Where to get help

- **In Libya:** the Bangladesh Embassy in Tripoli, the Foreign Ministry, the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare and IOM organise the return flights [I0027]. Libya's Anti-Illegal Migration Agency rescued 13 kidnapped Bangladeshis from a Tripoli hideout in 2026 [I0025].
- **In Italy:** Bangladesh Embassy, Rome, Via dell'Antartide 5-7, 00144 Rome, +39 06 808 3595, emergency +39 3511 766 537 [I0367, I0368]. Italy's anti-trafficking helpline 800 290 290 is free, anonymous and open 24 hours [I0369].
- **At home:** a family that paid ransom can file a case with local police, as one victim did at Haziganj police station, Chandpur, in December 2025, which led the CID to arrest a man accused of coordinating the ransom demands (see Where victims and families get help) [I0365, I0356].

### The Balkans and Eastern Europe

#### How it is sold

The product is a "work permit" or "work-permit visa" for an Eastern European country, sold by individuals and some agencies for lakhs of taka [I0305]. Victims report that the papers the agency hands over are declared fake once the file reaches the embassy, and that one permit is resold to several people with the name changed [I0306, I0307]. In April 2026 thirteen countries' missions, including Italy, Hungary and Germany, jointly warned Bangladeshis against false documents, unlicensed agents and payments to anyone claiming special access; none of them works with any agent [I0309, VI0025, I0310]. A Romanian job sold for EUR 11,000 (about Tk 15.4 lakh) turned out to be undeclared building work, 12 hours a day, 7 days a week [I0043, I0044, VI0031]. Thousands of Bangladeshis have paid up to EUR 20,000 for Italian work permits under Italy's quota scheme (Decreto Flussi) that never came [I0300].

#### The path

- **Legal entry, then onward.** Workers go legally to a cheaper EU country such as Romania, lose the job or the papers, and move on to Italy [I0299]. One man paid EUR 300 more for the Romanian nulla osta (permission) he needed to change jobs, then flew to Italy to join a cousin, Romania now being in the Schengen area (the EU zone without border checks) [I0045, I0046].
- **Overland through Bosnia.** In September 2026 Bosnian border police stopped 96 people, 36 of them Bangladeshis, crossing illegally at Bistrica near Gradiska, and seized two boats [I0280, I0281].
- **Refused, then the boat.** A Cumilla man spent three years trying Serbia, Romania, Slovenia and Belarus legally, was refused every time, then went irregularly [I0282].
- **Through Central Asia.** Brokers have taken men sold "Poland" or "Europe" to Kyrgyzstan instead (see Kyrgyzstan and Central Asia as a stepping stone) [I0122, I0118].
- **Fake visa on the plane.** In December 2022 a Bangladeshi flying from Abu Dhabi to Krakow, Poland, on a fully forged Polish work visa bought for about USD 2,000 was refused entry and charged [I0316].

#### What happens

- **Serbia:** more than 40 Bangladeshis recruited by Bangladeshi agencies reached the Linglong tyre factory in June 2025; rights groups reported possible trafficking [I0295]. Many had borrowed up to USD 10,000 and had passports taken on arrival; workers said they were paid below Serbia's minimum wage of about USD 660 a month, and their contracts named a Serbian middleman company, not the factory [I0296, I0297, I0298].
- **Eastern Europe generally:** workers find the pay lower than promised, and lower still after tax [I0308]. Employers in Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, Slovenia, Croatia, Kosovo, Albania and Montenegro are increasingly reluctant to hire Bangladeshis because many leave for Western Europe [I0303].
- **Being sent back:** about 5,000 Bangladeshis have been returned from Europe under a 2017 return agreement, most from Cyprus, Italy, Romania and France [I0060].
- **Visa refusals:** in 2025 Schengen consulates in Bangladesh refused 54.5% of applications, and the Italian ambassador links high refusals to fake documents [I0062, I0063]. A forged document leads to refusal and a flag in the Schengen system that affects all future EU travel [I0064].

#### The numbers

- **Legal flows:** 18,220 Bangladeshis migrated legally to Europe from January to 25 June 2026 [I0302].
- **The risky "new" countries:** from January 2025 to 30 October 2026, 44,122 Bangladeshis got BMET clearance for 12 countries the Ministry now restricts, 27,647 of them for Cambodia, Russia, Laos and Belarus [I0113]. Cambodia, Russia, Laos and Belarus are on the lowest tier (Tier 3) of the US State Department's 2026 Trafficking in Persons report [I0080].
- **Visa processing:** applications for several European countries are handled by embassies outside Bangladesh, mainly in India [I0304].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** on 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET to give no emigration clearance for 12 countries without the Ministry's recruitment permission or approval [I0114]. Every published list names Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Ukraine and Libya; most also name Russia and Cambodia; the rest differ between reports (Congo, Kosovo, Croatia or Bosnia) [I0114, I0115, I0116, VI0026]. Getting or selling a demand letter, visa or work permit by illegal means is a crime (see The law in Bangladesh) [I0189].
- **EU:** forging a visa or using one is punished in Italy by 1 to 6 years in prison [I0050].

#### Where to get help

Report a fake permit offer to BMET or the CID (see Where victims and families get help) [I0224, I0361].

### Visit and tourist visas sold as jobs

#### How it is sold

The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare warned in February 2025 that brokers lure people with pay and jobs and send them on tourist or visit visas to the Middle East, Russia, Greece, Libya, Lebanon, Egypt, Cambodia, Malaysia and elsewhere [I0085]. Brokers tell people they have a work visa: to Saudi Arabia, allegedly, a three-month tourist visa sold with a promised 15-month iqama (residence permit); to Cambodia, a one or two-month tourist visa sold as a two-year work visa [I0212, I0092]. Women are sent to Dubai on tourist or visit visas for promised jobs in dance bars, beauty parlours, restaurants or homes [I0352]. Some victims went with BMET clearance and still landed on a visit visa [I0076].

#### The path, country by country

- **UAE (Dubai):** the UAE restricted both work and visit visas for Bangladeshis in July 2024, and turning a visit visa into a work visa has been closed to Bangladeshis for years [I0216, I0217]. Dubai is the usual transit point to Libya and has also been used for Kyrgyzstan and Russia [I0290, I0120, I0269].
- **Saudi Arabia:** a visit visa can't be turned into an iqama; offers to convert one for a fee are fraud [I0213]. Saudi Arabia stopped new business, tourist and family visit visas for Bangladeshis from April to June 2025 after people used them to do Hajj [I0214, I0215]. An immigration official said traffickers sometimes use a valid visa for one country, such as a tourist or Umrah (pilgrimage) visa, before sending people elsewhere [I0355].
- **Oman:** since 31 October 2023 a visit visa can't be converted to a work visa in Oman; the person must leave and come back on a work visa [I0174, I0175].
- **Kuwait:** every visit visa holder is barred from working and must leave at the end of the visit [I0165]. Only some visit visas can be converted, for KD 150 (Kuwaiti dinars) [I0166].
- **Qatar:** a visit visa can't be converted inside Qatar to a work visa [I0194]. In 2014, 23 Bangladeshis brought on tourist visas with job promises had no work for three months, overstayed and were arrested [I0193].
- **Malaysia:** in October 2026 the CID arrested a Paltan travel agency owner accused of taking two people to Malaysia on visit visas with a promise of work permits, holding them and demanding Tk 5 lakh from their families [I0153]. In September 2026 Malaysian immigration detained 147 Bangladeshis on Sepang building sites where employers had misused social visit passes [I0159].
- **Maldives:** workers land through Kuala Lumpur, Chennai or Malé with a forged 12-month work visa that is really a three-month tourist visa [I0209]. The High Commission says a tourist can never get a work visa there, and trying to work on one can mean jail and a fine [I0207].

#### What happens

- **Debt bondage and sex work:** women taken to Dubai have passports and phones taken, are confined, and are given invented debts of USD 10,000 to 15,000 that some are forced to work off in sex work [I0352, I0353].
- **Ransom:** the Malaysia visit-visa case above ended in a ransom demand [I0153].
- **Overstay and deportation:** visit visas expire, the person becomes illegal and is arrested (Qatar 2014, Kuwait, Maldives) [I0193, I0165, I0207].
- **Airport collusion:** allegations reported in November 2026 say brokers pay some airport staff Tk 30,000 to 50,000 per passenger, called a "body contract", to pass immigration with incomplete papers [I0354].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** the Ministry's rule is never go to work on a tourist or visit visa on a broker's promise, and never go without BMET clearance [I0065, I0086]. Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad is punished by up to 5 years in prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [I0197, I0198].
- **Destinations:** Kuwait bars every visit visa holder from working, and in the Maldives trying to work on a tourist visa can mean jail and a fine (see The path, country by country) [I0165, I0207].

#### Where to get help

The Probash-Bondhu call centre answers workers and families 24 hours (see Where victims and families get help) [I0219].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a Dubai or Saudi tourist visa "with job" sell for in Dhaka in 2026?

### Free visas and visa trading in the Gulf

#### How it is sold

A "free visa" (also called "azad visa", meaning a free or independent visa) is a visa the worker buys that is not tied to a real job [I0202]. Under kafala, the Gulf sponsorship system, the visa is issued for one employer, and the sponsor lets the worker work elsewhere in return for a fee [I0170]. Brokers and some agencies tell workers they will easily find work and earn a lot once there, and charge Tk 4 to 7 lakh for Qatar [I0184]. OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant rights group) found free-visa migrants to the Gulf paid about Tk 5 lakh, against the government-set average of Tk 1.16 lakh [I0179]. Of those who knowingly bought one, 54% went through illegal broker networks, 31% through distant relatives, 8% through family and 7% directly through recruiting agencies [I0182]. In Kuwait an organised ring sells visas for lakhs of taka under the "free visa" name [I0161, I0162].

#### What happens

- **No job:** 43% of free-visa migrants found no work on arrival [I0168]. The visa is tied to a sponsor with no job, and most end up in low-paid work or broken contracts [I0162].
- **Paying again:** 21% had to pay about Tk 1.5 lakh more after arrival to get an iqama, and 14% could not pay, got no permit and stayed illegally [I0180, I0181]. In Qatar, sponsors sometimes demand regular payments to keep sponsoring [I0186].
- **Absconding cases:** brokers in Kuwait file false "absconding" (runaway) reports to free up visa quotas for resale, and some sponsors in Oman report free-visa workers as absconding so they are not punished if the worker is caught elsewhere [I0163, I0171].
- **No rights:** in Qatar a free-visa worker has no legal help if unpaid [I0178]. Free-visa workers there hunt for illegal day labour and many face harassment, arrest or deportation [I0185].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** BMET says there is no such thing as a free visa [I0191]. Buying or selling a visa or work permit inside Bangladesh, or getting one illegally, is punished by 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment [I0189]. These offences are non-bailable [I0195].
- **Qatar:** visa trading, and working on a free visa, carry up to 3 years in prison and a fine of up to QR 50,000 (Qatari riyals) [I0176, I0178].
- **Oman:** an Omani caught granting free visas is fined 1,000 rials, but the practice is widespread [I0172].
- **Kuwait:** the Bangladesh ambassador says there is no free visa in Kuwait law [I0160].
- **Maldives:** the High Commission says there is no free visa; working for another company brings arrest and deportation [I0206, I0208].

#### The numbers

- **OKUP 2025:** of 1,084 migrants studied, 51% travelled on so-called free visas [I0192].
- **Kuwait:** visa trading and airfares push costs to 5 to 6 times the government rate of Tk 1.06 lakh [I0164].

#### Where to get help

BMET asks anyone handed such a visa to have it and the other papers checked at BMET in Dhaka or the District Employment and Manpower Office; after it goes wrong, anyone cheated can complain to the government under the 2013 Act (see Where victims and families get help) [I0191, I0196].

### Malaysia: boats and land crossings

#### How it is sold

About 17,777 workers with BMET clearance failed to reach Malaysia before the 31 May 2024 deadline [I0371]. A Bangladeshi smuggling ring broken up in Malaysia in July 2026 is believed to have charged up to RM15,000 (Malaysian ringgit) per person and made about RM3.2 million since January 2026 [I0144]. A group caught in Thailand in April 2026 had paid brokers Tk 7 lakh each [I0147].

#### The path

1. **Fly to Cambodia** [I0148, I0145].
2. **Cross into Thailand on foot**, then be moved south in stages, kept in hotels and resorts [I0148, I0147].
3. **Cross into Malaysia** through the Kelantan border, then wait in a transit house [I0145, I0146].

The older route was by boat: in 2012 to 2015 thousands tried boat journeys, and trafficking camps and mass graves were found on the Thailand-Malaysia border [I0141]. The first mass grave, with over 30 bodies, was found in April 2015 near Wang Kelian [I0142].

#### What happens

- **Caught in Thailand:** 21 Bangladeshis found in a resort in Chana in April 2026 were charged with illegal entry and stay [I0149].
- **Caught in Malaysia:** 15 smuggled Bangladeshis were caught in a Cheras transit house in July 2026 and taken to the Putrajaya immigration depot [I0146]. Malaysia's Home Minister reported 465 deaths in immigration depots in 2021 to 2025 [I0152].
- **Going home:** Malaysia's PRM 2.0 (Migrant Repatriation Programme) lets undocumented migrants leave; Bangladeshis were the largest group in it [I0373, I0157].

#### The law

- **Malaysia:** anyone who entered unlawfully or holds forged travel documents is a prohibited immigrant [I0247]. Smuggling cases are brought under the Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007, the Immigration Act and the Passports Act [I0150].
- **Thailand:** in 2017 Thailand convicted 62 people, including nine officials, over the deaths and trafficking of Rohingya and Bangladeshis to Malaysia [I0143].

#### Where to get help

Bangladesh High Commission, Kuala Lumpur: No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur; consular +60326040949 [I0370]. In Thailand: the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok (see Where victims and families get help) [I0237].

### Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos: scam compounds

#### How it is sold

The job is "computer operator", "data entry", "call centre" or "restaurant" work, for about Tk 5.3 to 6 lakh [I0073, I0076, VI0028, VI0029]. A data-entry job was promised at USD 800 a month [I0090]. CID says traffickers advertise these jobs through fake websites, email, Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram [I0340]. Bangladeshis already working in the Gulf, including on a ship in Dubai, are recruited too with promises of higher pay [I0100, I0339]. Many went with BMET clearance [I0068, I0073]. A BRAC study found 66% of cyber-scam victims were recruited through middlemen and 98% never got the promised job [I0081].

#### The path

- **Cambodia:** the worker flies in on a one-month visit visa, not a work visa; Bangladeshi brokers meet him at the airport, keep him briefly in a hotel and sell him to a guarded compound, often Chinese-run [I0093, I0088, I0094]. Compounds are in Sihanoukville, along the Thai and Lao borders, and in rural areas [I0107].
- **Myanmar and Laos:** victims are taken across the Thai border into Myanmar, Laos or Cambodia; Myawaddy on the Thai-Myanmar border is a hub [I0104, I0342].

#### What happens

- **Sold:** men are sold to compounds for USD 1,500 to 10,000; one man was sold to three compounds in 2022 [I0089, I0097, I0102].
- **Forced to scam:** they are held at gunpoint and forced to take part in the scams [I0341]. Those who miss daily targets are beaten or given electric shocks [I0311].
- **Ransom:** a compound demanded USD 5,000 to release two youths; another family was pushed to pay Tk 95,000 more [I0098, I0095].
- **Abandoned:** a man who escaped after three months paid his own ticket and hotel home [I0069].

#### The numbers

- **Clearances:** BMET cleared 15,921 workers for Cambodia in 18 months, and BRAC says more than 1,000 of them were trapped in compounds [I0082].
- **Returns:** 583 trafficking victims came home from Cambodia in June 2026, including 221 in four days, and another 109 on 1 July [VI0027, I0075]. By April 2026 about 50 people rescued from Myawaddy, Myanmar, had been repatriated through the Embassy in Bangkok [I0342, VI0030].
- **Laos:** 7,532 Bangladeshis went to Laos in the 21 months before October 2026 [I0079]. Laos suspended recruitment, work permits and entry visas for Bangladeshi workers from 1 September to 31 December 2026 [I0078].
- **Region:** IOM helped more than 3,500 victims of forced criminality in Southeast Asia in 2022 to 2025, Bangladesh among the largest origin countries [I0108].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** the 2026 Act counts forcing a person into crimes such as online scamming as exploitation under the trafficking offence [VI0003, I0329]. Since July 2026 workers for Cambodia, Laos and Brazil need a proper work visa and full checks before BMET clearance [I0074]. Most published lists of the 12 countries needing Ministry approval since 4 November 2026 include Cambodia (see The Balkans and Eastern Europe) [I0114, I0116, VI0026].
- **Cambodia:** running a scam centre with violence or detention carries 10 to 20 years in prison [I0110]. The 2026 US Trafficking in Persons report says some senior Cambodian officials and police profit from and protect scam centres [I0112].

#### Where to get help

- **Bangladesh Embassy, Bangkok** (covers Cambodia; there is no embassy in Phnom Penh): 47/8 Ekamai Soi 30, Sukhumvit 63, Bangkok 10110; +66 (0)2 390 5107 to 8; welfare hotline +66-818-70-84-43 [I0237, I0238, I0239, I0240].
- **Cambodian police anti-trafficking hotline:** 1288 [I0236].
- **BRAC:** families who contacted BRAC got 18 men out of a Myanmar compound in March 2025, with the Foreign Ministry, the CID and the Embassy in Thailand [I0338].

### Kyrgyzstan and Central Asia as a stepping stone

#### How it is sold

The offer is Europe: a Shariatpur man paid Tk 4.8 lakh in 2025 and a Sylhet man Tk 6 lakh in 2023 to be taken to Italy or Poland [I0118, I0123]. CID says Kyrgyzstan became a trafficking route because it is seen as close to Europe [I0119]. Others are sold factory jobs and sent on 59-day tourist visas [I0121].

#### The path

1. **Dhaka to Dubai** on a visit visa [I0120].
2. **Dubai to Bishkek**, Kyrgyzstan's capital, on a tourist visa [I0121, I0118].
3. **The promised next step** (a Ukraine visa, or Italy "after some work") never comes; the Sylhet broker left for Dubai and disappeared [I0122].
4. **Overland**, some cross into Kazakhstan away from checkpoints to look for work, and have been caught as far as the Russian border [I0125, I0126].

#### What happens

- **Abandoned:** about 40 Bangladeshis were found abandoned at one Bishkek address in 2024, and had to go to Uzbekistan and back to get visas [I0129].
- **Prison and bans:** Bangladeshis caught crossing Kazakhstan to the Russian border in 2024 got six months in prison, expulsion and a five-year ban [I0126, I0127].
- **Fines and deportation:** breaking the purpose of entry, such as working on a tourist visa, is fined 10,000 som (Kyrgyz currency) in Kyrgyzstan, and in September 2025, 180 Bangladeshis were sent back [I0138, I0137].

#### The law

- **Kyrgyzstan:** a foreigner who works without a permit is to be expelled [I0250]. Its security chief says foreigners come as tourists and students and work illegally [I0249].
- **Kazakhstan:** prosecutors detained three organisers who smuggled 20 South Asians from Kyrgyzstan in November 2023 [I0128].

#### Where to get help

Bangladesh has no embassy in Kyrgyzstan; the Embassy in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, covers it, with a 24-hour emergency number +998930009780 [I0135, I0136]. BRAC asks stranded migrants to contact its Migration Welfare Centre [I0251].

### Russia: recruited into the war

#### How it is sold

The job is construction or welding, through a recruiting agency, at about 75,000 roubles a month [I0273, I0271]. Families paid about Tk 9.5 to 10 lakh, about USD 8,000 [I0271, I0273, I0267]. Survivors say brokers repeatedly promised it was not military work [I0379]. One family was offered medical school in Moscow for their son if he first served four months in the war [I0278].

#### The path

1. **Dhaka to Dubai, then Moscow**, or through other third countries [I0269, I0268].
2. **At Moscow airport**, brokers take more cash, in one case about USD 1,000 per person [I0270].
3. **Passports and phones taken**, and the men are handed to the military [I0264, I0134].
4. **Russian-language contracts** they can't read [I0268].

Some go legally on six-month work visas; when the visa ends they are offered a military contract to renew it [I0276].

#### What happens

- **Death:** 30 men flew out on 7 May 2026 [I0273]. In October 2026 the commander of the police's Special Response Battalion said 17 who arrived that May were killed on their first day of deployment after minimal training, 20 Bangladeshis were confirmed dead, and they had been used as bait to locate enemy positions [I0265, I0266]. On 9 July 2026 the Minister had told Parliament that four of the 30 had died [I0314]. A joint investigation by Fortify Rights and Truth Hounds, two rights groups, counted at least 104 Bangladeshis recruited and at least 34 killed [I0275].
- **Abuse:** survivors were sent to fight with little or no training, beaten, unpaid, kept from leaving and threatened when they tried to escape [I0272].
- **Bodies not returned:** many families still cannot get their sons' bodies home [I0279].

#### The numbers

BMET gave 7,449 clearances for Russia between 1 January 2025 and 27 October 2026 [I0274]. A UN commission found men from 29 countries recruited into Russia's forces through deception and false job offers [I0277].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** in June 2026 the government cancelled the licences of three agencies accused of sending the 30 men, and forfeited their deposits [I0158]; on 9 July 2026 the Expatriates' Welfare Minister told Parliament the three licences had been suspended [I0314]. In October 2026 police arrested two men accused of the trafficking [I0263]. Most published lists of the 12 countries needing Ministry approval since 4 November 2026 include Russia [I0114, I0115, VI0026].

#### Where to get help

A wounded survivor got home in August 2026 with help from a doctor and the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow [I0377]. BRAC's Migration Programme has asked the government to trace the Bangladeshis still in Russia [I0378].

### Student visas used as work routes

#### How it is sold

Agents tell students a visa lets them work freely, and charge families Tk 6 to 8 lakh or more, steering them to barely functioning colleges (Malaysia) [I0243, I0242]. In Jordan, brokers charged students large sums for often fake admissions [I0259].

#### What happens

- **Malaysia:** students have been moved after arrival from a university into a Bangladeshi-owned English language centre, whose students can't work [I0246]. A Student Pass allows only part-time work in semester breaks, up to 20 hours a week, in listed jobs [I0244].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** working on a study visa is working without a permit, and leads to expulsion [I0250, I0249].
- **Jordan:** since 1 April 2025 any foreign student found working is deported, and the employer is fined at least JD 800 (Jordanian dinars) per student [I0257, I0258].
- **Italy:** a student visa used only to get in is revoked if the student has not enrolled by 31 January [I0254].
- **Russia:** a medical-school offer came with four months of war service attached (see Russia: recruited into the war) [I0278].

### Forged documents and impersonation

#### How it is sold

- **Fake visas:** in May 2026 two men agreed Tk 30 lakh each for Italy, paid Tk 20 lakh up front, and were given fake Schengen visas [I0359, I0038].
- **Swapped pages:** a "page substitution" ring copies a genuine Italian visa page into a look-alike's passport [I0041]. In July 2026 the CID arrested members of a ring accused of sending a woman to Italy on another woman's visa, and seized 56 passports and 153 seals [I0042].
- **Fake certificates:** a ring sold fake police clearance certificates with counterfeit QR codes linking to a fake website [I0232]. In one study, 22% of workers said agents forged their medical certificates and 16% their pre-departure orientation certificates [I0233].
- **Changed ages:** girls have been sent abroad with ages raised through altered birth certificates [I0235].

#### What happens

- **At the airport abroad:** the two men with fake Schengen visas were detained at Rome airport for two days and deported [I0358]. In June 2026 a Biman ground officer was arrested, accused of helping them board [I0039].
- **At the embassy:** Italy suspended all Bangladeshi work nulla osta in October 2024 because of the number of false documents, and refuses or revokes a nulla osta obtained by fraud [I0255, I0256]. The Italian Embassy says forged documents mean immediate refusal, and the visa centre's rules say applicants caught with fake papers are banned from applying and reported to the police [I0252, I0253].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** making, obtaining, supplying or holding a document to help migrant smuggling carries 1 to 3 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 50,000 under the 2026 Act [VI0007]. A trafficking victim cannot be charged for getting or holding a false travel document as a direct result of the trafficking [VI0019].
- **Destinations:** Italy punishes forging or using a forged visa with 1 to 6 years in prison; Malaysia treats a holder of forged travel documents as a prohibited immigrant; Singapore jailed a worker in 2021 who bought a forged degree through an agent [I0050, I0247, I0262].

#### Where to get help

CID asks the public to report forged visas and migrant smuggling directly to it (see Where victims and families get help) [I0361].

### The law in Bangladesh

#### The 2026 trafficking and smuggling Act

The Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Prevention and Suppression Act, 2026 (Act No. 55 of 2026), passed in April 2026, repealed the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking Act 2012 and took effect at once [I0105, VI0001, VI0002, VI0032]. It replaced an ordinance of the same name, first approved by the interim government's Council of Advisers on 27 November 2025 [I0326, VI0022]. For the first time it separates trafficking and smuggling as two crimes [I0331]. It applies to anyone who commits one of its offences against a Bangladeshi abroad [VI0020].

- **Trafficking:** at least 5 years' rigorous imprisonment, up to life, and a fine of at least Tk 50,000; by an organised gang, death or life imprisonment, or at least 7 years, and a fine of at least Tk 5 lakh [VI0004, VI0005].
- **What counts as exploitation:** among other things, forced labour, debt bondage, forcing someone into crimes such as online scamming, and holding someone to demand ransom [VI0033, VI0003].
- **Migrant smuggling** (getting someone into another country illegally for gain): 3 to 10 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh; if the migrant is gravely hurt, dies, is put in danger or treated cruelly, 5 years to life [VI0006, VI0008].
- **Documents and ads:** making, obtaining, supplying or holding a document to help smuggling, 1 to 3 years; advertising for smuggling online, on social media or in print, 3 to 7 years and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [VI0007, VI0009, VI0010].
- **Victims:** a trafficking victim cannot be charged for illegal entry or stay abroad, or for getting or holding a false travel or identity document, if it was a direct result of the trafficking [VI0019]. Threatening a victim, the family or a witness carries 3 to 7 years, and pressuring a victim or family to settle 2 to 5 years [VI0011, VI0012].
- **Filing a case:** anyone can complain at a police station or to the tribunal; the police must protect the complainant and keep their identity confidential [VI0014]. All offences are cognisable, non-bailable and can't be settled privately [VI0013].
- **Powers:** on the investigator's application the tribunal can freeze bank accounts, block the sale of property, and bar an accused from leaving the country for up to 60 days; an accused who absconds can be tried in absence [VI0015, VI0023, VI0016, VI0017].
- **Courts:** the government can set up a Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Suppression Tribunal in each district, and it can try a connected offence in the same case [VI0021, VI0018]. In 2026 Justice and Care, an NGO, was supporting about 400 cases in 18 tribunals [I0330].

#### The Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013

- **False job promises (s.31):** taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, or keeping a migrant's passport or papers, is punished by up to 5 years in prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [I0197, I0198].
- **Visa trading (s.33, amended 2023):** getting a demand letter, visa or work permit illegally, or buying or selling one in Bangladesh, is punished by 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment [I0189].
- **Unregistered dalals (s.35, added 2023):** acting as an agency's sub-agent without registration is punished by 1 year in prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [I0190]. Agencies may appoint only registered sub-agents [I0204].
- **No bail:** offences under ss.31, 33 and 34 are non-bailable and can't be settled privately [I0195].
- **Complaint without a court case (s.41):** anyone cheated can complain to the government against any person, including an agency [I0196].
- **Legal aid (s.27):** migrants and people defrauded in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [I0200].
- **Mobile courts** under the Act give immediate remedies to would-be and returned migrants, free, in 7 working days [I0201].
