### Deciding

#### Which countries can I go to for work right now?

As of November 2026:
- **Saudi Arabia:** open, and the largest destination, though departures from January to 17 October 2026 were 45% below the same period of 2025 [F0016, F0015].
- **Singapore:** open; BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared 66,657 workers for it from January to 7 November 2026, the second destination [F0016].
- **Qatar:** open, the third destination, with 53,611 cleared from January to 6 November 2026 [F0017].
- **The Maldives:** issuing new work visas; 32,408 cleared from January to 7 November 2026 [F0018, F0019].
- **Kuwait:** open for company jobs; closed to new Bangladeshi domestic workers, since Kuwait's June 2026 list of approved countries leaves out Bangladesh and the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0020, F0021, F0022, F0197].
- **Jordan:** open for garment factories and for women in domestic work, closed to new recruits in most other jobs since 1 June 2026 [F0023, F0175].
- **Italy:** only through an employer in Italy who names you under the yearly quota; about 10,000 Bangladeshis got work visas in 2025 [F0176, F0177].
- **The UAE:** work and visit visas for Bangladeshis have been restricted since July 2024, with no published ban [F0178, F0179].
- **Malaysia:** a restart was announced on 15 October 2026, but the medical tests it needs had not been approved by 4 November 2026 [F0180, F0181].
- **Oman:** new visas for Bangladeshis suspended since 31 October 2023, apart from a few categories [F0182, F0183, F0184].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** BMET cleared only 11 workers from January to 9 November 2026 [F0185].
- **Cambodia:** BMET cleared none in October 2026, and the Ministry told BMET not to clear workers for 12 countries, Cambodia among them, without its own approval [F0186, F0187].

Fewer people are going. BMET says about 5 lakh workers took clearance in January to August 2026, against about 7,40,000 in the same months of 2025 [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083]. About 6,25,000 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work in 2024, over 7,55,000 in 2025 and about 2,15,000 in the first half of 2026, and recruiting agencies say the rise may not continue [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083]. Malaysia closed in June 2024 over irregularities, Oman has been effectively closed since 2024, and workers going to the UAE fell from about 1 lakh in 2023 to 47,000 in 2024 and 13,750 in 2025; analysts told Prothom Alo that these closures are a major reason workers have turned to risky countries [RU0079, RU0080]. RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a migration research group) counted 9,06,355 departures in January to November 2024, against 13,05,453 in the whole of 2023 [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. Its December 2024 report said no labour migration took place in 2024 to several countries including Oman, Bahrain, the UAE and the Maldives, that Italy was stalled by forged documents, and that Serbia was stalled because the application server was out of order [MA0078].

> **Need to know**
> - Did labour migration to the UAE stop in 2024, as RMMRU reported, or did about 47,000 workers go, as Prothom Alo reported in 2026 [MA0078, RU0079]?

Each country doc opens with its current status under "Is it open now, and who can go?" (for example, see the Qatar doc, Background, Status).

#### Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?

On 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET not to issue clearances for 12 countries without the Ministry's recruitment permission: Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [F0187]. The State Minister has named Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya as high-risk countries where visas must be strictly checked [F0012]. In Cambodia, a study by BRAC (the Bangladeshi development organisation) found 98% of returnees got no job and 90% were forced into online scams [F0189].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the list's Congo actually Kosovo, and is Cambodia on it or Croatia, as other reports of the same 4 November 2026 order give [F0188, F0187]?

#### Which countries can a woman go to?

- **Saudi Arabia:** as a domestic worker, aged 25 to 45, and she should pay nothing (see Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?) [F0190, F0191].
- **Jordan:** garment factories, through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government's recruiting agency); 12,784 women went from January to 8 November 2026, the second destination for women after Saudi Arabia [F0192, F0193].
- **Qatar:** domestic work, with the same 25 to 45 age rule for BMET clearance; in August 2025 BMET let one agency send 100 women housemaids to a Qatar employer [F0195, F0233].
- **Kuwait:** company jobs only (230 women from January to 8 November 2026); the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0196, F0197].
- **The Maldives:** women can go, but few do (148 in 2026 to 7 November) [F0198].
- **Singapore:** almost none: 126 women of 70,832 workers cleared in 2025 [F0199].
- **Oman:** 3 women cleared in 2026 to 8 November, after 6,636 in 2023 [F0200, F0201].

Fewer women go than a few years ago. BMET data show the most in one year, 1,21,925, went in 2017, and more than 1 lakh a year went until 2019; in 2023, 77,263 of more than 13 lakh workers were women [AA0028, AA0029, AA0030]. In January to November 2024, 54,696 women went, 6% of all workers and the lowest share in ten years apart from the pandemic, by RMMRU's count [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. Bangladesh began sending women workers in 1991; Saudi Arabia signed its agreement with Bangladesh in 2015, after Indonesia and the Philippines stopped sending domestic workers over allegations of abuse, and most Bangladeshi women migrants go there as domestic workers [AA0031, AA0032]. A joint secretary general of BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) said in January 2024 that Saudi employers want workers quickly once they place a demand, that delays stop the women being sent, and that employers therefore take workers from other countries [AA0045, AA0046].

> **Need to know**
> - Are UAE recruitment offices getting domestic-worker entry permits for Bangladeshi women in November 2026, or are they refused at the visa stage?

#### Is there a job for someone with no skills?

Saudi Arabia's skill certificate is not required of domestic workers, though women going to the Middle East for domestic work must take the housekeeping course [F0204, F0094]. Saudi Arabia's skill test, the SVP (Skills Verification Program, run by Takamol), is compulsory for listed trades, and the list includes street, hospital and office cleaners, loading and unloading workers and construction workers [F0202, F0203]. Qatar requires a training and test certificate only for professions its labour ministry lists [F0205]. A Singapore construction job needs a skills test at one of five centres in Bangladesh approved by Singapore's Building and Construction Authority, until the test moves to Singapore in January 2028 [F0024, F0025]. BMET's TTCs (Technical Training Centres) teach 55 trades, and project-funded courses are free and pay a monthly stipend [F0026].

#### How much does it really cost to go?

People pay several times the legal maximum (see What is the most an agency may charge me?): workers who went to five Gulf countries on a "free visa" (a visa bought from a sponsor with no real job behind it) spent Tk 4,99,802 on average, against a government-set average of Tk 1,16,224 [F0356]. By country:
- **Saudi Arabia:** about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [F0027].
- **Qatar:** Tk 4 to 7 lakh for a "free visa" [F0028].
- **Malaysia:** Tk 5.44 lakh on average in 2022-24 [F0029].
- **Singapore:** first-time workers are asked for S$9,000 to 13,000, about Tk 8.7 to 12.6 lakh, and some pay up to Tk 16 lakh [F0030, F0357, F0031].
- **Kuwait:** a visa is sold through brokers for KD 2,000 to 2,200, about Tk 8 to 9 lakh [F0032].
- **The UAE:** at least Tk 3.5 lakh for driving jobs, the State Minister said in May 2026 [F0033].
- **The Maldives:** about Tk 3 to 4 lakh in 2024 [F0034].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person, by the CID's (Criminal Investigation Department's) 2024 estimate [F0035].
- **Italy:** one worker paid an intermediary EUR 20,000 [F0036].
- **Jordan garment jobs through BOESL:** the factory pays; the worker pays Tk 200 for fingerprints and Tk 200 for the orientation, and the company repays these before departure [F0206].

#### What is the most an agency may charge me?

The government fixes the maximum by order [F0207]. If BMET has issued a permission letter for your job, the most is the amount written on it (see What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?) [F0208]. Otherwise the caps reported for each country, with the year each was set or reported, are:
- Saudi Arabia: Tk 1,65,000, set in 2016 [F0209].
- Singapore: Tk 2,62,270, as reported by Bangladesh Bank in 2019 [F0210].
- The UAE: Tk 1,07,780, set in 2017 [F0211].
- Kuwait: Tk 1,06,780, as stated by the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait in August 2025 [F0212].
- Malaysia: Tk 78,990, the cap for the 2022-24 recruitment [F0029].
- Italy: none; the 2017 caps don't cover it [F0213].

A woman going as a domestic worker may be charged nothing [F0191].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Ministry issued the new country-by-country maximums the State Minister promised in October 2026 [F0214]?

#### Which fees do I pay to the government?

The main Bangladesh-side fees (the full list for Saudi Arabia is in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Who pays what):
- OEP (Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's website) registration: Tk 200 [F0215].
- A 48-page, 5-year e-passport: Tk 4,025 (regular) [F0217].
- Police clearance: Tk 1,500 on the portal [F0218].
- Welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [F0219].
- Migrant insurance: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card (clearance) fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax at clearance: Tk 250 to 1,200, depending on the visa [F0222].

#### How much will I earn?

- **Saudi Arabia:** cleaners and general workers earn SR 800 to 1,000 a month; there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0223, F0224].
- **Qatar:** the legal minimum is QR 1,000 a month, plus QR 500 for housing and QR 300 for food if the employer doesn't provide them [F0225].
- **Malaysia:** the minimum wage is RM1,700 a month [F0226].
- **Singapore:** construction basic pay averages S$820 a month for Bangladeshis [F0227].
- **Kuwait:** the Bangladesh Embassy attests a government-contract cleaner's or labourer's visa only if it pays at least KD 90 a month [F0358, F0228].
- **Jordan garment factories:** JD 230 a month, of which at least JD 130 is cash and JD 100 is paid in kind as housing and food [F0229].
- **The Maldives:** migrant workers surveyed in 2024 averaged MVR 6,760 a month, and there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0230, F0231].
- **Oman:** a minimum of RO 90 a month was agreed between the two governments but is not rigorously enforced [F0232].

#### How long will it take to pay back what I spend?

In 2020 men took 19 months or more on average to recover what they spent [F0359]. A Saudi worker on SR 800 with free housing who buys his own food (SR 500 or more a month) and phone (SR 50 or more) keeps at most SR 250, about Tk 8,300 at Tk 33.20 a riyal [F0223, F0037]; at that rate a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes about 54 months to repay before interest, against a usual two-year contract [F0038]. Many low-skilled Gulf workers struggle to send even Tk 15,000 to 20,000 a month; at that rate the same debt takes 23 to 30 months [F0039]. Malaysia workers who paid high fees stayed in debt for two to three years (2024) [F0040]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column worked through a Saudi pest-control job at the set wage of SR 700 a month, which it gives as Tk 22,400: two years under the contract earn Tk 5,37,600, and after about Tk 2 lakh in living costs Tk 3,37,600 is left to send home [AA0201, AA0202]. By its arithmetic the worker is still about Tk 2,32,000 short of his cost after two years, and in effect has to work four years for almost no gain [AA0204].

#### Who pays for the air ticket?

It depends on the job. On Saudi jobs with a BMET permission letter, the letter can put the airfare on the employer, as Bonanza Overseas' September 2025 letter does [F0360]. On BOESL's Jordan garment jobs the factory bears all the costs [F0206]. On a visa a relative sends from Qatar, the worker usually buys the ticket through the Dhaka broker [F0041]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column says Saudi law puts the worker's visa and recruitment costs on the employer but in practice this is often not followed, and that under Malaysia's G2G (government-to-government) recruitment many workers went for just Tk 35,000 in airfare, a system it says ended because recruiting agencies opposed it [AA0205, AA0206, AA0207].

#### Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?

BOESL has sent 1.43 lakh workers in 14 years, 1.06 lakh of them to Jordan, where it sends women to garment factories at no cost [F0042, F0193]. In November 2026 it advertised 112 zero-cost jobs for skilled men at DHL in Malaysia, with applications on OEP [F0043]. Recruitment under the October 2025 Saudi agreement goes through private agencies, not BOESL [F0044], and BOESL lists no Qatar recruitment [F0045]. A police officer at an April 2026 roundtable in Cumilla said people should be encouraged to go through government channels such as BOESL, and that those who do are comparatively better off [AA0223].

### Agencies and dalals

#### How do I check whether an agency is real?

1. Ask for the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and find it on OEP's agency list at www.oep.gov.bd/agencies, which shows its name, address, office phone, licence status and validity date [F0046].
2. Read the status, not the date: a licence can show "Cancelled" while its validity date is years ahead [F0047]. Recruiting after cancellation is a crime carrying 1 to 2 years' prison [F0048]. Status can change back and forth in court: the withdrawal of 49 agencies' licences on 19 July 2026 was suspended by a High Court order on 15 September 2026, and restored when the Appellate Division's chamber court stayed that order on 30 September, according to the Attorney General [MB0096, MB0097].
3. Check the job too, because licensed agencies also cheat: workers paid agencies Tk 3 to 4.2 lakh for Qatar jobs and three of them got fake contract letters [F0049, F0050, F0051].

The most active agencies, with their cited red flags, are in the knowledge base's Recruiting agencies doc; the steps for one country are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1.

#### Is it legal to go through a dalal?

A dalal (দালাল, broker) is legal only if a licensed agency has registered and appointed him as its sub-agent [F0052, F0053]. Acting as a sub-agent without registration carries 1 year's prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh, and the agency that uses one risks 1 year or Tk 1 to 5 lakh [F0054, F0055]. Most people still go through one: 56% of 1,084 Gulf migrants in a 2025 OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) survey relied on informal or illegal agents [F0056, F0057], and in the Maldives 59% went through a sub-agent or dalal [F0058]. The broker is often a friend or relative [F0059]. Dr Selim Reza wrote in September 2026 that more than 2,000 agencies have been added to RAIMS (the Recruitment Agents Information System), that a 2024 amendment brought sub-agents and intermediaries under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act, and that a 2025 code of conduct provides for holding sub-agents to account [MB0087, MB0088, MB0089]. The Ami Probashi phone app, which works with BMET, has a free chat-based CV builder in Bangla and English, and Prothom Alo wrote in July 2025 that a proper CV reduces dependence on dalals [OA0115, OA0116].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a public list of registered sub-agents that a worker can check a dalal's name against [F0053]?
> - Was the sub-agent amendment passed in 2023 (see The law on sub-agents) or in 2024, and can a worker search RAIMS [S0093, MB0088]?

#### What is a demand letter?

A demand letter is the employer's written request for workers; for Qatar, the company or its agent files it at the Bangladesh Embassy with the government's visa approval, and the Embassy attests it [F0060]. The Bangladesh mission checks the employer and attests the job demand and visa before the agency selects workers [F0061]. Ask the agency to show you the attested copy (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4) [F0060].

#### What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?

It is BMET's approval for an agency to send a set number of workers to a named employer; it shows the job, the salary, the contract terms, who pays the migration cost, and the most the agency may take [F0233, F0234, F0208]. It also makes the agency promise, on stamp paper, to pay the worker's food, lodging, return and compensation if the job is not as promised [F0235]. BMET posts these letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, but the newest one there is dated 10 December 2025 [F0236]. A single visa (up to eight workers for one employer on one demand letter) needs no Ministry recruitment permission, so it has no such letter [F0237].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET still issue permission letters after 10 December 2025, and can a worker get a copy from BMET [F0236]?

#### A relative abroad is sending me a visa. Is that safe?

Many are bought "free visas", sold on by a sponsor who will not employ the worker (see "A free visa lets me work for anyone") [F0361]. From Cumilla and other districts about 80% go on visas from relatives and friends [F0238]. Relatives working abroad often get the visa by paying the employer or others and pass it on to people at home [F0239]. A bought visa also shows as genuine on Qatar's visa checker, so check that the company on the visa is the one you will work for and that the job matches [F0240]. Buying or selling a visa inside Bangladesh carries 2 to 7 years' prison [F0241]. What a relative may be sending is explained in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 5.

#### How should I pay the agency, and when?

Pay through the bank into the agency's own account and keep every receipt [F0242]. BMET asks people not to go to middlemen and to pay only through the proper channel against a receipt [F0243]. BMET's September 2025 permission letter for Saudi kitchen workers, for example, let the agency take its fee only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and only after the visa, clearance and flight were confirmed [F0208]. In practice most workers pay sub-agents in instalments before the visa is confirmed and get the contract only at the airport [F0244].

#### Is there a list of good agencies?

One agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment: Bonanza Overseas (RL-0579) was the Bangladeshi agency in a fair-recruitment pilot that the ILO (International Labour Organization) began in 2018 with QDVC, a construction company in Qatar [F0245]. The Recruiting agencies doc lists the most active agencies with their red flags. A valid licence proves little: the State Minister for expatriates' welfare said on 30 July 2026 that most of the roughly 3,000 licence holders are involved in irregularities, that no more than 400 or 500 licences would survive if the rules were followed, and that many agencies renew their licences without qualifying [MB0061, MB0062]. In May 2026 he said the government will introduce a grading system to identify good agencies, fix a standard migration cost, introduce a "Probashi card", bring the district manpower office, PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) and the welfare centre into one building for a one-stop service, appoint law firms to protect women workers abroad and create a fund to rehabilitate returnees [OA0193, OA0194].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the agency grading system announced in May 2026 been introduced, and where are the grades published [OA0193]?

#### The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?

1. Write down who took the money, from whom, when and how much; a Ministry joint secretary said in 2025 that action follows when these are given [F0246].
2. Apply to BMET's Director General with your proof at the workers' complaint cell; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [F0247].
3. Or send an application with proof, your address and the agency's RL number to the Ministry's enforcement branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd; action starts within 3 to 5 working days [F0062, F0063].
4. Mobile courts give immediate remedies to people about to go and returned migrants, free, within 7 working days [F0064].
5. If nothing happens, go up BMET's grievance ladder to the Ministry's Secretary and then the Cabinet Division's system at www.grs.gov.bd [F0065].

The full route is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Scams, If the agency cheats you.

#### Is cheating a migrant a crime?

Yes. Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, keeping a migrant's passport or papers, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay carries up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [F0066, F0067]. Publishing an overseas job advert without government or BMET approval carries up to 1 year [F0068]. Migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [F0069]; OKUP gives free legal advice and help filing cases on 01842773300 [F0070].

### Papers

#### How do I get a passport, and what does it cost?

Apply for an e-passport exactly as your NID (national ID card) reads; anyone over 20 must use the NID [F0071]. A 48-page, 5-year e-passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express) [F0217]. Pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay, or at a bank [F0072], and bring the original NID to the passport office [F0073]. Delivery after biometrics is within 15 working days (regular), 7 (express) or 2 (super express) [F0074]. Brokers outside passport offices sell a quicker way through the queue (see The passport office) [OA0089, OA0090].

#### How much validity must my passport have?

It depends on the step: the police clearance needs at least 3 months [F0218], and the Qatar Visa Center (QVC, where Qatar-bound workers sign the contract, give fingerprints and take the medical) books only passports with more than 195 days left [F0367, F0075]. Immigration's hotline gets calls from people asking whether four months is enough to travel [F0002]. Since 19 August 2026 the Special Branch runs a 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport, 01320007588, for questions on passport validity and travel bans [F0001].

#### How do I get a police clearance certificate?

Apply at pcc.police.gov.bd: register, fill in your details and addresses, upload the scans and submit [F0218, F0076]. Pay Tk 1,500 by treasury challan at Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank, or online by card [F0218]. The police verify you at your present address; track it by texting "PCC S <reference number>" to 26969, and collect it with a copy of your passport [F0077]. The PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825 [F0077]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2. Brokers sell forged certificates: in 2026 a Chattogram family paid Tk 1,23,000 for a "quick" one that turned out to be fake (see Forged documents and impersonation) [OB0014, OB0015].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the fee Tk 1,500 (the portal) or Tk 500 (Bangladesh Police's guidance) [F0218, F0078]?

#### What is attestation, what is an apostille, and which do I need?

An apostille is a single certificate from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) that member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention accept; it is applied for on mygov.bd at Tk 200 a page and takes 5 working days [F0079, F0080]. MoFA lists 114 countries that accept it, including Oman, Saudi Arabia and Italy, but not Qatar [F0081, F0082]. For a country that doesn't accept it, each document is first verified by its issuing body and then attested by MoFA: SSC and HSC (Secondary School Certificate and Higher Secondary Certificate) certificates by the Education Board, then the Ministry of Education, then MoFA [F0083, F0084, F0085]. The Qatar steps, including legalisation by the Qatari mission, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2, item 3; the apostille steps in the Italy doc, Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 4.

#### How do I register on OEP?

Registration on OEP is the first step: agencies must pick workers from BMET's database, and BMET clears no one outside it [F0248, F0249]. Open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp in Google Chrome, type your details exactly as on the NID, enter the code sent to your mobile, fill in the form, and pay by bKash, Nagad or another mobile wallet [F0250, F0251]. The fee is Tk 200 [F0215]. Download your Registration Card at the end [F0250]. If it fails, call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [F0252]. The DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office) also does registration and fingerprinting, and gives a three-day pre-decision training on legal migration, avoiding brokers and safety; Sylhet's DEMO said in April 2026 it had registered and fingerprinted 35,290 people in 2025 and 6,290 up to March 2026, and that many people fall in with brokers before the training [LB0232, LB0233].

#### What is the medical, and how do I book it?

For the Gulf countries it is the Wafid check, booked online and done at a medical centre assigned by your city; people still call it "GAMCA", after the system Wafid replaced [F0253, F0254]. Book the slip yourself at wafid.com: USD 10 for a centre assigned to you, or USD 25 to choose the centre and date [F0255]. The whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 [F0256], yet most workers pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 [F0257]. Check the result on wafid.com's status search; it can take up to 26 days [F0258, F0259]. Qatar-bound workers take their medical at the QVC instead (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4); the Wafid steps are in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

#### Can I pay to be passed "fit" in the medical?

The Daily Star reported in July 2025 that agencies offer "fit" packages, Tk 20,000 against a Tk 10,000 standard one [F0260], and that a syndicate reportedly charges Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed fit certificate [F0261]. A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [F0262]. The same report said the approved centres are allegedly controlled by a few owners who issue fake certificates [F0263].

#### What if I'm declared unfit?

You can't retake the Wafid test for three months [F0086]. If you think the result is wrong or the centre demanded extra money, complain in writing to the Gulf Health Council within two weeks of the test; anonymous complaints are refused [F0087, F0088]. Overcharging can also be reported to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's enforcement branch with your receipts and the centre's name and address (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4) [F0089].

#### Do I need a skill test?

- **Saudi Arabia:** the SVP is compulsory for listed trades; it costs USD 85 per attempt, runs at 27 TTCs and BRTC Gazipur (the Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation's training institute), and a score of 50% passes [F0090, F0091, F0092, F0093]. A test centre had opened in Cox's Bazar by June 2026, and the principal of the Ramu TTC said passing makes a Saudi job easier to get, with a chance of higher pay; his centre runs pre-departure training and three-month technical courses [MB0040, MB0041]. Steps: the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 3.
- **Singapore construction:** a skills test at a Bangladesh test centre until January 2028, when workers will be tested in Singapore after arrival [F0025].
- **Qatar:** only for professions on the labour ministry's list [F0205].
- **Domestic workers:** no Saudi skill test, but women must take the housekeeping course (see What is the housekeeping course?) [F0204, F0094].

There is no way to pass the Saudi test by paying extra, and a worker caught paying has the account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [F0095, F0096].

#### How do I join a TTC course?

Log in on OEP, open Training portal, then Training, then Trade/Skills, choose your division, district and centre, pick the trade and press Apply [F0097]. Pay the BMET fee for short courses against a money receipt; project-run courses are free and pay a stipend, and courses last from 3 days to 4 years [F0026]. If anyone offers to get you into or through a TTC for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0098]. Courses vary by centre: the Sylhet TTC said in April 2026 it runs regular Japanese, Italian, Korean and Arabic language courses, and, with ILO (International Labour Organization) support, level-three courses in electrical installation and maintenance and in food preparation that combine language with the trade, with a possibility of work in Italy afterwards [LB0236, LB0237]. Few migrants train first: a Sylhet TTC senior instructor said many who most need training don't come, students with no immediate plan to go abroad come instead, and in the centre's experience about 99% of people go abroad without acquiring a skill [LB0238, LB0239]. The Sandwip TTC trained 750 people in 2025, and BMET data show 2,080 people from Sandwip went abroad that year [FV0109, FV0110, FV0111].

#### What is the pre-departure orientation (PDO)?

It is a compulsory 3-day course at one of 70 training institutes, booked on OEP; the institute sets the fee and gives a money receipt [F0094, F0099]. Booked through the Ami Probashi phone app, the enrolment fee is Tk 100 plus a Tk 100 platform charge [F0216]. The general secretary of the Ovibashi Nari Sramik Federation (a federation of women migrant workers) said in May 2026 that the three-day training at the district employment office before departure mainly covers first notions of the language, culture and rules, with no in-depth training on the reality of the work, its risks or the long term; a UNDP gender expert said language, culture and practical work skills can't be learnt in three days [OA0218, OA0219].

#### How do I check my visa is real?

Take copies of the visa and the passport's visa page to BMET in Dhaka or your district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [F0362]; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [F0101]. BMET checks Saudi, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and UAE work visas online, and has a visa-check app on Google Play [F0101]. The State Minister said in 2026 that BMET can verify visas online for only four or five countries [F0011]. Each country doc gives its own online check (for example, see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7).

### Loans

#### Can I get a government loan to go abroad?

PKB, the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days of complete papers [F0113]. The same loan is offered for a re-entry visa over 2 years, with instalments starting after a two-month grace period [OA0110]. Women migrants have a separate loan on the same terms [F0114]. You must be 18 to 60, not in default, and live permanently in the branch's area with your guarantors [F0115]. You need two guarantors, one financially solvent and one family member [F0116]. A Prothom Alo guide of July 2025 lists the papers as your photo, voter ID, address, certificates, copies of the passport, visa and smart card, one guarantor's papers and three bank cheques, with no collateral, and says you must first open an account at the bank [OA0108, OA0109]. A senior PKB officer in Cumilla said in April 2026 that an applicant needs a BMET card, a valid visa, a passport, an NID from the branch's area and a capable guarantor who will repay if needed [AA0237]. Helpline: 16238 [F0117].

> **Need to know**
> - Does PKB ask for two guarantors or one [F0116, AA0237]?

Most borrowers need Tk 3 to 4 lakh, with a monthly instalment of about Tk 14,000, and many take four or five months or longer to find work abroad, while the loan weighs on them, PKB's acting regional head in Sylhet said in April 2026 [LB0242]. PKB gave migration loans to 2,63,689 people from 2011-12 to June 2025, Tk 384 crore in all, its deputy managing director said in August 2026 [AB0131, AB0132].

#### When can I apply for the PKB loan?

Only once you hold the visa and the BMET smart card, near the end of the process [F0118]. The bank doesn't arrange visas, and finding guarantors stops many people from borrowing [F0119, F0120]. Branches are few: Cumilla's 17 upazilas (sub-districts) have 4 PKB branches, and the Tomson Bridge branch serves 9 upazilas with three officers, a senior PKB officer said in April 2026; she said staff shortages make giving and recovering loans hard, people often complain that the bank won't lend or asks for extra papers, and some borrowers' late repayment makes new lending harder [AA0238, AA0239, AA0240, OA0183, OA0184]. You can apply at the branch or online on mygov.bd, paying nothing when you submit; processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each come after the decision [F0121, F0122].

#### Should I borrow from a moneylender?

In a 2020 government survey, 81% of men had borrowed to go abroad [F0123]. In a 2020 IOM (International Organization for Migration) survey, most family loans were interest-free, NGO and bank loans carried 10-15%, and 62% of moneylender debt carried 50-150% interest [F0124]. A microcredit NGO may charge at most 24% a year [F0125]. A quarter of borrowers risk losing the collateral they pledged if they can't repay [F0264], and families mortgage land and sell cattle: a Natore farmer mortgaged his land and sold four cows, then could not fly [F0265]. A former expatriates' welfare secretary said what workers send home often doesn't even cover the interest [F0014].

#### Can I borrow abroad?

In Singapore, a worker found borrowing from an unlicensed moneylender has his Work Permit revoked and is sent home [F0266]. Many workers in Malaysia borrow at 10% a month to renew their permits, an economist of the CPD (Centre for Policy Dialogue, a Dhaka think tank) said in October 2025 [F0267].

### At the airport

#### What should I carry?

Before you go, check you have [F0268]:
- a valid passport;
- the work visa;
- the medical certificate;
- the demand letter;
- the contract attested by the destination country's embassy;
- the agency's name, address and phone;
- the BMET smart card;
- the air ticket.

Keep one set of copies at home and at least three with you [F0269]. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know: drug offences abroad can carry the death penalty [F0270]. Airport police at Sylhet said in April 2026 that rings target first-time travellers to carry drugs or smuggled goods, and many don't realise they are joining a crime ring [LB0226]. In two cases Amnesty International documented in Saudi Arabia, the accused said they had been forced to carry the drugs, but the court neither examined nor accepted that [OB0058].

#### What does immigration ask?

Immigration police ask about your purpose and family, and they compare your visa, BMET clearance, papers and answers [F0271, F0004]. A woman with BMET clearance who said she was joining her husband in Oman was questioned closely, because Oman's general worker visas and women's domestic-worker recruitment are closed, and was stopped when the same spouse-visa approval turned out to have sent five women to Oman [F0008]. The Special Branch's 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport is 01320007588 [F0001].

#### Why do people get offloaded?

Immigration police can stop a traveller they doubt from flying and stamp "offload" in the passport [F0271]. The usual reasons are a visa that doesn't match the BMET clearance, fake papers, a breach of visa conditions, or answers that don't match the papers [F0004]. The Special Branch turned back 81,500 passengers from 2021 to June 2026, 7,819 of them in the first half of 2026 [F0005, F0006]. People with valid papers have been stopped too: a man with a valid Malaysia visa and BMET clearance was turned back and then let through after a senior officer intervened [F0009]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of people stopped unfairly are true [F0010].

#### Who helps me at the airport?

WEWB's welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards, help returnees with customs and lost luggage, send the sick to hospital by ambulance and take complaints from abused or cheated returnees [F0102, F0272, F0273]. The Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport gives departing and returning workers a short stay; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd [F0274]. The desk is open 24 hours, the Civil Aviation Authority said in August 2026 (see At the airport: the welfare desk) [AB0125].

### In the destination

#### My salary isn't paid. What do I do?

The routes:
- **Qatar:** complain to the labour ministry, which must first try to settle the dispute; a Qatari official advised complaining within a month, because 3 to 4 months' unpaid wages become hard to recover [F0275, F0276]. An unpaid worker may ask to change employer or end the contract, with the journey home at the employer's cost [F0277].
- **Saudi Arabia:** file a friendly-settlement claim with the labour ministry within 12 months; unsettled claims go to the labour court [F0363, F0364]. After 3 months unpaid you may move to another employer without consent [F0279].
- **Every country:** your family can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-1 branch in Dhaka with proof of your status, the employer's name and address and the amounts owed; it is free and the Ministry writes to the labour wing within 7 working days [F0280].

The steps for each country are under "If you aren't paid" in its doc (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, If something goes wrong, If you aren't paid, are abused or your passport is taken).

#### My employer has taken my passport. Is that allowed?

No, though it is common:
- **Qatar:** the employer must return it once the residence permit is done, unless you ask in writing for the employer to keep it, and hand it over whenever you ask; keeping it is punishable by a fine of up to QR 25,000 [F0281, F0282].
- **Saudi Arabia:** your passport and iqama (residence card) may never be withheld [F0283].
- **The UAE:** passport confiscation is banned, and you need no employer permission to leave the country [F0284].
- **Kuwait:** keeping it is illegal but widespread; complain to the PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) office where your residence was stamped [F0285, F0286].
- **Singapore:** employers should not keep workers' passports, yet over 31% of Work Permit holders didn't hold their own in 2024 [F0287, F0288].

In Bangladesh, withholding a migrant's passport without lawful reason carries up to 5 years' prison [F0066, F0067].

#### Can I change my employer?

- **Qatar:** since 2020 the law lets you move without the employer's consent, under the labour ministry's rules [F0289].
- **Saudi Arabia:** after 12 months, with 90 days' notice, if your contract is on Qiwa (the labour ministry's platform); sooner if you go 3 months unpaid or your work permit or visa expires [F0290, F0279]. One worker wrote in 2025 that he found out how for himself, moved to another company and had worked there about four years on good pay [AA0166].
- **The UAE:** transfers between companies have been restricted for Bangladeshis since July 2024 [F0291].
- **Kuwait:** normally after one year and with the employer's consent [F0292].
- **Malaysia:** the PLKS (Temporary Employment Visit Pass, the work pass) doesn't let you change employer or sector [F0293].
- **Singapore:** you may work only for the employer and in the job on your Work Permit [F0294].
- **Jordan garment factories:** only to another garment factory [F0295].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** the permit can't be moved to another employer [F0296].
- **Italy:** losing or leaving the job doesn't cancel your permit [F0297].

In Saudi Arabia you may not be employed in a profession other than the one on your work permit [F0298].

#### I arrived and there is no job, or the visa was fake. What now?

This is the most common failure of a bought visa: in OKUP's 2025 survey, 21% of free-visa workers paid about Tk 1.5 lakh more after arriving to get a work permit, and 14% never got one and stayed on illegally [F0299, F0300]. BMET says 90% of migrants' complaints in 2025 concerned Saudi Arabia, mostly no iqama, no work or unpaid wages [F0301]. The embassy can advise and help in a labour dispute (in Qatar, for example) [F0302], and your family can complain about the agency in Bangladesh (see The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?) [F0247]. If you can't get home, a relative can ask the Ministry for help to bring you back [F0126].

#### What happens if I overstay?

- **Saudi Arabia:** without a valid iqama you are undocumented, and police detain and deport undocumented workers who go out [F0127, F0366]; overstaying a visit or Umrah visa brings a 3 to 5 year ban [F0128].
- **The UAE:** AED 50 a day after the grace period that follows the end of a residence permit [F0129].
- **Qatar:** overstaying a visit visa costs QR 200 a day, up to QR 12,000 [F0130].
- **Cambodia:** USD 10 a day, and deportation can take more than six months [F0131].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** a fine, and an expulsion brings a 2-year entry ban [F0132, F0133].

Countries sometimes open grace periods: Qatar gave three months from 9 February 2025 for overstayers to leave [F0134]. How to get home is under "Going home without valid papers" in each country doc.

#### How do I send money home safely?

Use a bank, a licensed exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, not hundi (unlicensed money agents) [F0135]. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with another 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps it [F0136, F0137]. Sending more at once costs less: in April to June 2026 the average cost was 2.94% for up to USD 200 and 0.50% above USD 500 [F0138]. For bKash, give the partner branch the receiver's bKash number and full registered name [F0139]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [F0140]. The channels for each country are in its doc, under Sending money home.

#### Is hundi illegal?

Yes, in Bangladesh sending money outside banking channels is a punishable offence [F0141]. Abroad it can cost you the job: in Singapore a worker involved in hundi has his permit revoked [F0142].

#### Who can I call for help while abroad?

- Probash-Bondhu, WEWB's 24-hour call centre: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [F0143].
- The Ministry's Complaint Resolution Cell: minister.expat@gmail.com [F0144].
- The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in your country (for example, in Saudi Arabia, 8001000125 free) [F0145].

The labour wing's numbers are under Contacts in each country doc.

### Problems and coming home

#### What does the migrant insurance cover?

Workers who left with BMET clearance from 1 January 2023 are insured for Tk 10 lakh by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC), for workers aged 18 to 55, for a one-time Tk 1,000 [F0324]. The cover lasts 5 years from the smart card's date [F0220]. It pays the full Tk 10 lakh on death or total permanent disability, 10% to 90% for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going [F0325]. Job loss is not covered if you went entirely at the employer's cost [F0326]. WEWB helps the worker or family claim [F0327].

#### If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?

1. **At the airport:** a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial for every body that arrives, free, collected within 3 months of the body's arrival [F0328].
2. **From the Welfare Board:** a Tk 3 lakh grant, if the worker had BMET clearance or membership and died in the country the clearance was for [F0104, F0329]. If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the body was buried abroad, apply within 1 year [F0330].
3. **From the insurance:** Tk 10 lakh [F0324].
4. **From the employer:** labour wings recover death compensation, wages and benefits, and the Board pays the heirs; 1,852 cases brought in Tk 127.6 crore in 2023-24 [F0331].

The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents [F0332]. The Cumilla Probashi Kallyan Centre's assistant director said in April 2026 that help for bringing a body home goes to BMET card holders, that a free ambulance takes the body from the airport, and that Tk 25,000 is paid after the body arrives and up to Tk 3 lakh in eligible cases [AA0233]. Families are often not told when a worker dies or goes missing abroad, a Cumilla advocacy forum's president said [AA0253]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the airport payment for transport and burial Tk 35,000 or Tk 25,000 in 2026 [F0328, AA0233]?

#### How is the body brought home?

The family applies to the Welfare Board, which takes their wish (body home or burial abroad) to the embassy and follows the case, free [F0333, F0334]. The Ministry also brings home bodies of irregular, undocumented and destitute workers: the next of kin applies with the cause of death, proof of status and the death certificate; free, 7 working days [F0335]. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports is available on request [F0336]. The Minister said in August 2026 that the government brings home the body of any Bangladeshi who dies abroad, whether the person went legally or not, and gives the family financial help and help with the children's education [AB0102]. Bodies can take months: eight Bangladeshis who died on a boat off Tunisia in February 2024 reached Dhaka on 2 May 2024, and went to the Dhaka Medical College morgue for post-mortem before being handed to their families [LB0025, LB0026].

#### Why do so many families get no compensation from the employer?

Many deaths are recorded as "natural causes": 71% of deaths of Bangladeshis in Qatar from November 2016 to October 2020, by Amnesty International's reading of Bangladeshi government records [F0337]. Amnesty found in 2021 that without investigation or post-mortem, this rules out compensation from employers [F0338]. The Welfare Board's grant is paid anyway [F0104].

#### What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?

The Welfare Board asks the embassy to bring a sick or injured worker home on the worker's or family's application [F0339]. Back home, workers with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment, applying within 1 year of return [F0340, F0341]. The insurance pays for permanent disability (see What does the migrant insurance cover?) [F0342].

#### What help is there when I come back?

Airport welfare desks give food and water to returnees from jail, help with customs and lost luggage, and take complaints [F0272, F0273]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [F0102]. PKB's rehabilitation loan lends returnees up to Tk 50 lakh for a project at 9% simple interest, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral [F0343]. It needs a trade licence and a guarantor, and in 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees got PKB's special reintegration loan [F0344, F0345]. Prothom Alo reported in March 2025 that few workers benefit from the projects begun for returnees after the pandemic [AA0142]. Other lenders, the RAISE reintegration project and what returnee leaders say: see Help after you get home [AB0110, AB0136, OA0221].

#### What can I bring home without paying duty?

Under the Baggage Rules 2025 a returning worker aged 12 or over may bring up to 65 kg free, plus 35 kg more of clothes and personal items [F0346, F0347]. One of each of a list of goods is duty-free, including a laptop, an oven, a rice cooker, a fan and a TV up to 29 inches [F0348]. You may bring 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one, the new one once a year [F0349]. Bigger items such as a fridge or air conditioner pay a fixed duty [F0350]. A Bangladeshi passport holder may bring no alcohol [F0351]. The full list is in the Qatar doc, Coming home, What you can bring home.

#### How much gold and cash can I bring?

Up to 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery in all, declared, once a year, with no more than 12 pieces of one type, is duty-free [F0352]. One gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) is allowed once a year if declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; anything more, or a hidden bar, is confiscated [F0353, F0354]. Up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency needs no declaration [F0154].

> **Need to know**
> - Do customs officers still apply USD 5,000, the figure on Bangladesh Customs' own page [F0155]?

#### Is there help for my children?

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC level) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [F0156].

### Myths

#### "A free visa lets me work for anyone."

Brokers sell it as "go and work wherever you like," for Tk 5 to 7 lakh [F0157]. In fact a Saudi employer may not employ a worker in a profession other than the one on the work permit [F0298], and buying and working on a free visa is illegal and punishable in Qatar [F0158]. The company named can cancel the visa at any time, making the worker illegal [F0159]. Of 10 men one Cumilla broker sent to Saudi Arabia, three got a proper job with a contract (2024) [F0160].

#### "A visit visa can be changed to a work visa there."

- **Saudi Arabia:** a visit visa can't be converted to an iqama, and offers to convert one for a fee are fraud [F0161].
- **Qatar:** you must leave and re-enter on a work visa [F0162].
- **Oman:** conversion inside the country stopped for all nationalities on 31 October 2023 [F0182].
- **The UAE:** a visit visa gives no right to work, and working on one brings fines [F0163].

Working on a tourist or Umrah visa in Saudi Arabia brings a ban of 3 to 10 years [F0128].

#### "The agency guarantees the job."

The Bangladesh High Commission in the Maldives warned in October 2026 against paying anyone who promises a guaranteed job or a quick work permit [F0164]. A Bangladeshi construction worker in the UAE told a 2019 study that the contract signed in Bangladesh differs from the one the company keeps [F0165]. In Cambodia, 98% of returnees BRAC studied found no job [F0189].

#### "Pay a little extra and the medical will be fit."

A bought "fit" result can still get you deported: a worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and sent home [F0262].

#### "Pay extra and you'll pass the skill test."

There is no way to pass Saudi Arabia's SVP for extra money; a worker caught paying has the account locked [F0095, F0096].

#### "Saudi Arabia abolished the kafala."

The kafala (sponsorship system) still stands: the October 2025 story began in an article by a UK think tank and cited no Saudi source [F0166].

#### "The iqama is the worker's cost."

The employer pays the iqama, the work permit and their renewals [F0167]. A Bangladesh embassy official confirmed the employer should pay, though employers push it onto workers [F0168].

#### "If I have a job, my visa is safe."

In the UAE an employer can cancel a visa online without the worker knowing [F0169]. The UAE began cancelling many Bangladeshis' visas in July 2026, and on 13 October 2026 the Law Minister said 5,000 had been cancelled [F0170, F0171].

#### "Malaysia has opened again."

In early September 2026 the Ministry warned that the process of sending workers to Malaysia was not final, that it would be announced by notice, and that no one should pay anyone before then [MB0076, MB0077]. Recruitment was announced on 15 October 2026, but on 4 November 2026 the minister said the medical tests it needs had not yet been approved [F0180, F0181]. BOESL ran its own call for 112 DHL jobs (see Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?) [F0043].

#### "Oman is opening soon."

In August 2026 the Bangladesh Embassy in Muscat said Oman had made no formal announcement on recruiting Bangladeshis, and in September 2026 the State Minister said visas remain restricted [F0172, F0184].

#### "Kyrgyzstan is a door to Europe."

A man who paid a broker Tk 4.8 lakh on a promise of Italy was taken to Bishkek and spent 44 days in a Central Asian prison [F0173, F0174].

### Passport and police clearance

#### The passport office

**The rule.** An e-passport (48 pages, 5 years) costs Tk 4,025 regular, Tk 6,325 express or Tk 8,625 super express, VAT included [D0024]. Regular delivery is within 15 working days of biometric enrolment, express within 7 and super express within 2 [D0025]. The application must match the NID (national identity card) or online birth certificate exactly, and anyone over 20 must use the NID [D0026]. The head of the Sylhet divisional passport office said in April 2026 that the regular fee is Tk 4,025 for five years and Tk 5,750 for ten years, that super express costs Tk 10,350, and that with super express someone who gives biometrics in the morning can get the passport in Dhaka by the afternoon [LB0234]. He said anyone 18 or over applies with the NID and anyone younger with a birth registration, pays the fee at a bank and gives fingerprints [LB0235]. The Department of Immigration and Passports' additional director general said in August 2026 that super express passports are being delivered within 24 hours or two days [AB0122].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the super-express fee Tk 8,625 or Tk 10,350, and is Tk 10,350 the ten-year fee [D0024, LB0234]?

**One person, one passport.** Since e-passports began in 2020, no passport is issued without online verification of the NID and birth registration data, and the gaps that once let some people hold more than one passport are now easily detected, the Cox's Bazar regional passport office said in June 2026 [MB0037]. Because an e-passport takes all 10 fingerprints, the same person can no longer get a passport under a different identity, and there is no scope to issue one without valid information and papers, even to someone who went abroad by an illegal route, the department's additional director general said in August 2026 [AB0119, AB0120, AB0121, AB0123]. He said the department has cases of people who took passports by repeatedly changing their name, date of birth and other details, which is reducing foreign embassies' and immigration authorities' acceptance of Bangladeshi passports [AB0118].

**What happens.** In TIB's National Household Survey 2025 (November 2024 to October 2025), passport services were the most corrupt sector: 84.4% of households using them met corruption and 76.6% paid bribes or unauthorised money [D0134, D0135]. The average bribe was Tk 4,532, and 39.7% reported harassment by brokers ("dalals") [D0136]. Most irregularities happened while preparing and submitting the application (76.8%), and the biometric stage carried the highest average bribe, Tk 6,680 [D0137]. People reissuing an old MRP (machine-readable passport) paid the most, Tk 12,444 on average [D0138]. Barishal (93.3%) and Sylhet (91.7%) divisions were the worst [D0139]. TIB says that despite online services many people still depend on brokers, which keeps bribery alive [D0146].

**How the broker trade works.** At the Mymensingh regional passport office, a file sent through a dalal is called a "channel file" (চ্যানেল ফাইল); three dalals told Prothom Alo in 2024 that every shop outside the office does them for Tk 2,500 above the government fee, of which Tk 1,200 goes to the passport office and the rest to the dalals [OA0050, OA0051]. By their account, a fake "nationality and character certificate" in the name of Mymensingh City Corporation is attached to the file with a code on it; the clerk taking applications knows from the code whose dalal sent it and accepts it without scrutiny, the office keeps a daily count of coded forms, and an office employee collects the money by that count every Wednesday [OA0052, OA0053, OA0054]. The dalals sit in at least 30 computer, photocopy and online-application shops in front of the Mymensingh office in Maskanda, having moved off the street because of occasional raids, and by their account they take at least Tk 5 lakh a day [OA0057, OA0058, OA0059]. In Gazipur in 2025, 25 to 30 shops on both sides of the road in front of the office fill in forms and deposit the bank fee themselves [OA0094]. Applicants there said applying alone meets harassment on various pretexts while through a dalal everything is done at once for Tk 1,000 to 1,500; a reporter saw people wait about an hour for document checks and two to two and a half hours for fingerprints and photo, while those who paid the dalal went straight to the fingerprint and photo room [OA0089, OA0090].

**Cases:**
- **24 April to 16 May 2024, Mymensingh.** Azharul Islam of Tarakanda waited an hour and a half in line and was refused over an error in his papers, failed on two days with new errors found each time, and refused to use a dalal; on 28 April he asked the office's deputy assistant director why his application was not taken when all papers were in order, the officer accepted it quickly, and he got his passport on 16 May [OA0060, OA0061, OA0062].
- **4 May 2024, Mymensingh.** A reporter saw Hashem Ali of Phulpur's application accepted without scrutiny, though his nationality and character certificate had blank ward and neighbourhood fields and an unidentified signature, and carried a handwritten code of three capital letters and the number 53 beside the serial number, a code the report says the dalal gives [OA0055, OA0056]. ### BMET clearance and the smart card

#### The rule and the fee

The emigration clearance, printed as a smart card, is BMET's permission to leave for work [D0001]. If an agency recruited you, it applies; if you found the visa yourself, you apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [D0001, D0004]. Either way it takes 3 working days [D0001, D0004]. Fees (the welfare, insurance and smart card fees by bank pay order, the tax by treasury challan) [D0002]:
- welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [D0007];
- insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once [D0015];
- clearance (smart card) fee: Tk 250 [D0005];
- source income tax: Tk 400 for an unattested single visa, Tk 250 attested, Tk 500 for a Saudi visa; Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [D0003].

For women going as domestic workers, BMET needs proof of age 25 to 45 and a guardian's no-objection letter [D0002].

- In 2016 only 301 workers used BMET's one-stop service to get their own clearance [D0152].
- In late 2023 and January 2024, smart cards were issued through BMET's own system for 20 applications that had been rejected, and for 24 workers with no matching data on BMET's server (see Fake BMET cards and fake clearance) [OA0003, OA0016, OA0017].
- On 3 July 2025 the ACC filed a case against four BMET officials and the owners or officials of five agencies; it says its 29 May 2025 raid on BMET found clearances for Gulf domestic work approved on another woman's passport number without a check against BMET's database, and issued to four women under 25 [D0154, D0155, D0156]. #### Offloading: who decides and on what law

Immigration at Bangladesh's airports is run by the Special Branch of Bangladesh Police under the Ministry of Home Affairs, headed by an Additional Inspector General of Police (Additional IGP) [D0059, D0060, D0061]. "Offloading" is immigration's refusal to let a checked-in passenger leave; in cases reported in 2021 it was marked with an "offload" stamp or note in the passport [D0082, D0086]. SB lists the laws it applies, including the Passport Order 1973 and the Emigration Ordinance 1982 and Rules 2002 [D0063]. The Passport Order bars leaving without a valid passport or travel document, and lets a passport be impounded if a court has barred its holder from leaving [D0064, D0065]. The Ordinance, as SB quotes it, makes emigration for work lawful for a citizen with a work permit, employment visa or appointment letter, or selected through a recognised agent, and punishes departing otherwise with up to one year's simple imprisonment or a Tk 5,000 fine [D0066, D0067]. In the three women's case below, their lawyers argued that an adult woman's marital status, or travelling without a parent or guardian, cannot by itself be a lawful ground to stop her [D0197].

#### Who gets stopped

Reported reasons include mismatched visas, clearance discrepancies, forged documents and conflicting answers at the interview [D0031]. Cases show who else gets stopped:
- **Women travelling alone.** Women report officers asking whether their husband knows, and phoning husbands or parents to check [D0056, D0049]. A rights activist says officers are most suspicious of women going to Thailand or Nepal [D0057].
- **Nepal.** Police officers told Prothom Alo that Nepal has been flagged as a trafficking route and, because it gives visas on arrival, Nepal travellers' papers get special scrutiny [D0053]. A women's rights lawyer, Salma Ali of ATSEC South Asia, said in July 2026 that a suspicious passenger can be spotted in minutes, and that a young woman bound for Nepal had recently been suspected and was, in her words, rescued and brought back [AB0060].
- **Tourist-visa travellers going for work.** APBn's DIG (Operations) said in August 2026 that officers can often tell when someone on a tourist visa is really going for work, and then verify and, if needed, report or try to stop the person; passengers are also checked against a database of cases [AB0114].
- **Spouse visas to closed markets.** A woman with BMET clearance going to her "husband" in Oman, where worker and housemaid visas are shut, had no registered nikahnama; the same spouse-visa approval had been used for five women [D0032].
- **Students and first-time visitors.** A student with a valid Thai visa was told his trip would be cancelled until he named his university [D0034]. A first-time Malaysia traveller with a valid visa and BMET clearance was stopped and a GD (police general diary) started [D0035].
- **Risky destinations.** BMET was told to check visas strictly for Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya [D0039]. Immigration police link visa fraud by Bangladeshis to at least 23 countries, including Thailand, India, Malaysia and Saudi Arabia [D0033].

The SME Foundation trains returnees in business planning, financial literacy, trade licences, TIN, BSTI (Bangladesh Standards and Testing Institution) certificates and markets, work its managing director called still at a very early stage [AB0139].

**Loans.** PKB's returnee loans are the rehabilitation, women's rehabilitation, special rehabilitation and self-employment loans; it also lends where a migrant's family runs a business at home, and has lent Tk 702 crore to 24,971 people under them, its deputy managing director said in August 2026 [AB0133, AB0134, AB0135]. PKB's acting regional head in Sylhet said in April 2026 that the bank lends to returnees who suffered losses at 8% interest, but that in many cases this is not succeeding [LB0243]. Under an agreement between the NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) and PKB, returnees with a skills certificate can get a collateral-free loan of up to Tk 5 lakh, and RPL (recognition of prior learning, certifying skills learnt on the job) is being extended to workers in Saudi Arabia and Singapore, an NSDA director said in August 2026 [AB0129, AB0130]. An ILO officer said that 94% of returnees want to start a business but find loans hard to get because of paperwork, and that Karmasangsthan Bank now lends to them across the country at 7% interest on just three documents [AB0136].

> **Need to know**
> - Is PKB's rehabilitation loan at 9% or 8%, and is the collateral-free limit Tk 3 lakh or Tk 5 lakh with a skills certificate [F0343, LB0243, AB0129]?
> - Which three documents does Karmasangsthan Bank ask returnees for, and up to what amount [AB0136]?

**What reaches people.** The ILO officer said the government recently drew up a reintegration policy for returnees, that many people don't know PKB lends, and that support is usually project-based and stops when the project ends [AB0137, AB0138]. BRAC's Migration Programme said many people return empty-handed after finding no work, and many have no savings even after 20 to 25 years abroad [AB0141]. RAISE's project director said many women returnees miss out on support because their families sent them abroad but suspect and undervalue them on return, and that the project is considering a union-level officer to record which woman has returned to which house and when [AB0111, AB0112]. The general secretary of the Ovibashi Nari Sramik Federation said in May 2026 that women who come home after harassment and abuse get hardly any rehabilitation help and struggle to fit back into family and society, and that those returning at 45 to 50 are in a pitiable state; another speaker, Sheikh Romana, said many women return after abuse with a child, and questioned how many returnees really get the help that exists [OA0221, OA0222, OA0223].

### Fake BMET cards and fake clearance

BMET clearance (the "smart card" or emigration clearance) is the government's permission to leave as a worker; airport desks check it [S0111].

**How it works:** rings put fake visas in passports, then obtain BMET smart cards for them or forge the clearance itself, and send people to the airport [S0003, S0024]. Cards are issued offline by BMET and the district manpower offices, and online through Ami Probashi, a phone app run by a private organisation, with all data kept on a server at BMET's office [OA0001, OA0002]. In late 2023 and early 2024, rejected applications were approved through that system with a senior BMET official's login; BMET's inquiry said hackers used the app's lack of an automatic session time-out and of a one-time PIN (OTP), or other flaws, to do it [OA0005, OA0006, OA0007, OA0008]. People concerned told Prothom Alo in February 2024 that BMET's server could not be secured and ran on very old software, so fraud could not be stopped or traced; BMET was moving server management to the Bangladesh Computer Council (BCC), and two officials said fraud would stop once the move was done [OA0023, OA0024, OA0025].

**Red flags:**

- a BMET card or clearance supplied by the dalal or office itself [S0024];
- a "clearance" for a country where the ministry has stopped clearances without its approval: since 4 Nov 2026 these include Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [S0127].

**Check before paying:** whether a BMET emigration clearance or card exists can be checked on the page of the WEWB (Wage Earners' Welfare Board), portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [VS0035]. Police clearance certificates are forged too, with QR codes that lead to a fake website: apply only on pcc.police.gov.bd (Saudi Arabia doc: Work visa: company worker, Step 6: Give the agency your papers for the visa (start before Step 5)) [S0084].

**Cases:**

- Reported Oct 2022: workers who went to the UAE on fake smart cards got neither legal status nor jobs [S0147].
- 27 Dec 2023 to 4 Jan 2024: 20 smart-card applications rejected in the Ami Probashi app were later given clearance, according to sources cited by Prothom Alo; BMET formed a six-member inquiry committee on 10 January, which reported on 7 February [OA0003, OA0004]. Of the 20 cards, 8 were in the name of Bangladesh Export Corporation (RL-803), on 2 and 3 January, and 12 in the name of Saad International Limited (RL-1068), on 1 and 3 January [OA0012]. Saad International's managing director said his agency applied by the rules as always, had never had a complaint, and knew of no irregularity; Bangladesh Export Corporation could not be reached [OA0013, OA0014, OA0015]. The inquiry found 19 cards were approved from an Android phone through two IP addresses and one from the computer of the Additional Director General, whose ID and password were saved on it [OA0005]. The Additional Director General (Training), then covering for the one on leave, told the inquiry he had not finalised any rejected application and that the app had been hacked before; the app's chief technical officer said no one but a system user could have done it [OA0009, OA0010, OA0011]. The committee, which included the Ministry, Ami Probashi and the BCC, held no one responsible [OA0006, OA0007, OA0008].
- 31 Dec 2023: Alif Overseas (RL-847) took out smart cards for 24 Saudi-bound workers; in the third week of January BMET officials found no matching data on the server, the Director General was told in writing on 28 January, and BMET did not cancel the cards [OA0016, OA0017, OA0018]. On 5 February 2024 the Director General wrote to the Dhaka Metropolitan Police's cyber-crime deputy commissioner, saying Alif Overseas had taken the clearances by hacking the system; the deputy commissioner said the letter had not reached him [OA0019, OA0020, OA0021]. A BMET official said the cyber police had been asked to look at server fraud as a whole, and the BCC had been asked to audit the server [OA0026, OA0027]. The son of Alif Overseas's owner said the agency cannot hack BMET's server, never applied for the cards, and that someone inside BMET may have taken them out in its name [OA0022]. Tasneem Siddiqui of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) said workers sent without proper checks can be cheated, and the agencies named should be investigated and punished if guilty [OA0031, OA0032].
- FY2023-24: airport desks checked 1,391,988 clearances and stopped 1,098 travellers with no or fake BMET clearance [S0111].
- July 2026: the CID said that after checks tightened, some recruiters allegedly used fake BMET cards to send workers to Cambodia [S0118].
- August 2026: a Civil Aviation Authority deputy director said that in many cases passengers arrive at the airport with fake BMET cards, fake visas or other fake papers, which 24 agencies at the airport, including immigration and the airlines, check together [AB0124].

BMET's inquiry made eight recommendations, among them OTP and session time-out in the app, BCC testing of its weaknesses, an IT audit, and keeping all its data on BMET's server [OA0028, OA0029, OA0030].

> **Need to know**
> - Have the inquiry's recommendations been carried out, and has BMET's server moved to the BCC [OA0028, OA0023]?

**What to do:** see File a police case [S0159].

### Forged documents and impersonation

#### How it is sold

- **Fake visas:** in February 2024 three men arrested at Dhaka airport had each paid a broker Tk 16 to 18 lakh to fly to Qatar on fake visas and then go on illegally to France, Italy and Greece, a DB (Dhaka detective police) deputy commissioner said [FV0001, FV0002, FV0003, FV0004, FV0005]. In May 2026 two men agreed Tk 30 lakh each for Italy, paid Tk 20 lakh up front, and were given fake Schengen visas [I0359, I0038].
- **Swapped pages:** a "page substitution" ring copies a genuine Italian visa page into a look-alike's passport [I0041]. In July 2026 the CID arrested members of a ring accused of sending a woman to Italy on another woman's visa, and seized 56 passports and 153 seals [I0042].
- **Fake certificates:** a ring sold fake police clearance certificates with counterfeit QR codes linking to a fake website [I0232]. In one study, 22% of workers said agents forged their medical certificates and 16% their pre-departure orientation certificates [I0233].
- **A forged police clearance, Chattogram, 2026:** Zakir Hossain of Banshkhali, unemployed and with a criminal case against him, did not apply to the police because of the case, and went to an acquaintance at a local travel agency, according to his brother [OB0012, OB0013]. The acquaintance, Abdur Rahman, quoted Tk 1,23,000; the family paid Tk 80,000 on 21 July 2026 and the rest on receiving the certificate on 12 August [OB0014, OB0015]. Police said making the certificate and the fake website cost the ring Tk 3,000, leaving Tk 1,20,000 profit [OB0016, OB0017]. The certificates carried police officers' signatures and seals and a QR code that showed them on a clone of the Bangladesh Police website, so, police said, they could not be told from real ones by looking [OB0018, OB0019]. The ring had layers: brokers, and people skilled at making QR codes and websites, Chattogram's Additional Superintendent of Police (Special Branch) said [OB0020, OB0021].
- **Changed ages:** girls have been sent abroad with ages raised through altered birth certificates [I0235]. In May 2025 a dalal sent a Prothom Alo reporter posing as a customer to a computer shop in front of the Gazipur passport office, where a man quoted Tk 32,000, half in advance, to raise the age by one year on both the voter ID card and the passport, delivered within a month or within 15 days for Tk 2,000 more; correcting an NID properly takes several months, the report says [OA0095, OA0096, OA0097]. Ten-fingerprint e-passports now stop one person getting a second passport under another identity (see The passport office) [AB0121].
- **Fake players and officials:** some sports federations and associations have for years been alleged to take fake players and officials abroad with their teams as a way of trafficking people, mostly in the smaller sports, Prothom Alo reported in October 2025 [OA0122]. Until October 2025, federations sent the NSC (National Sports Council) only the players' names, and no one checked whether each was really a player; the chair of the Bangladesh Olympic Association's athletes commission said she often hears that some federations take fake players along [OA0126, OA0127].

#### What happens

- **At Dhaka airport:** in the February 2024 case DB said low-ranking employees of agencies working at the airport and people from private airlines had formed a ring that sent people to the Middle East, Europe and elsewhere on fake visas for lakhs of taka; one of the two alleged ringleaders arrested was a booking assistant of "Qatar Airlines", as the report names it, and the other a security worker at the airport [FV0006, FV0007]. Police seized three passports, three fake visas, a special card for entering the airport, phones, e-tickets, and work permit and visa papers [FV0008].
- **At the airport abroad:** the two men with fake Schengen visas were detained at Rome airport for two days and deported [I0358]. In June 2026 a Biman ground officer was arrested, accused of helping them board [I0039].
- **At the travel agency:** the forged Chattogram police clearance was detected when Zakir Hossain handed it to a travel agency; he complained in writing to the Chattogram District Superintendent of Police's office, and a case was filed at Banshkhali police station with his brother as plaintiff [OB0022, OB0023, OB0024].
- **At the embassy:** Italy suspended all Bangladeshi work nulla osta in October 2024 because of the number of false documents, and refuses or revokes a nulla osta obtained by fraud [I0255, I0256]. The Italian Embassy says forged documents mean immediate refusal, and the visa centre's rules say applicants caught with fake papers are banned from applying and reported to the police [I0252, I0253]. In February 2024 the US Embassy in Dhaka filed a case against five Bangladeshis after two of them, Mofizur Rahman and Ashraful Alam Bhuiyan, admitted at their US visa interview on 11 February that they trafficked people from Bangladesh to various countries; they had submitted fake documents describing themselves as garment businessmen [LB0001, LB0002, AA0057, AA0058].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** making, obtaining, supplying or holding a document to help migrant smuggling carries 1 to 3 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 50,000 under the 2026 Act [VI0007]. A trafficking victim cannot be charged for getting or holding a false travel document as a direct result of the trafficking [VI0019].
- **Destinations:** Italy punishes forging or using a forged visa with 1 to 6 years in prison; Malaysia treats a holder of forged travel documents as a prohibited immigrant; Singapore jailed a worker in 2021 who bought a forged degree through an agent [I0050, I0247, I0262].
- **Travellers are charged too:** in the February 2024 Dhaka airport case, the three men trying to travel on fake visas were arrested with the two alleged ringleaders, a case of fraud and forgery was filed against them, and a court granted one day's remand [FV0009, FV0010].
- **The Chattogram police clearance ring:** police arrested four members, Md. Ashraf Ali Khan, Md. Nishan, Mohammad Abdullah Al Mamun and Md. Abdur Rahman, on 16 August 2026 in Chattogram city and Banshkhali; police said the four were mainly brokers and were looking for the others [OB0025, OB0026].
- **Sports teams:** since 9 October 2025 the NSC requires a federation's proposal for the government order (GO) letting a team go abroad to reach it at least 10 days before the flight, with proof of the players' fitness and performance; an NSC source said the rule is mainly to stop trafficking, while its director (sports) said only that the question of trafficking in the name of sport does come up [OA0123, OA0124, OA0125].

#### Where to get help

CID asks the public to report forged visas and migrant smuggling directly to it (see Where victims and families get help) [I0361].
