### Women

#### How many women work abroad?

More than 10 lakh Bangladeshi women were working abroad in May 2026, by BMET data, and more than 10 lakh 95 thousand went abroad between 2001 and 2022, though the number of women migrating has fallen in the last few years; the programme director of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) puts women at about 13.5% of all migration [OA0211, OA0212, OA0213]. About 799 to 800 women migrants have come home dead in the past eight years, by Wage Earners' Welfare Board data cited in May 2026 by Jasiya Khatun, director of WARBE Development Foundation (a migrant workers' rights organisation); she said the cause is mostly given as heart attack or illness, sometimes suicide, and the real cause is not known [OA0210].

#### Can a woman go abroad for work without her guardian's permission?

For domestic work, no: BMET clears a woman domestic worker only with proof that she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0190]. A woman going on a single visa also needs her guardian's no-objection letter on stamp paper [F0146]. The housekeeping course asks for the same letter [F0303].

#### Who can go as a domestic worker?

A woman aged 25 to 45 when she enrols in the housekeeping course (returnees up to 50), who has passed class 5 (which can be relaxed) and is fit [F0304, F0305]. She can't enrol if she is pregnant, if her youngest child is under 6, or if she has a contagious or incurable disease [F0306].

#### What is the housekeeping course?

It is compulsory for women going to the Middle East for domestic work: one month (24 working days), residential, at one of 41 TTCs [F0094, F0307, F0308]. Apply on OEP's training portal and pay the set fee [F0309]. You must attend 90% of sessions and score 70% in the final test, and the course teaches how to contact the police and the Bangladesh embassy abroad [F0308, F0310]. The certificate is valid two years for a first departure [F0311]. Brokers have sold course certificates for money [F0312].

**In practice:** people in the migration sector said in January 2024 that the course is skipped in some cases before women are sent to Saudi Arabia, and its quality is questioned [AA0041]. Many women back from Saudi Arabia say the month is not enough; some cannot read or write, many can only give a thumbprint instead of a signature, and they do not know the language of the country they go to, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of January 2024 [AA0047, AA0048]. In April 2026 the principal of the Cumilla TTC said attendance at women workers' training is now monitored online, and that the rule that agencies take at least 10% trained workers must be enforced [AA0247, AA0248]. The project director of RAISE, a government returnee project, said in August 2026 that domestic work abroad means working in 50-degree heat, cooking local food and using modern appliances, which need training [AB0113].

#### Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?

No. An agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from her [F0191], and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker [F0313]. The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi worker [F0314]. Yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [F0315].

#### Which agencies may send women domestic workers?

Only agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved, each with a Tk 15 lakh security deposit and a woman on the staff; the September 2025 order approved 21 [F0316]. Ask for the approval letter and check the agency's name and RL number are in it (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Step 2) [F0316].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Ministry publish one current list of all agencies approved to send women domestic workers?

#### The agent wants to change my age on the passport. Is that a problem?

Yes: agents have raised girls' ages with a second birth certificate, in one case giving a girl under 13 a passport showing 27 [F0317, F0318]. Under a February 2025 circular the national ID data is the basis of the passport, and the person whose passport it is bears the responsibility for any mismatch, the assistant director of the Cox's Bazar regional passport office said in June 2026 [MB0038, MB0039]. A raised age also stays: one returnee, Fatema Akter, said in April 2026 that her passport shows a higher age and despite many attempts she can no longer go abroad [AA0246]. Check the birth record your passport will use at everify.bdris.gov.bd with the 17-digit registration number and your real date of birth [F0319]. The Probashi call centre, 16135 (free from Bangladesh), gives workers and families advice and help [F0320].

#### What are a domestic worker's rights?

Saudi Arabia's rules are an example:
- at most 10 hours' work a day, with at least 8 hours' continuous rest [F0321];
- one paid day off a week [F0147];
- the employer may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [F0148];
- the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy [F0149].

The rules for other countries are in each country doc's domestic-worker section (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Your rights at work). In practice most women workers work 12 to 14 hours instead of 8, according to the director of BNSK (Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra, a women workers' organisation) in May 2026 [OA0217].

#### What if I'm abused? Where can I go?

In Saudi Arabia, the embassy labour wing's free line is 8001000125, and the labour wings run safe homes for women in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [F0145, F0150]. The labour ministry's abuse line, 1919, takes reports round the clock in confidence [F0151]. A relative in Bangladesh can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) for help or to bring you home; it is free and the Ministry acts within 7 working days [F0152, F0153]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash at the airport welfare desk [F0102].

**How the way home works in Saudi Arabia:** officials said in January 2024 that most women who leave a household before the two-year contract ends run away to the embassy's safe home, are moved from there to the Saudi government's safe home, and are flown home at the Saudi government's cost; a woman who wants to go back within three months of arriving is sent home through the Saudi company that recruited her [AA0035, AA0036].

**A case:** Lutfa Begum of Brahmanbaria went to Saudi Arabia as a domestic worker in March 2020 and worked 1 month 5 days, in two neighbouring houses where she said the men behaved badly; the person who sent her did not find her other work, and when she asked to go home she was sent to a shelter, where she stayed about three months [AA0037, AA0038, AA0039]. She said she was paid nothing, and after coming home received Tk 30,000 in cash help in two instalments [AA0040].

**Counselling:** the Cumilla manpower office's assistant director said in April 2026 that there is almost no psychological counselling for migrant workers, especially women [AA0252].

#### Can women get garment jobs in Jordan?

Yes, through BOESL: its November 2026 notice took women aged 20 to 35 who can run plain and overlock sewing machines, at no cost to the worker [F0322, F0206]. In June 2026 the CID arrested three people who used a Jordan garment company's name and seal to take Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person without a licence [F0323]. Steps: see the Jordan doc, Work visa: garment and factory worker.

### Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes

**How it works:**

1. A broker arranges a marriage to a foreigner and promises a rich husband, a big house abroad or money for the family [S0021, VS0019].
2. The woman travels on a family visa, such as China's Q-1 family-reunion visa [S0019].
3. Abroad, the woman may be forced into sex work: a Chuadanga mother alleged in a 2024 case that this happened to her daughter in China [VS0020].

Marriage has also been used to take women to India and Malaysia: women have been drawn into a love affair, married and taken to India with a child, then forced into sex work [AA0105], and Rohingya girls in the Cox's Bazar camps have been married by phone to men in Malaysia and put on smuggling boats (see Malaysia: boats and land crossings) [MA0099, MA0100, MA0101].

**What dalals say:** "the husband is rich" (স্বামী বিত্তশালী) [S0021]; in one case the family was told the Chinese groom had become Muslim, worked in a Gazipur factory and would stay in Bangladesh [S0014].

**Red flags:**

- a foreign groom found through "arrangers": middlemen allegedly dealt in up to Tk 10-20 lakh per woman sent to China [VS0018, S0017];
- fake addresses, forged papers and suspect marriage documents [S0020];
- a job or study offer that turns into a marriage: in a May 2024 case, a Rangamati woman's sister alleged she was taken to Dhaka with a promise of a nursing course and forced to marry a Chinese man [VS0022, S0018];
- a poor village family: the Airport Armed Police Battalion (APBn, the police unit guarding Dhaka's Shahjalal airport) said in May 2025 that some rings, with local dalals, target women from simple, poor village families to take to China [AA0152, AA0153].

**Cases:**

- Mar 2024: a Chuadanga mother filed a case at a Dhaka tribunal alleging her daughter, married to a Chinese man in July 2023, was taken to China and forced into sex work; the daughter said Bangladeshi traffickers had sold her to him for Tk 10 lakh; the accused include a Jatrabari marriage registrar (kazi), and the CID was investigating in June 2024 [VS0020, S0015, S0016, VS0021].
- Reported Mar 2025: a woman taken to India after marriage said her six-month-old child was held hostage and dangled from a balcony, with a threat to drop the child if she did not obey [AA0105].
- 3 to 8 Apr 2025: a Rohingya woman from Balukhali camp (Camp 9), Ukhiya, was married by phone to a man in Malaysia she had never met, her parents agreeing on a broker's word; two days later she was handed to a broker, kept with other Rohingya women in a house in the Kachchhapia hills of Teknaf and put on a trawler for Malaysia that the navy seized on 8 April; her family had spent about Tk 50,000 by then [MA0099, MA0100, MA0101].
- 26 May 2025: a 19-year-old woman being taken through Shahjalal airport told the APBn at the roundabout by the main entrance that two Chinese men were trying to traffic her to China; that night the APBn arrested Hu Junjun (30), Zhang Leiji (54) and a Bangladeshi, Md Nayan Ali (30), in Bashundhara, Dhaka [AA0143, AA0144, AA0145]. By the APBn's account, the two Chinese men had lived in Bashundhara for a year and built ties with dalals around the country; with Nayan Ali's help the woman was pushed by threats and inducements to marry, a passport was made in her name at a false address, and she married Hu Junjun in March 2025 with a dower (denmohar) of Tk 10 lakh [AA0146, AA0147]. Zhang Leiji bought her air ticket without telling her, and the men took her by force to the airport [AA0148]. The APBn said a Bashundhara house held more traffickers and women victims; the others fled the raid, and police said they had initial proof the ring had long been trafficking [AA0149, AA0150]. Her mother filed a case at Airport police station on 28 May under sections 6(1), 6(2), 7, 8 and 10 of the 2012 trafficking act [AA0151]. The APBn said this was its second recent detention of Chinese nationals for trafficking at the airport [AA0152, AA0153].

At least 4,226 Bangladeshi women took China's Q-1 family visa in 2020-2025: 35 in 2022, 1,016 in 2023, 1,378 in 2024 and 1,721 in 2025, according to data presented at an anti-trafficking-law workshop; the figure is not a count of trafficking victims [S0019, VS0015, VS0016, VS0017].

**What to do:** exploitation through a fraudulent marriage counts as trafficking under the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 (see File a trafficking case) [VS0004, VS0014].

> **Need to know**
> - Are forged nikahnamas (Muslim marriage contracts) used for Gulf or Italian family visas, and how does a family check one against the kazi's register?

### Trafficking disguised as a job, especially for women

**How it works:**

1. A woman is offered a well-paid job: housekeeping, a restaurant, customer care [S0135, S0189, S0186].
2. She is often sent on a visit visa [S0189].
3. On arrival her passport is taken, and she may be sold or forced into sex work [S0189, S0135, VS0049].

Trafficking used to be done by force and is now done mainly in the name of migration, through job advertisements, according to Shariful Hasan, associate director of BRAC, in July 2026 [RU0058].

**Red flags:**

- a recruiter she already owes money to [S0186];
- a job with no BMET clearance or on a visit visa [S0117, S0189];
- any job for a woman in Malaysia: the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare said in February 2025 that the agreement between Bangladesh and Malaysia leaves no scope for women workers to be employed there [RU0004].

**Cases:**

- Reported Oct 2020: a Jhenaidah woman, lured by a fellow villager with a housekeeping job in Jordan at JD 250 (Jordanian dinars) a month, said she paid two brokers Tk 50,000, was sold in Amman and was held in a house from which women were sent out for sex work [VS0048, S0135, VS0047, VS0049].
- 15 Sept 2020: a RAB mobile court team arrested two owners of Fatema Overseas, Road 10, Banani, on a charge of trafficking a young woman to Saudi Arabia [S0205].
- July 2022: a 34-year-old Narayanganj woman went to Saudi Arabia on a promise of housework at Tk 30,000 a month and was physically and mentally abused; her husband said the people who sent her demanded Tk 3 lakh 20 thousand to bring her back, and that she was put out of the house after threatening suicide and came home [AA0019, AA0020, AA0021]. He filed a case at Hatirjheel police station on 24 November 2022 naming three men; in January 2024 the investigation was unfinished, and the investigating officer said institutions he had written to had not replied [AA0022, AA0023].
- 21 Nov 2024: a Satkhira woman went to Saudi Arabia to work as a cleaner, was put to domestic work in a house, said she was sexually abused there and came back in January 2025 [AA0106]. She filed a case in a Satkhira court on 16 February 2025; Monirul Islam, owner of Lamim Visa Care in Satkhira, was arrested, while another accused, Md Rafiqul Islam, owner of M/s Siam Trade International in Dhaka, said she came back because she was already ill [AA0107, AA0108].
- 29 May 2025: the Anti-Corruption Commission (ACC) raided BMET's Dhaka office, and on 3 July 2025 filed a case against four BMET officials and the owners and officials of five agencies for trafficking women abroad on forged papers; it alleges applications were filed with another woman's passport number in place of the real applicant's and cleared without a check against BMET's database [OA0105, OA0106, OA0107]. In a letter to BMET on 23 July 2026 the ACC said it had filed a case in June 2025 against five agencies over this, and that its investigation had found allegations against 52 more agencies [MB0073, MB0074, MB0075].
- 14 May 2026: a Keraniganj woman made to work in a Dubai dance bar for three years was rescued at Dhaka airport as the ring tried to take her back; investigators found at least 14 more women held in Dubai [S0189, S0190].
- July 2026: the CID arrested an NGO loan officer accused of trafficking a woman to Cambodia; she came home on 1 July with help from the BRAC Migration Welfare Centre [S0186, S0188].
- Told July 2026: Naznin Akter, separated from her husband and struggling to support two children, went to Malaysia to get free of debt after an acquaintance offered her restaurant work at about BDT 60 thousand a month; she paid more than BDT 2.5 lakh, raised by selling jewellery and borrowing [AB0041, AB0042]. In Malaysia her family was pressed for money, and she was kept in a hotel, then held in a closed room for six days and tortured for asking where she was; she got home on 21 November through her brothers' efforts [AB0048, AB0049].

> **Need to know**
> - Did the ACC file its case over forged clearances for women in June 2025 or on 3 July 2025, and is it one case or two?

**The numbers:**

- The CID's trafficking unit had 122 cases under investigation in 2025, against 114 in 2022, 86 in 2023 and 100 in 2024 [S0191].
- BRAC's Migration Programme said in January 2024 that women domestic workers are forced to return from Saudi Arabia almost every month after abuse by employers, and that some who were sexually abused come back pregnant [AA0042, AA0043].
- A 2025 study by Badabon Sangha, a women migrant workers' rights organisation, in seven districts found 27.6% of women migrated with no source of income; its programme manager said many took high-interest loans or sold assets, and abroad face low pay, loss of their passports, long hours and abuse [OA0214, OA0215, OA0216].
- Women's migration fell by about 41% in 2025 against 2022 because of various kinds of fraud, according to Professor Tasneem Siddiqui of RMMRU in July 2026 [MB0066].

**Action against agencies:** a Prothom Alo editorial of January 2024 said there is almost no example of action against the accused agencies after women come back empty-handed and abused [AA0049]. Traffickers do not get exemplary punishment, according to BRAC's Shariful Hasan in July 2026 [RU0058].

**What to do:** see File a trafficking case [VS0002].

### Malaysia: boats and land crossings

#### How it is sold

**By land:** about 17,777 workers with BMET clearance failed to reach Malaysia before the 31 May 2024 deadline [I0371]. A Bangladeshi smuggling ring broken up in Malaysia in July 2026 is believed to have charged up to RM15,000 (Malaysian ringgit) per person and made about RM3.2 million since January 2026 [I0144]. A group caught in Thailand in April 2026 had paid brokers Tk 7 lakh each [I0147].

**By sea:** brokers lure Rohingya refugees from the Cox's Bazar camps onto trawlers with good jobs in Malaysia, and girls with a promise of marriage there, taking Tk 20,000 to 30,000 a head before boarding, police and local representatives said in October 2024 [MA0052, MA0053]. In April 2025 police said the people on one seized trawler had been promised good jobs in Malaysia or marriage to a suitable man, many girls had been married over the phone, and most had paid Tk 30,000 to 50,000 [MA0097, MA0098]. A camp-in-charge of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commission (RRRC, the government office that runs the Rohingya camps) said in June 2026 that Bangladeshis as well as Rohingya take the sea route, especially teenage girls taken to Malaysia and Indonesia with the lure of a "better husband" [MB0007, MB0008].

**Other lures:**

- A social-media friendship and an invitation to visit, which ends in abduction or trafficking, according to the Cox's Bazar police chief in June 2026; he said some fishermen think trafficking people pays more than fishing [MB0020, MB0021].
- Australia by way of Malaysia: brokers offered one college student this for Tk 12 lakh [MB0005, MB0006]. People already in Malaysia are being drawn into fraud with assurances that they will be sent on to Australia, according to BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan in July 2026 [AB0076].

**The season:** traffickers are active when the Bay of Bengal is calm, from October or November to March, police and local representatives said in October 2024 [MA0052, MA0053]. The Deputy Commissioner of Cox's Bazar said in June 2026 that the risk of trafficking by sea rises in winter, when the sea is calm [MB0013].

#### The path

**By land:**

1. **Fly to Cambodia** [I0148, I0145].
2. **Cross into Thailand on foot**, then be moved south in stages, kept in hotels and resorts [I0148, I0147].
3. **Cross into Malaysia** through the Kelantan border, then wait in a transit house [I0145, I0146].

**By sea:**

1. **Held in the hills:** people brought from around the country are kept in the hills and other places near Cox's Bazar, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026 [MB0001, MB0002]. In 2025 rings worked in stages, bringing Rohingya out of the camps and driving them to the Kachchhapia area of Teknaf, police said [MA0094, MA0095, MA0096].
2. **Out to the boat at night:** small boats take them in the dark to a large trawler or ship; the coastline is 710 km long and a boat can be launched from any point on it [MB0001, MB0002, MB0016, MB0017]. Departures were rising in October 2024 from the coasts of Teknaf, Ukhiya, Ramu, Cox's Bazar Sadar and Maheshkhali, the Anwara and Banshkhali coasts of Chattogram, and the Karnaphuli river [MA0054, MA0055].
3. **Past St Martin's island through Myanmar waters to Thailand**, where passengers were promised they would be handed on and delivered to Malaysia [MA0094, MA0095, MA0096].

Some "Malaysia" journeys go overland into Myanmar instead: one man walked for several days over hills before he was detained there [MB0049, MB0050, MB0051].

The boat route is older: in 2012 to 2015 thousands tried boat journeys, and trafficking camps and mass graves were found on the Thailand-Malaysia border [I0141]. BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan, who reported on it then as a journalist, puts the number trafficked in that period at about one lakh, and said rescued Bangladeshis, many of them teenagers, were sent home in 2015 with their hands and feet in chains [MB0022, MB0023, MB0024]. The first mass grave, with over 30 bodies, was found in April 2015 near Wang Kelian [I0142]. The dead had no names and their graves were marked only with numbers [MB0011, MB0012].

#### What happens

- **On the boat:** there is no food, clean water or toilet, people die and the bodies are thrown into the sea, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026; the torture begins once people are on board [MB0018, MB0001, MB0002]. The state minister for expatriates' welfare said the same month, from victims' and families' accounts, that brokers hold people hostage and take large sums from their families, many die on the way, and those who reach a shore are detained by police there [MB0009, MB0010]. Reports of about 500 Rohingya deaths had come in during one week of July 2026, BRAC said [AB0075].
- **Ransom on the way:** before reaching Malaysia, Rohingya are held at the Thai or Myanmar border while ransom is demanded from relatives in the camps, and if that fails there are attempts to abduct family members, a Balukhali camp leader said in January 2025 [MA0086].
- **Ransom without leaving Bangladesh:** every winter rings hold camp Rohingya for ransom in the name of the sea journey, and sometimes sail a big trawler around for several days, leave the passengers somewhere in Cox's Bazar and run off with the money, Teknaf's police chief said in December 2024 [MA0076, MA0077]. People held in a Teknaf hill den in November 2024 said they were told to call relatives for money and say they were in Myanmar, were tortured and not fed properly, and were held for eight to more than 13 days [MA0065, MA0066, MA0067].
- **Abduction for ransom:** at least 193 people, 86 of them Rohingya, were abducted in Teknaf in the year to January 2025 and at least 98 freed on ransom; police found links to trafficking and to some local residents [MA0087, MA0088].
- **Caught in Thailand:** 21 Bangladeshis found in a resort in Chana in April 2026 were charged with illegal entry and stay [I0149].
- **Caught in Malaysia:** 15 smuggled Bangladeshis were caught in a Cheras transit house in July 2026 and taken to the Putrajaya immigration depot [I0146]. Malaysia's Home Minister reported 465 deaths in immigration depots in 2021 to 2025 [I0152].
- **Going home:** Malaysia's PRM 2.0 (Migrant Repatriation Programme) lets undocumented migrants leave; Bangladeshis were the largest group in it [I0373, I0157].
- **Silence at home:** families who lose someone at sea or are cheated often don't want to admit it, which makes the crime hard to investigate, the Deputy Commissioner of Cox's Bazar said in June 2026 [MB0014, MB0015].

#### Cases

- 20 June 2014: Nurul Islam of Lohagara, Chattogram, lured with a well-paid job in Malaysia, said he and another man were taken to the Dariyanagar coast in Cox's Bazar and put on a ship by trawler; on the Thai coast brokers tortured them and took Tk 4 lakh ransom before handing them to a broker in Malaysia [MA0027, MA0028]. Malaysian police arrested him as an illegal immigrant in June 2017, and after a year in prison the government brought him home in October 2018 [MA0029, MA0030]. He filed a trafficking case against six people in October 2018; in 2019 the 12-year-old brother of an accused was produced in the High Court in his place and given bail, and in May 2024 the Chattogram tribunal sent the man claiming to be that accused to jail and ordered police to find out who the real accused is [MA0031, MA0032, MA0033].
- 12 Oct 2024: the Coast Guard seized a trawler carrying 17 Rohingya from Teknaf's Mochhoni camp at Shikalbaha on the Karnaphuli river and arrested three members of a trafficking ring [MA0056, MA0057].
- 14 Oct 2024: brokers who had put more than a hundred Rohingya from the Ukhiya camps on a fishing trawler for Malaysia set them down at dawn on Inani beach after 10 days at sea; police and the Coast Guard detained 26 and the brokers hid [MA0046, MA0047, MA0048]. One woman said she had paid a broker Tk 30,000 for a good job in Malaysia; another passenger said the trawler left Teknaf, met the Myanmar navy south of St Martin's island and was turned back [MA0049, MA0050, MA0051].
- 12 to 17 Nov 2024: Ekhlas Mia of Ukhiya and his cousin were abducted on the Marine Drive and held in a Teknaf hilltop den with men, women and children tortured for money; unable to pay, he was taken to the beach with 15 to 20 others to be sent by sea, slipped away and asked RAB to rescue the rest [MA0068, MA0069, MA0070]. RAB rescued 31 people, 27 Rohingya and 4 Bangladeshis, from the den in the Kachchhapia hills; each had paid Tk 20,000 for the sea journey, the women promised good grooms and the men good jobs [MA0062, MA0063, MA0064]. Two men were arrested and a case filed under the trafficking law [MA0071, MA0072].
- 14 Dec 2024: Teknaf police rescued 30 Rohingya, 15 men, 3 women and 12 children, held for ransom in a house in Dakshin Lambari Para after being gathered for Malaysia with promises of jobs and marriage; the ring fled and nobody was arrested [MA0073, MA0074, MA0075].
- 2 to 9 Apr 2025: an auto-rickshaw driver from Ukhiya said two passengers held him at gunpoint, took him to the Kachchhapia hills, kept him four days in a hut with 20 to 25 others and forced him onto a trawler on 7 April [MA0102, MA0103]. On 8 April the navy seized that trawler, FB Kulsuma, 44 nautical miles south-west of St Martin's island with 214 people aboard, 118 men, 68 women and 28 children, and no life-saving equipment or enough food and water; it had left Shaplapur beach in Teknaf [MA0091, MA0092, MA0093]. The RRRC said action was being taken against 12 of the Rohingya for various crimes and the rest went back to the camps [MA0104, MA0105]. A woman married by phone was among them (see Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes) [MA0099, MA0100, MA0101].
- Told June 2026: Tarekur Rahman of Maheshkhali said a broker promising an easy Malaysian visa took him to Myanmar, where he was beaten and threatened with death and Tk 1 lakh was taken from his family [MB0044, MB0045]. In the first report of his account, a further Tk 7 lakh was taken in Thailand and he was caught at the border on the way to Malaysia, jailed and sent home [MB0003, MB0004]. In the fuller account, his family sold their house under torture, food sometimes came only after three or four days, and after another large payment for the onward leg he was caught near the border and held about seven months in a Myanmar prison [MB0046, MB0047].
- Told June 2026: Aung Aung U Jewel, a college student who could not afford the language tests needed to go legally, set off when brokers promised a cheap route to Australia by way of Malaysia for Tk 12 lakh; they took him into Myanmar, where after days of walking over hills he was detained and spent a month on remand and several months in prison [MB0005, MB0006, MB0049, MB0050, MB0051]. His family had no news of him until he sent his mother a letter through a Myanmar police officer, and the brokers took money from the family as a "rescue cost" [MB0052, MB0053]. When fighting reached Thandwe prison the prisoners were moved and made to work, and he later came home with the Bangladesh embassy's help [MB0054].

#### The numbers

- **2016 to 2023:** law enforcers rescued 1,134 people being taken to Malaysia by sea, 95% of them Rohingya; 85 cases were filed at Ukhiya and Teknaf police stations naming 1,200 accused, of whom 508 were caught [MA0058, MA0059].
- **2024:** sea trafficking from Teknaf rose again from January 2024 after six or seven years of being largely stopped, and in the year to January 2025 at least 600 people, including 45 traffickers, were caught in eight or nine operations on the Teknaf coast [MA0082, MA0083]. From Lambashia camp alone more than 500 young men and women were sent towards Malaysia in that year, and more than 300 were caught and brought back, a camp leader said [MA0084, MA0085].
- **Deaths and missing:** at least 300 people died in more than 20 trawler sinkings in the years to October 2024, according to Abdus Shukkur, a Cox's Bazar human rights lawyer, who said the rings especially target Rohingya girls [MA0060, MA0061]. About 5,787 people went missing on illegal sea journeys in 2023 alone, about 25% of them women and children and half of them Bangladeshi citizens, according to an assistant representative of UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) in June 2026 [MB0026, MB0027]. About two lakh people have crossed the Bay of Bengal in the past decade and many have gone missing, according to BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan [MB0025].

> **Need to know**
> - What does UNHCR's own publication give for people missing at sea on the Bay of Bengal route in 2023, and how many were women and children?

#### The law

- **Malaysia:** anyone who entered unlawfully or holds forged travel documents is a prohibited immigrant [I0247]. Smuggling cases are brought under the Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007, the Immigration Act and the Passports Act [I0150].
- **Thailand:** in 2017 Thailand convicted 62 people, including nine officials, over the deaths and trafficking of Rohingya and Bangladeshis to Malaysia [I0143].
- **Bangladesh, in practice:** even when cases are filed, traffickers often get out of jail and go back to the same work, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026 [MB0019]. Twenty abduction cases were filed at Teknaf police station in the year to January 2025, and its officer-in-charge said victims released on ransom do not want to complain [MA0089, MA0090].

#### Where to get help

Bangladesh High Commission, Kuala Lumpur: No 8, Lorong Yap Kwan Seng, 50450 Kuala Lumpur; consular +60326040949 [I0370]. In Thailand: the Bangladesh Embassy in Bangkok (see Where victims and families get help) [I0237]. In Myanmar the embassy's help varies: Tarekur Rahman said he got no real help from it in prison and came home on money his family sent, while Aung Aung U Jewel came home with its help [MB0048, MB0054].

### India: women and girls taken across the land border

#### How it is sold

Traffickers usually target women from low-income families in financial trouble [AA0126].

**Offers in reported cases:**

- a job in India, ending in a brothel [AA0088, AA0089];
- a job in Canada [AA0014];
- TikTok fame: a ring trafficked women to India "in the name of TikTok" [AA0024, AA0025];
- a dance course in India at Tk 50,000 a month, sent in January 2025 to a class-eight girl from the Facebook account of a girl she knew, whose mother used the same account and helped three girls set off [AA0093, AA0094, AA0095];
- cheaper cancer treatment in India and work there to pay for it, offered by a woman neighbour [AA0101, AA0102];
- work through a cousin the relatives believed was a domestic worker in Maharashtra [AA0103, AA0104];
- a love affair and marriage (see Marriage, family-visa and fake-nikahnama schemes) [AA0105].

#### The path

1. **A relay of handlers:** in February 2025 three girls took a bus from Dhaka to Jhenaidah; the conductor handed them to a man, who took them to a woman's house, and that evening a battery auto-rickshaw driver took them to a man at the Maheshpur border [AA0096, AA0097]. Each person has a separate task, so victims cannot say who took them from where, a Border Guard Bangladesh (BGB) battalion commander in Jashore said [AA0113].
2. **The crossing:** routes run through Putkhali, Daulatpur, Gatipara, Sadipur and Rudrapur around Benapole in Jashore, through Maheshpur in Jhenaidah and the Satkhira border, with scattered cases through Tamabil in Sylhet and the Cumilla border [AA0109].
3. **Sold:** rescued women have been found in brothels in Hyderabad, Maharashtra and Bengaluru [AA0090, AA0091].

**The prices:** traffickers take Tk 15,000 to 20,000 a person just to get someone across, far more than before because checks have tightened, and rescued women said they were told in the brothels that they had been bought for Tk 2 to 3 lakh, according to the director of Rights Jashore, a local rights organisation, in early 2025 [AA0110, AA0111].

#### What happens

- **Rescued in India:** the Indian Border Security Force (BSF) hands the person to the BGB and immigration police at the "zero line" of the Benapole border [AA0112].
- **Jailed in India:** a 28-year-old Savar woman promised Canada was trafficked to Nadia district, West Bengal, and came home after seven months in jail there [AA0014].

#### Cases

- 2021: a young woman trafficked by the TikTok ring escaped back to Bangladesh on 7 May 2021 and on 1 June filed a case at Hatirjheel police station naming 12 people, among them Rifadul Islam alias Hridoy (26), "TikTok Hridoy" [AA0024, AA0025]. Three years later the Bangladesh investigation was still unfinished, although accused arrested in India over the same events had been tried and punished there; Salma Ali of BNWLA (Bangladesh National Woman Lawyers' Association) blamed the lack of accountability of investigators [AA0026, AA0027].
- 10 Oct 2024: a 22-year-old divorced woman in Dhaka with cancer was trafficked to India after a neighbour's offer; Hyderabad police rescued her from a brothel on 21 November [AA0101, AA0102].
- Told Feb 2025: a 22-year-old woman from near the border in Jashore said she was taken to India on the promise of a job and sold to a brothel, where she saw three Bangladeshi girls; Indian police rescued her two years later and BNWLA rehabilitated her [AA0088, AA0089].
- 10 Feb 2025: BGB members chased the three Dhaka schoolgirls and the man taking them across the border at Maheshpur, Jhenaidah; one girl fell, all three were rescued, and the man, his face wrapped in a gamcha (cotton towel), escaped [AA0098, AA0099]. One girl filed a case at Maheshpur police station on 24 February; nobody had been arrested by 8 March [AA0100].
- 11 Feb 2025: a 15-year-old girl from a poor family was trafficked after her cousin's offer; Maharashtra police rescued her on 23 February, and Justice and Care, an anti-trafficking organisation, said she had been used for sex work [AA0103, AA0104].

#### The numbers

- **Border Guard Bangladesh:** from August 2024 to January 2025, 145 people, traffickers included, were detained while being taken across illegally, 55 of them women and children, and 162 people were brought back from India [AA0092].
- **Justice and Care:** in January 2024 to February 2025, 186 women and children were brought back from India and 133 rescued at the border, counting only BGB cases in the Jhenaidah, Chuadanga and Jashore areas [AA0114, AA0115, AA0116]. The return of at least 10 more women and girls rescued from brothels was in process in February 2025 [AA0090, AA0091].
- **Bangladesh overall:** a UN global report on trafficking published in 2024 counted 452 women (228 of them trafficked abroad) and 63 girls trafficked from Bangladesh from April 2023 to March 2024, more than in earlier years [AA0120].

#### The law

Taking someone across the border by deceit for sexual exploitation is trafficking under the 2026 Act (see File a trafficking case) [VS0002].

#### Where to get help

- **BNWLA** rehabilitated the Jashore woman rescued from an Indian brothel [AA0088, AA0089].
- **Justice and Care** was handling the return of at least 10 women and girls rescued from Indian brothels in February 2025 [AA0090, AA0091].

### Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos: scam compounds

#### How it is sold

The job is "computer operator", "data entry", "call centre" or "restaurant" work, for about Tk 5.3 to 6 lakh [I0073, I0076, VI0028, VI0029]. A data-entry job was promised at USD 800 a month [I0090]. CID says traffickers advertise these jobs through fake websites, email, Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram [I0340]. Bangladeshis already working in the Gulf, including on a ship in Dubai, are recruited too with promises of higher pay [I0100, I0339]. Many went with BMET clearance [I0068, I0073]. A BRAC study found 66% of cyber-scam victims were recruited through middlemen and 98% never got the promised job [I0081]. Of 473 people back from Cambodian scam centres, 80% had gone through a recruiting agency in the regular process, the Bangladesh representative of Winrock International, a development organisation, said in July 2026 [AB0023].

People in the sector say Cambodia's economy is no better than Bangladesh's and it has few jobs, yet workers are sent there for the scam centres; with the Middle East market shrinking, people who are a little better off have been going to countries such as Russia and Cambodia, where many are cheated [RU0064, AB0177, AB0178]. Volunteers of migration organisations said in November 2026 that people are also held in scam compounds in Vietnam [RU0193].

#### The path

- **Cambodia:** the worker flies in on a one-month visit visa, not a work visa; Bangladeshi brokers meet him at the airport, keep him briefly in a hotel and sell him to a guarded compound, often Chinese-run [I0093, I0088, I0094]. Compounds are in Sihanoukville, along the Thai and Lao borders, and in rural areas [I0107]. Many of about 16,000 who went in 18 months were shown an employment visa before leaving and later given a tourist visa and sold, although they had followed the government's rules, according to the president of Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, an organisation led by trafficking survivors [AB0083]. One returnee was kept three days in a Phnom Penh restaurant, then taken late at night to a scam centre for an "interview"; the people who sent him said this was the work and he would later be sent on to Europe [AB0091, AB0092].
- **Myanmar and Laos:** victims are taken across the Thai border into Myanmar, Laos or Cambodia; Myawaddy on the Thai-Myanmar border is a hub [I0104, I0342]. Eight Bangladeshis who went to Cambodia and Laos for work in 2025 worked there for a time, then were taken by road through Thailand and sold to a scam centre in Myanmar [AB0152].

#### What happens

- **Sold:** men are sold to compounds for USD 1,500 to 10,000; one man was sold to three compounds in 2022 [I0089, I0097, I0102].
- **Forced to scam:** they are held at gunpoint and forced to take part in the scams [I0341]. Those who miss daily targets are beaten or given electric shocks [I0311].
- **Ransom:** a compound demanded USD 5,000 to release two youths; another family was pushed to pay Tk 95,000 more [I0098, I0095].
- **Abandoned:** a man who escaped after three months paid his own ticket and hotel home [I0069].
- **Detained leaving:** on 3 September 2026 Cambodia's immigration police detained 32 Bangladeshis at Phnom Penh airport as they tried to fly home, and held 30 of them in an immigration detention centre [AB0142]. They had gone under the return process set by the Cambodian government but were held for illegal stay [AB0143]. They had been taken there by a broker ring with the lure of a good salary, many were forced into cyber scamming, and those who tried to escape were allegedly tortured [AB0144]. They landed in Dhaka on 20 October 2026 on Thai Airways TG-321, after being held since 3 September [AB0147, AB0148].
- **Escaping into Thailand:** eight Bangladeshis who escaped a Myanmar compound into Thailand had no visa, surrendered to the immigration police and were kept in a safe home on a charge of illegal entry [AB0153].
- **Victims turned recruiters:** many who were first victims later join the same ring and traffic and cheat others, a CID additional inspector general said in July 2026 [AB0030, AB0031].

#### Cases

- Told July 2026: Mannan Soyal, back from three years in Saudi Arabia, was sent to Cambodia by a relative from his area for BDT 5 lakh on the promise of a restaurant job, and left on 5 December 2025 after training [AB0033, AB0034]. A few days after arriving he was taken to a Chinese-run place with thousands of people at computers and told to call himself "Maria", build relationships with people on Instagram and get money from them [AB0036]. When he objected, his passport was already with them, and he was told to pay the money back if he wanted it [AB0037]. He was beaten and locked in a small room for missing his target, and later freed in a police raid [AB0038].
- Told Aug 2026: Tofayel Ahmed came to Dhaka to go to Saudi Arabia, but at a friend's urging went to Cambodia through two brokers, travelling with a BMET smart card [AB0087, AB0088]. He was held about one month and 27 days before he escaped; he was never given the one-year visa and work permit, and became illegal when his one-month single-entry visa ran out [AB0093].
- Reported Oct 2026: two young men had about BDT 6 lakh each taken, their passports taken on arrival, and were sold to a scam centre for USD 5,000 [AB0159, AB0160]. The CID said they were tortured if they refused the fraud work or made even a small mistake, and had no way to leave [AB0161, AB0162]. Their families paid a further USD 5,000 ransom, the traffickers kept control of them after release, and money was collected once more for the tickets home [AB0163, AB0164]. A case was filed at Paltan police station on 26 October 2026 naming Md Shaon Molla (23) and Md Sakib Sarkar (26) of Pubail, Gazipur, and the CID arrested both the next day [AB0167, AB0168, AB0169].

#### The numbers

- **Clearances:** BMET cleared 15,921 workers for Cambodia in 18 months, and BRAC says more than 1,000 of them were trapped in compounds [I0082]; Prothom Alo reported more than 15,000 cleared in the year and a half to July 2026 [RU0201]. In 2025 alone 12,263 took clearance for Cambodia, the eighth-largest destination that year, against 4,727 for Russia [RU0062, AB0172, AB0173].
- **Returns:** 583 trafficking victims came home from Cambodia in June 2026, including 221 in four days, and another 109 on 1 July [VI0027, I0075]. Prothom Alo reported 646 back in June after Cambodian raids [RU0063, AB0176]. About 1,800 had come home as victims of cyber fraud in 2026 by June, according to BRAC [AB0026, AB0027, AB0078, AB0079]. By April 2026 about 50 people rescued from Myawaddy, Myanmar, had been repatriated through the Embassy in Bangkok [I0342, VI0030].
- **What returnees went through:** BRAC found 88% of returnees from Cambodia were held captive with no freedom of movement, 38% suffered serious mental trauma and 27% physical torture [RU0056, AB0078, AB0079]. One report of BRAC's figures gives 86% who had their passports and papers taken, another 87% who had forged papers [RU0056, AB0078, AB0079].
- **Laos:** 7,532 Bangladeshis went to Laos in the 21 months before October 2026 [I0079]. About 3,000 took clearance for Laos in the first eight months of 2026 (13th destination), against 3,835 in 2025 (16th), mainly for construction and manufacturing, BMET sources said [OB0038, OB0039, OB0040].
- **Region:** IOM helped more than 3,500 victims of forced criminality in Southeast Asia in 2022 to 2025, Bangladesh among the largest origin countries [I0108].
- **Gaps in the data:** Bangladesh has no reliable national data on how many people are trafficked to scam centres, come home or get justice, according to UNODC (the UN Office on Drugs and Crime) in July 2026 [AB0086].

> **Need to know**
> - How many people came home from Cambodia in June 2026: 583 or 646?
> - In BRAC's returnee survey, is the 86-87% figure for people whose passports were taken, or for people carrying forged papers?

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** the 2026 Act counts forcing a person into crimes such as online scamming as exploitation under the trafficking offence [VI0003, I0329]. Since July 2026 workers for Cambodia, Laos and Brazil need a proper work visa and full checks before BMET clearance [I0074]. Most published lists of the 12 countries needing Ministry approval since 4 November 2026 include Cambodia (see The Balkans and Eastern Europe) [I0114, I0116, VI0026]. A Home Ministry joint secretary said in July 2026 that cooperation with Cambodia and others against scam centres has been strengthened, and that quick legal action is taken against traffickers even if they are government officials [AB0069, AB0070].
- **Cambodia:** running a scam centre with violence or detention carries 10 to 20 years in prison [I0110]. The 2026 US Trafficking in Persons report says some senior Cambodian officials and police profit from and protect scam centres [I0112]. Cambodian law enforcement shut scam centres under international pressure in mid-2026, after which 583 workers came home in June [RU0057].
- **Laos:** recruitment, work permits and entry visas for Bangladeshi workers are suspended from 1 September to 31 December 2026 [I0078]. The Ministry of Labour and Social Welfare's directive of 9 September 2026 cites illegal entry, trafficking, unauthorised labour suppliers and employers who evade responsibility, without saying why Bangladeshis were singled out [OB0029, OB0030, OB0031]. Nobody may let Bangladeshis enter or work in Laos on their own, and the LA-B2 labour visa stays closed to them until the end of 2026, except for large government-approved priority projects [OB0032, OB0033]. A worker found working outside his approved workplace is fined 1 million kip (USD 44) each time and sent home within 30 days at the cost of the employer or whoever broke the rule; employers pay 2.5 million kip (USD 110) per worker for hiring without approval, tripled for a repeat [OB0034, OB0035, OB0036]. Asked in September 2026, the Expatriates' Welfare Minister said nothing was officially known and the matter would be looked into [OB0037].

#### Where to get help

- **Bangladesh Embassy, Bangkok** (covers Cambodia; there is no embassy in Phnom Penh): 47/8 Ekamai Soi 30, Sukhumvit 63, Bangkok 10110; +66 (0)2 390 5107 to 8; welfare hotline +66-818-70-84-43 [I0237, I0238, I0239, I0240]. Tofayel Ahmed applied to return through the embassy on Facebook after police raids began and came home on 14 June once it was approved [AB0094].
- **Cambodian police anti-trafficking hotline:** 1288 [I0236].
- **BRAC:** families who contacted BRAC got 18 men out of a Myanmar compound in March 2025, with the Foreign Ministry, the CID and the Embassy in Thailand [I0338]. Mannan Soyal had no passport, and BRAC arranged a travel pass and a ticket home [AB0039]. Two young men stuck in a Cambodian hotel for about two months after release reached the BRAC Migration Centre online and came home on 3 September 2026 [AB0165, AB0166].
- **Through the Wage Earners' Welfare Board:** after an application from Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, the Board's expatriate welfare branch wrote to the labour wing of the Bangkok embassy asking it to free the 30 detained in Cambodia and send them home quickly [AB0145, AB0146]. For the eight held in a Mae Sot immigration safe home in Thailand, the Foundation applied to the Board on 24 August 2026 on complaints from victims and families, and the Board's director instructed the embassy's First Secretary (Labour) to free them and send them home [AB0154, AB0155, AB0156]. Three were due home on 22 October 2026, while the other five were still in the legal process [AB0157, AB0158].
- **After return:** returnees need to be treated as survivors, not criminals, with regular follow-up, rehabilitation and mental health care so they are not drawn back into the rings, the Foundation's president said in July 2026 [AB0085].
