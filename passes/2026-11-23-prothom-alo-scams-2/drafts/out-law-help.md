### Refund refusals after a failed departure

**How it works:**

1. The worker doesn't go, or comes back without the job [S0104].
2. The agency or dalal delays, offers a fresh visa "soon", or gives cheques that bounce [S0025].
3. A destination can close after people have paid: the Maldives stopped issuing new work visas to Bangladeshis from April 2024 because Bangladesh had filled the cap of 100,000 workers from any one country set by a Maldivian law, according to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry and the Bangladesh High Commission, which announced the stop on 22 May 2024 [OA0036, OA0037, OA0038].
4. Many victims bargain with the broker instead of going to law: the CID said so of Cambodia returnees in July 2026 [S0122]. A CID acting additional inspector general said in July 2026 that many people tortured abroad compromise after coming home or don't want to give evidence, which obstructs trials [AB0064].
5. Some turn to local politicians: the administrator of Cumilla City Corporation said in 2026 that politicians like him receive many complaints that agencies took people's money and didn't send them, and have to pressure the middlemen to keep their promise or return the money [AA0227, AA0228].

**Red flags:** a promise of a new visa instead of the money back [S0025].

**Cases:**

- 20 May 2024, Sherpur, Bogura: Lailul Haque said that after he came back from Malaysia he went to the dalal Abdul Alim several times for his money, that Alim gave assurances but didn't pay, then moved away from Sherpur and stopped answering his phone; local people said Alim was staying in Tarash, Sirajganj [MA0016, MA0017]. That night five young men who had first gone to Alim at least a year and a half earlier filed a written complaint at Sherpur police station; the officer-in-charge said the necessary legal action would be taken, and the report gives no case or arrest [MA0018, MA0019, MA0020].
- 22 May 2024, the Maldives: 6,436 people had taken BMET clearance to go since unskilled workers began to be sent in January 2024, and a large share of them were stuck without visas, a number no one could confirm [OA0043].
  - Monir Hossain of Chattogram said he paid Tk 2.6 lakh through an acquaintance, with Tk 2 lakh more due when the visa came; the visa promised for March had not come, he could not tell whether it ever would, and he was paying monthly interest on a Tk 1 lakh loan [OA0039, OA0040].
  - Sheikh Alamin of Chandpur said he paid Tk 35,000 to a dalal he knew, the market closed again only three months after reopening, many who had deposited money were left uncertain, and the dalal was still giving assurances [OA0041, OA0042].
- 31 May 2024, Malaysia: agencies failed to send about 17,777 workers with BMET clearance by Malaysia's deadline that day, and after a ministry probe the agencies were directed to refund them by 18 July 2024 [S0104]. In July 2024 the state minister said BAIRA reported 70% refunded but he could not yet say how many had been paid, and in Jan 2025 the ministry secretary said 81% of agencies had refunded [S0105, S0106].
  - Orders of the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC), announced on 3 June 2024, cite an allegation that in the last 10 days of May about Tk 150 crore was taken from about 30,000 passengers in the name of air tickets, an extra Tk 50,000 each, and call for an investigation and legal action [MA0037, MA0038, MA0039].
  - A Prothom Alo column of 4 June 2024, citing newspaper and television reports, said workers bought plane tickets at three to four times the price with money raised by selling or mortgaging land and livestock and borrowing at interest, and still could not go [MA0045].
- April to May 2025: Abdul Gani said that after a fake ticket and visa the dalal Shafiqul Islam was to return his money but fled the area from 10 April, and that with no refund and under pressure from his dadon (high-interest informal) loan he filed a written complaint with RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion, an elite police force); another complainant said he sold everything and borrowed to pay, was not sent abroad, and was threatened when he asked for his money [FV0059, FV0060].
- 18 Aug 2026: the Expatriates' Welfare and Overseas Employment Minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, said action was being taken against unauthorised and fake recruiting agencies and recruitment was being brought online; he gave no number or names [AB0099, AB0100].
- 15 Nov 2026: the minister told Parliament 56 agencies' licences had recently been cancelled for irregularities and fraud [S0184].

**What to do:** in a 2025 study by OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) only 39% of the complainants' BMET cases were resolved, typically with Tk 20,000 to just over Tk 1 lakh, though workers had paid three to six times the government cost [S0057]. A civil suit can claim the full loss (see Sue for compensation) [S0161]. Local mediation has also recovered money (see Local committees and mediation) [AA0229, AA0230].

### Where to complain and get help

#### Complain to BMET

1. **Online:** since 30 July 2026, the worker or a family member can file on OEP's Online Complaint Management Module (OEP is the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system), upload evidence, follow progress and get a notification at each stage [S0045, MB0068, MB0069]. The module, built by BMET and RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), is to bring every District Employment and Manpower Office (DEMO) and BMET's central complaint handling into one system [MB0068, MB0070].
2. **On paper:** BMET's complaint form (Form Ka) asks for your name, NID (national ID card), passport and BMET smart card numbers, addresses and the agency's or broker's details; hand it in at BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, or your DEMO [S0044, S0063].
3. **What happens:** free; an inquiry committee holds a hearing, BMET recommends and the ministry decides, within 30 working days [S0043].
4. **If nothing happens:** BMET's complaint officer, the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 (3 months); then the ministry Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd (1 month); then grs.gov.bd (3 months) [S0046].

Under section 41 of the OEM Act the inquiry must finish within 30 working days, and a proven complaint may be settled by order or arbitration within 3 months of the inquiry's end [S0167, S0168, VS0041]. If an agency refuses to pay an arbitration award, BMET may deduct it from the agency's security deposit, according to a 2026 study by RMMRU [VS0040].

**In practice:** many complaints stay unresolved for years [S0056]. BMET's Director General said in July 2026 that in 2025 BMET settled 500 complaints by the manual process and obtained Tk 10 crore in compensation for migrants [MB0067]. In Cox's Bazar, the DEMO's assistant director said in June 2026 that about 30 complaints lodged with BMET and his office had been settled and reports sent on them; he gave no period [MB0032].

#### Complain to the Ministry

- **Enforcement-2 branch** of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare: application with proof, your full address and the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and address; free; action in 3-5 working days, settlement in 30; 02-41030255, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd [S0047].
- **Mobile courts** under the OEM Act give immediate remedies; free; 7 working days [S0139].
- **Hotline:** 16135 (toll free), +880 9610102030 from abroad [S0048].

The Ministry's secretary said in July 2026 that most complaints it gets from migrants turn out to be true, and that the most common are money taken on a promise of sending someone abroad, a migrant's property at home being seized, and a person going missing abroad [MB0071, MB0072]. The state minister said in May 2026 that complaints of irregularities and excess cost do come, that there are syndicate, test and recruitment problems in Malaysia, Korea, Saudi Arabia and other countries, that insecurity for women migrants is more frequent in some Middle Eastern countries, that the scope for legal remedy used to be limited, and that agreements with some countries, which he did not name, have now created scope to file cases and recover compensation [OA0190, OA0191, OA0192].

> **Need to know**
> - Which countries' agreements now let a worker file a case and recover compensation there, and how does a worker start one?

#### File a police case

- **Section 31 of the OEM Act:** taking or trying to take money on a false promise of a job abroad, holding a migrant's passport or papers without lawful reason, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay: up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [S0159, S0160]. It applies to any person, not only agencies [S0052].
- **Section 33:** obtaining a demand letter, visa or work permit by illegal means and buying or selling it in Bangladesh: 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and at least Tk 3 lakh [S0054, VS0042].
- **Section 35:** an unregistered sub-agent: 1 year or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [S0170].
- **Section 34:** sending someone out other than through an official exit point: up to 10 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [S0164].
- **How it is handled:** sections 31, 33 and 34 are cognisable (police can register a case and arrest without a warrant), non-bailable and can't be settled privately; sections 32, 33A, 35 and 35A can be tried by a mobile court [S0165, S0166]. Cases are tried by a first-class judicial or metropolitan magistrate [S0169].
- **Emergency:** 999 [S0087].

#### File a trafficking case

- **The law:** the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 replaced the 2012 trafficking act [VS0014, VS0001].
- **What counts:** recruiting, moving or holding someone by deceit or by exploiting their vulnerability, for sexual, labour or other exploitation inside or outside Bangladesh, is trafficking (section 5) [VS0002]. So is luring or helping someone to go abroad for work knowing they may be exploited [VS0003]. Exploitation includes a fraudulent marriage, forcing someone into online scamming, and holding someone for ransom [VS0004, VS0005, VS0006].
- **Penalty:** at least 5 years' rigorous imprisonment up to life and at least Tk 50,000; for an organised group, death, life or at least 7 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [VS0007, VS0008]. Migrant smuggling, getting someone illegally into a country where they are not a citizen or resident, carries 3 to 10 years and at least Tk 1 lakh [VS0044].
- **Where:** a complaint can go to a police station or directly to the Tribunal, and police must protect the complainant and keep their identity secret (section 28) [VS0009]. Until Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Suppression Tribunals are set up, the government can appoint the district's Women and Children Repression Prevention Tribunal judge, or the district and sessions judge, to hear these cases [VS0010].
- **Who files:** cheated workers returning from various countries file trafficking cases, and in some cases so do the families of migrants killed abroad, Prothom Alo reported in July 2026 [RU0059].
- **Compensation:** on conviction the Tribunal can order the trafficker to pay the victim (section 46) [VS0011].
- **In practice:** in the roughly 4,400 to 4,500 trafficking cases of 2020 to 2024, 94 to 95% of the accused were acquitted, according to Home Ministry data, and in January to May 2026 about 500 new cases were filed while 17 were disposed of (see What happens to trafficking cases) [RU0051, LB0187, LB0188, RU0050].

#### Sue for compensation

Anyone harmed by a breach of the OEM Act or the employment contract can file a civil suit for compensation alongside a criminal case (OEM Act section 28) [S0161]. A trafficking or smuggling victim has the same right under section 56 of the 2026 trafficking act [VS0012]. In practice workers often can't keep a case going for lack of money and legal aid [S0109].

#### Local committees and mediation

- **Mediation:** Jesia Khatun of WARBE Development Foundation, a migrants' rights organisation, said in April 2026 that a significant amount of money had been recovered through mediation in the past three years; she gave no amount [AA0229].
- **Grievance redress committees:** Iqbal Mahmud of The Asia Foundation said at the same time that committees formed through municipalities, upazila (sub-district) and union parishads (the lowest tier of local government) are working at district and local level, but on a limited scale [AA0230].
- **Committees that exist but sit idle:** at an April 2026 roundtable in Cumilla, the principal of the Cumilla Technical Training Centre recommended activating union-level complaint settlement committees, making sub-agent registration compulsory, expanding airport help desks, opening one-stop service centres and making payment through banks compulsory, and WARBE's Cumilla officer called for migration information services and a "migration corner" in each union [OA0185, OA0186, OA0187]. At the same roundtable a union parishad member from Amratoli, Cumilla Sadar, named getting no help with complaints among the main problems, alongside torture, excessive workload and withheld wages, and WARBE's officer listed dalal fraud, excess cost, unpaid wages and missing papers abroad, going without skills and little rehabilitation on return [OA0188, OA0189]. In Cox's Bazar in June 2026 the regional head of the NGO YPSA said counter-trafficking committees exist at district, upazila and union level and need to be made active [MB0036].
- **District committees:** the Cox's Bazar DEMO's assistant director said in June 2026 that a District Migration Coordination Committee and a District Vigilance Task Force Committee are active against unsafe migration there, that upazilas have been told to form the same committees, and that a pilot programme helps people in Chakaria decide before going abroad [MB0033, MB0034, MB0035].

> **Need to know**

> - Does WARBE Development Foundation mediate refunds for walk-in complainants, and at which offices?

#### Free legal help

- **The right:** migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to reasonable legal aid (OEM Act section 27) [S0134].
- **Government legal aid helpline:** 16699 [S0051].
- **OKUP:** free, confidential legal advice and help filing a case, 01842773300 [S0049].
- **BNWLA** (the Bangladesh National Women Lawyers' Association): an advocate said in June 2026 that it was conducting the cases of about 100 returnees from abroad in the Cox's Bazar court, and that about 465 human trafficking cases were pending [MB0043].

> **Need to know**

> - What number does BNWLA give returnees who need a lawyer, and does it take cases outside Cox's Bazar?

### The law in Bangladesh

#### The 2026 trafficking and smuggling Act

The Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Prevention and Suppression Act, 2026 (Act No. 55 of 2026), passed in April 2026, repealed the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking Act 2012 and took effect at once [I0105, VI0001, VI0002, VI0032]. It replaced an ordinance of the same name, first approved by the interim government's Council of Advisers on 27 November 2025 [I0326, VI0022]. For the first time it separates trafficking and smuggling as two crimes [I0331]. It applies to anyone who commits one of its offences against a Bangladeshi abroad [VI0020]. A Home Ministry joint secretary said in July 2026 that it also covers fraud through fake online job adverts, and that the biggest challenge now is enforcing it and preventing fraud inside the regular migration process [AB0066].

- **Trafficking:** at least 5 years' rigorous imprisonment, up to life, and a fine of at least Tk 50,000; by an organised gang, death or life imprisonment, or at least 7 years, and a fine of at least Tk 5 lakh [VI0004, VI0005].
- **What counts as exploitation:** among other things, forced labour, debt bondage, forcing someone into crimes such as online scamming, and holding someone to demand ransom [VI0033, VI0003].
- **Migrant smuggling** (getting someone into another country illegally for gain): 3 to 10 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh; if the migrant is gravely hurt, dies, is put in danger or treated cruelly, 5 years to life [VI0006, VI0008].
- **Documents and ads:** making, obtaining, supplying or holding a document to help smuggling, 1 to 3 years; advertising for smuggling online, on social media or in print, 3 to 7 years and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [VI0007, VI0009, VI0010].
- **Victims:** a trafficking victim cannot be charged for illegal entry or stay abroad, or for getting or holding a false travel or identity document, if it was a direct result of the trafficking [VI0019]. Threatening a victim, the family or a witness carries 3 to 7 years, and pressuring a victim or family to settle 2 to 5 years [VI0011, VI0012].
- **Filing a case:** anyone can complain at a police station or to the tribunal; the police must protect the complainant and keep their identity confidential [VI0014]. All offences are cognisable, non-bailable and can't be settled privately [VI0013].
- **Powers:** on the investigator's application the tribunal can freeze bank accounts, block the sale of property, and bar an accused from leaving the country for up to 60 days; an accused who absconds can be tried in absence [VI0015, VI0023, VI0016, VI0017].
- **Courts:** the government can set up a Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Suppression Tribunal in each district, and it can try a connected offence in the same case [VI0021, VI0018]. In 2026 Justice and Care, an NGO, was supporting about 400 cases in 18 tribunals [I0330].

#### The Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013

- **False job promises (s.31):** taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, or keeping a migrant's passport or papers, is punished by up to 5 years in prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [I0197, I0198].
- **Visa trading (s.33, amended 2023):** getting a demand letter, visa or work permit illegally, or buying or selling one in Bangladesh, is punished by 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment [I0189].
- **Unregistered dalals (s.35, added 2023):** acting as an agency's sub-agent without registration is punished by 1 year in prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [I0190]. Agencies may appoint only registered sub-agents [I0204].
- **Leaving only by air:** BMET's website, as cited by Prothom Alo in July 2025, says a worker must leave with BMET's emigration clearance (smart card) by lawful means, and that going by sea or by any route other than the three international airports, Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet, is a punishable offence [OA0113, OA0114]. Speakers at a BRAC and Prothom Alo roundtable in Sylhet in April 2026 said the same [LB0189, LB0190, LB0191].
- **No bail:** offences under ss.31, 33 and 34 are non-bailable and can't be settled privately [I0195].
- **Complaint without a court case (s.41):** anyone cheated can complain to the government against any person, including an agency [I0196].
- **Legal aid (s.27):** migrants and people defrauded in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [I0200].
- **Mobile courts** under the Act give immediate remedies to would-be and returned migrants, free, in 7 working days [I0201].
- **Licences cancelled or withdrawn:** according to Ministry sources in July 2026, an agency's licence is cancelled when it breaks the licence conditions and withdrawn when it commits a serious offence or is a threat to national security; getting a withdrawn licence back may take a legal fight [MB0058].
- **Women:** Farida Yasmin, a Supreme Court lawyer advising the Bangladesh Migrant Women Workers Association, said in January 2024 that the amended Act still does not separately address the protection of women [AA0044].

#### What happens to trafficking cases

- **2018 to 2025 (CID):** the CID's Trafficking in Human Being (THB) unit counts 1,657 people charged in 536 cases, of whom only 353 could be arrested [RU0071].
- **2020 to 2024 (Home Ministry):** in about 4,400 to 4,500 cases under the 2012 Act, 94 to 95% of the accused were acquitted; a Prothom Alo editorial added that in most incidents no case is filed at all [RU0051, LB0187, LB0188].
- **2021 and 2022 (Home Ministry sources):** no accused was convicted in a single trafficking case [AA0004].
- **January to September 2023 (Home Ministry):** courts disposed of 332 cases, and in 316 of them (95.18%) all the accused were acquitted; 12,279 accused were acquitted, and in the 16 cases that ended in punishment 56 people were sentenced, two of them to life [AA0001, AA0002, AA0003, LB0009, LB0010]. In the same nine months 683 new cases were filed, with charge sheets in 31 and final reports in 18; of 30,539 cases in courts up to September 2023, 25,000 were under trial and 5,327 more under investigation [AA0005, AA0006]. Cases under the Act had been filed against 289,472 people, of whom 132,638 had been arrested [AA0007].
- **2023 (US State Department's 2024 trafficking report):** the government identified 1,210 trafficking victims (210 of sex trafficking, 795 of forced labour, 205 other), while civil society and international organisations counted at least 10,135, 1,784 of them of sex trafficking; the report says the government does not regularly publish its anti-trafficking data [AA0117, AA0118, AA0119].
- **2024 (Home Ministry):** 1,135 cases were filed at police stations and 4,291 were pending, 1,310 of them under investigation; one third of all the accused had been arrested; trials of earlier cases ended with 5 people sentenced to life, 55 to other terms and 1,250 acquitted [AA0121, AA0122].
- **2025 to May 2026 (Home Ministry):** 1,126 new cases were filed in 2025, and up to May 2026 5,284 cases were pending, 3,306 under trial and 1,978 under investigation [RU0049]. Prothom Alo reported that in 2026, 71 new cases were filed in January, 66 in February, 110 in March, 130 in April and 123 in May, while only 17 were disposed of in those five months [RU0050].

#### Why cases fail

- **Investigation:** experts told Prothom Alo in January 2024 that accused are acquitted because cases are not investigated properly, witnesses can't be produced in court, and cases are settled with the trafficking rings for money [AA0008]. Salma Ali, president of BNWLA (the Bangladesh National Women Lawyers' Association), said then that trafficking cases are very profitable for the police: investigating officers take money from both the victim and the accused and hold up the investigation for a long time, and ring members, who are very influential, threaten victims into a compromise [AA0009, AA0010, LB0013].
- **No dedicated investigators:** RMMRU's legal coordinator said in July 2026 that the trafficking tribunals have no designated investigation agency of their own, so investigation reports are late and often no proper investigation is done, and that the Dhaka tribunal had had no judge for five months [AB0058, AB0059].
- **Time and money:** he said a case takes two to three years to go from investigation to trial, many victims can't keep it going because they spent lakhs to go abroad and have nothing left, and many lose a day's income every time they go to court [AB0057]. Salma Ali said in July 2026 that cases drag on for lack of skilled public prosecutors and judges [AB0032].
- **Confidentiality:** she also said many victims stop pursuing a case in the end over the question of confidentiality, so organised criminals get away, and that trafficking in the name of migration continues through land ports, airports and sea routes [AB0061, AB0062].
- **Known traffickers left alone:** OKUP's chairperson said in July 2026 that at a recent meeting in Madaripur the deputy commissioner (the district's top administrator) said he knows the traffickers but has no power to act against them [AB0052, AB0053].
- **What investigators are told to establish:** after no convictions in 2021 and 2022 the Home Ministry instructed investigators that trafficking happens in six stages from going abroad to coming back, and to establish who trafficked the victim, who introduced the victim to the ring, whether the victim was threatened, taken by force or trafficked by exploiting a vulnerability, and the route the ring used [AA0011, AA0012, AA0013].

#### Cases

- 2019 to 2025: OKUP supported the case of an abused woman who came back from the Middle East in 2019; a long investigation found elements of trafficking, in 2025 the special tribunal's judge proposed an out-of-court settlement, and though she said she wanted justice and dignity, not money, the case was settled for a little over Tk 4 lakh, according to OKUP's chairperson [AB0054, AB0055].
- January 2024, Savar: the father of a woman trafficked to India on a promise of Canada said that after he filed a trafficking case the accused filed a false case against him at Savar police station, he was arrested and spent four months in jail at a cost of Tk 5 to 6 lakh, and he had heard all the accused were acquitted [AA0015, AA0016, LB0011, LB0012]. He said the investigating officer told him many times he would be taken to court to testify but never took him, and that two of the acquitted were back in the area; the sub-inspector who investigated said the case had long since gone to the CID and he had no update [AA0017, AA0018].
- July 2026: Mannan Soyal, back from a scam centre in Cambodia, told a Prothom Alo roundtable in Dhaka that after he filed a trafficking case against the people who cheated him, the accused filed a counter-case, so he is now himself an accused and has no money to fight it, and he alleged the fraudsters were pressing him to take money and withdraw [AB0028, AB0029, AB0040].

### Where victims and families get help

1. **In danger in Bangladesh:** call 999, the police's national emergency line, free, 24 hours [I0231].
2. **File a criminal case with the local police.** CID asks returnees to file cases with their local police so the brokers and agencies can be prosecuted [I0356]. Under the 2026 Act a case can be filed at a police station or directly with the tribunal, and the police must protect the complainant and keep their identity confidential [VI0014]. A Chandpur family's case in December 2025 led to an arrest [I0365]. Two youths trafficked to Cambodia came home on 3 September 2026, a case naming two men was filed at Paltan police station, Dhaka, on 26 October, and the CID arrested both the next day, according to the CID [AB0170, AB0171]. In 2020 to 2024, 94 to 95% of the accused in trafficking cases were acquitted (see What happens to trafficking cases) [RU0051].
3. **Report to the CID.** The CID's Trafficking in Human Being (THB) Unit leads trafficking investigations nationwide and asks the public to report trafficking, forged visas and smuggling to it [I0357, I0361].
4. **Airport police.** A deputy inspector general of the Airport Armed Police Battalion (APBn) said in August 2026 that it counsels trafficking victims and returnees, tries to get them rehabilitated through BRAC and other organisations, takes and investigates their cases, has brought victims back from Iran, Libya and Greece, and tries to arrest accused people in Bangladesh even when the crime happened abroad [AB0116, AB0117].
5. **Call the Probash-Bondhu call centre** of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, 24 hours: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [I0219].
6. **Apply to the Welfare Board.**
   - **District office:** the Probashi Kallyan Centre is the Board's district office; in Cumilla it began work in July 2023, taking over services the district manpower office used to give, and an application through it for a migrant in trouble abroad gets legal help with the embassy's assistance [AA0231, AA0232].
   - **Membership:** a Board director said in May 2026 that anyone, documented or undocumented, can become a member for Tk 400, and that the Board gives a member who falls ill, is jailed or is in danger abroad legal help through the embassy's labour wing [OA0195, OA0196]. He also said that with a Tk 300 membership a dead worker's body is brought home from any country, Tk 35,000 is paid for burial and transport, a further Tk 3 lakh goes to the heirs, and up to Tk 10 lakh more is paid if there is insurance (see If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?) [OA0198, OA0199].
   - **Money for illness, injury and children:** a worker who applies because of illness within one year of coming home gets a one-time grant of up to Tk 2 lakh (see What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?), and a family gets Tk 2,000 a month for up to seven years for each disabled child, for at most two children [OA0197]. A worker injured or maimed abroad gets up to Tk 4 lakh against a Tk 500 premium, and a five-year life insurance policy costs Tk 1,000 [OA0198, OA0199].
   - **Unpaid money abroad:** the Board helps recover dues owed abroad through 22 law firms in 12 countries, and has given more than 239,000 people training, counselling and financial help under its reintegration policy [OA0200, OA0201]. The Cumilla centre's assistant director said the Board helps through the embassy to recover unpaid insurance and compensation, that embassies can appoint lawyers, that safe homes for women workers operate abroad, that there is a dormitory for overnight stays near the airport, and that the Board gives education stipends to migrants' children and an allowance for disabled children [AA0234, AA0235, AA0236].
   - **An example:** on an application by Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, the Board wrote to the labour wing of the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok asking it to arrange the release and quick return of 30 Bangladeshis held in Cambodian immigration detention; they flew home on 20 October 2026 [AB0149, AB0150, AB0151].
7. **Complain against an agency to BMET** (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training): a written application with proof to the Director General [I0224]. Director (Emigration): +8802222222415, 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [I0223]. Hotline of OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system): 09614445271 [I0225].
8. **Complain to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare:** enforcement branch, with proof, your address and the agency's licence (RL) number [I0222]. From abroad, email the complaint cell at minister.expat@gmail.com [I0221].
9. **If the family is threatened at home,** apply to the Welfare Board directly or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's (district administration) office or the DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office); the Board writes to the local authorities [I0229].
10. **District and union committees:** Cox's Bazar has a District Vigilance Task Force Committee and a District Migration Coordination Committee against unsafe migration, and counter-trafficking committees exist down to union level, though an NGO head there said they need to be made active (see Local committees and mediation) [MB0033, MB0036].
11. **At the airport on return:** the Welfare Board's desk at Dhaka airport, +88 02 8901040, +880 1971 430203 [I0226]. Returnees from Cambodia and Europe got emergency help, counselling and money to get home there from BRAC's Migration Programme [I0241, I0061]. BRAC's Migration Programme said in March 2025 that 250 to 300 migrant workers a day were coming back empty-handed, without proper clothes or money, and that it gives each Tk 1,000 for the fare home [AA0141].
