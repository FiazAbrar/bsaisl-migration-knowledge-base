### Is it open now, and who can go?

**Closed to new workers since 31 May 2024. A restart was announced in October 2026, but as of 8 November no one has flown under it.** Until the Ministry issues instructions, don't pay anyone, do a medical or hand over your passport [U0042, Z0151, Z0159, YI0005]. A medical done before the official start doesn't count (Step 2) [Z0159, YI0005].

**Who can go:** Immigration sets three conditions [W0073, W0041]:

- aged 18 to 45 when the employer applies;
- passed fit at a medical centre in Bangladesh;
- a job in one of the five allowed sectors (manufacturing, construction, plantation, agriculture, services).

No agreement covers recruiting Bangladeshi women, and the High Commission tells women not to go for general work [N0028, L0076]. Which routes are legal, and for whom: see The channels. Extra tests and narrower conditions: see Skill tests and narrower job orders.

> **Need to know**
> - Do any Bangladeshi women hold Malaysian factory work passes, and through which agencies did they go?

#### Status

Nothing is open through private agencies yet [Z0159, YI0001].

- **31 May 2024:** Malaysia's last day for new workers to enter; the corridor has been shut since [U0042].
- **June 2026:** Prime Minister Tarique Rahman held talks with Malaysia's Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim during a visit to Malaysia; a Prothom Alo editorial of 22 July said the market was reopening to Bangladeshis after those talks [MB0017].
- **21 August 2026:** Malaysia published the names of 25 Bangladeshi recruiting agencies on FWCMS, its online foreign-worker system (see Who controls it) [MB0018].
- **15 October 2026:** Bangladesh announced that recruitment had restarted through agencies on Malaysia's list [Z0151].
- **31 October:** medical checks were due to start but did not, because no list of approved centres had been published [Z0159, YI0004].
- **4 November:** the expatriates' welfare minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, said a list of 46 centres had gone to Malaysia for vetting, and centres already testing Malaysia-bound workers were doing so illegally and would face action [YI0001, YI0003, YI0002].

The minister hopes the first flights under the restart leave in December [YD0005]. Some workers still go outside the restart (see Size) [B0089, YD0016].

Malaysia has agreed only in principle to take 10,000 workers at zero cost, the first through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government-owned recruiting agency), and no cost cap is set for private agencies (see The cap) [Z0146, Z0154, U0043, YD0007, YA0066]. The minister says these workers will pay nothing and be picked by online lottery from applicants registered on OEP, through BOESL circulars such as its 5 November call for 112 men at DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia), which took applications from 6 to 8 November (Step 1, item 1; Step 1, item 3) [YD0006, W0004, W0001, W0002]. Dr Selim Reza of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies wrote in Prothom Alo on 4 September 2026 that, by the government's account, the first 10,000 are to fly through BOESL on special chartered flights free of every cost including airfare, that more are to follow, and that various statements have mentioned about two lakh workers [MB0063, MB0064]. Anyone selling a "zero-cost" or "BOESL" place is selling something the government says is free (Step 1, item 3) [YD0006, W0020]. Selim Reza wrote that a claim that paying money will get a name onto the list of 10,000 should be treated as likely fraud; that unless who may apply, the qualifications needed, how people are chosen and where to apply are published, the scheme could become a new line of business for brokers; and that the Ministry's warning not to hand over money, a medical test or a passport until government instructions come must be enforced [MB0065, MB0066, MB0067].

The government contradicts itself on what "zero cost" means. The Ministry says the 10,000 go free of all migration costs including airfare, and the minister says not a single taka [Z0154, YD0006]. The state minister, Nurul Haque Nur, said in late October that they would still pay the airfare, the medical and a small BOESL service charge, and put the cost at Tk 1.55 to 1.60 lakh a worker [YD0008, U0043]. The DHL circular itself says zero-cost [W0001]. Employers have begun filing demand letters (চাহিদাপত্র, job orders) through FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralised Management System, Malaysia's online visa system) [YD0011]. The Bangladesh-Malaysia MoU (memorandum of understanding) runs to 31 December 2026; on 5 November 2026 the minister said a new one would be signed on 31 December and wants every licensed agency allowed in [Z0156, YA0068, YA0067].

> **Need to know**
> - Do zero-cost workers still pay the airfare, the medical and a small BOESL service charge, as the state minister said in late October [YD0008]?

#### The channels

- **A private agency on Malaysia's list:** closed since 31 May 2024 and not yet running again: a restart through listed agencies was announced on 15 October 2026, but nothing is open through them yet (see Status) [U0042, Z0151, Z0159, YI0001]. It is the route nearly everyone used in 2022-24 (numbers: see Size; cost: see What people really pay; how the job passes through the agencies: see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut) [A0001, A0059].
- **Through a relative in Malaysia:** a relative can find the job, but the visa still goes through a listed agency, so this route is shut while the agency route is (Step 1, item 5) [Z0148, RE0001].
- **BOESL:** circulars on boesl.gov.bd, a lottery among OEP-registered applicants, then the employer's interview; zero-cost by BOESL's and the minister's account (disputed: see Status), but few places: it sent only 1,381 under the 2022-24 round (Step 1, item 3) [W0004, YD0006, A0059]. It had demand for more than 10,000 workers, but had taken only 1,467 clearances by 24 April 2024 [MA0063, MA0064], and Prothom Alo put those it sent at about one and a half thousand [MB0059]. Two BOESL officials told Prothom Alo that because its workers went free, at the employer's cost, obstacles came in many ways: demand letters went into other agencies' names and took time to bring back, and BOESL itself had to spend money to get demand letters for employer-paid recruitment [MA0063, MA0064, MB0059, MB0060].

  > **Need to know**
  > - How many workers did BOESL send to Malaysia in the 2022-24 round: 1,381 [A0059], about 1,500 [MB0059], or the 1,467 cleared by 24 April 2024 [MA0063]?

- **CLAB (Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad, Malaysia's government construction recruiter), through BOESL:** construction only; it rejected about a third of those it interviewed on health and suitability [YD0016, D0036].
- **Employment Pass:** professionals on at least RM5,000 a month [E0011].
- **No legal route:** domestic work (Bangladesh is not an approved source country) [L0076, L0194]; women in general jobs (no agreement exists, and the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur tells women not to go) [N0028]; and jobs in Sabah, where there is no recruitment agreement and rings collect passports and cash [N0029]. In a Facebook warning of 1 August 2025, the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur said an organised ring was collecting passports and cash by promising Sabah jobs, that the two governments have no agreement or understanding on recruiting for Sabah and the Sabah authorities have not decided to hire Bangladeshi workers, and that any permission would be announced on the High Commission's website [MA0112, MA0113, MA0114]. The main irregular route is a tourist visa used to work (see What goes wrong most, and what people wrongly believe) [N0026].

#### Skill tests and narrower job orders

There is no skill test except in construction, where since January 2025 a worker needs a CIDB (Construction Industry Development Board) skills certificate, tested at Khulna Technical Training Centre (Step 1, item 2) [W0041, G0007, B0001]. Job orders can be narrower, such as men aged 22 to 35 with SSC or HSC in BOESL's DHL circular (Step 1, item 3) [W0003].

#### Size

About 8 lakh Bangladeshis held Malaysian work passes at the end of 2025, more than any other nationality [U0057]. About 4.5 lakh went in 2022-24 [A0001]. By BMET data cited by Prothom Alo, 12.5 lakh went legally from 1992 to January 2024, more than 3.5 lakh of them in 2023 and 50,000 in 2022 [MA0013]. BMET cleared 1,592 workers for Malaysia from 1 January to 6 November 2026, though the restart has sent no one [B0089, YI0001, Z0159]. Reported sending around this period: CLAB accepted about 800 construction workers in December 2025 and January 2026 [YD0016]; 120 to 150 stranded 2024 workers were still waiting to go through BOESL in October 2026 (see What closed it before) [A0037].

> **Need to know**
> - Who were the 1,592 workers BMET cleared for Malaysia in 2026: stranded 2024 workers, CLAB construction workers, or others?

#### What closed it before

Malaysia has closed to Bangladesh three times in 15 years, Prothom Alo reported on 31 May 2024 [MA0048, MA0049, MA0050]:

- **2009:** Malaysia froze hiring after about 4 lakh Bangladeshis went in 2007-08 and many found no job and became undocumented; the closure followed reports of labour exploitation and allegations that agencies had formed syndicates [YA0011, YA0010, U0041]. Agents had charged over Tk 2 lakh against a fixed Tk 84,000 [YA0009].
- **September 2018:** Malaysia's new government cancelled the 10-agency "G2G Plus" scheme from 1 September, calling it monopolistic, amid allegations of syndication and corruption [YA0003, U0041, YA0004]. About two lakh workers had gone through those 10 agencies, which were alleged to take Tk 3 to 4 lakh from each against a government-fixed Tk 37,000 [YA0001, YA0002]. The round had opened at the end of 2016 [U0041, MA0049]. Prothom Alo reported in 2024 that its online system went in 2016 to a firm named Synerflux, whose owner it names as Dato Seri Abdul bin Amin Nur, a Malaysian citizen of Bangladeshi origin; that with his help the 10 agencies formed a ring; that workers spent Tk 2 to 3.5 lakh against a set cost of Tk 33,375; and that the ring took about Tk 5,500 crore in two years [MA0011, MA0012].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Was the government-fixed cost in the 2016-18 round Tk 37,000 [YA0002] or Tk 33,375 [MA0011]?

- **31 May 2024:** the last round, under the 2021 MoU, ended. Malaysia set this date as the last day for new workers to enter after tens of thousands had paid up to USD 5,000 for jobs that did not exist [W0027, U0042, YA0052]. Malaysia had stopped approving new applications to hire foreign workers; visa applications for workers already approved had to be filed by 31 March 2024 and those with visas had to enter by 31 May, Prothom Alo reported on 21 March 2024, citing Ministry sources [MA0001, MA0002]. From 1 June 2024 no worker from any other source country could enter either, and Prothom Alo reported that the other countries blamed the Bangladeshi syndicate [MA0003].

**What happened to the stranded workers of 2024:**

- About 17,777 workers who had BMET clearance could not fly by the deadline [A0045].
- Other counts differ. By a Prothom Alo column of 4 June 2024, the Ministry and BMET put those with final clearance at 16,970, a BMET source said 31,304 in all could not go because of visa and approval complications, and BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) officials said about 40,000 [MA0068, MA0069]. By June 2025 Malaysia said about 11,000 had been unable to go despite valid calling visas, while agency owners and the press put the number much higher [MA0102, MA0103, MA0104].
- Of the 16,970 with final clearance, some could not get air tickets and others did not get the employer's final consent: the employer had not sent its assurance that it would receive them at the airport, Prothom Alo reported [MA0111].
- Many got no refund and some a small one [YI0011].
- In June 2025 the expatriates' welfare adviser said Malaysia would at first arrange work for about eight thousand of them, though no visible step had yet been taken [MA0102, MA0103, MA0104].
- A revised High Commission notice of 1 August 2025 said Malaysia had approved employment through BOESL for workers among those who could not come by 31 May 2024, in construction or tourism [MA0105, MA0106, MA0107]. Employers were to submit the demand-letter attestation checklist for tourism workers on FWCMS, apply for construction workers through CLAB onto the same portal, and lodge the originals and one set of photocopies with the High Commission [MA0108, MA0109, MA0110].
- Malaysia cleared 7,873 of them to go through BOESL, which charged them another Tk 1,62,500 each [YI0011, W0018, A0056].
- The first of them flew on 25 November 2025, 18 months after the deadline [W0018, YI0012].

### How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut

A job reaches a village down a chain, and every link takes a cut: the employer's quota becomes calling visas; the employer's demand letter is bought in Malaysia; the visa is processed in FWCMS; then a licensed agency; then a village broker (দালাল) [W0043, A0026, YE0029, A0028, Z0148, YE0018, YE0016]. The choke point is FWCMS, built by the company Bestinet: every Bangladeshi worker's visa passes through it, and only BOESL and 25 private agencies chosen by Malaysia can process a visa in it, so every other agency pays one of them [Z0001, YA0024, Z0144, Z0148, A0029]. On the street the whole arrangement is called the সিন্ডিকেট (syndicate) [YI0019].

#### The quota

Quotas are approved on the employer's paper eligibility, not on a checked need, so a quota, and the visa sold from it, can exist with no job behind it; Malaysia's private employment agencies' association says agents apply for 100 workers when a company needs 10 [YE0013, YE0014]. The employer pays a yearly levy per worker, RM1,850 in manufacturing, construction and services and RM640 in plantation and agriculture in Peninsular Malaysia [W0043, M0227]. In October 2026 Immigration broke up an "A-to-B" ring, believed to be run by two Bangladeshi men, that got quotas on false company details and hired the workers out to other firms at RM3,800 to 5,000 profit a head [YE0007, YE0008, YE0009, D0046].

#### Which employer

The employer decides whether a job and pay are waiting when you land, and whether you get your recruitment fee back. The ones to avoid are firms whose quota is far above the work they have: they have left workers with no job. The ones that have paid fees back are large export manufacturers under pressure from foreign buyers [YB0016, YA0071, YI0013].

- **Bad: ghost jobs:** services firms brought in 142,204 workers in 2023 against 20,000 needed; 161 Bangladeshis promised construction jobs had no work or pay for seven months; and a Kuala Lumpur company hired about 600 and gave them no work [YB0016, YB0018, YG0003].
- **No work on arrival, 2024:** in March 2024 two officials of the Bangladesh mission in Malaysia, speaking anonymously, told Prothom Alo that 1.5 to 2 lakh Bangladeshi workers there were unemployed and living in inhuman conditions, that many came to the mission regularly for help, and that the mission could do little [MA0014, MA0015]. That month a member of the Facebook group "Malaysia Probashi Bangladeshi" wrote that his newly arrived brother had found no work for four months [MA0016]. From January to 24 March 2024, 453 workers came home empty-handed and without passports, on travel permits [MA0045]. In May 2024 the Ministry's own count was five thousand who had found no work, while long-settled Bangladeshis said thousands were jobless, some selling goods on pavements or umbrellas in the rain to pay for food [MA0043, MA0044].
- **Good: repaid fees:** after US import bans for forced labour, Top Glove paid workers over USD 30 million and FGV set aside RM112 million to reimburse fees to current and former migrant workers [YA0071, YI0013].

#### Company visa, agent visa, free visa

Workers sort jobs by who holds the visa; what separates the three is which company holds your quota and whether you work at its address [YI0015, YG0005]:

- **কোম্পানি ভিসা (company visa):** you work for the company that applied for you; it pays the visa and the yearly renewal, and keeps your passport [YI0015].
- **"Agent visa":** puts you under an agent or subcontractor who hires you out. Outsourcing companies were abolished in 2019, but some workers who go legally still land with one, are sent to work elsewhere, and can't tell until they arrive [YG0025, YG0026].
- **ফ্রি ভিসা (free visa):** sells the right to work anywhere, which no work pass gives. Every work pass (PLKS) is tied to the employer that applied, free-visa workers pay an agent about RM5,000 (Tk 1.53 lakh) a year to renew, and agents hold back weeks or months of their pay [W0052, YI0016, YI0017].

Working away from the company on your permit is an offence: Immigration's Ops Mahir raid in Ipoh in September 2026 detained 175 foreigners, mostly Bangladeshis, including people working outside the company named on their permit [W0052, YG0005, YG0006]. The calling visa (VDR, the visa approval) comes first and the e-visa after it, both through BOESL or one of the 25; check both (Step 3; Step 5) [Z0148, W0045].

#### The demand letter: the first price

The demand letter is the employer's job order for a set number of workers, and buying it is the first cost in the chain: a Bangladeshi agency buys it from Malaysia [YD0011, YE0029, G0013]. Agency owners say one now costs RM8,000 to 12,000 per worker (Tk 2.45 to 3.67 lakh), against RM2,000 to 3,000 before (2022-24 prices: see [Where a Tk 5.5 lakh deal goes](#where-a-tk-55-lakh-deal-goes)) [G0013, YE0027]. Big agencies buy the demand and pay the middlemen around it, so small agencies can't send workers cheaply [YE0029].

#### Attestation

Every demand letter must be attested by the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur, against the employer's notarised commitment to give the workers work and pay [U0044, W0059]. Attestation is not proof that the job was checked: in 2019 the High Commissioner said the mission lacked the staff to check every case [YE0035].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the High Commission check before attesting a 2026 demand (a site visit, the quota approval, the levy receipt)?
> - How long does attestation take?

#### The processing agency: the toll gate

Every visa must go through BOESL or one of 25 private "processing" agencies, which charge other agencies a fee per worker [Z0148, A0019, A0026]. Malaysia lists 338 Bangladeshi agencies: BOESL, the 25 "processing" agencies, and 312 associates (সহযোগী এজেন্সি), 10 under each of the 25 and 62 under BOESL [Z0144, W0008, YE0024]. An associate may find employers, collect demand and select workers, but the calling visa and e-visa go through BOESL or one of the 25, which issues a no-objection certificate so the associate can get BMET clearance under its own licence [Z0145, Z0148, Z0149]. For 2026, claims for the toll run from a Tk 6,000 processing fee to RM5,500 (about Tk 1.68 lakh), the figure BAIRA members allege (2022-24 toll: see [Where a Tk 5.5 lakh deal goes](#where-a-tk-55-lakh-deal-goes)) [A0019, A0026]. Even the government channel buys visas: of the Tk 1,62,500 BOESL charged stranded workers, its managing director said Tk 1 lakh was for buying visas [A0056].

**How the toll worked in 2022-24.** Once a Malaysian demand letter was finalised, FWCMS distributed it among the approved agencies, not equally, and the workers had to be sent through the agency in whose name it arrived [MA0053, MA0054]. Agency owners outside the list told Prothom Alo that Malaysian companies sent demand letters to them, because of their long business there, but the letters went to a listed agency because their own names were not in FWCMS, so they paid that agency to send their workers: Tk 1.52 to 1.65 lakh a worker, three owners said in May 2024; Tk 1,52,000, four owners said in September 2026 [MA0024, MA0053, MA0054, MB0051, MB0052, MB0053]. Prothom Alo reported that the agencies inside the syndicate took at least Tk 1.5 lakh in "syndicate fee" a worker without doing anything, an allegation the ACC (Anti-Corruption Commission) is investigating [MB0051, MB0052, MB0053]. BAIRA's joint secretary general, Fakhrul Islam, said in May 2024 that with no set rules for making the list, some agencies with no experience of sending workers got on it in collusion with the ringleaders and then earned Tk 1,52,000 a worker sitting at home [MA0060].

> **Need to know**
> - Was the 2022-24 syndicate fee a flat Tk 1,52,000 a worker, or up to Tk 1,65,000 as three agency owners said in May 2024 [MA0024]?

Listed agencies said they only processed visas for others:

- **Sarkar International**, which sent 7,745 workers as a single agency: its proprietor, Mohammad Ali Sarkar, said many who had demand from Malaysia sent workers through it, that it took written undertakings from those agencies on responsibility for the workers, and that it took only the government-fixed amount [MA0055, MA0056].
- **Five M International:** agencies inside the syndicate blamed the workers' extra cost on agencies outside it, which they said bought demand letters from Malaysia at high prices in unhealthy competition; Five M's managing director, Masud Uddin Chowdhury, said on 30 May 2024 that he had not bought even 100 jobs, that all came automatically through the software, that those whose jobs came sent workers through Five M, and that it only did visa processing and took only the government-fixed fee [MA0057, MA0058, MA0059].

**2026: the same position, agency owners say.** The four agency owners told Prothom Alo in September 2026 that an associate will be in the same position, since it cannot send workers directly, and that listed agencies approved without experience are unknown to Malaysian employers, so they will send workers through experienced agencies and take the syndicate fee in return [MB0054, MB0055]. Riazul Islam, BAIRA's immediate past senior vice-president, said no agency applied to be an associate, they were simply made associates; that jobs will come in the names of the 25, not of the associates; and that at least 817 agencies sent workers in the earlier round by paying the syndicate fee [MB0056, MB0057]. BAIRA members said about 1,400 Bangladeshi agencies were once listed on FWCMS, but only 10 were approved in 2016, and in 2022, 25 at first and finally 100 [MB0058].

#### Who controls it

**Bestinet and Amin.** Bestinet was founded by Aminul Islam Abdul Nor ("Amin"), a Bangladesh-born Malaysian; Malaysia's Auditor-General found it collected RM100 per worker without a formal government contract [Z0001, YE0021, D0024]. Bestinet owns Migram (later reported as Migrams), the software of FWCMS; the Bangla press calls Amin "Amin Nor" or "Amin Nur" [MA0051, MB0039, MB0040].

**Amin and Ruhul Amin Swapan, owner of Catharsis International.** The Daily Star reports that the two led the 2022-24 syndicate; Bangladesh police sought their extradition in 2024, both deny wrongdoing, and Amin has not been charged [YA0054, YA0042, YA0045, YA0046, YA0055]. Prothom Alo reported in May 2024 the allegation that Amin controls the whole worker-sending trade from Malaysia, that Swapan, a former BAIRA secretary general, acts as his representative in Bangladesh, and that the two decided who would be in the syndicate [MA0051, MA0052]. BAIRA members allege that Swapan led the forming of the 2022 syndicate, fixed the names of 69 of its 100 agencies and was the channel for money transacted in Malaysia; he did not respond to Prothom Alo [MA0034, MA0035]. RMMRU, a migration research body, said in its report "Trends in labour migration from Bangladesh 2024", presented on 28 December 2024, that Amin, owner of Malaysia's foreign-worker recruitment software, and Swapan jointly built the syndicate, which sent five and a half lakh workers and embezzled USD 200 crore (Tk 24,000 crore) from migrants [MA0070, MA0071]. Prothom Alo reported in September 2026 that Swapan has been in hiding abroad since the fall of the Awami League government in the 2024 uprising, that his agency's licence has been withdrawn, a case against him is under way, and the ACC is also investigating [MB0037, MB0038].

**How the 2022-24 syndicate was formed.** When Malaysia reopened in July 2022 after nearly four years, the choice of agencies was left to the Malaysian government; the Ministry sent it a list of 1,520 recruiting agencies and only 25 were chosen [MA0021, MA0022, MB0011, MB0012]. Prothom Alo reported that there was no policy for the selection and many allegations of influence and bribery in it [MA0021, MA0022]. BAIRA members said the MoU gave Malaysia the job of finalising the agencies, which let one group trade illegally, and Prothom Alo reported that a ring of private agencies in Malaysia assisted the ring [MA0007, MA0008, MA0009]. The list grew in three steps to 100, and it is alleged that the ring's leaders took a large sum from each agency to put its name in [MA0019, MA0020, MB0011, MB0012]; in a memorandum of 5 May 2025, BAIRA members put that price at Tk 5 crore an agency [MA0091, MA0092]. The agencies inside received at least Tk 1.5 lakh "syndicate fee" per worker while sitting idle, and part of it went to the ring's controllers in Malaysia, Prothom Alo reported (how it was charged: see The processing agency: the toll gate) [MA0019, MA0020]. Prothom Alo reported that no other country sending workers to Malaysia (14, by its count in 2024 and July 2026; 11, by its count in September 2026) had such a syndicate arrangement [MA0023, MB0015, MB0016, MB0049, MB0050]. Malaysia's High Commissioner in Dhaka, Haznah Md Hashim, said on 12 March 2024 that syndicates active in both countries were one cause of the problem, and on 29 May 2024 that there may be syndicates beyond the control of both governments [MA0010, MA0067]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 22 July 2026 noted that the UN human rights office (OHCHR) had issued a statement on the plight of Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia [MB0015, MB0016].

**The 2026 list of 25.** A Malaysian committee chose the 25 without the Bangladesh government, 17 of them had never sent a worker to Malaysia, and Prothom Alo reported allegations that Amin and Swapan again decided who joined [A0029, A0027, YB0028]. How it was made:

- Malaysia set 10 conditions; 163 agencies first qualified, and after Malaysia relaxed three conditions and then one more at Bangladesh's request, the Ministry under the interim government sent a final list of 423, Prothom Alo reported [MB0044, MB0045].
- Malaysia published the 25 on FWCMS on 21 August 2026 [MB0018].
- The Ministry's notice of 28 August 2026 said a high-level joint committee of Malaysia's human resources, foreign and home ministries, its anti-corruption commission and law-enforcement agencies vetted the agencies, with not the slightest involvement of the Bangladesh government [MB0042, MB0043].
- Malaysia approved 25 agencies each for Nepal and Bangladesh and 10 each for India, Pakistan and Myanmar, while everyone on the lists sent by countries such as Sri Lanka and the Philippines was approved; the agency owners' associations of four countries protested in a joint statement, and Nepal has begun investigating its list of 25, Prothom Alo reported in September 2026 [MB0046, MB0047, MB0048].

Objections and allegations:

- **5 May 2025:** BAIRA members' memorandum to the Chief Adviser's office alleged that the ringleaders, Amin Nor and the fugitive Swapan, and their Awami League associates were forming a syndicate with some influential political leaders, and that agencies, made members for Tk 5 crore each last time, were this time being charged Tk 14 crore each; it asked that Malaysia be asked to scrap the disputed software [MA0091, MA0092, MA0093, MA0094]. It proposed instead that BMET build a database of interested workers, agencies take workers from it, each worker deposit the government-fixed cost through a bank, and BMET supervise the whole process; 453 agency owners had signed a memorandum with the same demand to the Ministry on 21 April 2025 [MA0095, MA0096].
- **22 and 23 August 2026:** the opposition National Citizen Party (NCP) and Jamaat-e-Islami issued statements of concern [MB0018, MB0019]. The NCP's spokesperson, Asif Mahmud, said the process, criteria and basis on which only 25 were chosen from the 423 were still not clear [MB0020, MB0021]. Jamaat feared the arrangement could push a worker's cost to Tk 6 to 7 lakh, and said Bangladesh has more than two thousand valid recruiting agencies [MB0022, MB0023].
- **September 2026:** several BAIRA members told Prothom Alo that the earlier syndicate agencies and the firms of ministers or MPs are not directly on the list, but the old ringleaders are behind the unfamiliar agencies, and that workers may have to spend Tk 6 to 7 lakh [MB0033, MB0034]. Shariful Hasan, head of BRAC's migration programme, said the choice of 25 proved the syndicate is stronger than the two governments and that workers are again being recruited through Amin Nur's system [MB0061, MB0062]. The state minister for expatriates' welfare, Md Nurul Haque, said the Ministry would set a cost ceiling, and that if any agency accused of trafficking or corruption is on the list, Malaysia would be told and its licence withdrawn [MB0041].

#### Who sent the workers in 2022-24

Prothom Alo reported in July 2026 that agencies of former MPs, Awami League leaders and a city corporation councillor, and many firms new to the sector, sent large numbers of workers after getting into the syndicate [MB0013, MB0014]. The agencies it named in May 2024, with what they sent and what they said:

#### The broker and the village

Most workers (78% in a 2023 survey) reach the agency through a sub-agent, sometimes through five or six levels of brokers [YE0033, YE0016]. The broker is usually a friend, neighbour, relative or returnee, and by October 2026 brokers were already collecting passports, and the owner of one associate agency said he had already collected over 1,000 [RF0030, YE0030, YE0028]. Across Bangladesh's visa trade, when a visa comes through relatives, BAIRA's secretary general says, the money changes hands mainly among them and the agency only does the paperwork [YI0020]. Only a sub-agent registered with BMET may now recruit (Step 1, "Before paying") [YB0001, YB0002].

### What you pay, and what you earn and keep

No cap is set for 2026, and both governments say the employer pays, yet people are told to keep Tk 6 to 7.5 lakh ready [YD0007, A0097, G0011, YD0010]. Much less than the headline wage reaches home: a 2024 permission letter leaves food to the worker, and over 40% of income goes on the mess (shared lodging), transport and food [W0035, YF0002].

> **Need to know**
> - What does a plantation worker take home after food in 2026?

#### The cap

No cap is set for 2026 private-agency recruitment: the agencies proposed at most Tk 2.5 to 3 lakh, the figure the minister expects, and the state minister says nobody followed the old cap [YD0007, YA0066, G0012]. In April 2026 both governments said employers will bear the full cost [A0097]. The old cap, in the Ministry's July 2022 order, was Tk 78,990 for the worker's costs inside Bangladesh, with the employer paying everything in Malaysia, airfare both ways included (Step 1, "Before paying") [R0008, R0009, W0027]. Some 2024 permission letters set less, Tk 41,240 or Tk 38,710 (Step 1, item 6) [W0028, W0034]. For the BOESL zero-cost route, see Status.

#### What people really pay

For 2026, brokers collecting passports tell people to keep Tk 7 to 7.5 lakh ready, and a listed agency's owner says no less than Tk 6 lakh [G0011, YD0010]; BAIRA members (September 2026) and Jamaat-e-Islami (23 August 2026) put it at Tk 6 to 7 lakh (see Who controls it) [MB0033, MB0034, MB0022, MB0023]. In 2022-24 the average was Tk 5.44 lakh, with press and workers' groups giving Tk 3.5 to 6 lakh [A0001, YF0021, B0124]. BAIRA's joint secretary general, Fakhrul Islam, said in March 2024 that though a worker was supposed to go for Tk 80,000, nobody could go for less than Tk 4.5 lakh [MA0006]. Workers call the price the চুক্তি (deal) struck with the broker: "went on a Tk 5 lakh deal" [YF0034, YF0038].

#### Where a Tk 5.5 lakh deal goes

Official fees are about Tk 15,000; the rest pays for the visa trade (the demand letter, the syndicate toll and agents) and usually the airfare, the visa and the medical [A0002, A0028, R0008, YI0025, YI0022]. The 2022-24 pieces reported per worker [A0002, A0028, YE0020, YE0018, Z0164, R0008, YE0019]:

- the demand letter: Tk 1.6 to 2 lakh to middlemen in Malaysia;
- the syndicate toll: Tk 1.07 to 1.52 lakh;
- agents in Bangladesh: about Tk 1.42 lakh, of which an unauthorised middleman takes Tk 50,000 to 1 lakh;
- official fees: about Tk 15,000
  - medical Tk 8,000
  - welfare fee Tk 5,500
  - insurance Tk 1,000
  - clearance Tk 250
  - source tax Tk 800 to 1,200

Together these come to about Tk 4.2 to 5.1 lakh [A0002, A0028, YE0020, Z0164, R0008]. The deal usually also covers the airfare (Tk 45,000 in BOESL's own 2025 costing), the visa and the medical, though the MoU puts all three on the employer [YI0025, YI0022, YI0023, W0027]. Even official items get padded, such as the medical (Step 2) [D0021]; Prothom Alo reported in March 2024 that a business in medical tests ran under the same ring of 100 agencies that took Tk 1.5 lakh a worker, and that many who paid Tk 4 to 5 lakh on average found no job on arrival [MA0004]. When each part is paid: see How it is paid.

**The syndicate's share.** In the CID's August 2025 case, Five M International is alleged to have taken Tk 1.5 lakh a worker, of which Tk 1,07,510 was laundered (see The cases against the syndicate) [MA0117, MA0118]. In total:

- BAIRA members said in March 2024 that the ring alone took Tk 1.5 lakh a worker, more than Tk 6,000 crore on over four lakh workers, and that the corridor made Tk 18,000 crore of business in the previous year and a half [MA0005].
- People in the sector told Prothom Alo in May 2024 that sending about 4.5 lakh people in a year and a half made Tk 24,000 crore of business, about Tk 20,000 crore of it above the government-fixed fee, including more than Tk 6,500 crore of syndicate fee at Tk 1.5 lakh a head [MA0042]; Prothom Alo repeated the Tk 24,000 crore and the Tk 5,44,000 average against the Tk 78,990 cap in July 2026 [MB0013, MB0014].

For 2026, BAIRA members said that on top of a syndicate fee of RM5,500 (Tk 1,68,000), buying each demand letter, that is each visa, will cost RM8,000 to 9,000 (Tk 2,65,000 to 2,75,000), so more than Tk 4,30,000 a worker will go to Malaysia [MB0035, MB0036, A0026].

#### How it is paid

Stages: the deal is paid in stages, for visa processing, the medical and other costs, much of it to a broker [YF0031, YI0022]. A delay raises the price: a Cumilla man's Tk 3.6 lakh became Tk 5.7 lakh [YF0036].

> **Need to know**
> - In 2026, how much of the চুক্তি is paid with the passport, how much when the calling visa comes, and how much before the flight or the BMET card?

Receipts: few are given. Down payments for the passport and medical carry none, and workers get receipts for little of the rest [YF0028, YF0031, YF0032]. Before flying, some workers are made to sign, on video, that they paid only the official fee; in 2025 police cited those signatures to close a trafficking case (see Done to workers) [A0061, YA0029].

#### How they borrow

Nearly all borrow (96% in a 2024 survey of Malaysia workers), most (82%) with two or more loans [A0064]. A typical package is a land mortgage (জমি বন্ধক), cattle sold and loans from relatives: a Natore farmer mortgaged his 16 decimals for Tk 1 lakh and sold four cows for Tk 4.5 lakh to pay a Paltan agency Tk 5.5 lakh, then could not fly [YF0026, YF0027]. Informal loans are usually paid straight to the broker, so nothing records them [A0066, YF0030]. Workers in Malaysia borrow at 10% a month to renew their permits; a microcredit NGO in Bangladesh may charge at most 24% a year, the cap set by the MRA (Microcredit Regulatory Authority) [YG0001, YI0021]. The government's PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) loan comes only after the visa, and many can't find its guarantors (Step 1, "Before paying") [B0017, G0039, G0040].

> **Need to know**
> - What monthly rate does a village moneylender (মহাজন) charge on a Malaysia loan in 2026?
> - What are the usual terms of a land mortgage taken for one: the amount per decimal, who works the land, and when it is returned?

#### What the job pays: a correct payslip

A correct payslip shows basic pay of at least RM1,700 a month (about Tk 52,000), plus overtime at least 1.5 times the hourly rate, 2 times on a rest day and 3 times on a public holiday, up to 104 hours a month (Your rights at work) [Z0137, M0001, M0013, M0020, YI0014]. The deductions allowed [I0014, I0015, M0170, J0090, M0050]:

- 2% for EPF (Employees Provident Fund, Malaysia's retirement fund), about RM34 on the basic, compulsory since October 2025 wages and matched by the employer's 2%;
- 0.5% for SOCSO (Malaysia's social security);
- rent of at most RM100 a month;
- no more than half the wage in total.

Both EPF shares are paid back in full when you leave for good (Leaving at the end of the job) [I0018, I0037]. The levy, the pass renewal and the FOMEMA medical are the employer's to pay [L0068, M0144]. At a Pahang plywood factory in 2026, workers on 8-hour contracts got RM65 a day for 12-hour days: the minimum day rate for normal hours alone, so four hours a day went unpaid [F0031, Z0137]. What workers are made to pay instead: see What goes unpaid.

#### What goes unpaid

43% got less than promised in the 2024 survey [A0064]. The usual losses:

- Overtime unpaid or paid at a flat rate (the Pahang case, see What the job pays: a correct payslip) [F0031].
- Wages held for months, often before the company closes: up to eight months at Kawaguchi, nearly three at Aerofoam in 2026 [F0046, F0038].
- The employer's costs taken from pay: the levy, the yearly renewal, hostel charges, uniforms (RM500 in one case) and "flight-ticket deposits" [F0082, YG0001, J0059, F0039].
- Time with no job, earning nothing while the loan runs [YF0034].

Workers who ask for the promised pay have been fired or told to do more overtime [YF0012]. How to claim: If you aren't paid.

#### Money sent home

Most send through banks, and legal transfers earn the government's 2.5% incentive (how: see Sending money home); over 16% use hundi (an unlicensed money agent), which is an offence in Bangladesh [YF0014, B0104, H0045]. Hundi survives because it converts at the kerb rate, which in 2022 was well above the bank rate, because the hundi man comes to the mess, and because an employer holding your passport shuts you out of banks [YI0006, YI0007, YF0024, YF0016]. The risk: families could not cash money received in agent and MFS (mobile money) accounts frozen for hundi, and Malaysian Immigration arrests hundi operators working behind restaurants and shops [B0103, H0045, H0044, T0019].

#### How long the debt takes

At the 2022-24 price of about Tk 5.44 lakh the cost equals at least a year's wages; counting living costs, repayment takes from 21-22 months to three years [YF0047, YF0003, YF0025]. The 2026 asking price of Tk 6 to 7.5 lakh is higher (see What people really pay) [G0011, YD0010]. Meanwhile 73% spend half or more of their monthly pay on repayments [A0064]. Every month without work or pay, and every renewal the worker is made to pay, adds to it [YF0034, YG0001].

### Step 1: Find a real job

There are three routes to a job, and all start with OEP registration (Step 1, item 1) [W0027, W0030]:

- **A BOESL circular** (Step 1, item 3): zero-cost by BOESL's account, few places [W0004, W0001].
- **A private agency** (Step 1, item 4): only the 25 processing agencies can process the visa [Z0148].
- **A relative's employer** (Step 1, item 5): still goes through those agencies [Z0148].

Whatever the route, the checks that tell a real job from a fake are Step 1, items 4, 6 and 7, Step 1, "The trap" and Step 1, "Before paying".

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP),** BMET's website for job-seekers. Agencies must pick workers from BMET's database of registered job-seekers, and BMET clears no one outside it [W0027, W0030].

   You'll need [G0035, B0139, G0001, G0037]:

   - your NID (national ID card);
   - a phone that can receive codes;
   - a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-money account with Tk 200;
   - your passport details, education and work skills.

   1. In Google Chrome, open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) [B0139, G0035].
   2. Type your name in English exactly as on your NID (no dots; spaces where the NID has them), your mobile and NID numbers, and your date of birth, division and district as on the NID [G0035, B0139]. Type the captcha numbers, accept the terms and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [G0035].
   3. Type the code (OTP) sent to your phone, press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue) [B0139].
   4. Fill in the form, then press "পেমেন্ট করতে এগিয়ে যান" (Proceed to payment), "পেমেন্ট করুন" (Pay), Mobile Banking and your service; enter your account number, the code and your PIN to pay the Tk 200 government fee [B0139, G0001].
   5. Download and print your registration card, which carries your BMET number; you can download it again at [oep.gov.bd/employee-card](https://www.oep.gov.bd/employee-card) with that number, without logging in [B0139, G0036, G0038].
   6. Take 2 printed copies of the registration slip to give your fingerprints [G0001] at any DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office), TTC (Technical Training Centre) or IMT (Institute of Marine Technology) [D0064]. DEMO addresses, phones and emails: [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [G0047].

      > **Need to know**
      > - Does giving fingerprints at a DEMO, TTC or IMT cost anything?
      > - What hours do DEMOs, TTCs and IMTs take fingerprints?
      > - What does the worker get back after giving fingerprints?

   **Check it is real:** register at employee.oep.gov.bd, the address BMET's district office gives [G0001]; use no middlemen and pay fees only through the proper channel, against a receipt [B0042]. **If it fails:** if "Verify" shows an error, check your name, NID number and date of birth match the NID exactly [B0139]; otherwise call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [G0036, B0144].

2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET's TTCs teach 55 trades [B0037]; every TTC, with its principal's name, address, phone and email, is listed at [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6) [G0048, G0049]. Each TTC posts admission notices with courses, minimum education, dates and fees; the Bangladesh-Korea TTC (BKTTC) in Dhaka, for example, posts them at bkttc.dhaka.gov.bd [G0002, G0003]. BKTTC's 3- or 6-month courses (360 or 720 hours), in trades such as welding and plumbing, end with an NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) skills certificate [G0006, G0005].

   You'll need [G0002, G0005, B0140]:

   - your OEP registration;
   - the course's minimum education (at BKTTC, JSC, the Junior School Certificate, for welding);
   - a current, clear photo in your OEP profile.

   1. Log in at [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) with your mobile number and the code sent to it [B0140].
   2. Open the training portal (ট্রেনিং পোর্টাল), then Training, then Trade/Skills; choose your division, district and centre and press Search [B0140, G0002].
   3. Pick the trade, press Apply, check your photo matches your face now, then press Continue, "Process to Enroll" and Confirm; "My Course" shows your applications [B0140].
   4. Sit the admission test on the date in the notice; BKTTC posts results on its notice board and website and sends them by SMS [G0003].
   5. Pay the fee against a money receipt (BKTTC: Tk 748 for 3 months, Tk 1,484 for 6 months); courses run by funded projects are free [G0003, B0037]. At BKTTC, regular trainees with at least 80% attendance get Tk 150 a day for transport and food when funds are allocated [G0004].

   **Scams:** if anyone offers a TTC place or exam pass for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement officer [B0010]. To complain about a TTC:

   1. Complain to its principal (phone and email on BMET's TTC list, Step 1, item 2) [G0049].

**The trap.**
- *Resold on arrival.* Thirty-six men who went with a contract naming their employer were each "sold" to another company for about Tk 2 lakh, at under half the promised pay [RE0012].

#### If an agency or broker takes your money

**Expect slow, partial recovery.** BMET cases take 4 months to a year, and in August 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 against Tk 3.5 to 5 lakh paid; workers with full documents get more back [A0073, A0076, A0077]. Keep proof of every payment from the start (Step 1, "Before paying") [W0028, B0003].

**If it doesn't send you, or sends you to a different job:**

1. **Complain to BMET.** It takes complaints from people cheated before going, after going or after coming back; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0034].

   You'll need:

   - a copy of your NID or passport [G0014, G0023];
   - every receipt, bank slip or mobile-money record of what you paid [G0014, G0023];
   - the contract, visa or work permit if you have them [G0014, G0023];
   - any letters, messages, audio or video from the agency or broker [G0014, G0023];
   - written down: the agency's name and RL number, the name and phone of whoever took the money, dates and amounts, and the employer's name and address [G0020, G0024].

   1. Fill in BMET's complaint form against a recruiting agent, or write an application to BMET's Director General, with your proof [G0020, B0034, G0026]. Give your name, father's or husband's name, address, phone and email, the type of complaint (for example money misappropriation, or not sending workers abroad), everyone affected, the agency's address, and the details above [G0020, G0021, G0024].
   2. Hand it in at BMET's complaint cell, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, and get the acknowledgement receipt with your complaint ID [B0034, G0026, G0022]. The responsible officer is BMET's Director (Employment), 01711111544, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [G0046]; BMET's main number is 02-49357972 [G0026].

      > **Need to know**
      > - Is the online form at ovijog.bmet.gov.bd, which takes evidence files and shows status, in service [G0023]?

   3. BMET investigates, hears both sides, usually in Dhaka, and recommends a decision to the Ministry, which decides [B0034, B0035, G0015]. If a guilty agency won't pay, BMET can take the money from its security deposit [G0016].

   **What happens in practice:** most victims get back less than half of what they lost [A0075]. Workers who paid middlemen with no written agreement have only their testimony, passport and BMET card to show [A0079]. A complaint proved against a registered sub-agent can cost him his registration [YB0008].

2. **If BMET does not settle it,** go up the ladder.

   You'll need [G0022, B0034]:

   - your complaint ID;
   - copies of everything you gave BMET.

   1. Write to BMET's Additional Director General (Employment), adgemp@bmet.gov.bd, 02-48321949 or 01720105492 [G0046].
   2. Then write to the Ministry's Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd, who has 1 month [B0041].
   3. Then file on the Cabinet Division's Grievance Redress System at [grs.gov.bd](https://www.grs.gov.bd/), which has 3 months [B0041].

3. **Or complain straight to the Ministry.** It is free; action starts within 3 to 5 working days, both sides are heard, and it is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].

   You'll need [B0044]:

   - an application;
   - documents proving the complaint;
   - your full address;
   - the agency's RL number and address.

   Send them to the Ministry's Enforcement-2 branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255, or to the Ministry at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Old Elephant Road, Dhaka-1000 [B0044, G0045, G0025].

   The branch also holds mobile courts (on-the-spot courts under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act 2013) on its own initiative, free, within 7 working days, giving immediate remedies to people cheated before or after going [B0047, G0045]. For advice, call the 24-hour migrant call centre, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

4. **Courts and NGOs.** You can go to court alongside a BMET complaint, but many workers can't keep a case going without money or legal aid [G0017]; with NGO legal help, some have sued agents or brokers under the 2013 Act [A0080], which since a 2024 amendment also covers sub-agents and other intermediaries [A0093].

   You'll need: the same papers as for the BMET complaint [G0014].

   - Free legal advice: the government legal aid helpline, 16699 (+8809612316699 from abroad), run by NLASO (National Legal Aid Services Organization), which has a legal aid office in each district [G0050, G0051, G0052].
   - A village court can order money owed under a contract, receipt or other document repaid, up to Tk 3 lakh [G0018].
   - Migrant NGOs such as WARBE (Welfare Association for the Rights of Bangladeshi Emigrants) and the Ovibashi Odhikar Forum settle some disputes by village mediation and pass agency cases to BMET [A0080]; WARBE does not take fraud by local sub-agents [G0019].
