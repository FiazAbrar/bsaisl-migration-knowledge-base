### Step 3: Do the one-month housekeeping course (women)

The course is compulsory for women going as domestic workers and runs at BMET's Technical Training Centres (TTCs) [D0050, D0055]. Brokers sell course certificates; don't buy one [D0072].

The length and the order have been in flux. BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies, the agencies' trade body) said in January 2025 that the training for women going abroad had gone from one month to two [SP0188]. At a meeting on 30 January 2025 Bangladesh promised Saudi Arabia to shorten domestic-worker training while improving its quality [SP0213]. In May 2025 the expatriates' welfare adviser, Asif Nazrul, told Saudi Arabia's vice minister of human resources that women would finish the one-month training before getting the Saudi visa and be sent within two weeks of the visa after a short training [SP0321, SP0322, SP0323].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the housekeeping course one month or two in 2026?
> - Must a woman finish the course before her Saudi visa is issued, and does a short training follow the visa?

1. **Gather your papers;** bring the originals to the interview [D0052, D0054]:
   - your class 5 certificate (if any)
   - your guardian's no-objection letter
   - a photocopy of your birth certificate, NID or passport
2. **Apply on OEP** ([employee.oep.gov.bd](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp)) [A0027]:
   1. Log in with your mobile number and code.
   2. Open Training, then Trade/Skills.
   3. Choose division, district and centre, and press Search.
   4. Pick the housekeeping course, press Apply, then Process to Enroll and Confirm. "My Course" shows your applications.

   You pay the fee and choose the centre and start date online, from BMET's admission calendar [D0053].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What is the housekeeping course fee?
   > - What is the course's exact name in the OEP list?

3. **Interview** in person at the TTC: a committee checks your original papers and that you can read and write [D0054].
4. **Live at the centre for one month** (24 working days), attending 90% of sessions; it covers cooking, appliances, language, culture, personal safety, reaching the police and embassy, and first aid [D0055, D0056].
5. **Pass the final test:** 100 marks, pass mark 70%, 30 for language and 30 for practical work [D0057].
6. **Download the certificate** online; it is valid 2 years. If you haven't gone abroad within 2 to 3 years, do a 3-day refresher; after 3 years, the full course. A returnee with a year abroad does the refresher [D0058].

### If something goes wrong

**You can end the contract and keep your rights** if the employer breaks the contract, lied about the work, assaults you or behaves immorally towards you, gives you dangerous work or rents you out [D0017]. But don't just walk out (see If you are reported absent). Only your employer can issue your final exit visa [SK0021].

**If you are reported absent (huroob).** Leaving without telling anyone can bring an absence (huroob) report, which makes you illegal [D0063, D0037]. The employer can withdraw it within 15 days of filing, after which it is final [D0036]. The company-worker rules in "If your employer reports you absent (huroob)" (60 days to transfer, issuing your own final exit) are for company workers on Qiwa, not for you [SK0021]. If you must leave, go to the embassy or consulate (Complain in this order, item 2). Going home once the report is final: see Going home without valid papers.

**The labour ministry can move you to a new employer without your employer's consent** if [D0031, D0032, D0033, D0034, D0035, D0163, D0164]:

- wages are unpaid for three months (in a row or not)
- nobody collects you from the recruitment office or shelter within 15 days
- no iqama is issued or renewed within 30 days
- you are rented out, given dangerous work or ill-treated
- the employer misses two dispute sessions
- the employer ends the contract during probation

Any other move: see Domestic worker to another household and Domestic worker to a company job.

#### What returning women report

Officials of BRAC, OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) and Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra said in January 2025 that women coming back from the Middle East complain of [SP0195, SP0196, SP0197]:

- being recruited on lies
- being made to work in more than one house
- 14 to 16 hours' work a day
- low and irregular pay, and irregular food
- physical and mental abuse, for some
- being detained by police and sent home after running from the kafil's (sponsor's) house, for some

Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra's executive director said in December 2024 that women workers in the Middle East cannot even leave the house [SP0177, SP0178, SP0179]. BRAC's associate director Shariful Hasan said in December 2025 that brokers and even relatives often effectively sell women domestic workers under the name of sending them free, and that when they are abused abroad, the embassy's or authorities' intervention is limited, with no trial and no accountability [SP0465, SP0466]. An OKUP study in 2025 found that, across destinations, 94% of women workers forced to return had suffered regular physical and mental abuse, 47% sexual harassment, and 82% had worked from dawn to midnight [SP0773].

### Your rights at work

- **Who arranges what:** the employer pays your medical test, iqama and renewal, and health insurance or treatment [D0029]. The Saudi recruitment office must tell you the work and pay, and house and support you if you leave or refuse the employer [D0025, D0026].
- **Contract on Musaned:** you get an SMS link, enter your residence number and the code sent to you, and accept within 5 days; it is free (Musaned 920002866) [D0047].
- **Insurance:** a contract made through a recruitment office on Musaned is insured for its first two years [G0024]. It covers:
  - permanent disability from an accident [G0025]
  - your unpaid wages if the employer dies or is disabled [G0025]
  - sending your body and belongings home if you die [G0026]
- **Probation:** probation lasts up to 90 days; either side may end the contract [D0003].
- **Hours:** you work at most 10 hours a day, with a half-hour break after every 5 hours, at least 8 hours' continuous rest, and one paid day off a week [D0004, D0005].
- **Pay:** you are paid the contract wage monthly, into a bank account or digital wallet in your name linked to Musaned through your mobile and iqama numbers [D0158, D0045, D0046]. At most a quarter can be deducted, only for damage you caused, an advance or an official order [D0013]. The Saudi-Bangladesh recruitment agreement set SR 800 a month [D0193]; that agreement was signed on 10 February 2015, and the SR 800 was about half the SR 1,500 minimum the Philippines agreed in May 2013, according to a Prothom Alo column [SP0505].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Is SR 800 a month still the agreed wage for a Bangladeshi domestic worker?

- **Housing, food and contact:** the employer must give you suitable housing and food or a food allowance [D0011]; the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy, and may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [D0010, D0009]. The expatriates' welfare minister told parliament on 5 April 2026 that Bangladesh's agreement with Saudi Arabia secures a woman domestic worker's right to keep a mobile phone, so she can alert her family, the embassy or the ministry at any time [SP0672].
- **Leave:** you get up to 30 days' sick leave a year with a medical report, 15 on full pay, the rest on half [D0007]; after two years, if you renew, 30 days' paid leave and a return ticket [D0006].
- **Only your own job:** the employer may not make you work for others or in another job [D0012]. If you work for anyone else yourself: up to SR 2,000 fine, a work ban or both [D0020]; working on your own account, SR 10,000 and deportation [D0030].
- **At the end:** you get your wages and dues within a week, or two if you end the contract, and end-of-service pay of one month's wage per four years [D0015, D0014]. Ending the contract without a valid reason costs two months' wages to the other side unless the contract says otherwise [D0016]; if you leave without one, you pay your ticket home [D0019].

## Umrah visa

The Umrah visa is a pilgrimage visa for Muslims, bought only through a licensed agency (item 1 below) [D0087, D0092]. It doesn't allow work or staying on for Hajj [D0086, D0094]; for what working on it brings, see Tourist visa, Can you work?

It is issued all year except the Hajj season (15 Dhul Qa'dah to 15 Dhul Hijjah) [D0082]. It costs USD 80 plus USD 10.50 visa service fee, USD 10.50 insurance service fee and the insurance; it is single entry, valid 90 days, with a stay of up to 90 days [D0084, D0085]. Packages cost about Tk 1.2 lakh to Tk 3 lakh or more [D0155].

1. **Choose a licensed agency.** Bangladeshis can't apply on Nusuk, the Saudi Umrah platform, themselves [D0087, D0083]. Pay only an agency on the Ministry of Religious Affairs' yearly list of licensed Hajj and Umrah agencies, on its website and [hajj.gov.bd](https://hajj.gov.bd/) [D0092]. Fake agencies advertise on Facebook and YouTube [D0093].
2. **Give the agency your papers;** the agent books a Nusuk-approved hotel and transport [D0088, D0089]. *You'll need* [D0088, D0089]:
   - a passport valid 6 months with 2 blank pages
   - a white-background photo
   - a meningitis (ACWY) certificate from at least 10 days before arrival
3. **Enrol face and fingerprints** after applying, in Saudi Visa Bio, the Foreign Ministry's free app on Google Play and the App Store, with your email and a photo of your passport; anyone 6 or older, under-18s with an adult [D0168, D0090].
4. **Travel within 30 days** of issue, or the visa is cancelled [D0091].

**Umrah visas used for trafficking.** According to the CID (the police Criminal Investigation Department), a ring promising jobs in Russia at Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a month first sent 10 Bangladeshis to Saudi Arabia on Umrah visas, had them perform Umrah, then took them to Russia, where they were sold on and forced to fight against Ukraine; one, Humayun Kabir of Singra, Natore, was killed and another badly injured (February 2025) [SP0227, SP0228, SP0229]. A second group of 10 refused to go on once they learned recruits were forced to fight; their passports were taken, so they could neither work in Saudi Arabia nor go home, the CID said in February and again in June 2025 [SP0230, SP0231, SP0357, SP0358].

**Pilgrims hurt or stranded in October 2026.** Of the 24 Bangladeshis injured in the attack on Riyadh airport on 10 October 2026, 13 had come on Umrah visas, according to Foreign Ministry sources citing the embassy, which was in touch with the hospitals over their treatment [SP0799, SP0800]. On 19 October 2026 the expatriates' welfare minister said many Umrah pilgrims were stranded in Saudi Arabia, many unable to return because of flight cancellations, and would be brought home when the situation normalised [SP0487].

**If the agency cheats you,** write to the Secretary, Ministry of Religious Affairs, with what you paid, when and how, what wasn't delivered, and the agency's name, licence number and address; the ministry can order a refund within 7 working days or act against the agency [D0176]. Hajj and Umrah section: +880 2 55100899, morahajsection@gmail.com [D0177].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a refused Umrah visa be appealed, and how?
> - Who helps a pilgrim stranded in Saudi Arabia when the agency fails?

## Hajj visa

Hajj visas are issued only within Bangladesh's yearly quota, through the Ministry of Religious Affairs; the 2027 quota of 78,500 is full and registration has closed [D0106]. Children under 15, people with serious chronic or infectious illness, and women late in or at high risk in pregnancy can't go [D0105]. For Hajj 2025 the quota was 127,198, the Hajj agreement with Saudi Arabia was due on 13 January 2025, Hajj visas from 19 February and Hajj flights from 29 April [SP0131, SP0132]. Bangladesh is among the countries in Saudi Arabia's Route to Makkah Initiative, a Bangladesh foreign ministry official said in January 2025 [SP0203].

> **Need to know**
> - When does pre-registration for the next Hajj open?
> - How is the opening of pre-registration announced?
> - What does the Route to Makkah Initiative change for a pilgrim leaving from Bangladesh?

1. **Pre-register:** Tk 30,000, with NID or passport (birth registration under 18) [D0095, D0096].
   - Government package: on [hajj.gov.bd](https://hajj.gov.bd/) or at a DC office, Islamic Foundation office, Union Digital Centre or the Dhaka Hajj Office [D0097].
   - Private package: through a licensed agency on the ministry's list [D0092].
2. **Primary registration:** deposit Tk 3.5 lakh [D0102].
   - Government package: at any Sonali Bank branch with a voucher [D0104].
   - Private package: into the agency's account shown on the Hajj portal [D0104]. For Hajj 2025 the government's rule was that the whole package money be paid only into the agency's bank account or at the agency itself, the pilgrim keeping the receipt, with no dealings with middlemen, and women and children depositing together with their mahram (a close male guardian relative) [SP0121, SP0122].
3. **Final registration:** pay the rest by the final-registration deadline the ministry sets; for Hajj 2027 it is 10 December 2026 [D0103]. 2027 government packages cost Tk 5,05,648 to Tk 6,15,263, the private standard package Tk 5,13,648 [D0107, D0108]. On 18 April 2026 the Prime Minister's spokesman said the cost of Hajj travel had been cut by Tk 12,000 per ticket [SP0673, SP0674]. For Hajj 2025 each pilgrim paid the sacrifice (qurbani), SR 1,000, separately from the package; people aged 65 or over could go; and passports had to be valid to 31 December 2025 [SP0123, SP0124].

4. **Give the agency your passport and registration certificate, and do biometrics** in Saudi Visa Bio [D0098].

**Choosing a package.** Pilgrims who had performed Hajj several times said in November 2024 [SP0127, SP0128, SP0129, SP0130]:

- Buy an Islamic Development Bank (IDB) coupon for the sacrifice to avoid fraud over it.

## Sending money home

**Use a legal channel,** never hundi (the illegal informal channel) or unlicensed agents [E0046]. Saudi police have arrested people for sending money outside legal channels, and Bangladesh has frozen bKash, Nagad and Rocket accounts linked to hundi [E0079, E0078]. Without a valid iqama you can't use a bank or send money formally [B0112]. Saudi Arabia's ambassador in Dhaka said in August 2024 that about 32 lakh Bangladeshis in Saudi Arabia send about USD 5 billion a year through legal channels and another USD 5 billion through informal ones [SP0105, SP0106].

**The numbers.** Saudi Arabia was the largest source of Bangladesh's remittances in 2025, 15.52% of USD 32.82 billion, according to RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), and Bangladesh Bank's governor called it the biggest source of remittances and foreign currency in October 2025 [SP0459, SP0424]. By Bangladesh Bank figures it had fallen from USD 4.54 billion in 2021-22, when it was first, to USD 3.76 billion in 2022-23, and then by USD 1.03 billion, about 27%, in 2023-24, to fourth place [SP0033, SP0034, SP0035, SP0090, SP0091, SP0092]. In March 2024 RMMRU's founding chair Tasneem Siddiqui put the fall down to many sending through hundi, over the gap in exchange rates and distrust of banks, and to many workers who had recently gone on legal visas not getting jobs [SP0036]. In March 2025 the governor said not all Saudi remittances come straight to Bangladesh: a dishonest ring buys them in Saudi Arabia, routes them through Dubai, hoards them and sells them to Bangladeshi banks at a higher price [SP0260, SP0261, SP0262].

**Compare costs** on the World Bank's price comparison, [remittanceprices.worldbank.org/corridor/SA/BD](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/corridor/SA/BD). Sending SR 750 cost [E0080]:

- about 7-9% through STC Pay, TeleMoney, Western Union or MoneyGram; STC Pay delivers in under an hour to cash pickup, a bank account or a mobile wallet;
- about 15-16% at bank branches, which charge SR 75.

Bangladesh Bank's governor said in October 2025 that expatriates in Saudi Arabia pay 6 to 10% to send money, comparatively much higher and a huge burden on them [SP0421, SP0422].

Your family gets the money plus the 2.5% government incentive automatically, and can cash out at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs for Tk 7 per thousand [E0076, E0081, E0085, E0084]. A banker's column in April 2025 also gave the incentive as 2.5% [SP0284], but the expatriates' ministry said in June 2025 that the government continues a 2% incentive on remittances sent through banks [SP0377]. **Sending to a brother, sister or in-law.** Many workers in Saudi Arabia and the Gulf send money to brothers', sisters', in-laws' and friends' bank accounts, because many elderly parents in villages have no bank account, children are minors and wives don't open accounts [SP0066, SP0067]. ## Going home without valid papers

Without a valid iqama, the way out is the Bangladesh Embassy's Special Exit Programme (SEP), if your sponsor's labour office is covered (see item 1 below) [E0027]. There is no time limit; workers have waited months, mostly without work [E0096].

Overstaying any visa can bring a fine up to SR 50,000, up to 6 months in prison and deportation [E0053]. The re-entry ban, from departure, is usually 3 to 5 years for overstaying or huroob (the employer's report that a worker is absent from work) and 3 to 10 years for working on a tourist or Umrah visa [E0057, E0058, E0059]. Leaving without a legitimate reason means paying your own ticket [E0136].

**To leave through SEP:**

1. **Check you qualify:**
   - You qualify if your sponsor's company is under the labour office of Riyadh, Al Qassim, Hail, Al Jouf, Northern Borders or the Eastern Province, and your iqama has expired, was never issued or carries a huroob report; house drivers and male domestic workers with an expired iqama also qualify [E0027].
   - Excluded: women domestic workers; anyone who didn't leave on an earlier exit visa; anyone with a police or court case or wanted (matlub), who must settle it with that authority first [E0028].
2. **You'll need** [E0029, E0030]:
   - your iqama number;
   - your original iqama;
   - a passport copy showing its validity.
3. **Apply online** at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/sepsrv.html) ("Special Exit programme(SEP) Application Form"); no visit or call needed [E0026, E0031].
4. **Check progress** with your iqama number at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/seppub/); when an appointment appears, print it and go to the embassy on that day with the documents [E0031].
5. **Get the exit visa:**
   - For a Riyadh company, the embassy gets labour office clearance; you then hand in your original iqama and passport copy, and the embassy sends them to Jawazat for the exit visa [E0029].
   - For a company under another labour office, or a huroob case with a sponsor outside Riyadh, the embassy gives you a letter to take to that labour office or Jawazat, where you collect the exit visa [E0029, E0030].

> **Need to know**
> - Does applying through SEP cost anything?
> - Must Saudi fines be paid before SEP issues an exit visa?
> - What can a worker do if an SEP application is refused?

**Sponsors under the Makkah, Madinah, Tabuk, Asir, Jazan, Najran or Al Baha labour office** aren't covered by SEP [E0027]. Contact the Jeddah consulate's labour wing (Contacts) [E0166]. Workers seeking special exit, and domestic workers, call the consulate's toll-free hotline, 8002440051, from Jeddah and from outside it, to get the labour welfare wing's service [SP0436, SP0435].

> **Need to know**
> - What is the exit procedure for a worker without valid papers whose sponsor is under the Makkah, Madinah, Tabuk, Asir, Jazan, Najran or Al Baha labour office?

**Women domestic workers,** excluded from SEP (item 1), can report their case on the labour wing's online form ([bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/)), choosing the domestic worker problem type [E0089]; how they get an exit visa is not known. In May and June 2025 the expatriates' welfare adviser and his ministry said that, after his talks in Saudi Arabia, a chance had been created to regularise women workers living there illegally, giving no procedure, deadline or cost [SP0328, SP0376]; the ministry's one-year report in August 2025 said measures had been taken to regularise irregular women workers in Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Oman [SP0389, SP0390].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a woman domestic worker without valid papers get an exit visa?

**Any other papers problem:**

1. Use the embassy's online form at [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/srspub/), choosing the problem (huroob, no iqama, in jail, salary, domestic worker), describing it in Bangla or English and attaching iqama and passport copies [E0089].
2. If there's no reply, call the labour wing free on 8001000125 [E0024].
3. Then have a relative write to the ministry (see Help from Bangladesh) [E0004, E0005].

> **Need to know**

**Without a passport** you need a travel permit (outpass) from the embassy's passport wing [E0088, E0023]. Either [E0023, E0131]:

- call 8001000126 free; or
- book "Consular & Welfare Service" at [passportriyadh.com/appointment](https://passportriyadh.com/appointment/) with your name, phone, passport number and OID number.

In a deportation centre, see If you are detained.

### Help from Bangladesh

A relative in Bangladesh can ask the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, free, to have the worker brought home; within 7 working days the ministry asks the embassy, the Welfare Board or BMET to do it [E0004, E0005].

1. **You'll need** [E0005]:
   - proof the person is a migrant worker (passport, BMET smart card, iqama or work permit copies);
   - the Saudi workplace and home addresses;
   - the agency's name and address;
   - the employer's mobile number.
2. **Send an application** describing what happened, with these papers, to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) [E0004, E0005].
3. **If nothing happens within 7 working days,** write to the head of its Mission and Welfare wing (adsmission@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030219, 01711427769) [E0002].

### Amnesties and grace periods

The last known window to leave without a fine ended on 18 April 2026 [E0087]. In it, people whose visit, Umrah or final exit visa expired on 25 February 2026 could leave without a fine by going to the airport with a ticket [E0087]; the embassy's notice of 23 March 2026 listed every kind of visit visa, Umrah, transit and final exit visas past their validity on 25 February, and warned that anyone not leaving by 18 April could face serious legal trouble and punishment [SP0657, SP0658, SP0659]. The embassy announces such windows on [bangladeshembassy.org.sa/notice.html](https://www.bangladeshembassy.org.sa/notice.html); check it for a new one [E0176, E0175].

Separately, until the end of 2026 an employer can regularise a work permit that expired more than 12 months ago or was never issued within 6 months of joining; cases not regularised then face legal measures [G0014, G0015, G0016]. The embassy relayed this labour ministry call for every work permit (rukhsa amal) to be issued or renewed within 2026 on its verified Facebook page on 30 June 2026 [SP0704, SP0705, SP0706].

Bangladesh's expatriates' welfare adviser said on 31 May 2025 that Saudi Arabia had assured him it would regularise irregular Bangladeshi workers and had already started [SP0352]. A working team of officials from the Saudi labour ministry (the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development) and the Bangladesh embassy, promised at a meeting on 30 January 2025, was formed to solve specific problems of irregular Bangladeshi workers, and a high-powered task force on illegal workers and recruitment irregularities had been formed under the two countries' latest foreign office consultations, according to a press release reported in May 2025 [SP0324, SP0325].

### If you are detained

People picked up in raids have usually been held one to two weeks in a deportation centre, then flown straight home, often with nothing [SG0032, SG0033]. Giving yourself up doesn't guarantee that: Jabbar, a worker without papers who believed the police would send him home if they caught him, gave himself up; they took his fingerprints and released him, leaving him with no money for food, his wife said (May 2025) [SP0341, SP0342, SP0343]. Embassy officials visit the centres and issue your travel permit there [E0088]; for legal help or to tell your family, see Work visa: company worker, If you or a relative is arrested [E0089, E0024]. Wages owed can still be claimed after you leave: wage insurance if the whole company stopped paying (see If you aren't paid, item 5), or a relative's application to the ministry's Welfare-1 branch (see If you aren't paid, item 4) [C0177, C0122].

> **Need to know**
> - Can a friendly-settlement claim be filed, or continued, from Bangladesh after deportation?

Embassy efforts freed 49,503 Bangladeshis from 20 countries in 2012-2016, 17,335 of them from Saudi Arabia, most of them workers whose iqama or visa had expired, for whom "release" meant arranging an air ticket and an outpass (travel permit), according to a Prothom Alo column [SP0499, SP0500, SP0501]. In October 2011 a Dammam high court asked what the Bangladesh government was doing to bring home about 500 Bangladeshis held in Dammam prison whom Saudi Arabia was willing to send home under a general pardon; tickets and outpasses weren't arranged because of embassy indifference and errors in the paperwork, according to the same column [SP0506, SP0507].

### If a worker dies in Saudi Arabia

1. **Call the embassy:** +966570212180, or the labour wing free on 8001000125 [E0086, E0024]. It registers the death, follows up with the employer and the authorities, and issues the no-objection certificate (NOC) needed for burial or repatriation [E0033, E0034].

   The cause of death is recorded in Saudi Arabia, usually as "natural", and Bangladesh does not check it (see The recorded cause of death) [E0093]. If the worker had no valid papers, bringing the body home takes much longer [E0090].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How can a family ask for a post-mortem in Saudi Arabia?
   > - How can a family challenge the recorded cause of death?

2. **Ask Bangladesh to bring the body home, or say you want burial in Saudi Arabia.** Under Saudi rules the employer pays to prepare and fly the body home unless the family agrees to burial there [E0135]. The assistant director of the Faridpur Probashi Kallyan (expatriate welfare) Centre said in June 2026 that the company usually sends the body home and gives other help, and that if it won't, the government brings the body home on the family's application [SP0698, SP0699]. On the Bangladesh side, the route depends on the worker's papers:

   - **The worker left with BMET clearance or joined the Welfare Board through the embassy:** tell the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Dhaka) whether the family wants the body brought home or buried in Saudi Arabia [E0145, E0017]. The Board pays to bring the body home, relays the family's wish to the embassy and follows up until the body arrives, free [E0091, E0017]. A written application handed in at a district welfare centre also works: in March 2025 the Mymensingh centre took a father's application and forwarded it to the Board, which then makes all arrangements, the centre's assistant director said [SP0244, SP0245, SP0246].
   - **The worker had neither, including workers without valid papers:** the family can still apply to the Welfare Board, which asks the ministry to pay [E0091]. The ministry's Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) also takes applications by email for irregular, undocumented and destitute workers under a 2022 policy; free, action within 7 working days [E0004]. **You'll need** [E0004]:
     - a description;
     - the cause of death;
     - the death certificate;
     - copies of the worker's passport, work permit or iqama, BMET smart card and Saudi ID number.

     For a worker who died without papers, the Dohar UNO (upazila nirbahi officer, the upazila's chief government officer) said in April 2026 that if someone contacts the embassy in Riyadh on the worker's behalf, it will arrange to send the body home with government help [SP0667].

One body in October 2026 was received at cargo gate 8 of Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, Dhaka [SP0527].

#### The recorded cause of death

- **Mostly "natural".** Bangladeshi authorities, working from Saudi papers, recorded 76% of deaths in Saudi Arabia from January to October 2022 as natural, and the average age of those "natural" deaths in 2022 was 44 [SP0045, SP0046, SP0047]. Across all destinations, the papers with returned bodies gave natural death in 66% of cases in January to September 2026, against about 70% before, by the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry's data [SP0785, SP0786, SP0787].
- **Mostly unexplained.** The Guardian found in March 2024 that in most cases the cause was not explained: Saudi death certificates give "natural causes", "heart problems" or "respiratory problems", so no post-mortem is done; FairSquare, a UK rights group, says the cause is unexplained in over half of migrant deaths in the Gulf [SP0051, SP0052].
- **The Saudi position.** Saudi Arabia's labour ministry said in March 2024 that post-mortems are done where needed, death certificates are issued transparently, and it had found no negligence [SP0053, SP0054].
- **Suicide.** Once the destination country sends a death certificate saying "suicide", the family can do nothing more about it, OKUP's chairperson said in January 2024 [SP0015]. The bodies of 799 women migrant workers came home in eight years, most from Saudi Arabia, and many death certificates gave suicide as the cause, according to Welfare Board data cited in October 2026 [SP0478].
- **Signs of violence.** In cases the New York Times investigated in 2025, Saudi authorities declared foreign domestic workers' deaths natural despite signs of violence: Ugandan Aisha Meeme's Saudi autopsy found three broken ribs and severe electrical burns, and her husband said the employer offered him the choice of her body or her USD 2,800 in unpaid wages [SP0274, SP0275, SP0276].
- **Suspected causes.** Rights groups told the Guardian in 2024 that hard labour, living conditions, exploitation, stress and heat stroke may be driving the death rate; Shariful Hasan, associate director of BRAC's Migration Programme, said that since workers go only after passing a medical at a Saudi-designated centre, both countries must investigate why they die [SP0055, SP0056, SP0057]. More than 16% of returned bodies in January to September 2026 died in road or work accidents, fires, falls from buildings or electrocution, and the minister said on 1 October 2026 that steps to verify causes of death abroad would be decided [SP0785, SP0786, SP0787].

#### How many die

At least 13,685 Bangladeshi workers died in Saudi Arabia in 2008-2022, 1,502 of them in 2022, more than four a day, by Bangladesh government data reported by the Guardian [SP0045, SP0046, SP0047]. About 2,000 died there in 2026 up to the end of September, more than in any other country; the head of BRAC's Migration Programme said over 70% of bodies come from the Middle East, most from Saudi Arabia, and that young men aged 25 to 30 are also dying [SP0783, SP0784]. From the start of the 2026 war to 12 October 2026, 14 Bangladeshis were killed in attacks in the Middle East, 8 of them registered as migrant workers, by Expatriates' Welfare Ministry sources [SP0823].

#### If the worker was executed

Saudi law doesn't guarantee that the body of an executed person is handed over: in all three executions of January-June 2025 among the cases Amnesty International documented, Saudi Arabia neither returned the body nor told the family where the grave is [SP0579, SP0580]. Asked in October 2026 whether the families of two executed Bangladeshis could get compensation, the state minister for foreign affairs said there was no chance because the sentence had already been carried out [SP0484].

### What the family can claim

- **Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant,** for workers who left with BMET clearance or joined the Board through the embassy; the Board writes listing the papers [E0037, E0015]. **You'll need** [E0015]:
  - a family-members certificate from the Union Parishad chairman, mayor or councillor;
  - an undertaking on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper (samples on [wewb.gov.bd](https://www.wewb.gov.bd/));
  - a statement of the receiving account at any scheduled bank showing account and routing numbers;
  - 2 passport-size colour photos of each heir.

  Send them to the Board; it is free and pays into the account, with an SMS, within 60 working days of complete papers [E0015], though families often have to chase it repeatedly [E0092]. In April 2026 the Board also gave one family Tk 50,000 in cash at the funeral, on top of the Tk 35,000, with the Tk 3 lakh to follow [SP0647].

  > **Need to know**
  > - When does the Board pay Tk 50,000 in cash at the funeral, and to which families?
- **Tk 10 lakh life insurance,** for workers who left with BMET clearance from 1 January 2023, covering 5 years from the BMET card's issue [ZZ0018]. No application if the body comes home; if buried in Saudi Arabia or died after returning, apply at [portal.wewb.gov.bd](https://portal.wewb.gov.bd/) or the Board with the BMET card and passport copy; free, paid within 30 working days [E0013]. Claims recorded as suicide are often rejected. Middlemen offer to speed up payouts for a large cut; the claim is free and needs no middleman [E0097, E0098]. Amirun Begum of Narsingdi died in September 2023 by suicide, by the Saudi death certificate; when her father asked at Probashi Kallyan Bhaban in Dhaka in January 2024 about the Tk 3 lakh, a staff member looked at the certificate and said that because she had killed herself she wouldn't get the insurance money [SP0013, SP0014].
- **Compensation and unpaid wages from Saudi Arabia.** With the heirs' power of attorney, the embassy pursues them in Saudi courts and sends the money through BMET [E0032]. The ambassador said in January 2024 that the embassy had contracted two Saudi law firms to recover dead workers' unpaid wages and dues, and that Tk 33 crore owed to the families of 65 dead Bangladeshis had been recovered and sent home the previous December [SP0001, SP0002]. The Board tells the family when compensation is possible; the family prepares a power of attorney (the Board's legal cell helps) and the same four papers as for the grant; the Board pays heirs within 60 working days of receiving the money, free [E0016]. A relative can also email the ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) with proof of migrant status, the employer's name and full address and what is owed; it instructs the embassy and Board within 7 working days, free [E0003].
- **GOSI work-death compensation.** For a work-injury death, GOSI's work-injury insurance (who it covers: Injured or ill) pays the family 84 times the average monthly wage registered with it, plus repatriation [E0157, E0158, E0159]. Claim at a GOSI office (toll-free 199044), or from Bangladesh through the embassy with a power of attorney, as for compensation and unpaid wages above [E0161, E0162, E0032, E0016].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Which papers does GOSI need from the family for a work-death claim?
  > - How long does GOSI take to pay?

### Injured or ill

**To get home:** if you are injured or seriously ill in Saudi Arabia, you or your family apply to the Welfare Board with BMET clearance or membership and a passport copy; free, the Board gets the embassy to bring the worker home, sometimes into hospital, and the airport desk takes them to hospital by ambulance [E0014, E0040].

**To get home without valid papers:** the Board can't help directly; it forwards the family's application to the ministry, which writes to the embassy's labour wing [SP0297, SP0298, SP0299]. An employer's case against the worker can hold up the return, as in Mizanur Rahman's case below [SP0295, SP0296].

**Treatment in Saudi Arabia:** the employer must insure you, but the cover doesn't pay for serious illness (see Housing, food and health care) [C0015, C0101]. A company worker gets up to 120 days' sick leave a year, 30 at full pay, 60 at three-quarters, 30 unpaid [C0011]; a domestic worker up to 30 days with a medical report, 15 on full pay, the rest on half [D0007].

**Treatment of a work injury:** the rules in this paragraph come from the Social Insurance Law of 2024 (Royal Decree M/273 of 2 July 2024), in force from the day after it was issued [ZX0001, ZX0002]. That law does not apply to a worker who already had GOSI contribution periods from before it came into force and has not been paid compensation for them; the earlier Social Insurance Law (Royal Decree M/33 of 3/9/1421 Hijri, 2000) still applies to such a worker [ZX0003, ZX0004]. Under the 2024 law, GOSI covers a work injury only if it happened after your GOSI registration was complete, or within the time the rules give the employer to register you (or you, if the employer refuses or delays) [ZY0001]. If covered, GOSI gives medical care for as long as your condition needs it, at the hospitals and clinics it specifies [ZZ0005, ZZ0006, ZZ0007]. If the injury was reported in the way and within the time the rules set, then for each day you can't work, holidays included, from the day after the injury, GOSI pays a daily allowance of 100% of your daily registered wage in the month before the month of the injury, 75% while it pays for your treatment in any hospital, medical centre or the like [ZY0002, ZZ0008, ZZ0009, ZZ0010]. The allowance stops when you can work again, recover or die, or when permanent disability is established [ZY0003].

> **Need to know**
> - What are the time limits and method for registering a worker and for reporting a work injury to GOSI?
> - What medical care, daily allowance and lump sums does the earlier Social Insurance Law (2000) give a worker who was registered with GOSI before July 2024?

**GOSI work-injury compensation:** GOSI insures workers against work injury at the employer's cost; domestic workers only if its board decides so [E0100, E0101, E0160]. If a work injury leaves 50-99% or total permanent disability, GOSI pays a non-Saudi worker a lump sum; claim at a GOSI office (toll-free 199044) [E0161, E0162]. Under the 2024 law (for who it covers, see Treatment of a work injury), the monthly benefit behind the lump sum is 100% of your average registered wage over the three months before the month of the injury for total disability, or that times your disability percentage for 50% or more [E0158, ZZ0015, ZZ0012]. The lump sum is 84 times that monthly benefit for total disability and 60 times for partial disability [ZZ0011]. Below 50% disability you get a lump sum of 60 times the partial-disability monthly benefit if you were 45 or under on the day of the injury [ZZ0013, ZY0008]. If you were older, take off one month's benefit for each year over 45, counting part of a year of six months or more as a full year [ZZ0016, ZY0004]. Whatever the age, the under-50% lump sum is at least 36 times the assumed monthly benefit [ZZ0014].

> **Need to know**
> - Are domestic workers now covered by GOSI work-injury insurance?
> - Which papers does GOSI need, and how long does it take to pay?
> - Can a worker who has already come home claim from Bangladesh through the embassy?

**Treatment money after coming home:** the Board pays up to Tk 2 lakh [E0014]; a Board assistant director said in June 2025 that it could give a registered worker up to Tk 1.5 lakh quickly [SP0356].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the Board's treatment grant after coming home up to Tk 2 lakh or up to Tk 1.5 lakh?

**You'll need** [E0014]:

- BMET clearance card or membership;
- passport copy;
- medical papers;
- a 6R colour photo.

Apply on the Board's portal, at a district welfare centre or at the Board; it checks by video call and pays within 60 working days [E0014].

**Disability insurance:** permanent disability within the 5 years pays 10% to 100% of Tk 10 lakh [E0045, E0012]. **You'll need** [E0012]:

- BMET clearance card;
- passport copy;
- medical papers;
- a 6R photo showing the disability.

Apply free at the Board, paid within 30 working days (Deputy Director Welfare-2, +88 02 48317085, 01321-158354, dd.welfare2@wewb.gov.bd) [E0012].

**What illness costs.** An OKUP study in December 2024 found returnees came back with back pain, workplace injuries, heart disease, kidney problems, cancer, high blood pressure, tumours and skin disease; some borrowed from home for treatment, and many came home because they couldn't get treatment [SP0170, SP0171].

**Cases:**

- **2021, Abul Hashem (42) of Lohagara, Chattogram,** in Saudi Arabia since 2005 with two shops in Mecca, had to wind up the business and come home after a stomach cancer diagnosis, and spent about Tk 68 lakh on treatment; by January 2026 he farmed two acres with technical advice, incentives and training from the upazila agriculture office [SP0453, SP0454, SP0455].

### Mental health

The Saudi Health Ministry's psychological consultation line, 920033360, takes calls from anyone in Saudi Arabia, 8 am to 8 pm (Saturday from 1 pm) [SG0031]. After coming home, BRAC's Migration Programme gives counselling (At Dhaka airport) [E0111].

In an OKUP survey presented in December 2024 of 1,096 returnees from six Middle East countries who came for health services, 55% had come back from Saudi Arabia and 48% came with mental health problems, more often men; domestic workers reported mental problems most [SP0167, SP0168, SP0169]. ### How many women go

Women going abroad for work fell from 1,05,466 in 2022 to 76,108 in 2023 and 61,158 in 2024, by BMET figures; Saudi Arabia took 66% of them in 2024, more than 40,000, and 17,786 of the 24,617 who went in January-May 2025 [SP0186, SP0187, SP0364, SP0365, SP0366]. BAIRA's secretary general said in January 2025 that Saudi employers were no longer placing demand for Bangladeshi women domestic workers as before and were taking workers from African countries, and that bad publicity had cut women's interest in going [SP0189]. Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra (BNSK, a women migrant workers' organisation) said in June 2025 that Saudi domestic work pays little for the labour it demands and that Middle Eastern countries are taking workers from Africa instead [SP0369, SP0370].
