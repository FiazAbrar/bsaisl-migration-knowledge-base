### In one minute

**Malaysia is reopening to new Bangladeshi workers after being shut since 31 May 2024. Recruitment through the agencies on Malaysia's list was announced on 15 October 2026, medical tests began at 51 approved centres by 19 November, and the minister expected the first workers to go by the end of November, with BOESL's first flights due from the first week of December** [U0042, Z0151, MP0465, MP0462, MP0453]. On 15 November the minister said the cost had been fixed at Tk 1,54,000 [MP0459]. BOESL, the government's recruiting agency, ran its own call: a 5 November circular for 112 men at DHL, with applications taken on OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's job-seeker website) from 6 to 8 November and the shortlist drawn by lottery (Step 1, item 3) [W0001, W0002, MP0453]. BOESL and the minister say that job is zero-cost, but what zero cost covers is disputed (see Status) [W0001, YD0006].

A Malaysia job starts as a quota: permission for one Malaysian employer to bring in a set number of foreign workers [W0043]. Each slot becomes a "calling visa" (কলিং ভিসা) that is resold down a chain of agencies and brokers to the village, and every link takes a cut (see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut) [YE0029, A0028, YE0018, YE0016]. The legal cap was Tk 78,990, but in 2022-24 workers paid about Tk 4.5 to 6 lakh, nearly all of it borrowed [R0008, A0001, YF0021, A0064]. The job pays the RM1,700 minimum (about Tk 52,000) plus overtime, and the debt takes one to three years to clear, longer if the job or the pay doesn't come [Z0137, YI0024, YF0003, YF0025]. The work pass ties you to one employer, and a second medical in Malaysia decides whether you get it at all [W0052, L0043]. The corridor has shut three times, each after a surge of departures and allegations against the agencies; the 2024 shutdown stranded 17,777 workers who had paid [U0041, YA0003, A0045, MP0539]. Under the restart, the medical checks set for 31 October 2026 did not start until Malaysia had vetted the centres [Z0159, YI0001, MP0465].

### Is it open now, and who can go?

**Reopening. Closed to new workers since 31 May 2024, recruitment through the agencies on Malaysia's list was announced on 15 October 2026; medical tests had started by 19 November at 51 centres Malaysia approved, and the minister said on 15 November that workers should be able to go from the end of the following week to the end of November** [U0042, Z0151, MP0465, MP0466, MP0462]. Before the tests started, the Ministry told people not to pay anyone, do a medical or hand over a passport until it issued formal instructions, and a medical done before the official start of 31 October does not count (Step 2) [YI0005, Z0159].

**Who can go:** Immigration sets three conditions [W0073, W0041]:

- aged 18 to 45 when the employer applies;
- passed fit at a medical centre in Bangladesh;
- a job in one of the five allowed sectors (manufacturing, construction, plantation, agriculture, services).

No agreement covers recruiting Bangladeshi women, and the High Commission tells women not to go for general work [N0028, L0076]. Which routes are legal, and for whom: see The channels. Extra tests and narrower conditions: see Skill tests and narrower job orders.

> **Need to know**
> - Do any Bangladeshi women hold Malaysian factory work passes, and through which agencies did they go?

#### Status

As of 19 November 2026 [Z0151, MP0441, MP0465, MP0459, MP0453]:

- **Agencies:** Malaysia allowed 337 Bangladeshi agencies to work, BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government-owned recruiting agency) and the 25 processing agencies among them; under the MoU (memorandum of understanding) of 18 December 2022, choosing and listing the agencies is the job of Malaysia's human resources ministry (see The processing agency: the toll gate; see Who controls it) [MP0441, MP0438].
- **Medical tests:** started by 19 November at the 51 centres Malaysia approved (Step 2) [MP0465, MP0466]. On 4 November the expatriates' welfare minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, had said centres already testing Malaysia-bound workers were doing so illegally and would face action [YI0002].
- **Cost:** the minister told parliament on 15 November 2026 that the cost of sending a worker had been cut and fixed at Tk 1,54,000, and that strict action would be taken against anyone who collects more (see The cap) [MP0459]. On 5 November he had said the private-agency cost could be Tk 2.5 to 3 lakh, the range most agencies proposed, and no cost for private agencies was then set [YA0066, YD0007, U0043].
- **First flights:** the minister said on 15 November that workers should be able to go from the end of the following week to the end of November; BOESL's managing director, Mohammad Rashedul Haque, said on 8 November that flights for the selected DHL workers would start from the first week of December [MP0462, MP0453]. On 5 November the minister had hoped the first zero-cost flights would leave in December [YD0005].

Some workers still go outside the restart (see Size) [B0089, YD0016].

**The zero-cost places.** Malaysia has agreed in principle to take 10,000 workers at zero cost, the first through BOESL [Z0146, Z0154]. The minister says these workers will pay nothing and be picked by online lottery from applicants registered on OEP, through BOESL circulars such as its 5 November call for 112 men at DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia), which took applications from 6 to 8 November (Step 1, item 1; Step 1, item 3) [YD0006, W0004, W0001, W0002]. BOESL's managing director said this was the first time a lottery had been used to choose workers for government-channel sending to Malaysia [MP0453]. He also said on 8 November that BOESL had received demand letters for 558 more workers in Malaysia's tourism, manufacturing and caregiving sectors, with the employers bearing all costs including the medical and airfare, so these workers too would go with no migration cost [MP0454]. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman told parliament on 18 November 2026 that 10,000 people could be sent at almost no cost, and that 1,000 of them were to come from the families of victims of enforced disappearance and killing under the previous government and of those killed or injured in the 2024 movement [MP0463, MP0464]. Dr Selim Reza of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies wrote in Prothom Alo on 4 September 2026 that, by the government's account, the first 10,000 are to fly through BOESL on special chartered flights free of every cost including airfare, that more are to follow, and that various statements have mentioned about two lakh workers [MB0063, MB0064]. Anyone selling a "zero-cost" or "BOESL" place is selling something the government says is free (Step 1, item 3) [YD0006, W0020]. Selim Reza wrote that a claim that paying money will get a name onto the list of 10,000 should be treated as likely fraud; that unless who may apply, the qualifications needed, how people are chosen and where to apply are published, the scheme could become a new line of business for brokers; and that the Ministry's warning not to hand over money, a medical test or a passport until government instructions come must be enforced [MB0065, MB0066, MB0067].

The government contradicts itself on what "zero cost" means. The Ministry says the 10,000 go free of all migration costs including airfare, and the minister says not a single taka [Z0154, YD0006]. The state minister, Nurul Haque Nur, said in late October that they would still pay the airfare, the medical and a small BOESL service charge, and put the cost at Tk 1.55 to 1.60 lakh a worker [YD0008, U0043]. The Prime Minister said "almost no cost" [MP0463]. The DHL circular itself says zero-cost [W0001].

**The agreement.** Employers have begun filing demand letters (চাহিদাপত্র, job orders) through FWCMS (Foreign Workers Centralised Management System, Malaysia's online visa system) [YD0011]. The Bangladesh-Malaysia MoU runs to 31 December 2026; on 5 November 2026 the minister said a new one would be signed on 31 December and wants every licensed agency allowed in [Z0156, YA0068, YA0067]. Prothom Alo reported in April 2025 that the MoU was valid until August 2026, that agency selection would stay in Malaysia's hands if sending started within that period, and that the clause could be amended by agreement at renewal [MP0492].

#### How the restart came about

Ministers announced or forecast a reopening several times from 2025 before the restart of 15 October 2026 (where it stands: see Status) [MP0630, MP0402, MP0412, MP0427, Z0151].

**2024 to March 2026:**

- **31 May 2024:** Malaysia's last day for new workers to enter; the corridor has been shut since (why: see What closed it before) [U0042].
- **5 October 2024:** Asif Nazrul, the expatriates' welfare adviser, said Bangladesh had made three proposals to Malaysia's Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim: reopen the closed market, open recruitment to all agencies instead of a few, and consider raising Bangladeshi workers' wages [MP0205, MP0206].
- **1 December 2024:** former ambassador M Humayun Kabir, president of the Bangladesh Enterprise Institute, said in an interview that the market had closed and had reopened on the initiative of the Chief Adviser, Muhammad Yunus [MP0253, MP0254, MP0197].
- **December 2024:** the expatriates' welfare secretary, Md Ruhul Amin, told Prothom Alo that the two countries would meet soon to reopen the market, closed since June 2024 [MP0260, MP0261].
- **24 April 2025:** at a human chain outside Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, Eskaton, Dhaka, general members of BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) said 13 countries had accepted Malaysia's conditions and kept sending workers while Bangladesh lagged behind [MP0506, MP0507, MP0508].
- **13 to 15 May 2025:** a three-member delegation led by Asif Nazrul, with senior secretary Neyamat Ullah Bhuiyan and deputy secretary Md Sarwar Alam, went to Malaysia on 13 May to meet its human resources and home ministers; Ministry officials said Bangladesh would propose at the joint technical committee that all legal agencies be allowed to send workers, and that since the MoU ran to December there was no scope for a new agreement, only for amending the existing one [MP0536, MP0537, MP0538]. Bangladesh Civil Society for Migrants (BCSM), a platform of 23 organisations, recommended to the delegation that Malaysia be persuaded to take Bangladeshi workers by the same process as from its 14 other source countries, that cost be cut and a job on arrival guaranteed, and that a letter be sent to Malaysia's Prime Minister to drop one clause of the MoU [MA0017, MP0533, MP0534, MP0535]. After meeting Malaysia's home, human resources and trade ministers on 15 May, Asif Nazrul said in a Facebook video that Malaysia might take 1 to 1.5 lakh foreign workers in the coming months, that the human resources minister, Steven Sim Chee Keong, had assured Bangladesh top priority, and that Bangladesh had asked that all its agencies be allowed to send workers, which Malaysia said it would consider [MP0543, MP0544, MP0545].
- **21 May 2025:** opening the two countries' joint working group meeting at the InterContinental hotel, Dhaka, the Chief Adviser's special envoy Lutfey Siddiqi said Malaysia wanted to take workers through a limited number of agencies because Bangladesh has so many, that amending the MoU would be discussed if needed for transparency, and that Malaysia had set no conditions, including withdrawal of the cases against those who formed the syndicate [MP0559, MP0560, MP0561].
- **24 June 2025:** in a rejoinder to Prothom Alo, the Ministry said several joint working group and high-level meetings had been held, that it hoped the market would reopen soon by a transparent, broker-free method, and that a proposal for a joint, technology-based recruitment process was at its final stage [MP0568, MP0569, MP0570].
- **August 2025:** the Chief Adviser was to visit Malaysia for three days from 11 August and meet Anwar Ibrahim in Putrajaya on 12 August; a senior foreign ministry official said removing Bangladeshis' problems in Malaysia's labour market and taking more workers would get priority, alongside five MoUs on other subjects [MP0606, MP0607, MP0608].
- **20 August 2025:** the Ministry's first-year report said a process had been started to open the market to new workers after top-level bilateral talks, and Asif Nazrul wrote in Prothom Alo that sending would start within about a month [MP0627, MP0630, MP0631].
- **14 December 2025:** Prothom Alo reported that the market had later reopened on various conditions [MP0740].
- **14 March 2026:** Prothom Alo reported that the market had been closed since June 2024 over allegations of syndicate trading and could not be reopened under the interim government (the government led by the Chief Adviser) despite its efforts [MP0769].

#### The channels

- **A private agency on Malaysia's list:** closed since 31 May 2024; a restart through listed agencies was announced on 15 October 2026 and medical tests had started by 19 November (see Status) [U0042, Z0151, MP0465]. It is the route nearly everyone used in 2022-24 (numbers: see Size; cost: see What people really pay; how the job passes through the agencies: see How a job reaches you, and who takes a cut) [A0001, A0059]. Until the restart, nothing was open through them [Z0159, YI0001]. The minister told parliament on 23 June 2026 that the government itself sends no workers to Malaysia and that workers can go through government-approved recruiting agencies [MP0395].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Did the minister's statement of 23 June 2026 that the government itself sends no workers to Malaysia [MP0395] mean BOESL had stopped sending, while stranded 2024 workers were still due to go through it [A0037]?

- **Through a relative in Malaysia:** a relative can find the job, but the visa still goes through a listed agency, so this route is open only as far as the agency route is (Step 1, item 5) [Z0148, RE0001]. For example, Abu Taher, who had worked 10 years at the same company in Malaysia, took his nephew Abdus Salam there about a year and a half before October 2024 because the company was good, relatives said [MP0223].
- **BOESL:** circulars on boesl.gov.bd, a lottery among OEP-registered applicants, then the employer's interview; zero-cost by BOESL's and the minister's account (disputed: see Status), but few places: it sent only 1,381 under the 2022-24 round (Step 1, item 3) [W0004, YD0006, A0059]. BOESL said on 16 May 2024 that it had been given the chance to send 10,000 workers at zero migration cost, had received demand for 1,695, and had sent 1,308 since October 2022 (331 in 2022, 839 in 2023 and 138 in 2024) [MP0037, MP0038, MP0039]. It had demand for more than 10,000 workers, but had taken only 1,467 clearances by 24 April 2024 [MA0063, MA0064], and Prothom Alo put those it sent at about one and a half thousand [MB0059]. Two BOESL officials told Prothom Alo that because its workers went free, at the employer's cost, obstacles came in many ways: demand letters went into other agencies' names and took time to bring back, and BOESL itself had to spend money to get demand letters for employer-paid recruitment [MA0063, MA0064, MB0059, MB0060]. The 73 BOESL workers flying on 17 May 2024 at the employer's cost were to earn RM1,500 (then Tk 37,500) a month with housing from the company; BOESL said it holds job fairs around the country, selects marginal and farm workers, and trains them on the work, the Malaysian language and culture, and sending money home legally [MP0040, MP0041, MP0042]. Its then managing director, Mallik Anwar Hossain, said in May 2024 that under the agreement, if a worker works properly for three years, another member of his family can go to the same company at no cost [MP0043]. For 2026 BOESL has the DHL call and demand for 558 more employer-paid workers (see Status) [W0001, MP0454].

- **CLAB (Construction Labour Exchange Centre Berhad, Malaysia's government construction recruiter), through BOESL:** construction only; it rejected about a third of those it interviewed on health and suitability [YD0016, D0036].
- **Employment Pass:** professionals on at least RM5,000 a month [E0011].

#### Skill tests and narrower job orders

There is no skill test except in construction, where since January 2025 a worker needs a CIDB (Construction Industry Development Board) skills certificate, tested at Khulna Technical Training Centre (Step 1, item 2) [W0041, G0007, B0001]. Job orders can be narrower, such as men aged 22 to 35 with SSC or HSC in BOESL's DHL circular (Step 1, item 3) [W0003].

Bangladesh is pressing Malaysia to take more skilled workers. On 15 May 2025 it proposed security guards, nurses and caregivers, Asif Nazrul said [MP0548]. Before the June 2026 visit, Bangladesh's High Commissioner to Malaysia, Manjurul Karim Khan Chowdhury, said Malaysia's new industrial policy is changing its hiring, that the market for the kind of workers Bangladesh sends is shrinking, and that Bangladesh had started contacts on recruiting skilled and semi-skilled workers [MP0372, MP0373]. At the 22 June 2026 talks Tarique Rahman asked Malaysia to recruit skilled workers from Bangladesh, and in August 2026 the two tourism ministries discussed qualified workers for hotels and tourism (see How the restart came about) [MP0401, MP0421].

#### Size

Bangladesh is Malaysia's largest source of foreign workers, and Malaysia has for a decade been regarded as Bangladesh's second destination after Saudi Arabia, though it was not in the top five in 2025 [U0057, MP0642, MP0375, MP0756].

**Bangladeshis working in Malaysia:**

- About 8 lakh Bangladeshis held Malaysian work passes at the end of 2025, more than any other nationality [U0057]. Malaysia's Home Ministry told its parliament that 803,332 Bangladeshis held valid work permits at the end of June 2025, 37% of all foreign workers, making Bangladesh the largest source of unskilled foreign workers, The Star reported [MP0642, MP0643]. Prothom Alo put about eight lakh Bangladeshis working in Malaysia in October 2024, and the foreign workforce at more than 21 lakh in September 2025, over 38% of it Bangladeshi [MP0200, MP0666].
- In January 2024 more than 70% of foreign workers came from Bangladesh, Indonesia and Nepal, and the rest from India, Vietnam, Pakistan and Thailand, Prothom Alo reported [MP0008].
- Counts of all Bangladeshis in Malaysia, documented or not, run from 10 to 15 lakh: about 14 lakh plus about 5,000 students (a July 2024 press release) [MP0142, MP0143]; about 15 lakh (a Bangladeshi with 21 years in Malaysia, November 2024) [MP0247, MP0248]; about 12 lakh (a column of October 2025) [MP0685, MP0686]; about 10 lakh workers, legal and illegal (Bangladeshis doing specialised work there, June 2026) [MP0374]. **Against other destinations:**

- 2019-23: 97% of Bangladesh's workers went to 10 countries, and Malaysia was one of eight that took more than 10,000 a year on average (BMET data) [MP0023, MP0024].
- 2023: Malaysia was the second-largest destination after Saudi Arabia, in a year when more than 13 lakh workers left Bangladesh, the most ever, RMMRU (Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a Dhaka research body) reported [MP0010, MP0011].
- 2024: 90% of the 10 lakh who went abroad went to Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Qatar, Singapore and the UAE; with Malaysia and the UAE closed and Oman stopped, Saudi Arabia was the only large market open in January 2025 [MP0263, MP0264, MP0265]. Malaysia stayed second, Ami Probashi said [MP0287]. President Mohammed Shahabuddin called it the second-largest destination when meeting Anwar Ibrahim on 4 October 2024 and asked Malaysia to take more [MP0201, MP0202].
- 2025: of 11,30,757 who went abroad, 67% went to Saudi Arabia, then Qatar (10%), Singapore (6%), Kuwait (4%) and the Maldives (4%), RMMRU reported [MP0756, MP0757].
- 2026: BOESL's managing director still called Malaysia Bangladesh's second-largest labour market on 12 November [MP0458]. BAIRA's general members estimated in April 2025 that a reopened Malaysia could take at least 2 lakh Bangladeshis a year, 10 lakh in five years, bringing at least USD 500 crore a year in remittances [MP0506, MP0507, MP0508].

**In 2026, before the restart:** BMET cleared 1,592 workers for Malaysia from 1 January to 6 November 2026, though the restart had sent no one by then [B0089, YI0001, Z0159]. CLAB accepted about 800 construction workers in December 2025 and January 2026 [YD0016]; 120 to 150 stranded 2024 workers were still waiting to go through BOESL in October 2026 (see What closed it before) [A0037].

#### What closed it before

Malaysia has closed to Bangladesh three times in 15 years, Prothom Alo reported on 31 May 2024 [MA0048, MA0049, MA0050]. Each closure came after a surge: more than 4 lakh went in 2007-08 before the first, about 3 lakh in 2017-18 before the second, and about 5 lakh from August 2022 to May 2024 before the third; about 13 lakh went from 2004 to May 2024 [MP0539, MP0540, MP0541]. The first 23 Bangladeshi workers went in 1978, and the formal manpower agreement was signed in 1992 [MP0739]. Three North South University researchers wrote in October 2024 that Bangladesh had tried business-to-business (B2B), government-to-government (G2G) and G-plus-B2B models and none had yet worked, and that powerful syndicates, brokers and middlemen forced high costs on workers [MP0212, MP0213, MP0214].

- **2009:** Malaysia froze hiring after about 4 lakh Bangladeshis went in 2007-08 and many found no job and became undocumented; the closure followed reports of labour exploitation and allegations that agencies had formed syndicates [YA0011, YA0010, U0041]. Agents had charged over Tk 2 lakh against a fixed Tk 84,000 [YA0009]. Selim Reza dates this closure to 2008 [MP0344, MP0345].
- **September 2018:** Malaysia's new government cancelled the 10-agency "G2G Plus" scheme from 1 September, calling it monopolistic, amid allegations of syndication and corruption [YA0003, U0041, YA0004]. About two lakh workers had gone through those 10 agencies, which were alleged to take Tk 3 to 4 lakh from each against a government-fixed Tk 37,000 [YA0001, YA0002]. The round had opened at the end of 2016 [U0041, MA0049]. Prothom Alo reported in 2024 that its online system went in 2016 to a firm named Synerflux, whose owner it names as Dato Seri Abdul bin Amin Nur, a Malaysian citizen of Bangladeshi origin; that with his help the 10 agencies formed a ring; that workers spent Tk 2 to 3.5 lakh against a set cost of Tk 33,375; and that the ring took about Tk 5,500 crore in two years [MA0011, MA0012].
- **31 May 2024:** the last round, under the 2021 MoU, ended (details below) [W0027, U0042].

**Why Malaysia closed in 2024.** Malaysia set 31 May 2024 as the last day for new workers to enter after tens of thousands had paid up to USD 5,000 for jobs that did not exist [W0027, U0042, YA0052]. On 16 January 2024 its human resources minister, Steven Sim, and home minister, Saifuddin Ismail, said Malaysia was reviewing its foreign-worker agreements with 15 countries including Bangladesh because labour was unevenly spread across the economy, and the home minister said recruitment fees, other costs, contract clauses and health would be reviewed [MP0001, MP0002, MP0003]. Malaysia stopped approving new applications to hire foreign workers; visa applications for workers already approved had to be filed by 31 March 2024 and those with visas had to enter by 31 May, Prothom Alo reported on 21 March 2024, citing Ministry sources [MA0001, MA0002]. From 1 June 2024 no worker from any other source country could enter either, and Prothom Alo reported that the other countries blamed the Bangladeshi syndicate [MA0003]. The explanations given:

- a Prothom Alo editorial of 24 March 2024: pressure on Malaysia's government from international human rights organisations [MP0026];
- three North South University researchers, 10 June 2024: to curb recruitment irregularities and frame policy, mainly as part of re-evaluating labour policy and filling the foreign-worker quota set in the 12th Malaysia Plan [MP0099, MP0100];
- Prothom Alo editorial of 6 January 2025 and report of May 2025: allegations of a syndicate in sending workers, which closed the market for all source countries [MP0266, MP0267, MP0542].

**The last weeks, May 2024:**

- The Dhaka-Kuala Lumpur fare, normally Tk 20,000 to 25,000 at most, rose to Tk 80,000 to 1 lakh by mid-May, above Tk 1 lakh by Prothom Alo's later report, and all seats from 27 to 31 May showed sold out on Biman's website [MP0046, MP0047, MP0048, MP0299]. Ethar Fariel Hamid, managing director of Fariel Tours and Travels, complained to the Prime Minister's Office on 26 May that Biman's special-flight tickets were not shown on the GDS (global distribution system, the booking network travel agents use) and opened only for a bribe of Tk 20,000 to 25,000 a ticket, pushing the price to Tk 1.05 to 1.10 lakh; Biman sources said special-flight fares started at Tk 68,000 and its acting marketing director said 99% were sold through the GDS [MP0049, MP0050, MP0051].
- The civil aviation minister, Muhammad Faruk Khan, said on 30 May that Biman was running three or four flights a day, that agencies had known a month ahead that 31 May was the last date but made no arrangements, and that a charter on an Air Cambodia aircraft had been allowed on 29 May [MP0054, MP0055]. The state minister for expatriates' welfare, Shafiqur Rahman Chowdhury, said on 1 June that BAIRA could not give the Ministry a list of who had visas and when they would fly, which caused the flight problems, and that the Ministry had arranged about 22 special flights [MP0060, MP0061].

Malaysia's High Commissioner in Dhaka, Haznah Md Hashim, said on 5 June that the deadline would not be extended because it applied to all 15 source countries, and that Malaysia could not accept without proof BAIRA's allegation that e-visas had been issued on 2 June [MP0091, MP0092, MP0093].
- Near the end Malaysia blacklisted 47 companies over complaints, so workers with visas under them could not go, the panel of 101 agencies said on 4 June [MP0078, MP0079, MP0080]. The expatriates' welfare secretary said the Ministry had not allowed workers to be sent in the names of blacklisted companies [MP0068].
- On 31 May thousands crowded Dhaka airport, many without tickets [MP0056]. Habibur Rahman of Natore, who had paid Tk 6.5 lakh raised by mortgaging land and a bank loan, had waited three days at the airport while the firm stopped answering his calls; Rubel Mia of Narsingdi had paid Tk 6 lakh to a firm named Ahad Enterprise to go as a construction worker [MP0056, MP0057, MP0058]. Tanvir Mia (22) of Debgram, Akhaura, Brahmanbaria, for whom Tk 6.5 lakh had been paid a year earlier to broker Alamgir Hossain of Nayadil village and his brother in Malaysia for a supermarket job, according to his uncle, had a January flight and another at the end of May cancelled; on 31 May he fell from the train home into the Meghna at Bhairab and went missing, and rescuers believed he had drowned [MP0069, MP0070, MP0071].

**What happened to the stranded workers of 2024:**

- About 17,777 workers who had BMET clearance could not fly by the deadline [A0045]. A six-member Ministry committee formed on 2 June 2024 under Additional Secretary (Employment) Nur Md Mahbubul Haque found that the failure lay entirely with the recruiting agencies concerned, and that some agencies took Ministry permission but never applied for BMET smart cards for 34,270 workers, which it called clear negligence [MP0064, MP0511, MP0517, MP0518].
- Other counts differ. By a Prothom Alo column of 4 June 2024, the Ministry and BMET put those with final clearance at 16,970, a BMET source said 31,304 in all could not go because of visa and approval complications, and BAIRA officials said about 40,000 [MA0068, MA0069]. At the 4 June 2024 briefing for the 101 agencies, it was said that 2 to 3% of cleared workers usually fail to go; Ruhul Amin Swapan, owner of Catharsis International (see Who controls it), put the real number at 5,000 to 6,000 and said that if diplomatic efforts to send them failed their costs would be returned and those affected could apply to the original agency for their money; BAIRA's secretary general said the number was not certain and BAIRA would help the Ministry's committee list them [MP0078, MP0081, MP0082, MP0083]. BAIRA members said in October 2024 and April 2025 that about 50,000 who had paid and were in process were harmed, 17,000 of whom had completed every step [MP0189, MP0190, MP0491]. Anwar Ibrahim, the protesting workers and Malaysia's The Edge spoke of about 18,000 [MP0197, MP0281, MP0360, MP0361]; Prothom Alo reported 15,000 in August 2025 [MP0625]. A Prothom Alo column of 2 June 2025 said Malaysia had put at about 11,000 those unable to go despite valid calling visas, while agency owners and the press put the number much higher [MA0102, MA0103, MA0104].
- Of the 16,970 with final clearance, some could not get air tickets and others did not get the employer's final consent: the employer had not sent its assurance that it would receive them at the airport, Prothom Alo reported [MA0111].
