### Deciding

#### Which countries can I go to for work right now?

As of November 2026:
- **Saudi Arabia:** open, and the largest destination, though departures from January to 17 October 2026 were 45% below the same period of 2025 [F0016, F0015].
- **Singapore:** open; BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, the government body that registers and clears migrant workers) cleared 66,657 workers for it from January to 7 November 2026, the second destination [F0016].
- **Qatar:** open, the third destination, with 53,611 cleared from January to 6 November 2026 [F0017].
- **The Maldives:** issuing new work visas; 32,408 cleared from January to 7 November 2026 [F0018, F0019].
- **Kuwait:** open for company jobs; closed to new Bangladeshi domestic workers, since Kuwait's June 2026 list of approved countries leaves out Bangladesh and the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0020, F0021, F0022, F0197].
- **Jordan:** open for garment factories and for women in domestic work, closed to new recruits in most other jobs since 1 June 2026 [F0023, F0175].
- **Italy:** only through an employer in Italy who names you under the yearly quota; about 10,000 Bangladeshis got work visas in 2025 [F0176, F0177].
- **The UAE:** work and visit visas for Bangladeshis have been restricted since July 2024, with no published ban [F0178, F0179].
- **Malaysia:** a restart was announced on 15 October 2026, but the medical tests it needs had not been approved by 4 November 2026 [F0180, F0181].
- **Oman:** new visas for Bangladeshis suspended since 31 October 2023, apart from a few categories [F0182, F0183, F0184].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** BMET cleared only 11 workers from January to 9 November 2026 [F0185].
- **Cambodia:** BMET cleared none in October 2026, and the Ministry told BMET not to clear workers for 12 countries, Cambodia among them, without its own approval [F0186, F0187].

Prime Minister Tarique Rahman told parliament on 15 April 2026 that steps had been taken to sign bilateral agreements to expand labour markets in Serbia, Greece, North Macedonia, Romania, Portugal, Brazil and Russia, and a recruitment agreement to open Thailand's labour market [CQ0152, CQ0153].

**How many go, and where.** Fewer people are going, and most go to a handful of Gulf countries:
- **2022 to 2024:** by BMET data reported on 18 December 2024, more than 11 lakh workers went abroad in 2022, 13 lakh in 2023 and 9 lakh in the first 11 months of 2024; overseas employment fell further after Malaysia closed in June 2024 and rose again on Saudi Arabia, which took 60% of the year's workers and more than 80% in October and November [CQ0055, CQ0056, CQ0057]. RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit, a migration research group) counted 9,06,355 departures in January to November 2024, against 13,05,453 in the whole of 2023 [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. The Ami Probashi 2024 annual report gives 10,09,146 workers in 2024, 27% fewer than the 13,90,811 of 2023, with 62% going to Saudi Arabia [CQ0070, CQ0071, CQ0072]. RMMRU's December 2024 report said no labour migration took place in 2024 to several countries including Oman, Bahrain, the UAE and the Maldives, that Italy was stalled by forged documents, and that Serbia was stalled because the application server was out of order [MA0078].
- **Saudi Arabia's share:** about 6,25,000 Bangladeshis went to Saudi Arabia for work in 2024, over 7,55,000 in 2025 and about 2,15,000 in the first half of 2026, and recruiting agencies say the rise may not continue [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083].
- **2025:** the expatriates' welfare minister told parliament on 5 April 2026 that 11,32,519 workers went abroad for jobs in 2025, 62,352 of them women, and that more than 1.5 crore (15 million) Bangladeshi workers were then working in 176 countries [CQ0150, CQ0151]. BMET data cited in a Prothom Alo column of 3 April 2026 give 11,16,399 for 2025, about 82.4% of them (9,19,981) to the Middle East or GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) countries, and say about 46% (USD 15.07 billion) of the USD 32.8 billion sent home in 2025 came from GCC countries [CQ0148, CQ0149]. According to BMET, workers went to 141 of the 168 countries on its list in 2025, 90% of them to just five countries, and 13 countries received one worker each [CQ0210]; a Prothom Alo editorial of 28 March 2026 put Saudi Arabia's share of 2025 departures at 67% [CQ0142, CQ0143, CQ0144]. RMMRU said in March 2026 that 4,663 Bangladeshis went to Russia in 2025 against 993 the year before, and that a ring had begun recruiting migrants with promises of good pay and Russian citizenship, part of which, with Russian rings, was sending Bangladeshi workers to the Russia-Ukraine war (see Russia: recruited into the war) [CQ0211, CQ0212].
- **The last fiscal year:** the expatriates' welfare minister, Ariful Haque Chowdhury, told parliament in a written answer on 3 September 2026 that 9,85,120 Bangladeshis went abroad for work in the last fiscal year, including 6,46,062 to Saudi Arabia, 89,023 to Qatar, 75,041 to Singapore, 42,130 to the Maldives and 39,875 to Kuwait [CQ0176].
- **2026:** BMET says about 5 lakh workers took clearance in January to August 2026, against about 7,40,000 in the same months of 2025 [RU0081, RU0082, RU0083]. BMET data cited in a Prothom Alo editorial of 28 March 2026 show that clearances in the first 10 days of March 2026 fell by about 50% from the same period a year earlier, after the Middle East war closed airspace and several hundred flights from Dhaka's Shahjalal airport were cancelled [CQ0140, CQ0141].
- **Markets that closed or shrank:** Malaysia closed in June 2024 over irregularities, Oman has been effectively closed since 2024, and workers going to the UAE fell from about 1 lakh in 2023 to 47,000 in 2024 and 13,750 in 2025; analysts told Prothom Alo that the closure or shrinking of these markets is a major reason workers have become interested in going to risky countries [RU0079, RU0080].

> **Need to know**
> - Did labour migration to the UAE stop in 2024, as RMMRU reported, or did about 47,000 workers go, as Prothom Alo reported in 2026 [MA0078, RU0079]?
> - How many workers went abroad in 2025: 11,32,519, as the minister told parliament, or 11,16,399, as the BMET data in the April 2026 column give [CQ0150, CQ0148]?
> - How many went in 2023: 13,05,453 (RMMRU), 13,90,811 (the Ami Probashi report) or about 13 lakh (BMET) [MA0079, CQ0070, CQ0055]?
> - How many Bangladeshis went to Russia in 2025: 4,663 or 4,727, the two figures RMMRU is reported as giving [CQ0211, RU0046]?

Each country doc opens with its current status under "Is it open now, and who can go?" (for example, see the Qatar doc, Background, Status).

#### Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?

On 4 November 2026 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare told BMET not to issue clearances for 12 countries without the Ministry's recruitment permission: Serbia, Moldova, Belarus, Congo, Russia, Japan, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Ukraine and Libya [F0187]. The State Minister has named Serbia, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam and Libya as high-risk countries where visas must be strictly checked [F0012]. In Cambodia, a study by BRAC (the Bangladeshi development organisation) found 98% of returnees got no job and 90% were forced into online scams [F0189].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the list's Congo actually Kosovo, and is Cambodia on it or Croatia, as other reports of the same 4 November 2026 order give [F0188, F0187]?

#### Which countries can a woman go to?

- **Saudi Arabia:** as a domestic worker, aged 25 to 45, and she should pay nothing (see Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?) [F0190, F0191].
- **Jordan:** garment factories, through BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the government's recruiting agency); 12,784 women went from January to 8 November 2026, the second destination for women after Saudi Arabia [F0192, F0193].
- **Qatar:** domestic work, with the same 25 to 45 age rule for BMET clearance; in August 2025 BMET let one agency send 100 women housemaids to a Qatar employer [F0195, F0233].
- **Kuwait:** company jobs only (230 women from January to 8 November 2026); the Bangladesh Embassy attests no women's domestic-worker visas [F0196, F0197].
- **The Maldives:** women can go, but few do (148 in 2026 to 7 November) [F0198].
- **Singapore:** almost none: 126 women of 70,832 workers cleared in 2025 [F0199].
- **Oman:** 3 women cleared in 2026 to 8 November, after 6,636 in 2023 [F0200, F0201].

Fewer women go than a few years ago. BMET data show the most in one year, 1,21,925, went in 2017, and more than 1 lakh a year went until 2019; in 2023, 77,263 of more than 13 lakh workers were women [AA0028, AA0029, AA0030]. In January to November 2024, 54,696 women went, 6% of all workers and the lowest share in ten years apart from the pandemic, by RMMRU's count [MA0079, MA0080, MA0081]. Bangladesh began sending women workers in 1991; Saudi Arabia signed its agreement with Bangladesh in 2015, after Indonesia and the Philippines stopped sending domestic workers over allegations of abuse, and most Bangladeshi women migrants go there as domestic workers [AA0031, AA0032]. A joint secretary general of BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) said in January 2024 that Saudi employers want workers quickly once they place a demand, that delays stop the women being sent, and that employers therefore take workers from other countries [AA0045, AA0046].

> **Need to know**
> - Are UAE recruitment offices getting domestic-worker entry permits for Bangladeshi women in November 2026, or are they refused at the visa stage?

#### Is there a job for someone with no skills?

Saudi Arabia's skill certificate is not required of domestic workers, though women going to the Middle East for domestic work must take the housekeeping course [F0204, F0094]. Saudi Arabia's skill test, the SVP (Skills Verification Program, run by Takamol), is compulsory for listed trades, and the list includes street, hospital and office cleaners, loading and unloading workers and construction workers [F0202, F0203]. Qatar requires a training and test certificate only for professions its labour ministry lists [F0205]. A Singapore construction job needs a skills test at one of five centres in Bangladesh approved by Singapore's Building and Construction Authority, until the test moves to Singapore in January 2028 [F0024, F0025]. BMET's TTCs (Technical Training Centres) teach 55 trades, and project-funded courses are free and pay a monthly stipend [F0026]. Most who go are not counted as skilled: by BMET data reported in December 2025, about 26% of the 11,98,900 workers who went abroad in FY2023-24 were skilled, against 18% of the 10,15,312 in FY2024-25, when 59% went as low-skilled and only 3% as professionals [CQ0124, CQ0125].

#### How much does it really cost to go?

People pay several times the legal maximum (see What is the most an agency may charge me?): workers who went to five Gulf countries on a "free visa" (a visa bought from a sponsor with no real job behind it) spent Tk 4,99,802 on average, against a government-set average of Tk 1,16,224 [F0356]. The ILO (International Labour Organization) and IOM (International Organization for Migration) heads in Bangladesh wrote in December 2025 that, according to the Labour Force Survey 2024, the average cost of migration is over USD 3,500 (Tk 4.62 lakh), about 10 months' earnings, and that Bangladeshi workers pay two to three times what Indian or Nepali workers pay for the same jobs in the GCC countries and Malaysia [CQ0128, CQ0129]. Asif Nazrul, then the expatriates' welfare adviser, said in August 2025 that people spend Tk 8 to 10 lakh to go abroad and many get no work when they arrive [CQ0122]. By country:
- **Saudi Arabia:** about Tk 5 lakh on average, up to Tk 9 lakh [F0027].
- **Qatar:** Tk 4 to 7 lakh for a "free visa" [F0028].
- **Malaysia:** Tk 5.44 lakh on average in 2022-24 [F0029].
- **Singapore:** first-time workers are asked for S$9,000 to 13,000, about Tk 8.7 to 12.6 lakh, and some pay up to Tk 16 lakh [F0030, F0357, F0031].
- **Kuwait:** a visa is sold through brokers for KD 2,000 to 2,200, about Tk 8 to 9 lakh [F0032].
- **The UAE:** at least Tk 3.5 lakh for driving jobs, the State Minister said in May 2026 [F0033].
- **The Maldives:** about Tk 3 to 4 lakh in 2024 [F0034].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** Tk 5 to 7 lakh a person, by the CID's (Criminal Investigation Department's) 2024 estimate [F0035].
- **Italy:** one worker paid an intermediary EUR 20,000 [F0036].
- **Jordan garment jobs through BOESL:** the factory pays; the worker pays Tk 200 for fingerprints and Tk 200 for the orientation, and the company repays these before departure [F0206].

On 24 March 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare said the government was working, as part of its 180-day action plan, to fix migration costs, and that once final this would stop extra money being taken (see What is the most an agency may charge me?) [CQ0139].

#### What is the most an agency may charge me?

The government fixes the maximum by order [F0207]. If BMET has issued a permission letter for your job, the most is the amount written on it (see What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?) [F0208]. Otherwise the caps reported for each country, with the year each was set or reported, are:
- Saudi Arabia: Tk 1,65,000, set in 2016 [F0209].
- Singapore: Tk 2,62,270, as reported by Bangladesh Bank in 2019 [F0210].
- The UAE: Tk 1,07,780, set in 2017 [F0211].
- Kuwait: Tk 1,06,780, as stated by the Bangladesh Embassy in Kuwait in August 2025 [F0212].
- Malaysia: Tk 78,990, the cap for the 2022-24 recruitment [F0029].
- Italy: none; the 2017 caps don't cover it [F0213].

A woman going as a domestic worker may be charged nothing [F0191].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the Ministry issued the new country-by-country maximums the State Minister promised in October 2026 [F0214]?

#### Which fees do I pay to the government?

The main Bangladesh-side fees (the full list for Saudi Arabia is in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Who pays what):
- OEP (Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's website) registration: Tk 200 [F0215].
- A 48-page, 5-year e-passport: Tk 4,025 (regular) [F0217].
- Police clearance: Tk 1,500 on the portal [F0218].
- Welfare fee to the Wage Earners' Welfare Board: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026 [F0219].
- Migrant insurance: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card (clearance) fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax at clearance: Tk 250 to 1,200, depending on the visa [F0222].

#### How much will I earn?

- **Saudi Arabia:** cleaners and general workers earn SR 800 to 1,000 a month; there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0223, F0224].
- **Qatar:** the legal minimum is QR 1,000 a month, plus QR 500 for housing and QR 300 for food if the employer doesn't provide them [F0225].
- **Malaysia:** the minimum wage is RM1,700 a month [F0226].
- **Singapore:** construction basic pay averages S$820 a month for Bangladeshis [F0227].
- **Kuwait:** the Bangladesh Embassy attests a government-contract cleaner's or labourer's visa only if it pays at least KD 90 a month [F0358, F0228].
- **Jordan garment factories:** JD 230 a month, of which at least JD 130 is cash and JD 100 is paid in kind as housing and food [F0229].
- **The Maldives:** migrant workers surveyed in 2024 averaged MVR 6,760 a month, and there is no minimum wage for foreigners [F0230, F0231].
- **Oman:** a minimum of RO 90 a month was agreed between the two governments but is not rigorously enforced [F0232].

#### How long will it take to pay back what I spend?

In 2020 men took 19 months or more on average to recover what they spent [F0359]. A Saudi worker on SR 800 with free housing who buys his own food (SR 500 or more a month) and phone (SR 50 or more) keeps at most SR 250, about Tk 8,300 at Tk 33.20 a riyal [F0223, F0037]; at that rate a Tk 4.5 lakh debt takes about 54 months to repay before interest, against a usual two-year contract [F0038]. Many low-skilled Gulf workers struggle to send even Tk 15,000 to 20,000 a month; at that rate the same debt takes 23 to 30 months [F0039]. Malaysia workers who paid high fees stayed in debt for two to three years (2024) [F0040]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column worked through a Saudi pest-control job at the set wage of SR 700 a month, which it gives as Tk 22,400: two years under the contract earn Tk 5,37,600, and after about Tk 2 lakh in living costs Tk 3,37,600 is left to send home [AA0201, AA0202]. It puts the cost of that job at about Tk 5,70,000, including about Tk 4,60,000 to the recruiting agency and about Tk 50,000 to sub-agents [AA0203]; by its arithmetic the worker is still about Tk 2,32,000 short of that cost after two years, and in effect has to work four years for almost no gain [AA0204].

#### Who pays for the air ticket?

It depends on the job. On Saudi jobs with a BMET permission letter, the letter can put the airfare on the employer, as Bonanza Overseas' September 2025 letter does [F0360]. On BOESL's Jordan garment jobs the factory bears all the costs [F0206]. On a visa a relative sends from Qatar, the worker usually buys the ticket through the Dhaka broker [F0041]. A December 2025 Prothom Alo column says Saudi law puts the worker's visa and recruitment costs on the employer but in practice this is often not followed, and that under Malaysia's G2G (government-to-government) recruitment many workers went for just Tk 35,000 in airfare, a system it says ended because recruiting agencies opposed it [AA0205, AA0206, AA0207].

**What the ticket costs.** BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) members said in a memorandum on 5 February 2025 that every year a syndicate hoards air tickets and sells each at double the price or more, ruining poor outgoing workers, and asked for action against it and for a labour fair for migrant workers [CQ0073, CQ0074, CQ0113]. Ticket agencies said in April 2025 that under the previous government a ring formed around ticket sales in which Biman (Biman Bangladesh Airlines, the state carrier) held back cheap tickets and released dear ones and the ring resold cheap tickets at high prices, and the aviation expert Kazi Wahidul Alam said a Tk 1 lakh ticket abroad carries Tk 40,000 in government taxes [CQ0091, CQ0092, CQ0093]. In February 2025 the civil aviation and tourism ministry issued 10 directives against abnormal ticket prices, requiring tickets to be sold at the Civil Aviation Authority's approved price list and discounted tickets to be given to migrant workers, and Biman's managing director, Md Safiqur Rahman, said in April 2025 that Biman was giving workers discounted tickets [CQ0088, CQ0089, CQ0090]. Biman's T-class fares announced in parliament on 18 June 2026 cut Dhaka-Doha from Tk 53,000 to Tk 46,000, Dhaka-Muscat from Tk 39,000 to Tk 30,000 and Dhaka-Kuala Lumpur from Tk 28,000 to Tk 21,000 [CQ0218].

> **Need to know**
> - Who may buy Biman's T-class fare, and what must a worker show (BMET smart card, visa) to get it at a Biman counter [CQ0218]?

#### Can I go through the government agency, BOESL?

BOESL has sent 1.43 lakh workers in 14 years, 1.06 lakh of them to Jordan, where it sends women to garment factories at no cost [F0042, F0193]. It sent 16,066 workers to various countries in FY2024-25, 4% more than the year before, Prothom Alo reported in August 2025 [CQ0123]. In November 2026 it advertised 112 zero-cost jobs for skilled men at DHL in Malaysia, with applications on OEP [F0043]. Recruitment under the October 2025 Saudi agreement goes through private agencies, not BOESL [F0044], and BOESL lists no Qatar recruitment [F0045]. An additional superintendent of police in Cumilla said at an April 2026 roundtable that people should be encouraged to go through government channels such as BOESL, and that those who do are comparatively better off [AA0222, AA0223].

### Agencies and dalals

#### How do I check whether an agency is real?

1. Ask for the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and find it on OEP's agency list at www.oep.gov.bd/agencies, which shows its name, address, office phone, licence status and validity date [F0046].
2. Read the status, not the date: a licence can show "Cancelled" while its validity date is years ahead [F0047]. Recruiting after cancellation is a crime carrying 1 to 2 years' prison [F0048]. Status can change back and forth in court: the withdrawal of 49 agencies' licences on 19 July 2026 was suspended by a High Court order on 15 September 2026, and restored when the Appellate Division's chamber court stayed that order on 30 September, according to the Attorney General [MB0096, MB0097].
3. Check the job too, because licensed agencies also cheat: workers paid agencies Tk 3 to 4.2 lakh for Qatar jobs and three of them got fake contract letters [F0049, F0050, F0051].

The number of licensed agencies has grown: RMMRU said in January 2026 that Bangladesh's licensed recruiting agencies, once limited to 850-900, were raised to 2,300 by the previous government, and that 252 new licences were approved in 2025 with 300 more in process, against 192 agencies in India, 464 in Pakistan, 416 in Nepal and 248 in Sri Lanka [CQ0130, CQ0131, CQ0132].

The most active agencies, with their cited red flags, are in the knowledge base's Recruiting agencies doc; the steps for one country are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1.

#### Is it legal to go through a dalal?

A dalal (দালাল, broker) is legal only if a licensed agency has registered and appointed him as its sub-agent [F0052, F0053]. Acting as a sub-agent without registration carries 1 year's prison or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh, and the agency that uses one risks 1 year or Tk 1 to 5 lakh [F0054, F0055]. Most people still go through one: 56% of 1,084 Gulf migrants in a 2025 OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) survey relied on informal or illegal agents [F0056, F0057], and in the Maldives 59% went through a sub-agent or dalal [F0058]. The broker is often a friend or relative [F0059]. Dr Selim Reza wrote in September 2026 that more than 2,000 agencies have been added to RAIMS (the Recruitment Agents Information System), that a 2024 amendment brought sub-agents and intermediaries under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act, and that a 2025 code of conduct provides for holding sub-agents to account [MB0087, MB0088, MB0089]. The Ami Probashi phone app, which works with BMET, has a free chat-based CV builder in Bangla and English, and Prothom Alo wrote in July 2025 that a proper CV reduces dependence on dalals [OA0115, OA0116].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there a public list of registered sub-agents that a worker can check a dalal's name against [F0053]?
> - Was the sub-agent amendment passed in 2023 (see The law on sub-agents) or in 2024, and can a worker search RAIMS [S0093, MB0088]?

#### What is a demand letter?

A demand letter is the employer's written request for workers; for Qatar, the company or its agent files it at the Bangladesh Embassy with the government's visa approval, and the Embassy attests it [F0060]. The Bangladesh mission checks the employer and attests the job demand and visa before the agency selects workers [F0061]. Migration experts told Prothom Alo in February 2024 that migrants already abroad collect job demand from their employers and send it to acquaintances and relatives at home, and that about 90% of demand letters reach Bangladesh this way [CQ0016]. Ask the agency to show you the attested copy (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 4) [F0060].

#### What is BMET's permission letter, and where do I find it?

It is BMET's approval for an agency to send a set number of workers to a named employer; it shows the job, the salary, the contract terms, who pays the migration cost, and the most the agency may take [F0233, F0234, F0208]. It also makes the agency promise, on stamp paper, to pay the worker's food, lodging, return and compensation if the job is not as promised [F0235]. BMET posts these letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, but the newest one there is dated 10 December 2025 [F0236]. A single visa (up to eight workers for one employer on one demand letter) needs no Ministry recruitment permission, so it has no such letter [F0237].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BMET still issue permission letters after 10 December 2025, and can a worker get a copy from BMET [F0236]?

#### A relative abroad is sending me a visa. Is that safe?

Many are bought "free visas", sold on by a sponsor who will not employ the worker (see "A free visa lets me work for anyone") [F0361]. From Cumilla and other districts about 80% go on visas from relatives and friends [F0238]. Relatives working abroad often get the visa by paying the employer or others and pass it on to people at home [F0239]. A bought visa also shows as genuine on Qatar's visa checker, so check that the company on the visa is the one you will work for and that the job matches [F0240]. Buying or selling a visa inside Bangladesh carries 2 to 7 years' prison [F0241]. What a relative may be sending is explained in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 1, item 5.

#### How should I pay the agency, and when?

Pay through the bank into the agency's own account and keep every receipt [F0242]. BMET asks people not to go to middlemen and to pay only through the proper channel against a receipt [F0243]. BMET's September 2025 permission letter for Saudi kitchen workers, for example, let the agency take its fee only by cheque, bank draft or pay order, and only after the visa, clearance and flight were confirmed [F0208]. In practice most workers pay sub-agents in instalments before the visa is confirmed and get the contract only at the airport [F0244].

#### Is there a list of good agencies?

One agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment: Bonanza Overseas (RL-0579) was the Bangladeshi agency in a fair-recruitment pilot that the ILO (International Labour Organization) began in 2018 with QDVC, a construction company in Qatar [F0245]. The Recruiting agencies doc lists the most active agencies with their red flags. The State Minister for expatriates' welfare said on 30 July 2026 that most of the roughly 3,000 licence holders are involved in irregularities, that no more than 400 or 500 licences would survive if the rules were followed, and that many agencies renew their licences without qualifying [MB0061, MB0062]. In May 2026 he said the government will introduce a grading system to identify good agencies, fix a standard migration cost, introduce a "Probashi card", bring the district manpower office, PKB (Probashi Kallyan Bank) and the welfare centre into one building for a one-stop service, appoint law firms to protect women workers abroad and create a fund to rehabilitate returnees [OA0193, OA0194]. The Prime Minister's spokesman had said on 18 April 2026 that work had begun on a "Probashi card" for migrants, giving no details [CQ0217]. The Prime Minister's deputy press secretary, Shahadat Swadhin, wrote in Prothom Alo on 19 August 2026 that the card was to start on a pilot basis from August 2026, under the Prime Minister's direct planning, to improve migrants' social standing, legal protection and living standards [CQ0174, CQ0175].

> **Need to know**
> - Has the agency grading system announced in May 2026 been introduced, and where are the grades published [OA0193]?
> - Has the Probashi card pilot started, who can get the card, where, and what does it give the holder [CQ0174]?

#### The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?

1. Write down who took the money, from whom, when and how much; a Ministry joint secretary said in 2025 that action follows when these are given [F0246].
2. Apply to BMET's Director General with your proof at the workers' complaint cell; it is free and meant to be settled within 30 working days [F0247].
3. Or send an application with proof, your address and the agency's RL number to the Ministry's enforcement branch, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd; action starts within 3 to 5 working days [F0062, F0063].
4. Mobile courts give immediate remedies to people about to go and returned migrants, free, within 7 working days [F0064].
5. If nothing happens, go up BMET's grievance ladder to the Ministry's Secretary and then the Cabinet Division's system at www.grs.gov.bd [F0065].

The full route is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Scams, If the agency cheats you.

#### Is cheating a migrant a crime?

Yes. Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad, keeping a migrant's passport or papers, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay carries up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [F0066, F0067]. Publishing an overseas job advert without government or BMET approval carries up to 1 year [F0068]. Migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to legal aid [F0069]; OKUP gives free legal advice and help filing cases on 01842773300 [F0070].

### Papers

#### How do I get a passport, and what does it cost?

Apply for an e-passport exactly as your NID (national ID card) reads; anyone over 20 must use the NID [F0071]. A 48-page, 5-year e-passport costs Tk 4,025 (regular), Tk 6,325 (express) or Tk 8,625 (super express) [F0217]. Pay online through A-Challan, DGePay or ShurjoPay, or at a bank [F0072], and bring the original NID to the passport office [F0073]. Delivery after biometrics is within 15 working days (regular), 7 (express) or 2 (super express) [F0074]. At the Gazipur regional passport office in May 2025, people who paid a dalal Tk 1,000 to 1,500 more went straight to the fingerprint and photo room while others waited hours (see The passport office) [OA0089, OA0090]. A mismatch with the NID traps workers already abroad: the opposition leader, Shafiqur Rahman, told parliament on 10 June 2026 that many expatriates could not get e-passports because their MRP (machine-readable passport) and NID data did not match, through small spelling differences in vowel signs or names or a mismatch in age, and that many abroad were becoming or risked becoming undocumented as a result [CQ0173].

> **Need to know**
> - How does a worker abroad whose MRP and NID differ get the NID corrected or the e-passport issued: at the mission, or only in Bangladesh, and with which papers [CQ0173]?

#### How much validity must my passport have?

It depends on the step: the police clearance needs at least 3 months [F0218], and the Qatar Visa Center (QVC, where Qatar-bound workers sign the contract, give fingerprints and take the medical) books only passports with more than 195 days left [F0367, F0075]. Immigration's hotline gets calls from people asking whether four months is enough to travel [F0002]. Since 19 August 2026 the Special Branch runs a 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport, 01320007588, for questions on passport validity and travel bans [F0001].

#### How do I get a police clearance certificate?

Apply at pcc.police.gov.bd: register, fill in your details and addresses, upload the scans and submit [F0218, F0076]. Pay Tk 1,500 by treasury challan at Bangladesh Bank or Sonali Bank, or online by card [F0218]. The police verify you at your present address; track it by texting "PCC S <reference number>" to 26969, and collect it with a copy of your passport [F0077]. The PCC helpdesk is 01320001824 or 01320001825 [F0077]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2. Brokers sell forged certificates: police said a ring, whose arrested members were mainly brokers, took Tk 1,23,000 from a man in Banshkhali, Chattogram, in 2026 on a promise of a quick police clearance and gave him a forged one; his brother said he had a criminal case and so had not applied to the police directly (see Forged documents and impersonation) [OB0012, OB0016, OB0025, OB0026].

> **Need to know**
> - Is the fee Tk 1,500 (the portal) or Tk 500 (Bangladesh Police's guidance) [F0218, F0078]?

#### What is attestation, what is an apostille, and which do I need?

An apostille is a single certificate from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) that member countries of the Hague Apostille Convention accept; it is applied for on mygov.bd at Tk 200 a page and takes 5 working days [F0079, F0080]. MoFA lists 114 countries that accept it, including Oman, Saudi Arabia and Italy, but not Qatar [F0081, F0082]. For a country that doesn't accept it, each document is first verified by its issuing body and then attested by MoFA: SSC and HSC (Secondary School Certificate and Higher Secondary Certificate) certificates by the Education Board, then the Ministry of Education, then MoFA [F0083, F0084, F0085]. The Qatar steps, including legalisation by the Qatari mission, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 2, item 3; the apostille steps in the Italy doc, Work visa: Decreto Flussi worker (non-seasonal), Step 4.

#### How do I register on OEP?

Registration on OEP is the first step: agencies must pick workers from BMET's database, and BMET clears no one outside it [F0248, F0249]. Open employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp in Google Chrome, type your details exactly as on the NID, enter the code sent to your mobile, fill in the form, and pay by bKash, Nagad or another mobile wallet [F0250, F0251]. The fee is Tk 200 [F0215]. Download your Registration Card at the end [F0250]. If it fails, call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [F0252]. The DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office) also does registration and fingerprinting, and gives a three-day pre-decision training on legal migration, avoiding brokers and safety; Sylhet's DEMO said in April 2026 it had registered and fingerprinted 35,290 people in 2025 and 6,290 up to March 2026, and that many people fall in with brokers before the training [LB0232, LB0233].

**What OEP is.** OEP, built under the Ministry with the ILO and the Swiss government, has run since 19 April 2025, after the interim government scrapped the "Ami Probashi" app, through which workers had paid the private operating company a fee on top of BMET's; it was formally launched on 19 November 2025, and its data stay with BMET [CQ0224, CQ0225]. On it a worker registers with a mobile number, fills in a profile, pays the Tk 200 BMET registration fee online, applies for BMET clearance and downloads the clearance smart card, can contact recruiting agencies directly about jobs and can enrol at a TTC; foreign employers can register and, once the Bangladesh mission attests them, post demand letters that go to agencies [CQ0226, CQ0227, CQ0228]. By 19 November 2025, 2,647 recruiting agencies had joined OEP, 10,91,161 workers had applied to BMET for clearance through it and 10,24,468 had got it, and 8,37,042 had registered for TTC training, by the figures shown on the OEP site [CQ0229, CQ0230].

#### What is the medical, and how do I book it?

For the Gulf countries it is the Wafid check, booked online and done at a medical centre assigned by your city; people still call it "GAMCA", after the system Wafid replaced [F0253, F0254]. Book the slip yourself at wafid.com: USD 10 for a centre assigned to you, or USD 25 to choose the centre and date [F0255]. The whole medical should cost no more than Tk 8,500 to 9,000 [F0256], yet most workers pay at least Tk 17,000 to 20,000 [F0257]. Check the result on wafid.com's status search; it can take up to 26 days [F0258, F0259]. Qatar-bound workers take their medical at the QVC instead (see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4); the Wafid steps are in the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4.

#### Can I pay to be passed "fit" in the medical?

The Daily Star reported in July 2025 that agencies offer "fit" packages, Tk 20,000 against a Tk 10,000 standard one [F0260], and that a syndicate reportedly charges Tk 25,000 to 30,000 for a guaranteed fit certificate [F0261]. A worker who paid Tk 25,000 for one was found unfit in Saudi Arabia and deported [F0262]. The same report said the approved centres are allegedly controlled by a few owners who issue fake certificates [F0263].

#### What if I'm declared unfit?

You can't retake the Wafid test for three months [F0086]. If you think the result is wrong or the centre demanded extra money, complain in writing to the Gulf Health Council within two weeks of the test; anonymous complaints are refused [F0087, F0088]. Overcharging can also be reported to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's enforcement branch with your receipts and the centre's name and address (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 4) [F0089].

#### Do I need a skill test?

- **Saudi Arabia:** the SVP is compulsory for listed trades; it costs USD 85 per attempt, runs at 27 TTCs and BRTC Gazipur (the Bangladesh Road Transport Corporation's training institute), and a score of 50% passes [F0090, F0091, F0092, F0093]. A test centre had opened in Cox's Bazar by June 2026, and the principal of the Ramu TTC said passing makes a Saudi job easier to get, with a chance of higher pay; his centre runs pre-departure training and three-month technical courses [MB0040, MB0041]. Steps: the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 3.
- **Singapore construction:** a skills test at a Bangladesh test centre until January 2028, when workers will be tested in Singapore after arrival [F0025].
- **Qatar:** only for professions on the labour ministry's list [F0205].
- **Domestic workers:** no Saudi skill test, but women must take the housekeeping course (see What is the housekeeping course?) [F0204, F0094].

There is no way to pass the Saudi test by paying extra, and a worker caught paying has the account locked and can't go to Saudi Arabia [F0095, F0096].

#### How do I join a TTC course?

Log in on OEP, open Training portal, then Training, then Trade/Skills, choose your division, district and centre, pick the trade and press Apply [F0097]. Pay the BMET fee for short courses against a money receipt; project-run courses are free and pay a stipend, and courses last from 3 days to 4 years [F0026]. If anyone offers to get you into or through a TTC for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0098]. Courses vary by centre: the Sylhet TTC said in April 2026 it runs regular Japanese, Italian, Korean and Arabic language courses, and, with ILO (International Labour Organization) support, level-three courses in electrical installation and maintenance and in food preparation that combine language with the trade, with a possibility of work in Italy afterwards [LB0236, LB0237]. A Sylhet TTC senior instructor said many who most need training don't come, college and university students with no immediate need to go abroad come instead, and in the centre's experience about 99% of people go abroad without acquiring a skill [LB0238, LB0239]. The Sandwip TTC trained 750 people in 2025, and BMET data show 2,080 people from Sandwip went abroad that year [FV0109, FV0110, FV0111]. Nationally, according to Ami Probashi's 2024 annual report, enrolment in TTC courses fell to 1,12,166 in 2024 from 2,36,270 in 2023, while BMET registrations rose to 7,98,276 from 6,60,088, with women's share of registrations rising from 2.78% to 4.79% [CQ0110, CQ0111, CQ0112]. RMMRU's acting executive director, Tasneem Siddiqui, said in December 2025 that the skills of TTC-trained workers are not internationally recognised [CQ0126, CQ0127].

#### What is the pre-departure orientation (PDO)?

It is a compulsory 3-day course at one of 70 training institutes, booked on OEP; the institute sets the fee and gives a money receipt [F0094, F0099]. Booked through the Ami Probashi phone app, the enrolment fee is Tk 100 plus a Tk 100 platform charge [F0216]. The general secretary of the Ovibashi Nari Sramik Federation (a federation of women migrant workers) said in May 2026 that the three-day training at the district employment office before departure mainly covers first notions of the language, culture and rules, with no in-depth training on the reality of the work, its risks or the long term; a UNDP gender expert said language, culture and practical work skills can't be learnt in three days [OA0218, OA0219]. Expatriates' Welfare Ministry officials said in October 2026 that BMET's pre-departure training is only a three-day workshop in which nothing is taught well, and that workers need training on the destination's type of work, conditions, working hours and staying healthy [CQ0241, CQ0242]. An HIV-negative certificate is compulsory before going abroad, but pre-departure briefings say very little about HIV, safe behaviour or the right to health care abroad, Prothom Alo reported in February 2026 [CQ0206, CQ0207].

> **Need to know**
> - Can the PDO still be booked through the Ami Probashi app, which the interim government is reported to have scrapped in April 2025, or only on OEP [F0216, CQ0224]?

#### How do I check my visa is real?

Take copies of the visa and the passport's visa page to BMET in Dhaka or your district employment and manpower office (DEMO) [F0362]; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [F0101]. BMET checks Saudi, Qatar, Bahrain, Singapore and UAE work visas online, and has a visa-check app on Google Play [F0101]. The State Minister said in 2026 that BMET can verify visas online for only four or five countries [F0011]. Each country doc gives its own online check (for example, see the Saudi doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7).

### BMET clearance and the smart card

#### What is the smart card, and do I need it?

The smart card is BMET's emigration clearance, and welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check it before departure [F0102]. It also makes you a member of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (WEWB, the Ministry's welfare body for migrants), whose members get help when sick, easier loans from Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), scholarships for their children and Tk 3 lakh for the heirs if they die [F0103, F0355]. It gives you the migrant insurance too [F0220]. Families of workers without clearance or membership don't get the Welfare Board's death grant [F0104].

#### How do I get BMET clearance?

- **Through an agency:** the agency applies after the visa, with the visa copy, the attested contract, your registration card and the fees; BMET clears within 3 working days [F0105, F0194].
- **On a visa you got yourself:** apply at BMET's emigration branch in Dhaka or the Chattogram district employment and manpower office with the visa copy, the contract if any, your registration card, a declaration on Tk 300 stamp paper and the fees; 3 working days [F0106].

Since July 2023 the application can also be made online through the Ami Probashi phone app, and the card downloaded, without going to BMET [F0365]. Prothom Alo reported in August 2025, and the expatriates' welfare adviser said on 20 August 2025, that the interim government had shut "Ami Probashi", the private app the previous government had allowed for worker registration, and that BMET now issues emigration clearance 100% online on the government's own integrated platform for overseas employment (see How do I register on OEP?) [CQ0219, CQ0220, CQ0222, CQ0223]. The full list of papers is in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 6, item 3.

At a press conference of the expatriates' welfare adviser on 24 September 2024, the Ministry stated that sending a worker abroad needed approvals from three bodies and that its own recruitment permission would no longer be required, that brokers would be registered, and that recruiting agencies would be given category certificates based on experience [CQ0025, CQ0026, CQ0027].

> **Need to know**
> - Which three bodies must approve before a worker can be sent, and is the Ministry's recruitment permission, dropped in September 2024, now needed again only for the 12 countries in the 4 November 2026 order (see Why won't BMET clear workers for some countries?) [CQ0025, F0187]?
> - Does the Ami Probashi app still give clearance in 2026, or is OEP the only route [F0365, CQ0222]?

#### How much does clearance cost?

- Welfare fee: Tk 5,500 from 1 October 2026, up from Tk 3,500 [F0219].
- Insurance premium: Tk 1,000, once [F0220].
- Smart card fee: Tk 250 [F0221].
- Source income tax: Tk 400 on a single visa not attested by the embassy, Tk 250 if attested, and Tk 1,200 (skilled) or Tk 800 (unskilled) on a group visa [F0222]; Tk 500 on a Saudi single visa [F0107].

#### How long does clearance take?

BMET's charter promises clearance within 3 working days of a complete application, whether the agency applies or you do [F0105, F0106].

#### How do I check that my smart card is real?

Check it at portal.wewb.gov.bd/bmet-wewbm-check [F0108]. Airport welfare desks checked 1,391,988 cards in 2023-24 and stopped 1,098 travellers with no or a fake clearance [F0109]. If something is wrong, contact BMET's Director (Emigration), 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [F0110].

#### Do I have to pay a bribe to get clearance?

BMET's charter lists only the fees above, and BMET asks anyone offered clearance for money to tell the nearest law-enforcement office [F0222, F0098]. The State Minister said in 2026 that some dishonest BMET officials give clearance on fake papers [F0013]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of passengers paying police at the airport to get through are true [F0010].

#### I went abroad without clearance. Can I still join the Welfare Board?

Yes. Apply online at portal.wewb.gov.bd; the application is processed through the Bangladesh embassy or high commission [F0111]. Members get a card with a QR code and the Board's benefits [F0112]. The steps for Qatar, including the fee, are in the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, Step 7, Welfare Board sign-up if you left without BMET clearance.

### Loans

#### Can I get a government loan to go abroad?

PKB, the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years including a grace period, and decides within 7 working days of complete papers [F0113]. The same loan is offered for a re-entry visa over 2 years, with instalments starting after a two-month grace period [OA0110]. Women migrants have a separate loan on the same terms [F0114]. You must be 18 to 60, not in default, and live permanently in the branch's area with your guarantors [F0115]. You need two guarantors, one financially solvent and one family member [F0116]. A Prothom Alo guide of July 2025 lists the papers as your photo, voter ID, address, certificates, copies of the passport, visa and smart card, one guarantor's papers and three bank cheques, with no collateral, and says you must first open an account at the bank [OA0108, OA0109]. A senior PKB officer in Cumilla said in April 2026 that an applicant needs a BMET card, a valid visa, a passport, an NID from the branch's area and a capable guarantor who will repay if needed [AA0237]. Helpline: 16238 [F0117].

> **Need to know**
> - Does PKB ask for two guarantors or one [F0116, AA0237]?

Many borrowers have to take Tk 3 to 4 lakh, with a monthly instalment of about Tk 14,000, and many take four or five months or longer to find work abroad, while the loan weighs on them, PKB's acting regional head in Sylhet said in April 2026 [LB0242]. PKB gave migration loans to 2,63,689 people from 2011-12 to June 2025, Tk 384 crore in all, its deputy managing director said in August 2026 [AB0131, AB0132]. The expatriates' welfare adviser, Asif Nazrul, announced on 24 September 2024 that migrants could now repay their PKB loan instalments by sending remittance directly to the bank, which had not been possible before, and that 12 commercial banks had agreed to lend to migrants once Bangladesh Bank gave a credit guarantee [CQ0023, CQ0024].

**For students.** On 24 March 2026 the state minister for expatriates' welfare, Md Nurul Haque Nur, said the Prime Minister had directed that students going abroad for higher study (to South Korea, Japan, China, Germany and other countries) get loans of up to Tk 10 lakh through PKB as proof of bank solvency, paid not in cash but against tuition fees or other required payments [CQ0136, CQ0137, CQ0138]. On 18 April 2026 the Prime Minister's adviser Mahdi Amin said a collateral-free bank guarantee of up to Tk 10 lakh had been arranged for higher study abroad, and that a "Probashi Card" for expatriates was being introduced (see Is there a list of good agencies?) [CQ0160, CQ0161].

> **Need to know**
> - Did the 12 commercial banks start lending to migrants under a Bangladesh Bank credit guarantee, and which banks [CQ0023]?
> - How does a student apply for PKB's Tk 10 lakh solvency loan or guarantee, at which branch and with which papers [CQ0136, CQ0160]?

#### When can I apply for the PKB loan?

Only once you hold the visa and the BMET smart card, near the end of the process [F0118]. The bank doesn't arrange visas, and finding guarantors stops many people from borrowing [F0119, F0120]. Branches are few: Cumilla's 17 upazilas (sub-districts) have 4 PKB branches, and the Tomson Bridge branch serves 9 upazilas with three officers, a senior PKB officer said in April 2026; she said staff shortages make giving and recovering loans hard, people often complain that the bank won't lend or asks for extra papers, and some borrowers' late repayment makes new lending harder [AA0238, AA0239, AA0240, OA0183, OA0184]. You can apply at the branch or online on mygov.bd, paying nothing when you submit; processing and documentation fees of 0.5% each come after the decision [F0121, F0122].

#### Should I borrow from a moneylender?

In a 2020 government survey, 81% of men had borrowed to go abroad [F0123]. In a 2020 IOM (International Organization for Migration) survey, most family loans were interest-free, NGO and bank loans carried 10-15%, and 62% of moneylender debt carried 50-150% interest [F0124]. A microcredit NGO may charge at most 24% a year [F0125]. A quarter of borrowers risk losing the collateral they pledged if they can't repay [F0264], and families mortgage land and sell cattle: a Natore farmer mortgaged his land and sold four cows, then could not fly [F0265]. A former expatriates' welfare secretary said what workers send home often doesn't even cover the interest [F0014].

#### Can I borrow abroad?

In Singapore, a worker found borrowing from an unlicensed moneylender has his Work Permit revoked and is sent home [F0266]. Many workers in Malaysia borrow at 10% a month to renew their permits, an economist of the CPD (Centre for Policy Dialogue, a Dhaka think tank) said in October 2025 [F0267].

### At the airport

#### What should I carry?

Before you go, check you have [F0268]:
- a valid passport;
- the work visa;
- the medical certificate;
- the demand letter;
- the contract attested by the destination country's embassy;
- the agency's name, address and phone;
- the BMET smart card;
- the air ticket.

Keep one set of copies at home and at least three with you [F0269]. Never carry a packet for anyone whose contents you don't know: drug offences abroad can carry the death penalty [F0270]. Airport police at Sylhet said in April 2026 that rings target first-time travellers to carry drugs or smuggled goods, and many don't realise they are joining a crime ring [LB0226]. In two cases Amnesty International documented in Saudi Arabia, the accused said they had been forced to carry the drugs, but the court neither examined nor accepted that [OB0058].

#### What does immigration ask?

Immigration police ask about your purpose and family, and they compare your visa, BMET clearance, papers and answers [F0271, F0004]. A woman with BMET clearance who said she was joining her husband in Oman was questioned closely, because Oman's general worker visas and women's domestic-worker recruitment are closed, and was stopped when the same spouse-visa approval turned out to have sent five women to Oman [F0008]. The Special Branch's 24-hour immigration hotline at Shahjalal airport is 01320007588 [F0001].

#### Why do people get offloaded?

Immigration police can stop a traveller they doubt from flying and stamp "offload" in the passport [F0271]. The usual reasons are a visa that doesn't match the BMET clearance, fake papers, a breach of visa conditions, or answers that don't match the papers [F0004]. The Special Branch turned back 81,500 passengers from 2021 to June 2026, 7,819 of them in the first half of 2026 [F0005, F0006]. People with valid papers have been stopped too: a man with a valid Malaysia visa and BMET clearance was turned back and then let through after a senior officer intervened [F0009]. A Home Ministry additional secretary admitted that some complaints of people stopped unfairly are true [F0010].

#### Who helps me at the airport?

WEWB's welfare desks at Dhaka, Chattogram and Sylhet airports check smart cards, help returnees with customs and lost luggage, send the sick to hospital by ambulance and take complaints from abused or cheated returnees [F0102, F0272, F0273]. The Wage Earners' Centre near Shahjalal airport gives departing and returning workers a short stay; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd [F0274]. A deputy director of the Civil Aviation Authority of Bangladesh said in August 2026 that the Probashi Kallyan desk at the airport is open 24 hours (see At the airport: the welfare desk) [AB0125].

**Lounge and promised services.** The expatriates' welfare adviser said on 24 September 2024 that VIP service for migrants at the airport would start within a month, first for those arriving from the Middle East, with every VIP facility except the lounge and a special immigration desk [CQ0028]. On 5 October 2024 he said an assistant would accompany outgoing workers at every stage at the airport and that a special lounge for migrants was being set up, with the space chosen, and might open within about a month [CQ0029, CQ0030]. The chief adviser of the interim government opened a lounge for migrants at the airport on 11 November 2024 [CQ0109]. The Ministry's August 2025 report said a "Probashi Lounge and Waiting Lounge" had been set up at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, with healthy food at a 30% discount [CQ0221].

**Women coming home.** BRAC's Shariful Hasan said in December 2025 that there is no clear gender-sensitive SOP (standard operating procedure) at the airport for a dignified return, and that immigration or police often do not treat women returning with abuse and trauma humanely [CQ0135].

> **Need to know**
> - Where in Shahjalal airport is the Probashi Lounge, who may use it, and do departing workers get an assistant or a special immigration desk in 2026 [CQ0221, CQ0029, CQ0028]?

### In the destination

#### My salary isn't paid. What do I do?

The routes:
- **Qatar:** complain to the labour ministry, which must first try to settle the dispute; a Qatari official advised complaining within a month, because 3 to 4 months' unpaid wages become hard to recover [F0275, F0276]. An unpaid worker may ask to change employer or end the contract, with the journey home at the employer's cost [F0277].
- **Saudi Arabia:** file a friendly-settlement claim with the labour ministry within 12 months; unsettled claims go to the labour court [F0363, F0364]. After 3 months unpaid you may move to another employer without consent [F0279].
- **Every country:** your family can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-1 branch in Dhaka with proof of your status, the employer's name and address and the amounts owed; it is free and the Ministry writes to the labour wing within 7 working days [F0280].

The steps for each country are under "If you aren't paid" in its doc (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: company worker, If something goes wrong, If you aren't paid, are abused or your passport is taken).

#### My employer has taken my passport. Is that allowed?

No, though it is common:
- **Qatar:** the employer must return it once the residence permit is done, unless you ask in writing for the employer to keep it, and hand it over whenever you ask; keeping it is punishable by a fine of up to QR 25,000 [F0281, F0282].
- **Saudi Arabia:** your passport and iqama (residence card) may never be withheld [F0283].
- **The UAE:** passport confiscation is banned, and you need no employer permission to leave the country [F0284].
- **Kuwait:** keeping it is illegal but widespread; complain to the PAM (Public Authority for Manpower) office where your residence was stamped [F0285, F0286].
- **Singapore:** employers should not keep workers' passports, yet over 31% of Work Permit holders didn't hold their own in 2024 [F0287, F0288].

In Bangladesh, withholding a migrant's passport without lawful reason carries up to 5 years' prison [F0066, F0067].

#### Can I change my employer?

- **Qatar:** since 2020 the law lets you move without the employer's consent, under the labour ministry's rules [F0289].
- **Saudi Arabia:** after 12 months, with 90 days' notice, if your contract is on Qiwa (the labour ministry's platform); sooner if you go 3 months unpaid or your work permit or visa expires [F0290, F0279]. One worker wrote in 2025 that he found out how for himself, moved to another company and had worked there about four years on good pay [AA0166].
- **The UAE:** transfers between companies have been restricted for Bangladeshis since July 2024 [F0291].
- **Kuwait:** normally after one year and with the employer's consent [F0292].
- **Malaysia:** the PLKS (Temporary Employment Visit Pass, the work pass) doesn't let you change employer or sector [F0293].
- **Singapore:** you may work only for the employer and in the job on your Work Permit [F0294].
- **Jordan garment factories:** only to another garment factory [F0295].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** the permit can't be moved to another employer [F0296].
- **Italy:** losing or leaving the job doesn't cancel your permit [F0297].

In Saudi Arabia you may not be employed in a profession other than the one on your work permit [F0298].

#### I arrived and there is no job, or the visa was fake. What now?

This is the most common failure of a bought visa: in OKUP's 2025 survey, 21% of free-visa workers paid about Tk 1.5 lakh more after arriving to get a work permit, and 14% never got one and stayed on illegally [F0299, F0300]. BMET says 90% of migrants' complaints in 2025 concerned Saudi Arabia, mostly no iqama, no work or unpaid wages [F0301]. The embassy can advise and help in a labour dispute (in Qatar, for example) [F0302], and your family can complain about the agency in Bangladesh (see The agency took my money and didn't send me. What can I do?) [F0247]. If you can't get home, a relative can ask the Ministry for help to bring you back [F0126].

#### What happens if I overstay?

- **Saudi Arabia:** without a valid iqama you are undocumented, and police detain and deport undocumented workers who go out [F0127, F0366]; overstaying a visit or Umrah visa brings a 3 to 5 year ban [F0128].
- **The UAE:** AED 50 a day after the grace period that follows the end of a residence permit [F0129].
- **Qatar:** overstaying a visit visa costs QR 200 a day, up to QR 12,000 [F0130].
- **Cambodia:** USD 10 a day, and deportation can take more than six months [F0131].
- **Kyrgyzstan:** a fine, and an expulsion brings a 2-year entry ban [F0132, F0133].

Countries sometimes open grace periods: Qatar gave three months from 9 February 2025 for overstayers to leave [F0134]. How to get home is under "Going home without valid papers" in each country doc.

#### How do I send money home safely?

Use a bank, a licensed exchange house or a mobile wallet such as bKash, not hundi (unlicensed money agents) [F0135]. Money sent through legal channels earns the receiver a 2.5% government cash incentive, sometimes with another 2.5% from the bank, and the 2026-27 budget keeps it [F0136, F0137]. In a rejoinder of 24 June 2025 the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare said the government was continuing a 2% cash incentive for money sent through banking channels and had stepped up surveillance of hundi [CQ0203]. Sending more at once costs less: in April to June 2026 the average cost was 2.94% for up to USD 200 and 0.50% above USD 500 [F0138]. For bKash, give the partner branch the receiver's bKash number and full registered name [F0139]. Banks in Bangladesh credit remittances within 3 days at most [F0140]. The channels for each country are in its doc, under Sending money home.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the government's remittance cash incentive 2.5% or 2% in 2026 [F0136, CQ0203]?

#### Is hundi illegal?

Yes, in Bangladesh sending money outside banking channels is a punishable offence [F0141]. Abroad it can cost you the job: in Singapore a worker involved in hundi has his permit revoked [F0142].

#### Who can I call for help while abroad?

- Probash-Bondhu, WEWB's 24-hour call centre: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [F0143].
- The Ministry's Complaint Resolution Cell: minister.expat@gmail.com [F0144].
- The Bangladesh embassy's labour wing in your country (for example, in Saudi Arabia, 8001000125 free) [F0145].

The labour wing's numbers are under Contacts in each country doc. Legal aid through the missions reaches few people: 395 migrant workers, 11 of them women, received legal aid through Bangladesh missions abroad in the 2024-25 financial year, according to Wage Earners' Welfare Board data cited in a Prothom Alo column of 17 October 2026, and in September 2026 parliament was told legal-aid agreements had been made with 22 law firms in 13 countries [CQ0199, CQ0200].

> **Need to know**
> - Are the Welfare Board's 22 law firms in 13 countries or 12 (see Where victims and families get help), and how does a worker ask for one [CQ0200, OA0200]?

### Women

#### How many women work abroad?

More than 10 lakh Bangladeshi women were working abroad in May 2026, by BMET data, and more than 10 lakh 95 thousand went abroad between 2001 and 2022, though the number of women migrating has fallen in the last few years; the programme director of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) puts women at about 13.5% of all migration [OA0211, OA0212, OA0213]. By BMET data, 1,05,466 Bangladeshi women went abroad for work in 2022 and 62,317 in 2025, a fall of about 41%, while in the first eight months of 2026 more than 42,000 women went to 48 countries, about 14% (5,214) more than a year before, over 90% of them to Saudi Arabia and Jordan [CQ0231, CQ0232, CQ0233]. The expatriates' welfare minister told parliament on 5 April 2026 that 62,352 of the 11,32,519 workers who went abroad in 2025 were women [CQ0215, CQ0216]. About 799 to 800 women migrants have come home dead in the past eight years, by Wage Earners' Welfare Board data cited in May 2026 by Jasiya Khatun, director of WARBE Development Foundation (a migrant workers' rights organisation); she said the cause is mostly given as heart attack or illness, sometimes suicide, and the real cause is not known [OA0210]. A 2023 study by RMMRU found that 31% of migrant women workers' deaths abroad were unnatural, from accidents and suicide [CQ0104].

> **Need to know**
> - How many women went abroad for work in 2025: 62,317 (BMET data reported in September 2026) or 62,352 (the minister in parliament) [CQ0231, CQ0215]?

#### Can a woman go abroad for work without her guardian's permission?

For domestic work, no: BMET clears a woman domestic worker only with proof that she is 25 to 45 and a no-objection letter from her legal guardian [F0190]. A woman going on a single visa also needs her guardian's no-objection letter on stamp paper [F0146]. The housekeeping course asks for the same letter [F0303].

#### Who can go as a domestic worker?

A woman aged 25 to 45 when she enrols in the housekeeping course (returnees up to 50), who has passed class 5 (which can be relaxed) and is fit [F0304, F0305]. She can't enrol if she is pregnant, if her youngest child is under 6, or if she has a contagious or incurable disease [F0306].

#### What is the housekeeping course?

It is compulsory for women going to the Middle East for domestic work: one month (24 working days), residential, at one of 41 TTCs [F0094, F0307, F0308]. Apply on OEP's training portal and pay the set fee [F0309]. You must attend 90% of sessions and score 70% in the final test, and the course teaches how to contact the police and the Bangladesh embassy abroad [F0308, F0310]. The certificate is valid two years for a first departure [F0311]. Brokers have sold course certificates for money [F0312].

**In practice:** people in the migration sector said in January 2024 that the course is skipped in some cases before women are sent to Saudi Arabia, and its quality is questioned [AA0041]. Many women back from Saudi Arabia say the month is not enough; some cannot read or write, many can only give a thumbprint instead of a signature, and they do not know the language of the country they go to, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of January 2024 [AA0047, AA0048]. In April 2026 the principal of the Cumilla TTC said attendance at women workers' training is now monitored online, and that the rule that agencies take at least 10% trained workers must be enforced [AA0247, AA0248]. The project director of RAISE, a government returnee project, said in August 2026 that domestic work abroad means working in 50-degree heat, cooking local food and using modern appliances, which need training [AB0113].

#### Does a woman going as a domestic worker pay anything?

No. An agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from her [F0191], and Saudi recruitment offices may take no commission from any domestic worker [F0313]. The Saudi household pays its recruitment office up to SR 11,750 for a Bangladeshi worker [F0314]. Yet none of the returnee women IOM interviewed in 2020 had gone free [F0315].

#### Which agencies may send women domestic workers?

Only agencies the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare has approved, each with a Tk 15 lakh security deposit and a woman on the staff; the September 2025 order approved 21 [F0316]. Ask for the approval letter and check the agency's name and RL number are in it (see the Saudi doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Step 2) [F0316].

> **Need to know**
> - Does the Ministry publish one current list of all agencies approved to send women domestic workers?

#### The agent wants to change my age on the passport. Is that a problem?

Yes: agents have raised girls' ages with a second birth certificate, in one case giving a girl under 13 a passport showing 27 [F0317, F0318]. Under a February 2025 circular the national ID data is the basis of the passport, and the person whose passport it is bears the responsibility for any mismatch, the assistant director of the Cox's Bazar regional passport office said in June 2026 [MB0038, MB0039]. Check the birth record your passport will use at everify.bdris.gov.bd with the 17-digit registration number and your real date of birth [F0319]. The Probashi call centre, 16135 (free from Bangladesh), gives workers and families advice and help [F0320].

#### What are a domestic worker's rights?

Saudi Arabia's rules are an example:
- at most 10 hours' work a day, with at least 8 hours' continuous rest [F0321];
- one paid day off a week [F0147];
- the employer may not keep your passport, papers or belongings [F0148];
- the employer must let you contact your family and the embassy [F0149].

The rules for other countries are in each country doc's domestic-worker section (for example, see the Qatar doc, Work visa: domestic worker, Your rights at work). In practice most women workers work 12 to 14 hours instead of 8, according to the director of BNSK (Bangladesh Nari Sramik Kendra, a women workers' organisation) in May 2026 [OA0217].

#### What if I'm abused? Where can I go?

In Saudi Arabia, the embassy labour wing's free line is 8001000125, and the labour wings run safe homes for women in Riyadh, Jeddah and Madinah [F0145, F0150]. The labour ministry's abuse line, 1919, takes reports round the clock in confidence [F0151]. A relative in Bangladesh can apply to the Ministry's Welfare-2 branch (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd) for help or to bring you home; it is free and the Ministry acts within 7 working days [F0152, F0153]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash at the airport welfare desk [F0102]. A study by OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation) found that 94% of women workers forced to return had suffered regular physical and mental abuse abroad and 47% sexual harassment, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of 19 October 2026 [CQ0201].

**How the way home works in Saudi Arabia:** officials said in January 2024 that most women who leave a household before the two-year contract ends run away to the embassy's safe home, are moved from there to the Saudi government's safe home, and are flown home at the Saudi government's cost; a woman who wants to go back within three months of arriving is sent home through the Saudi company that recruited her [AA0035, AA0036].

**A case:** Lutfa Begum of Brahmanbaria went to Saudi Arabia as a domestic worker in March 2020 and worked 1 month 5 days, in two neighbouring houses where she said the men behaved badly; the person who sent her did not find her other work, and when she asked to go home she was sent to a shelter, where she stayed about three months [AA0037, AA0038, AA0039]. She said she was paid nothing, and after coming home received Tk 30,000 in cash help in two instalments [AA0040].

**Counselling:** the Cumilla manpower office's assistant director said in April 2026 that there is almost no psychological counselling for migrant workers, especially women [AA0252].

#### Can women get garment jobs in Jordan?

Yes, through BOESL: its November 2026 notice took women aged 20 to 35 who can run plain and overlock sewing machines, at no cost to the worker [F0322, F0206]. In June 2026 the CID arrested three people who used a Jordan garment company's name and seal to take Tk 2 to 2.5 lakh a person without a licence [F0323]. Steps: see the Jordan doc, Work visa: garment and factory worker.

### Problems and coming home

#### What does the migrant insurance cover?

Workers who left with BMET clearance from 1 January 2023 are insured for Tk 10 lakh by Jiban Bima Corporation (JBC), for workers aged 18 to 55, for a one-time Tk 1,000 [F0324]. The cover lasts 5 years from the smart card's date [F0220]. It pays the full Tk 10 lakh on death or total permanent disability, 10% to 90% for partial disability, and Tk 50,000 if you lose the job and return within 6 months of going [F0325]. Job loss is not covered if you went entirely at the employer's cost [F0326]. WEWB helps the worker or family claim [F0327].

**Death after the cover ends, and how the money is paid.** Under the insurance contract of 12 March 2023, a worker who dies naturally or in an accident during the cover period, or within 90 days after it ends, is paid the full Tk 10 lakh, and when a worker dies abroad the money goes to the worker's bank account after the body comes home, without any application, according to the Welfare Board and JBC in January 2024 [CQ0097, CQ0098].

**What it does not pay for.** Under the same contract, as Prothom Alo reported in January 2024, there is no insurance benefit for [CQ0094, CQ0095, CQ0096]:
- suicide or self-harm within six months of cover starting;
- death or illness from HIV/AIDS or a similar disease;
- death in dangerous sports or adventure activities;
- death from alcohol or drug addiction;
- death in war, a riot or an attack by criminals;
- execution under a court sentence.

**In practice.** By JBC's count, 32,24,343 workers went abroad from the start of the compulsory migrant insurance in late 2019 to the end of 2023, and only 1,397 workers or families claimed on it; in FY2022-23 the Welfare Board paid Tk 31 crore 72 lakh 10 thousand in death insurance to the families of 860 workers [CQ0099, CQ0100]. Disability and early-return benefits must be claimed, the government agencies hold no information on workers who come back disabled or within six months, and people involved said there is no special effort to tell workers about the insurance before they leave, Prothom Alo reported in January 2024 [CQ0101, CQ0102, CQ0103].

> **Need to know**
> - Where and with which papers does a worker who came home disabled or within six months claim the insurance, and how long does JBC take to pay [CQ0101]?

#### If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?

1. **At the airport:** a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial for every body that arrives, free, collected within 3 months of the body's arrival [F0328].
2. **From the Welfare Board:** a Tk 3 lakh grant, if the worker had BMET clearance or membership and died in the country the clearance was for [F0104, F0329]. If the family took the airport cheque, no separate application is needed; if the body was buried abroad, apply within 1 year [F0330].
3. **From the insurance:** Tk 10 lakh [F0324].
4. **From the employer:** labour wings recover death compensation, wages and benefits, and the Board pays the heirs; 1,852 cases brought in Tk 127.6 crore in 2023-24 [F0331].

The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents [F0332]. The Cumilla Probashi Kallyan Centre's assistant director said in April 2026 that financial help for bringing a body home is given for BMET card holders, that a free ambulance takes the body from the airport, and that Tk 25,000 is paid after the body arrives and up to Tk 3 lakh in eligible cases [AA0233]. The president of a Cumilla advocacy forum called it a major humanitarian problem that the family is not told when someone dies or goes missing abroad [AA0253]. By Welfare Board data, in the first six months of 2026 it paid Tk 9.45 crore for burial, Tk 61.56 crore in grants and more than Tk 44 crore in insurance, and recovered about Tk 75 crore in compensation, unpaid wages and insurance from abroad; people in the sector said families must visit the Board day after day after applying [CQ0238, CQ0239]. The full steps are in the Qatar doc, If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job.

> **Need to know**
> - Is the airport payment for transport and burial Tk 35,000 or Tk 25,000 in 2026 [F0328, AA0233]?

#### How is the body brought home?

The family applies to the Welfare Board, which takes their wish (body home or burial abroad) to the embassy and follows the case, free [F0333, F0334]. The Ministry also brings home bodies of irregular, undocumented and destitute workers: the next of kin applies with the cause of death, proof of status and the death certificate; free, 7 working days [F0335]. A free ambulance from Dhaka and Chattogram airports is available on request [F0336]. The Minister said in August 2026 that the government brings home the body of any Bangladeshi who dies abroad, whether the person went legally or not, and gives the family financial help and help with the children's education [AB0102]. Bodies can take months: eight Bangladeshis who died on a boat off Tunisia in February 2024 reached Dhaka on 2 May 2024, and went to the Dhaka Medical College morgue for post-mortem before being handed to their families [LB0025, LB0026]. Welfare Board officials said in October 2026 that bodies from Middle Eastern countries need approval from several offices before they can be sent, which takes some time though less than before, and that outside pandemic years under 1% of migrants who die are buried in the destination country [CQ0236, CQ0237].

**The numbers.** From January to 30 September 2026, 4,060 bodies of migrant workers reached Bangladesh, more than 14 a day, Prothom Alo reported, while Welfare Board data show 3,651 bodies came home in 2019 and 3,140 in 2020 [CQ0234, CQ0235]. In a Welfare Board analysis of 2018 to 2022, 18,166 bodies came home, of which 3,698 died in road accidents, workplace accidents, fires, falls from buildings or electric shock, 2,649 of them in road accidents [CQ0240].

#### Why do so many families get no compensation from the employer?

Many deaths are recorded as "natural causes": 71% of deaths of Bangladeshis in Qatar from November 2016 to October 2020, by Amnesty International's reading of Bangladeshi government records [F0337]. Amnesty found in 2021 that without investigation or post-mortem, this rules out compensation from employers [F0338]. The Welfare Board's grant is paid anyway [F0104].

#### What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?

The Welfare Board asks the embassy to bring a sick or injured worker home on the worker's or family's application [F0339]. Back home, workers with clearance or membership can get up to Tk 2 lakh for treatment, applying within 1 year of return [F0340, F0341]. The insurance pays for permanent disability (see What does the migrant insurance cover?) [F0342].

#### What happens if I'm arrested abroad?

The cases below are from Saudi Arabia, where Bangladeshis are the largest group of foreign workers, at least 25 lakh [OB0064, OB0065, OB0066].

- **Executions:** according to ESOHR (the European Saudi Organisation for Human Rights, based in Berlin), 59 of the 108 people Saudi Arabia executed from January to June 2026 were foreigners, including 49 of the 71 executed for drug offences, and Bangladeshis were among the 108, though how many is not known [OB0059, OB0060, OB0061]. Two Bangladeshis convicted of an "immoral act" in a mosque in Mecca were executed shortly before October 2026 [OB0063]. According to Amnesty International, 597 people were executed in Saudi Arabia for drug offences alone from January 2014 to June 2025, about 75% of them foreigners, in a country whose population of about 3 crore 53 lakh is about 44% foreign [OB0062].
- **Your rights on paper:** Saudi Arabia's 2013 Law of Criminal Procedure gives an accused person the right to legal help during both investigation and trial, and bans torture [OB0067, OB0068, OB0069].
- **In practice:** ESOHR documented that death-row prisoners in Khamis Mushait, Najran and Tabuk prisons had no professional interpreter or legal help, did not understand the proceedings, and often did not know when their sentence was upheld or their plea for pardon rejected [OB0067, OB0068, OB0069]. Amnesty, documenting 25 foreigners convicted of drug offences (from Egypt, Ethiopia, Jordan, Pakistan and Somalia), found many went through the system alone, without understanding or receiving the court papers or charges, without consular help, and at risk of confessions taken without an adequate interpreter or lawyer [OB0070, OB0071]. It found that low education, poor language skills and lack of money harm these migrants twice: brokers cheat or exploit them from the start of going to Saudi Arabia, and if they face legal trouble they cannot hire a good lawyer or defend themselves properly, and in most cases have less access to consular help [OB0077, OB0078, OB0079]. In December 2024 the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination found racial and ethnic discrimination in Saudi Arabia's criminal justice system, with migrant and domestic workers, including women, subjected to arbitrary detention, torture and ill-treatment, and called on Saudi Arabia to impose a moratorium on the death penalty [OB0082, OB0083].
- **What the family is told:** Amnesty says Saudi law has no clear rule on warning the prisoner or family before an execution; families said the prisoner may at most phone 24 hours before, some prisoners were told by prison officers the day before "your turn has come", and many families learned of an execution from other prisoners' calls or from the state news agency [OB0072, OB0073, OB0074].
- **The body:** Saudi law does not guarantee the body is returned after an execution; in all three of Amnesty's 25 cases executed between January and June 2025, Saudi Arabia neither returned the body nor told the family where it was buried [OB0075, OB0076].

ESOHR could learn or record nothing before the execution about 91 of the 108 people executed in early 2026, so there is no answer on how many Bangladeshis are held, on what charges, or whether they had lawyers, interpreters or consular help [OB0064, OB0065, OB0066].

> **Need to know**
> - When a Bangladeshi is arrested in Saudi Arabia, does the embassy's labour wing learn of it, and does it arrange a lawyer or interpreter? Whom should the family contact first?
> - How many Bangladeshis are on death row in Saudi Arabia, and for which charges?

#### What help is there when I come back?

Airport welfare desks give food and water to returnees from jail, help with customs and lost luggage, and take complaints [F0272, F0273]. Women back within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 in cash [F0102]. PKB's rehabilitation loan lends returnees up to Tk 50 lakh for a project at 9% simple interest, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral [F0343]. It needs a trade licence and a guarantor, and in 2021 only about 2% of pandemic returnees got PKB's special reintegration loan [F0344, F0345]. PKB's managing director, Md. Mujibur Rahman, said in March 2024 that loan conditions for returnee women had been eased: a woman whose husband or family won't take her back can borrow without a fixed address, her loan carries no service charge, and loans up to Tk 1 lakh need no equity, though few women apply [CQ0105, CQ0106]. Prothom Alo reported in March 2025 that few workers benefit from the projects begun for returnees after the pandemic [AA0142]. Hasan Imam of the Center for Development Communication said in December 2025 that the government's reintegration policy, begun in 2022, received final approval in 2025, with six objectives: employment and financial self-reliance, entrepreneurship, social reintegration and health care, psychosocial support, support for exploited and abused women, and sustainability [CQ0133, CQ0134]. Other lenders, the RAISE reintegration project and what returnee leaders say: see Help after you get home [AB0110, AB0136, OA0221].

**Returnees with HIV.** According to the national AIDS/STD control programme of the Directorate General of Health Services, 1,891 people were found infected with HIV in Bangladesh from December 2024 to November 2025, 12% of them people who had come back from abroad [CQ0208, CQ0209]. The government supplies antiretroviral (ARV) drugs free, but counselling, mental health support and livelihood rehabilitation for returnees are very limited, Prothom Alo reported in February 2026; Mir of the youth organisation Brave Dimension Global said one deported worker got counselling not from any government agency but from a private organisation [CQ0204, CQ0205].

> **Need to know**
> - Where does a returnee with HIV get the free ARV drugs and counselling: which hospital or centre, and with which papers [CQ0204]?

#### What can I bring home without paying duty?

Under the Baggage Rules 2025 a returning worker aged 12 or over may bring up to 65 kg free, plus 35 kg more of clothes and personal items [F0346, F0347]. One of each of a list of goods is duty-free, including a laptop, an oven, a rice cooker, a fan and a TV up to 29 inches [F0348]. You may bring 2 used mobile phones and 1 new one, the new one once a year [F0349]. Bigger items such as a fridge or air conditioner pay a fixed duty [F0350]. A Bangladeshi passport holder may bring no alcohol [F0351]. The full list is in the Qatar doc, Coming home, What you can bring home.

#### How much gold and cash can I bring?

Up to 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery in all, declared, once a year, with no more than 12 pieces of one type, is duty-free [F0352]. One gold bar of up to 117 g (10 tola) is allowed once a year if declared, at Tk 5,000 duty per 11.664 g; anything more, or a hidden bar, is confiscated [F0353, F0354]. Up to USD 10,000 in foreign currency needs no declaration [F0154].

> **Need to know**
> - Do customs officers still apply USD 5,000, the figure on Bangladesh Customs' own page [F0155]?

#### Is there help for my children?

The Welfare Board pays scholarships to migrants' high-achieving children (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC level) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [F0156].

### Recruited while abroad or just back

- **In the Gulf.** Abdullah Al Zobayer, who came back from captivity in Libya in 2024, said the ring that took him has many members and also targets Bangladeshi expatriates in the Middle East, taking them to Libya with talk of good jobs or passage to Europe and holding them hostage to collect money from their families [LB0042]. Afaz Uddin Molla, a worker in Kuwait, said he answered a Facebook advert in August 2024 offering a cheap route to Canada and was promised he would be taken there from Kuwait; according to the case statement, the man calling himself Asadul took BDT 24 lakh from him by December 2024 [FV0088, FV0089, FV0090]. According to his family and local sources, Elias Howlader of Kalkini, Madaripur, went to Qatar about three years before March 2026, decided from there to go to Italy illegally after being lured by a Bangladeshi broker, and reached Libya from Qatar in early August 2025; his father said the family learned of it only after he had gone [PA0197, PA0198, PA0199]. According to the family, brokers took BDT 20 lakh from them in instalments while he was held and tortured in a Libyan detention camp, where he died on 23 March 2026 [PA0200, PA0201, PA0202]. In a case over a promised job in Australia reported in June 2026, the victim was a Bangladeshi working in Oman whom a person he already knew approached in December 2025 with an offer of work in Australia, according to CID (the police's Criminal Investigation Department) citing the case [FV0124].
- **In Libya.** One of a group of 113 returnees told the ambassador he had lived in Libya for 14 years before deciding, in old age, to try the "game", the boat crossing to Italy (see Libya to Italy by boat) [LB0202].
- **In Malaysia.** Shariful Islam Hasan, associate director of BRAC's Migration Programme and Youth Platform, told a roundtable at the Prothom Alo office in Dhaka on 22 July 2026 that many people in Malaysia are being cheated with assurances that they will be sent to Australia [AB0077]. Kuddus Bepari (33) of Majumdarkandi, Rajoir, Madaripur, who had worked in Malaysia for 7 years and came home in August 2024 to see his newborn child, was frustrated because the Malaysian labour market was not going well, and the local broker Monir Sheikh, learning of it, lured him into a BDT 16 lakh "body contract" to reach Italy through Libya, according to police, family and local sources (see Deaths) [CQ0075, CQ0076, CQ0077].
- **On the flight home.** According to CID sources, a man from Senbag, Noakhali, who had worked in Saudi Arabia for about three years met a fellow passenger on the flight home, and through him was introduced to a member of a trafficking ring (see A flight to a different country first) [PA0203, PA0204].
- **Back home.** Mannan Soyal, who returned from a scam centre in Cambodia, told the July 2026 roundtable that he went to Saudi Arabia in 2022, stayed three years and decided to go abroad again after coming home, and that a relative from his area then sent him to Cambodia for BDT 5 lakh with talk of a restaurant job (see Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos: scam compounds) [AB0035].
- **In Russia, after the job ends.** Ali Hossain of Mymensingh said in November 2025 that his son Afzal Hossain Meraj, who went to Russia on 10 August 2024 through the travel agency Friendship and Co-operative Recruitment, worked four months at the company he was recruited for, was laid off, and then went to fight through a ring; he was wounded in a mine blast and spent 25 days in hospital [RU0026, RU0027]. An unnamed young man told Prothom Alo that the firm he worked for in Russia put 15 to 20 workers out after two to three months, and that when four or five of them went to the airport in late February 2025 to fly home, a Russian broker took them to a hotel with talk of work [PA0205, PA0206]. He said the broker took their passports and all their papers "for visa renewal", three or four Russians had them sign a contract, and they were then taken to a forest to cut trees, where guns and ammunition arrived some days later [PA0208, PA0209]. Another man, from Trishal, Mymensingh, met a Russian broker while jobless in Moscow for about a month and a half after losing his job [PA0207]. According to his family, after the construction firm employing Shahriar Sumon of Trishal, Mymensingh, stopped work six months in, a broker in Moscow first took him away with a promise of vineyard work and then, promising to send him to Italy, took BDT 5 lakh from him and a Bangladeshi companion; they were made to sign papers in Russian whose meaning they did not understand, and later learned they had been enrolled in the Russian army [PA0210, PA0211, PA0212, PA0213, PA0214]. According to the wife of Kamrul Hasan of Brahmanbaria, after he went to Moscow looking for work and was cheated by one broker, another took BDT 5 lakh from him with a promise to send him to Italy, and he was then handed over to the Russian army (see Russia: recruited into the war) [PA0215, PA0216].
- **In Russian custody.** The UN's Independent International Commission of Inquiry on Ukraine says foreigners already in Russia were also taken into the armed forces by deception or coercion: some were in police custody or in temporary detention centres for foreign nationals and some were serving prison sentences, and several were told that signing a military contract would spare them prosecution, a long sentence, solitary confinement or torture, with threats of torture or a longer sentence if they did not sign [RU0171, RU0172].

### Where to complain and get help

#### Complain to BMET

1. **Online:** since 30 July 2026, the worker or a family member can file on OEP's Online Complaint Management Module (OEP is the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system), upload evidence, follow progress and get a notification at each stage [S0045, MB0068, MB0069]. The module, built by BMET and RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), is to bring every District Employment and Manpower Office (DEMO) and BMET's central complaint handling into one system [MB0068, MB0070].
2. **On paper:** BMET's complaint form (Form Ka) asks for your name, NID (national ID card), passport and BMET smart card numbers, addresses and the agency's or broker's details; hand it in at BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, or your DEMO [S0044, S0063].
3. **What happens:** free; an inquiry committee holds a hearing, BMET recommends and the ministry decides, within 30 working days [S0043].
4. **If nothing happens:** BMET's complaint officer, the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 (3 months); then the ministry Secretary, secretary@probashi.gov.bd (1 month); then grs.gov.bd (3 months) [S0046].

Under section 41 of the OEM Act the inquiry must finish within 30 working days, and a proven complaint may be settled by order or arbitration within 3 months of the inquiry's end [S0167, S0168, VS0041]. If an agency refuses to pay an arbitration award, BMET may deduct it from the agency's security deposit, according to a 2026 study by RMMRU [VS0040].

**In practice:** many complaints stay unresolved for years [S0056]. BMET's Director General said in July 2026 that in 2025 BMET settled 500 complaints by the manual process and obtained Tk 10 crore in compensation for migrants [MB0067]. In Cox's Bazar, the DEMO's assistant director said in June 2026 that about 30 complaints lodged with BMET and his office had been settled and reports sent on them; he gave no period [MB0032].

#### Complain to the Ministry

- **Enforcement-2 branch** of the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare: application with proof, your full address and the agency's RL (recruiting licence) number and address; free; action in 3-5 working days, settlement in 30; 02-41030255, sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd [S0047].
- **Mobile courts** under the OEM Act give immediate remedies; free; 7 working days [S0139].
- **Hotline:** 16135 (toll free), +880 9610102030 from abroad [S0048].
- **Coming:** the expatriates' welfare minister said on 26 October 2026 that an open email service to settle any complaint is being launched and publicised, and that work is under way on technology at BMET to check visa validity quickly [CQ0202].

The Ministry's secretary said in July 2026 that most complaints it gets from migrants turn out to be true, and that the most common are money taken on a promise of sending someone abroad, a migrant's property at home being seized, and a person going missing abroad [MB0071, MB0072]. The state minister said in May 2026 that complaints of irregularities and excess cost do come, that there are syndicate, test and recruitment problems in Malaysia, Korea, Saudi Arabia and other countries, that insecurity for women migrants is more frequent in some Middle Eastern countries, that the scope for legal remedy used to be limited, and that agreements with some countries, which he did not name, have now created scope to file cases and recover compensation [OA0190, OA0191, OA0192].

> **Need to know**
> - Which countries' agreements now let a worker file a case and recover compensation there, and how does a worker start one?
> - What is the address of the Ministry's open complaint email announced in October 2026, and is it live [CQ0202]?

#### File a police case

- **Section 31 of the OEM Act:** taking or trying to take money on a false promise of a job abroad, holding a migrant's passport or papers without lawful reason, or luring someone with false promises of higher pay: up to 5 years' prison and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh [S0159, S0160]. It applies to any person, not only agencies [S0052].
- **Section 33:** obtaining a demand letter, visa or work permit by illegal means and buying or selling it in Bangladesh: 2 to 7 years' rigorous imprisonment and at least Tk 3 lakh [S0054, VS0042].
- **Section 35:** an unregistered sub-agent: 1 year or a fine of Tk 50,000 to 2 lakh [S0170].
- **Section 34:** sending someone out other than through an official exit point: up to 10 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [S0164].
- **How it is handled:** sections 31, 33 and 34 are cognisable (police can register a case and arrest without a warrant), non-bailable and can't be settled privately; sections 32, 33A, 35 and 35A can be tried by a mobile court [S0165, S0166]. Cases are tried by a first-class judicial or metropolitan magistrate [S0169].
- **Emergency:** 999 [S0087].

#### File a trafficking case

- **The law:** the Prevention and Suppression of Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Act 2026 replaced the 2012 trafficking act [VS0014, VS0001].
- **What counts:** recruiting, moving or holding someone by deceit or by exploiting their vulnerability, for sexual, labour or other exploitation inside or outside Bangladesh, is trafficking (section 5) [VS0002]. So is luring or helping someone to go abroad for work knowing they may be exploited [VS0003]. Exploitation includes a fraudulent marriage, forcing someone into online scamming, and holding someone for ransom [VS0004, VS0005, VS0006].
- **Penalty:** at least 5 years' rigorous imprisonment up to life and at least Tk 50,000; for an organised group, death, life or at least 7 years and at least Tk 5 lakh [VS0007, VS0008]. Migrant smuggling, getting someone illegally into a country where they are not a citizen or resident, carries 3 to 10 years and at least Tk 1 lakh [VS0044].
- **Where:** a complaint can go to a police station or directly to the Tribunal, and police must protect the complainant and keep their identity secret (section 28) [VS0009]. Until Human Trafficking and Migrant Smuggling Suppression Tribunals are set up, the government can appoint the district's Women and Children Repression Prevention Tribunal judge, or the district and sessions judge, to hear these cases [VS0010].
- **Who files:** cheated workers returning from various countries file trafficking cases, and in some cases so do the families of migrants killed abroad, Prothom Alo reported in July 2026 [RU0059].
- **Compensation:** on conviction the Tribunal can order the trafficker to pay the victim (section 46) [VS0011].
- **In practice:** in the roughly 4,400 to 4,500 trafficking cases of 2020 to 2024, 94 to 95% of the accused were acquitted, according to Home Ministry data, and in January to May 2026 about 500 new cases were filed while 17 were disposed of (see What happens to trafficking cases) [RU0051, LB0187, LB0188, RU0050].

#### Sue for compensation

Anyone harmed by a breach of the OEM Act or the employment contract can file a civil suit for compensation alongside a criminal case (OEM Act section 28) [S0161]. A trafficking or smuggling victim has the same right under section 56 of the 2026 trafficking act [VS0012]. In practice workers often can't keep a case going for lack of money and legal aid [S0109].

#### Local committees and mediation

- **Mediation:** Jesia Khatun of WARBE Development Foundation, a migrants' rights organisation, said in April 2026 that a significant amount of money had been recovered through mediation in the past three years; she gave no amount [AA0229].
- **Grievance redress committees:** Iqbal Mahmud of The Asia Foundation said at the same time that committees formed through municipalities, upazila (sub-district) and union parishads (the lowest tier of local government) are working at district and local level, but on a limited scale [AA0230].
- **Calls to activate committees:** at an April 2026 roundtable in Cumilla, the principal of the Cumilla Technical Training Centre recommended activating union-level complaint settlement committees, making sub-agent registration compulsory, expanding airport help desks, opening one-stop service centres and making payment through banks compulsory, and WARBE's Cumilla officer called for migration information services and a "migration corner" in each union [OA0185, OA0186, OA0187]. At the same roundtable a union parishad member from Amratoli, Cumilla Sadar, named getting no help with complaints among the main problems, alongside torture, excessive workload and withheld wages, and WARBE's officer listed dalal fraud, excess cost, unpaid wages and missing papers abroad, going without skills and little rehabilitation on return [OA0188, OA0189]. In Cox's Bazar in June 2026 the regional head of the NGO YPSA said counter-trafficking committees exist at district, upazila and union level and need to be made active [MB0036].
- **District committees:** the Cox's Bazar DEMO's assistant director said in June 2026 that a District Migration Coordination Committee and a District Vigilance Task Force Committee are active against unsafe migration there, that upazilas have been told to form the same committees, and that a pilot programme helps people in Chakaria decide before going abroad [MB0033, MB0034, MB0035].

> **Need to know**

#### Free legal help

- **The right:** migrants and people cheated in the name of migration have a right to reasonable legal aid (OEM Act section 27) [S0134].
- **Government legal aid helpline:** 16699 [S0051].
- **OKUP:** free, confidential legal advice and help filing a case, 01842773300 [S0049].
- **BNWLA** (the Bangladesh National Women Lawyers' Association): an advocate said in June 2026 that it was conducting the cases of about 100 returnees from abroad in the Cox's Bazar court, and that about 465 human trafficking cases were pending [MB0043].

> **Need to know**

> - What number does BNWLA give returnees who need a lawyer, and does it take cases outside Cox's Bazar?

### Libya to Italy by boat

#### How it is sold

The offer is Italy, and the price quoted is usually USD 10,000 (about Tk 12.4 lakh): USD 3,000 before leaving and USD 7,000 after reaching Libya [I0007, I0067]. An "all-inclusive" package is about USD 14,000 (about Tk 17.4 lakh) [I0009, I0067]. In 2026 the Italian Embassy's migration expert said young Bangladeshis pay traffickers Tk 20 to 30 lakh, and families from Cumilla and Chuadanga paid brokers Tk 18 to 34 lakh [I0010, I0023, I0022, I0347]. A common pitch is half the money before departure and the rest "after reaching Italy" [I0344]. Others are told they will travel legally, with temporary work in Libya while they wait for the "next visa"; their passports are taken on arrival [I0017]. Some are told they are going to Kuwait or Malaysia, and find out Libya was the destination only when their passports are taken in Dubai or Cairo [I0292]. A man left unpaid in Dubai was offered Libya by a facilitator there [I0283]. In 2025 two men promised jobs in Greece for Tk 15 lakh each were flown to Libya instead [I0362].

A Prothom Alo editorial of 21 February 2026 said local dalals win trust through social ties and point to relatives' success abroad, that the journey begins with a promise of a valid visa and safe travel and the route is changed midway, and that in many cases people are held in Libya or another country and videos of their torture are sent to the family to extract ransom [AA0214, AA0215, AA0216]. Dr Selim Reza, coordinator of the Centre for Migration Studies at North South University, wrote in Prothom Alo on 9 September 2026 that brokers' networks show flashy YouTube videos of an easy way to Europe and use Facebook, imo (a messaging app) and WhatsApp groups to make the offer, keep contact and pass on travel information, and that a Prothom Alo investigation about four years earlier had found Facebook groups used for sending people to Europe across the Mediterranean [MB0085, MB0086].

**How people were drawn in:**

- **Work in Libya first.** Khurshed Mia (35), a vegetable seller in Bhairab, Kishoreganj, said he first went to Libya on 14 June 2023 for BDT 4 lakh on the understanding that the broker would find him work in Libya until he went on to Italy, and that he got work there as a painter [LB0171, LB0172].
- **A legal visa that failed.** The brother of Abul Bashar Akon (32) of Rajoir, Madaripur, who died with his nephew Titu Howlader (19) in the January 2025 sinking, said a BDT 14 lakh contract had been made a year and a half earlier with a broker from Mahendradi to send the two to Italy on an agricultural visa, and that when that broker could not deliver they were forced to decide on the Libya route and made a new contract through another broker [LB0120, LB0121]. He said the broker had told the family the two would reach Italy without any problem, that the family first heard of the nephew's death from the broker, and that the broker could no longer be found; the mother said both had told her by phone that they were fine and the broker would give them a "game" (the boat crossing) very soon [LB0122, LB0123, LB0124].
- **Told by phone after leaving.** Fatema Akter said her husband Shahin Sikdar (26) left home with the broker on 3 February 2024, phoned some days later to say they would be sent from Libya to Italy illegally by sea, and last spoke to the family on the night of 22 March 2024; she said the broker took BDT 18 lakh from them at various times and that she could not stay in her area because of debt pressure [LB0152, LB0153, LB0154].
- **A neighbour in Libya.** Azmir Hossain (24) of Mirsarai, Chattogram, said Libya-based Shahadat Hossain kept tempting his mother with a job in Italy paying BDT 50,000 a month, took BDT 1.5 lakh in advance, and had Azmir carry the rest of the money himself; a local union parishad (the lowest tier of local government) member said Shahadat is from the same area [LB0027, LB0028, LB0029].
- **Friends already there.** Hridoy Chowdhury, who returned from Libya, told a roundtable at the Prothom Alo office in Dhaka on 18 August 2026 that friends who had gone to Libya told him there would be work there and that later one could go on to Greece or Italy, and that he went with the help of a broker from his own area, Habiganj in Sylhet, whom he trusted because he knew him; the broker is still in Libya [AB0095, AB0096].

#### The contract price and the final price

**Prices quoted:**

- The migration analyst Asif Munir told Deutsche Welle (DW, Germany's public international broadcaster) that each person needs BDT 16 to 20 lakh for this journey and that international as well as local rings are involved, as quoted in a Prothom Alo editorial of 6 February 2025 [LB0140].
- In February 2025, according to police and local sources in Madaripur, the ringleaders of the broker ring taking people to Italy through Libya, from Rajoir and from Bhanga in Faridpur, take BDT 18 to 25 lakh from each person under a contract for Europe, and several times more must be paid if the person is caught by Libyan police or held by mafia there [LB0128, LB0129].
- A broker of another Madaripur ring, speaking anonymously in February 2025, said local brokers only collect people and money for Bangladeshi brokers living in Libya, taking BDT 12 lakh to send a person to Libya and BDT 4 lakh more for the "game" (the sea crossing), and that most local brokers in Madaripur now work for Miraj Howlader of Mridhakandi, Shikarmangal union, Kalkini, whom he called a big mafia figure in Libya [CQ0084, CQ0085].
- To pay the broker Monir Sheikh of Rajoir, Kuddus Bepari (see Recruited while abroad or just back) wrote two bighas of land worth BDT 40 lakh to the broker's wife, Hamida Begum; Monir ran a tailoring shop at Rajoir bazar until about two years earlier, married his three daughters to men living in Libya whom locals say are linked to traffickers and mafias there, has bought land worth at least BDT 5 crore in his wife's name in seven months, and, locals say, makes each contract for BDT 16 lakh [CQ0081, CQ0082, CQ0083].
- On 18 April 2026 the director of Osmani International Airport, Sylhet, said many of the victims heard at that day's roundtable had spent BDT 10 to 15 lakh, and up to BDT 20 lakh, to go abroad [LB0244].
- According to an account by Bangladesh's ambassador to Libya, Major General Habib Ullah, quoted by former foreign affairs adviser Md Touhid Hossain in Prothom Alo on 27 April 2026, among 113 irregular migrants sent home from Libya one had paid brokers and mafia BDT 82 lakh in stages and another BDT 73 lakh, and when asked how many had paid a broker more than BDT 20 lakh, over fifty of the 113 raised their hands [LB0196, LB0197, LB0198].
- On 18 August 2026 Shariful Islam Hasan, associate director of BRAC's Migration Programme and Youth Platform, said people from eight to ten districts, including Madaripur, Shariatpur, Sylhet, Sunamganj, Habiganj, Kishoreganj and Narsingdi, try to reach Europe across the Mediterranean, that many first go to Libya through Egypt or Dubai and are physically tortured there, and that some pay BDT 25 lakh, some BDT 30 lakh, and some as much as BDT 65 to 67 lakh [AB0107, AB0108].

**Cases.** In each case below, the family paid, or was asked for, more money after the migrant reached Libya [PA0169, LB0148, LB0175, LB0108, PA0233, LB0049, LB0162, LB0063, LB0268, PA0238, PA0241, PA0229, LB0253]:

- **Spain promised, Libya delivered.** The father of a young man from Jagannathpur, Sunamganj, alleges in a case filed in Sylhet in December 2024 that he made a verbal agreement on 15 August 2018 to send his son to Spain for BDT 15 lakh through a man who is the labour affairs secretary of a union Awami League unit, and that the son was instead sent to Libya, where ring members tortured him and threatened to kill him unless more money was paid [PA0169, PA0170, PA0171].
- **BDT 12 to 20 lakh, then money for release.** In 2022 and 2023, 24 young men from Shariatpur and Madaripur were taken from home to be sent to Italy from Libya (see Held, jailed or missing); the broker ring took BDT 12 lakh to 20 lakh from the 24 families in various ways, some of the men were detained by police in Libya on two occasions and jailed, some were held hostage and tortured, and they were freed from captivity and jail after money was collected from their families [LB0143, LB0148, LB0149].
- **BDT 4 lakh to Libya, BDT 19 lakh in all.** Khurshed Mia, who went to Libya on 14 June 2023 (see Libya to Italy by boat), said that while working in Libya he agreed with a broker to be taken by sea to Italy for BDT 9 lakh and paid BDT 6 lakh in two rounds without reaching Italy; he said the whole attempt cost him BDT 19 lakh, plus several lakh for treatment, and that he had to sell the land his house stood on [LB0171, LB0175, LB0176].
- **BDT 3 lakh agreed, BDT 12 lakh ransom.** According to their families, three men from Noli Joynagar village, Mathbaria, Pirojpur, left for Libya through a broker in July 2023 on a contract of BDT 3 lakh and went first to Dubai, after which contact was lost; some time later they called on imo from Libya to report torture, and the ring demanded BDT 12 lakh ransom per person [LB0108, LB0109, LB0110]. The wife of one said in January 2025 she had sold 15 katha of land to raise the BDT 12 lakh ransom, and the father of another said he had paid BDT 15 lakh since his son left and did not know how much more he would have to pay [LB0111, LB0112].
- **BDT 9.5 lakh agreed, BDT 45 lakh taken.** The family of Dipu Mia of Bhairab, Kishoreganj, contracted with a broker from the same village for BDT 9.5 lakh to reach Italy through Libya, and relatives said in August 2025 that the brokers had so far taken BDT 45 lakh and were demanding BDT 25 lakh more [PA0233, PA0234, PA0235]. According to the family, he reached Libya on 23 September 2023, was detained a few days later and passed to a mafia group, and a month and a half after that they got a call showing a torture video and demanding BDT 11 lakh within one day [PA0236, PA0237].
- **BDT 22 to 24 lakh, then BDT 10 lakh for release.** Prothom Alo reported in July 2024 that the Bhairab broker Rasel Mia took BDT 24 lakh from the father of Faruk Mia of Bhairab and BDT 22 lakh each from Tofayel Ahmed and Sabuj Mia for Italy; the father of Shafiqul Islam of Shibpur, Narsingdi, said Rasel took BDT 23 lakh and that BDT 10 lakh was now being demanded to free his son [LB0049, LB0050, LB0051].
- **BDT 15 lakh, then BDT 30 lakh more.** According to his family, Md Rasel Mia (25) of Dharmandal village, Nasirnagar, Brahmanbaria (not the Bhairab broker of the same name), went to Libya in early 2024 through a broker from his own village for BDT 15 lakh, raised by selling the ancestral homestead and borrowing from relatives; he was sold to a local ring on arrival, which sent videos of his torture to Bangladesh and took a further BDT 30 lakh from the family in rounds [LB0162, LB0163, LB0164]. According to the family, the ring demanded BDT 10 lakh more a few days before his death, and when the family could not pay he was tortured and died in Libya on 21 February 2025; the family learned of the death the next day through an expatriate, and his father, who said he had paid BDT 50 lakh in stages by selling his homestead and farmland, alleged his son was killed with a poisonous injection [LB0165, LB0166, LB0167].
- **BDT 4 lakh, then 4.5, then 2.5 more.** Prothom Alo reported on 21 September 2024 that a broker ring sent 26 young men from 25 families in Goalnagar union, Nasirnagar, Brahmanbaria, and Ratani village, Austagram, Kishoreganj, to Libya on the promise of Italy, each paying BDT 11 lakh; nine came home after torture and harassment, some of the rest were captive and several were missing [LB0061, LB0062]. Returnees said each of them gave the broker Farhad Mia BDT 4 lakh before leaving, that he took BDT 4.5 lakh more from each while holding them in a camp in Libya, and that after they were caught on the boat and jailed and he got them out, he again took BDT 2.5 lakh from each family promising to send them to Italy through the "game" [LB0063, LB0064, LB0065].
- **BDT 30 lakh demanded, then BDT 42 lakh.** Md Saiful (39) of Sundarganj, Gaibandha, went to Libya through a Bangladeshi broker about two years before November 2026; the ransom calls to his family from August 2025 are set out under no proof of life (see Ransom and resale) [LB0261, LB0268, LB0269, LB0270].
- **The "body contract".** According to relatives and neighbours, the family of Saidul Bepari of Madaripur paid BDT 20 lakh ransom to free him in Libya and then agreed a BDT 16 lakh "body contract" through a Bangladeshi broker, an arrangement under which brokers promise to deliver the migrant alive to Italy however long it takes and bear lodging, food and airfare; he died in a Libyan detention camp on 14 February 2025 after torture [PA0238, PA0239, PA0240].
- **BDT 22 lakh, then BDT 20 lakh more.** According to his family, a BDT 22 lakh contract was made to send Imran Khan of Madaripur to Italy, brokers took BDT 20 lakh more while he was held in a detention camp in Benghazi, and he was shot dead by pirates on a migrant boat on 1 November 2025, which the family learned from a broker on 18 November [PA0241, PA0242, PA0243].
- **BDT 25 lakh, then BDT 22.5 lakh more.** According to relatives and local sources, a broker first took BDT 25 lakh from Tachhir Fakir of Rajoir, Madaripur, for the journey to Italy; brokers in Libya then held him in a detention camp and took BDT 20 lakh ransom from his family and another BDT 2.5 lakh said to be for treatment, and he died in a Libyan hospital in May 2026 [PA0229, PA0230, PA0231]. The family sold all its land to pay [PA0232].
- **BDT 60 lakh in three rounds.** Mizanur Rahman of Phulsuti village, Nagarkanda, Faridpur, alleged at a human chain outside the Faridpur Deputy Commissioner's office on 14 October 2026 that a Faridpur trafficking ring, having promised to take him to Italy, held and tortured him in Libya and took BDT 60 lakh in three rounds [LB0253, LB0254].

#### The path

1. **Dhaka to Dubai** on a tourist visa [I0290]. Azmir Hossain said he reached Dubai on 21 March 2024, where the broker's men met him at the airport and kept 30 to 35 people together in a room of a small house for two days [LB0030, LB0031].
2. **Dubai to Benghazi**, directly or through Egypt; when checks at Dhaka airport tighten, groups are sent through India and Sri Lanka instead [I0003, I0291]. One 2026 victim was routed Chennai, Sri Lanka, Dubai, Alexandria, Benghazi and Tripoli; another group went through Mumbai, Saudi Arabia and Egypt [I0020, I0349]. According to Palong Model police station and relatives, 24 Shariatpur and Madaripur men taken from home in 2022 and 2023 were sent first to the United Arab Emirates, then through Egypt to Libya [LB0146, LB0147]. Azmir Hossain said his group was flown from Dubai to Egypt, held 24 hours in a warehouse-like place in the airport area, and put on another plane to Libya [LB0030, LB0031].
3. **Across Libya by road**, west through Ajdabiya, Sirte and Shwerayif to Zuwara and Zawiya, the main departure points since 2023 [I0004, I0005]. Entry to Benghazi is on clearances from the Libyan National Army's Military Investment Authority, the armed force that controls the east [I0376].
4. **The boat to Lampedusa**, Italy's southern island, usually 24 to 36 hours in fair weather [I0006]. The crossing itself is known as the "game"; small boats are mainly used and are loaded beyond capacity [LB0125, LB0126].

Md Touhid Hossain, the former foreign affairs adviser, wrote in April 2026 that the main route to Italy by sea used to be a flight to Kenya, then overland to Libya, then the boat, that a person boards the plane to Kenya on a false story of a safari or tourism, and that along the way people change hands several times like goods [LB0211, LB0212, LB0213]. He also wrote that because surveillance and restrictions on the route to Italy had recently increased, traffickers were now using the Greece route [LB0209, LB0210]. UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) data show that in 2026 Bangladeshis were for the first time the top nationality among sea crossings to Greece, Prothom Alo reported on 30 July 2026 [RU0052].

The chain is run in pieces: brokers in Bangladesh recruit and collect money, facilitators in the UAE, Egypt, Kuwait, Turkey, India and Sri Lanka move groups, and Libyan airport sponsors, armed groups, drivers and coastal smugglers control everything inside Libya [I0289]. The Counter Terrorism and Transnational Crime (CTTC) unit of the police identified a Libya-based trafficker who had appointed 40 brokers across Bangladesh whose job was to find young people wanting to go abroad, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of 25 February 2024 [LB0008].

#### Ransom and resale

- **Ransom.** Smugglers call Bangladeshis "gold" because a family at home will answer ransom calls [I0293]. Men are held, tortured and forced to phone home for more money; five Chuadanga men were held 10 months in a room with 57 others [I0346, I0343, I0345]. In July 2026 families of six men from Manoharganj, Cumilla, were asked Tk 25 lakh per person, and in November 2026 the same sum was reportedly being demanded for each of more than 300 men from Bhairab, Kishoreganj [I0021, I0024, I0348]. People in the migration sector told Prothom Alo in July 2026 that workers bound for Italy or Greece are sent to Libya, tortured in many camps there, and held hostage until BDT 25 to 30 lakh is extracted [RU0054]. Bangladeshi and Libyan smugglers stage kidnappings together [I0014]. Md Touhid Hossain wrote in April 2026 that many Bengalis are among those who torture the hostages in Libya, and that the people who collect ransom from families in Bangladesh by showing torture videos live undisturbed in the same society [LB0214, LB0215].
- **No proof of life.** According to investigators, on 5 August 2025 the wife of Md Saiful of Sundarganj, Gaibandha, got a call from a Libyan mobile number saying he was in the callers' custody, that BDT 30 lakh ransom must be paid or he would be killed, and that no one in the family would be allowed to speak to him until half the ransom was paid [LB0261, LB0262, LB0263]. The CID said the family sent BDT 5 lakh in all between 10 and 25 August 2025 from a bKash (mobile money) shop at Bamandanga Hall Mor, Sundarganj, to several mobile numbers the accused gave, and that even after the money was received the family was not put in touch with Saiful [LB0264, LB0265]. The father was then contacted from another number and asked for a further BDT 42 lakh in the name of rescuing his son, and was told his son was held by two men in Libya named Mokarram and Anwar from whom the caller had got the father's number; the father said he would pay nothing more unless he could speak to his son directly, and no more calls came to him or to his son's wife [LB0268, LB0269, LB0270].
- **No one guarantees release.** Families of two hostages in the Bhairab case said in July 2024 that large ransoms were being demanded but that they could find no one who could assure them that paying would get the men released [LB0052]. According to local people in Bhairab, when the hostages phoned home begging to be freed, the broker Rasel Mia and his ring members hid and cut off contact with the families; the families then went to community leaders for a remedy and got no result [LB0053, LB0054].
- **Families stay silent.** The wife of one Mathbaria hostage refused to name the brokers to Prothom Alo in January 2025, saying she wanted her husband back, and the family of another refused to speak at all, believing someone known to the brokers lived near their home and that the torture would increase if word got out [LB0113, LB0114].
- **Sold between gangs.** Seven Bangladeshis were sold for USD 17,000 together, and the buyer demanded USD 12,000 each [I0294]. Azmir Hossain said everyone's passport was taken as soon as they reached Libya in March 2024, that he was beaten whenever he mentioned the promised job, and that BDT 6 lakh was collected from his family in stages by phone [LB0032, LB0033]. He said the broker then sold him to another group at a high price and left him in a Libyan desert to be handed over; he escaped with a Libyan citizen's help and took shelter with a Bangladeshi he knew, and the broker then came and took him back [LB0034, LB0035]. Suman Mia of Nasirnagar said in September 2024 that he paid the broker BDT 11 lakh in all and was told he would be taken to Italy within a week; 60 people were put on a boat from Benghazi, and after it had run for an hour they understood they had all been sold [LB0068, LB0069]. Enamul Haque of Lalurtuk village, Nasirnagar, said he spent BDT 22 lakh to send two sons (land sold for BDT 7 lakh, a BDT 12 lakh loan and savings), that one son was "sold four times", and that he last spoke to the other a year earlier; both were missing in September 2024 [LB0072, LB0073, LB0074].
- **Extra charges.** Charges added in Libya push the total to USD 15,000 to 17,000 (about Tk 18.7 to 21.2 lakh) [I0008, I0067]. In the last holding houses before the sea ("game houses") smugglers demand more even from people told the trip was fully paid [I0013]. Those told they can pay after reaching Europe are made to work in Libya to pay off the debt [I0016].
- **Paying does not free you.** In a 2025 case the families paid Tk 21.8 lakh and Tk 16 lakh in ransom, and the men were then handed to Libyan police and held 45 days [I0363, I0364]. People caught at sea by the Libyan Coast Guard report being handed to Bangladeshi networks on the coast who demand more [I0015].
- **Killed when the family could not pay.** At the Faridpur human chain on 14 October 2026, Taslima Begum said her husband Habib was killed because the family could not pay a ransom of BDT 10 lakh; she now lives with their three children at her father's house [LB0257, LB0258].

#### Held, jailed or missing

- **In the camps.** Suman Mia said the 60 sold off the Benghazi boat were held in a camp for a month and a half, beaten every day and fed once in 24 hours, given two litres of salt water for bathing and the toilet and one bread at night, and made to do cleaning work from morning to evening at the house of a man named Ahammad, whose son beat them with a pistol when they did not work [LB0070, LB0071]. Azmir Hossain said he was locked in a room and beaten regularly for trying to escape and went eight days without food; he reached home on 5 May 2024 in a critical condition, 41 days after leaving home according to Prothom Alo, and his mother said he is now mentally ill from blows to the head and that she owes BDT 6 lakh [LB0036, LB0037, LB0038]. Returnees told the ambassador to Libya before their flight home in spring 2026 that one had eaten rice once after twenty days, five men sharing one bowl with no curry, that another got a handful after twenty-five days, and that a piece of bread was thrown at them "the way food is given to a dog" and they were beaten as well [LB0200, LB0201].
- **Raids and jail.** Khurshed Mia said law-enforcement officers raided the fourth-floor flat where 11 of them lived at about 1:30 am on 10 July 2024 and he hid for two hours on the window ledge; later he was kept in a place with 210 other people, where a raid at 10 pm took away 160 while he hid for four hours in a tree [LB0177, LB0178, LB0179]. Returnees from Nasirnagar and Austagram said that two and a half to three months after the last payment the broker sold them, that after three months in jail they reached their families through a Pakistani man with the help of a man from Bhairab, and could come home only after each paid a further BDT 1 lakh 20 thousand for the air ticket and other costs; they returned between 7 August and 14 September 2024 after release from Libyan jail [LB0066, LB0067]. Hridoy Chowdhury said that after reaching Libya he worked for a company for two months in a row, was then taken to Benghazi and boarded a boat for Greece, and that after about 14 hours on the boat the coast guard detained them and sent them back to Libya, where he was held as a prisoner in a jail in Tobruk [AB0097]. He said he finally got out of the jail by contacting his broker, and that he had to come home empty-handed despite spending a huge amount of money [AB0098].
- **Escape.** Mizanur Rahman said that, still not released after the payments and seeing how severe the torture was, he broke a window, escaped and gave himself up to Libyan police, and later came home in 2026 through IOM; eleven people tortured by the same ring came home from Libya through IOM in 2026 and joined the human chain in Faridpur [LB0255, LB0256].
- **Missing.** Prothom Alo reported on 13 July 2024 that at least 24 people from Bhairab and elsewhere in Kishoreganj, Habiganj and Narsingdi went to Libya at about the same time through a broker ring led by Rasel Mia of Bhairab to cross to Italy by sea, that none had reached Italy, and that some were hostages of kidnappers and others were in Libyan jails [LB0047, LB0048]. According to relatives and police, 24 young men from Shariatpur and Madaripur who were taken from home in 2022 and 2023 went missing in Libya; relatives claim none of the 24 has contacted family since 21 and 22 March 2024 [LB0143, LB0144, LB0145]. Riaz Akon, one of their relatives, said his family gave the broker BDT 20 lakh, raised by selling land and a cattle farm, to send his younger brother to Italy, had had no trace of him for a year, and that the broker gave no information when asked [LB0155, LB0156, LB0157].

#### Work in Libya

Khurshed Mia said that in Libya, after a month without work, he got a job at a shopping mall "in exchange for 500 dinars" that was to pay 1,400 dinars at the end of the month but paid him half, and then worked four months at an oil company for 1,200 dinars a month [LB0173, LB0174]. He said a mafia group attacked the building the broker had moved him to and he jumped from the first floor to save himself, injuring his arm and leg; he got no treatment for five days, the broker's men moved him from a private hospital to a government hospital because they could not bear the cost, he had five operations in three and a half months there, and he came home on 13 February 2025 unable to walk [LB0180, LB0181, LB0182].

#### Deaths

- **May 2020, Mizda.** According to Libya's then government, a trafficker's family killed 26 Bangladeshis and four Africans in Mizda, Libya [I0332].
- **February 2024, off Tunisia.** On 15 February 2024 a boat carrying 52 migrants caught fire and sank in the Mediterranean off the Tunisian coast; 8 Bangladeshis died, 26 others were rescued and one was seriously injured, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of 25 February 2024 [LB0007]. According to relatives, police and local sources, several young men from Rajoir, Madaripur, and Muksudpur, Gopalganj, left home for Italy on 14 January 2024, flew through Dubai to Libya, and on 14 February left Libya on an engine boat through brokers; eight of them died on the boat off Tunisia (five from Rajoir and three from Muksudpur), along with one Pakistani [LB0014, LB0015, LB0016]. Relatives of the eight told journalists at Dhaka airport on 2 May 2024 that brokers had put 52 people on a small boat that could take 30, that the eight who died had been forced to stay below the deck, and that when they tried to come out for lack of oxygen the brokers beat them and sent them back down; the relatives said they died of the torture and lack of oxygen [LB0017, LB0018, LB0019]. The brother of one of the dead, Sajal Bairagi (22) of Sendia village, Rajoir, said the family gave BDT 14 lakh to a broker named Mosharraf by selling land and cows and borrowing [LB0020, LB0021].
- **January 2025, off Libya.** According to the Bangladesh embassy in Libya, a boat carrying 56 people trying to reach Italy from Libya sank in the Mediterranean on 25 January 2025 and 23 bodies washed up on the beach between 28 and 31 January; the bodies were decomposed and not formally identified, but the embassy, citing local sources, said nearly all were Bangladeshi [LB0118, LB0119]. According to relatives, police and local sources, two Rajoir men left home on 1 January 2025, flew from Dhaka through Dubai to Libya, were sent by the broker to the coast, and left the Libyan coast on an engine boat on 24 January [LB0127]. Intelligence sources said in February 2025 that, besides the two dead, eight more people from Rajoir upazila, all aged 20 to 30, were reported missing, though it was not confirmed whether they were on the boat that sank on 25 January [LB0133]. Relatives said at least 12 people from Madaripur and Faridpur were among those killed, according to a Prothom Alo editorial of 6 February 2025 [LB0137]. The same editorial reported that the father of Hridoy Howlader of Kamarkhali, Faridpur, said the group was to be taken to Italy by ship on the night of 24 January, that many refused to board when a small boat was offered instead, and that those who refused were shot dead while those who boarded drowned [LB0134]. It also reported that Sujan Faraji of Madaripur, who died in the sinking, set out with BDT 16 lakh taken from his aunt without telling his father, who had not agreed to the journey, and that a broker named Monir Sheikh of Majumdarkandi, Rajoir, had contracted to take him to Italy for BDT 15 lakh [LB0135, LB0136]. Abul Bashar Akon and his nephew Titu Howlader also died (see Libya to Italy by boat) [LB0120]. Kuddus Bepari, sent from Dhaka to Egypt in early November 2024 and on to Libya, and his brother-in-law Sujon Howlader (39), who also paid the broker Monir BDT 16 lakh, were put on an engine boat from the Benghazi coast on 24 January 2025 after about two months in a detention house; the boat sank within hours, Sujon's death was confirmed by his wife Munni Begum, and Kuddus was missing, believed dead by his family [CQ0078, CQ0079, CQ0080]. Madaripur's Additional Superintendent of Police (Administration and Finance), Bhaskar Saha, said in February 2025 that by family accounts three people from Rajoir upazila had died and seven were missing, presumed dead, after the recent sinking, that no family had yet come to file a case, and that police were hunting several named brokers [CQ0086, CQ0087].
- **November 2025, off Al Khums.** A boat with 26 Bangladeshis capsized off Al Khums and four died [I0337].
- **March 2026, Libya to Greece.** At least 18 Bangladeshis died on a boat that left Tobruk, Libya, for Greece, carrying 38 Bangladeshis among 43 people [I0333, VI0024, I0334]. Twelve of the dead were from Sunamganj [RU0055]. Md Touhid Hossain wrote in Prothom Alo on 27 April 2026 that a boat that left Libya for Greece on 21 March 2026 with 48 passengers drifted for six days before 26 people were rescued alive, 21 of them Bangladeshis, and that 20 of the 22 dead or missing were reported to be Bangladeshi [LB0207, LB0208]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 1 April 2026 said the family of Shayek Ahmed (23) of Sunamganj gave brokers BDT 12 lakh by selling their cows and land, that the rubber boat he left on from Libya for Greece on 21 March lost its way and drifted for six days, that he died of starvation when the food ran out, and that 12 young men from Sunamganj died on that journey and their bodies were thrown into the sea [LB0183, LB0184, LB0185]. According to a survivor, the boat carrying 38 people from Libya lost its way and drifted six days; 18 died for lack of food and clean water, their bodies were kept on the boat two days and then thrown into the sea, and the survivors were taken to a camp after the boat reached the Greek coast on 27 March 2026 [CQ0213, CQ0214].
- **April 2026, from Tajoura.** On 4 April 2026 a wooden boat that left Tajoura, Libya, for the Italian island of Lampedusa with about 105 passengers sank; 32 people were rescued alive and 2 bodies recovered, the other 71 were missing, and Bangladeshis were among both the rescued and the missing, Md Touhid Hossain wrote [LB0205, LB0206].
- **At sea.** The State Minister for Expatriates' Welfare alleged that smugglers abandon weak or sick passengers at sea and often throw bodies into the water [I0335].

#### Arrival in Italy

People landing are taken to a hotspot (a first-reception centre) for checks, photos and fingerprints [I0032]. Italy lists Bangladesh as a safe country of origin and rejected 88.66% of Bangladeshi asylum claims at first decision in 2025 [I0034, I0033]. Italy returned over 120 Bangladeshis in 2025 up to early November, including four on 31 October, two of whom had arrived from Libya without a visa a month before [I0035, I0066].

#### Coming home

Men who went this way and failed have come home with debts of more than Tk 10 lakh [I0284]. Five Chuadanga families lost about Tk 1.47 crore in fees and ransom [I0343]. Mashiur Rahman of Madaripur, who came home on 1 September 2026 with a group of Bangladeshis returned from detention in Libya with IOM's help, said he had left his own restaurant business to try for Italy and came back having lost BDT 32 lakh [RU0068].

#### The numbers

- **Arrivals in Italy by sea:** 20,164 Bangladeshis in 2025 and 6,160 from 1 January to 6 November 2026, more than any other nationality both years [I0002, I0001]. BRAC, citing Frontex (the EU border agency), said 20,259 Bangladeshis entered Europe across the Mediterranean in 2025, and that in the first six months of 2026, 4,249 Bangladeshis entered Italy this way and 3,608 went from Libya to Greece by sea [RU0053]. Prothom Alo reported on 5 September 2026 that 66,316 people of all nationalities crossed the Mediterranean to Italy in 2025 and 1,126 of them died or went missing at sea, and that of 14,194 who crossed up to June 2026 the largest share, 32% or 3,691 people, were Bangladeshis [RU0065, RU0066]. According to CID information cited in a Prothom Alo editorial of 1 April 2026, 17,000 Bangladeshis were sent to Italy by illegal routes in the previous year [LB0186]. According to UNHCR, as cited in a Prothom Alo editorial of 25 February 2024, 13 per cent of the 5,236 refugees and migrant workers who went to Italy by sea were Bangladeshi citizens; the editorial does not state the period [LB0006].
- **Share of the route:** Bangladeshis were 50% of people leaving Libya in December 2024 and 39% in December 2025; 88% of all arrivals in Italy in 2025 left from Libya [I0054, I0056, I0057]. Frontex counted 2,510 Bangladeshis among 8,489 crossings on the Central Mediterranean route in January to April 2026 [I0285]. Bangladeshis were again in 2026 the top nationality among people crossing the Mediterranean to Italy, according to UNHCR data reported by Prothom Alo on 30 July 2026 [RU0052].
- **Over the years:** 4,141 Bangladeshis arrived by this route in 2020, 7,838 in 2021 and 15,228 in 2022; Frontex counts about 110,000 from 2009 to June 2026 [I0287, I0351]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 6 February 2025 stated, giving no source, that 84,000 Bangladeshis went to Europe by sea from 2009 to 2024 and that on average at least 100 Bangladeshis die each year when boats sink [LB0139]. Shariful Islam Hasan of BRAC said on 18 April 2026 that Bangladeshis are the largest group in the world crossing the Mediterranean to Europe, a situation that has lasted a decade [LB0220, LB0221].
- **All irregular routes to Europe:** IOM counted 24,318 Bangladeshis reaching Europe by irregular sea and land routes in 2025, up 59% on 2024 [I0301]. The general secretary of the Sylhet District Press Club said on 18 April 2026, giving no source, that over the previous 10 years at least 10,000 people had died or gone missing, more than two and a half lakh had been trafficked abroad by illegal routes and at least 15,000 had been jailed in various countries [LB0245]. Shariful Islam Hasan of BRAC said at a roundtable in Cox's Bazar on 6 June 2026 that about one lakh Bangladeshis have been detained while trying to cross the Mediterranean in the past 16 years, and that about six and a half lakh Bangladeshis currently have asylum applications lodged in various countries [MB0028, MB0029].
- **Clearance:** Prothom Alo reported on 5 September 2026 that only 100 people took BMET clearance to go to Libya in 2025 and 63 in 2026, which it reads as meaning the workers reach Libya through the Middle East and other countries [RU0067].
- **Where people come from:** Shariful Islam Hasan of BRAC said on 18 April 2026 that people from 8 to 10 districts, among them Madaripur, Shariatpur, Sylhet, Sunamganj and Habiganj, are losing their lives going this way [LB0220, LB0221]. By the ambassador to Libya's account, about two-thirds of 113 returnees on one spring 2026 flight identified themselves as from Madaripur [LB0199].
- **Deaths:** 1,718 people died or went missing on the Central Mediterranean in 2024, one for every 39 arrivals, and about 765 by 7 April 2026, over 460 more than a year earlier [I0055, I0029]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 21 February 2026 says, citing UNHCR data, that Bangladeshis were the largest group among migrants who crossed the Mediterranean to Italy from January to October 2024, as in 2023, and states, without naming a source, that more than 25,000 people drowned in the Mediterranean in the past decade, of whom at least 500 each year were Bangladeshi [AA0212, AA0213].
- **Returns:** at least 10,000 Bangladeshis have been flown home from Libya with IOM's help since 2017, including 175 on 26 February 2026 [I0288, I0026]. In 2023 the Embassy in Tripoli could not get Libyan exit permits to send home about 2,000 people [I0018]. According to Bangladesh embassy sources in Tripoli cited in a Prothom Alo editorial of 6 February 2025, 4,200 Bangladeshis had been freed from various detention centres in Libya and sent home in the previous year and a half, and more than a thousand irregular Bangladeshis were still held in Libya [LB0138]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 25 February 2024 stated that BRAC's Migration Programme rescued and brought home 629 people in 2022 who had been living abroad without valid papers, and that they had been taken to Libya with the promise of attractive jobs in Europe [LB0004, LB0005].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** under the 2026 Act, organising this journey is migrant smuggling, holding people for ransom is a form of exploitation under the trafficking offence, and the Act also covers offences committed abroad against Bangladeshis (see The law in Bangladesh) [VI0006, VI0003, VI0020]. The Minister for Expatriates' Welfare said on 18 April 2026 that the government had declared zero tolerance of the "mafia syndicate" at home and abroad and was determined to identify the Bangladeshi members of trafficking rings in Libya and elsewhere, bring them back and try them [LB0218, LB0219]. A Prothom Alo editorial of 6 February 2025 stated that some licensed manpower-exporting firms are behind these broker rings and send people abroad through them, and that cases against many such firms in the past led to no action because many of those involved were influential people linked to the then ruling party [LB0141, LB0142].
- **Libya:** in April 2026 a Libyan court jailed four traffickers for up to 22 years [I0059].
- **Italy:** entering or staying without permission is an offence fined EUR 5,000 to 10,000 (about Tk 6.98 to 13.96 lakh) [I0049, VI0031]. If removal can't happen at once, the Questore (head of the provincial immigration police) can hold the person in a CPR, a detention centre before removal, for three months [I0051, I0052]. Expulsion brings a 3 to 5-year ban entered in the Schengen Information System, which bars every EU and Schengen country [I0047, I0048]. An Italian court did recognise one Bangladeshi kidnapped and tortured in Libya until his family paid as a trafficking victim [I0031].

### Malaysia: boats and land crossings

#### How it is sold

About 17,777 workers with BMET clearance failed to reach Malaysia before the 31 May 2024 deadline [I0371].

**By land:** a Bangladeshi smuggling ring broken up in Malaysia in July 2026 is believed to have charged up to RM15,000 (Malaysian ringgit) per person and made about RM3.2 million since January 2026 [I0144]. A group caught in Thailand in April 2026 had paid brokers Tk 7 lakh each [I0147].

**By sea, to Rohingya in the camps:** brokers lure Rohingya refugees from the Cox's Bazar camps onto trawlers with good jobs in Malaysia, and girls with a promise of marriage there, taking Tk 20,000 to 30,000 a head before boarding, police and local representatives said in October 2024 [MA0052, MA0053]. In April 2025 police said the people on one seized trawler had been promised good jobs in Malaysia or marriage to a suitable man, many girls had been married over the phone, and most had paid Tk 30,000 to 50,000 [MA0097, MA0098]. Researchers of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies wrote in January 2024 that Rohingya men already in Malaysia persuade parents in the camps to marry them their young daughters without dowry on the promise that the wife can then go safely to Malaysia, and use Facebook, Imo and WhatsApp to draw young women; some who set out die at sea, some are sold as slaves in Myanmar, and some who reach Malaysia are sold there and cut off from their families [CQ0012, CQ0013, CQ0014]. The same researchers wrote that traffickers use the family and friends of Rohingya already in Malaysia to persuade others in the camps to go, with promises of help after arrival [CQ0015].

**By sea, to Bangladeshis:** a camp-in-charge of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commission (RRRC, the government office that runs the Rohingya camps) said in June 2026 that Bangladeshis as well as Rohingya take the sea route, especially teenage girls taken to Malaysia and Indonesia with the lure of a "better husband" [MB0007, MB0008]. Local brokers mainly target teenagers and young men, promising a job paying a lakh of taka with little outlay; people aged 18 to 35 with money are offered onward travel through Malaysia and Indonesia to Australia, the United States or New Zealand, and women are promised good jobs in hotels, motels, parlours or shopping malls, Prothom Alo reported in October 2024 [CQ0043, CQ0044]. The eight Bangladeshis rescued in a Teknaf raid on 15 October 2024 came from Patenga in Chattogram and from Teknaf, Cox's Bazar municipality, Maheshkhali, Ramu and Cox's Bazar Sadar, and the police officer-in-charge said an organised ring had gathered them for Malaysia in exchange for large sums of money [CQ0051, CQ0052, CQ0053].

**"Go free, pay after you arrive":**
- Brokers target unemployed, needy young men of poor families and offer to take them to Malaysia by boat for free, saying they can pay after reaching Malaysia and promising very high wages, Parvez Siddiqui, executive director of the NGO Films for Peace Foundation, said in April 2026; the wife of one missing man, Belal Uddin, said he went because brokers told him he could go without money [CQ0164, CQ0165].
- Nur Kamal (21) of Daganbhuiyan, Feni, told Prothom Alo in November 2026 that someone added him to a WhatsApp group offering to take him to Malaysia free of cost, with payment due only after arrival; he left home on 2 November and on reaching Cox's Bazar was taken to a hut in the Teknaf hills, held under 10 to 12 armed guards and beaten [CQ0189, CQ0190].
- Kamal (a pseudonym) of Teknaf made a Tk 1 lakh deal to go to Malaysia, to be repaid after he found a good job [CQ0036, CQ0037, CQ0038].

**A first payment, then extortion:** police said a house in Baharchhara, Teknaf, raided in December 2024 was a ring's den: people were gathered there through brokers with promises of a better life, jobs and, for unmarried women, marriage; those brought from the Rohingya camps were first charged Tk 20,000 each, and the ring intended to hold them and extort more money by force on the pretext of getting them to Malaysia [CQ0060, CQ0061]. People on a trawler that left Teknaf in November 2026 had paid brokers Tk 10,000 to 15,000 each [CQ0196, CQ0197, CQ0198].

**Other lures:**

- A social-media friendship and an invitation to visit, which ends in abduction or trafficking, according to the Cox's Bazar police chief in June 2026; he said some fishermen think trafficking people pays more than fishing [MB0020, MB0021].
- A day out: in September 2026 traffickers took five teenagers and young men from Mithachhari, Ramu, by CNG auto-rickshaw on the pretext of visiting Teknaf's Marine Drive [CQ0177, CQ0178, CQ0179].
- Australia by way of Malaysia: brokers offered one college student this for Tk 12 lakh [MB0005, MB0006]. People already in Malaysia are being drawn into fraud with assurances that they will be sent on to Australia, according to BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan in July 2026 [AB0076].

**The season:** traffickers are active when the Bay of Bengal is calm, from October or November to March, police and local representatives said in October 2024 [MA0052, MA0053]. The Deputy Commissioner of Cox's Bazar said in June 2026 that the risk of trafficking by sea rises in winter, when the sea is calm [MB0013].

#### The path

**By land:**

1. **Fly to Cambodia** [I0148, I0145].
2. **Cross into Thailand on foot**, then be moved south in stages, kept in hotels and resorts [I0148, I0147].
3. **Cross into Malaysia** through the Kelantan border, then wait in a transit house [I0145, I0146].

**By sea:**

1. **Held in the hills:** people brought from around the country are kept in the hills and other places near Cox's Bazar, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026 [MB0001, MB0002]. In 2025 rings worked in stages, bringing Rohingya out of the camps and driving them to the Kachchhapia area of Teknaf, police said [MA0094, MA0095, MA0096].
2. **Out to the boat at night:** small boats take them in the dark to a large trawler or ship; the coastline is 710 km long and a boat can be launched from any point on it [MB0001, MB0002, MB0016, MB0017]. Departures were rising in October 2024 from the coasts of Teknaf, Ukhiya, Ramu, Cox's Bazar Sadar and Maheshkhali, the Anwara and Banshkhali coasts of Chattogram, and the Karnaphuli river [MA0054, MA0055].
3. **Past St Martin's island through Myanmar waters to Thailand**, where passengers were promised they would be handed on and delivered to Malaysia [MA0094, MA0095, MA0096]. Centre for Migration Studies researchers wrote in January 2024 that traffickers first take Bangladeshis and Rohingya to Teknaf, then by boat to the Myanmar-Bangladesh sea border and on in small boats to Myanmar's Samila coast, where migrants pay traffickers in advance for the leg to Malaysia or Australia, in some cases more than was agreed in Teknaf; returnees put the cost of reaching Malaysia this way at Tk 4 to 5 lakh [CQ0009, CQ0010, CQ0011].

Some journeys sold as going through Malaysia end in Myanmar instead: one man promised Australia by way of Malaysia later realised he had been taken into Myanmar, walked for several days over hills and was detained there [MB0005, MB0049, MB0050, MB0051]. Eight Bangladeshis rescued off Teknaf in September 2026 told police they had been taken to Myanmar about a year earlier on the promise of Malaysia and kept there for a year [CQ0180, CQ0181, CQ0182].

The boat route is older: in 2012 to 2015 thousands tried boat journeys, and trafficking camps and mass graves were found on the Thailand-Malaysia border [I0141]. BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan, who reported on it then as a journalist, puts the number trafficked in that period at about one lakh, and said rescued Bangladeshis, many of them teenagers, were sent home in 2015 with their hands and feet in chains [MB0022, MB0023, MB0024]. The first mass grave, with over 30 bodies, was found in April 2015 near Wang Kelian [I0142]. The dead had no names and their graves were marked only with numbers [MB0011, MB0012].

#### What happens

- **On the boat:** there is no food, clean water or toilet, people die and the bodies are thrown into the sea, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026; the torture begins once people are on board [MB0018, MB0001, MB0002]. Kamal of Teknaf said that during three months at sea passengers got plain rice, chilli and a glass of water once a day, those who died of hunger were thrown into the sea, anyone who asked for food or water was beaten, and the brokers collected money from families while torturing passengers [CQ0039, CQ0040]. The state minister for expatriates' welfare said in June 2026, from victims' and families' accounts, that brokers hold people hostage and take large sums from their families, many die on the way, and those who reach a shore are detained by police there [MB0009, MB0010]. Reports of about 500 Rohingya deaths had come in during one week of July 2026, BRAC said [AB0075].
- **Ransom on the way:** before reaching Malaysia, Rohingya are held at the Thai or Myanmar border while ransom is demanded from relatives in the camps, and if that fails there are attempts to abduct family members, a Balukhali camp leader said in January 2025 [MA0086].
- **Ransom without leaving Bangladesh:** every winter rings hold camp Rohingya for ransom in the name of the sea journey, and sometimes sail a big trawler around for several days, leave the passengers somewhere in Cox's Bazar and run off with the money, Teknaf's police chief said in December 2024 [MA0076, MA0077]. People held in a Teknaf hill den in November 2024 said they were told to call relatives for money and say they were in Myanmar, were tortured and not fed properly, and were held for eight to more than 13 days [MA0065, MA0066, MA0067]. Organised rings bring Bangladeshis and Rohingya to the Teknaf coast with promises of high-paid jobs abroad and a better life, then lock them in secret hideouts and extract ransom through torture, the Coast Guard's Teknaf station in-charge said on 6 June 2026 [CQ0172].
- **Sold into the dens:** victims freed from a Teknaf den on 5 November 2026 said people were handed to the traffickers for money: S M Asaduzzaman (50) of Saltha, Faridpur, said a man he met in Kutupalong camp lured him with work and handed him over for Tk 10,000, after which he agreed a Tk 2 lakh release deal; Md Saiful Islam of Ukhiya said a broker named Nur Ali handed him over and the traffickers said they had bought him and another man for Tk 1 lakh, and that captives who tried to flee were beaten and chained [CQ0191, CQ0192, CQ0193].
- **Abduction for ransom:** at least 193 people, 86 of them Rohingya, were abducted in Teknaf in the year to January 2025 and at least 98 freed on ransom; police found links to trafficking and to some local residents [MA0087, MA0088].
- **Caught in Thailand:** 21 Bangladeshis found in a resort in Chana in April 2026 were charged with illegal entry and stay [I0149].
- **Jailed in Myanmar:** Mohammad Jamal of Cox's Bazar, who spent 11 months 19 days in a Myanmar jail after his trawler broke up in 2023, said Bangladeshi prisoners there did all the work, from growing vegetables to cooking, and were fed rice-water at about 7 am, some rice with dried chillies at 11 am and rice once more at 4 pm; the government brought 173 Bangladeshis home from Myanmar's prisons in April 2024 [CQ0034, CQ0035].
- **Caught in Malaysia:** 15 smuggled Bangladeshis were caught in a Cheras transit house in July 2026 and taken to the Putrajaya immigration depot [I0146]. Malaysia's Home Minister reported 465 deaths in immigration depots in 2021 to 2025 [I0152].
- **Survivors charged at home:** the nine survivors of a sinking in April 2026 were taken to Teknaf police; the three Rohingya were sent back to the camps after giving statements in court, and the six Bangladeshis were charged under the trafficking prevention law and sent to jail, with 10 to 15 unnamed accused added, police inspector Sukanta Chowdhury said [CQ0162, CQ0163].
- **Going home:** Malaysia's PRM 2.0 (Migrant Repatriation Programme) lets undocumented migrants leave; Bangladeshis were the largest group in it [I0373, I0157].
- **Families of the missing:** Manik Molla of Teknaf said in October 2024 that he had been looking for seven and a half years for his youngest brother Shakil, who left by sea for Malaysia at 17; about two years earlier a broker told him Shakil was in a Malaysian prison, took money from the family, and then disappeared [CQ0041, CQ0042]. Sohel, son of the widow Hasina Akter of Karachi Para village, Teknaf, boarded a boat for Malaysia and was not heard from again; the government told her he was held in a Malaysian prison, and she said the government would bring him back once all papers were submitted, Prothom Alo reported in October 2024 [CQ0046, CQ0047].
- **Silence at home:** families who lose someone at sea or are cheated often don't want to admit it, which makes the crime hard to investigate, the Deputy Commissioner of Cox's Bazar said in June 2026 [MB0014, MB0015].

#### The numbers

- **2016 to 2023:** law enforcers rescued 1,134 people being taken to Malaysia by sea, 95% of them Rohingya; 85 cases were filed at Ukhiya and Teknaf police stations naming 1,200 accused, of whom 508 were caught [MA0058, MA0059]. Police and Rohingya leaders said in January 2024 that in the previous five years law enforcers had rescued at least 5,000 Rohingya being trafficked to Malaysia by trawler from the Teknaf, Ukhiya, Cox's Bazar Sadar and Maheshkhali coasts, and that none of those rescued had been charged or jailed [CQ0006, CQ0007].
- **2018 to 2022:** by UNHCR's figures, cited by researchers of the Centre for Migration Studies in January 2024 and by Prothom Alo in October 2024, 8,312 Rohingya refugees tried to reach Malaysia by sea, and 589 of them died at sea [CQ0008, CQ0045].
- **2024:** sea trafficking from Teknaf rose again from January 2024 after six or seven years of being largely stopped, and in the year to January 2025 at least 600 people, including 45 traffickers, were caught in eight or nine operations on the Teknaf coast [MA0082, MA0083]. From Lambashia camp alone more than 500 young men and women were sent towards Malaysia in that year, and more than 300 were caught and brought back, a camp leader said [MA0084, MA0085].
- **January 2025:** Baharchhara union parishad chairman Amjad Hossain said that trafficking syndicates bring Rohingya from the Ukhiya-Teknaf camps to several points on the Baharchhara coast, lure them with promises of Malaysia, hold them in the hills and demand ransom, and that police, RAB and Navy operations had rescued about 150 such victims in January 2025 alone [CQ0068, CQ0069].
- **2025:** about 900 Rohingya refugees went missing or died in the Andaman Sea and Bay of Bengal, the deadliest year on record for sea journeys in South and South-East Asia, with one in seven of the more than 6,500 who attempted the crossing dying or going missing, according to UNHCR as reported by Prothom Alo on 17 April 2026 [CQ0154, CQ0155].
- **2026:** UNHCR said that more than half of the Rohingya crossing by sea in recent years were women and children, that more than 2,800 had taken the journey in 2026 up to 13 April, and that the boats usually leave Cox's Bazar in Bangladesh or Rakhine State in Myanmar for Indonesia or Malaysia [CQ0156, CQ0157, CQ0158].
- **Deaths and missing:** at least 300 people died in more than 20 trawler sinkings in the years to October 2024, according to Abdus Shukkur, a Cox's Bazar human rights lawyer, who said the rings especially target Rohingya girls [MA0060, MA0061]. About 5,787 people went missing on illegal sea journeys in 2023 alone, about 25% of them women and children and half of them Bangladeshi citizens, according to an assistant representative of UNHCR (the UN refugee agency) in June 2026 [MB0026, MB0027]. About two lakh people have crossed the Bay of Bengal in the past decade and many have gone missing, according to BRAC's Shariful Islam Hasan [MB0025].

#### The law

- **Malaysia:** anyone who entered unlawfully or holds forged travel documents is a prohibited immigrant [I0247]. Smuggling cases are brought under the Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007, the Immigration Act and the Passports Act [I0150].
- **Thailand:** in 2017 Thailand convicted 62 people, including nine officials, over the deaths and trafficking of Rohingya and Bangladeshis to Malaysia [I0143].
- **Bangladesh, against passengers:** in January 2024 police in Cox's Bazar said that Rohingya who flee the Ukhiya camps and try to go to Malaysia or another country by sea would from then on be charged under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act and sent to jail [CQ0001, CQ0002]. Three Rohingya girls rescued that month were jailed with the trafficker, and six Bangladeshi survivors of an April 2026 sinking were charged under the trafficking prevention law and jailed [CQ0003, CQ0004, CQ0005, CQ0162, CQ0163].
- **Bangladesh, in practice:** even when cases are filed, traffickers often get out of jail and go back to the same work, the Coast Guard's East Zone commander said in June 2026 [MB0019]. Twenty abduction cases were filed at Teknaf police station in the year to January 2025, and its officer-in-charge said victims released on ransom do not want to complain [MA0089, MA0090].

### Russia: recruited into the war

#### How it is sold

**The jobs offered:**

- construction or welding, through a recruiting agency, at about 75,000 roubles a month [I0273, I0271];
- high-paid work in oil, construction or logistics, according to "Promises Written in Blood", a November 2025 report by BRAC's Migration Programme based on survivors' accounts, family testimony and document checks [RU0015, RU0016];
- work as a cook or cleaner, according to an Associated Press (AP) investigation summarised in a Prothom Alo editorial of 4 February 2026 [RU0037]; Jahangir Alam of Lakshmipur was promised work as a cook in a cantonment in 2025 [RU0043, RU0044];
- security guard, driver, mechanic, electrician, construction worker, welder, repair or transport worker or technician, and sometimes IT, medicine, sport or agriculture, offered through social media adverts or recruiting agents and firms, according to the UN's Independent International Commission of Inquiry on Ukraine; some recruiters said the work was under the military but civilian [RU0156, RU0157, RU0158]. The commission describes a professional athlete told he could sign a contract after arriving if he liked the work, who was handed to the military on arrival, and a cook who did not want to be a soldier but ended up on the front line [RU0194, RU0195].

**The promises:** the UN commission says the offers spoke of high pay, Russian citizenship, comfortable housing, a pension after leaving the job and other benefits, and some recruits were shown photos or a catalogue of the housing [RU0159]. Survivors say brokers repeatedly promised it was not military work [I0379]. The commission describes a group of 30 given a letter of assurance that they would not be sent to the battlefield; by the time of its report 17 had been killed on the front line, 5 were missing and 1 had returned wounded; the report does not name the group's country [RU0154, RU0155]. One family was offered medical school in Moscow for their son if he first served four months in the war [I0278].

**On Facebook:** Shariful Hasan of BRAC said in November 2025 that a Bangladeshi using the name MD Sohel was campaigning on Facebook about the benefits of joining the Russian army and fighting, and that many people were being newly lured to Russia to fight because of it [RU0023].

**Going to fight on purpose:** Prothom Alo reported in October 2026 that travel to Russia had not decreased, and that because six months at the war brings a chance of Russian citizenship and pay of Tk 3 to 4 lakh a month, many go to the war of their own accord [RU0117]. Bangladesh's ambassador to Russia, Md Nazrul Islam, said in October 2026 that more than 90% of the workers go to the war of their own accord, and that Russia says the latest 30 also went voluntarily and holds written proof of it [RU0103].

**The cost:** families paid about Tk 9.5 to 10 lakh, about USD 8,000 [I0271, I0273, I0267]. BRAC's November 2025 report puts it at Tk 7 to 8 lakh [RU0017]. The UN commission found that some recruits paid in advance and others were told the money would be deducted from their pay later [RU0162, RU0163].

#### Who sends them

- **Agencies in Bangladesh:** a Prothom Alo investigation found that clearances for Russia were taken in the names of 93 recruiting agencies over four years, that only 17 took clearance for more than a hundred workers each, and that Bonna Bijoy Overseas alone took the most, about 2,500 workers or 27% [RU0118, RU0119, RU0120]. Prothom Alo reported in October 2026 that Bonna Bijoy Overseas is accused of trafficking in the name of sending workers to Algeria, has a human trafficking case against it, faces allegations over Russia too, and has had its server locked so it can take no new clearances; its managing director, Barun Debnath, did not answer the paper's call or text message [RU0121, RU0122]. BOESL, the government's recruiting agency, took the second-highest number of clearances, 771, and a responsible BOESL official told Prothom Alo that one of its workers became involved in the war (see When the visa, the agency and the clearance are all real) [RU0124, RU0125, RU0126]. Shariful Hasan, head of BRAC's Migration Programme, said in October 2026 that the agencies accused of trafficking are sending the most workers to Russia [RU0129, RU0130].
- **The ring:** the February 2026 Prothom Alo editorial, citing the AP investigation, said a Bangladeshi living in Moscow is the trafficking ring's main organiser, that investigators suspect it has links with the Russian government, and named "SP Global" as one of the firms that ran such trafficking openly [RU0041, RU0042]. BRAC's report says international facilitators as well as local brokers are involved [RU0017].
- **A Banani travel agency and a ringleader:** the CID arrested a woman named in its statements both as Fabiha Jerin and as Tamanna, a partner in Dream Home Travels and Tours Ltd of Banani, Dhaka, at Dhaka airport on 5 February 2025 as she was about to flee to Nepal, in a case filed at Banani police station by the wife of a wounded victim; the CID said she confessed and that her brother Tuhin, in Russia, had encouraged people to go to the war [CQ0114, CQ0115, CQ0116, CQ0119]. In June 2025 the CID arrested the alleged ringleader, Muhammad Alamgir Hossain (40) of Lohagara, Chattogram, at Chattogram's Shah Amanat airport; it said Alamgir, who went to Russia on a student visa in 2008 and became a citizen by marriage, had taken 50 people to Russia on three visa categories [CQ0119, CQ0120, CQ0121].
- **Agents abroad:** the UN commission says recruiting agents in the various countries were local, regional or Russian nationals operating as travel or tourism businesses; some held local licences, some presented themselves as legal or government-approved, and in many cases visas issued by the Russian embassy were handed over through them [RU0166, RU0167].

#### The path

1. **A fast departure.** The UN commission found many recruits were pressed to travel within a few days of accepting, with agents arranging the visa, air ticket, housing and the rest of the journey; visas were business visas, short-term tourist visas or none, varying by country [RU0162, RU0163]. It obtained copies of business visas of recruited Bangladeshis that mention an invitation from a Russian civilian company [RU0153].
2. **Dhaka to Dubai, then Moscow**, or through other third countries [I0269, I0268]. Tasneem Siddiqui, executive director of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit), said in November 2025 that many are first taken to Saudi Arabia on tourist or Umrah visas and later made to sign a contract and forced to the battlefield [RU0030]. The CID said in June 2025 that a ring lured 10 people with jobs as cleaners or cooks in a chocolate factory in Russia at Tk 2-2.5 lakh a month, sent them first to Saudi Arabia on Umrah visas, had them perform Umrah, then took them to Russia and sold them to a man called Sultan, who handed them to the Russian army [CQ0117, CQ0118]. Testimony and documents the UN commission obtained name Bahrain, Brazil, Ethiopia, France, Kenya, Mexico, Morocco, Oman, Panama, Spain, Turkey and the UAE as transit countries; agents sometimes travelled with the recruits or met them there, and some recruits were told to go by road to a neighbouring country and fly from there so as not to be caught at the airport [RU0164, RU0165].
3. **At Moscow airport**, brokers take more cash, in one case about USD 1,000 per person [I0270]. The UN commission found many recruits had no freedom of movement from the airport on: they were kept in temporary accommodation and handed to the armed forces [RU0168].
4. **Passports and phones taken**, and the men are handed to the military [I0264, I0134].
5. **Russian-language contracts** they can't read [I0268]. According to the UN commission, within hours or days of arriving recruits were made to sign a one-year military service contract with Russia's Defence Ministry, and could not use translation because their phones were not in their hands [RU0168, RU0169]. Those who refused were pressured, threatened or tortured; one recruit said four people were shot dead in front of him and he was told he would be next, after which he signed [RU0170].

Some go legally on six-month work visas; when the visa ends they are offered a military contract to renew it [I0276]. Workers laid off from Russian jobs have also been recruited there (see Recruited while abroad or just back) [RU0026, RU0027].

#### What happens

- **Training:** the UN commission found initial training lasted from 20 minutes to a few days, in some cases up to a month [RU0173, RU0174, RU0175]. Jahangir Alam of Lakshmipur said he was handed a weapon without training and then taught to use it for one day, that 16 men were together in his war camp, and that he lived in fear after four of them died [RU0043, RU0044]. Khadija Akter said in February 2025 that her husband, Jafar Sardar of Jashore, was kept in a barracks, given an army haircut and war training, and that his last message said "bring me home at any cost" [RU0010].
- **Work at the front:** the UN commission says recruits were made to dig trenches, guard areas, deliver food and water and collect bodies, their main tasks being to carry out assaults and advance [RU0173, RU0174, RU0175]. In the AP investigation, returned workers said they were made to collect bodies and move the wounded, those who refused were tortured, and a Russian commander told one victim he had in fact been "sold" [RU0038, RU0039]. The Special Response Battalion (SRB), a police unit, the detective branch and others told Prothom Alo that the men sent from Bangladesh have neither the physical fitness, the training nor the tactics for war, and that when the enemy attacks them with drones Russian soldiers identify the enemy's position [RU0131, RU0132].
- **Death:** 30 men flew out on 7 May 2026 [I0273]. In October 2026 the commander of the police's Special Response Battalion said 17 who arrived that May were killed on their first day of deployment after minimal training, 20 Bangladeshis were confirmed dead, and they had been used as bait to locate enemy positions [I0265, I0266]. On 9 July 2026 the Minister had told Parliament that four of the 30 had died [I0314]. A joint investigation by Fortify Rights and Truth Hounds, two rights groups, counted at least 104 Bangladeshis recruited and at least 34 killed [I0275]. The news agency Agence France-Presse (AFP) reported that Mohammad Yasin Sheikh, 22, was killed on 27 March 2025 fighting for Russian forces [RU0031, RU0032]. According to Jafar Sardar's family, the broker took 10 Bangladeshis to Russia together; one, Akram of Narsingdi, escaped and came home, and the other nine were forced to the battlefield, where one was killed and one was shot [RU0009].
- **Abuse:** survivors were sent to fight with little or no training, beaten, unpaid, kept from leaving and threatened when they tried to escape [I0272]. Recruits told the UN commission of days without food and water, beatings, detention and death threats [RU0173, RU0174, RU0175].
- **Pay:** the UN commission found recruits were taken to open bank accounts, but many were not paid and some got little; money was withdrawn from some accounts without permission, some salaries were withheld or accounts closed, and in some cases recruiting agents or military personnel took the bank cards [RU0176, RU0177, RU0178].
- **Missing:** many recruits lost contact with their families after going to the war, the UN commission found [RU0176, RU0177, RU0178]. BRAC's November 2025 report records that Ayon Mondal of Bagerhat, who went on a valid visa, said in his last message that he was being taken towards the Ukrainian border and has not been traced since, and that Amit Barua of Cumilla has been missing since a photo showed him in Russian military uniform [RU0024, RU0025]. Adnan Hossain, a wounded returnee, said Russia does not publish information on Bangladeshis' deaths and that anyone who does not return from the front line is declared missing [RU0090, RU0091].
- **Bodies not returned:** many families still cannot get their sons' bodies home [I0279]. Adnan Hossain said Russia does not bring back the bodies, and those Prothom Alo spoke to said the bodies of foreigners killed are left on the field [RU0090, RU0091]. The ambassador said the bodies cannot be sent home because the Russian authorities are not handing them over [RU0103].

#### The group of 30

The 30 men who flew from Dhaka on 7 May 2026 went through Jabale Noor International of Khilkhet and RS International of Ashulia, through brokers, according to 28 letters their families submitted to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry in July 2026; the letters say the men were sold to the Russian army with the lure of high pay (see When the visa, the agency and the clearance are all real) [RU0115, RU0116]. Prothom Alo said nearly all the families are low-income [RU0197, RU0198, RU0199].

**What they paid:**

- Rajon Hossain (21) of Jhenaidah paid a broker Tk 10 lakh for a high-paid job [RU0086, RU0087].
- Pabitra Chandra (30), a carpenter from a very poor family in Gopalpur, Tangail, went with Tk 1.3 lakh from selling his wife's jewellery and Tk 9.5 lakh borrowed from an NGO and local moneylenders [RU0138, RU0139].
- Robin Mir, an autorickshaw driver from Siddhirganj, Narayanganj, sold his village land and cattle and borrowed, and went with three other men from Narayanganj [RU0147].
- Md Palash Sheikh's family spent Tk 10 lakh [RU0113, RU0114].

**What happened, in order:**

1. **The first days.** Robin Mir's nephew, Rahat Chowdhury, said his uncle was given good food and shown around for three or four days, and then there was no news for five or six days [RU0145, RU0146]. Pabitra Chandra told his brother-in-law, Tapan Chandra Sutradhar, that he and several others were kept in a hotel and would be given work once the papers were sorted out; contact then stopped for some time [RU0140, RU0141]. Rajon Hossain got no job and, after four days in a hotel, was sent to an army camp after his signature was taken on a paper [RU0086, RU0087].
2. **15 to 18 May.** On 15 May Robin Mir said they were being taken to work, and on 17 May he was seen in an army camp in a video [RU0145, RU0146]. On 18 May a worker's family phoned the Bangladesh Embassy in Russia to say that 30 people who had come through Jabale Noor and RS International were being sent to the battlefield; according to the embassy's 25 May letter, it was writing to Russia's Foreign Ministry asking that the workers be returned to work under their appointment letters instead [RU0099, RU0100].
3. **Training.** After 21 days of training the men were split into groups of 16, 8 and 6 and sent to the war [RU0084, RU0085].
4. **29 May.** Pabitra Chandra phoned to say "we are being taken to the war by force; the chance of dying there is 95 per cent" [RU0140, RU0141], and afterwards asked the Bangladesh embassy to arrange for him and the others to be brought home [RU0142, RU0143]. Robin Mir phoned in tears to say they had been taken through deep forest on the promise of work and sold [RU0145, RU0146].
5. **June.** Two wounded returnees and six families said the men pleaded with their families before being sent to war, that the agency promised to take responsibility when the families contacted it, and that from the first week of June the men began to go missing; the families got news of deaths but no proof [RU0104]. Many died around 12 and 13 June [RU0084, RU0085]. At its 14 October 2026 press conference the SRB said 17 of the 30 died in a drone attack on their first day on the battlefield and three more died later [RU0136, RU0137]. Rajon Hossain said he was sent to the front line with five other Bangladeshis, four were killed in a drone attack, and he was wounded [RU0086, RU0087]. Abdul Majid of Lalmonirhat said his son Abdullah Al Mamun phoned his younger brother the day before being sent to fight and was not heard from again; a man who went with him, Al Amin, later said Mamun had died [RU0092, RU0093, RU0094].

**Where they were in October 2026:** Prothom Alo reported 20 dead, two wounded and in Russian hospitals (Palash Sheikh and Ali Hasan Sohel), two back in Bangladesh (Rajon Hossain and Adnan Hossain), and six still being made to fight [RU0133, RU0134, RU0135].

- **The dead,** according to the families: Pabitra Chandra, Robin Mir, Sohel, Md Mehedi Hasan, Md Al Mamun, Md Abdullah Al Mamun, Md Nazmul Alam, Md Wasim Akram, Md Arif Hossain, Md Sayed Molla, Tarikul Islam Mintu, Sabbir Hossain, Mofiz, Md Arman Ali, Md Nazrul Islam, Md Aminur Islam, Kazi Rezaul Karim, Main Uddin, Ripon Hossain and Arif Hossain Patwari [RU0197, RU0198, RU0199]. Robin Mir's family learned of his death through the three Narayanganj men he went with [RU0147]. Pabitra Chandra's family heard of his death through his companions and, by 21 October 2026, had not had it confirmed by the embassy or any government channel [RU0142, RU0143]. In Abdullah Al Mamun's village a funeral in absentia was arranged, but because his death was not confirmed the people who came prayed instead of performing the funeral prayer; five months had passed by October 2026 [RU0092, RU0093, RU0094].
- **The six still fighting:** Roni Sheikh and Sourav Molla of Gopalganj, Al Amin Islam of Lalmonirhat, Kamal Hossain and Helal Sheikh of Madaripur and Abu Hanif of Kishoreganj, some of whom keep in contact with their families [RU0200]. Sourav's brother-in-law, Taslim Sardar, who talks to him over the messaging app imo, said Sourav was three to four kilometres behind the front line being made to dig earth for bunkers and had twice tried to escape without success [RU0148]. He said Kamal was digging bunkers with Sourav, Roni Sheikh and Al Amin were carrying food, water and weapons on foot from the camp to the front line, and Hanif and Helal were made sometimes to dig bunkers and sometimes to carry food [RU0149].
- **The wounded:** Md Palash Sheikh was wounded in his first spell at the war, sent back after recovering, and lost a hand; in October 2026 he was in a Moscow hospital, and his cousin Abu Saleh Islam said asking the embassy and the Ministry had brought no help, that he cannot escape because the hospital is under army guard, and that the family has received no money [RU0113, RU0114].
- **The debts:** Tapan Chandra Sutradhar said in October 2026 that lenders stand behind Pabitra Chandra's family for their money when they sit down to eat, and that the family is thinking of selling its remaining 3 decimals of land, which would still not clear the debt [RU0144].

#### The numbers

- **Clearances:** BMET gave 7,449 clearances for Russia between 1 January 2025 and 27 October 2026 [I0274]. BMET data show 37 people took clearance for Russia in 2023, 1,395 in 2024, 4,725 in 2025 and 3,105 in 2026 up to 15 October [RU0112]. According to BMET, 4,727 Bangladeshis went to Russia in 2025, which made it the 13th largest market for Bangladeshi workers that year, ahead of Malaysia, South Korea and Lebanon [RU0061, AB0174, AB0175]. RMMRU says 4,727 went in 2025 against 993 the year before, and that 2,125 took clearance for Russia in the first six months of 2026 [RU0046, RU0047].
- **Families asking for help:** the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow told AFP in April 2025 that about a dozen families had contacted it to get their sons back, all alleging the sons were deceived into the Russian army [RU0031, RU0032]. By November 2025, 10 families had asked BRAC to bring relatives back from Russia, men who had gone on valid visas to work; BRAC's report says there are more, but the number could not be confirmed [RU0018, RU0019].
- **Deaths:** a Prothom Alo editorial of 4 February 2026 said at least 40 Bangladeshis were believed to have died in the previous few months after being deceived by recruiting agencies into the war [RU0040]. Foreign Ministry sources said in October 2026 that over four to five months the Russian government had told the embassy of 40 Bangladeshis missing or killed, without separating the two, and had said nothing yet about the latest 20 deaths [RU0101]. Prothom Alo had news of at least 54 Bangladeshi deaths in the war over the three years to October 2026 [RU0095]. Shariful Hasan of BRAC said in October 2026 that nobody has a count of how many found work, where the rest are or how many have died [RU0129, RU0130].
- **Worldwide:** a UN commission found men from 29 countries recruited into Russia's forces through deception and false job offers [I0277]. Its draft report, published on the website of the UN human rights office (OHCHR) on 26 October 2026, examined recruitment of nationals of 48 countries and found evidence of deception or coercion for 29, Bangladesh among them, but gives no separate figures, names or outcomes for Bangladeshis [RU0151, RU0152]. Its findings rest on 245 testimonies from recruits detained by Ukraine or back home, their families, lawyers, local authorities, researchers and journalists, plus documents such as invitation letters, visas, air tickets and military contracts; the victims were all men aged 18 to 60, mostly from financially weak and marginal groups [RU0183, RU0184].

#### The law

- **Bangladesh:** at a Dhaka briefing on 9 February 2025 the Foreign Ministry spokesperson, Mohammad Rafiqul Alam, said some Bangladeshis had been forced into the war after falling into traffickers' hands, that the Embassy in Moscow had been asked for details, that the relevant ministries had been asked to act against the recruiting and travel agencies involved, and that one member of a ring trafficking people to Russia had been arrested [RU0033, RU0034]. He said the airports had been asked to take special caution over Bangladeshis going to Russia on travel visas and those with a valid Russian travel visa in their passports [RU0035]. In June 2026 the government cancelled the licences of three agencies accused of sending the 30 men, and forfeited their deposits [I0158]; on 9 July 2026 the Expatriates' Welfare Minister told Parliament the three licences had been suspended [I0314], and later that month told Prothom Alo they had been cancelled [RU0048]. In October 2026 police arrested two men accused of the trafficking [I0263]. The SRB said Mizanur Rahman, founder of Jabale Noor International and described as one of the ring's leaders, and Abu Taher alias Lentu were arrested on 13 October 2026 over the sending of the 30 men [RU0109, RU0110]. Sources in the police's Criminal Investigation Department (CID) said in October 2026 that at least six cases had been filed over trafficking to Russia [RU0111]. Most published lists of the 12 countries needing Ministry approval since 4 November 2026 include Russia [I0114, I0115, VI0026].
- **What the Minister says is being done:** Expatriates' Welfare Minister Ariful Haque Chowdhury said in October 2026 that nobody is given clearance without verification and attestation of confirmed job information, that all embassies have been told to keep watch through the expatriate community, that extra caution is being taken over Russia with appointment letters checked much more thoroughly, and that action has begun against the agencies involved [RU0128, RU0185]. Selim Reza, coordinator of North South University's Centre for Migration Studies, said in October 2026 that the government went on giving clearance for Russia even after learning men were being taken to the war by force, and that demand in Russia's labour market had not been properly assessed [RU0186].
- **International:** the UN commission concluded that the Russian authorities committed human trafficking together with recruiting agents; that trafficked men, having become members of the Russian armed forces by signing the contract, cannot be called mercenaries; and that if they surrender or are captured they are entitled to prisoner-of-war protection until the war ends [RU0179, RU0180]. It recommended that Russia stop recruiting foreigners through deception or coercion, discharge and assist the trafficked men in its forces, and prosecute those involved; according to international media citing Russia's UN mission, Russia does not recognise the commission's mandate and says the report rests on false information [RU0181, RU0182].

#### Bodies and compensation

- **From Russia:** Foreign Ministry sources said in October 2026 that compensation can be claimed if someone dies in the war or is missing for more than six months, but that it means a complicated process, including establishing who the heir is and the heir going to Russia and staying at least six months, and that the bodies cannot be recovered [RU0102].
- **From Bangladesh:** the Wage Earners' Welfare Board says it pays to bring home the body of a worker who went with BMET clearance or registered as a member with the embassy abroad, on the family's application, and that a family with neither can still apply, in which case the Board sends a proposal to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry, which pays from a yearly allocation [RU0105, RU0106]. It gives Tk 35,000 in cash for the burial of each body brought home and then a Tk 3 lakh grant, but by October 2026 no application to bring a body home from Russia had reached it, because with no proof of death nobody can apply, and so nobody is getting any compensation (see If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?) [RU0107, RU0108].

#### Where to get help

**How men have got home:**

- Afzal Hossain Meraj's father applied to the Foreign Ministry on 22 April 2025, alleging his son had been sold to the Russian army; by November 2025 the son had come home, two months earlier, with the help of the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow [RU0028, RU0029].
- Jahangir Alam of Lakshmipur fell ill after four men in his war camp died, spent two months in hospital, escaped from there and came home through the Bangladesh embassy, seven months after going [RU0060, RU0045].
- A wounded survivor got home in August 2026 with help from a doctor and the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow [I0377].
- Adnan Hossain of Narayanganj, admitted to a Moscow hospital with a splinter in his abdomen, had three operations, escaped after two months in hospital, went to the embassy and came home on 15 September 2026 on a travel pass, a valid travel permit [RU0088, RU0089]. Rajon Hossain was wounded, admitted to hospital and escaped from there to come home [RU0086, RU0087].

**Where families have gone:** in February 2025 relatives of nine men sent to the battlefield had formed human chains in Dhaka several times, gone more than once to the Russian embassy and to Bangladesh's Foreign Ministry, and written to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry, and on 16 February 2025 Jafar Sardar's family wrote to the Chief Adviser's office asking for his return and their money; the report of 17 February 2025 records no result [RU0011, RU0012]. In July 2026 the families of the group of 30 sent 28 letters to the Expatriates' Welfare Ministry asking for their quick and safe return and for financial help [RU0115, RU0116]. Taslim Sardar organised a human chain in Dhaka with the affected families in October 2026 [RU0150].

**BRAC's Migration Programme** has received many applications from families with almost the same account since late 2024; it submits applications to the Foreign Ministry for the men's rescue, gives case documents to the police's Criminal Investigation Department (CID), and gives families legal and psychological support [RU0020, RU0021, RU0022]. It has asked the government to trace the Bangladeshis still in Russia [I0378], and in October 2026 its head, Shariful Hasan, called for a hotline and a list of the missing [RU0129, RU0130].

> - What are the Bangladesh Embassy in Moscow's emergency phone and email for families of men sent to the front?

### Where victims and families get help

1. **In danger in Bangladesh:** call 999, the police's national emergency line, free, 24 hours [I0231].
2. **File a criminal case with the local police.** CID asks returnees to file cases with their local police so the brokers and agencies can be prosecuted [I0356]. Under the 2026 Act a case can be filed at a police station or directly with the tribunal, and the police must protect the complainant and keep their identity confidential [VI0014]. A Chandpur family's case in December 2025 led to an arrest [I0365]. Two youths trafficked to Cambodia came home on 3 September 2026, a case naming two men was filed at Paltan police station, Dhaka, on 26 October, and the CID arrested both the next day, according to the CID [AB0170, AB0171]. In 2020 to 2024, 94 to 95% of the accused in trafficking cases were acquitted (see What happens to trafficking cases) [RU0051].
3. **Report to the CID.** The CID's Trafficking in Human Being (THB) Unit leads trafficking investigations nationwide and asks the public to report trafficking, forged visas and smuggling to it [I0357, I0361].
4. **Airport police.** A deputy inspector general of the Airport Armed Police Battalion (APBn) said in August 2026 that it counsels trafficking victims and returnees, tries to get them rehabilitated through BRAC and other organisations, takes and investigates their cases, has brought victims back from Iran, Libya and Greece, and tries to arrest accused people in Bangladesh even when the crime happened abroad [AB0116, AB0117].
5. **Call the Probash-Bondhu call centre** of the Wage Earners' Welfare Board, 24 hours: 16135 free in Bangladesh, +8809610102030 from abroad [I0219].
6. **Apply to the Welfare Board.**
   - **District office:** the Probashi Kallyan Centre is the Board's district office; in Cumilla it began work in July 2023, taking over services the district manpower office used to give, and an application through it for a migrant in trouble abroad gets legal help with the embassy's assistance [AA0231, AA0232].
   - **Membership:** a Board director said in May 2026 that anyone, documented or undocumented, can become a member for Tk 400, and that the Board gives a member who falls ill, is jailed or is in danger abroad legal help through the embassy's labour wing [OA0195, OA0196]. He also said that with a Tk 300 membership a dead worker's body is brought home from any country, Tk 35,000 is paid for burial and transport, a further Tk 3 lakh goes to the heirs, and up to Tk 10 lakh more is paid if there is insurance (see If a worker dies abroad, what does the family get?) [OA0198, OA0199].
   - **Money for illness, injury and children:** a worker who applies because of illness within one year of coming home gets a one-time grant of up to Tk 2 lakh (see What help is there if I'm injured or fall ill abroad?), and a family gets Tk 2,000 a month for up to seven years for each disabled child, for at most two children [OA0197]. A worker injured or maimed abroad gets up to Tk 4 lakh against a Tk 500 premium, and a five-year life insurance policy costs Tk 1,000 [OA0198, OA0199].
   - **Unpaid money abroad:** the Board helps recover dues owed abroad through 22 law firms in 12 countries, and has given more than 239,000 people training, counselling and financial help under its reintegration policy [OA0200, OA0201]. The Cumilla centre's assistant director said the Board helps through the embassy to recover unpaid insurance and compensation, that embassies can appoint lawyers, that safe homes for women workers operate abroad, that there is a dormitory for overnight stays near the airport, and that the Board gives education stipends to migrants' children and an allowance for disabled children [AA0234, AA0235, AA0236].
   - **An example:** on an application by Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation, the Board wrote to the labour wing of the Bangladesh embassy in Bangkok asking it to arrange the release and quick return of 30 Bangladeshis held in Cambodian immigration detention; they flew home on 20 October 2026 [AB0149, AB0150, AB0151].
7. **Complain against an agency to BMET** (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training): a written application with proof to the Director General [I0224]. Director (Emigration): +8802222222415, 01711-135901, diremi@bmet.gov.bd [I0223]. Hotline of OEP (the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system): 09614445271 [I0225].
8. **Complain to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare:** enforcement branch, with proof, your address and the agency's licence (RL) number [I0222]. From abroad, email the complaint cell at minister.expat@gmail.com [I0221].

11. **At the airport on return:** the Welfare Board's desk at Dhaka airport, +88 02 8901040, +880 1971 430203 [I0226]. Returnees from Cambodia and Europe got emergency help, counselling and money to get home there from BRAC's Migration Programme [I0241, I0061]. BRAC's Migration Programme said in March 2025 that 250 to 300 migrant workers a day were coming back empty-handed, without proper clothes or money, and that it gives each Tk 1,000 for the fare home [AA0141].
12. **BRAC Migration Programme:** airport help, counselling, money or materials to restart earning, and legal help, for returnees and trafficking survivors [I0228]. It said in April 2026 that it had helped about 39,000 people at the airport in the previous eight years, and gives returnees reintegration support and training, and then counselling, and is trying to set them up as entrepreneurs [LB0222]. At Dhaka's Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, when a mentally ill worker or one with no address is found, the Probashi Kallyan desk or the Airport Armed Police phone Al-Amin (Nayan), manager of BRAC's Migration Welfare Centre, who by the US State Department's account at its June 2024 TIP (Trafficking in Persons) Hero award had helped at least 34,000 returnees and trafficking victims [CQ0017, CQ0018, CQ0019]. Al-Amin said he was himself trafficked when he went to work in Malaysia, which Prothom Alo reported was in 2007; he came home the same year, started work in BRAC's migration programme in 2017, and is president of Anirban Survivor Voice, the largest network of trafficking survivors in Bangladesh [CQ0020, CQ0021, CQ0022]. Roksana Akter of Bishwanath, Sylhet, abused at her workplace in Saudi Arabia and forced to borrow to come home, said in April 2026 that the EU and BRAC's Prottasha-2 project helped her set up a shop whose income now runs her household [LB0223, LB0224, LB0225].
