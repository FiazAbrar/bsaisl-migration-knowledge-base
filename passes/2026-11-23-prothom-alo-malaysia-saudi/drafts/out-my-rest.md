### Step 1: Find a real job

There are three routes to a job, and all start with OEP registration (Step 1, item 1) [W0027, W0030]:

- **A BOESL circular** (Step 1, item 3): zero-cost by BOESL's account, few places [W0004, W0001].
- **A private agency** (Step 1, item 4): only the 25 processing agencies can process the visa [Z0148].
- **A relative's employer** (Step 1, item 5): still goes through those agencies [Z0148].

Whatever the route, the checks that tell a real job from a fake are Step 1, items 4, 6 and 7, Step 1, "The trap" and Step 1, "Before paying".

1. **Register on the Overseas Employment Platform (OEP),** BMET's website for job-seekers. Agencies must pick workers from BMET's database of registered job-seekers, and BMET clears no one outside it [W0027, W0030].

   You'll need [G0035, B0139, G0001, G0037]:

   - your NID (national ID card);
   - a phone that can receive codes;
   - a bKash, Nagad or other mobile-money account with Tk 200;
   - your passport details, education and work skills.

   1. In Google Chrome, open [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration) [B0139, G0035].
   2. Type your name in English exactly as on your NID (no dots; spaces where the NID has them), your mobile and NID numbers, and your date of birth, division and district as on the NID [G0035, B0139]. Type the captcha numbers, accept the terms and press "যাচাই করুন" (Verify) [G0035].
   3. Type the code (OTP) sent to your phone, press Submit, then "সংরক্ষণ করুন ও এগিয়ে যান" (Save and continue) [B0139].
   4. Fill in the form, then press "পেমেন্ট করতে এগিয়ে যান" (Proceed to payment), "পেমেন্ট করুন" (Pay), Mobile Banking and your service; enter your account number, the code and your PIN to pay the Tk 200 government fee [B0139, G0001].
   5. Download and print your registration card, which carries your BMET number; you can download it again at [oep.gov.bd/employee-card](https://www.oep.gov.bd/employee-card) with that number, without logging in [B0139, G0036, G0038].
   6. Take 2 printed copies of the registration slip to give your fingerprints [G0001] at any DEMO (District Employment and Manpower Office, BMET's district office), TTC (Technical Training Centre) or IMT (Institute of Marine Technology) [D0064]. DEMO addresses, phones and emails: [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e139933eb65569e2af6c) [G0047].

      > **Need to know**

   **Check it is real:** register at employee.oep.gov.bd, the address BMET's district office gives [G0001]; use no middlemen and pay fees only through the proper channel, against a receipt [B0042]. **If it fails:** if "Verify" shows an error, check your name, NID number and date of birth match the NID exactly [B0139]; otherwise call the OEP hotline, 09614445271 [G0036, B0144].

2. **Learn a trade if you need one.** BMET's TTCs teach 55 trades [B0037]; every TTC, with its principal's name, address, phone and email, is listed at [bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd2c933eb65569e13cd6) [G0048, G0049]. Each TTC posts admission notices with courses, minimum education, dates and fees; the Bangladesh-Korea TTC (BKTTC) in Dhaka, for example, posts them at bkttc.dhaka.gov.bd [G0002, G0003]. BKTTC's 3- or 6-month courses (360 or 720 hours), in trades such as welding and plumbing, end with an NSDA (National Skills Development Authority) skills certificate [G0006, G0005].

   You'll need [G0002, G0005, B0140]:

   - your OEP registration;
   - the course's minimum education (at BKTTC, JSC, the Junior School Certificate, for welding);
   - a current, clear photo in your OEP profile.

   1. Log in at [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/login-with-otp) with your mobile number and the code sent to it [B0140].
   2. Open the training portal (ট্রেনিং পোর্টাল), then Training, then Trade/Skills; choose your division, district and centre and press Search [B0140, G0002].
   3. Pick the trade, press Apply, check your photo matches your face now, then press Continue, "Process to Enroll" and Confirm; "My Course" shows your applications [B0140].
   4. Sit the admission test on the date in the notice; BKTTC posts results on its notice board and website and sends them by SMS [G0003].
   5. Pay the fee against a money receipt (BKTTC: Tk 748 for 3 months, Tk 1,484 for 6 months); courses run by funded projects are free [G0003, B0037]. At BKTTC, regular trainees with at least 80% attendance get Tk 150 a day for transport and food when funds are allocated [G0004].

   **Scams:** if anyone offers a TTC place or exam pass for money, tell the nearest law-enforcement officer [B0010]. To complain about a TTC:

   1. Complain to its principal (phone and email on BMET's TTC list, Step 1, item 2) [G0049].
   2. Then complain to BMET's Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 [B0041].

   The government's migrant call centre, 16135, takes calls on any migration problem [B0083].

   **Construction jobs:** Malaysia requires foreign construction workers to hold a construction skills certificate (SKKP) from their own country, checked by CIDB (Malaysia's Construction Industry Development Board), and to finish an online safety and health course (eSICW) [G0007]. The CIDB skill test runs at Khulna TTC (Teligati, Khulna; 041-2870047 or 01716966627; ttckhu@gmail.com) and is to start in Dhaka, Chattogram and Rajshahi [B0001, G0049].

3. **Apply for government (BOESL) jobs.** BOESL posts Malaysia job circulars on [boesl.gov.bd](https://www.boesl.gov.bd/) [Z0003]. Its portal, brms.boesl.gov.bd, has been closed since 11 October 2026 for a technical fault; follow its notice board and [facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd](https://www.facebook.com/boesl.gov.bd/) [W0007, G0059]. Each circular sets its own rules; the example below is its 5 November 2026 zero-cost circular for DHL Supply Chain (Malaysia), for men, at RM1,700 basic pay a month [W0001]. It had two posts [MP0443, MP0444, MP0445]:

   - **Operations Assistant:** at least SSC (Secondary School Certificate); English reading, writing and communication; physical fitness for warehouse work; one to two years' experience; forklift, power pallet truck or reach truck skills a plus.
   - **Technical Skill Workers:** at least HSC (Higher Secondary Certificate) and good knowledge of warehouse network cabling, racking and IT equipment; electrical vocational, polytechnic or degree holders preferred.

   It offered a three-year renewable contract at RM1,700 (about Tk 52,000) a month, eight hours a day, six days a week, with overtime as the company needs and Malaysian labour law allows; the employer provides housing, first aid and transport [MP0446, MP0447].

   You'll need [W0003, G0031, W0006]:

   - a passport valid for at least 18 to 24 months;
   - your OEP registration;
   - your education and experience details;
   - for the interview:
     - 4 colour passport photos on white;
     - colour copies of every passport you have held;
     - original education and experience certificates;
     - your NID;
     - a CV showing computer skills (MS Excel), numeracy, any driving licence and any overseas or warehouse experience.

   1. Check the circular's posts, age limit (22 to 35 in the example), education, skills and window [W0003, Z0033, W0002].
   2. Apply online through the circular's link (in the example, [employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration](https://employee.oep.gov.bd/employee/registration)) with your passport number, education and experience, for one post only, within the window; incomplete or wrong applications are rejected [W0001, G0031, W0002].
   3. A software lottery picks a shortlist, which BOESL posts on its website; check your name there [W0004]. In the example BOESL ran the lottery electronically on 8 November 2026 at the Probashi Kallyan Bhaban auditorium, with Ministry representatives, BOESL officials and journalists present; it picked 300 of 4,029 applicants (200 for Operations Assistant, 100 for Technical Skill Workers) for the final 112 places [MP0451, MP0452, MP0448].
   4. If shortlisted, bring the documents above to the interview, where the employer's representative tests you and chooses [W0004, W0006]; a driving licence and work experience count in your favour [G0033]. In the example a nine-member delegation from Malaysia held practical tests and interviews of the 300 from 12 to 15 November 2026 and chose the final 112; the Ministry said BOESL would not interfere in the selection and the candidates the employer picks are final [MP0455, MP0456, MP0448].

   In the example, workers pay nothing at any stage [W0005]. Even selection does not guarantee a job [G0032]. UN experts said thousands recruited through BOESL paid over five times the official fees [YB0015, A0060]. BOESL has no agents or sub-agents, never takes cash and takes money only after final selection, by bank pay order; gangs use its name to sell Malaysia jobs [W0068]. BOESL's managing director said on 12 November 2026 that if any broker, middleman or unauthorised person tries to take money at any stage of the recruitment, or any candidate takes part in an unethical transaction, strict legal action will be taken against both on proof [MP0457]. Office: Probashi Kallyan Bhaban (5th floor), 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka; 02-58311838, 02-48319125, 02-48317515 or 01765411653; info@boesl.gov.bd [W0006, W0068, G0034].

4. **Or go through a private agency.** Only agencies on Malaysia's list can recruit: open [fwcms.com.my/affiliates](https://fwcms.com.my/affiliates/#recruitment-agencies), choose Recruitment Agencies, then Bangladesh [W0008, Z0144].

   You'll need: the agency's name and RL number (the recruiting licence number every licensed Bangladeshi agency has) [X0001].

   1. Find the agency on the FWCMS list [W0008].
   2. On BMET's list, [oep.gov.bd/agencies](https://www.oep.gov.bd/agencies), check the same RL number shows status Active (not Expired, Cancelled or Suspended) and an unexpired validity date; note its registered address and office phone [X0001, X0002, Z0035].
   3. Go to the agency at that address, not to a middleman's office [X0001, B0042].
   4. Check the demand letter, contract, job details, salary and migration cost [B0002] against the permission letter (Step 1, item 6).

   **What happens in practice:** for overcharging and ghost jobs, see [What you pay, and what you earn and keep](#what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep), Which employer, and Step 1, "The trap" [W0072, A0001]. Some workers arrive with valid work permits to find no job (After you arrive, item 1) [A0095, A0014].

   **Which agencies.** Only BOESL and the 25 processing agencies can process the calling visa and e-visa; the 312 associates cannot send workers directly (see The processing agency: the toll gate) [Z0144, Z0148, W0008, YC0030]. In BMET's records from January 2004 to 23 August 2026, only eight of the 25 had sent workers to Malaysia, 1,612 between them; the other 17 sent none [YC0001, YC0002, A0027]. Five of the eight sent 11, 74, 100, 150 and 202 workers, and the other three 436, 333 and 306 [MB0029, MB0030]. FWCMS and OEP give different addresses for several of the 25, including these; check the RL number on OEP [YC0027, YC0019, YC0013, YC0021, YC0028, YC0023, YC0029, YC0026]:

   - Rifa International: Banani on FWCMS, Purana Paltan on OEP;
   - The Anchor Care: Savar DOHS on FWCMS, Dhamrai on OEP;
   - Brothers Trading & Contracting: Banani on FWCMS, Shantinagar on OEP;
   - Goodness Service: Banani on FWCMS, Naya Paltan on OEP.

   The eight, and anything reported about them:

   - **General Trading Company (RL0320)** and **Rifa International (RL0693):** among 57 agencies the ACC asked BMET about on 23 July 2026, in a case alleging illegal wealth from trafficking women abroad with forged documents [YC0005, YC0006, YC0018, YC0019].
   - **United Gulf Services (RL1036):** Prothom Alo (September 2026) reports its owner, Laila Arzuman Banu, is the wife of Abdul Hai, whose Greenland Overseas was in the earlier syndicate and is now run by their daughter, Rehana Arzuman Hai; neither answered Prothom Alo's calls [YC0004, YC0020, MB0031, MB0032]; Greenland's owners are among those the ACC charged in September 2025 over Malaysia fees [YC0007].

   - **Al-Shupto Overseas (RL0580)**, **Brothers Trading & Contracting (RL0671)**, **Country Employment Agency (RL1197)** and **Neighbour Associates (RL0746)**: nothing reported against them [YC0002, YC0022, YC0023, YC0024, YC0025].

   The 17 without a Malaysia record, as Prothom Alo printed them: Tania Trade, Satkhira International, A-Plus, Al Hayat Overseas, Valley Trade, Khan Jahan Ali Overseas, Bengal Human Resource, Earth Smart, At Taqwa, Motherland, Mangrove, Jewel Rinku Enterprise, Bangladesh One Overseas, Annesha Air, Chandpur International, Godness Services and Bhaluka Overseas [MB0027, MB0028]. Bangladesh One Overseas Limited had taken clearance for 795 workers in all, none for Malaysia [MB0029, MB0030]. Bhaluka Overseas, licensed in 2022, had taken clearance for 235 workers up to 23 August 2026, none for Malaysia; its proprietor, Md Abu Sohag Khan, told Prothom Alo he had earlier sent more than a thousand workers to Malaysia in the names of various other agencies, was selected automatically from Malaysia and paid no one for it [MB0024, MB0025, MB0026].

5. **Or through a relative or friend in Malaysia.** A relative can find you the job, but he can't send you himself. His employer applies for a visa in your name, with your passport copy and photo [W0055]; the trade calls this an "own visa" (নিজস্ব ভিসা). The recruiting agency and the processing agency (Step 1, item 4) still handle it: the recruiting agency holds the demand letter, must pick you from BMET's database and gets your BMET clearance under its own licence; the processing agency (Step 1, item 4) does the calling visa and e-visa and gives the recruiting agency a no-objection certificate [W0030, Z0145, Z0147, Z0149, Z0148]. Employers can't name an associate agency as the approved agency, though they were reported in September 2026 to be getting the right to name the agency of their choice [YC0030, A0005, A0007]. In 2022-24 an agency outside the list running a named worker's case had to hold his power of attorney while a listed agency did the "visa handling" [RF0037].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Will BMET clear a worker named by the employer whom the recruiting agency did not find itself?
   > - Does the agency then charge less?

   **Check you fit.** The age and sector rules (see Is it open now, and who can go?) apply, and the job must be in a sector the employer's own quota covers [W0041]. New quotas were frozen from March 2023, and the High Commission began attesting new demand letters again only in October 2026 [YD0013, A0035].

   You'll need:

   - from the relative, photos of his own i-Kad and PLKS sticker;
   - the employer's full name, Malaysian registration number and work address;
   - the job and the basic monthly pay, which must be at least RM1,700 (about Tk 52,000), not counting overtime [Z0137, L0130, Z0142, YI0024];
   - the names and RL numbers of both agencies;
   - a photocopy of the attested demand letter [X0001, U0044].

   1. **Check the relative's own employer.** The i-Kad (foreign worker identity card) must be carried at all times, so he has it [L0128]. The employer on it should be the company he says he works for and the employer on your offer. Some legal workers are on an outsourcing company's books and moved between client companies, or made to work informally for other firms, and only find out after arriving [L0082, YG0026]. Have the relative buy the employer's SSM Company Profile (RM15.40, Step 1, item 7) and photograph the business at the address it gives [W0067, G0053].

      > **Need to know**
      > - Do the i-Kad and PLKS sticker print the employer's name?

   2. **Register in BMET's database** on OEP and give your fingerprints at a DEMO, as in Step 1, item 1; the fee is Tk 200 [B0139, G0001, W0040].
   3. **Check both agencies.** Find each on the FWCMS list and check its RL number shows Active on OEP (Step 1, item 4) [W0008, X0001]. Ask whose name is on your power of attorney; the agency must hand it and the demand letter to BMET before clearance [RF0037, W0030].
   4. **Have the demand letter checked.** The agency keeps the original, which covers a batch of workers [W0030, A0035]. Ask for a photocopy showing the High Commission's attestation stamp, the employer, the number of workers and the agency it is addressed to; no unattested demand letter is accepted [U0044]. Take it with the RL number to your DEMO or BMET, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka: the check is free, by hand (Malaysia is not on BMET's online visa check) and takes 3 working days [B0005, B0032].

   5. **Wait for LULUS on MyIMMs.** Have the relative get the employer's company registration number and the application number, and check the status (Step 3, item 2) [W0065]. If he can't or won't get them, stop. Only the employer or its own appointed representative may deal with Immigration, and using an agent is an offence, so a relative who says he or "his agent" is handling Immigration is not describing the real process [J0061].
   6. **Don't hand over your passport ("submitting the book", বই জমা) or pay for a medical before the visa papers exist.** Brokers and agencies have taken passports and money with a promise of the medical; the Ministry says hand neither over without confirming approval [D0008, Z0161, RE0027]. The agency takes the VDR (calling visa) papers to the medical centre [D0009]. The medical is Tk 8,000 and no more, at any FWCMS-listed centre you choose; no centre belongs to an agency, and the MiGRAMS slip is free [D0001, D0002, W0009].
   7. **Pay in stages, against proof.**
      - *The agency's share:* only by cheque, bank draft or pay order into its own account, against a receipt [B0003, B0042]. A permission letter lets the agency take its money only once the visa and flight are confirmed [W0028]. Get the contract and its BMET-attested Bangla translation before clearance ("Before paying") [W0030].
      - *Money to the relative:* the Ministry warns against paying before approval is confirmed [Z0161], so pay in stages:

        1. Nothing large before LULUS shows on MyIMMs [W0065].
        2. The next part after the visa is issued [W0064].
        3. The rest after BMET clearance [Z0149].

        For every payment, get a dated note signed by the relative, with the amount and what it is for, and a witness's signature; a village court hears only claims backed by a receipt or other document, and without one a BMET complaint rests on your word [G0018, A0079]. Pay by bank transfer or mobile banking to his account in Bangladesh, never by hundi, which is illegal and leaves no record [H0045, B0007, G0014].

   **What it costs.** The cap, the market price and where the money goes are in Background (see [What you pay, and what you earn and keep](#what-you-pay-and-what-you-earn-and-keep)). If the employer's agency is not one of the 25, one of them takes a fee on top, alleged at RM5,500 (about Tk 1.68 lakh) in 2026 [A0026].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What does a relative in Malaysia charge in 2026 for a place on his employer's quota?
   > - How does that payment split between the boss, the relative and the two agencies?

   **After you land.** If you fail the FOMEMA medical (Malaysia's foreign worker health check) within 30 days, the employer sends you home on a Check Out Memo (After you arrive, item 2) [M0223], so put in the relative's signed note what he repays if that happens.

   **If it fails.** Taking money on a false promise of a job abroad is a crime in Bangladesh for anyone, relatives included: up to 5 years' jail and a fine of at least Tk 1 lakh, tried by a magistrate [RG0001, RG0003, RG0004]. The Tk 3 lakh security that compensates workers covers only a sub-agent registered with BMET, not a relative [YB0007]. Where to go:

   - BMET's online form, against a named person: it accepts "a person" and takes receipts and recordings as evidence [G0023].
   - The Ministry (see If an agency or broker takes your money, item 3); the 2013 Act requires an investigation within 30 working days [RG0006, RG0007].
   - A civil suit for compensation, which the 2013 Act allows above the village court's Tk 3 lakh limit [G0018, RG0005].
   - Free legal advice and the village court (see If an agency or broker takes your money, item 4).

   If you land and there is no job, see After you arrive, item 1.

   > **Need to know**
   > - How has a Bangladeshi court or BMET enforced an order against a relative who lives in Malaysia?

   **The zero-cost alternative** is BOESL's circulars (Step 1, item 3) [W0001, B0141]. Malaysia's own direct-hiring platform, TURAP, was approved by its Cabinet in April 2026 but has not launched [RE0023]. Malaysia's Human Resources Minister, Ramanan Ramakrishnan, proposed it to stop the exploitation of Bangladeshi workers at village level; it is built by Bestinet, the company that built FWCMS, and some stakeholders in Bangladesh and Malaysia suspect it is another way to keep recruitment concentrated in a few firms, Prothom Alo reported from The Edge on 16 June 2026 [MP0356, MP0357, Z0001].

6. **Find and check the permission letter for your job.** For each Malaysia job order the Ministry or BMET issues a permission letter naming the agency and its RL number, the employer, the jobs, salary, age range, hours, food, housing, airfare, contract length and the most the agency may take from each worker [W0026, W0028, W0029, W0033, W0038]. As of 7 November 2026, BMET's list had no Malaysia permission letter from the 2026 reopening; the newest was dated 27 February 2025 [G0030]. So for a 2026 job this check can't be done online yet [G0030].

   You'll need [W0026]:

   - the agency's RL number;
   - the employer's name and salary the agency gave you.

   1. Open [bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos](https://bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos) and search "Malaysia" (or মালয়েশিয়া) [W0026].
   2. Open the entry with your agency's RL number; titles give the letter number and RL number, like "DN25-225 RL1274" [W0026].
   3. Compare its employer, salary and maximum fee with what the agency told you [W0029, W0033, W0038]. Its English "Recruitment Permission Letter" page shows a permission ID, the block visa number (Malaysia's approval reference for the group of visas), the visa issue date and the employer's name and city; check they match your visa and demand letter [W0032, Z0005].
   4. Take the demand letter or visa (if stamped, with a copy of the passport page) to your DEMO or BMET's head office, 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000; BMET checks it free within 3 working days [B0005, B0032, B0033, G0026]. If it is fake, complain as under Step 1, "If an agency or broker takes your money".

7. **Check the employer exists** through SSM e-Info, the online shop of Malaysia's Companies Commission (SSM, Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia) [W0067, G0053].

   You'll need [G0055, G0056]:

   - the employer's full name or Malaysian registration number from the demand letter;
   - an email address;
   - a credit or debit card or Malaysian online banking (FPX).

   1. Register and log in at [ssm-einfo.my](https://www.ssm-einfo.my/) [G0055].
   2. Top up your account; card and FPX payments clear instantly [G0056].
   3. Search the name or number and buy the Company Profile, RM15.40 [G0055, W0067].
   4. Save the PDF at once; it stays in Transaction History for only 7 days [G0057].
   5. Check the status, registered and business addresses and nature of business [G0053]; "Existing" means not being wound up, struck off or dissolved [G0054]. Compare name and address with the demand letter.

   **If it fails:** if you can't get the document, call the e-Info call centre, 1300 30 4636 [G0058].

   > **Need to know**
   > - What can a worker do if the company can't be found on SSM e-Info?

**How long.** In order:

1. The employer gets its quota approved by the Human Resources Ministry's One Stop Centre and pays the levy [W0041, L0068].
2. The High Commission in Kuala Lumpur attests the demand letter [U0044].
3. The agency selects you from BMET's database [W0030].
4. The employer applies for your VDR through FWCMS, which IMI aims to approve in 5 working days [W0045, C0045].
5. The medical [D0009, W0009].
6. The High Commission of Malaysia in Dhaka issues the visa [W0064].
7. BMET clears you in 3 working days [B0026].
8. The flight, paid by the employer [W0036].

These times are the fastest possible, not promises; delays raise the price (see How it is paid) [YF0036].

**What you get back** (Step 3; Step 6) [W0065, W0064, Z0149]:

- a calling visa (VDR) in your name for the named employer, shown as LULUS (approved) on MyIMMs;
- the visa issued in Dhaka;
- BMET clearance through the recruiting agency.

**The trap.**
- *Resold on arrival.* Thirty-six men who went with a contract naming their employer were each "sold" to another company for about Tk 2 lakh, at under half the promised pay [RE0012].
- *A-to-B (এটুবি) without telling you.* An A-to-B, "free" or "agent" visa means the company that brings you gives you no work [RE0017]. Agents put new workers on one without saying so, and a worker can't tell it from a direct-company visa [RE0020, RE0019]. Working for anyone but the employer that applied is illegal [W0052].
- *The free visa (ফ্রি ভিসা).* What it is and what it costs are in Background (see [Company visa, agent visa, free visa](#company-visa-agent-visa-free-visa)). A "renewal" through an agent can be a fake PLKS sticker: one ring sold them for RM5,000 to 6,000 [YG0024]. Getting a pass by a false statement or holding a forged one is an offence [K0030]. When renewals stop, you are undocumented: detained, deported at your own or your family's cost, and blacklisted from Malaysia, usually for 1 to 5 years, with your biometrics on file [H0012, H0015, H0020].
- *Passports held for ransom.* On arrival, agency people have taken passports "for the visa" and asked Tk 70,000 to 1 lakh to return them [RE0013]. What the employer may do with your passport, and the penalty, are in After you arrive, item 4. In Bangladesh, anyone keeping a migrant's passport without lawful reason commits an offence under the 2013 Act [RG0002].

**Before paying:**

- **Know what you should pay.** No cap is set for 2026, and the old one was not followed (see The cap; What people really pay) [YD0007, G0012]. The 2022 cap covered everything the worker pays in Bangladesh (passport, medical, registration, welfare fee, insurance, smart card, pre-departure training, clothing, agency service charge and other costs); the employer paid the security deposit, pass, insurance and air tickets [R0008, R0009]. Your job's permission letter states its maximum, in some letters nothing [W0026, W0034]. The agency must display in writing at its office the migration cost fixed for your destination and the monthly wage [YB0003]; compare both with your permission letter (Step 1, item 6).
- **Pay only by cheque, bank draft or pay order into the agency's own bank account, and keep every receipt** [W0028, B0003]. Agencies and middlemen have started collecting passports from people who want to go [G0011]; on 10 September 2026 a BMET mobile court sealed Triple A Overseas (RL-2630), fined it Tk 50,000 and seized passports it had collected with promises of Malaysian visas [A0043]; the expatriates' welfare secretary said on 16 September that three boxes of passports were seized [MP0433]. The Ministry has warned against paying anything before the Malaysia process is finalised, or handing over a passport or money without confirming approval [A0031, Z0161].
- **If a local broker brings you the job.** An agency may use only a sub-agent registered with BMET, and must list its registered sub-agents on its own website [YB0001, YB0002]. A registered sub-agent has an office with a signboard showing his name, registration number and date and the agency's name and licence number, and his BMET registration certificate on display inside [YB0005, YB0006]. The agency and the sub-agent are both liable for what he does, and a worker cheated by a sub-agent is compensated from the Tk 3 lakh security he lodges with the agency [YB0004, YB0007].

  > **Need to know**
  > - Has BMET, any DEMO or any agency yet published a list of registered sub-agents [YB0006]?

- **Get the contract and a Bangla translation before you go.** The agency must sign a contract with you before BMET clearance and give you a Bangla translation attested by BMET [W0030]; check the contract is attested by the destination country's embassy [B0002]. Keep your copy: a wage claim in Malaysia asks for it [M0113], and some employers swap it on arrival for one the worker can't read [J0070].

  > **Need to know**
  > - How does a worker check the BMET attestation on the contract?
  > - What can a worker do if the agency won't hand over the contract?

- **If you need to borrow,** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB), the government bank for migrant workers, lends up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral at 9% simple interest, repaid monthly over up to 3 years, and decides within 7 working days [B0017]. It lends only once you have a valid visa and does not arrange visas [G0039].

**Expect slow, partial recovery.** BMET cases take 4 months to a year, and in August 2023 the average award was about Tk 74,000 against Tk 3.5 to 5 lakh paid; workers with full documents get more back [A0073, A0076, A0077]. Keep proof of every payment from the start (Step 1, "Before paying") [W0028, B0003].

   You'll need:

   - a copy of your NID or passport [G0014, G0023];

   - your complaint ID;

3. **Or complain straight to the Ministry.** It is free; action starts within 3 to 5 working days, both sides are heard, and it is meant to be settled within 30 working days [B0044, B0045].

   You'll need [B0044]:

   - an application;

For advice, call the 24-hour migrant call centre, 16135 (+880 9610102030 from abroad) [B0083].

### Step 2: Medical check in Bangladesh (Bio-Medical), once your VDR is approved

- **28 August 2026:** the Ministry said Bangladesh had asked to change the approved medical centres, a joint delegation of Malaysia's health, human resources and home ministries had inspected every centre in Bangladesh, and Malaysia had decided to start with the remaining 16 centres on its existing list after dropping, at Bangladesh's request, controversial centres linked to the previous government [MP0423, MP0424].

### Scams

#### Done to workers

The main scams, each covered where it happens:

- overcharging and ghost jobs (Step 1, item 4; Step 1, "Before paying") [W0072, A0001];
- A-to-B and free visas, resale on arrival, and passports held for ransom (Step 1, "The trap") [RE0017, RE0012, RE0013];
- unauthorised medicals that take passports and money (Step 2) [Z0161, D0022];
- fake "zero-cost" or BOESL places: BOESL has no agents and takes no cash (Step 1, item 3) [W0068];
- fake tickets and last-minute ticket money (Step 6, item 4) [I0012, I0013].

Complain in Bangladesh as under Step 1, "If an agency or broker takes your money", and in Malaysia to the labour wing by email [L0021]. Other scams:

- **Social media posts on Malaysia-approved agencies** have been false [W0021]; check the FWCMS list instead (Step 1, item 4) [W0008].
- **The fee video.** Agencies make workers record a video at their Dhaka office saying they paid only the official fee when they paid far more [J0060]; keep your own proof of every payment [G0014].
- **Fake passes and permit "agents".** Rings sell fake e-PLKS (electronic work pass) copies for about RM150 and fake police reports for about RM70 [D0045]; a Bangladeshi-run ring in Kulim, Kedah, sold fake work permits and fake UNHCR cards for RM80 to 250 [N0049]. Only your employer deals with your pass; anyone else offering is breaking the law [J0062, J0061, L0079]. Check it yourself (Step 5) [W0066].
- **Sabah jobs.** There is no agreement for recruiting Bangladeshis to Sabah; rings have taken passports and cash for Sabah jobs [N0029, A0102].
- **Trafficking rings inside Malaysia.** Malaysia's Immigration Department said that in a coordinated operation at three places in Cheras, Kuala Lumpur, on the night of 11 December 2024 it rescued 11 Bangladeshis aged 30 to 38 from a trafficking ring believed to be led by three Bangladeshis, and arrested three main suspects aged 35 to 38 and a 32-year-old Bangladeshi woman with one of them [MP0255, MP0256, MP0257]. It said it seized 17 Bangladeshi passports, 20 mobile phones, RM800 and USD 800 in cash and two cars, that the victims had been trafficked and exploited by the syndicate for financial gain, and that the rescued were in its care while the investigation continued; its Director General, Datuk Zakaria Shaban, confirmed the rescue of the 11 [MP0258, MP0259].
- **"Free" Malaysia offers that end at the sea.** In November 2026 a young man from Feni was added to a WhatsApp group offering to take him to Malaysia free of cost, paying only after arrival, and ended up held by traffickers in the Teknaf hills awaiting a boat, he told Prothom Alo [MP0449, MP0450].
- **No job on arrival.** Employers sign a High Commission commitment to put workers to work at once; if broken, email the labour wing (After you arrive, item 1) [W0059, L0021].

#### Tried by workers

Forged visas, passes and passports carry jail and caning (see What can get you jailed or deported), including passports bought to resit a failed FOMEMA medical [L0088, L0089, D0035, N0035].

#### Hours, rest and leave

**Hours and overtime**

At most 8 hours a day and 45 a week, no more than 5 hours in a row without a 30-minute break, and never over 12 hours a day except in emergencies [M0003, M0004, L0092]. Overtime is only at the employer's request with your agreement, paid at least 1.5 times the hourly rate (for monthly pay: the monthly wage divided by 26, then by your normal daily hours) [L0134, M0001, M0007].

**Rest days and holidays**

One rest day a week, on which you can't be made to work except in continuous shift work or emergencies [L0135, M0010]. Work on it within normal hours pays, on monthly pay, half a day's pay for up to half the normal hours and a full day's for more, on top of the monthly wage, and on daily pay one or two days' wages; beyond normal hours, at least 2 times the hourly rate [M0012, M0011, M0013]. 11 paid public holidays a year; working one earns 2 extra days' wages, and overtime that day at least 3 times the hourly rate [M0014, M0019, M0020]. Absence without consent on the working day before or after a holiday loses that holiday's pay unless you have a reasonable excuse [M0018].

**Eid in practice**

Migrants told Prothom Alo in June 2024 that a large share of workers in Malaysia get no holiday even on Eid day and go to work instead of to Eid prayers; Md Raihan Kashem, six to seven years in Malaysia and working at a supershop in Malacca, said he gets no leave on Eid and has never in any year been given time to pray [MP0108, MP0109]. On Eid day 2024 a migrant, Iftekhar Ahmed, wrote in the Facebook group "Malaysia Kajer Khobor" (Malaysia work news) that he was going to duty from 10 am to 10 pm and had been refused a single day off, and another, Md Ashraful Ahmed, replied that he worked from 6 am to 8 pm [MP0110, MP0111].

**Leave**

Paid annual leave is 8, 12 or 16 days a year (under 2, 2 to 5, over 5 years' service) [L0109]; paid sick leave 14, 18 or 22 days on the same bands, plus up to 60 days if a doctor certifies hospital care [M0023, M0239]; it needs a doctor's certificate and notice to the employer within 48 hours, or it counts as absence [M0025]. Maternity leave is 98 days [L0112]. A married man gets 7 days' paid paternity leave per birth, up to 5 births, after 12 months' service, if he tells the employer at least 30 days before the expected birth [L0095].

#### How long you can stay and renewing

**12 months at a time, up to 10 years**

The PLKS lasts 12 months [L0044]. Only the employer renews it, from 3 months before expiry, and pays any late penalty [L0079, L0139]. Renewal needs a passport valid over 12 months, a FOMEMA medical every year, and SPIKPA hospital insurance (except in plantations) [L0047, Z0130, M0220]. A new 48-page, 5-year e-passport from the High Commission in Kuala Lumpur costs a worker RM172 (RM140 fee plus a RM32 ESKL charge for the appointment, enrolment and delivery) [Z0020]; Bangladesh missions charge workers USD 50 for 10 years (express USD 45 for 5 years, USD 75 for 10) [D0052]. A renewal filed after expiry counts as overstay [L0045]. The limit is 10 years in total, or 3 for workers registered under the 6P legalisation programme [L0050, M0229, M0157].

**Renewing your passport in Malaysia**

- **Late 2024 backlog.** Asif Nazrul, then the interim government's law adviser, said on 20 November 2024 that he hoped delivery of renewed passports to migrant workers in Malaysia and other countries would start from 10 December, that officials had told him foil paper for printing passports was being brought in, and that passport problems affected many Middle Eastern countries too [MP0243, MP0244, MP0245]. He said there was no crisis in issuing e-passports, but that migrant workers want MRP (machine-readable) passports rather than e-passports; the MRP is outdated, and the national aim is to move from MRP to e-passports [MP0246].
- **At the passport office.** A Bangladeshi who had lived 21 years in Malaysia wrote in Prothom Alo in November 2024 that migrants coming for services at the office in charge of e-passports in Malaysia were perhaps being lightly struck by security guards of the private company handling passports while many crowded and pushed; he guessed, without knowing for sure, that the pushing may have been the reason [MP0247, MP0249, MP0250]. He wrote that because Bangladesh missions expect migrants to know all the rules and have no time to explain them, many migrants hire brokers and pay extra to avoid harassment over passports, travel passes and document attestation [MP0251, MP0252].
- **Complaints about ESKL.** In a 13-point proposal handed to the foreign adviser on 21 May 2025, the AB Party (Amar Bangladesh Party) demanded that passport renewal be completed within seven days with courier delivery, and that the corruption and syndicate "in the name of business" at ESKL in Malaysia be stopped and the service decentralised under the High Commission's control [MP0563, MP0564].

**If the employer doesn't renew,** you risk arrest [K0015]. Check your pass on Immigration's ePass check (Step 5) [W0066]; if it is near expiry and unrenewed, email the labour wing and contact Tenaganita (If you aren't paid) [L0021, J0007]. In June 2026 about 35 Aerofoam Manufacturing workers who had entered Malaysia legally had no valid PLKS because the company did not renew it on time, leaving them in fear of arrest or fines; their written complaint to the Labour Department (Jabatan Tenaga Kerja, JTK) asked that the 35 workers' visas and permits be regularised quickly [MP0335, MP0336].

#### Going home on leave and coming back

The yearly PLKS renewal includes a multiple-entry visa for Bangladeshi workers, with no separate application [H0027, H0028]. Agree your return date with the employer before you go; missing it counts as running away [L0058, M0214].

**How the multiple-entry visa came in.** Asif Nazrul, the interim government's adviser for expatriates' welfare, said on 15 July 2025 that a circular signed on behalf of Malaysia's Immigration Director General and issued on 10 July 2025 gave Bangladeshi workers multiple-entry visas [MP0600, MP0601, MP0602]:

- workers already issued a single-entry visa and a PLKS (temporary employment visit pass) before the circular need not apply [MP0600, MP0601, MP0602];
- the multiple-entry visa is issued automatically when the PLKS is renewed [MP0600, MP0601, MP0602];
- workers with a single-entry visa and a valid PLKS can travel home and back without a new multiple visa [MP0600, MP0601, MP0602];
- instructions were sent to all of Malaysia's international airports and land ports [MP0600, MP0601, MP0602].

He said that of the 15 countries Malaysia takes workers from, only Bangladeshi workers had been given single-entry rather than multiple-entry visas before then, causing them much hardship [MP0603]. Chief Adviser Muhammad Yunus, head of the interim government, had asked Malaysia's High Commissioner in Dhaka, Mohd Shuhada Othman, at a meeting reported on 13 January 2025, to give Bangladeshi workers multiple-entry visas so that they could go home easily when needed [MP0268, MP0269]; on 15 May 2025 Asif Nazrul said he had asked Malaysia's home minister for them, and the minister told his officials to act quickly [MP0549, MP0550]. The Ministry's first-year report of August 2025 said a multiple-entry visa had been introduced for Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia for the first time [MP0628].

**The fare.** In June 2026 the civil aviation minister, Afroza Khanom, told parliament that Biman Bangladesh Airlines had introduced a low-fare "T-class" ticket for migrant workers, bringing the Dhaka-Kuala Lumpur fare down from Tk 28,000 to Tk 21,000 [MP0365, MP0366, MP0367].

### What can get you jailed or deported

#### No valid pass or passport

Overstaying an expired or cancelled pass: fine of at least RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, or a compound (out-of-court fine) up to RM3,000 [K0015, R0004, L0086]. Entering illegally, or failing to show a valid passport and pass when asked: fine up to RM10,000, up to 5 years' jail or both, and up to 6 strokes of the cane, or a compound up to RM3,000 [L0083, L0084]. Carry your i-Kad at all times [L0128].

A pass ends when the employer doesn't renew it (Your residence permit), when Immigration cancels it after the employer reports you absent (If your employer reports you absent (lari)), or at once when you break a pass condition, such as working for anyone but the employer named on the pass [K0015, J0035, K0025, L0055]. PLKS holders may not work as frontliners (customer-facing staff) [L0054].

**Raids and detentions, in date order:**

- **August 2025:** Malaysia's Home Ministry said 790 Bangladeshis who had overstayed their visas or whose work permits had expired had been detained [MP0647].
- **20 November 2025:** Immigration raided commercial areas, construction sites and vegetable farms in four areas of Cameron Highlands, Pahang, checked 1,886 foreigners and arrested 468, 174 of them Bangladeshi, for offences such as expired passports and no travel documents; some showed temporary work permits that appeared fake, Immigration's Director General, Zakaria Shaaban, said [MP0715, MP0716, MP0717]. He said intelligence showed Cameron Highlands had become an attractive work centre for foreigners, drawn by its hills and distance from towns, that local businesses often prefer to hire them, and that the raids followed complaints from local people [MP0720, MP0721, MP0722].
- **December 2025:** citing Malaysian media, Prothom Alo reported that 182 Bangladeshis were detained in November alone and 377 in one raid in September, and that Bangladeshis are detained in almost every Malaysian Immigration raid and are later sent home [MP0731].

#### After arrest

Someone whose pass has expired or been cancelled is removed whether or not prosecuted, held as long as removal takes, with no appeal [K0018, K0020, K0019]. On entering an Immigration depot your biometrics are taken and you are blacklisted [H0015]. Depots have held more people than their capacity, and former detainees describe medical care denied or delayed [F0056, F0092]. Those aged 20 to 54 among the people arrested in the November 2025 Cameron Highlands raid were to be sent to depots in Kelantan, Perak and Selangor; Immigration figures showed 83,994 foreigners detained across Malaysia from the start of 2025 to 18 November [MP0718, MP0719]. Family can call the High Commission's consular line, +603 2604 0949, or email the labour wing; its officials visit camps [B0118, L0021, H0023]. For visiting a detainee and the flight home, see If you are detained.

> **Need to know**
> - Does Immigration's permission letter to visit a detainee cost anything?
> - How long does Immigration take to issue the permission letter to visit a detainee?

#### False papers

Making, forging or using a forged visa or pass: fine of at least RM30,000, 5 to 10 years' jail, up to 6 strokes [L0088]. A false passport: RM15,000 to RM100,000, 2 to 10 years, up to 6 strokes [L0089]. Letting someone else hold your passport, intending or knowing it may be used by another person, is also an offence [K0047].

#### Drugs

**Carrying drugs in or out.** Morphine, heroin, opium, ganja and other dangerous drugs may not be brought in or taken out [L0155]. Trafficking means death or life imprisonment, plus at least 12 strokes if not sentenced to death [J0052, K0041, L0155]. Never carry a packet for someone else if you don't know what is inside [B0006].

**Testing positive at the FOMEMA medical.** The FOMEMA urine test checks for opiates, methamphetamine and cannabis (see After you arrive, item 2) [Z0135]. A worker FOMEMA finds unfit gets no work pass and is sent home with a Check-Out Memo [L0043].

> **Need to know**

> - What are the penalties under the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952 for having or using a small amount of drugs (not trafficking), and is a foreign worker convicted of it deported?

#### Militancy and terrorism charges

**What was alleged.** Malaysia's home minister, Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, said on 27 June 2025 that police had arrested 36 Bangladeshis on allegations of direct involvement in a "radical militant movement", in three phases of operations in Selangor and Johor from 24 April 2025 [MP0571, MP0572, MP0573]. He said police found the group, inspired by Islamic State ideology, had formed a cell to spread extremism within its own community, radicalise people, raise money for terrorist activities and work to overthrow the legitimate government of its home country [MP0574, MP0575]. Malaysia's police chief, Khalid Ismail, said on 4 July 2025, citing intelligence, that the ring targeted other Bangladeshi workers to recruit members and spread extremist ideology through social media and online messaging platforms, that those detained sent money to Islamic State in Syria and Bangladesh, and that 100 to 150 people might be involved [MP0576, MP0577, MP0578]. He said all those detained had been working in factories, construction and services in Malaysia [MP0585].

**What happened to those arrested:**

- **Malaysia's account (27 June 2025):** five were charged under 6A of Malaysia's Penal Code, which applies to terrorism-related offences, at the Shah Alam and Johor Bahru sessions courts; removal orders were issued for 15; 16 were still under investigation [MP0571, MP0572, MP0573]. The police chief said those less involved would be sent home and those deeply involved dealt with under Malaysian law [MP0576, MP0577, MP0578].
- **The High Commission's account (July 2025):** sources at the Bangladesh High Commission in Kuala Lumpur said the Royal Malaysia Police counter-terrorism division had arrested 35, not 36, Bangladeshis between April and June 2025; 14 had overstayed their visas and were handed to immigration, six of whom had been sent back to Bangladesh; five were charged with terrorism offences; 16 were under further investigation [MP0586, MP0587, MP0588].
- **In prison:** the five charged were held in two prisons, two in Selangor and three in Johor Bahru; High Commission officials were due to visit them on 9 July 2025, after the prison authorities gave permission, to learn whether they needed legal or other basic help [MP0589, MP0590, MP0591].
- **Back in Bangladesh:** Asif Nazrul, the interim government's adviser for expatriates' welfare, said on the night of 4 July 2025 that three of the arrested workers had already returned and were being questioned, that everyone returned on this allegation would be questioned, and that those against whom proof of involvement was found would be put on trial [MP0579, MP0580, MP0581].

**What the High Commission could do.** Bangladesh's foreign ministry said on 5 July 2025 that the High Commission contacted Malaysian authorities right after the arrests, that Bangladesh had formally asked for the identities of those held and the charges against them, that it would cooperate fully with Malaysia's investigation, and that the High Commission would give Bangladeshis the help they needed [MP0582, MP0583, MP0584]. A diplomatic source said in July 2025 that the High Commission had contacted Malaysia's foreign, home and human resources ministries from the start for help over the 35 but had not received the expected response, and had not been told at the time that a counter-terrorism operation was under way [MP0592, MP0593].

Obscene pictures or films on any media, daggers, flick knives and cloth printed with Quran verses may not be brought in; declare anything dutiable and expect your bags to be opened [L0158, L0159, L0160, L0156].

#### If you aren't paid

**Complain through any of these four routes, alone or several at once:** the WFW app, the High Commission's labour wing, the Labour Court and, from Bangladesh, the Ministry. In Sabah and Sarawak, go to the state's own Labour Department instead (In Sabah and Sarawak, at the end of this section) [L0163, L0021, M0232, B0048, YB0013]. For free help, see Free help in Malaysia [J0007].

Photograph your contract, payslips, attendance records, passport and pass, and note the employer's registered name and address, from SSM e-Info if you don't have them (Step 1, item 7) [M0113, J0046, W0067]. Without a written contract you are still presumed an employee if the other person controls your work or hours, provides tools, or pays you regularly as most of your income [M0111]. If you work for a subcontractor, the main contractor is also liable for up to 3 months' unpaid wages if you complain within 90 days of the due date [M0100]. If the employer seems about to run off to avoid paying, complain to a Magistrate, who can require a bond or order arrest [M0110].

#### If you want to change employer

**You can't apply yourself**

PLKS holders may not change employer or sector on their own, and the agreement contract bars it [L0055, W0052, L0146]. Only the employer or its appointed company representative deals with Immigration; using an agent is an offence [J0062, J0061].

**When it is allowed**

Only if all of these hold:

- the employer is bankrupt, closed, has suffered a disaster such as a fire or economic crisis and can't keep its workers, has been restructured or renamed, or has died [M0123, M0124];
- the new employer is in the same sector and allowed foreign workers [M0126];
- your PLKS has 3 months left [M0130].

Not open in your 10th year or later, to workers legalised through 6P, RTK (Labour Recalibration) or other programmes, or to domestic helpers [M0127, Z0009]. Malaysia's home minister said in January 2024 that the government would also allow workers to be transferred between sectors in line with quotas [MP0009].

> **Need to know**
> - Are cross-sector moves now possible, given Immigration says they need Home Ministry (KDN) permission [J0063] but they have been reported as allowed [F0077, MP0009]?

**How the employers do it**

1. Both employers register on ePPAx (eppax.gov.my, the Labour Department's employer system); the old employer applies naming the new one, which uploads its documents within 7 days [M0121, Z0010].
2. The Labour Department investigates and issues a support letter [M0121, M0128].
3. The old employer (or heir) submits the free Ministry of Human Resources form and documents at the foreign workers division, Level 5, Setia Perkasa 3, Kompleks Setia Perkasa, 62546 Putrajaya, 03-8886 6501, Monday to Thursday 8.30 am to 4.30 pm, queue numbers 8.30 am to noon only [M0132, M0134, M0142].

> **Need to know**
> - How long does a change of employer take?

## Student visa (Student Pass)

A Student Pass lets you stay in Malaysia to study full time at an approved institution; it is not a work visa [S0023, S0042, S0013]. You apply through EMGS (Education Malaysia Global Services, a company under Malaysia's Ministry of Higher Education, KPT), for public and private institutions alike [Z0013, S0002, S0060]; the Immigration Department of Malaysia (IMI) gives final approval [S0003]. More than 10,000 Bangladeshi students were studying at Malaysian universities in June 2026, Prothom Alo reported; Bangladeshi diplomats put it at at least 11,000, the second-largest group of foreign students after the Chinese (about 62,000) [MP0364, MP0376].

### Step 1: Check the institution and the course

Check before you pay anyone: agents earn commission from institutions and push the colleges paying most [O0020].

1. **Check the institution may take foreign students.** A private institution needs KPT approval, an accredited programme and the Home Ministry's (KDN's) permission to take international students; a public university needs no separate check [S0001]. KPT's Department of Higher Education (JPT) lists private institutions with that permission, updated monthly [O0001, O0042].
   1. On [jpt.mohe.gov.my, Senarai Daftar dan Statistik IPT Swasta](https://jpt.mohe.gov.my/portal/index.php/ms/ipt/ipt-swasta/25-senarai-daftar-dan-statistik-ipt-swasta), click the download icon beside "Senarai Daftar IPTS Kelulusan KDN" [O0001].
   2. Open "Tahun 2026", then the "AKTIF" (active) file with the highest number (in November 2026, file 7, the list up to 31 July 2026, updated 20 August 2026) [O0040, O0041, O0004].
   3. Search it for the institution's name; the campus address must match the offer letter [O0003].

   If the institution is missing, is in the "TIDAK AKTIF" (inactive) file of the same number, or is on the page's list of cancelled approvals ("Senarai IPTS Yang Telah Diluluskan Tindakan Pembatalan Kelulusan Penubuhan"), it can't get you a Student Pass, since IMI needs its KDN approval letter, in force, with every new application [O0041, O0005, O0002, O0006].
2. **Check the course is accredited** on the [Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR)](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr/) of MQA (the Malaysian Qualifications Agency): type the course in "Name of Qualification", choose the level and press Search [S0087]. Only accredited programmes appear, and only for the campus named; a course run anywhere else is not accredited [S0084, S0085]. "No record" can mean provisional accreditation only, never accredited, or failed [S0086].
3. **A language or training centre** needs approval from the Ministry of Education (KPM) and the Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA), plus KDN's permission to take international students [O0043], and its students can't work part time or bring family [S0012, S0010]. To check one, contact KPM's Private Education Division: 03-8884 9536, bps@moe.gov.my, Aras 3, Blok E2, Kompleks E, 62604 Putrajaya; it acknowledges enquiries within 3 working days [O0044, O0045].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there a public list of KPM- or KESUMA-approved centres allowed to take international students?
   > - Where can you check a KESUMA-approved centre?

4. **Check you qualify:** 12 years of schooling and a recognised exam [S0043], and an English certificate, without which the application may be delayed and the pass won't cover the whole course [S0050]. The certificates accepted are:
   - MUET (the Malaysian University English Test) [S0050];
   - IELTS [S0050];
   - TOEFL iBT or Essentials [S0050];
   - PTE Academic [S0050];
   - Cambridge English [S0050];
   - Linguaskill [S0050];
   - OET [S0050];
   - ELS CIEP [S0050].
5. **Check the offer letter is real.** Agents have given Bangladeshi students fake ones [O0021]. The institution's international office can confirm by email or phone that it is genuine and the course is running [O0017].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is there an official EMGS or KPT channel for checking an offer letter?
   > - Where can you find the international office's genuine contacts?

### Step 2: Apply from Bangladesh

Apply from outside Malaysia [S0002]. You or the institution can create the application [O0063], but only one you create and pay for yourself on the Education Malaysia website can be refunded to you [S0078]. You can track it without an agent (Step 4) [S0080, O0046].

1. **Get and accept an offer letter;** the form can't be submitted without it [S0024, S0047].
2. **You'll need:**
   - a 35 x 45 mm photo on white [S0045];
   - your passport's data, visa and observation pages [S0045], the passport valid at least 18 months (for a pass covering the whole course, EMGS recommends 12 months beyond the course) [S0046];
   - the offer letter, all pages, as one PDF [S0045, O0009];
   - EMGS's Health Declaration Form [S0045, S0048];
   - your certificates and transcripts [S0045];
   - your English certificate [S0050].

   Keep your passport: agents have held students' passports to demand more money [O0018, O0023].
3. **Open an account and apply** on [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/) or the EMGS HUB app [S0024]. Enter the institution and course exactly as in the offer letter, your name exactly as in the two coded lines at the bottom of your passport's data page, and the passport number on the offer letter [O0007, O0008]. EMGS then asks the institution to confirm [O0012].
4. **Pay** in one of three ways [O0010]:
   - through the institution [O0010];
   - by telegraphic transfer (bank-to-bank international transfer): converted to US dollars, plus USD 50 [O0047, O0048];
   - by Visa or MasterCard on the EMGS site, plus 3% [O0049].

   Tuition handed to agents has failed to reach institutions [O0022]. EMGS's [fee calculator](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/student-visa/calculate-application-fees) gives, for a new application at a university or private higher education institution:
   - processing: RM1,728 [S0060];
   - eVAL (electronic Visa Approval Letter, IMI's approval to enter as a student): RM159 [S0061, S0062];
   - medical screening in Malaysia: RM340 (RM350 in Sarawak) [S0061];
   - Student Pass: RM60 [S0061, S0062];
   - i-Kad (student identity card): RM50, or RM60.60 couriered [S0061, S0063];
   - EMGS health insurance: RM498 to RM644, or a policy through your institution [S0064];
   - visa: RM20 [V0017].

   8% service tax is added to processing fees [Z0094]. Public-university students may do the medical at their university [Z0095]. If a card payment fails but your bank took the money, email enquiry@emgs.com.my with the bank's confirmation, transaction ID and application number [O0011].
5. **Refunds.** The first year's processing and eVAL fees aren't refunded once invoiced; convenience fees and service tax never are [S0077, O0050, O0051]. Before the institution decides, you get a full refund if you ask within 90 days of paying [O0053], through the online refund form in your account, logged in with the email you applied with; card payments come back by telegraphic transfer [O0052, O0058].
6. **Lodge the RM750 personal bond** (the Bangladeshi rate) with the institution, which signs a bond with Immigration for you; it is returned when you finish or withdraw without breaking immigration rules [S0059, S0049]. The form can be handed in after arrival, for the Student Pass sticker [O0059].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How and when does the institution take the money?
   > - What receipt do you get?
   > - How do you claim it back?

7. **If EMGS asks for more documents,** send them within 14 working days of the return date [S0020], or the application closes, and a refund must be asked for within 6 months of closing [O0054, O0055].
8. **Wait.** EMGS checks your academic papers, then IMI checks your passport and security and issues the eVAL [S0003]; EMGS's charter is 14 working days for a complete application [S0026]. If you're refused, see If the visa is refused below.

### Step 3: eVAL, visa and arrival card

The eVAL is valid 6 months; you may not enter before it is issued [S0027, S0028]. Then apply on [MyVISA](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my/), from outside Malaysia, for a Single Entry Visa under Visa With Reference (VDR), the category needing an approval letter (the eVAL) [S0004, S0029, S0053, V0007, C0006]. Account and payment work as in [Tourist visa, Step 2: Apply on MyVISA](#step-2-apply-on-myvisa) [V0021, V0022]; the fee is RM20 [V0017].

> **Need to know**
> - Does MyVISA's RM105 processing charge apply to students?

Before you fly, register the MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) within 3 days before arriving (see [Tourist visa, Step 4: Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)](#step-4-fill-in-the-malaysia-digital-arrival-card-mdac)) [V0058]; long-term pass holders are exempt [V0060].

### Step 4: Check your visa is real

1. **Check the eVAL on EMGS's own tracker.** On [visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my](https://visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my/): Student Visa, Track My Application, enter passport number and nationality, tick the terms box, press Track My Application [S0080, O0060]. The Immigration Department's (IMI) own status page sends Student Pass checks to EMGS, so this tracker is the official check [S0081].
2. **At 70%** Immigration has approved your eVAL; download it as a PDF from your own EMGS account, so the copy you carry comes straight from EMGS [O0013, O0014]. If the tracker does not show approval or progress stops, ask the institution's international office, which must confirm your application, or EMGS via [educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us](https://educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/contact-us) [O0012, O0084].
3. **Check the MyVISA visa** from Step 3 with passport number and sticker number, as in [Tourist visa, Step 3: Check your visa is real](#step-3-check-your-visa-is-real) [V0067].

> **Need to know**
> - Is there an official way to check an eVAL PDF itself (for example a reference number or QR code), beyond the EMGS tracker status?

### After you arrive

1. **At Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA),** go to EMGS's 24-hour International Student Arrival Centre (ISAC) before immigration (Terminal 1: Level 3 arrival hall; Terminal 2: Level 3A after the Skybridge); its staff help you through immigration, with priority lanes for students [S0082, S0083, O0061, O0062].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Which documents does ISAC ask to see?
   > - Does ISAC charge anything?

2. **Within 7 working days do the medical** at an EMGS panel clinic, listed on EMGS's site and app; without it EMGS can't start your pass [S0055, S0058, S0056]. If you fail, appeal at the same clinic within 3 days of being told [S0030]; if that fails, ask for any refund within 90 days of EMGS's notice [O0057].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How do you book the medical?
   > - What does the medical appeal cost?
   > - Which documents does the medical appeal need?
   > - What are the medical appeal's possible outcomes?

3. **The institution gets your pass sticker** with your original passport, its letter, EMGS's support letter, your stamped personal bond and at least 12 months' Malaysian health insurance [S0017]; IMI's client charter gives 5 working days [C0047].

### How long you can stay

The pass usually lasts up to a year, and renewing it needs a CGPA (grade point average) of at least 2.0 and 80% attendance, or an explanation letter from the institution [S0032, S0051]. Renew as early as 3 months before expiry [S0033], yourself or through the institution, on STARS (EMGS's Student Application Registration System) or the EMGS website [O0063, O0064]. You'll need:

- a 35 x 45 mm photo on white [O0085];
- copies of your passport's data, visa and observation pages [O0085];
- your certificates and transcripts [O0085].

Renewal costs RM270 to EMGS (private institution) and RM60 to IMI [S0060, S0007]. Breaking any condition can get the pass cancelled [S0016].

> **Need to know**
> - How long does a renewal take?

**Changing course:** the institution may file a Variation application once, within 18 months after your first semester, if you finished at least one semester at bachelor's level or above; otherwise it ends your pass with a Check-Out Memo and you leave Malaysia before applying again [S0034, S0036]. With an eVAL but no pass yet, you must leave to change course [S0035].

### Can you work?

Only with Immigration's permission for the specific job, under these rules [S0039, S0015]:

- **Who:** only university and private higher education students, not language or training centre students [S0012].
- **When:** in semester breaks or holidays over 7 days [S0013, S0039].
- **How much:** at most 20 hours a week [S0013, S0039].
- **Where:** restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels or on campus [S0013, S0039].
- **Never:** as a cashier, front desk worker, hotel singer, masseur, musician or guest relations officer, or student recruiter [S0014, S0040].

Students working outside these rules are deported, sometimes fined or jailed [O0019]. Claims of an agreement giving Bangladeshi students graduate passes to work are false [N0034]. Prothom Alo reported in August 2025 that about 10,000 Bangladeshi students were studying at Malaysian universities and that, unlike students of other countries, they had no access to Malaysia's labour market [MP0618]. A "graduate plus" visa for Bangladeshi students was among the problems discussed at Chief Adviser Muhammad Yunus's meeting with Bangladeshis in Kuala Lumpur on 12 August 2025, with more staff at the mission and regularising those who had become irregular [MP0609, MP0610]. On 13 August 2025 he raised a Graduate Plus visa with Malaysia's Higher Education Minister, Zambry Abdul Kadir, and Asif Nazrul, the interim government's adviser for expatriates' welfare, said afterwards that the minister had agreed in principle but that both sides had formalities to complete before it could take effect [MP0616, MP0617].

> **Need to know**
> - Has Malaysia given Bangladeshi graduates any post-study visa since the August 2025 agreement in principle [MP0616], given that Bangladesh is not among the Graduate Pass nationalities [S0044] and claims of an agreement have been called false [N0034]?

To get permission first, you'll need:

- the job offer letter [S0015];
- the institution's supporting letter with the break dates [S0015];
- your results and attendance report [S0015];
- copies of your data page and Student Pass [S0015];
- the employer's Labour Department (JTK) approval to employ a foreigner under section 60K of the Employment Act 1955 [S0015].

Go in person with an institution representative to IMI's Student Pass Unit at headquarters or the state Immigration office, which decides after checking the documents [S0041, S0015].

## MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)

A long-stay pass for people with substantial savings, run by MOTAC (the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture), for citizens of any country with diplomatic relations with Malaysia [E0101, E0100], aged 25 or over (21 for the SEZ/SFZ, Special Economic Zone / Special Financial Zone, tier) [Z0041, Z0043]. Whether the deposit can legally leave Bangladesh: see How to apply, item 1.

Malaysia's tourism minister told its parliament that 3,604 Bangladeshis held MM2H passes on 31 January 2024, the fifth-largest group after China, Australia, South Korea and Japan, out of 56,066 active pass holders including dependants; Bangladeshis had once been third [MP0027, MP0028, MP0029]. Prothom Alo gave the same 3,604 in September 2024 and in December 2025, when it ranked Bangladeshis fourth [MP0187, MP0744].

**Tiers** [E0106, E0108, E0114]:

- **Silver:** USD 150,000 fixed deposit and a home of at least RM600,000.
- **Gold:** USD 500,000 and RM1,000,000.
- **Platinum:** USD 1,000,000 and RM2,000,000.
- **SEZ/SFZ:** USD 65,000 (age 21 to 49) or USD 32,000 (50 and over) and a home in Forest City, Johor [E0106, E0115].

Prothom Alo reported in March 2024 that under the December 2023 restructuring applicants had to be 30 or older, hold a fixed deposit of RM5 million (Platinum), RM2 million (Gold) or RM500,000 (Silver), and spend 60 days a year in Malaysia [MP0030, MP0031, MP0032]. In December 2025 it reported that a 5-year visa comes with an investment of Tk 3 crore 60 lakh and a 20-year visa with more, on condition of investing in property [MP0744].

> **Need to know**

**Family** [E0104, E0110]:

- a spouse;

### How to apply

1. **Check the money can legally leave Bangladesh.** Bangladesh Bank controls money leaving Bangladesh under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act 1947 [Z0103]; in 2024 it was reported not to have approved sending money abroad for MM2H [N0051]; a Malaysian MM2H licence doesn't by itself mean an agent meets Bangladesh's rules [Q0050].    > **Need to know**
   > - What is Bangladesh Bank's written rule on sending money abroad for MM2H?
   > - How do you ask Bangladesh Bank for permission?

2. **Choose a licensed agent.** Every application goes through an MM2H operator licensed by MOTAC [E0101]. Check at [mm2h.gov.my/agencies](https://www.mm2h.gov.my/agencies) that the company name, licence number (MM2H plus digits) and licence dates match what the agent shows [E0116]. No agent can guarantee approval [Q0047]; for agents who vanished with clients' money, see Scams.

3. **The agent applies** through the One Stop Centre MM2H on MOTAC's online system; the Ministry of Home Affairs approves through Immigration [Q0041, E0118]. Fees:
   - processing: RM5,000 for the main applicant and RM2,500 per dependant [E0107, E0109];
   - participation: RM1,000 (Silver, SEZ/SFZ), RM3,000 (Gold) or RM200,000 (Platinum) [E0107, E0109];
   - MOTAC's system also lists RM500 plus SST (sales and service tax) per application [E0118].

   You need a Certificate of Good Conduct (in Bangladesh, a police clearance certificate; see Work visa: company worker (PLKS), Step 4) and Malaysian police screening [Q0049].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Is the RM500 in MOTAC's system extra to the processing and participation fees?
   > - What is the full document list?
   > - What is the processing time?

4. **After approval.**
   1. Everyone has a medical check at a MOTAC panel clinic or hospital [Q0039].
   2. Open the fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank; up to half can later be withdrawn for a home, education, medical care or tourism in Malaysia [Q0040].
   3. Buy a home within a year of endorsement and keep it 10 years unless selling to buy a dearer one [E0119, E0103].

   Immigration's client charter gives 1 working day to endorse the pass [C0047].

### How long you can stay

The pass lasts 5 years (Silver), 15 (Gold), 20 (Platinum) or 10 (SEZ/SFZ) [E0106, E0108, E0114, E0115].

- Under 50, spend at least 90 days a year in Malaysia [E0103].
- Renewal is RM500 a year plus a visa fee of RM0 to RM50 by nationality [Q0042].
- MM2H years don't count toward permanent residence [Q0015].

### Can you work?

Only Platinum holders may work or run a business [E0112, E0113, Z0019, Z0064].

### If the visa is refused

The Ministry of Home Affairs makes the final decision through the Immigration Department, and any appeal, if one is allowed at all, goes to the Ministry [Z0042, E0102].

  4. Track it under "Semak Aduan/Maklum Balas" [Q0045].
- Or call the MM2H office: +603 8891 7568 or +603 8891 7434; MOTAC, No. 2, Tower 1, Jalan P5/6, Presint 5, 62200 Putrajaya [Q0043, Q0044].

## Sending money home

**Use a licensed remitter, never hundi** (sending through an unlicensed agent) [B0007]. In 2022 Bangladesh Bank was reported to warn that hundi is a punishable offence, and money received in about 480 bKash and Nagad agent accounts suspended over hundi could not be cashed [H0045, H0044]. A money-sending shop must display its Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM, the central bank) licence or its certificate as a licensed company's agent; without one it is not authorised [T0061]. Licensed remitters such as MoneyGram, Western Union, NBL Money Transfer, Agrani Remittance House, Merchantrade and CBL Money Transfer cost about 5% to 5.5% to send RM610; banks about 9% to 16% [B0097].

You'll need:
- the receiver's bKash number (a Bangladeshi mobile-money account) and full name as registered with bKash [B0103];
- for Touch 'n Go eWallet (a Malaysian wallet app), a passport valid 12 more months and a valid work visa [T0016].

1. **Check the licence** in BNM's List of MSB (money services business) Licensees, [bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/msb-licensees](https://www.bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/msb-licensees), which also lists head offices and branches, or for an agent shop the List of Approved MSB Agents, [bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/approved-msb-agents](https://www.bnm.gov.my/regulations/fsp-directory/approved-msb-agents) [T0060, B0098, B0101].
2. **Send.** Ria, CBL Money Transfer, Merchantrade, NBL Money Transfer, WorldRemit, Wise, Touch 'n Go eWallet (no Malaysian bank account needed), Western Union, MoneyGram and others send to bKash [B0102, T0016]. At a partner bank, exchange house or agent, give the bKash number and registered name and hand over the cash [T0068, B0103]. Up to Tk 250,000 per transfer, any number of times [T0066].
3. **The family** gets the government's 2.5% incentive on legal remittances [B0103, B0104] and can cash out at BRAC Bank, City Bank and Q-Cash ATMs for Tk 7 per thousand [T0067]. Money can also be paid out through Bangladesh Krishi Bank, which has 1,038 branches and 5 sub-branches across Bangladesh, more than any other bank in remote areas [MP0726]. Krishi Bank, the second-largest collector of remittances in January to November 2025 (USD 277 crore), receives the most from Malaysia's Merchantrade, and also from CBL Money and NBL Money in Malaysia; its general manager said in December 2025 that families can now receive the money directly into their own account, 24 hours a day through Bangladesh Bank, instead of collecting it with a secret PIN [MP0723, MP0724, MP0725].

> **Need to know**
> - What does the remittance receipt show, such as a tracking reference?
> - How long does money sent to bKash from Malaysia take to arrive?

### If the money doesn't arrive

1. Complain to the remitter's or bank's Complaints Unit by phone (note the officer's name, date and time), then in writing with your name, account number and receipt copies; keep a copy. BNM won't take a complaint that skipped this step [T0017, T0062].
2. No reply in 14 days: go to BNMLINK, BNM's customer centre, at [bnmlink.bnm.gov.my](https://bnmlink.bnm.gov.my/) or Ground Floor, D Block, Bank Negara Malaysia, Jalan Dato' Onn, 50480 Kuala Lumpur (9 am to 5 pm, Monday to Friday), or call BNMTELELINK, 1-300-88-5465 [T0063, T0065, T0018]. For a bank, also ABMConnect (Association of Banks in Malaysia), 1-300-88-9980 [T0064, T0017].

Report hundi businesses to BNMTELELINK, 1-300-88-5465 [B0099, B0100].

> **Need to know**
> - Does BNMLINK take complaints against a licensed remitter?
> - Which Bangladesh Bank circular sets this year's 2.5% incentive?
> - How can a worker without a valid pass send money home legally?

### Saving and pension in Bangladesh

Bangladesh's Probash pension takes monthly payments from a worker abroad and pays a monthly pension from age 60; the rates and how to join are under [Your rights at work, Probash pension (Bangladesh)](#probash-pension-bangladesh) [J0071, J0072, J0075]. Your EPF retirement savings in Malaysia come back in full when you leave for good (Leaving at the end of the job, item 4) [J0017].

Bangladesh Krishi Bank has run several savings schemes for migrants, which held about Tk 220 crore by December 2025 [MP0726, MP0727].

> **Need to know**
> - Which Bangladeshi savings schemes or bonds for migrants can a worker in Malaysia buy, and how?
> - What are Krishi Bank's savings schemes for migrants, and can a worker in Malaysia join one from abroad [MP0727]?

## If a worker dies, is injured or loses the job

PERKESO (SOCSO, see [After you arrive, item 7](#after-you-arrive-item-7)) pays for injuries, disability and death in Malaysia, and the labour wing chases employers for compensation [U0001, U0011, B0136]. In Bangladesh the Wage Earners' Welfare Board (the Welfare Board; Probashi Kallyan Bhaban, 71-72 Eskaton Garden, Ramna, Dhaka) pays grants and handles the insurance bought at BMET clearance, which Jiban Bima Corporation, the state life insurer, pays out [U0099, B0060, B0059, U0026].

On 5 October 2024 the Welfare Board and PERKESO signed a cooperation memorandum on the health, occupational safety and welfare of Bangladeshi workers in Malaysia, under which every Bangladeshi worker there is to be registered with PERKESO [MP0207, MP0208, MP0209]. As stated at the signing, 1,215,536 Bangladeshi workers had registered with PERKESO, 711,655 of them active; by August 2024, 11,439 had received temporary disablement benefit totalling about RM15 million (Tk 37 crore), 364 permanent disablement benefit totalling RM1.27 million, and 175 funeral benefit totalling RM870,000 [MP0210, MP0211].

### If you are injured or fall ill

1. **Get treatment.** Free at a government clinic or hospital, or at a PERKESO panel clinic (a private clinic PERKESO appoints), which after a workplace accident checks your cover itself on PERKESO's SEHATi system [U0003, U0062]. Panel clinics are listed by state (Selangor: perkeso.gov.my/images/Klinik_Panel/Senarai_Klinik/SENARAI_KLINIK_PANEL_SELANGOR.pdf); PERKESO's line is 1-300-22-8000 [U0063, U0062]. Non-panel clinic costs can be claimed back from PERKESO [U0061]. At government hospitals SPIKPA covers you without a deposit (see [After you arrive, item 7](#after-you-arrive-item-7)), and sick leave must be reported within 48 hours (see Your rights at work) [I0019, M0025].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Where is a current list of PERKESO panel clinics for each state?

2. **Make sure the accident is reported and the claim filed.** The employer must report it to PERKESO at once, within 30 days at the latest, or face a fine or prosecution [U0021, U0059]. Covered: accidents at work, between home and work and during an emergency, work diseases, and since 1 June 2026 accidents outside work [U0001, U0002]. The claim is on Form 34-PA (perkeso.gov.my/images/borang/Borang34/290626-BORANG_34-PA.pdf); the worker or dependant signs as claimant, and the employer fills in its sections if you still work there [U0068, U0069].

   You'll need [U0015, U0016]:
   - Form 34-PA;
   - passport and PLKS copies;
   - your medical or sick-leave certificate;
   - for a road accident, the original police report or a copy certified by the issuing police station.

   Quote your 12-digit FWSS (Foreign Workers Social Security) number in every dealing with PERKESO [M0184].

   - Where and when: hand it in at a PERKESO counter and keep the stamped slip, or post it to the nearest PERKESO office (the form asks which office you want to deal with); head office: Menara PERKESO, 281 Jalan Ampang, 50538 Kuala Lumpur [U0022, U0060, U0071, U0074]. File within 12 months of the benefit falling due (the appeal board can extend this for good reason); incomplete, unclear or late claims can be rejected [U0064, U0022].
   - Cost: free; don't use agents or middlemen who charge [U0020]. A false claim can bring up to 2 years' jail, a fine of up to RM10,000 or both [U0023]. Progress: 1 300 22 8000 or perkeso@perkeso.gov.my [U0024].
   - If PERKESO refuses or underpays, appeal to the Social Security Appellate Board within 3 years; a Medical Board's disability assessment goes to the Appellate Medical Board [U0065, U0066, U0067].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does PERKESO accept Form 34-PA without the employer's sections when the employer refuses to sign?
   > - How does a worker find the nearest PERKESO branch's address?
   > - How long does PERKESO take to decide a claim?
   > - How is an appeal filed to the Social Security Appellate Board?

3. **What you can get.**
   - Temporary Disablement Benefit for medical leave of at least 4 days, counting the accident day [U0004].
   - For permanent disability, assessed by a Medical Board in Malaysia: up to 20% is paid as a lump sum; above 20%, one fifth can be taken as a lump sum and the rest monthly for life [U0005].
   - A 100% disabled worker needing constant care gets RM500 a month more, and PERKESO pays for physiotherapy, artificial limbs, wheelchairs and similar aids [U0006, M0172].
   - A worker certified invalid who hasn't paid Invalidity Scheme contributions long enough gets a one-time Invalidity Grant of both sides' contributions with interest [U0013].

   Payments can go to a bank outside Malaysia: give the account holder, bank, address, branch, account number and SWIFT code on the form, with a bank verification letter [U0070, U0018].
4. **If the employer won't report it or pay you while you are off work,** file a case at the nearest Labour Department office (offices by state at jtksm.mohr.gov.my/ms/hubungi-kami/alamat-ibu-pejabat-dan-cawangan) with your payslips, contract and attendance record, and give a copy to the labour wing (Minister (Labour) Syed Shariful Islam, counselorkl@probashi.gov.bd, +60 12-290 3252), which pursues compensation claims [U0075, L0037, B0117, F0029]. The Labour Court works as in route 3 (see If you aren't paid); if the employer ignores its Order, the Labour Department can seize and sell its property, take the money from its bank or prosecute it [U0029, U0030, U0076]. Sample message to the labour wing: "I am [name], passport [number], PLKS with [employer, address]. I was injured at work on [date] at [place]. My employer has not reported it to PERKESO. Please help me claim." Tenaganita and the Bar Council's Legal Aid Centres help with cases (see Where to complain, in Malaysia and from Bangladesh, Free help in Malaysia) [J0007, J0047]; the ILO (International Labour Organization) does not take complaints from individual workers [G0027].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Does Tenaganita charge workers for its help?

5. **Going home injured.** On your or your family's application, with proof of BMET clearance or Board membership and a passport copy, the Welfare Board asks the High Commission to bring you home [B0073]. The Board's ambulance at Dhaka and Chattogram airports is free on request [B0070].
6. **Treatment money in Bangladesh.** A returnee with BMET clearance or Board membership who is injured, sick or disabled can get up to Tk 2 lakh [B0071, B0072]. Within 1 year of return, the family applies to the Board's Director General via its web portal, the district probashi kallyan (migrant welfare) centre or the Board [B0071, U0092].

   You'll need [B0071, U0092]:
   - medical reports and prescriptions;
   - a bank statement;
   - a passport copy and a copy of its arrival stamp;
   - proof of clearance or membership;
   - one 6R (6 by 8 inch) colour photo of the worker.

   A Board committee checks the case by WhatsApp video call and recommends an amount; free, up to 60 working days after all papers are in [B0071, U0092].
7. **Insurance.** It pays Tk 10 lakh for total permanent disability and 10% to 90% of that for partial permanent disability [B0009]; see Claiming the insurance.

**Case:**

- **February 2026:** Md Jahangir Alam, 40, of Satkhira, who went to Malaysia through a broker at great cost in 2018, fell seriously ill there with a severe stomach problem and lost everything to brokers in the name of treatment in Malaysia without getting proper care; he came home on 17 February 2026 and died on 19 February at Dhaka Medical College Hospital, where no ICU bed could be found for him [MP0764, MP0765, MP0766]. His son, Afzal Hossain, 18, said the family had to mortgage land and sell cattle to pay for his treatment in Malaysia and his hospital costs after he came home, and that he had been unable to send money home regularly [MP0767, MP0768].

### If a worker dies

These steps are for the family in Bangladesh.

1. **Ask the Welfare Board to bring the body home.** Call 16135 (from abroad +8809610102030) [B0083]. Then apply in one of these ways, even if the worker had no BMET clearance or Board membership [F0074]:
   - on myGov (the government's online services portal), service "Application to bring home a migrant worker's body", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638167134, with a myGov account (register with your mobile number at idp-v2.live.mygov.bd/registration), the worker's passport and any clearance or membership; a tracking number comes by SMS and you can follow the case on myGov; free; up to 35 working days [U0049, U0091, U0051]; or
   - to the Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare, aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd, which also handles undocumented workers, describing what happened and the cause of death, with proof the person was a migrant worker (passport, work permit, BMET smart card, Malaysian ID, death certificate); free [B0051].

   Sample: "My [relation], [name], passport [number], worked for [employer, phone] in [town], Malaysia, and died on [date]. We want the body brought home to [district]. The receiver is [name, phone]."
2. **Home or burial in Malaysia.** The Board passes the family's choice to the High Commission, and the family sends the High Commission a claim or consent letter naming the receiver and their phone number [B0074, B0075, L0039]. For burial in Malaysia, apply on myGov with the service "Application for permission to bury a migrant worker's body in the destination country", https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638246958, free, 3 working days [U0083].

   You'll need [U0083]:
   - the worker's passport or visa copy;
   - any clearance or membership;
   - the district office's investigation report;
   - a certificate signed by all heirs on the union parishad (rural local council) chairman's letterhead.
3. **The Malaysian side.** The employer writes to the High Commission, appoints a funeral company and sends its letters, the insurance claim and compensation letters, the hospital letter, the death certificate, the Malaysian police report and the passport [L0038, B0119]. It pays the funeral and repatriation [M0148]. For its no-objection certificate (NOC) the High Commission also needs [L0023, L0024, L0025, L0026, L0027]:
   - the employer's PERKESO death report;
   - for an abnormal death (hanging, fall, accident, murder), a police report with NOC;
   - for an undocumented worker, the original passport (or a police report of its loss), the receiver's details, a police report with NOC, the original death certificate, the funeral company's letter and the employer's name and phone.

   The Board forwards the request within 3 to 5 working days, and the labour wing takes about 30 [U0050]. An undocumented worker's body can take much longer to come home [F0073].

   If there is no employer or it refuses, the Welfare Board pays [B0125, L0040]. PERKESO repays whoever paid (a person, company or the High Commission) up to RM4,500 of repatriation, even if the death isn't otherwise covered, on its PPJ (Funeral Repatriation Cost Claim) form, on which the employer states whether it paid; hand it in at a PERKESO counter or post it, with [U0010, U0072, U0073]:
   - a certified copy of the death certificate and of the claimant's ID or company registration;
   - the original receipt;
   - bank proof;
   - proof the body was sent home (the Ministry of Health's export permit or the airline's air waybill).

   Malaysian authorities trace the family through the permanent address and emergency contact in the worker's passport, so write a full name and a working phone number in the passport's emergency contact field; one body waited 15 days in a Kuala Lumpur morgue, at RM75 a day, because that field held only "0088" [U0088, F0068, F0069]. If no family is found, the worker may be buried in Malaysia as an unclaimed person [F0070].

   Death papers usually give the cause as illness or natural death [YB0023]; migrants say deaths are recorded as stroke or heart attack to avoid workplace-accident compensation, and Bangladesh doesn't check the cause [F0076, H0037]. If the family doubts it, apply to the Welfare Board for legal help abroad: myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638179893, at the Board, or through the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office (the district administration) or the DEMO, with proof of clearance or membership and a passport or travel permit copy with arrival and departure stamps; free; sent to the labour wing within 1 to 2 days; up to 20 working days [U0085, B0077].

   > **Need to know**
   > - Can a family ask the Malaysian police for a post-mortem or inquest?
   > - How does the family make that request?

4. **At the airport.** The Board's desk gives a Tk 35,000 cheque for transport and burial, collectable within 3 months, and a free ambulance [B0066, B0070]. You'll need [B0067, B0066]:
   - the worker's passport copy;
   - a heirship (warish) certificate from the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor;
   - the NID of the wife, father or mother;
   - the death certificate;
   - the NOC;
   - the original air waybill;
   - proof of clearance or membership;
   - a photo and NID or passport copy of the named receiver, and of the collector if different.
5. **The Tk 3 lakh Welfare Board grant,** for the family of a worker with clearance or membership who died in Malaysia, the country of the clearance [B0060, B0061]. If the family took the airport cheque, no application is needed: the Board writes asking for the papers; after burial in Malaysia, apply within 1 year of the death to the Board's Director General or through the district office [B0062]. The grant goes to the spouse, children and parents; for an unmarried worker with no living parents, to full siblings [B0063].

   You'll need [B0064, U0096]:
   - a family-members certificate on the letterhead of the union parishad chairman, mayor or city councillor, countersigned by the UNO or, in a city corporation, the regional executive officer;
   - an indemnity, undertaking and authorisation on Tk 400 non-judicial stamp paper, countersigned the same way (samples on wewb.gov.bd);
   - the NOC or death certificate copy;
   - a bank certificate or original statement for the receiver's account with the routing number (minor children's own account where they apply);
   - each family member's colour photo, attested by the chairman and the district welfare centre;
   - the worker's passport copy.

   Paid by bank transfer (BEFTN), with an SMS, within 60 working days of all papers reaching the Board [B0065, U0096].
6. **The insurance:** Tk 10 lakh for a death within 5 years of the smart card date [B0085, B0009]; who is covered: see Claiming the insurance.
7. **Malaysian compensation.** PERKESO pays:
   - a Dependants' Benefit for a work-related death [U0007];
   - a funeral benefit against receipts of up to RM7,500 for burial in Bangladesh (up to RM4,500 to whoever paid repatriation, RM3,000 to the dependants) or up to RM3,000 for burial in Malaysia [U0008, U0009];
   - if the worker died in Malaysia before 60 from any cause, a Survivors' Pension of at least RM550 a month, if Invalidity Scheme contributions were paid for at least 24 of the 40 consecutive months before the claim or two-thirds of the months since they became due [U0011, U0012, U0089].

   Claim within 12 months [U0064]. You'll need [U0015, U0017, U0018, U0019]:
   - Form 34-PA;
   - the worker's passport and work pass copies;
   - the death certificate or burial permit;
   - proof of relationship (marriage certificate, widow's ID and children's birth certificates; or, with no wife or child, the worker's birth certificate and parents' IDs);
   - bank proof;
   - Bangladeshi papers translated into English by an accredited translator and certified by the High Commission or a Bangladesh authority [U0019].

   For an accident, the Labour Department sends its Form H to the heirs, who fill it in and return it through the High Commission [L0036, B0120].

   How it works: when the High Commission reports that compensation may be due, the Welfare Board asks the family for a power of attorney and the papers, with free help from its legal cell, sends them to the High Commission and follows the case; the labour wing recovers compensation and unpaid wages, and the Board pays them to the heirs within 60 working days of having the papers and the money [B0076, U0097, B0136]. The family can also ask the Ministry (aswelfare@probashi.gov.bd), which writes to the High Commission and the Board within 7 working days [B0050].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How long does PERKESO take to pay a death claim filed through the High Commission?

### If you lose the job

1. **The employer must** tell the Labour Department within 30 days [M0065]. Under the MoU's standard contract it gives 2 months' notice or 2 months' wages, pays all money owed and pays your fare home unless you broke the contract [L0144, L0152, L0142]. It sends you home on a Check-Out Memo [M0229]; with under 4 weeks' notice you may stay in its housing up to 4 weeks [M0205]. You can't simply move to another employer (If you want to change employer) [M0123].
2. **To claim money owed,** go to the Labour Court (see If you aren't paid, route 3) [U0029, U0030, M0113] with:
   - your passport;
   - the workplace's name and address;
   - payslips and income details;
   - appointment letter and terms of service;
   - any termination letter.
3. **If you were dismissed without just cause,** file a written representation to the Director General of Industrial Relations, by hand at the Industrial Relations Department (JPP) office nearest the workplace, within 60 days of the dismissal or the end of the notice period, and keep the acknowledgement; union membership isn't needed and you can represent yourself [U0077, U0078, U0081, U0080]. JPP tries to settle, then sends the case to the Industrial Court, which can award compensation only if you asked for reinstatement [U0079, U0082]. A letter showing the case is pending usually gets you a Special Pass (see If you aren't paid, route 3) [J0033].

   > **Need to know**
   > - How does a worker find the address of the JPP office nearest the workplace?
   > - Can the representation be filed online?
   > - Is there a fee to file it?

4. **Insurance:** coming home within 6 months of going after losing the job pays Tk 50,000 [B0009, B0085]; see Claiming the insurance.

## Coming home

- **Leaving Malaysia with cash.** You may carry any amount of foreign currency, and ringgit up to the equivalent of USD 10,000; above USD 10,000 in total, declare it on Customs form No. 22, available before the customs checkpoint, or face a fine of up to RM1 million, up to a year in jail or both [U0032, U0033, U0034].
- **Customs in Bangladesh.**
  - Duty-free: 65 kg of baggage, 2 used phones and 1 new phone a year, and once a year, if declared, 100 g of gold or 200 g of silver jewellery (no more than 12 pieces of one type) [U0039, U0035, U0036].
  - Once a year you may bring a declared gold bar up to 117 g (10 tola; duty Tk 5,000 per tola) or silver bar up to 234 g; a bigger or hidden bar is confiscated [U0037, B0112].
  - No alcohol on a Bangladeshi passport [U0038].
  - Declare foreign currency over USD 10,000 on the FMJ form [B0115].
  - Customs emergency line: +88 01319-056591 [B0116].

  > **Need to know**
  > - May BMET card holders who have been abroad at least 6 months bring 2 new phones a year duty-free?

- **Airport help.** The Welfare Board's desks help with boarding cards, customs and lost luggage, take complaints of abuse or cheating, and send sick returnees to hospital by ambulance; Dhaka desk +8802 8901040, 01404 413388 [B0068, B0069, B0070]. Women returning within a year after abuse or fraud get Tk 5,000 cash [B0038].
- **A place to stay.**
  - The Wage Earners' Centre (Lonjonipara, Borua, Khilkhet, Dhaka-1229, near Shahjalal airport): Tk 100 admission, Tk 200 a night; book at wec.wewb.gov.bd or in person with proof of clearance or membership, a passport copy and the ticket; 01310 350555, 01754 715720 (24 hours) [B0080].
  - The Probashi Lounge at Shahjalal airport gives free wifi and phone calls and food at up to 30% off when you show proof of clearance or membership [B0082].
- **If your family is threatened or its property seized,** apply to the Welfare Board for legal help (myGov https://www.mygov.bd/services/info?id=BDGS-1638181581, the Board, the expatriate welfare branch of the Deputy Commissioner's office or the DEMO) with the worker's passport copy and any clearance or membership; free, up to 3 working days; the Board writes to the local administration, such as the Deputy Commissioner or Superintendent of Police [U0084, B0078, B0132].
- **A business loan.** Probashi Kallyan Bank (PKB, the government bank for migrants) lends returnees who start a project up to Tk 50 lakh, up to Tk 3 lakh without collateral, at 9% simple interest over up to 10 years; apply at the PKB branch for the business's area on its free form [B0021, B0022, U0055, U0052].

  You'll need [U0053, U0054, U0056]:
  - 3 passport photos;
  - passport and NID copies;
  - a certificate of your present and permanent address from the pourashava (municipality) or union parishad;
  - a current trade licence;
  - project details with 2 years' income and expenses;
  - the premises' lease or ownership papers;
  - a declaration of your own investment and other loans;
  - copies of your return papers;
  - 3 signed cheque leaves from the business account;
  - a guarantor (a relative or anyone able to repay) with 2 photos, ID copy, address certificate, a clean CIB report (Bangladesh Bank's credit report showing no unpaid loans) and 3 signed cheque leaves.

  Fees: 0.50% processing and 0.50% documentation, each capped at Tk 15,000, each plus VAT, paid when you take the loan; no service charge [U0090]. Decision within 15 working days of a complete application [B0022]. Helpline 16238, or +880 9677-787878 from abroad [B0018].
- **BRAC's help.** BRAC's Migration Programme gives returnees and trafficking survivors airport help at Dhaka through its Migration Welfare Centre, counselling, money or materials to start earning, and legal help [B0122, U0047]. In one case, Amir Hossain, a worker found in a Penang forest (Going home without valid papers), was received at Dhaka airport on the night of 21-22 April 2026 by BRAC's Migration Programme and his family, with the help of Civil Aviation Security (AVSEC) and the Probashi Kallyan desk [MP0329].

  > **Need to know**
  > - How does a returnee contact BRAC's Migration Programme?

- **Children's scholarships.** The Welfare Board pays migrants' children scholarships (Tk 27,500 a year at SSC level, Tk 34,000 at HSC) and reserves 0.5% of HSC admission seats for them [B0131].

  > **Need to know**
