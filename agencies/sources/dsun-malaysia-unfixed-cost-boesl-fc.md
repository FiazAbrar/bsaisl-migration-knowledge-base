# Unfixed migration cost grips Malaysia-bound workers with uncertainty

Rajib Kanti Roy. Published: 01 Nov 2026, 12:00 AM

The absence of a fixed migration cost for Bangladeshis seeking jobs in Malaysia has raised concerns about a return of syndicates, excessive recruitment charges and the exploitation of workers, as the two countries prepare to resume labour migration early this month.

“It is urgent to determine a realistic migration cost for workers going to Malaysia. The entire payment should also be made through the Overseas Employment Platform. The charges for each item, including the service fees of primary recruiting agencies and sub-agents, must be clearly specified. If necessary, the platform’s service charge could be fixed on a pilot basis,” migration expert Asif Munier told the Daily Sun.

When the government announced the resumption of the Malaysian market in 2022, it fixed the migration cost at Tk78,990 per worker. Workers, however, reportedly spent an average of around Tk5.44 lakh. The syndicate that controlled recruitment was reportedly run by a limited number of agency owners, including lawmakers of the then ruling party.

**BOESL cost put at Tk1.55-1.60 lakh**

Under the proposed arrangement, around 10,000 workers were initially expected to be recruited through the state-owned Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited (BOESL) without paying migration fees.

Speaking to reporters after a programme in the capital on Wednesday, State Minister for Expatriates’ Welfare and Overseas Employment Nurul Haque said sending a worker through BOESL is estimated to cost Tk1.55-1.60 lakh. The zero-cost arrangement will therefore not apply.

**Private agency costs undecided**

The state minister could not give a specific figure for recruitment through private agencies, saying the government has yet to finalise it. He said the ministry would consult agency owners and other stakeholders before reaching a decision.

Several of the 337 private agencies selected to send workers to Malaysia told the Daily Sun they are paying around 10,000 ringgit, or about Tk3.04 lakh, in commission to key individuals in the process for each work order.

One agency owner, who asked not to be named, said airfare and medical expenses would be added to this. “Therefore, if even the government fixes anything around Tk4 lakh, the cost will go over Tk6 lakh. And the rest of the things will depend on individuals,” he said.

Mamunur Rashid, an aspirant from Chandina in Cumilla, said a local broker has already asked him for Tk7 lakh.

“A transparent system should specify who will bear the costs of medical examinations, visas, air tickets, training and other necessary services. A centralised recruitment mechanism, publicly disclosed job orders, digital payment records and an effective complaints system must be developed,” Asif Munier said.
