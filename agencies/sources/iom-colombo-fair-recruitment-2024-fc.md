Bangladesh has established mobile courts to monitor the implementation of regulations and imposes sanctions in case of violations to national fair and ethical recruitment codes of conduct.

Only a select few recruitment actors in Bangladesh are licensed to recruit women migrant workers as they need to meet additional criteria and conditions to fulfill this role.

Bangladesh has been promoting zero-cost migration for workers in a number of destination countries including Jordan and Saudi Arabia. The securement by the Government of Bangladesh of bilateral agreements with South Korea, Jordan, Hong Kong, and Seychelles through the Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Limited has significantly lowered costs levied to workers, setting Bangladesh on a path towards the employer-pay recruitment model.

Since 2021, the Bangladesh government has also placed caps on costs levied to migrant workers across specified corridors through bilateral labour migration agreements with 16 countries.

To complement the revision and support its enforcement, the Ministry of Expatriates’ Welfare and Overseas Employment recently formed a technical committee to draft the ‘Rule for regulation of Sub Agent’ which are expected to further regulate the conduct of recruitment agencies and promote fair and ethical labour migration. As of 2023, only 60 recruitment agencies in Bangladesh are licensed to screen, match, recruit and place women migrant workers as they are required to fulfil specific criteria to ensure a gender-sensitive approach to recruitment.

Bangladesh issues top recruiting agents with ‘Commercially Important Certifications’ to recognize their performance and the quality of their recruitment practices. On the other end of the spectrum, the Government has also established a national Vigilance Task Force and Mobile Courts at field level to help identify and charge recruitment agents and intermediaries who engage in unethical recruitment practices, including the illegal extortion of recruitment and related fees.

With this objective in mind, Bangladesh’s Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training with the support of IOM and ILO developed and rolled out its Recruiting Agents’ Information Management System (RAIMS) in 2021. RAIMS works as an information management system for the Bangladesh Association of International Recruitment Agencies under which recruitment agencies, associations of recruitment agencies, and sub-agents in Bangladesh are required to digitally register. Through RAIMS, prospective migrant workers gain access to accurate information on available recruitment agencies, including on their licenses and licensed representatives, their rating (if available) and track record in successfully matching, recruiting and placing Bangladeshi workers overseas. The software also supports the protection of users against fraudulent brokers and is regularly updated to reflect developments on the recruitment market. Since its inauguration, over 1,300 recruitment entities have registered to the platform and the Bangladeshi Government expects to see all agencies register by early 2024.
