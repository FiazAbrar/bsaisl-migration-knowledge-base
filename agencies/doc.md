# Recruiting agencies

This list shows which licensed recruiting agencies hold the most job orders approved by BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) for each country, counted from the permission letters BMET posted between May 2023 and December 2025 [P0001, A0110]. Where one BMET posting covers several agencies, each agency's part counts as one letter [P0001]. A high count shows activity, not fair treatment: several of the busiest agencies face corruption cases [N0053, C0013].

## How the numbers were counted

The letter and worker counts in this list are our own count; no one publishes them. We downloaded every permission letter BMET posted at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, from 7 June 2023 to 10 December 2025, and read each one: 3,045 letters once reposts are removed [P0001]. A permission letter is BMET's approval for an agency to process clearance for a set number of workers for a named employer [A0112, P0004]. So:

- **Letters** are the permission letters naming the agency for that country. A posting that covers several agencies counts once for each.
- **Workers** are the posts those letters approve, not people who actually went. A permission BMET posted twice is counted once.
- **Gaps:** BMET posts only some of its approvals, and its list stops in December 2025. About 100 letters, mostly 2023 scans, couldn't be read for a country or a worker number and are left out of the country counts.

The data is in `agencies/activity.csv`, one row per letter with a link to its PDF; `tools/agency_counts.py` recounts every figure here from it.

## How to check an agency

1. **Check the licence on OEP.** Open oep.gov.bd/agencies (the agent list on the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system) and find the agency by its RL number, the recruiting licence number written RL plus four digits; the list gives name, address, office phone, licence status and validity date [A0109]. Compare these with the office you visited. An Active licence is not a clean record: Greenland Overseas and Navira, both in the ACC's (Anti-Corruption Commission's) Sep 2025 cases, still show Active [N0053, A0001, A0031].
2. **Ask the agency for the BMET permission letter for your job.** BMET issues one for each job order it approves, naming the agency and RL number, the employer with its visa and demand numbers, each job with number of posts and salary, contract length, working hours, what the employer pays for, and the most the agency may charge the worker (Tk 58,610 for a 2025 Saudi construction order) [A0112, P0004, P0005]. BMET posts letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos [P0001]. The newest letter there is dated 10 Dec 2025 and the newest entry of any kind 8 Feb 2026 [A0110, A0111]. **Need to know:** where can a worker check a permission letter issued after Feb 2026?
3. **Search the news for ACC and court cases.** The ACC has filed cases against about 100 agencies over Malaysia fees since Mar 2025, 60 of them not named in the BSS report [C0013, N0052]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare revoked 49 licences on 19 Jul 2026; each named agency is listed under red flags below [C0001]. No official grading of agencies exists, and the state minister said he could not confidently recommend any agency [Q0037, Q0038].

## Agencies with red flags

Unless a court is named, each item is an allegation or an investigation, not a verdict.

### Malaysia syndicate

The ACC alleges that agency owners used their posts in BAIRA (the Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) to form a syndicate that charged Malaysia-bound workers nearly five times the government rate of Tk 78,990 [A0113]. It approved cases against 12 agencies in Mar 2025, filed cases against 13 more in Sep 2025, added more in Nov 2025, and on 10 Dec 2025 filed a case against 124 people from 60 unnamed agencies [C0013, N0053, K0005, N0046, N0052]. Owners of three agencies outside the 2022-24 syndicate told Prothom Alo in May 2024 they paid syndicate agencies Tk 152,000-165,000 per worker to send workers in their name [K0002]. On 19 Jul 2026 the Ministry revoked 49 licences by gazette after a court accepted the charge sheet [C0001]. The High Court stayed the revocation on writs by New Edge International and others; the Appellate Division chamber judge stayed those orders by 4 Oct 2026, so the revocation stands [C0007, C0006]. In the separate Paltan trafficking case (103 accused), the CID filed a final report on 15 Jul 2025 finding no evidence, and the plaintiff challenged it [N0016].

## Agencies with evidence of fair recruitment

- **Bonanza Overseas (RL0579)**, Active on OEP [A0023]. It was the Bangladeshi agency in the 2018-21 fair-recruitment pilot of the ILO (International Labour Organization) and Qatar's labour ministry, for construction jobs with one Qatari employer [N0023]. It advertised jobs in local media and recruited directly in workers' villages to cut out fee-charging middlemen [N0024]. Workers' average cost fell from USD 3,408 before the employer changed its policy to about USD 300 after the pilot [N0025]. The evidence covers that one Qatar channel only [N0023].
