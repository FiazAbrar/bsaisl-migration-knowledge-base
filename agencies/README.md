# Recruiting agencies

This doc covers the licensed recruiting agencies named on the job orders BMET (the Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training) posted publicly, who is behind them, the gatekeepers above them, and what has been alleged or found against them \[[1](#o1)\]. The posted job orders cover a small share of the people who actually left: about 259,000 workers on the letters from June 2023 to December 2025, against 1,011,969 departures in 2024 and about 1.12 million in 2025 alone \[[1](#o1), [N1](#n1)\]. So the lists below show who holds the most posted job orders, not who sends the most people. A high count shows activity, not fair treatment: several of the agencies with the most letters face corruption cases, and only one agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment \[[N2](#n2), [N3](#n3), [N4 (2022)](#n4)\]. BRAC Probashbandhu Ltd (BPL, RL1199), which appears in this list, is a recruiting agency owned by BRAC, the organisation behind this guide \[[N5](#n5), [2](#o2)\].

## How to use this list

**Who you are actually dealing with.** Most people never meet an agency first: 78% of returnees in a survey by the ITUC (International Trade Union Confederation) heard of their job through a sub-agent or broker (দালাল) \[[N6](#n6)\]. Since 2023 an agency may appoint only a person registered under the rules as its sub-agent \[[3 (2023)](#o3)\]. **Need to know:** is there a public list of registered sub-agents a worker can check a broker's name against? The agency whose licence files your BMET papers is often not the one that sold you the visa \[[N7](#n7), [N8](#n8)\]. A Saudi worker who paid a broker Tk 5.5 lakh found no company and no iqama (residence card), and the licensed agency's owner told Channel 24 "they bought the visa from Saiful of Maulana Overseas; we only processed it" \[[N7](#n7)\]. Many routes into irregular work do not touch a recruiting licence at all: they run through travel agencies on tourist or visit visas, such as the Dubai tourist visa that starts the Libya route and the UAE visit visas sold with BMET smart cards in 2021-22 (see Libya and the UAE red flags) \[[N9](#n9), [N10 (2022)](#n10)\]. OEP's agency list covers recruiting licences only, so it will not show these travel agencies \[[2](#o2)\]. BMET's complaint form asks for the agency's name, RL number and address, or the broker's name, age, parents' names and address, so write down the broker, the agency you paid and the agency on your BMET papers \[[4](#o4)\].

**Before you pay anything:**

1. **Get the agency's RL number and check its licence.** The RL number is the recruiting licence number, written RL plus four digits \[[2](#o2)\]. Open oep.gov.bd/agencies (the agent list on the Overseas Employment Platform, BMET's online system) and compare its name, address, office phone, status and validity date with what you were told \[[2](#o2)\]. An Active licence is not a clean record: Greenland Overseas and Navira, both in the ACC's (Anti-Corruption Commission's) Sep 2025 cases, show Active, and so do agencies whose owners were arrested over girls who died in Saudi Arabia \[[N2](#n2), [2](#o2), [N11 (2020)](#n11)\].
2. **Ask for the BMET permission letter for your job (নিয়োগানুমতি).** BMET issues one for each job order it approves: it names the agency and RL, the employer with its visa and demand numbers, each job with posts and salary, the contract length, what the employer pays, and the most the agency may take from you (সর্বোচ্চ অভিবাসন ব্যয়), payable only by cheque, bank draft or pay order \[[5](#o5), [6](#o6)\]. The cap differs by job order: Tk 58,610 on Bonanza Overseas' Sep 2025 Saudi construction letter, Tk 19,560 on its Nov 2024 Qatar hospitality letter \[[5](#o5), [7 (2024)](#o7)\]. One Saudi letter lets the agency collect only once the visa, BMET clearance and flight are confirmed, and makes it give each worker a Bangla translation of the contract \[[6](#o6)\]. BMET posted letters at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos until 10 Dec 2025 \[[1](#o1)\]. Most Saudi-bound workers go on single visas, which come one at a time and are processed without a bulk job order (see Saudi Arabia) \[[N12 (2023)](#n12), [N13](#n13)\]. **Need to know:** does BMET clearance on a Saudi single visa produce a permission letter the worker can see, and if not, which document shows the most the agency may take? **Need to know:** where can a worker see a permission letter issued after Feb 2026?
3. **Check the visa and contract.** BMET says there is no such thing as a "free visa" (ফ্রি ভিসা) and asks workers to have any visa offered checked at BMET in Dhaka or the district manpower office (DEMO), and to see the demand letter and a contract attested by the destination's embassy \[[8](#o8)\]. In Saudi Arabia a citizen can get a visa to hire one worker for SR 2,000 with no check that the job exists, then sell it on; BMET's director general said in 2023 that more than half of Saudi visas come one at a time and only the visa's authenticity is checked \[[N12 (2023)](#n12)\]. **Need to know:** is the SR 2,000 the Saudi labour ministry's non-refundable work-visa issuance fee, or a refundable deposit as Prothom Alo reported? In 2026 the state minister said BMET can verify visas for only four or five countries, and airport immigration found one Oman spouse-visa approval used for five women on different passports \[[N14](#n14)\].
4. **If you are a woman going as a domestic worker, you should pay nothing.** An agency approved to send women domestic workers may take no migration cost from her \[[9](#o9)\]. In 2019 agency owners said they paid a broker Tk 40,000-50,000 for each woman and the Saudi recruitment office deposited at least USD 2,000 in the Bangladeshi agency's account once she arrived \[[N15 (2019)](#n15)\]. So the money flows to whoever delivers her, and the documented lure is a promise, not a charge: RAB (the Rapid Action Battalion, an elite police unit) said Concorde Apex lured women with high pay and a free Hajj \[[N16 (2022)](#n16)\]. If she comes back before the contract ends, the agency owes the Saudi employer a replacement worker or USD 2,000 \[[N17 (2021)](#n17)\]. **Need to know:** in 2026, do brokers hand women or their families cash to sign, how much, and what do they promise? **Need to know:** what does a Saudi office pay a Bangladeshi agency per woman in 2026? Anyone who offers to change your age on the passport or use another woman's passport number is using the trick at the centre of ACC cases (below) \[[N18](#n18), [N17 (2021)](#n17)\].
5. **Pay through the bank, and record every cash payment too.** BMET advises paying the agency through the bank and keeping the receipt \[[8](#o8)\]. But agencies typically take Tk 4-5 lakh against an official Tk 1.2-1.5 lakh and give no written receipt for the rest, and visa payments above the cap are usually made outside the banking channel \[[N19 (2024)](#n19), [N20 (2022)](#n20)\]. A bank receipt for the capped amount only proves the capped amount; a complaint can only recover what you can prove \[[N19 (2024)](#n19)\]. So for every cash payment, note who took it, when, where and how much, and keep a witness or a bKash or Nagad (mobile-money) trail \[[N19 (2024)](#n19), [4](#o4)\]. **Need to know:** at a BMET hearing in 2026, what does a worker with no receipt get: the permission-letter figure, or nothing?
6. **Government routes cost less, but brokers work there too.** BOESL (Bangladesh Overseas Employment and Services Ltd, the state-owned agency) charges candidates no fee, police say, though its routes carry costs: about USD 900 for Korea, Tk 2.31 lakh for Romania in 2023 \[[N21](#n21), [N22 (2023)](#n22), [N23 (2023)](#n23)\]. Brokers sell access to BOESL's selection: one woman paid a broker Tk 65,000 to get through BOESL's sewing test for Jordan, a worker alleged brokers bribe BOESL interviewers and mark their candidates with a nail-polish colour on a set finger, and in Oct 2026 a mobile court sentenced 12 brokers caught outside the Bangladesh-Korea Technical Training Centre who took Tk 40,000 to Tk 10 lakh promising jobs and exam passes \[[N24 (2018)](#n24), [N21](#n21)\]. BOESL is also among the 57 agencies whose records the ACC sought in its Jul 2026 inquiry into sending women to the Middle East on fake "returnee" passports \[[N25](#n25)\]. For Malaysia's 2026 zero-cost jobs, BOESL picks candidates by software lottery from online applications and then the employer tests them \[[N26](#n26)\].

**How to read activity and flags.** "Letters" and "workers" below show job orders BMET approved and posted, not workers who went and not how they were treated (see How the numbers were counted) \[[1](#o1)\]. A red flag is an allegation, an investigation or an official action; unless a court verdict is named, nothing is proven, and denials are given where reported \[[N27](#n27), [N28 (2024)](#n28)\]. Flags reflect which cases the ACC, police and press have taken up so far, mostly against owners tied to the Awami League government that fell on 5 Aug 2024; no flag does not mean an agency active now is clean \[[N3](#n3), [N2](#n2), [N29 (2024)](#n29), [N30](#n30)\]. An Active status says nothing about old flags: HA International, suspended in 2019 over women's deaths and sued by the ACC in 2025, shows Active \[[N15 (2019)](#n15), [N18](#n18), [2](#o2)\]. No official grading of agencies exists, and the state minister said he could not confidently recommend any agency \[[N31](#n31)\]. OEP shows each licence as Active, Expired, Suspended, Cancelled or Server Locked \[[2](#o2)\]. A BMET director said in Jan 2024 that BMET had locked many agencies' servers (সার্ভার লক), meaning their system access, over serious complaints \[[N32 (2024)](#n32)\]. **Need to know:** while an agency is Server Locked, can it still get clearances through another licence, and what must it do to be unlocked?

**Where to complain, and what happens.** Complaining is free \[[10](#o10)\]. Online, OEP's complaint module (opened 30 Jul 2026) lets a worker or family file, upload documents and track the case \[[N33](#n33), [11](#o11)\]. On paper, fill in BMET's complaint form (Form Ka), which asks for your NID, passport and BMET smart card numbers, your address and a relative's contact if you are abroad, and the agency's name, RL number and address or the broker's name, age, parents' names and address, and hand it with copies of receipts and the contract to BMET at 89/2 Kakrail, Dhaka-1000, your DEMO, or the Bangladesh embassy's labour wing abroad \[[4](#o4), [8](#o8), [10](#o10)\]. BMET is meant to settle it in 30 working days; if not, write to its complaint officer, the Director (Employment), diremi@bmet.gov.bd, 01711111544 \[[10](#o10)\]. The Ministry of Expatriates' Welfare's Enforcement-2 branch also takes complaints (sasenforcement@probashi.gov.bd, 02-41030255), and its hotline is 16135, or +880 9610102030 from abroad \[[12](#o12), [8](#o8)\]. OKUP (Ovibashi Karmi Unnayan Program, a migrant workers' organisation), 01842773300, gives free legal advice and help filing a case \[[N34](#n34)\]. In an emergency call 999 \[[13](#o13)\].

What actually happens: BMET writes to the agency and calls both sides to a hearing (শুনানি); agencies often ignore the letter or skip hearings, and the hearing ends in a negotiated settlement (আপস, which workers call সালিশ) \[[N35](#n35), [N19 (2024)](#n19), [N36 (2021)](#n36)\]. In 2025 BMET received 4,900 complaints, settled 2,054 and recovered Tk 9.92 crore \[[N35](#n35)\]; over 90% of 2025 complaints were about Saudi Arabia: no iqama, no work, late pay \[[N37](#n37)\]. Over 2019-23, 61% of complainants got no remedy \[[N19 (2024)](#n19)\]. Of 38 settled cases of returnee women that OKUP followed, 54% got Tk 5,000-20,000, because BMET counts unpaid salary as the compensation and not the migration cost \[[N38 (2021)](#n38)\]; OKUP's chair says proven cases settle for Tk 30,000-40,000 for women and Tk 50,000-60,000 for men, against Tk 3-5 lakh paid \[[N39](#n39)\]. At one hearing the agency's representative told a woman, in front of the BMET officer, to come to the agency's office to settle \[[N38 (2021)](#n38)\]. Where BMET does act, money comes back: after relatives of five Cumilla cleaners left without work or iqama in Saudi Arabia complained, BMET had them moved to another company and recovered Tk 1.3 lakh each from Arabian Services Company in Oct 2023 \[[N32 (2024)](#n32)\]. Since late 2021 police may not arrest a licensed agent directly on a trafficking victim's case; it goes to BMET for negotiation first \[[N17 (2021)](#n17)\]. **Need to know:** is that BMET-first rule still applied by police in 2026?

## How the numbers were counted

The letter and worker counts in this list are our own count; no one publishes them \[[1](#o1)\]. We downloaded every permission letter BMET posted at bmet.gov.bd/pages/miscellaneous-infos, from 7 June 2023 to 10 December 2025, and read each one: 3,045 letters once reposts are removed, naming 618 agencies and approving about 259,000 posts \[[1](#o1)\]. A permission letter is BMET's approval for an agency to process clearance for a set number of workers for a named employer \[[5](#o5), [14](#o14)\]. So:

- **Letters** are the permission letters naming the agency for that country. A posting that covers several agencies counts once for each \[[1](#o1)\].
- **Workers** are the posts those letters approve, not people who actually went. A permission BMET posted twice is counted once \[[1](#o1)\].
- **Gaps:** BMET posts only some of its approvals, and its list stops in December 2025. About 100 letters, mostly 2023 scans, couldn't be read for a country or a worker number and are left out of the country counts \[[1](#o1)\].

The data is in `agencies/activity.csv`, one row per letter with a link to its PDF; `tools/agency_counts.py` recounts every figure here from it.

**What the letters cover, and what they miss.** About 259,000 posts on 2.5 years of letters set against 1,011,969 departures in 2024 and about 1.12 million in 2025 means the letters cover well under a tenth of the people who left \[[1](#o1), [N1](#n1)\]. Each country section opens with its own ratio. The gap is widest for Saudi Arabia, where most workers go on single visas that rarely appear as posted letters \[[N12 (2023)](#n12), [N13](#n13)\]. No official source publishes workers sent by agency; per-agency counts appear only in ACC cases and in press reports quoting BMET clearance data \[[N8](#n8), [N40 (2024)](#n40), [N3](#n3)\]. For the 21 agencies with an RL number in the ACC's Malaysia cases, the ACC counts 113,477 workers they allegedly overcharged in 2022-24, while BMET's posted letters for the same agencies in Malaysia add up to 20,663 workers, about 18% \[[1](#o1), [N3](#n3), [N41](#n41), [N42](#n42)\]. **Need to know:** will BMET release clearances by agency and country for 2023-26 (under the Right to Information Act, or as it gave Prothom Alo and Kaler Kantho), so the biggest senders can be ranked by people actually sent?

**Most licences send almost nothing.** OEP listed 2,863 licences on 7 Nov 2026: 2,204 Active, 264 Expired, 235 Cancelled, 151 Suspended and 9 Server Locked \[[2](#o2)\]. Only 542 of the 2,204 Active licences appear on any posted letter in 2023-25, and the 50 agencies with the most posted workers hold 51% of them \[[2](#o2), [1](#o1)\]. On 30 Oct 2025 the Ministry cancelled 45 licences for not sending even 100 workers since they were issued \[[N43](#n43)\]. Idle licences are what the licence-renting and file-processing trade runs on (see Who is behind the agencies).

**The name on the clearance may not be the agency that recruited.** In the 2022-24 Malaysia round, agencies owned by MPs, a city councillor and Awami League leaders said publicly they sent no workers themselves; other agencies sent workers in their names \[[N8](#n8)\]. Agencies outside the syndicate that had their own Malaysian employers paid a syndicate agency a "chokro fee" (চক্র ফি, syndicate fee) of Tk 152,000 per worker to process under its licence, and a former BAIRA (Bangladesh Association of International Recruiting Agencies) senior vice-president says at least 817 agencies sent workers that way \[[N8](#n8)\]. Manpower traders told Kaler Kantho in 2024 that some MPs' agencies sent no workers and sold their demand (চাহিদাপত্র, the demand letter that people mean when they say "visa") to other agencies at Tk 35,000 per worker \[[N40 (2024)](#n40)\]. Bhaluka Overseas, a 2026 Malaysia principal with no Malaysia record of its own, says it earlier sent over 1,000 workers to Malaysia under other agencies' names \[[N8](#n8)\]. **Need to know:** when agency A sends a worker under agency B's licence, whose name appears on the worker's BMET smart card, and is the worker told?

**Other counts in `activity-other.csv`.** It holds the ACC's per-agency worker counts for 22 agencies (allegations of overcharging), Kaler Kantho's and Prothom Alo's counts from BMET clearance data, Japan's list of approved sending organisations, and the 45 licences cancelled on 30 Oct 2025 \[[N40 (2024)](#n40), [N8](#n8), [15](#o15), [16](#o16), [N43](#n43)\].

## Who is behind the agencies

**One licence each, on paper.** The 2025 licence rules bar anyone from holding more than one licence or a share in more than one; a family member (spouse, parents, dependent children, full siblings) may get a separate licence only with an office in a separate building, their own income, trade licence and staff \[[17](#o17)\]. A licence cannot be transferred, and no share or director may change without government approval \[[17](#o17)\]. Two Kakrail owners told Naya Diganta in Aug 2025 that they each held two licences; one said BMET server-locked one after complaints and he now trades on the other \[[N44](#n44)\]. A white-paper committee member says it recommended removing licences from people holding four or five, and the Ministry's senior secretary called granting licences "mainly a political decision" \[[N45](#n45)\].

**Renting licences and processing files.** With three quarters of Active licences on no posted letter, the trade is in using a licence rather than recruiting through it \[[2](#o2), [1](#o1)\]. A BMET licence-branch officer says some owners rent out their licence (লাইসেন্স ভাড়া) by making the renter a "partner" in a deal on stamp paper (স্ট্যাম্পে চুক্তি), and some renters took migrants' money in the licence's name and vanished \[[N44](#n44)\]. The other form is file processing: a visa trader with no licence, or a dormant licence holder, buys the visa and pays an active licence to put the worker through BMET, as Al-Ihsan's owner said he "only processed" a Maulana Overseas visa \[[N7](#n7)\]. Kamal Hossain, who has long worked by renting licences, alleged to Amar Sangbad in 2023 that BMET officials run the clearance syndicates, and an RV Overseas official said a last-minute clearance cost an extra Tk 25,000 per passenger \[[N46 (2023)](#n46)\]. Since January 2026 the law lets one licensed agency recruit through another as an "associate recruiter" (এসোসিয়েট রিক্রুটার) under a written agreement; the worker's contract is then with the associate, which shares liability for its part \[[18](#o18)\].

- **Need to know:** what does a Kakrail RL charge per file to process another operator's Saudi single-visa clearance in 2026?
- **Need to know:** what does renting a whole licence cost in 2026, and is it a flat monthly fee?
- **Need to know:** are licence rentals now being written up as associate-recruiter agreements under the 2026 rule?

**BAIRA.** BAIRA is the recruiting agencies' trade body, and its posts have been the route into syndicate places: the ACC alleges agency owners used their BAIRA posts to form the Malaysia syndicate, and BAIRA's elected president and secretary general become CIP (Commercially Important Person) ex officio \[[N2](#n2), [N47 (2021)](#n47)\]. It is not run by elected leaders now. The commerce ministry appointed Joint Secretary Mohammad Badrul Haque as administrator for four months from 28 Apr 2026 to hold an election, after the High Court extended the previous administrator's term and stayed the 2026-28 election schedule \[[N48](#n48)\]. BAIRA members allege a small group of agencies still dominates its decisions \[[N48](#n48)\]. In Aug 2026 BAIRA members told Desh Rupantor that agencies of five BNP MPs and a party leader, Matco Enterprise among them, were trying to form a new 25-agency Malaysia syndicate, with up to MYR 5 million (about Tk 17 crore) demanded per place; Matco's owner, MP Shafiqur Rahman Kiron, denies it, saying 423 agencies were approved on 10 criteria \[[N30](#n30)\]. **Need to know:** has BAIRA held its election since Aug 2026, and who sits on its committee now?

**The gates above the agencies.** These decide which agencies can operate at all, and agencies pass their cost to the worker:

- **Saudi embassy enlistment and visa centres.** In 2022 about 700 of 1,500 agencies were enlisted with the Saudi embassy in Dhaka and could submit passports, each capped at 30 a week (no cap for women), and agencies alleged they paid USD 220-250 per passport through middlemen to get visas stamped, which added at least Tk 20,000 to each worker's cost; the embassy said there are no fees \[[N49 (2022)](#n49)\]. Earlier attempts to route all visa submissions through a few agencies were contested as cartels: a 2018 "Drop Box" selection of 20-25 recruiters, and 2019 visa centres to be led by Unique Eastern's Noor Ali and Greenland Overseas' Abdul Hye at Tk 1,100 per passport \[[N50 (2018)](#n50), [N51 (2019)](#n51)\]. **Need to know:** in 2026, how does a Bangladeshi agency get Saudi visas stamped, and what does it pay per passport?
- **Malaysia's FWCMS.** See Malaysia: Bestinet, the company that runs the system, and its founder \[[N52 (2022)](#n52), [N29 (2024)](#n29)\].
- **Gulf medical centres.** Every worker going to Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Oman or Bahrain needs a medical check at one of the 133 centres approved by the GHC (Gulf Health Council, the Gulf states' joint health body), booked through its Wafid system (Qatar-bound workers take theirs at the Qatar Visa Center instead) \[[N53 (2023)](#n53), [N54](#n54)\]; the fixed fee was Tk 8,500 in 2023, and workers paid Tk 1,500 to their agency just to be registered for it \[[N53 (2023)](#n53)\]. Bangladesh Post (Nov 2024) alleges nearly 100 of the 133 centres are controlled by one syndicate \[[N55 (2024)](#n55)\]. In Aug 2025 approved centres alleged that a group tampers with the software that issues medical "slips" (the booking that assigns a centre) to send applicants to chosen centres, and that unapproved Dhaka centres charge several times the fee and issue certificates without tests, so workers are found unfit on arrival and lose the job \[[N56](#n56)\]. One worker was told by a centre in 2022 he could be shown TB-negative for Tk 1 lakh, and an Oman-bound worker paid Tk 6,000 over the fee \[[N53 (2023)](#n53)\]. The Ministry raised extra fees and harassment with the GHC in Nov 2025 \[[N57](#n57)\]. A worker declared unfit cannot retest for three months \[[N54](#n54)\]. **Need to know:** what does a medical slip at a chosen centre cost through an agency in 2026, and what does reversing an "unfit" result cost?
- **Air tickets.** Agencies and travel agents block group seats with nameless bookings: in Jan 2025 ATAB (the Association of Travel Agents of Bangladesh) said the Dhaka-Saudi fare rose from Tk 45,000-50,000 to about Tk 1.9 lakh, with some 60,000 seats on 11 airlines blocked, and in 2024 a syndicate of Biman officials inflated Malaysia fares so thousands missed the 31 May deadline \[[N58](#n58)\]. **Need to know:** what does a worker pay for a group ticket to Riyadh in 2026, and is it in the agency's package or extra?
- **Skill tests.** Saudi Arabia's Takamol skill test is covered in the Saudi Arabia country doc. **Need to know:** do test centres or agencies sell passes for the Takamol test in 2026, and at what price?

**What OEP and BAIRA records show.** The public OEP list has no owner field, so owners here come from BAIRA's member directory (a contact person and title, which may be out of date) and the press \[[2](#o2), [N59](#n59)\]. Matching phone numbers and addresses in the two directories shows agencies run from the same office \[[2](#o2), [N59](#n59)\]:

- **Matco Enterprise (RL0250) and Al Saad Labour Supply (RL0939)**, both Active, share an OEP phone (01711891957) and the same holding at 7014 Islambagh \[[2](#o2), [N60](#n60), [N61](#n61)\]. BAIRA lists Matco's proprietor as "Md. Shafiqur Rahman Kiron, M.P."; BSS, the state news agency, calls Matco the business of Shariatpur-2 BNP MP Kiron with Farhana Kadir Rahman as chairman; Asian TV says she is his wife; BAIRA lists her as Al Saad's proprietor \[[N60](#n60), [N62](#n62), [N63](#n63), [N61](#n61)\]. Together they hold UAE letters for 6,585 workers \[[1](#o1)\]. The Desh Rupantor report on the attempted new Malaysia syndicate also names Airway International (RL1016) and Surma International (RL0299), both Active \[[N30](#n30), [2](#o2)\].
- **The Abul Bashar family, BH Plaza, Jagannathpur.** The late three-time BAIRA president owned Sarkar Recruiting Agency (RL0226, Expired), on the 8th floor of BH Plaza \[[N64](#n64), [2](#o2)\]. BNS Overseas (RL1450, his son's per Prothom Alo) is on the 7th floor with the next phone number in the same series, and PR Overseas (RL1928, his son-in-law's) gives the same address; both are Cancelled \[[N28 (2024)](#n28), [2](#o2), [N65](#n65)\].
- **5M International (RL1327, ex-MP Masud Uddin Chowdhury's per the ACC, Cancelled) and 1 Plus Overseas (RL1857, Active to Mar 2027)** share an OEP phone and the same BAIRA address down to the floor, Sikder Plaza, House 79, Level 8, Banani; BAIRA lists Tasnia Masud as 1 Plus's MD \[[N3](#n3), [2](#o2), [N66](#n66), [N67](#n67)\]. **Need to know:** is Tasnia Masud related to Masud Uddin Chowdhury, and who holds 1 Plus's shares?
- **Amin Tours and Travels (RL0981, Cancelled; owner Ruhul Amin per the ACC) and Malaysia Bangladesh Holdings (RL1675, Active)** share an address, 95 Road 7, Uttara, and a phone \[[N42](#n42), [2](#o2)\]. **Need to know:** who owns Malaysia Bangladesh Holdings?

**Politicians and officials.** In 2024 the ACC alleged that besides the agencies of three MPs and a family member of another, many Awami League leaders, city councillors and new companies were sending large numbers of workers to Malaysia \[[N68 (2024)](#n68)\]. Snigdha Overseas, licensed to ex-MP Nizam Hazari in Dec 2018, sent 100 workers in three and a half years before the round, and Ahmed International (ex-MP Benazir Ahmed, BAIRA president when the network formed) 238, then 7,849 \[[N68 (2024)](#n68)\]. In Kuwait, court sources said Job Bank International (RL1183), owned by then MP Papul's brother Kazi Badrul, sent people at Tk 5-7 lakh each to Papul's company Marafie Kuwaitia; its accountant confessed to it (Daily Star, Aug 2020) \[[N69 (2020)](#n69)\]. In Jul 2026 the ACC served an asset-statement notice on Golam Mostafa, MD of Prantik Travels and Tourism (RL0310, Active), alleging assets beyond his known income in his own and others' names \[[N70](#n70), [2](#o2)\].

**Who the big players are, by the government's own measure.** Since 2021 the Ministry can name up to 20 recruiting agents a year CIP (foreign employment), and the main test is volume: at least 2,000 workers sent in the period assessed \[[N47 (2021)](#n47)\]. The list is published as a gazette \[[N47 (2021)](#n47)\]. **Need to know:** which agency owners were named CIP (foreign employment) in 2022-26, and where is the gazette?

## Saudi Arabia

Posted letters cover about 5% of the flow: 70,145 workers on letters over 2.5 years, against about 628,000 departures in 2024 and 752,715 in 2025 \[[1](#o1), [N71](#n71), [N1](#n1)\]. So the letters cannot show who the big Saudi senders are.

**Group and single visas.** Saudi visas for Bangladeshi workers come as group visas (more than 25 workers for one company), which need attestation by the Bangladesh embassy, or single visas, issued one or a few at a time \[[N13](#n13)\]. BMET's director general said in 2023 that more than half come singly \[[N12 (2023)](#n12)\]. Attestation for single visas was relaxed for years; when BMET enforced it again from late January 2025 \[[N72](#n72)\], Saudi departures fell from 76,618 in January to 44,258 in February, and after BAIRA protests BMET let single visas issued up to 28 February go without attestation \[[N13](#n13), [N72](#n72)\].

**How a Saudi visa is sold.** The chain, from the root down:

- A Saudi employer, or a citizen with no real job to offer, gets visa numbers; Prothom Alo reported SR 2,000 per visa with no check that the job exists \[[N12 (2023)](#n12)\].
- Middlemen in Saudi Arabia, Saudi or Bangladeshi, buy the visas and sell them to Dhaka agencies or traders; one agency owner said in 2022 that agencies collecting visas from companies paid at least SR 5,000 (Tk 1.2 lakh) each \[[N73 (2023)](#n73), [N20 (2022)](#n20)\].
- A Dhaka visa trader sells the visa on, and a licensed agency processes it at BMET; the Maulana Overseas case above is this link \[[N7](#n7)\].
- The broker in the village collects from the worker. In 2022 the government cost for Saudi Arabia was Tk 1.65 lakh while workers paid Tk 3.5-4 lakh \[[N49 (2022)](#n49)\].
- On a so-called free visa (ফ্রি ভিসা, where the sponsor has no job and the worker must find work), the worker also pays about Tk 2 lakh for the iqama and spends Tk 3-3.5 lakh more in Saudi Arabia \[[N20 (2022)](#n20)\].

In March 2023 Saudi Arabia's anti-corruption authority, Nazaha, arrested the former head and deputy head of the Saudi embassy's consular section in Dhaka, who it said took SR 54 million for finalising work visas, along with Bangladeshi residents, one the owner of a recruitment office in Bangladesh, who admitted visa trading with embassy staff's complicity \[[N73 (2023)](#n73), [N74 (2023)](#n74)\]. Bangladesh and Saudi Arabia agreed in 2019 to stop visa trading, with no visible result by 2022 \[[N75 (2019)](#n75), [N20 (2022)](#n20)\]. **Need to know:** what does each Saudi visa type sell for in Dhaka in 2026: cleaner, company labourer, house driver, private driver?

## Libya

Libya is the first leg of the boat route to Italy. Bangladeshis made up 2,510 of the 8,489 crossings Frontex (the EU border agency) recorded on the Central Mediterranean route in Jan-Apr 2026, nearly one in three, and 20,259 Bangladeshis reached Europe by the Mediterranean in 2025 \[[N9](#n9), [N96](#n96)\]. The usual path is a tourist visa to Dubai, then Egypt, then Benghazi; many were told they were going to Kuwait or Malaysia and found out in Dubai or Cairo \[[N9](#n9)\]. People who enter Libya legally by air are promised work while they wait for a "next visa" to Italy, then lose their passports and are sold on \[[N97](#n97)\]. A full Italy package costs USD 10,000-14,000 and reaches USD 15,000-17,000 with charges inside Libya; on Facebook and TikTok it is sold as "Full Italy package Tk14 lakh" and the crossing is called a "game", and people told they can pay on arrival in Italy are held in Libya while their families are asked for Tk 25-30 lakh \[[N9](#n9), [N96](#n96)\]. After 26 Bangladeshis were killed at Mizda, Libya, in 2020, the CID arrested a recruiting agency's chairman, Sheikh Mahbubur Rahman, and his brother, its MD, and seized about 300 passports from two agencies \[[N98 (2020)](#n98)\].

BMET's posted Libya letters approve 1,209 posts across 13 agencies, led by Godhuli Overseas (RL1092, 385) and Arabian Overseas (RL2435, 370); Rabbi International (RL0258, 165) is a revoked Malaysia-syndicate agency \[[1](#o1), [2](#o2), [N92](#n92)\]. **Need to know:** are the employers on these Libya letters real, and how many of the workers cleared for Libya in 2023-25 are now in Italy?

### Saudi Arabia and the Gulf: women domestic workers

## Agencies with evidence of fair recruitment

Only one private agency has outside evidence of fair recruitment \[[N4 (2022)](#n4), [N127](#n127)\].

- **Bonanza Overseas (RL0579)**, Active on OEP \[[2](#o2)\]. It was the Bangladeshi agency in the 2018-21 fair-recruitment pilot of the ILO and Qatar's labour ministry, for construction jobs with one Qatari employer \[[N4 (2022)](#n4)\]. The employer moved first: STS, the company placing workers on QDVC construction sites and Bonanza's long-time client, adopted a no-fee policy in Jan 2018, and their service agreement was rewritten to bar any fee to the worker and list every cost the employer pays \[[N128](#n128)\]. Bonanza advertised jobs in local media and recruited directly in workers' villages to cut out fee-charging brokers, and set up grievance forms and a call centre before departure \[[N128](#n128)\]. Workers' average cost fell from USD 3,408 before the employer changed its policy to about USD 300 after the pilot, mostly their own passport and travel in Bangladesh, and STS dismissed 21% of earlier recruits in their first 18 months against none of the pilot's \[[N128](#n128)\]. The low cap continues on a new Qatar line: its Nov 2024 permission for 210 hospitality jobs in Doha lets it take at most Tk 19,560 from each worker, against Tk 58,610 on its Sep 2025 Saudi letter \[[7 (2024)](#o7), [5](#o5)\]. The evidence covers its Qatar work only \[[N4 (2022)](#n4)\]. **Need to know:** what did Bonanza's 2024-25 Doha hospitality recruits actually pay, including any broker's cut?

No Bangladeshi agency appears on the list of recruiters that have applied for IOM's IRIS ethical-recruitment certification \[[N127](#n127)\]. **Where costs did fall, the channel was the government's.** Under the 2012 Malaysia G2G (government-to-government) plantation scheme, BMET put the cost at Tk 32,100 per worker, about Tk 40,000 with passport and trips to Dhaka, against Tk 2.5-3 lakh through private agencies; it raised migrants' three-year net earnings by 87%, but only 7,616 went by Jun 2015 out of 1.45 million registered \[[N129 (2016)](#n129), [N22 (2023)](#n22)\]. Korea EPS through BOESL costs about USD 900, and Jordan garments through BOESL cost women Tk 14,000-25,000, though brokers sell places in BOESL's selection (How to use this list, step 6) \[[N22 (2023)](#n22), [N24 (2018)](#n24)\]. BOESL itself received demand for over 10,000 Malaysia workers in 2022 and sent about 1,500 \[[N8](#n8)\]. Prof. C R Abrar of RMMRU (the Refugee and Migratory Movements Research Unit) says the employer-pays principle has never been implemented in Bangladesh \[[N130](#n130)\].
